**Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Question:** What analytic Hénon-specific structure can be added to the
existing self-adjoint Riemann--von Mangoldt-clock family without importing
zero data or relabelling a known dynamical zeta as the Riemann zeta function?

## 2. What the internal 345-route death log excludes

The earlier prime-dynamics programme has already tested finite spectral
clouds, Riesz packets, determinant truncations, graded superloops, selectors,
counterloops, moving prefixes, annular Hardy controls, and first-alias phase
compensation.  Its sharp counterexamples show that none of the following can
serve as a P or Z bridge:

- matching any fixed number of moments or determinant coefficients;
- matching rank, Hilbert--Schmidt mass, spectral cap, or a finite Riesz packet;
- fitting a finite endpoint or a finite spectral cloud;
- adding a scalar parity/cubic phase or a hand-chosen selector;
- replacing the actual operator by a synthetic normal realization;
- asserting that a small finite Euler--Ulam gap identifies a global divisor.

Root-of-unity hidden shells can leave an arbitrarily long fixed prefix
unchanged while altering the later divisor.  The first-alias analyses also
show that termwise absolute-value bounds destroy the signed cancellation one
would need.  A new candidate must therefore exhibit a fixed operator and an
exact analytic invariant, not another longer finite prefix.

## 3. The special difficulty at \(a=1.02\)

The legacy Hénon paper fixed \(a\approx1.02\) by a finite-resolution,
zero-exposed lineage and reported a numerical homoclinic threshold near
\(1.00561\).  The parameter is scientifically interesting but is not derived
by the present mean-clock theorem and is not statistically blinded.

Existing periodic-orbit/operator work also gives a direct warning.  At
\(a=1.02\), period cutoff eight recovers two hyperbolic primitive orbits and
one elliptic orbit; the leading Ulam modulus is about \(0.996834\), while the
formal Euler and Fredholm values differ materially.  The same construction is
clean only in the strongly hyperbolic control window near \(a=5\)--6.  Hence a
global Ruelle product at \(a=1.02\) is not admissible.  Any orbit route must
first isolate a certified local hyperbolic invariant set.

## 5. Three live structural gaps

### Gap S: analytic spectral activity

**Closed for operator spectral activity by P300-GS and R300-P1.**  For the
same fixed operator, the Hénon warp has two consequences that cannot be
removed by a coordinate change: a strict ground-state increase and a uniform
relative heat asymptotic.  The radial and Hénon potentials are equimeasurable,
so the entire classical heat term cancels, while the first quantum gradient
invariant is nonzero.  This does not close the stronger classical-chaos or
arithmetic-prime gates.

### Gap P: self-adjoint prime-power trace

The existing generalized third-resolvent spectral shift is a valid signed
container, but it has no prime times or amplitudes.  Fixed-complexity Hénon
periods also shrink in the relevant high-energy scaling and cannot by
themselves generate fixed times \(r\log p\).  This gap remains open.

### Gap \(P^*\): local generalized primes

A transverse homoclinic orbit at \(a=1.02\) would imply a local horseshoe.
Primitive cycles on that invariant set, equipped with an unstable-Jacobian
roof, give an exact Ruelle log-derivative of von-Mangoldt form.  This is a
useful analytic module, but its primitive cycles are generalized dynamical
primes, not rational primes, and it is not yet coupled to the Paper 7
Schrödinger trace.

## 6. Search decision

The highest-information order is:

1. test whether the relative heat trace closes S analytically;
2. in parallel, test whether \(a=1.02\) admits a numerically robust local
   transverse homoclinic intersection suitable for later interval proof;
3. do not resume P or Z until one of these exact structural modules survives.
