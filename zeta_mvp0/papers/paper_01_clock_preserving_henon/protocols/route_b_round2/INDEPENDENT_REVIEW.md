## Main criticisms of the selected idea

### C1. A formal heat coefficient is not a theorem

The present potentials grow exponentially after a polynomial warp, and the
radial/Hénon difference is not a short-range perturbation.  Standard compact
or polynomial-potential heat expansions cannot simply be quoted.  A uniform
Feynman--Kac or resummed-parametrix remainder estimate is the main proof task.

**Response:** R300 separates the exact gradient carrier from the remainder
gate.  It may pass the former and fail the latter; the claim ledger records
those outcomes separately.

### C2. Spectral activity is weaker than dynamical activity

A nonzero relative heat trace proves non-isospectrality and an active Hénon
geometry, but does not prove positive-measure classical chaos, quantum unique
ergodicity, or a prime trace.

**Response:** define S narrowly as non-removable spectral activity for this
round.  Keep stronger chaos statements numerical and keep P/Z open.

### C3. The result may be a standard rearrangement corollary

The global heat-trace ordering is likely inherited from classical
rearrangement theory.

**Response:** cite that theorem as a tool.  The candidate contribution is the
explicit Hénon coefficient plus a noncompact asymptotic proof within the exact
RvM-clock family.

### C4. \(a=1.02\) remains historically exposed

The coefficient is nonzero for every \(a\ne0\); the calculation does not
select 1.02 arithmetically.

**Response:** state this as a feature and a limitation.  The theorem is a
family theorem; \(a=1.02\) is the prior-frozen case study, not a newly derived
constant.
