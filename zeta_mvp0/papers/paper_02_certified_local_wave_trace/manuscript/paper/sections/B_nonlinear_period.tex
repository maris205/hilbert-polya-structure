\section{Normal-coordinate tensors and nonlinear period}
\label{app:nonlinear-period}

Expand the potential in the orthonormal normal coordinates of
\cref{eq:harmonic-normal-form}:
\begin{equation}
 V_a(Q)=2\pi+\frac{1}{2}\sum_j\omega_j^2Q_j^2
 +\frac{1}{6}\sum_{ijk}C_{ijk}Q_iQ_jQ_k
 +\frac{1}{24}\sum_{ijkl}D_{ijkl}Q_iQ_jQ_kQ_l
 +O(|Q|^5).
 \label{eq:potential-taylor}
\end{equation}
Use the strained time \(\tau=\Omega t\) and the ansatz
\begin{align}
 Q_+(t)&=A\cos\tau
 +A^2(b_{+0}+b_{+2}\cos2\tau)+O(A^3),
 \label{eq:pl-fast}\\
 Q_-(t)&=A^2(b_{-0}+b_{-2}\cos2\tau)+O(A^3),
 \label{eq:pl-slow}\\
 \Omega&=\omega_++\nu_+A^2+O(A^3).
 \label{eq:pl-frequency}
\end{align}
There is no order-\(A\) slow component because the branch is tangent to the
fast eigenspace.

At order \(A^2\), \(\cos^2\tau=(1+\cos2\tau)/2\) separates the constant and
second-harmonic equations.  Solving them yields
\begin{equation}
 b_{j0}=-\frac{C_{++j}}{4\omega_j^2},
 \qquad
 b_{j2}=-\frac{C_{++j}}{4(\omega_j^2-4\omega_+^2)}.
 \label{eq:pl-b-proof}
\end{equation}
At order \(A^3\), the resonant \(\cos\tau\) coefficient must vanish:
\begin{equation}
 -2\omega_+\nu_+
 +\frac{D_{++++}}8
 +\sum_{j\in\{-,+\}}C_{++j}
 \left(b_{j0}+\frac12b_{j2}\right)=0.
 \label{eq:resonance-solvability}
\end{equation}
This proves \cref{eq:nu-plus}.  Since
\begin{equation}
 E-2\pi=\frac12\omega_+^2A^2+o(A^2),
 \qquad T=\frac{2\pi}{\Omega},
 \label{eq:energy-amplitude}
\end{equation}
differentiation gives \cref{eq:period-slope}.  Integrating
\(\dd S/\dd E=T\) gives the factor one-half between the linear correction in
\cref{eq:period-expansion} and the correction to \(S/\delta\) in
\cref{eq:action-expansion}.

The normal basis has a deterministic sign convention in the archive.
Reversing one eigenvector changes individual cubic tensors but leaves the
final period slope invariant.  The reported \(\nu_+\) is quoted to reproduce
the frozen coordinate convention, not as a coordinate-free observable.
