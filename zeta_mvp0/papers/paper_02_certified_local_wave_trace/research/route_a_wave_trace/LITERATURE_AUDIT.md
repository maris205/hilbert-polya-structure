# Route A4 Primary-Source Literature and Novelty Audit

## Bottom line

An energy-localized fixed-energy semiclassical trace formula for a smooth
confining Schrödinger operator is established theory.  The project must not
claim that framework as new.  The model-specific contribution is instead:

1. exact bottom normal-mode splitting for this equimeasurable Hénon warp;
2. an explicit fast Lyapunov period/action/stability oracle;
3. a time window excluding the radial reference near the bottom;
4. a relative microlocal specialization with auditable claim boundaries;
5. eventually, a uniform high-energy theorem for a simultaneous hard-wall
   and Hénon-metric limit.

No directly matching treatment of this exponential Hénon-warped
equimeasurable pair was located in the searched primary-source corpus.  This
is a scoped search statement, not an absolute priority claim.

## Direct fixed-energy trace theorems

### Brummelhuis and Uribe (1991)

R. Brummelhuis and A. Uribe, “A Semi-Classical Trace Formula for Schrödinger
Operators,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* **136**(3), 567–584
(1991). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02099074).

**Supports:** semiclassical trace expansions for smooth Schrödinger
operators on \(\mathbb R^n\), including clean periodic flow, when the fixed
energy is below the limiting potential at infinity.

**Does not support:** automatic cleanliness of the present radial flow, a
uniform \(E\to\infty\) expansion, or arithmetic interpretation.

### Combescure, Ralston, and Robert (1999)

M. Combescure, J. Ralston, and D. Robert, “A Proof of the Gutzwiller
Semiclassical Trace Formula Using Coherent States Decomposition,”
*Communications in Mathematical Physics* **202**(2), 463–480 (1999).
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s002200050591),
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/9807005).

**Supports:** the regularized density

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
 \chi(\widehat H)\widehat A\chi(\widehat H)
 g\!\left(\frac{E-\widehat H}{\hbar}\right)
 \right]
\]

and, for isolated nondegenerate periodic orbits, the explicit data

\[
 T_\gamma^\#,\quad S_\gamma,\quad \mu_\gamma,\quad
 |\det(I-P_\gamma)|^{-1/2}.
\]

Remark 2.5 is particularly important here: for Schrödinger potentials, a
compact energy surface below \(\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)\) removes the need
for their global temperate-symbol assumption.  The present exponential wells
meet that fixed-energy condition.

**Does not support:** an unlocalized full trace without a global orbit audit,
fixed \(\hbar=1\) accuracy, or high-energy uniformity.

### Paul and Uribe (1995)

T. Paul and A. Uribe, “The Semi-Classical Trace Formula and Propagation of
Wave Packets,” *Journal of Functional Analysis* **132**(1), 192–249 (1995).
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1006/jfan.1995.1105).

**Supports:** the rigorous wave-packet/semiclassical propagation framework
underlying fixed-energy trace formulas.

### Duistermaat and Guillemin (1975)

J. J. Duistermaat and V. W. Guillemin, “The Spectrum of Positive Elliptic
Operators and Periodic Bicharacteristics,” *Inventiones Mathematicae*
**29**(1), 39–79 (1975).
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01405172).

**Supports:** the foundational wave-trace/periodic-bicharacteristic and clean
fixed-point framework.

**Boundary:** its original compact-manifold setting is not, by itself, a
direct theorem for the noncompact exponential potential.  The Schrödinger
papers above supply the closer fixed-energy route.

## Continuous orbit families and symmetry

### Guillemin and Uribe (1989)

V. Guillemin and A. Uribe, “Circular Symmetry and the Trace Formula,”
*Inventiones Mathematicae* **96**(2), 385–423 (1989).
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01393968).

**Supports:** the requirement to treat rotational symmetry and orbit
families separately rather than inserting them into an isolated-orbit
Gutzwiller amplitude.

### Cassanas (2007)

R. Cassanas, “Semi-classical trace formula, isochronous case. Application to
conservative systems,” [arXiv:math-ph/0703036](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0703036)
(2007).

**Supports:** clean-flow contributions whose \(\hbar\)-order depends on the
dimension of the fixed-point family.

**Project consequence:** a global radial/Hénon subtraction must not treat
both sides as collections of isolated orbits.  Route A4 initially avoids
this issue through an off-radial-period time window and a microlocal
observable.

## Normal modes near an equilibrium

### Weinstein (1973)

A. Weinstein, “Normal Modes for Nonlinear Hamiltonian Systems,”
*Inventiones Mathematicae* **20**, 47–57 (1973).
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01405263).

**Supports:** periodic normal modes near a nondegenerate Hamiltonian
equilibrium and the broader Hamiltonian normal-mode setting.

### Alligood and Yorke (1986)

K. T. Alligood and J. A. Yorke, “Hopf Bifurcation: The Appearance of
Virtual Periods in Cases of Resonance,” *Journal of Differential Equations*
**64**, 375–394 (1986).
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0396(86)90081-1).

**Supports:** Section 4 states the classical Lyapunov centre theorem in the
exact form used for Proposition A4.4: if \(\pm i\beta\) are simple and every
other eigenvalue \(\mu\) obeys \(\mu/(i\beta)\notin\mathbb Z\), a
one-parameter periodic family bifurcates with limiting period
\(2\pi/|\beta|\).  The project checks this condition explicitly for the fast
pair and separately verifies that energy, rather than only an abstract
bifurcation amplitude, parametrizes the positive branch.

## Relative spectral-shift context

### Yafaev (2005)

D. R. Yafaev, “A Trace Formula for the Dirac Operator,” *Bulletin of the
London Mathematical Society* **37**(6), 908–918 (2005).
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0024609305004911),
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0406112).

**Supports:** generalized spectral shift under an odd resolvent-power
trace-class condition, the basis of the existing \(m=3\) container.

**Does not support:** a periodic-orbit formula for this pair or any prime
interpretation.

### Frank and Pushnitski (2019)

R. L. Frank and A. Pushnitski, “Schatten Class Conditions for Functions of
Schrödinger Operators,” *Annales Henri Poincaré* **20**(11), 3543–3562
(2019). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00023-019-00838-8),
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.05789).

**Supports:** the standard short-range/Schatten comparison regime.

**Boundary:** the present unbounded two-sided potential difference is outside
that regime.  Energy localization avoids needing first-resolvent
comparability; it does not prove it.

## High-energy analogies and non-coverage

The closest high-energy trace results found rely on homogeneous or harmonic
oscillator structure:

- M. Doll, O. Gannot, and J. Wunsch, “Refined Weyl Law for Homogeneous
  Perturbations of the Harmonic Oscillator,” *Communications in Mathematical
  Physics* **362**(1), 269–294 (2018).
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-018-3100-5),
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.06825).
- M. Doll and S. Zelditch, “Schrödinger Trace Invariants for Homogeneous
  Perturbations of the Harmonic Oscillator,” *Journal of Spectral Theory*
  **10**(4), 1303–1332 (2020).
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4171/JST/328),
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.06946).
- A. Pushnitski and I. Sorrell, “High Energy Asymptotics and Trace Formulas
  for the Perturbed Harmonic Oscillator,” *Annales Henri Poincaré* **7**(2),
  381–396 (2006).
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00023-005-0253-5),
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0505131).

None of these sources covers the simultaneous

\[
 \hbar_{\mathrm{eff}}\to0,\qquad
 e^{L_E(|Q|^2-1)}\to\text{hard wall},
\]

with an energy-dependent anisotropic metric generated by a quadratic Hénon
warp.  A uniform theorem in that regime is a genuinely nonstandard target.

## Computer-assisted proof methods addendum

Krawczyk's original paper on his interval-Newton operator supplies the
historical basis for verified nonlinear zeros: R. Krawczyk,
“Newton-Algorithmen zur Bestimmung
von Nullstellen mit Fehlerschranken,” *Computing* **4**, 187–201 (1969),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02234767).  The precise theorem used in the
manuscript should be stated with all regularity, interval-Jacobian,
preconditioner, rounding, and uniqueness/contraction hypotheses; a strict
self-inclusion certifies a zero locally in the declared box, not global
uniqueness.  See also R. E. Moore, R. B. Kearfott, and M. J. Cloud,
*Introduction to Interval Analysis*, SIAM (2009), Chapter 8,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9780898717716.ch8).

For the validated flow layer, P. Zgliczyński, “\(C^1\)-Lohner algorithm,”
*Foundations of Computational Mathematics* **2**(4), 429–465 (2002),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s102080010025), supports joint enclosures of a
flow and its first variational equations.  T. Kapela, M. Mrozek,
D. Wilczak, and P. Zgliczyński, “CAPD::DynSys: A flexible C++ toolbox for
rigorous numerical analysis of dynamical systems,” *Communications in
Nonlinear Science and Numerical Simulation* **101**, 105578 (2021),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cnsns.2020.105578), is the primary software-
method citation.  Neither source validates this project's vector field,
build, return equations, or stored outputs; those remain certificate-level
obligations.

There is direct prior art for the continuation strategy.  D. Wilczak and
R. Barrio, “Systematic Computer-Assisted Proof of Branches of Stable
Elliptic Periodic Orbits and Surrounding Invariant Tori,” *SIAM Journal on
Applied Dynamical Systems* **16**(3), 1618–1649 (2017),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/17M1113254), combines rigorous Poincaré maps,
\(C^r\)-Lohner derivatives, and parameter-uniform interval Newton tests to
obtain a locally unique \(C^1\) branch in prescribed boxes.  It does not
supply global uniqueness, primitivity, automatic bridge identification, or
real analyticity for the present branch.  Validated flow/Poincaré maps plus
interval Newton for periodic-orbit families are therefore prior methodology,
not a novelty claim.

MPFR's correctly rounded arithmetic is documented by L. Fousse et al.,
“MPFR: A Multiple-Precision Binary Floating-Point Library With Correct
Rounding,” *ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software* **33**(2), article
13 (2007), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/1236463.1236468).  No primary or
normative source located in this audit mandates the project's exact-decimal,
exact-rational replay at 128 and 256 bits.  That replay should be described
as a project-specific proof-object audit which does not independently rerun
the validated ODE integration.
