\section{The analytic one-orbit theorem at nonquantitative small energy}
\label{sec:analytic-trace}

We now isolate the fast branch in the time window \((0,0.75]\).  The proof
has three parts: remove radial returns, classify all warped returns on the
complete shrinking shell, and specialize the fixed-energy trace formula.
The resulting energy threshold is positive but not numerically bounded.

\subsection{Radial exclusion}

Write \(E=2\pi+\delta\).  For the radial Hamiltonian, rescale
\(q=\sqrt\delta Q\) and \(p=\sqrt\delta P\).  The energy shell becomes
\begin{equation}
 K_\delta^{\rm rad}(Q,P)
 =\frac{|P|^2}{2}
 +\frac{2\pi}{\delta}\left(e^{\pi\delta|Q|^2}-1\right)=1.
 \label{eq:radial-blowup}
\end{equation}
Because \(e^x-1\ge x\), all such shells lie in a common compact set.  Their
vector fields converge in \(C^1\) to
\begin{equation}
 F_0(Q,P)=(P,-4\pi^2Q),
 \label{eq:radial-limit-field}
\end{equation}
the isotropic oscillator of period one.

\begin{proposition}[Radial short-time exclusion]
\label{prop:radial-exclusion}
For every \(T_{\max}<1\), there is
\(\bar\delta(T_{\max})>0\) such that the radial shell
\(h_0=2\pi+\delta\), \(0<\delta<\bar\delta(T_{\max})\), has no return with
\(0<|T|\le T_{\max}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If a contradicting sequence has \(\delta_n\downarrow0\) and return times
bounded away from zero, compactness and flow convergence give a nonzero
return of \cref{eq:radial-limit-field} before time one.  If instead
\(T_n\to0\), then
\[
 0=\frac{1}{T_n}\int_0^{T_n}
 F_{\delta_n}(\Phi_{\delta_n}^s(Z_n))\dd s
 \longrightarrow F_0(Z_*),
\]
which is impossible on the normalized energy-one shell.  Both alternatives
contradict the assumed returns.
\end{proof}

The radial part alone can also be made quantitative.  If
\(\|\nabla^2V\|_{\rm op}\le L\) on a convex domain containing a periodic
trajectory, the periodic Wirtinger inequality gives
\(T\ge2\pi/\sqrt L\).  On the radial shells through
\(\delta=0.010201\), this yields
\begin{equation}
 T_{\rm radial}>
 \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{(2\pi+0.010201)(2\pi+0.020402)}}
 >0.99.
 \label{eq:radial-explicit-bound}
\end{equation}
Thus \(\bar\delta(0.75)\ge0.010201\).  This explicit bound controls only the
radial factor in the eventual minimum defining \(\delta_{\rm tr}\).

\subsection{Complete-shell warped return classification}

For the H\'enon well, put \(\epsilon=\sqrt\delta\),
\(q=\epsilon Q\), \(p=\epsilon P\), and
\begin{equation}
 K_\epsilon(Q,P)
 =\frac{h_a(\epsilon Q,\epsilon P)-2\pi}{\epsilon^2}.
 \label{eq:normalized-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
The exact decomposition
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_a(\epsilon Q)
 =\epsilon(A_aQ+\epsilon B_a(Q)),
 \qquad B_a(Q)=(-aQ_x^2,0),
 \label{eq:normalized-henon}
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{equation}
 K_\epsilon
 =\frac{|P|^2}{2}
 +\frac{2\pi}{\epsilon^2}
 \left[e^{\pi\epsilon^2|A_aQ+\epsilon B_a(Q)|^2}-1\right].
 \label{eq:normalized-k}
\end{equation}
The apparent singularity at \(\epsilon=0\) is removable, and the extension
has the harmonic value \cref{eq:harmonic-normal-form}.  The rescaling leaves
physical time unchanged.

\begin{theorem}[Whole-shell fast-return uniqueness]
\label{thm:whole-shell}
Fix \(a=1.02\).  There exists \(\delta_*>0\) such that, for every
\(0<\delta<\delta_*\),
\begin{equation}
 h_a(z)=2\pi+\delta,\qquad 0<T\le0.75,\qquad \Phi_a^T(z)=z
 \label{eq:short-return-system}
\end{equation}
holds if and only if \(z\) lies on the fast Lyapunov orbit
\(\gamma_+(2\pi+\delta)\) and
\(T=T_+(2\pi+\delta)\).  This return is primitive, and the geometric orbit
is unique modulo time translation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The proof must control the complete shell, not only a shooting tube.  The
original energy equation gives
\[
 |p|\le\sqrt2\epsilon,\qquad
 |\Psi_a(q)|^2\le\frac1\pi
 \log\left(1+\frac{\epsilon^2}{2\pi}\right).
\]
Using the exact inverse in \cref{eq:henon-inverse-jac} shows
\(|q|=O(\epsilon)\) uniformly on the entire shell.  Hence the normalized
shells \(\Sigma_\epsilon=\{K_\epsilon=1\}\) lie in a common compact set and
the vector fields converge in \(C^1\) on a neighborhood of them.

Any sequence of returns with \(\epsilon_n\downarrow0\) and
\(0<T_n\le0.75\) has a convergent subsequence.  The averaged-vector-field
argument excludes \(T_n\to0\).  At a positive limiting time, the harmonic
classification \cref{eq:window-separation} forces
\(T_n\to T_+^0\) and the limiting state onto the fast phase circle.

At the positive fast turning point, use the local section
\[
 \cS_\epsilon=\{K_\epsilon=1,\ P_+=0,\ Q_+>0\}.
\]
The energy gradient and event slope are nonzero at \(\epsilon=0\).  In
normalized slow coordinates, the derivative of the limiting return map is
the rotation \(R_{2\pi/\rho_a}\), so
\[
 \det(I-D\Pi_0)=4\sin^2(\pi/\rho_a)>0.
\]
The implicit-function theorem gives one local fixed point.  A proper iterate
would converge to a fractional return of the fast harmonic circle, which is
impossible; thus the nearby return is primitive.  Finally, a hypothetical
second sequence of orbits converges uniformly, after phase alignment, to the
fast circle.  Transverse \(C^1\) convergence gives exactly one oriented
crossing of a sufficiently small section neighborhood per primitive circuit;
that crossing is consequently a fixed point of the local first-return map
and contradicts local uniqueness.  \Cref{app:whole-shell} gives the detailed
compactness, crossing-stability, iterate, and globalization steps.
\end{proof}

The explicit warped Hessian estimate gives a useful but weaker quantitative
fact.  Every warped orbit for \(0<\delta\le0.010201\) lies in
\(|x|<0.02274\), \(|y|<0.042427\), where
\(\|\nabla^2V_a\|_{\rm op}<103\).  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 T_{\rm warped}>\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{103}}>0.60.
 \label{eq:warped-period-floor}
\end{equation}
This period floor reduces a future exhaustive cover to
\([0.60,0.75]\); it does not exclude additional returns in that interval and
does not quantify \(\delta_*\).

\subsection{Finite-time trace localization}

The trace integral \cref{eq:density-wave} only samples the time support of
\(\widehat g\).  The following finite-time form of the fixed-energy theorem
is the precise bridge we need.

\begin{lemma}[Finite-time specialization of the CRR trace theorem]
\label{lem:finite-time-crr}
Suppose \(\supp\widehat g\subset[-T_0,T_0]\), the energy is regular and
compactly localized, the geometric periodic orbits with
\(0<|T_\gamma|\le T_0\) form a finite collection modulo time translation,
and every such orbit is transversally nondegenerate.  Equivalently, at a
relevant period the associated flow-orbit component of the fixed set is
clean in the transverse directions.  Then the nondegenerate-orbit expansion
of \citet{CombescureRalstonRobert1999} requires no hypothesis on returns with
\(|t|>T_0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In the coherent-state proof, the time variable is integrated against
\(\widehat g(t)\).  The integrand vanishes identically outside its support.
Periodic-orbit discreteness modulo the flow direction and the transverse
stationary-phase reductions are therefore invoked only for
\(|t|\le T_0\).  The remaining symbol and regular-energy hypotheses are
unchanged.  This is a support restriction of the proof, not a new long-time
trace formula.
\end{proof}

Set
\begin{equation}
 \delta_{\rm tr}
 =\min\{\delta_*,\bar\delta(0.75),\delta_{\rm nd}\},
 \label{eq:delta-tr}
\end{equation}
where \(\delta_{\rm nd}>0\) is a sufficiently small transverse-
nondegeneracy threshold from \cref{prop:lyapunov-branch}.  Both
\(\delta_*\) and \(\delta_{\rm nd}\) are positive but currently
nonquantitative.

\begin{theorem}[Eigenvalue-only local relative trace]
\label{thm:relative-gutzwiller}
Fix \(0<\delta<\delta_{\rm tr}\) and put \(E=2\pi+\delta\).  Choose
\(\chi\in C_c^\infty(\R;\R)\), equal to one near \(E\), with support in a
small compact regular band.  Let
\begin{equation}
 \widehat g\in C_c^\infty((0,0.75)),
 \qquad
 \supp\widehat g\ \text{concentrated near }T_+(E),
 \qquad
 \widehat g(T_+(E))\ne0.
 \label{eq:g-choice}
\end{equation}
Then, with the Fourier convention \cref{eq:fourier-convention},
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \rho_{\mathrm{rel},\h}(E;g)
 =i\widehat g(T_+(E))
 \frac{T_+(E)}{2\pi\sqrt{|\det(I-P_+(E))|}}
 e^{iS_+(E)/\h}
 +O_{\delta,\chi,g}(\h).}
 \label{eq:main-trace-formula}
\end{equation}
The left-hand side is determined only by the eigenvalue lists of
\(P_{a,\h}\) and \(P_{0,\h}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(\delta\) first and then let \(\h\downarrow0\).  By
\cref{thm:whole-shell}, the warped flow has one primitive geometric orbit in
the Fourier time range; by \cref{prop:radial-exclusion}, the radial flow has
none.  Transverse nondegeneracy holds after taking the minimum in
\cref{eq:delta-tr}.  \Cref{prop:regular-shells} and the confinement of the
well verify the regular compact energy hypotheses.  Apply
\cref{lem:finite-time-crr} separately to the warped and radial traces and
subtract.

The single-orbit positive-time coefficient in the project Fourier convention is
\[
 \widehat g(T_+)
 \frac{T_+}{2\pi\sqrt{|\det(I-P_+)|}}
 e^{i(S_+/\h+\pi\sigma_+^{\rm CRR}/2)}.
\]
The exact harmonic trace fixes
\(e^{i\pi\sigma_+^{\rm CRR}/2}=i\); nondegenerate continuation keeps this
phase constant.  Because no microlocal observable occurs, the exact
identities in \cref{prop:staircase-identity} apply and make the spectral side
eigenvalue-only.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Quantifier order]
\label{rem:quantifier-order}
The theorem states: first choose a fixed
\(0<\delta<\delta_{\rm tr}\), then choose the cutoffs, and finally let
\(\h\to0\).  It does not state a uniform joint limit
\((\delta,\h)\to(0,0)\).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why the phase is \(+i\)]
At the harmonic limit, the Abel-regularized transverse sum is
\[
 \sum_{m\ge0}e^{-i(2\pi/\rho_a)(m+1/2)}
 =-\frac{i}{2\sin(\pi/\rho_a)}.
\]
Poisson summation in the fast quantum number supplies
\(-1/\omega_+=-T_+^0/(2\pi)\).  Their product has positive phase \(+i\)
after the positive determinant factor is removed.  This also locks the
single factor \(T_+/(2\pi)\): multiplying by another period would double
count the longitudinal orbit measure.
\end{remark}

Nothing in \cref{thm:relative-gutzwiller} identifies a prime.  Its return
period varies smoothly from \(0.6638\ldots\), whereas an arithmetic explicit
formula would require an endogenous family organized by prime powers and
the corresponding amplitudes.  We return to this distinction in
\cref{sec:scope}.
