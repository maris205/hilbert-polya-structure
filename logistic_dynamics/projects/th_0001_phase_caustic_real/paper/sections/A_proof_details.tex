\section{Algebraic proof details}

Expanding the phase gives
\[
 \Phi=\frac{q_0^3}{6}+q_0q_1-q_0
      +\frac{q_1^3}{2}+q_1q_2-q_1
      +\frac{5q_2^3}{6}+q_2q_3-q_2.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \partial_{q_1}\Phi=q_0+\frac32q_1^2+q_2-1,
 \qquad
 \partial_{q_2}\Phi=q_1+\frac52q_2^2+q_3-1,
\]
and
\[
 \Hess_{(q_1,q_2)}\Phi
 =\begin{pmatrix}3q_1&1\\1&5q_2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Imposing its determinant to vanish and writing \(q_1=t\ne0\) yields
\(q_2=(15t)^{-1}\).  The stationary equations are affine in the endpoints,
which proves the formulas in Section~3.

For the witness, substitution of
\[
 q_0=-17/30,\quad q_1=1,\quad q_2=1/15,\quad q_3=-1/90
\]
and
\[
 p_0=-289/1800,\quad p_1=-17/30,\quad p_2=1,\quad p_3=1/15
\]
into \(F_a(q,p)=(1-aq^2-p,q)\) at \(a=1/2,3/2,5/2\) gives the three displayed
equalities.  At \((q_1=1,q_2=1/15)\), the Hessian annihilates
\(v=(-1,3)^T\), and the cubic directional derivative is
\[
 \partial_{q_1}^3\Phi\,v_1^3+
 \partial_{q_2}^3\Phi\,v_2^3=3(-1)^3+5(3)^3=132.
\]
