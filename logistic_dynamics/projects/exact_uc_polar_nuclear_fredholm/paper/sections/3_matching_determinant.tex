\section{Complemented matching determinant}
Define \(\delta(v)=v_L(0)-v_R(0)\) and let \(e=(1,0)\). Since
\(\delta(e)=1\), the map \(P_B=I-e\delta\) is a bounded projection and
\(X=B\oplus\C e\). Both outputs of \(\LL_s\) are restrictions of the same
holomorphic expression. Therefore \(\LL_s(X)\subset B\), and relative to
this decomposition it has the form
\[
 \LL_s=\begin{pmatrix}\LL_{s,B}&b_s\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Consequently all power traces, and hence the canonical determinants, agree:
\[
 \Tr_X(\LL_s^n)=\Tr_B(\LL_{s,B}^n),\qquad
 \Det(I-\lambda\LL_s)=\Det(I-\lambda\LL_{s,B}).
\]
We define
\[
 \Delta(\lambda,s)=\Det(I-\lambda\LL_{s,B}),
 \qquad D_{\rm pol}(s)=\Delta(1,s).
\]
The order-zero nuclear determinant theorem and the ideal-valued entire
dependence from the preceding section make \(\Delta\) jointly entire in
\((\lambda,s)\). This is a canonical Grothendieck determinant, not a
regularized determinant, spectral-zeta determinant, Euler product, or a
physical-return determinant.
