## Abstract

This is a breadth-first rejection screen, not a new Hamiltonian construction.
It follows the genuine prime-symbolic lineage from the primorial sieve gap
recursion (019) to the roofed packet carrier (032), then asks for a
positive-dimensional conservative/symplectic realization.  The direct disk
thickening already failed because it turns a finite cycle into a continuum of
periodic points (033).  Published Hamiltonian surface theorems show that in
important closed-surface regimes a nontrivial map has periodic points of
unbounded period.  Thus any candidate in such a regime must account for those
orbits with its *own* roof and primitive convention; choosing packet centres
is not a repair.  No concrete map and full ledger are supplied, so P0 is
stopped immediately.  This does not prove that every positive-dimensional
symplectic realization is impossible.

## 2. Discriminating question

Can a positive-dimensional Hamiltonian or symplectic base retain *exactly*
the finite, one-packet-per-stage orbit information of 032, rather than merely
contain it as a selected subset?

The answer for the proposed-but-unspecified lift is no: no map, topology,
boundary behavior, roof, or enumeration of all its periodic points has been
given.  This is already enough to prevent P0.  The following external theorem
control explains why that missing enumeration is substantive rather than a
minor bookkeeping task.

## 3. Surface theorem control and its precise use

Franks--Handel prove that every nontrivial Hamiltonian diffeomorphism of a
closed oriented surface of positive genus has periodic points of arbitrarily
high period; for `S^2`, their statement includes the hypothesis of at least
three fixed points.  Hence a proposed lift inside those hypotheses has
primitive periodic data beyond any finite wheel packet.

This result is used only conditionally:

```text
if a future proposed base lies in the theorem's hypotheses,
then its full ledger contains unbounded periods,
so its candidate card must own them all with the same roof.
```

It is not used to claim that all symplectic maps have infinitely many periodic
orbits.  The Conley-conjecture survey explicitly discusses both broad
infinite-orbit results and settings with finite periodic-orbit examples.  Nor
does either source turn the extra periods into prime packets or provide a
prime-time clock.

## 4. Ownership consequence

Suppose a proposed base `F` contains a copy of a wheel cycle.  There are only
two acceptable ways to proceed:

1. Prove that the full primitive-orbit ledger of `F`, including its extra
   periodic orbits if present, is the intended arithmetic ledger; give one
   endogenous roof on all of it and derive the repetitions and analytic owner.
2. Define a different invariant subsystem and prove that it is intrinsically
   selected by the same map and arithmetic mechanism, while explicitly
   defining its own phase space, roof, and closed-orbit convention.

Selecting centres of periodic disks, deleting all unselected periodic points,
or retaining the 032 product after changing the carrier violates the
same-object ledger.  In particular, the disk construction in 033 fails option
1 because its packet iterate is identity on each disk, and it fails option 2
because no intrinsic arithmetic rule selects a finite section.
