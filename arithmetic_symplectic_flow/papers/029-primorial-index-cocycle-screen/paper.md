# The natural primorial index logarithm cannot time a closed wheel packet

**Paper ID:** `029-primorial-index-cocycle-screen`  
**Candidate ID:** `AFC-20260914-PIC01`  
**Date / status:** `2026-09-14; STOPPED AT T1 — INDEX LOGARITHM LIVES ONLY ON NONRETURNING ARROWS`  
**Route state:** `Broadened audit only; Route B NOT INVOKED`

## Test

For the primorial relation `P_(k+1)=p_(k+1)P_k`, define the intrinsic index
cocycle on the directed wheel category by

```text
omega(E_k)=log [P_(k+1):P_k]=log p_(k+1),   omega(R_k)=0.
```

This is the strongest natural attempt to obtain a logarithm without inserting
`T_p=log p`: it is the logarithm of an index already owned by the exact sieve
transition.  The lineage remains the same prime-symbolic wheel recursion as
028.

## Decisive incompatibility

Let `L(k,a)=k`.  Any path containing an `E_k` raises `L` by at least one; hence
it cannot be closed.  Every closed path in the directed carrier lies in one
fibre and is generated by packet arrows `R_k`, so its omega length is zero.
Thus no closed packet has a positive prime-index logarithmic time.

One might groupoid-complete the transition arrows.  That changes the directed
sieve relation, and in any event a closed word has equal forward and reverse
stage displacement.  Since the cocycle on an inverse is `-omega(E_k)`, its
net cocycle is zero.  Formal inverses therefore cannot turn the index into a
positive closed-orbit clock.

## Decision

**Portfolio decision: stop/fork.** `AFC-20260914-PIC01` fails T1 precisely at
clock-to-packet ownership.  The 028 carrier retains its T0/T2 partial value,
but this index-cocycle fork cannot advance to T3.  Any new clock must be
nonzero on an actual closed packet while arising from the same sieve mechanism;
it may not be imported from the forward transition alone.
