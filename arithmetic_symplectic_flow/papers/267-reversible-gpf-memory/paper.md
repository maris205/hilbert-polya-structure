## Abstract

We audit the previously deferred unsigned greatest-prime-factor memory
law on its entire integer-pair carrier. It is bijective, genuinely evaluates
arithmetic on the evolving coordinate, and has a complete unit suspension.
Its fixed states are precisely the nonnegative prime diagonals together
with the zero and one diagonals. Every such state gives a primitive
time-1 circle. Thus the fixed subledger already violates prime-exclusive
packets and a growing prime clock, and makes the ordinary unweighted
repetition sum nowhere absolutely convergent. No classification of higher
periods is needed or claimed. The full state space and roof remain intact;
invertibility of a discrete arithmetic action is not a symplectic lift.

## 2. Full owner and unit flow

**Proposition 1.** F is a bijection on all X, with inverse

    F^(-1)(u,v)=(2H(u)−v,u).

**Proof.** Both compositions cancel the same term2H and recover the input
pair. No inverse to gpf is used or required. A bijection of a discrete
space is a homeomorphism and preserves counting measure. QED.

For the full suspension, the equivalence is generated by
n·(z,u)=(F^n z,u−n). Translation in u commutes with this action, so Phi
is a well-defined complete group of time maps. The integer action is free
because the real coordinate changes by n; its real-coordinate translation
also prevents finite-time accumulation of crossings.

More explicitly, decompose X into its actual F-orbits. Each is open in X.
An orbit of least period m contributes a copy of R/mZ to Y; an infinite
orbit contributes a copy of R. This proves the quotient is Hausdorff and
the flow complete without classifying which higher-period orbits exist.
The resulting disjoint union is a broadened flow owner, not a positive-
dimensional realization of a symplectic BASE map. A fixed base state
produces a moving time-1 circle, not a stationary flow point.

## 3. Complete fixed-state set and decisive subledger

**Proposition 2.**

    Fix(F) = {(0,0),(1,1)} union {(p,p):p prime}.

**Proof.** A fixed pair has x=y and x=H(x). Since H≥0, x cannot be
negative. The definitions include0 and1. For an integer x≥2, its greatest
prime factor equals x if and only if x is prime. Conversely every displayed
pair satisfies both fixed equations. This includes p=2. QED.

Every displayed pair is a different primitive base orbit and hence a
different primitive flow circle of least time1. All its repeats have
time r. Cyclic identification does not identify different prime labels
or remove the zero/one circles.

This COMPLETE FIXED-state result is not a complete PERIODIC-state result.
Other periods and possible nonperiodic orbits remain unclassified. The
frozen first-gate test already fails: all prime circles have a common
time rather than prime-dependent growing time, while0 and1 give extra
nonprime primitives on the full carrier. Deleting just those two circles
would neither preserve the owner nor repair the collapsed prime clock.
A global time rescaling cannot make one common period equal to both
log2 and log3, or produce a logarithmic large-prime scale.

## 5. Immediate ordinary-product obstruction

The card's only analytic convention is the ordinary unweighted full
primitive repetition sum. Its absolute r=1 subseries from the prime
fixed circles alone is

    sum_(p prime) |exp(−s)| = infinity

for every finite complex s. The infinitude of primes is the usual
elementary fact; the other periodic orbits cannot cancel positive absolute
terms. Hence there is no absolute-convergence half-plane for this full
ordinary series. No regularized product, weight, selected factor or
operator is introduced. This diagnostic needs no higher-period census
and is not a theorem excluding every possible analytic construction.
