The prescribed counting-product inverse version owns its IMAGE identity, and the lifted clock is consequently the parent clock pulled back on actual single-lag arrows.

## 5. All histories, actual arrows, kernels, isotropy and phases

Define \(\sigma_0(x)=\mathrm{id}_E\) and \(\sigma_n(x)=\sigma(T^{n-1}x)\cdots\sigma(x)\) for \(n\ge1\).
Induction gives \(F^n(x,e)=(T^nx,\sigma_n(x)e)\), and \(S_n^F(x,e)=S_n^T(x)\).
For \(U=T,F\), set \(G_U=\{(z,m-n,w):U^mz=U^nw,\ m,n\ge0\}\), identifying equal triples, with source \(w\) and range \(z\).
The clock \(c_U=S_m^U(z)-S_n^U(w)\) descends: different witnesses shift both depths equally and add the same common-tail sum.
Composition is proved by extending the shorter middle history to the larger middle depth and cancelling the middle sums.
Thus clocks add, inverse arrows negate them, and \((Uz,-1,z)\) has clock \(-\kappa_U(z)\).

The full lifted arrow condition is exactly
\[
 ((x,e),k,(y,f))\in G_F
 \iff \exists\,m,n\ge0:\quad m-n=k,\quad T^mx=T^ny,\quad
 \sigma_m(x)e=\sigma_n(y)f .
\]
Equivalently, for any witness of a parent arrow, the two transported fibre states must coalesce after some common additional number of steps.
Indeed equal states remain equal under further total maps; conversely such a coalescence supplies a lifted witness with the same lag.
This is not an arbitrary product of independent lags.
Projection \(\pi_*((x,e),k,(y,f))=(x,k,y)\) is a functor on these actual arrows and
\[
 c_F=\pi_*^*c_T .

    T^nx=T^ny,\ \sigma_n(x)e=\sigma_n(y)f\text{ for some }n\},

\[

## 6. Complete return functional graphs and full packet assignment

Fix a parent least-\(q\) core \(\Gamma=(x_0,\ldots,x_{q-1})\), \(\alpha_j=\sigma(x_j)\), parent signed sum \(C=\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\kappa_T(x_j)\), and
\[
 \Pi=\alpha_{q-1}\cdots\alpha_0:E\to E.
\]
Every vertex of this finite functional graph eventually reaches a unique directed cycle: its forward sequence must repeat, and deterministic continuation fixes the eventual cycle.
For each cycle \(O\) of length \(\ell\), let \(E_O=\{e:\Pi^ne\in O\text{ for some }n\ge0\}\).
These basins partition all of \(E\), retaining every transient vertex.

For \(e\in O\), the points \(F^j(x_0,e)\), \(0\le j<q\ell\), form one lifted cycle.
Any return must have time divisible by \(q\), because its projection returns to \(x_0\); time \(qa\) then returns its fibre precisely when \(\Pi^ae=e\).
Thus its least source period is exactly \(q\ell\), and its signed cycle clock is exactly \(\ell C\).
Different vertices of \(O\) describe phases of the same lifted core, not additional packets.
Distinct \(\Pi\)-cycles cannot produce the same lifted core, since its intersection with the fibre above \(x_0\) is exactly that cycle.
Conversely, every lifted cycle projects to a parent periodic core and intersects that reference fibre at a periodic point of \(\Pi\).
This proves coverage and converse: there is one lifted core for each complete functional-graph cycle and none contributed by a transient vertex alone.

For an arbitrary parent state \(x\) eventually entering \(\Gamma\), let \(t\) be its actual first arrival at the core, with \(T^tx=x_j\).
Transport \(e\) to \(v=\sigma_t(x)e\); if \(j>0\), transport a further \(q-j\) steps to \(w=\sigma_{q-j}(x_j)v\), and if \(j=0\), put \(w=v\).
The complete lifted packet associated to \(O\) consists exactly of those \((x,e)\) for which this \(w\) lies in \(E_O\).
Every such state eventually reaches the stated lifted core; every actual predecessor of that core must satisfy this criterion.
Thus all incoming parent branches and all finite fibre transients are included, with no selected sheets or finite-basin assumption.
Distinct eventual lifted cores cannot merge their full packets under a deterministic map.

If a parent source is not eventually periodic, neither can any point above it be eventually periodic, since a lifted repetition would project to a parent one.
Consequently such lifted packets have no positive physical returns, although they can have nonunit merging kernels.
Their complete histories and packet relation are the Section5 criteria, and their phases are full copies of \(\mathbb R\); no parent cycle is presumed to exist.

## 7. Exact prime conditions and the rational-parent boundary

Without rationality, the complete positive multiset is indexed by distinct parent cores \(\Gamma\), counted once modulo reference phase, with \(C_\Gamma\ne0\), and all cycles \(O\) of their return maps:
\[
 \mathcal L_F=\{(\Gamma,O,\ |O|\,|C_\Gamma|)\}.
\]
Hence nonemptiness is equivalent to the existence of a nonzero-clock parent core: every finite endomorphism has at least one cycle.
Prime-only support means \(\exp(|C_\Gamma|)^{|O|}\) is an ordinary prime for every such pair, and prime uniqueness means at most one such pair produces each prime.
Equal lengths do not merge their distinct packets. This is the general criterion, including irrational parent multipliers.

Now impose the frozen rational hypothesis \(M_\Gamma=\exp(|C_\Gamma|)\in\mathbb Q\), \(M_\Gamma>1\), for every nonzero parent core.
If \(M=a/b\) in lowest positive terms and \(M^\ell=p\) is prime, then \(a^\ell=p b^\ell\) forces \(b=1\); the prime factorization of \(a^\ell\) then forces \(\ell=1\) and \(a=p\).
Conversely a prime \(M=p\) and \(\ell=1\) give exactly that prime multiplier.
It follows that the nonempty, prime-only and prime-unique benchmark holds if and only if all four conditions hold:

1. At least one parent core has nonzero \(C_\Gamma\).
2. Every such parent core has an ordinary prime multiplier \(M_\Gamma\).
3. Its return functional graph has exactly one cycle and that cycle is a fixed vertex; every other fibre vertex is transient to it.
4. Distinct nonzero parent cores have distinct prime multipliers.

Necessity of condition3 includes multiplicity: each return graph has at least one cycle, every allowed cycle has length1, and two such cycles already duplicate the same prime.
Conversely the four conditions contribute one packet for each distinct parent prime and no other positive packet.
Zero-clock parent cores may have arbitrary return functional graphs; their lifted cycles have \(H=0\) and do not enter this positive ledger.
All-prime coverage is equivalent, in addition, to the set of these parent multipliers being all ordinary primes.
For a permutation fibre with \(D\ge2\), condition3 is impossible because no vertex is transient; for an arbitrary endomorphism it is not impossible.
This conclusion is derived here from the full retained graph, not imported from the earlier permutation paper.
No rational implication is extended to irrational \(M\): only the general power criterion above applies there.
