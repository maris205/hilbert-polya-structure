## Abstract

For an independently owned full return system with integer fractional-linear
inverse branches and its fixed metric-derivative clock, a positive primitive
time log p forces the exact cycle matrix to satisfy
|det A|=p h^2 and |tr A|=h(p+sign(det A)), h a positive integer.
In particular a cycle of unimodular branches cannot have such a time.
This is a necessary condition, not a construction, a prime-only orbit ledger
or a result about every geometric clock. We derive full lag, physical and
extension stabilizers, treat zero-clock and orientation boundaries, and give
three independent local-germ controls. No numerical census is used.

## 3. Exact cycle derivative and arithmetic necessity

Take a least source cycle x_0,...,x_(q-1). Its actual inverse composition
B=I_(x_0) o ... o I_(x_(q-1)) fixes x_0 and has exact matrix
A=A_0...A_(q-1). Product order follows the displayed composition. By the
chain rule, |B'(x_0)|=exp(-C). Direct differentiation gives
f_A'(x)=D/(cx+d)^2. Since f_A(x_0)=x_0, the vector (x_0,1) is an
eigenvector with real nonzero eigenvalue lambda=cx_0+d. The other eigenvalue
is nu=D/lambda, also real and nonzero. Set s=sign D and

    R=exp(C)=lambda^2/|D|>0;    t=tr A=lambda+nu.

Then nu=s lambda/R, and elementary squaring yields

    t^2=|D| (R+s)^2/R.                                    (1)

No sign of derivative has been replaced by a sign of physical time. In
particular negative D is allowed and C may be negative. If L=log p for an
ordinary integer prime p, the full-H calculation gives R=p OR R=1/p.
Both alternatives in(1) give the same necessary equation

    p t^2=|D| (p+s)^2.                                    (2)

Because p and p+s are coprime, p divides |D|. Write |D|=p e. Equation(2)
now says e=(t/(p+s))^2. A rational number whose square is an integer is an
integer: in a reduced fraction u/v, v^2 divides u^2 only when v=1.
As e>0, there is h>=1 integer with

    |D|=p h^2,       |t|=h(p+s).                          (3)

Conversely(3) makes the eigenvalue ratios p or1/p in absolute value, but
does NOT place the corresponding eigenline in a legal domain or establish
an actual least cycle. Thus it is not a sufficient dynamical criterion.
The two eigenvalues must be signed integers: for D>0 they are ±hp and ±h
with a common sign; for D<0 they are opposite-sign numbers of magnitudes
hp,h. This follows by substituting(3) into z^2-tz+D and factoring; it is
an algebraic consequence, not a globally realized orbit.

If every actual branch is unimodular, |D|=1 for every exact product.
The necessary divisibility of |D| by p is then impossible. Thus EVERY positive
physical primitive in this conditional unimodular class fails the prime-log
target, rather than just the first word failing it. There may also be no
positive primitives at all. This statement does not make a vacuous source
successful and does not exclude non-projective or non-unimodular clocks.

More locally any possible p must divide a branch determinant in that cycle,
since determinants multiply. This necessary search filter does not license
inserting prime-dependent determinants or deleting other packets. Exact
products matter: scaling A by an integer r leaves R unchanged but multiplies
D by r^2 and t by r. Condition(3) correspondingly changes h to |r|h.
Our frozen representative rule avoids interpreting artificial scale content
as a newly discovered mechanism; the derivative itself is scale-invariant.

## 4. Unit multiplier, scalar and parabolic boundaries

If R=1 the clock C=0 and H={0}, but the source lag isotropy above remains
q Z. For D>0, (1) gives t^2=4D, a repeated nonzero real eigenvalue.
A scalar matrix represents the identity map. A nonscalar such matrix is
parabolic: write A=lambda(I+N), N^2=0,N!=0, so its projective powers
are represented by I+kN and are distinct for distinct integer k. To check
the latter, equality up to a scalar forces that scalar1 by the eigenvalue,
and then kN=lN forces k=l. Its fixed-point derivative is nonetheless1.

For D<0, R=1 gives t=0. Direct matrix multiplication (or its characteristic
identity A^2-tA+D I=0, verified entrywise) gives A^2=-D I. The nontrivial
projective germ thus has order2 and fixed-point derivative -1. A scalar
matrix cannot have D<0. These finite germ relations must not erase retained
point-lags: the actual lag owner can still have isotropy q Z. This is why
the class theorem uses actual triples, not a silently substituted germ group.
