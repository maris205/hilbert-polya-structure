## Exact common formula

Over the integers define

```text
H_Z(x,y)=(y,-x+y^2).
```

Its inverse is `(X,Y)->(X^2-Y,X)` and its Jacobian determinant is one. Hence
the real fibre `H_R:R^2 -> R^2` preserves `dx wedge dy`. It has the fixed points
`(0,0)` and `(2,2)`, so its unit-roof suspension would have elementary
primitive closed orbits. Reducing coefficients modulo any prime produces a map
`H_p:A^2(F_p)->A^2(F_p)`.

## Ownership failure

The real symplectic phase space and every finite field phase space are distinct
fibres. Their periodic points, counting conventions, and possible periods are
not one orbit ledger. In particular, the fact that a prime indexes a reduction
does not select a real closed orbit, derive a real roof, or produce `log p`.
Attaching finite-field orbit counts to the real suspension would combine objects
after the fact, contrary to the one-object invariant.

## Lineage failure

This map is Hénon-type and integral, but it has no retained arrow from the
programme's prime/composite sieve word through the sequential deformation. It
therefore cannot be relabelled a candidate merely because both its reductions
and the sieve mention primes. It is an external control for fibre ownership.

## Decision

Do not freeze P0. A future admissible integral Hénon candidate would need an
actual sieve-symbolic coding intrinsic to its single specified real/algebraic
carrier, plus a proof that its prime fibre data and roofed closed orbits share
that owner.
