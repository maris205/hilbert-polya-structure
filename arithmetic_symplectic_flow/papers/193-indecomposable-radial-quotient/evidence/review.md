## Claims independently checked

### 1. The quotient topology is genuinely separating

Lemma 1 correctly identifies I squared with the coordinate span of composite
basis vectors: every product of two nonunits is composite, and every
composite has such a factorization. Prime coefficients of a representative
are consequently invariant modulo I squared. Removing all composite
coefficients realizes the infimum in equation (1), yielding exactly
the weighted prime coefficient norms in equation (2).

Thus even the zero-th seminorm separates points; the full family defines
the stated Hausdorff finite-support topological vector space. There is no
completion, support cutoff or coordinate-product topology hidden in the
argument. Equation (4) proves continuity of D using the next seminorm,
not an incorrect boundedness assertion in the single zero-th norm.

### 2. Full quotient separation does not assume local compactness

The mass inequalities (5) prove freeness and pairwise disjoint translates
of a sufficiently thin mass annulus. They also restrict all possible
intersections between two neighborhoods with masses in a fixed positive
bounded interval to finitely many integer powers. Shrinking against those
finitely many powers proves the Hausdorff conclusion in Proposition 2
directly. No local-compactness or manifold theorem is being invoked.

The compact-intersection statement is also correct. If the stronger
compact-preimage definition of a proper action is desired, it follows by
the same argument: for compact C in P times P, its two projections have
positive lower and finite upper mass bounds. Only finitely many j can
occur in the inverse image under (j,v) mapping to (v,D to the j v).
For each such j, the inverse image is a closed subset of the compact
first projection. Their finite union is compact. The paper does not need
to replace its explicit separation proof by that terminology.

### 3. The radial flow is complete on the whole carrier

Positive scalar multiplication preserves every allowed finite support and
commutes with D. Its joint continuity follows from the actual seminorm
topology. The quotient map is open, and its product with the identity on
real time is an open quotient map; this justifies joint continuity after
descent. The formula exists at every positive and negative real time.
Topological incompleteness of the finite-support vector space is not a
finite-time escape objection to this explicitly global flow.

### 4. The section owns the stated first return, with the correct direction

nontrivial D translate. The open quotient chart therefore embeds the

### 5. The packet theorem covers every support and every amplitude

A return means exp(t)v=D to the j v for one common integer j. Each nonzero
coefficient imposes exp(t)=p to the j. Distinct primes cannot satisfy
this equality for a nonzero integer j, so mixed supports have trivial
time stabilizer. This uses injectivity of integer powers, not an
unproved logarithmic-independence assertion.

A singleton support has exactly the stabilizer (log p)Z. All its positive
amplitudes are connected by actual radial time already before the quotient,

supports are never identified. Positive repetitions have times r log p.
Every full state is either singleton or mixed, and no state is fixed at
every time. The paper explicitly retains the mixed aperiodic states;
it does not replace X by the union of closed circles.
