## The class

For `A in SL(2,Z)`, let `F_A(x)=Ax` on `(R^2,dx wedge dy)` and let `V` be primitive integer vectors. Since both `A` and `A^{-1}` have integer entries, the ideals generated by the coordinates of `v` and `Av` agree. Hence `A(V)=V`.

## Dichotomy

**Theorem.** Exactly one of the following holds.

1. No eigenvalue of `A` is a root of unity. Then no nonzero point of `R^2`, hence no element of `V`, is periodic.
2. An eigenvalue of `A` is a root of unity. Then there is `n>0` for which `A^n-I` has nonzero kernel, and `F_A^n` fixes a nonzero real linear subspace. The ambient symplectic phase space consequently has an uncountable periodic family.

**Proof.** A point of period dividing `n` lies in `ker(A^n-I)`. In Case 1 no eigenvalue of `A^n` is one, so this kernel is zero for every `n`. In Case 2 a root-of-unity eigenvalue has a positive power equal to one, supplying a nonzero kernel; all real scalar multiples of a nonzero vector in it are periodic. ∎

## Candidate consequence

The first branch is the hyperbolic cat-map behavior of 057: arithmetic decoration remains visible but no visible orbit closes. The second includes finite-order and parabolic behavior: visible vectors can close, but the full positive-dimensional base has a continuum of periodic points. Selecting only the primitive lattice cycles then changes the orbit owner exactly as in the periodic-family control 033.

This is not a statement about nonlinear symplectic maps, nonclassical carriers, or a different arithmetic mechanism. It only rules out a broad linear attempt to lift the 056 spatial sieve into one same-object discrete closed-orbit ledger.

**Portfolio position: fork.** Future spatial lifts must be nonlinear or otherwise structurally distinct, and must still derive their arithmetic observable rather than select a discrete invariant subset from a continuum.
