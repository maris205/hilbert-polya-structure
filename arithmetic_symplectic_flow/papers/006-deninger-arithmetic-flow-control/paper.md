# Deninger arithmetic dynamics: A0-positive, P0-incomplete

**Paper ID:** `006-deninger-arithmetic-flow-control`  
**Candidate ID:** `NONE — pre-P0 control`  
**Status:** `A0 POSITIVE CONTROL; P0 NOT ADMITTED; Route B NOT INVOKED`

Deninger's construction gives a continuous-time arithmetic dynamical system in which periodic orbits occur in compact packets indexed by closed points and have length \(\log\#k(x)\). For \(X=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\), closed points are rational primes, so the prime clock is endogenous rather than inserted. This is a genuine A0 control.

It nevertheless fails the ASFS admission test presently: the construction is not supplied here as a finite-dimensional smooth symplectic \((M,\omega)\), a single symplectic map plus positive roof, or a suspension. Moreover, packeted periodic orbits are not automatically the isolated primitive-orbit convention required for A1. `fork`: seek a faithful finite-dimensional symplectic realization only; do not collapse packets to arbitrary representatives or borrow a return map.

## Literature boundary on the geometric fork

The missing finite-dimensional realization is not merely absent from this
workspace. Deninger's foliated-space programme presents the required arithmetic
flow for the rational zeta setting as a sought-for construction. The cited
laminated-space literature states that the relevant existence problem remains
unknown for the zeta setting, with an exception for geometric objects such as
elliptic curves over finite fields. Such local/function-field realizations are
not an identification with the `Spec Z` packet flow. They therefore remain
comparators, not a Poincaré section or symplectic base map for this control.

## Nearest geometric comparator and non-transfer rule

Concrete three-dimensional foliated dynamical systems inspired by Deninger's
arithmetic-topology programme do exist and have transverse two-dimensional
geometry. They are useful evidence that the *geometric* side of a prospective
bridge is not nonsensical. But their closed orbits are geometric analogues of
finite primes, not an established realization of the closed-point packets of
\(\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z\). There is no proven identification of phase
spaces, flows, orbit packets, or clock normalizations. Consequently they cannot
provide a Poincaré return map for this candidate.

The next admissible proposal must supply all of the following in one proof or
definition: (i) a finite-dimensional smooth symplectic carrier and single
symplectic map; (ii) a positive roof whose closed-orbit periods agree with the
arithmetic \(\log\#k(x)\) clock; (iii) a specified correspondence from closed
points to primitive flow orbits, including packet multiplicity and repetition;
and (iv) no prime-indexed choice of sections, parameters, or roofs.
