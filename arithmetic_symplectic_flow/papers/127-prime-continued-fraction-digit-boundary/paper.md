# Prime-digit periodicity in the Gauss map proves too much to be a prime-symbolic source

**Paper ID:** `127-prime-continued-fraction-digit-boundary`  
**Record ID:** `ASFS-SCOUT-20260914-91`  
**Date / status:** `2026-09-14; PRE-P0 STOP — PRIME-DIGIT PERIODIC WORDS BELONG TO THE GAUSS CODING, NOT TO ORDINARY PRIME DISTRIBUTION`  
**Route state:** `No classical P0 or formal Route-A coordinate; Route B NOT INVOKED`

## Abstract

This screen tests whether continued-fraction dynamics can provide a geometric
route from a prime-labelled symbolic observable to an ASFS candidate.  For the
Gauss map `G(x)={1/x}` on irrational `x in (0,1)`, the regular
continued-fraction digits form a positive-integer symbolic coding.  Any finite
periodic word of positive integers, in particular any word all of whose digits
are prime, gives a periodic symbolic orbit.  Thus the availability of
prime-digit periodic packets is inherited from the full Gauss alphabet, not
generated by a prime/composite sieve action.  It is indiscriminate enough to
admit every chosen finite prime word, so it cannot represent the required
ordinary-prime distribution mechanism.  The source-lineage gate fails before
P0; no roof, suspension, operator, symplectic lift, or Route coordinate is
created.

## 1. Frozen screened object and same-object audit

Let `X=(0,1)\setminus Q` and define

\[
G:X\to X,\qquad G(x)=\{1/x\}.
\]

For the regular continued fraction `x=[0;a_1,a_2,\ldots]`, set

\[
a_n(x)=\left\lfloor 1/G^{n-1}(x)\right\rfloor\in\mathbb N_{\geq1}.
\]

The proposed arithmetic-looking observation would be the digit label
`P(a_n)=1` if `a_n` is prime and `0` otherwise.

| Required owner | What this screen has | Why it is insufficient |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carrier/action | the fixed Gauss map on `X` | one-dimensional and not a chosen symplectic base |
| Symbolic coding | the full digit alphabet `N_{>=1}` | exists before the prime label |
| Prime datum | membership of a digit in the prime subset | an observable/filter, not a prime-generating update |
| Periodic packets | periodic digit words / quadratic irrational orbits | owned by the full coding |
| Roof/suspension/operator | none selected | cannot be supplied from another object |
| Prior-work lineage | no sieve action, sequential deformation, Logistic obstruction bridge, or Hénon lift | absent |

## 2. Decisive coding test

For a finite word `w=(b_1,...,b_k)` of positive integers, the purely periodic
continued fraction

\[
x_w=[0;\overline{b_1,\ldots,b_k}]
\]

has digit sequence `wwww...`; hence it is periodic under the digit shift and
under the Gauss map (with a period dividing `k`).  If every `b_i` is prime,
the designated observable is identically one around that packet.  For example,
the word `(2,3,5)` yields a prime-digit periodic packet without consulting the
location of `2,3,5` in the ordinary prime sequence.

This is the decisive adverse control: the construction admits any selected
finite word of prime labels.  It does not encode prime gaps, sieve
admissibility, prime/composite evolution, or a non-autonomous deformation of
the project's Logistic-type seed.  Calling the digits “prime” therefore does
not create the required first arrow

```text
ordinary prime/composite observable -> symbolic admissibility constraint.
```

Instead it begins with an unrelated complete symbolic system and then marks
some of its symbols by an external arithmetic predicate.

## 3. Relation to the prior-work lineage

The prior-work route requires a preserved/deformed source relation from
prime distribution through symbolic admissibility and, when pursued, a
sequential and conservative/Hénon lift.  This object starts at a fixed
continued-fraction coding.  Prime-digit statistics may be mathematically
interesting, but they are statistics of an observable in that pre-existing
coding.  They do not supply the project's prime-symbolic seed or an
intertwining deformation to a Hénon-type area-preserving map.

This is therefore retained only as an `EXTERNAL CONTROL`.  It sharpens the
admission rule: a candidate may not obtain source credit simply because its
native symbolic alphabet contains numbers that can be tested for primality.

## 5. Decision

**Portfolio position: stop, then fork.**  Do not tune a digit subset, induce a
different continued-fraction map, attach a roof, or use its standard transfer
operator to repair this failure: each would still leave the prime label as a
filter on a pre-existing coding.  Continue the breadth search only for a
construction whose own state transition generates a prime-symbolic observable
and has a documented deformation or realization path to a single conservative
or symplectic owner.
