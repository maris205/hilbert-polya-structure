## CP2 — endpoint realization and primitive-word identification

The boundary propagation argument is valid: for an edge v->w, containment
gives f_v(a_v)<=a_w and f_v(b_v)>=b_w. A closure candidate already lying on

## CP2 — IR, cyclic SCCs and the exact target boundary

If a closed word had a boundary candidate under IR, extracting a simple cycle
from it would give that cycle its own boundary fixed point, contradicting IR.
Thus IR on simple cycles suffices for interior realization of every finite
closed word. This remains a finite-walk argument in a countable graph.

A multi-vertex SCC contains a simple directed cycle of length at least two,
by an internal edge followed by a shortest return path. IR realizes it and
the distinct vertices force its least source period to be that length.
Its product of at least two integers >=2 is composite. This is a primitive
obstruction, not the erroneous use of a repeated self-loop product.
For singleton cyclic SCCs, the sole primitive is the unique self-loop fixed
point, with time log d_v. Conversely every closed walk stays in one SCC;
if all cyclic SCCs are singleton prime-digit loops, only those primitives exist.

Hence the prime-only equivalence is correct UNDER IR. In that regime,
nonemptiness is exactly existence of a cyclic vertex and per-prime uniqueness
is injectivity of the digits on such vertices. Empty support is not nonempty
success. All-prime coverage additionally needs surjectivity; it is neither

Transient composite digits are allowed and affect phases, not extra core H.
Outside IR the exact endpoint test remains, not the graph-only equivalence.
The theorem presupposes the given actual interval realization; it does not
construct a geometric realization of an arbitrary abstract grammar.
