## 4. Actual histories, kernels and the entire isotropy image

Fix any one owner. Let \(D^{(j)}\) consist of sources with \(j\) legal steps,
\(D^{(0)}=X\), and set \(S_j(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\kappa(F^iz)\), \(S_0=0\).
The full groupoid has arrows
\[
\mathcal G=\{(z,r-s,w):z\in D^{(r)},w\in D^{(s)},F^rz=F^sw\},
\qquad c(z,r-s,w)=S_r(z)-S_s(w).                             \tag{8}
\]
Source is \(w\), range is \(z\); equal triples are identified, retaining lag.
Two representations of the same triple differ by an equal number of iterates

that \(c\) descends even for periodic histories. For composition, extend the
two meeting representations to the larger of their middle-source depths;
the matching middle sums cancel. Hence lags and \(c\) add; inversion negates
both. The actual forward arrow \((Fz,-1,z)\) has clock \(-\kappa(z)\).

## 5. The GLOBAL fixed sets

### MAIN: no fixed point

At a fixed point put \(s=1+R\). The fixed equations give
\[
                 (s-d)x=s,\qquad(s-q)y=-s.                   \tag{12}
\]
Neither coordinate is zero, and \(s\ne d,q\). Since
\(2|x|\leq1+x^2+y^2\), with equality only at \((\pm1,0)\), regularity
gives \(|x-1|<d/2\); similarly \(|y+1|<q/2\).
For any real \(u\), \(|\lfloor u\rfloor|<|u|+1\).
Thus \(dq<2+d/2\) and \(d<2+q/2\), so
\[
                 d(2q-1)<4,\qquad d<2+q/2.                  \tag{13}
\]
These exact inequalities exhaust all labels: \(q\geq3\) is impossible;
\(q=2\) forces \(d=1\); \(q=1\) first permits \(d\leq3\), then forces
\(d\leq2\). Only \((d,q)=(1,1),(1,2),(2,1)\) remain.

For \((1,1)\), (12) yields \(x=s/(s-1)>1\) and \(y=-x<-1\),
contradicting \(d=1\). For \((1,2)\), \(x=s/(s-1)>1\) but
\(x<3/2\), contradicting \(n=dq=2\).
For \((2,1)\), \(y=-s/(s-1)<-1\), hence \(R>1\) and
\(x=s/(s-2)>0\). Yet \(x<2\), contradicting \(n=2\).
Therefore \(\operatorname{Fix}(F_{\mathrm{MAIN}})=\varnothing\).
The argument covers negative cells and every integer boundary. Terminals
cannot be fixed because they have no step. This proves no MAIN higher-period
statement and provides no fixed core whose incoming basin could be selected.

### G: one fixed core and its whole incoming class

The origin is not fixed. Any fixed point satisfies \(x=s/(s-1)>1\) and
\(y=-x\). Consequently
\[
f=(a,-a),\qquad 2a^3-2a^2-1=0,\qquad 1<a<3/2.               \tag{14}
\]
The polynomial is strictly increasing for \(a>1\), is negative at \(1\)
and positive at \(3/2\); this proves existence and uniqueness globally.
Put \(\rho=2a^2>2\). Both inverse sheets at \(f\) are exactly
\[
                    f,\qquad b=f/\rho=(1/(2a),-1/(2a)).      \tag{15}
\]
Indeed the radial paired sources have reciprocal squared radii, as follows
directly from (2); substitution also verifies \(F_Gb=f=F_Gf\).
Every \(G\)-image has first coordinate strictly between \(1/2\) and \(3/2\).
But \(b_x<1/2\), so \(b\) has no predecessor. Equations (2) and (15) therefore
prove that the FULL incoming source class is exactly \(\{f,b\}\).

Write \(\gamma=\kappa_G(f)=\log((\rho-1)/(1+\rho)^3)<0\) and
\(\beta=\kappa_G(b)=\log((1-\rho^{-1})/(1+\rho^{-1})^3)<0\).
Both points have isotropy lags \(\mathbb Z\), entire \(H=\gamma\mathbb Z\),
trivial extension isotropy and all phases \(\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z\), where
\[
                         L=-\gamma=\log\frac{(1+\rho)^3}{\rho-1}>0. \tag{16}
\]
For explicit full arrows set \(\nu(f)=0,\nu(b)=1\), \(A(f)=0,A(b)=\beta\).
Every pair \(z,w\in\{f,b\}\) has every integer lag \(k\), and
\[
c(z,k,w)=A(z)-A(w)+(k-\nu(z)+\nu(w))\gamma.                   \tag{17}
\]
This follows by meeting at \(f\) after the indicated arrival depths, then
adding any nonnegative numbers of fixed steps. It specifies all three
kernels on this whole class by (9); its height phase is
\(h-A(z)\bmod L\). In fact \(\beta-\gamma=2\log\rho\), and \(\beta<0\)
implies \(0<2\log\rho<L\). Thus the clock and joint kernels on this class
contain only its two units; the lag kernel contains all four ordered pairs
at lag zero. The fixed point and its feeder are one full packet.

This control primitive is not a logarithm of an ordinary prime, or even of
a rational number. The cubic in (14) has no rational root: its only rational
root candidates are \(\pm1,\pm1/2\), and none is a root. Hence \(a\) has
degree three. Reduction by \(a^3=a^2+1/2\) gives
\((1+2a^2)^3=34a^2+10a+13\). If \(e^L=m\in\mathbb Q\), then
\((34-2m)a^2+10a+(13+m)=0\), a nonzero quadratic contradiction.
This adverse arithmetic-off result is not a MAIN witness.

### N: no fixed point

A fixed point would have \((1-d)x=1\). The case \(d=1\) is impossible.
For \(d\geq2\), \(x=-1/(d-1)\in[-1,0)\), so its actual \(n=1\),
contradicting \(n=dq\geq2\). Thus \(\operatorname{Fix}(F_N)=\varnothing\),
including its allowed circle points and assigned cuts. Its entire source,
inverse law, zero clocks, kernels and conditional higher-cycle ledger are
still (1), (3), (5), and (7)–(11); no absent fixed-core basin is fabricated.
