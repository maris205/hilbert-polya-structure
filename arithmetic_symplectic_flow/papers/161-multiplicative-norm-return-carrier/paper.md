## Abstract

We freeze a new scale-quotient suspension over all saturated divisibility
chains, rather than prescribe a length to each prime orbit. Rational
divisibility covers derive the prime ratio alphabet. One uniform
multiplicative flow on an additional positive scale coordinate induces
the roof log y_1 on the normalized chain section. The flow is complete,
and all primitive closed orbits are primitive ratio words modulo cyclic
phase. Their actual lengths are logarithms of their full word products.
Consequently each constant prime word has length log p with the correct
repetitions, but mixed primitive words such as (2,3) remain as genuine
closed orbits. Every bounded-time packet set is finite, and the complete
ordinary zeta has the elementary identity Z(s)=1/(1-P(s)), where
P(s)=sum_p p^(-s), in its absolute logarithmic convergence region
P(Re s)<1. The candidate stops at its prime-exclusive target. The
scale design is explicit engineering, not a proof that arithmetic
uniquely forces this clock. No operator or spectral result is asserted.

## 4. Exact construction and proofs

### Proposition 1 — Derived alphabet and the entire integer action

A divisibility cover has prime ratio. The map

\[
a_j=y_{j+1}/y_j
\tag{3}
\]

is a homeomorphism from X to the full two-sided shift on the derived
prime alphabet, conjugating F to the left shift. For every integer m,

\[
F^m(y)_j=y_{j+m}/y_m,\qquad
G^m(y,r)=(F^m y,r/y_m).
\tag{4}
\]

**Proof.** A strict ratio n is an integer at least 2. If n=ab with
a,b>=2, ax is an intermediate rational between x and nx. Conversely
any intermediate rational gives such a factorization. Therefore absence
of an intermediate point is exactly primality.

For an arbitrary two-sided sequence of these derived atoms, its unique
normalized chain is y_j=product_{i=0}^{j-1}a_i for j>0, y_0=1,
and y_j=(product_{i=j}^{-1}a_i)^(-1) for j<0. Each coordinate in this
formula or (3) depends on finitely many discrete coordinates, proving
the homeomorphism. Direct cancellation proves the shift relation.

F has inverse F^(-1)(y)_j=y_{j-1}/y_{-1}. Induction in either direction
then proves (4), including m=0. Hence G is an invertible continuous
action generator on the full product. QED.

### Proposition 2 — Complete scale flow and its actual section

Equation (2) defines a continuous flow for all real t. It is the
positive-roof suspension of F with tau(y)=log y_1>=log 2. A
fundamental strip is

\[
\{(y,r):1\le r\le y_1\},\qquad
(y,y_1)\sim(Fy,1).
\tag{5}
\]

Its semi-open version 1<=r<y_1 gives unique representatives.

**Proof.** Multiplication by e^t commutes with every G^m in (4).
It therefore descends to the quotient, with inverse time -t, group law
and continuity inherited from the product action. No finite-time
endpoint is removed from that flow.

Every successive ratio is at least 2. Consequently y_j tends to
infinity as j tends to positive infinity and to zero as j tends to
negative infinity. For each r>0 there is a unique integer k with
y_k<=r<y_{k+1}. Applying G^k gives scale r/y_k between 1 and
y_{k+1}/y_k=(F^k y)_1. This proves the unique representative claim.
At the upper boundary G(y,y_1)=(Fy,1), as required in (5).

In coordinates u=log r, the strip is 0<=u<=log y_1, and time translation
is u->u+t. Its section starting at (y,1) first reaches a next section
point at t=log y_1: the only crossings have e^t=y_m, and the smallest
strictly positive one uses m=1. The roof is continuous because y_1 is
a discrete coordinate. The lower bound log 2 ensures infinitely many
crossings cannot accumulate in finite forward or backward time. QED.

For completeness, the quotient is Hausdorff. In u coordinates,
G^m changes u by at least |m|log 2 in absolute value for m!=0. Two
bounded u neighborhoods can therefore meet only finitely many
translates. For two nonequivalent points, their product-space
neighborhoods can be shrunk to avoid those finitely many translates
using the Hausdorff topology and continuity of G^m. Their saturated
neighborhoods descend to disjoint quotient neighborhoods. This does
not assert local compactness: X is the full infinite-alphabet product.

### Proposition 3 — Full primitive ledger and owned logarithmic times

All primitive closed oriented flow orbits are primitive finite prime
words w=(a_0,...,a_{d-1}) modulo cyclic rotation. Put
N_w=product_j a_j. Then

\[
T_w=\log N_w,\qquad T_{w^r}=r\log N_w,\quad r\ge1.
\tag{6}
\]

There are no further closed orbits or multiplicities omitted by this
word classification.

**Proof.** A positive time t closes [y,r] precisely when, for some
integer m,

\[
G^m(y,e^t r)=(y,r).
\]

By (4), this is equivalent to F^m y=y and e^t=y_m. Since y_m>1
exactly when m>0, the positive-time condition forces m>0. By
Proposition 1, the chain is periodic if and only if its ratio sequence
is a repetition of a finite word. Let d be its least positive shift
period. The positive return indices are exactly rd, and
y_{rd}=N_w^r. Their least positive elapsed time is therefore log N_w.

Changing a section phase cyclically rotates the least word; all those
phases lie on the same flow orbit. Conversely, every point on Y meets
the section, so two section states on one flow orbit are such shift
phases, and no extra equivalence of words arises. Every periodic
chain gives one such closed orbit, and a nonperiodic chain gives none.
Reverse orientation is not separately identified. QED.

For each prime p, the constant chain y_j=p^j thus supplies one
primitive orbit of time log p, with r-fold time r log p. But the
word (2,3) is primitive, with time log 6. This is an immediate
prime-exclusive failure on the full frozen object, not a cutoff
artifact. The repeating word (2,3,2,3) gives its second traversal,
not a second primitive orbit.

Different primitive words can have the same product. For example,
(2,3,5) and (2,5,3) are not cyclic rotations, so they are distinct
primitive orbits of the same time log 30. Norm is not a complete
orbit label.

### Proposition 4 — Finite time cutoffs and the minimum zeta diagnostic

Every bounded positive-time cutoff contains only finitely many
primitive packets and their positive repetitions. Define

\[
P(s)=\sum_{p}p^{-s}.
\tag{7}
\]

The complete ordinary orbit logarithm converges absolutely exactly
when P(Re s)<1, interpreting the positive sum as possibly infinite.
In that region it satisfies

\[
\log Z(s)=
\sum_{[w]\ {\rm primitive}}\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{N_w^{-rs}}r
=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{P(s)^m}{m},
\qquad Z(s)=\frac1{1-P(s)}.
\tag{8}
\]

Equivalently, there is a unique h in (1,2) with P(h)=1, and the
absolute-logarithmic convergence region is Re s>h. In particular it
contains Re s>=2.

**Proof.** A return with time at most T has integer norm at most e^T.
Its ratio word has length at most T/log 2, and each ratio is a prime
at most e^T. There are finitely many such words. This retains every
factorization order and all repetitions; cyclic identification only
reduces the finite count.

For a word length m, summing the weight of every phase-marked periodic
word gives P(s)^m whenever its absolute version is finite. A primitive
word of least length d contributes d such phases to length m=rd,
each with weight N_w^(-rs). Dividing by m gives precisely its
contribution N_w^(-rs)/r to the ordinary orbit logarithm.

For real sigma, all weights are positive. Reordering them is valid
as an equality of extended nonnegative sums, and gives
sum_{m>=1} P(sigma)^m/m. If P(sigma) is infinite the length-one
sum already diverges. If it is finite but at least 1 the last series
diverges. If it is less than 1 it converges. This proves the exact
absolute-convergence condition and licenses the complex reordering.
The elementary power series sum z^m/m=-log(1-z), for |z|<1,
then gives (8) with the logarithm branch defined by that series.

Finally,
P(2)<=sum_{n>=2}n^(-2)<sum_{n>=2}(n(n-1))^(-1)=1.
For sigma>=2 the sum can only decrease, proving the displayed
sufficient half-plane. On (1,infinity), uniform convergence on compact
subintervals by comparison with sum_n n^(-1-delta) makes P continuous,
and every term strictly decreases. At sigma=1 the three terms
1/2+1/3+1/5=31/30 already exceed 1; by continuity of that finite
subsum the same holds for sigma slightly above 1. The intermediate
value theorem and strict decrease give a unique h in (1,2) with
P(h)=1. For sigma<=1 the same three terms also exceed 1, so the
criterion previously proved is exactly sigma>h. QED.

This diagnostic stops at (8). No analytic continuation, Fredholm
realization or spectral interpretation is claimed. The formula is a
full-word counting identity for this flow, not the prime-only Euler
product. For real s>=2, the omitted mixed (2,3) primitive alone
contributes a strictly positive amount to log Z; keeping only
constant words therefore gives a strictly smaller orbit logarithm.
