## Abstract

We replace the full divisibility-cover chain carrier by the entire set
of chains whose successive ratios are nondecreasing in the ordinary
numerical order. This is a declared admissibility design made before
classifying orbits, not a restriction to constant or periodic words.
The cover relation derives prime ratios; the new carrier is invariant
under the normalized shift and its inverse and retains nonperiodic mixed
chains. A full multiplicative-scale quotient supplies a complete
continuous flow and its actual positive return roof. Every periodic
admissible chain is constant, so the complete primitive flow ledger has
exactly one oriented circle of length log p for each prime p, and no
other closed orbits. The ordinary unweighted orbit product equals the
integer Dirichlet series in its absolute logarithmic convergence
half-plane Re s>1. The ordering and scale law are explicit construction
choices; arithmetic does not here uniquely force them. No chronological
sieve execution, classical symplectic realization, operator, trace,
Fredholm determinant, continuation, target-divisor or Route passage
is established.

## 3. Full carrier and complete quotient

### Proposition 1 — Arithmetic ratios and two-sided invariance

A strict divisibility cover has a prime ratio. Ratio coordinates
identify Y homeomorphically with all nondecreasing two-sided sequences
of these derived atoms. F is a homeomorphism of the entire Y and acts
as the left shift in ratio coordinates. For every integer m,

\[
F^m(y)_j=y_{j+m}/y_m,\qquad
G^m(y,r)=(F^m y,r/y_m).
\tag{4}
\]

**Proof.** A strict divisible ratio is an integer n>=2. If n=ab with
a,b>=2, ax is an intermediate rational between x and nx. Conversely,
an intermediate z has z/x=a>=2 and nx/z=b>=2 integers, so n=ab.
Absence of an intermediate is therefore exactly irreducibility in
the positive integers, namely primality.

For any two-sided sequence of the derived ratios, the only normalized
chain having those ratios is

\[
y_j=
\begin{cases}
\prod_{i=0}^{j-1}a_i,&j>0,\\
1,&j=0,\\
(\prod_{i=j}^{-1}a_i)^{-1},&j<0.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
\]

Each coordinate in (5), and each ratio in (1), depends on finitely many
discrete coordinates. These mutually inverse maps are continuous.
Condition (2) is precisely weak monotonicity of the ratio sequence.
Its violations are detectable in finitely many coordinates, so Y is
also a closed subspace of X.

The ratios of Fy are a_{j+1}; those of
F^{-1}(y)_j=y_{j-1}/y_{-1} are a_{j-1}. Both preserve all inequalities
in (2), including for negative j. The formulas are inverse continuous
maps. Direct cancellation and induction in both directions give (4).
This proves the full integer action, not only a forward semigroup.
QED.

The carrier is not merely its constant sequences. For example

\[
a_j=2\ (j<0),\qquad a_j=3\ (j\ge0)
\tag{6}
\]

is allowed and gives y_j=2^j for j<0 and y_j=3^j for j>=0. A nonzero
shift moves the unique change from 2 to 3, so this chain is not periodic.
All its shift phases and all their scale states belong to (3).

### Proposition 2 — Hausdorff quotient, complete flow and actual roof

The quotient Q is Hausdorff. Equation (3) defines a continuous flow
for every real t. Its section r=1 has first positive return time

\[
\tau(y)=\log y_1=\log a_0(y)\ge\log2.
\tag{7}
\]

The whole quotient is the positive-roof suspension of F, with no
discarded state. A closed fundamental strip is

\[
\{(y,r):1\le r\le y_1\},\qquad
(y,y_1)\sim(Fy,1).
\tag{8}
\]

**Proof.** In coordinates u=log r, formula (4) reads

\[
G^m(y,u)=(F^m y,u-\log y_m).
\tag{9}
\]

Every ratio is at least 2. For m>0, log y_m>=m log2; for m<0,
log y_m<=m log2. Thus nonzero translates move u in the corresponding
direction by at least |m|log2, independently of the starting chain.
In particular the action is free.

For completeness, take two nonequivalent product-space points and
bounded open u-neighborhoods around them. Only finitely many integers
m can have a translate of the first neighborhood intersect the second:
their u-intervals bound |m|log2 by a fixed finite constant. For each
remaining m the two relevant points are distinct. The product space
is Hausdorff and G^m is a homeomorphism, so shrink their neighborhoods
to avoid that translate. A finite intersection of these choices gives
neighborhoods U,V with G^m U disjoint from V for every m. Their
saturations are open and disjoint, hence project to disjoint quotient
neighborhoods. This proves Hausdorffness without assuming local
compactness.

Positive scaling commutes with each G^m in (4). Therefore (3)
descends to the quotient with the flow group law, inverse time -t,
and joint continuity. One can see the last assertion by using that
the quotient projection is open: its saturation is a union of open
translates, so the product with the time coordinate is also an open
quotient map.

For every y the numbers y_j strictly increase to infinity as j tends
to positive infinity and decrease to zero as j tends to negative
infinity. For any r>0 there is exactly one integer k with
y_k<=r<y_{k+1}. By (4), G^k sends (y,r) to

\[
(F^k y,r/y_k),\qquad
1\le r/y_k<(F^k y)_1.
\]

This proves that the semi-open strip gives unique representatives of
every point. Equation (8) supplies its actual endpoint gluing.
In u coordinates, (8) is the roof strip 0<=u<=log y_1.
No two distinct section states are identified: an equality
[y,1]=[z,1] requires y_m=1, hence m=0 and z=y.
Also, a strip |u|<epsilon with 2epsilon<log2 has no overlap with any
nonzero translate. Its restriction of the open quotient projection
is a homeomorphism onto its image. This supplies local flow charts
and makes the section an embedding, not merely an injective set map.

Starting at [y,1], a section crossing occurs precisely when
e^t=y_m for an integer m. For positive t this forces m>0; the first
such value is m=1. It arrives at [Fy,1], with time (7).
The roof is continuous since y_1 is a discrete coordinate. Every
forward and backward crossing costs at least log2. Thus there is
no finite-time accumulation of infinitely many returns. The descended
formula is defined for all real t and every state, so the flow is
complete on the full quotient. QED.

No local compactness, finite-dimensional manifold structure, symplectic
form or finite invariant measure is inferred from Proposition 2.

## 4. Complete primitive and repetition ledger

### Proposition 3 — Exactly one primitive circle per prime

The entire primitive oriented closed-orbit set of Phi consists of one
circle gamma_p for each prime p. Its least positive time and all its
positive repeated-traversal times are

\[
T_{\gamma_p}=\log p,\qquad
T_{\gamma_p^r}=r\log p,\quad r=1,2,\ldots.
\tag{10}
\]

There are no mixed primitive packets and no additional scale
multiplicity. The nonperiodic mixed states, including (6), remain in
Q but supply no closed orbit.

**Proof.** A chain with F^m y=y, m>0, has a_{j+m}=a_j for every j.
The full admissibility rule then gives

\[
a_j\le a_{j+1}\le\cdots\le a_{j+m}=a_j.
\]

All these inequalities are equalities, for every j. Thus all ratios
equal one integer p, which is prime by Proposition 1; conversely the
chain y_j=p^j belongs to Y and is fixed by F. This is a classification
of every periodic point of the full carrier, not a restriction made
after counting them.

For any quotient point [y,r], a positive time t closes it if and only
if there is an integer m for which

\[
G^m(y,e^t r)=(y,r).
\]

Using (4), the complete equivalence is

\[
F^m y=y,\qquad e^t=y_m.
\tag{11}
\]

Positive t forces m>0. The preceding argument therefore exhausts all
possible closed flow orbits, independently of the representative r.
For a constant p chain, y_m=p^m, and the time stabilizer is exactly
(log p)Z. Its least positive generator is log p, proving (10).

For fixed p, every positive scale r lies on the same flow orbit as
every other scale, and their quotient is
R_{>0}/p^Z, or equivalently R/(log p)Z. This is one circle, not
one primitive orbit for each scale representative. Distinct p cannot
be identified: the action fixes each of the distinct constant chains
separately, and the flow never changes its chain except by an actual
shift phase. Every point of Q meets the section, so no further closed
orbit is hidden away from this argument. The orientation is that of
increasing t; repeated traversal is not a new packet, and no reversal
equivalence is imposed. QED.

For any fixed T>0, primitive times <=T correspond to primes p<=e^T
and are finite. Counting all positive repetitions with time <=T
still gives a finite set: necessarily p<=e^T and
r<=T/log2. This is full coverage, not a numerical cutoff inference.

The repeated mixed word (2,3) does not belong to Y because it has a
3-to-2 transition. In contrast, the single-transition chain (6) does
belong. Thus the new weak ordering genuinely changes admissibility
and does not discard all mixed states.

## 5. Ordinary same-object Euler product

### Proposition 4 — Exact ordinary product on its defining half-plane

For the full primitive ledger in Proposition 3, define the ordinary
unweighted orbit logarithm by

\[
L(s)=\sum_{\gamma\ {\rm primitive}}\sum_{r\ge1}
\frac{e^{-srT_\gamma}}r.
\tag{12}
\]

Its absolute convergence region is exactly Re s>1. On that region,

\[
Z(s):=\exp L(s)
=\prod_p(1-p^{-s})^{-1}
=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s}.
\tag{13}
\]

The logarithm and Z are holomorphic there, and Z has no zero there.
This is the ordinary product of (3), not a Fredholm determinant.

**Proof.** Proposition 3 puts exactly the terms

\[
L(s)=\sum_p\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{p^{-rs}}r
\tag{14}
\]

in (12), with no weights added. For sigma=Re s>1,

\[
\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{p^{-r\sigma}}r
\le\frac{p^{-\sigma}}{1-p^{-\sigma}}
\le\frac{p^{-\sigma}}{1-2^{-\sigma}}.
\]

Summing over p and comparing with the convergent integer series
sum_{n>=2} n^{-sigma} proves absolute convergence. The same bound
with sigma replaced by 1+delta gives local uniform convergence on
each half-plane Re s>=1+delta. The exponential is consequently
holomorphic and nonzero. Summing each elementary power series gives
the product expression in (13).

For a finite set of primes, multiplication of the absolutely convergent
geometric series gives the sum of n^{-s} over integers whose prime
factors all lie in that set. Unique integer factorization supplies
coefficient one for every such integer. As the finite sets exhaust all
primes, the absolutely convergent full integer series bounds the tails
and gives the last equality in (13). In particular the normalization
has constant term 1 and no additional entire or exponential factor.

At sigma=1, the finite product using all primes <=N is at least
sum_{n=1}^N 1/n, because each of those integers occurs in its positive
geometric expansion. Taking logarithms shows that the partial-prime
sums of (14) are unbounded as N tends to infinity. For sigma<=1,
each positive term in the absolute version of (14) is at least its
sigma=1 term, so that series also diverges. This proves the exact
absolute convergence boundary. QED.

The last series in (13) is the usual defining Dirichlet series of
the Riemann zeta function on Re s>1. This statement concerns only
that defining half-plane. No continuation, functional equation,
completed function, target zeros or spectral realization is supplied
by the proof, and none is claimed as a dynamical result.

The strongest objection is not a hidden orbit defect: it is that the
new ordering rule was chosen to make returns compatible with
irreducible increments, and the multiplicative scale law was chosen
to measure them logarithmically. Those choices are openly part of
the object. The proofs establish their consequences, not a theorem
that arithmetic requires these choices or that they are physically
canonical.

The carrier can be called arithmetic because its exact covers derive
integer irreducibility. F still only shifts a pre-existing admissible
chain. It is not a sequential prime generator, nor a continuation
of the Logistic/Henon geometric branch. A complete positive-dimensional
conservative lift would need a new exact owner and full periodic
multiplicity proof. A selected family of circles, a disk thickening,
or a borrowed operator is not supplied by this paper.

No prime table, per-prime tuning, von Mangoldt coefficient or
Riemann-zero data were used. No numerical experiment was needed,
and no finite check proves the infinite statements above.
