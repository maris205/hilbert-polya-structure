## Source object

Mateos defines a “Dynamical Prime Number Function” using the displayed form

\[
P(s)=\sum_{p\ \mathrm{prime}}\left[p^s+\sin\!\left(\frac1{p^s}\right)\right],
\]

and describes a periodic function as starting at each prime time (p) to mark
multiples. The paper identifies the time line with the positive integers
(n=t), and then decodes primes by whether a time has been zero-crossed.

## Decisive gate test

The construction cannot claim to generate the prime parameters it uses: the
index condition (p\ \mathrm{prime}) occurs before the signals are combined,
and a new signal is added only after the corresponding prime time is identified.
This is precisely the prohibited external prime-index pattern. Replacing the
sum by a finite truncation leaves an externally supplied finite prime list;
retaining the infinite sum does not define a single finite-dimensional
symplectic map or a recurrent phase-space action.

The source's one-way zero-cross/decode chronology also supplies no closed
state-orbit convention. Its periodic sine components are individual supplied
signals, not primitive orbits of one frozen carrier. Therefore neither a roof
nor a determinant may be borrowed from a symbolic or Hénon comparator.
