## Abstract

The frozen permutation adds every nonzero residue increment a on every
cyclic residue space modulo n, without removing nonunits or selecting prime
moduli. Its full primitive period is n/gcd(n,a), with gcd(n,a) packets for
each pair (n,a). Consequently, n is prime exactly when every nonzero
increment in its complete n-fibre has primitive period n. Divisibility
therefore affects actual returns, not merely an observable attached to an
unrelated dynamics. This positive result also exposes the decisive failure:
for n=2k and a=k there are k primitive two-packets. The full unit-roof
suspension has infinitely many packets of length two, so its precommitted
ordinary unweighted primitive product diverges at every positive real
argument. The object stops without deleting nonunits, identifying packets
across moduli, or changing its roof.

## 3. Full action and packet calculation

### Proposition 1 — Complete owner-level periodic ledger

F is a homeomorphism of Y. For fixed n and a, put
\(g=\gcd(n,a)\) and \(m=n/g\). Every state in this fibre has primitive
F-period m. There are exactly g distinct primitive packets in the
\((n,a)\)-fibre. Since \(1\leq a<n\), every such m is at least two.

**Proof.** The exact inverse is
\[
F^{-1}(n,a,j)=(n,a,j-a\bmod n).
\]
Any map between discrete spaces is continuous, so both directions are
continuous. Induction gives \(F^k(n,a,j)=(n,a,j+ka\bmod n)\).
Thus return is equivalent to \(n\mid ka\).

Write \(n=gm\), \(a=gb\), where \(\gcd(m,b)=1\). The return condition
is \(m\mid kb\). Bézout's integer relation for the coprime pair (m,b)
shows that this is equivalent to \(m\mid k\). The least positive
return is therefore m, independently of j.

The residues visited by a starting point form one coset of the subgroup
generated by a. It consists of exactly m residues, so the n possible
values of j split into n/m=g disjoint cyclic orbits. Equivalently,
j modulo g indexes these packets. Nonzero a modulo n cannot give
a one-step return. This enumerates the entire periodic set because
every state of the frozen Y has one of the displayed n and a. \(\square\)

For the transformation groupoid, the isotropy at a point in this fibre is
the subgroup \(m\mathbb Z\) of time translations. The unit-roof
suspension is the quotient prescribed in the card, not a borrowed
flow. Each F-packet becomes an oriented circle of length m; its r-fold
traversal has length rm. Forward and backward flow are complete:
each unit of elapsed time crosses one further section, so no finite-time
accumulation of crossings is possible. Distinct F-packets remain distinct
flow orbits. In particular, a and n-a are not identified just because their
addition actions have opposite direction on an unlabelled residue circle.

## 4. Arithmetic changes complete returns

### Proposition 2 — Prime/composite criterion from the full fibre

For any \(n\geq2\), the following are equivalent:

1. n is prime;
2. every allowed increment \(1\leq a<n\) has primitive period n;
3. all primitive packets in the complete n-fibre have length n.

For prime n=p there are exactly p-1 packets in that fibre, each of length p.
For composite n there is an allowed increment giving a strictly shorter
primitive packet.

**Proof.** For prime p every \(1\leq a<p\) is coprime to p, so
Proposition 1 gives period p and one packet for each of the p-1
increments. If n is composite, choose any proper divisor d with
\(1<d<n\). The allowed increment a=d has gcd(n,a)=d and period
n/d<n. This proves both directions and the packet count. \(\square\)

No primality predicate occurs in F or in the definition of Y.
The gcd in the answer is derived from the same return congruence;
it was not used to remove states. The composite witnesses are part of the
full action. The positive conclusion is therefore an arithmetic distinction
in the owned return ledger, not only in the labels.

Its scope matters. Every modulus, including every composite, still has
periodic packets, and the prime criterion quantifies over the complete
n-fibre. It does not produce one distinguished prime packet, nor identify
primes from the globally unlabelled set of available periods. The fixed
unit roof gives lengths n/gcd(n,a), not \(\log p\). A coordinate
relabelling that transports the distinguished fibres does not erase this
criterion; conversely, discarding the fibres discards part of the frozen
arithmetic observation.

## 5. Decisive ordinary-product obstruction

### Proposition 3 — Infinitely many primitive packets at length two

The complete unit-roof suspension has countably infinitely many distinct
primitive packets of length two. Consequently, its ordinary unweighted
primitive product cannot converge to a finite value for any real s>0.

**Proof.** For each \(k\geq1\), use the allowed pair
\(n=2k,\ a=k\). Proposition 1 gives gcd(2k,k)=k, primitive
period two and exactly k packets. They are represented by
\[
\{(2k,k,j),(2k,k,j+k)\},\qquad 0\leq j<k.
\]
Different k belong to different n-fibres, so they cannot be cyclic phases
of the same packet. This gives infinitely many length-two packets. There
are at most countably many packets because Y is countable.

For any L distinct packets from this family, the corresponding exact
finite subproduct at real s>0 is
\[
\prod_{\ell=1}^L(1-e^{-2s})^{-1}
   =(1-e^{-2s})^{-L}.
\]
Here \(0<e^{-2s}<1\), so these positive subproducts tend to infinity
as L increases. Every other factor in an ordinary positive-real product
is greater than one as well. Thus the full ordinary product cannot
converge to a finite value at any real s>0. In particular, no standard
right-half-plane domain of ordinary product convergence can contain a
positive real ray. \(\square\)

This is the card's first decisive analytic stop. There is no need to
enumerate longer periods or attempt an operator. A measured, weighted,
relative or regularized trace was not frozen and is not evaluated.
The argument does not exclude all such different constructions.
