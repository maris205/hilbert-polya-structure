## Independent mathematical checks

### 1. Source, carrier, and mean norm

The unit classification follows from qA=A exactly when q and q inverse
belong to A. Actual localization equality removes repeated integer labels
without removing different prime supports. In rank one, invariance is
periodicity with L=log p. For a smooth L-periodic product f times conjugate h,
the remainder after complete periods has bounded integral, so the uniform
mean is precisely L inverse times the integral over one period. It is
positive definite, and the usual smooth-periodic completion has the stated
normalized Haar L2 norm.

For two different primes, irrationality of their logarithm ratio gives a
dense translation subgroup by the elementary pigeonhole argument. Continuity
then makes every invariant smooth function constant. Its mean norm is its
ordinary complex norm. This does not erase the localization index: different
mixed supports still produce different one-dimensional summands.

### 2. Fourier and distribution normalizations

On a rank-one component, exp(2 pi i m u/L) is an orthonormal basis for the
normalized measure du/L. The Gaussian convolution and translation
eigenvalues multiply to

\[
e^{-\epsilon(2\pi m/L)^2}e^{2\pi i mt/L}.
\]

Their absolute values are summable for each fixed L and epsilon>0. The
periodized Gaussian has Fourier coefficients
L inverse times exp(-epsilon(2 pi m/L)^2), so the trace is exactly
L times its periodization, as in (8). Consequently the distributional limit
on the original real time line is

\[
L\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\delta(t-kL),
\]

not an unweighted Dirac comb. This includes the zero-time term and both time
orientations; it is not being substituted for a positive-primitive-only
zeta expansion. The Gaussian tail argument in the paper suffices for each
compactly supported test function and fixed L. No uniform limit across all
localizations is asserted or needed.

### 3. Two distinct negative conclusions

The map J:f to f du is unitary because the norm and coefficient action are
identical in both degrees. It intertwines the smoothed translations exactly.
Thus every finite-component ordinary supertrace vanishes, including the
rank-one prime return distributions. This is an operator-pairing statement,
not a proof that the basic de Rham complex has zero cohomology.

Separately, the full Hilbert direct sum has an infinite orthonormal family
c_A, one constant in each distinct localization component. Both smoothing
and translation fix every c_A. Their images remain separated by distance
sqrt(2), proving noncompactness at every epsilon>0 and every real t in each
degree. The obstruction uses no limiting interchange or cancellation of
infinite series.

## Strongest counterargument and its disposition

An exact degree pairing permits the operator difference
R^0-J inverse R^1 J to be formed before taking a trace. This difference is
the zero operator, so its ordinary trace does exist on the full space.
That observation does not contradict the paper: it defines a paired
relative observable, rather than the frozen difference of two separate
ordinary degree traces. Its value is zero and therefore also fails to retain
the prime-circle return signal. The paragraph following Proposition 4 makes
both distinctions explicitly. The negative conclusion is consequently
neither an assertion that every imaginable supertrace is impossible nor a
claim that an undefined infinity-minus-infinity expression equals zero.
