## Abstract

We test the actual predecessor formula of a complete ordered physical
return-block flow, retaining every transverse state and using elapsed
Hamiltonian time. For every real s>0 its value on the constant function
is unbounded: on one fixed transverse level the actual branch times
have a uniform upper bound, whereas the number of predecessors grows
without bound. Thus no invariant realization in bounded continuous
functions containing constants exists in the frozen class. This stops
before compactness. A separate control on the retained least-atom sector
does admit a bounded weighted composition, but its continuously varying
actual clock forces a spectral interval and noncompactness. That sector
is not substituted for the full owner. Neither result disproves the
physical flow or excludes spaces outside the stated class. No new
determinant or Route coordinate is supplied.

## 2. Exact definitions, data and full natural extension

Let x <=_D y mean y/x is a positive integer. A strict cover has no
intermediate point for this order. Its ratio n is indecomposable among
integers >=2, hence prime: n=bc yields the intermediate bx, and an
intermediate conversely gives such a factorization. The least atom is 2.
This derives A from the source rule, not from a supplied prime table.

Let Y+ and Y_Z be all weakly nondecreasing words in A indexed by N_0
and Z, with product-subspace topology and shifts S and F. Put
U*={-1<I=QP<1}. Every n>=2 uses the same functions

\[
c_n=1+n^{-2},\quad W(q)=\frac{4q^2}{(1+q^2)^2},\quad
\delta_n=\frac1{4n^2},\quad\epsilon_n=\frac5{8n^2-3},
\]
\[
\eta(t)=\begin{cases}e^{-1/t}&t>0,\\0&t\le0,\end{cases}
\quad\chi(t)=\frac{\eta(t)}{\eta(t)+\eta(1-t)},
\quad g_n(u)=\epsilon_n+(1-\epsilon_n)
\chi\bigl((u+2\delta_n)/\delta_n\bigr),
\quad f_n(I)=\int_0^I g_n(u)\,du.
\tag{1}
\]

These are exactly the frozen full-strip transports: f_n is an increasing
smooth bijection (-1,1)->(-n^-2,1), f_n(0)=0 and g_n=f_n'>0.
Indeed chi(t)+chi(1-t)=1 gives
integral_(-1)^0 g_n=epsilon_n(1-3delta_n/2)+3delta_n/2=n^-2,
while f_n equals the identity for I>=-delta_n. The associated physical
transport S_n in coordinates (log|Q|,I) is
(s/g_n(I),f_n(I)); it is symplectic and exactly the identity near both
axes, as established on the entire strip in 177, Proposition 1.

The fixed actual scalar periods and return data are

\[
T_n(E)=4\int_0^{A_n(E)}\frac{dq}{\sqrt{2(E-c_n W(q))}},
\quad c_n W(A_n(E))=E,\quad 0<A_n(E)<1,
\]
\[
\tau_n(I)=T_n(1-f_n(I)),\quad h_n(I)=g_n(I)\tau_n(I),
\quad K_n(Q,P)=(e^{h_n(I)}Q,e^{-h_n(I)}P).
\tag{2}
\]

The physical equations are qdot=p, pdot=-c_n W'(q), Qdot=Q,
Pdot=-P on H_n=1. Cutting the entire inner-return saturation at
q=0,p>0, then using S_b S_n^-1 at each outgoing-to-incoming seam,
gives exactly (2): conjugating the actual physical return translates
s by g_n(I) times its elapsed time. Each K_n is a smooth invertible
whole-strip map preserving I. The actual roof is tau_n, not h_n.
The flow is the complete endpoint-glued suspension of
B(y,z)=(Fy,K_(y_0)z) with this roof; the uniform lower bound pi/5

There is no additional physical-state restriction in this contract.

**Lemma 1 (full presentation).** The natural extension of
B_+(x,z)=(Sx,K_(x_0)z) on all X=Y+ times U* is homeomorphic,
with the return dynamics, to B on Y_Z times U*.

**Proof.** A compatible inverse history records each missing initial
symbol, so its symbolic coordinates are precisely one nondecreasing
bilateral word. Conversely every such word has an admissible future at
every integer time. Fix its present transverse state z_0. For negative
times recursively define z_(-m)=K_(y_(-m))^-1 z_(-m+1), and for
positive times use K_(y_m). These operations exist on the whole strip
and keep I fixed. They give every compatible transverse history and
are unique. Each coordinate and its inverse depend on finitely many
symbols and smooth K maps; hence both directions are continuous in
the inverse-limit/product topology and conjugate the shifts. QED.

The pointwise predecessor formula is therefore forced, not an assigned

\[
(L_s h)(x,z)=\sum_{a\in A,\ a\le x_0}
e^{-s\tau_a(I)}h(ax,K_a^{-1}z),\qquad I=QP.
\tag{3}
\]

All predecessors have the same I as z. For each x the sum is finite;

It need not preserve boundedness globally. We only assume bounded

## 3. Earlier decisive gate: the image of the constant is unbounded

**Proposition A (full-state failure).** For every real s>0,
L_s 1 does not belong to C_b(X). Consequently no H,J,e,T_s
meeting the frozen representation conditions exists, even without
asking for compactness. In fact boundedness of T_s is not needed
for the contradiction once T_s e is required to belong to H.

**Proof.** Fix z=(1,1/2), so I=1/2. Formula (1) gives f_n(I)=I
for every n>=2, hence tau_n(I)=T_n(1/2). We prove the explicit
uniform bound T_n(1/2)<=sqrt(2) pi. Write c=c_n>=1 and let
A=A_n(1/2). Since W(A)=1/(2c)<=1/2<W(1/2)=16/25,
we have 0<A<1/2. Direct subtraction gives

\[
W(A)-W(q)=
\frac{4(A^2-q^2)(1-A^2q^2)}{(1+A^2)^2(1+q^2)^2}
\ge \frac{192}{125}(A^2-q^2)\ge A^2-q^2
\quad(0\le q\le A).
\tag{4a}
\]

The coefficient bound uses A^2,q^2<=1/4. Since E=cW(A),

\[
T_n(1/2)\le4\int_0^A\frac{dq}{\sqrt{2(A^2-q^2)}}
=\sqrt2\pi=:M.
\tag{4b}
\]

Enumerate the derived atoms increasingly as a_1,a_2,... . They are
infinite: a prime factor of one plus the product of any finite list
is outside that list. At x=a_N a_N ... every a_1,...,a_N is an
allowed predecessor, and (3) yields

\[
(L_s1)(a_N^\infty,(1,1/2))
=\sum_{j=1}^N e^{-sT_{a_j}(1/2)}
\ge N e^{-sM}\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{4c}
\]

But J T_s e would be bounded, since T_s e belongs to H and J maps
H into C_b(X). Intertwining and Je=1 contradict (4c). QED.

This full-state failure was found by the independent review after the
initial draft focused on compactness. It is the final, earlier stopping
reason. It does not assert the existence of a full bounded noncompact
operator or a full realized spectral interval. The next two sections
retain the original transverse mechanism as an additional internal
sector control, not as a replacement analytic owner.

## 4. The actual least-atom clock has an interval of values

**Lemma 2.** tau_2 is continuous on (-1,1), finite at every point,
and tends to infinity as I decreases to -1. In particular its values
on (-1,0] contain every t>=tau_2(0)=T_2(1).

**Proof.** Smooth dependence of the scalar ODE and its transverse
first return proves continuity on 0<E<c_2; each regular inner oval
has finite period. Also f_2(I)->-1/4 as I->-1, so
E=1-f_2(I)->c_2 from below. To check the divergence directly, put
v=(1-q^2)/(1+q^2), d=sqrt(1-E/c_2), and
k(v)=(1+v)^(-3/2)(1-v)^(-1/2). Then

\[
T_2(E)=\frac4{\sqrt{2c_2}}
\int_d^1\frac{k(v)}{\sqrt{v^2-d^2}}\,dv.
\tag{4}
\]

On [0,1/2] the continuous k has a positive lower bound m. For
d<1/2 the integral is at least m arcosh(1/(2d)), tending to
infinity. Finally apply the intermediate value theorem between I=0
and points approaching -1. No monotonicity of T_2 or numerical
asymptotic fit is needed. QED.

## 5. Additional least-atom sector obstruction, not a full-owner replacement

The retained subset X_2={2^infinity} times U* has exactly one
predecessor at each point, also in X_2. Thus the values of (3) on
X_2 depend only on values there. Identifying X_2 with U*, its
actual sector operator is

\[
(R_s h)(z)=e^{-s\tau_2(QP)}h(K_2^{-1}z).
\tag{4d}
\]

It is bounded on C_b(U*) with norm at most one, since s>0 and
tau_2>0. This is an internal comparison, not an operator for all X;
replacing X by X_2 would abandon the frozen arithmetic owner.

Use complex-linear dual functionals, with transpose T' ell=ell composed
with T. This notation avoids any Hilbert conjugation convention.

**Theorem 3 (sector control).** Fix a real s>0. Let H be a complex
Banach space, J_2:H->C_b(U*) bounded, J_2 e=1 for some e in H,
and T:H->H bounded with J_2 T=R_s J_2. Then

\[
[0,e^{-sT_2(1)}]\ \subseteq\ \sigma(T).
\tag{5}
\]

Consequently T is not compact. If H is Hilbert it is not ordinary
trace class. In particular the actual R_s on C_b(U*) is noncompact.
No injectivity of J_2 is needed. A putative full representation would
restrict to such J_2, but Proposition A has already ruled it out.

**Proof.** Fix any I in (-1,0], take z_0=(1,I), and set
z_j=K_2^j z_0 for all integers j. These are all allowed strip points,
though they need not lie in a compact set. Let x*=222... . Its only
admissible predecessor symbol is 2. With lambda_I=exp(-s tau_2(I))
and delta_j(v)=(J_2 v)(z_j), formula (4d) gives

\[
\delta_j(T v)=\lambda_I\delta_{j-1}(v),\qquad
\|\delta_j\|\le\|J_2\|,\qquad\delta_j(e)=1.
\tag{6}
\]

Define M_N=N^-1 sum_(j=0)^(N-1) delta_j. The norm bound is uniform,
so Banach--Alaoglu gives a weak-star convergent subnet with limit ell_I
in H'. No sequential compactness, separability, transverse measure or
compact physical orbit is assumed. Equations (6) imply

\[
\ell_I(e)=1,\qquad
\|M_NT-\lambda_I M_N\|
\le\frac{2\lambda_I\|J_2\|}{N}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]

Indeed the difference telescopes to
lambda_I(delta_(-1)-delta_(N-1))/N. Boundedness of T lets us pass
to the weak-star limit, obtaining the nonzero dual eigenfunctional
ell_I T=lambda_I ell_I. Thus T-lambda_I cannot be onto: its
range is annihilated by ell_I, whereas ell_I(e)=1. In particular
lambda_I belongs to the spectrum of T, not necessarily to its
primal point spectrum.

By Lemma 2 these lambda_I include (0,exp(-sT_2(1))]. Closedness
of the bounded-operator spectrum adds zero. The Riesz--Schauder
theorem permits only countably many nonzero spectral values for a
compact operator on a complex Banach space, with zero as their only
possible accumulation point. This contradicts (5). Hilbert trace-class
operators are compact, giving the last statement. QED.

Only standard functional-analysis facts are imported in this proof:
weak-star compactness of a bounded dual ball and the compact-operator
spectral restriction. They are stated in
[Shirbisheh, Lectures on C*-algebras, Theorems 2.0.4 and 2.4.24(iii)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.3404).
Only these localized statements and the adjacent compact-spectrum proof
were read, not the full notes. An author-maintained primary teaching record is
[Genoud and van Gaans, Functional Analysis 2015, Lectures 4--6](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~gaansowvan/FA2015.html).
Its compact-operator discussion includes the complex Banach setting and
finiteness of spectral eigenvalues outside any disk about zero. The
new sector construction (6)--(7) and physical clock lemma above
are local arguments, not results claimed to occur in that reference.

## 6. Why this does not create new periodic packets

The full physical orbit ledger is unchanged. A periodic B point first
forces a constant source by weak ordering. On that source, positive
h_a(I) makes K_a^m z=z possible only for Q=P=0. There is one
central primitive physical oval per derived atom, with its ordinary
positive repetitions and time T_a(1). This is the whole-strip argument
of 177, Proposition 4, not the definition of a selected centre carrier.

In contrast, the proof above deliberately takes z_0=(1,I), not the
origin. The orbit-average functionals can be limits at transverse
infinity and are not asserted to be countably additive physical
probabilities, closed trajectories or new arithmetic packets. The sector
spectral interval is an additional bounded-observable obstruction, not a
continuum of primitive closed orbits or the spectrum of a realized full operator.

The ordinary full-orbit product already defined in 177 remains a
scalar object on its proved convergence domain. Theorem 3 supplies
no representation for it and does not remove it. The operator of 179
acts on a different scale owner and lacks these transverse levels;
its trace-class theorem therefore gives no counterexample to (5).
