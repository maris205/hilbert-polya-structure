\begin{abstract}
Let $n\mid(q-1)$ and let inversion on the cyclic group $C_n$ induce the
canonical involution $*$ of the split group algebra
$\A_{q,n}=\F_q[C_n]$.  We determine the complete finite dynamics of the
nonlinear norm map $T(a)=aa^*$.  Character inversion partitions the Fourier
coordinates into self-inverse blocks $z\mapsto z^2$ and paired blocks
$(u,v)\mapsto(uv,uv)$.  If $s=\gcd(n,2)$ and $o=(n+s)/2$, then
\[
 \#\Fix(T^k)=\bigl(1+\gcd(2^k-1,q-1)\bigr)^o
\]
for every $k\geq1$.  Writing $q-1=2^\alpha m$ with $m$ odd, the recurrent
core has size $(m+1)^o$, while the maximum transient depth is exactly
$\alpha+\mathbf1_{\{n>s\}}$.  M\"obius inversion gives every cycle and a
finite product for the Artin--Mazur zeta function.  Finally, phase-space
size, the fixed sequence, and maximum depth recover $(q,n)$, including the
ambiguous two-orbit branch.  Literal cyclic convolution over prime fields
and explicit models of $\F_4$ and $\F_{16}$ independently check the formulas.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and ownership boundary}

Let $q=p^h$ be a prime power, let $n\geq1$ divide $q-1$, and choose a
generator $g$ of $C_n$.  On
\[
 \A_{q,n}=\F_q[C_n]
   =\left\{\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}a_rg^r:a_r\in\F_q\right\}
\]
consider the canonical inversion involution
\[
 \left(\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}a_rg^r\right)^*
   =\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}a_rg^{-r}
\]
and the self-map
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:T}
 T\colon\A_{q,n}\longrightarrow\A_{q,n},\qquad T(a)=aa^*.
\end{equation}
The phase space has $q^n$ elements.  The assumption $n\mid(q-1)$ is kept
throughout: it makes the cyclic algebra split over the stated ground field
and excludes modular nilpotents from $x^n-1$.

Group-algebra involutions and their symmetric and unitary units are classical;
see, for example, \citet{BovdiGrishkov2019}.  Finite Fourier decomposition is
standard \citep{Terras1999}, and scalar power-map functional graphs over
finite fields have an established literature
\citep{VasigaShallit2004,QureshiReis2023}.  We assign those ingredients no
novelty credit.  Our bounded target is their conjunction for the map
\eqref{eq:T} on the \emph{whole} group algebra, including zero divisors:
the paired-coordinate synchronization, all fixed and cycle counts, the
sharp transient depth, and a recovery statement.  A targeted source search
completed on 29 August 2026 did not locate this exact package, but that
database- and query-bounded observation is not a priority or novelty claim.
External circulation remains on \textbf{HOLD} pending a specialist review.

There are two proof routes.  A coefficient-free involution calculation first
collapses every orbit to ordinary powering inside the symmetric algebra.
Fourier idempotents then expose independent inversion-orbit blocks and make
the census exact.  The computational control reverses this order: it starts
from literal coefficient convolution and cyclic reversal, then compares the
result with the Fourier and arithmetic predictions.

\section{One-step collapse and Fourier blocks}

Write
\[
 \A_{q,n}^+=\{b\in\A_{q,n}:b^*=b\}.
\]
The first route requires neither a primitive root nor a choice of
coordinates.

\begin{proposition}[Algebraic collapse]
\label{prop:collapse}
For every $a\in\A_{q,n}$,
\[
 T(a)\in\A_{q,n}^+,
 \qquad
 T^k(a)=(aa^*)^{2^{k-1}}\quad(k\geq1).
\]
Moreover $T(b)=b^2$ for every $b\in\A_{q,n}^+$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The cyclic group algebra is commutative, so
$(aa^*)^*=a^*a=aa^*$.  Thus $T(a)$ is symmetric.  If $b=b^*$, then
$T(b)=bb^*=b^2$.  Starting with $b=aa^*$ and iterating squaring proves the
displayed formula.
\end{proof}

For the second route, choose a primitive $n$th root $\omega\in\F_q$.  The
Fourier transform
\[
 \what a(j)=\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}a_r\omega^{jr},\qquad j\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,
\]
is an algebra isomorphism from $\A_{q,n}$ to $\F_q^n$.  Indeed,
$p\nmid n$ and the usual Fourier matrix is invertible.  Multiplication becomes
coordinatewise, while
\[
 \what{a^*}(j)=\what a(-j).
\]
Let
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:so}
 s=\gcd(n,2),\qquad o=\frac{n+s}{2}.
\end{equation}
Here $s$ is the number of self-inverse characters and $o$ is the number of
orbits of $j\mapsto-j$.

\begin{theorem}[Fourier block normal form]
\label{thm:normal}
Under the Fourier isomorphism, a self-inverse character carries the block
\[
 z\longmapsto z^2,
\]
whereas a two-element orbit $\{j,-j\}$ carries
\[
 (u,v)\longmapsto(uv,uv).
\]
In particular, for every $k\geq1$ and every $j$,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:iterate}
 \what{T^k(a)}(j)
 =\bigl(\what a(j)\what a(-j)\bigr)^{2^{k-1}}.
\end{equation}
The fixed algebra $\A_{q,n}^+$ is isomorphic, as an algebra, to $\F_q^o$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Coordinatewise multiplication gives
\[
 \what{T(a)}(j)=\what a(j)\what a(-j).
\]
If $j=-j$, this is $z^2$.  Otherwise the two output coordinates coincide,
giving the paired block.  After the first step both kinds of blocks square,
which proves \eqref{eq:iterate}.  Finally, $a=a^*$ is equivalent to
$\what a(j)=\what a(-j)$ on every inversion orbit; choosing one common
coordinate on each orbit identifies the fixed algebra with $\F_q^o$.
\end{proof}

The paired block exhibits the first qualitative anomaly: two independent
input coordinates synchronize in one step and never separate.  This is the
only source of an extra transient level beyond scalar squaring.

\section{Fixed points and the complete recurrent census}

For $k\geq1$, put
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:Lk}
 L_k=1+\gcd(2^k-1,q-1).
\end{equation}
