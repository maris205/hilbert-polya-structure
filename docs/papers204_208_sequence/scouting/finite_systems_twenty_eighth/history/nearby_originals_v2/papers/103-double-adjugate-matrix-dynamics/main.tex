\begin{abstract}
For $d\ge3$ and a finite field $\F_q$, we study the self-map of the full
matrix space
\[
 \Psi(A)=\adj(\adj A),\qquad A\in M_d(\F_q).
\]
Jacobi's classical identity gives $\Psi(A)=\det(A)^{d-2}A$; we assign that
identity zero novelty credit and determine its finite temporal consequences.
Every singular matrix enters zero in one step.  With
$\alpha=(d-1)^2$ and $E_k=(\alpha^k-1)/d$, every invertible matrix satisfies
\[
 \Psi^k(A)=\det(A)^{E_k}A.
\]
Consequently
\[
 |\Fix(\Psi^k)|=1+|\SL_d(q)|\gcd(E_k,q-1),
 \qquad
 |\im(\Psi^k)|=1+\frac{|\GL_d(q)|}{\gcd(\alpha^k,q-1)}.
\]
The second formula yields the recurrent-core size and a sharp
prime-valuation stabilization time.  The first gives every cycle count and
the Artin--Mazur zeta function by M\"obius inversion, including alternating
and third-period fixed-count anomalies.  A second route computes two
adjugates literally from minors and exhausts small matrix spaces.  The note
is owner-subtracted against Jacobi identities, projective adjugate/Cremona
maps, and scalar power-map functional graphs; external release remains on
hold.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The adjugate is simultaneously a polynomial matrix operation and, on
invertible matrices, determinant times inversion.  Its projectivization is
a classical Cremona transformation \cite{Dolgachev2012}.  Jacobi's complementary-minor identity
and its hyperadjugate extensions are established material
\cite{Lawrence1964}; standard finite-field facts used below can be found in
\cite{LidlNiederreiter1997}.  Functional graphs of power maps on finite
groups likewise have a direct modern literature \cite{QureshiReis2023}.
None of those ingredients is claimed here.

We instead ask what happens when the adjugate is applied twice at every
time step on the \emph{entire} finite matrix space.  Two regimes coexist.
All singular ranks collapse immediately to zero, whereas every invertible
projective scalar line is invariant and carries an affine power map on its
nonzero scalars.  This separation makes both the fixed sequence and the
shrinking image sequence exact.  Their arithmetic is different: fixed
counts see $(\alpha^k-1)/d$, while image sizes see $\alpha^k$ itself.

The residual contribution is the conjunction of the iterate normal form,
singular basin, fixed and image formulas, valuation stabilization, and full
cycle/zeta census over finite fields.  A bounded source search did not find
this temporal package, but it is not an absolute novelty certificate.
External submission, public release, and priority language remain
\textbf{HOLD}.

\section{Normal form and singular collapse}

Fix a prime power $q$ and an integer $d\ge3$.  Put
\[
 X_{q,d}=M_d(\F_q),\qquad \Psi(A)=\adj(\adj A).
\]
The restriction $d\ge3$ is structural: for $d=2$, adjugation is already a
linear involution and its square is the identity.

\begin{lemma}[Double-adjugate identity]\label{lem:jacobi}
For every $A\in M_d(\F_q)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:double-adj}
 \Psi(A)=\det(A)^{d-2}A.
\end{equation}
In particular, every singular matrix maps to zero, and zero is fixed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For invertible $A$, the identities
$\adj(A)=\det(A)A^{-1}$ and
$\det(\adj A)=\det(A)^{d-1}$ give \eqref{eq:double-adj} immediately.
Both sides of \eqref{eq:double-adj} are polynomial in the matrix entries,
and Jacobi's identity extends the equality to all matrices
\cite{Lawrence1964}.  Alternatively, the rank-$d-1$ case has
$\operatorname{rank}(\adj A)=1$, whose adjugate vanishes for $d\ge3$;
lower ranks already have $\adj A=0$.  If $\det A=0$, the right side of
\eqref{eq:double-adj} is zero.
\end{proof}

Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha-E}
 \alpha=(d-1)^2,\qquad E_k=\frac{\alpha^k-1}{d}\quad(k\ge1).
\end{equation}
The quotient is integral because $\alpha\equiv1\pmod d$.

\begin{theorem}[Iterate normal form]\label{thm:iterate}
If $A\in\GL_d(q)$ and $\delta=\det A$, then for every $k\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iterate}
 \boxed{\Psi^k(A)=\delta^{E_k}A},
 \qquad
 \det(\Psi^k(A))=\delta^{\alpha^k}.
\end{equation}
Every nonzero singular matrix has transient depth one and then stays at
zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:jacobi}, if $B$ is invertible with determinant $\eta$, then
$\Psi(B)=\eta^{d-2}B$ and
\[
 \det\Psi(B)=\eta^{d(d-2)+1}=\eta^{(d-1)^2}=\eta^\alpha.
\]
Thus the scalar exponent in the $k$th iterate obeys
$E_{k+1}=E_k+(d-2)\alpha^k$, with $E_1=d-2$.  Since
$\alpha-1=d(d-2)$, summing the geometric progression gives
$E_k=(\alpha^k-1)/d$.  The singular assertion follows from
\cref{lem:jacobi}.
\end{proof}

\section{Fixed sequence and cycle census}

Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:group-sizes}
 |\GL_d(q)|=\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(q^d-q^j),
 \qquad |\SL_d(q)|=\frac{|\GL_d(q)|}{q-1}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[All iterate-fixed counts]\label{thm:fixed}
For every $k\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 |\Fix(\Psi^k)|
 =1+|\SL_d(q)|\gcd(E_k,q-1).}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the number $C_r$ of cycles of exact length $r$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycles}
 C_r=\frac1r\sum_{e\mid r}\mu(r/e)
 \left(1+|\SL_d(q)|\gcd(E_e,q-1)\right),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeta}
 \zeta_\Psi(z)=\prod_{r\ge1}(1-z^r)^{-C_r}
 =\exp\left(\sum_{k\ge1}
 \frac{1+|\SL_d(q)|\gcd(E_k,q-1)}{k}z^k\right).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A singular matrix fixed by a positive iterate must equal zero.  For
$A\in\GL_d(q)$, \cref{thm:iterate} gives
$\Psi^k(A)=A$ exactly when $(\det A)^{E_k}=1$.  The cyclic group
$\F_q^\times$ has $\gcd(E_k,q-1)$ such determinants, and every determinant
fiber in $\GL_d(q)$ has cardinality $|\SL_d(q)|$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:fixed}.  M\"obius inversion of iterate-fixed counts gives
\eqref{eq:cycles}, and the standard finite-map cycle factorization gives
\eqref{eq:zeta}.
\end{proof}

The formula can show large low-period jumps even though projective matrix
classes never move.  Two registered examples are
\begin{align*}
 (q,d)=(5,4):&\quad
 |\Fix(\Psi^k)|=58\,032\,000\,001,
 116\,064\,000\,001,\ldots,\\
 (q,d)=(7,3):&\quad
 |\Fix(\Psi^k)|=5\,630\,689,5\,630\,689,
 16\,892\,065,\ldots.
\end{align*}
The first sequence alternates; the second has a jump precisely at multiples
of three.  These are direct gcd effects in \eqref{eq:fixed}, not numerical
experiments.

\section{Projective image staircase and recurrent core}

Let $[A]=\{cA:c\in\F_q^\times\}$ be the projective scalar line through an
invertible matrix.  Every such line has $q-1$ points, and distinct lines are
disjoint.

\begin{theorem}[Exact images]\label{thm:image}
For every $k\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:image}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 |\im(\Psi^k)|
 =1+\frac{|\GL_d(q)|}{\gcd(\alpha^k,q-1)}.}
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:b}
 b=\prod_{\ell\mid\alpha}\ell^{v_\ell(q-1)},
\end{equation}
where the product is over primes dividing $\alpha$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrent}
 |\Rec(\Psi)|=1+\frac{|\GL_d(q)|}{b}.
\end{equation}
With the empty maximum interpreted as zero, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tstar}
 t_*=\max_{\ell\mid\gcd(\alpha,q-1)}
 \left\lceil\frac{v_\ell(q-1)}{v_\ell(\alpha)}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
For $k\geq0$, write $I_k=\Psi^k(\GL_d(q))$, so that
$I_0=\GL_d(q)$.  The nested invertible image chain
$I_0\supseteq I_1\supseteq\cdots$ stabilizes for the first time at $t_*$,
and the maximum transient depth on all of $M_d(\F_q)$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:max-depth}
 \boxed{\max\{1,t_*\}.}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $A\in\GL_d(q)$ with determinant $\delta$.  For $c\in\F_q^\times$,
\cref{thm:iterate} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:line-action}
 \Psi^k(cA)=\delta^{E_k}c^{dE_k+1}A
 =\delta^{E_k}c^{\alpha^k}A.
\end{equation}
Thus the image on each projective line is a coset of the subgroup of
$\alpha^k$th powers.  It has
$(q-1)/\gcd(\alpha^k,q-1)$ points.  There are
$|\GL_d(q)|/(q-1)$ invertible projective lines, and every singular matrix
contributes only the common image zero.  This proves \eqref{eq:image}.

For each prime $\ell$, the exponent of $\ell$ in
$\gcd(\alpha^k,q-1)$ is
$\min\{k v_\ell(\alpha),v_\ell(q-1)\}$.  Hence these gcds stabilize at
$b$, first at the time \eqref{eq:tstar}.  In a finite functional graph the
stable image is exactly the recurrent set, proving \eqref{eq:recurrent}.
Because images of a finite self-map are nested, strict image loss through
time $t_*-1$ and equality from time $t_*$ imply that the maximum invertible
tail depth is exactly $t_*$.  This includes $t_*=0$, when the restriction to
the invertible set is a permutation.  Singular points contribute depth one,
proving \eqref{eq:max-depth}.
\end{proof}

In discrete-log coordinates on a projective line,
\eqref{eq:line-action} is an affine power map
$x\mapsto\alpha x+h$ modulo $q-1$.  This is the second proof route for the
valuation staircase: an affine translation changes cycle placement but not
the image index of multiplication by $\alpha^k$.

\section{Exact controls and owner boundary}

The accompanying program computes determinants by modular elimination and
adjugates literally from signed minors.  It exhausts
$M_3(\F_2)$, $M_3(\F_3)$, and $M_4(\F_2)$, comparing two literal
adjugations with \eqref{eq:double-adj}.  It then checks singular collapse,
six fixed counts, and six image sizes.  Separate determinant-representative
lanes test twelve iterates for $(q,d)=(5,4),(7,3),(11,4),(13,5)$.
Independent scalar-line lanes at $(q,d)=(5,4),(7,3),(17,3),(257,3),(19,4)$
exercise image stabilization times $t_*=0,1,2,4,1$, respectively, including
literal strict losses and the first stable image.
All arithmetic is exact.  The computations are finite falsifiers, not a
replacement for the proofs.

Jacobi's identity, the projective adjugate map, cyclicity of
$\F_q^\times$, and general power-map functional graphs are established
background.  We claim none of them separately.  The residual claim is the
full-matrix temporal package \eqref{eq:fixed}--\eqref{eq:max-depth}, under
a bounded collision search and an external-release hold.

Two internal boundaries are explicit.  P99 obtains valuation staircases from
a unipotent permutation of fixed-index sublattices; it has no singular basin,
adjugate identity, or shrinking projective lines.  P97 studies sumset
squaring on subsets of a prime cyclic group; here the power map occurs only
on scalar coordinates inside an invariant matrix line.  Neither paper owns
the full-matrix fixed/image pair proved here, while the shared valuation and
power-map primitives receive no separate credit.

\section{Conclusion}

Double adjugation separates the finite matrix space into a singular basin
that dies immediately and invertible projective lines carrying affine power
maps.  This yields two complementary exact time series: the fixed sequence
detects $E_k=(\alpha^k-1)/d$, while the image staircase detects the prime
valuations of $\alpha^k$ against $q-1$.  Together they determine every
cycle count, the zeta function, the recurrent core, and the sharp transient
depth.
