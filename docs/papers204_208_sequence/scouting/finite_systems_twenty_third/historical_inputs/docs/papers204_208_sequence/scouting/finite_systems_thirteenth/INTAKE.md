## Mechanisms charged before execution

**HMP.** Equal hook lengths cannot occupy the same row or column: moving
right reduces the arm length and never increases the leg length; moving
down reduces the leg length and never increases the arm length. For any
$k$ hook values their cells occupy at most $k$ places in each column, so
their total count is at most $\sum_j\min(k,\lambda'_j)
=\sum_{i\le k}\lambda_i$. Maximizing over the $k$ most frequent hook
values proves $T\lambda\preceq\lambda$ in dominance; row counting also
gives $T\lambda\preceq\lambda'$. Thus generic strict-dominance descent
already supplies fixed recurrence and a coarse integer-potential clock.
Neither that nor P113's different principal-diagonal hook theorem earns
new credit. The unresolved target is a genuinely sharper complete clock/
fixed language with an independently evaluated all-target inverse/extremum.

**FBT.** If $d$ is the number of distinct positive part sizes, the new
support has size at most $d$. If no support loss occurs, the nonzero
multiplicities are distinct and the next transpose returns the original
partition. Otherwise support strictly drops. This is the complete generic
cohort-merger time mechanism. Its inverse chooses disjoint sets of distinct
old part labels with prescribed positive sums; a weighted subset-assignment
coefficient is a classical/static restatement. The relation to old DGR is
precomposition by Ferrers conjugation, **not** an asserted conjugacy.

**SSC.** Every output is even. More generally, if a source is in the
$k$th derived subgroup of the group generated by it and $c$, normality
puts its conjugate there too, and their commutator is in the next derived
subgroup. Solvable cases therefore have an automatic derived-length
erasure bound. No all-$S_n$ nilpotence follows. The 2006 Bandman et al.
recursive commutator update already contains this update form as the
$x=1,y=c$ specialization with a free dynamical state; their prescribed
initial word is not an arbitrary initial permutation. This primitive,
not the full all-permutation atlas, is charged in advance.

**CSP.** Canonical cycle support plus ordered nonminimum labels is an exact
coordinate chart. In that chart the map is a fixed permutation of slots,
independent of label values. Applying its inverse in each cycle proves
bijection and singleton fibres. Every point on a fixed collection of cycle
supports has period the least common multiple of the corresponding slot
permutation orders, since the labels are all distinct. Both proposed axes
are fully conjugate fixed-shuffle bookkeeping.

**AMC.** $U$ is old G01 maximum-matching allowed-edge support, not a new
primitive. AMC is ambient complementation after $U$; old G02 instead
deletes $U(A)$ only from $A$. These literals are not confused, and target
complementation is not a time conjugacy. The inverse is exactly
$U^{-1}(E\setminus Y)$, so merely renaming allowed-support constraints
is not a new inverse solution.

There is also a generic finite optimization time argument: for any fixed
hereditary feasible family and its maximum-cardinality union $U$, put
$A_{t+1}=E\setminus U(A_t)$ and $r_t$ equal to the optimum size in
$A_t$. Since $U(A_t)\subseteq A_{t+2}$, $r_{t+2}\ge r_t$. If equality
holds, every old optimum remains optimal in $A_{t+2}$, hence
$U(A_t)\subseteq U(A_{t+2})$. Once both parity ranks stabilize, those
supports grow monotonically in a finite set and the orbit reaches period
at most two. This is not a sharp matching-specific clock or an evaluated
inverse/extremal theorem. Those are the only possible remaining axes.

**EIM.** The intersection is a subset of any one optimum matching, hence
is itself a matching. Its unique maximum matching is itself, so applying
EIM twice gives EIM once. The entire temporal axis is a consensus-image
projection, with no pilot warranted.
