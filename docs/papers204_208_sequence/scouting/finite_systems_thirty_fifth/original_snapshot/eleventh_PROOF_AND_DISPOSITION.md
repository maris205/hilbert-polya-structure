## 1. WZS: exact facts, not an all-d recurrent atlas

### Claim and status

Let $d\ge0$, $V=\mathbb F_2^d$, $N=2^d$, and let $T$ be WZS.
The complement identity, odd-weight forcing for $d\ge2$, and affine-subspace
formulas below are **PROVABLE AS STATED**. A classification of every orbit
for every $d$, a sharp global height, and every target's fibre/extremizers
are **NOT CURRENTLY JUSTIFIED**. The original pilot checks do not establish
these statements beyond their five fixed boxes.

### Notation, strategy and dependency map

Write $\chi_\xi(x)=(-1)^{\xi\cdot x}$ and
$\widehat{1_S}(\xi)=\sum_{x\in S}\chi_\xi(x)$.
The character orthogonality identity implies
$$W_S(\xi)=N1_{\xi=0}-2\widehat{1_S}(\xi).$$
The deductions use this identity, finite character cancellation, and
nondegeneracy of the standard dot product. No probabilistic or numerical
premise enters the proof.

### Proof

**Step 1: complement and parity.** Replacing $S$ by $V\setminus S$
negates every signed input, so $W_{V\setminus S}=-W_S$ and
$T(V\setminus S)=T(S)$. In particular every nonempty fibre has even
cardinality. If $d\ge2$ and $|S|$ is odd, then
$W_S(0)=N-2|S|\equiv2\pmod4$. For nonzero $\xi$, the integer
$\widehat{1_S}(\xi)$ is a sum of an odd number of signs and is odd;
hence $W_S(\xi)=-2\widehat{1_S}(\xi)\equiv2\pmod4$.
No coefficient is zero, so $T(S)=\varnothing$.

**Step 2: the empty/punctured cycle and boundary dimensions.** The empty
state has $W_\varnothing(0)=N$ and all other coefficients zero, giving
$T(\varnothing)=V\setminus\{0\}$. For $d\ge2$ this latter state has
odd size and maps to $\varnothing$, yielding a genuine two-cycle.
For $d=0$, both states map to $\varnothing$, which is fixed. For $d=1$,
using labels $0,1$, the exact transitions are
$\varnothing\mapsto\{1\}\mapsto\{0\}\mapsto\{0\}$ and
$V\mapsto\{1\}$. These exhaust the boundary carriers.

**Step 3: affine subspaces.** Let $S=a+U$ with $U\le V$ of dimension
$k$, and put $q=2^k$. For $\xi\notin U^\perp$, choose $u\in U$
with $\chi_\xi(u)=-1$; pairing $x$ with $x+u$ in the coset cancels
the character sum. For $\xi\in U^\perp$, the character is constant
on the coset. Thus
$$\widehat{1_{a+U}}(\xi)=q\chi_\xi(a)1_{\xi\in U^\perp}.$$
At zero, the signed transform is $N-2q$. At nonzero frequencies its
nonvanishing set is exactly $U^\perp\setminus\{0\}$. Therefore
$$T(a+U)=
\begin{cases}
V\setminus U^\perp,&k\ne d-1,\\
V\setminus(U^\perp\setminus\{0\}),&k=d-1.
\end{cases}$$
This includes the full affine subspace and every degeneracy allowed by
$0\le k\le d$; Step 2 handles small $d$ explicitly.

**Step 4: a deducted recurrent family.** If $2\le k\le d-2$, both
$k$ and $d-k$ avoid the hyperplane exception. Complement invariance and
nondegeneracy give
$$T(V\setminus U)=V\setminus U^\perp,\qquad
T(V\setminus U^\perp)=V\setminus U.$$
Thus these complementary subspaces are fixed exactly when
$U=U^\perp$, otherwise paired in two-cycles. Every coset $a+U$ maps
in one step into this recurrent family. This is precisely a classical
orthogonal-complement involution on this subfamily; it receives no fresh
temporal credit.

**Step 5: explaining the observed $d=4$ core without generalizing it.**
There are $(15\cdot14)/(3\cdot2)=35$ two-dimensional subspaces of
$\mathbb F_2^4$, since each has six ordered bases. A self-orthogonal
plane lies in the even-weight hyperplane $E$. The restriction of the
dot product to $E$ is alternating with radical generated by
$\mathbf1=(1,1,1,1)$. The quotient $E/\langle\mathbf1\rangle$
is nondegenerate alternating of dimension two. Any isotropic plane
must contain this radical: otherwise its two-dimensional quotient image
would be isotropic in a nondegenerate two-dimensional space, which is
impossible because nondegeneracy forbids a two-dimensional orthogonal
annihilator. Conversely each of the three one-dimensional subspaces of
that quotient lifts to an isotropic plane. There are consequently three
self-dual planes and sixteen pairs of distinct orthogonal planes.
Together with the empty/punctured two-cycle this constructs three fixed
points and seventeen two-cycles, exactly the finite census in the
original $d=4$ output. This equality with the census proves exhaustion
only for that executed finite carrier, not for all $d$.

### Independent inverse boundary

For an arbitrary target $A$, the fibre is exactly the set of sign vectors
$\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^V$ satisfying
$$\sum_x\epsilon_x\chi_\xi(x)=0\quad(\xi\in A),\qquad
\sum_x\epsilon_x\chi_\xi(x)\ne0\quad(\xi\notin A).$$
This is the ordinary intersection of Walsh hyperplanes with the Boolean

## 2. ACP: literal correctness and a completely deducted quadratic inverse

### Claim and status

For prime $p>r\ge2$, the resultant defines a monic degree-r polynomial
self-map and the quotient-algebra implementation is valid. For $r=2$
the every-target fibre, exact image size and sharp fibre maximum below
are **PROVABLE AS STATED** for every odd prime. An all-parameter full
temporal atlas, for $r=2$ or general $r$, is **NOT CURRENTLY JUSTIFIED**.

### Notation, strategy and dependency map

For general $r$, put $h=f'/r$, a monic polynomial of degree $r-1$.
The proof uses the roots-with-multiplicity product formula for a
resultant, multiplication in $\mathbb F_p[X]/(h)$, and, in degree two,
only Vieta and the elementary square-root count in a prime field.

### Proof

**Step 1: resultant and quotient matrix.** Over an algebraic closure,
write $h(X)=\prod_j(X-\alpha_j)$ with repetitions. The resultant
product gives
$$r^{-r}\operatorname{Res}_X(f',Y-f)=\prod_j(Y-f(\alpha_j)).$$
All coefficients are in $\mathbb F_p$, either directly from the
resultant formula or by Frobenius invariance. The polynomial is monic
of degree $r-1$. Multiplication by $f$ in $\mathbb F_p[X]/(h)$ has
this characteristic polynomial: after extension to a splitting field,
Chinese remainders separate distinct roots; in the local factor at
$\alpha$, basis $1,(X-\alpha),\ldots$ makes multiplication upper
triangular with diagonal $f(\alpha)$. Multiplicities are preserved.
Multiplying by $Y-f(0)$ therefore gives the literal ACP map even for
repeated or nonsplit critical points. This is classical quotient-ring
linear algebra, not a new theorem contribution.

**Step 2: quadratic formula.** Write $f=X^2+aX+b$. Its sole critical
point is $-a/2$ and critical value is $b-a^2/4$. Therefore
$$T(a,b)=(u,v)=\left(a^2/4-2b,\ b^2-a^2b/4\right),$$
where $(u,v)$ means target $Y^2+uY+v$. Every target predecessor is
obtained uniquely by choosing
$$b^2+ub+v=0,\qquad a^2=4(u+2b).$$
Necessity follows from substitution and the anchor root. Conversely,
these two equations imply both displayed target coefficients, so no
predecessor is omitted or counted twice.

**Step 3: every-target count.** A nonsplit target has no predecessor.
A target $(Y-b)^2$ has exactly one, with $a=0$. For a target with
distinct roots $b,c\in\mathbb F_p$, choosing anchor $b$ requires
$a^2=4(b-c)$; choosing anchor $c$ requires $a^2=4(c-b)$. If $\chi$
is the quadratic character, its fibre size is
$$2+\bigl(1+\chi(-1)\bigr)\chi(b-c).$$
The expression is independent of the chosen root order: when
$\chi(-1)=-1$ the character term vanishes, and otherwise changing
the order leaves it unchanged.

**Step 4: sharp extrema and complete size distribution.** If
$p\equiv3\pmod4$, every distinct split target has fibre two, giving
maximum two with $p(p-1)/2$ maximizers. Exactly $p$ targets have fibre
one, $p(p-1)/2$ have fibre two, and $p(p-1)/2$ have fibre zero;
the image size is $p(p+1)/2$.

If $p\equiv1\pmod4$, a distinct split target has fibre four when its
root difference is a square and zero otherwise. There are
$p(p-1)/4$ unordered root pairs of each character: there are $p$
choices of the first root and $(p-1)/2$ choices of a square difference,
and dividing by two is legitimate since $-1$ is a square. Thus the
maximum is four, attained at $p(p-1)/4$ targets. The fibre histogram is
one at $p$ targets, four at $p(p-1)/4$ targets and zero at
$3p(p-1)/4$ targets. The image size is $p(p+3)/4$. These maxima are
attained because odd primes in this residue class are at least five.
Both histograms sum to $p^2$ targets and conserve $p^2$ sources.
This entire axis is an elementary two-root/square-character adapter,
not independent novelty in the presence of the classical critical-value
map.

**Step 5: an occupied invariant temporal slice, not the whole map.**
The line $b=0$ is invariant and sends $a$ to $a^2/4$. Writing $a=4z$
conjugates it to ordinary squaring $z\mapsto z^2$ on $\mathbb F_p$.
Consequently its cycles are standard finite-field power-map cycles.
The four-cycle at $p=11$ in the original canonical with ascending
coefficient pairs $(b,a)$,
$(0,1)\to(0,3)\to(0,5)\to(0,9)\to(0,1)$, lies on this slice.
It rules out an all-fixed or all-period-at-most-two full-carrier claim,
but is not new temporal content.

There are also off-slice recurrent states in the same original box:
$(b,a)=(5,6)\to(2,10)\to(9,10)\to(10,7)\to(5,6)$.
Direct substitution in Step 2 verifies all four transitions and their

## 3. BPF desk deduction: explicit known-rule adapter and generic energy

This is a desk-only proof, with **zero BPF numerical executions**.
The Banzhaf literal and source normalization adapter are in the intake.
For completeness, its tempting convergence signal also has a generic
energy explanation. Let $\Omega=\{-1,1\}^n$ and put
$g_w(x)=1$ when $w\cdot x>0$, and $-1$ otherwise. Define
$$c(w)=2^{-n}\sum_{x\in\Omega}g_w(x)x.$$
Pairing inputs that differ only in coordinate $i$ shows
$c_i(w)=2^{-(n-1)}B_i(w)$: the pair contributes zero unless $i$ is
a swing, in which case it contributes two. For an image vector
$z=c(w)$, let $z'=c(z)$. Pointwise $g_z$ maximizes
$g(x)(z\cdot x)$ among signs, including zero ties. Therefore
$$z\cdot z'=2^{-n}\sum_x|z\cdot x|
\ge2^{-n}\sum_xg_w(x)(z\cdot x)=\|z\|^2.$$
Cauchy--Schwarz implies $\|z'\|\ge\|z\|$. If equality holds
and $z\ne0$, equality in Cauchy--Schwarz with nonnegative dot product
forces $z'=z$. If $z=0$, its next value is zero by cancellation of
the cube. The finite set of possible swing vectors hence strictly
increases this norm whenever it changes, so every orbit stabilizes.
This argument does not establish a sharp clock or inverse extremal
theorem, and the underlying reweighting process was already explicit in
the inspected primary source. **DESK_EXCLUDE_DIRECT_FACTOR**.
