\title[Involutive norms in cyclic group algebras]{Involutive Norm Dynamics in Split Cyclic Group Algebras:\ Fixed Sequences, Sharp Depth, and Rigidity}
\author{Anonymous}

\begin{abstract}
Let $n\mid(q-1)$ and let inversion on the cyclic group $C_n$ induce the
canonical involution $*$ of the split group algebra
$\A_{q,n}=\F_q[C_n]$.  We determine the complete finite dynamics of the
nonlinear norm map $T(a)=aa^*$.  Character inversion partitions the Fourier
coordinates into self-inverse blocks $z\mapsto z^2$ and paired blocks
$(u,v)\mapsto(uv,uv)$.  If $s=\gcd(n,2)$ and $o=(n+s)/2$, then
\[
 \#\Fix(T^k)=\bigl(1+\gcd(2^k-1,q-1)\bigr)^o
\]
for every $k\geq1$.  Writing $q-1=2^\alpha m$ with $m$ odd, the recurrent
core has size $(m+1)^o$, while the maximum transient depth is exactly
$\alpha+\mathbf1_{\{n>s\}}$.  M\"obius inversion gives every cycle and a
finite product for the Artin--Mazur zeta function.  Finally, phase-space
size, the fixed sequence, and maximum depth recover $(q,n)$, including the
ambiguous two-orbit branch.  Literal cyclic convolution over prime fields
and explicit models of $\F_4$ and $\F_{16}$ independently check the formulas.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and ownership boundary}

Let $q=p^h$ be a prime power, let $n\geq1$ divide $q-1$, and choose a
generator $g$ of $C_n$.  On
\[
 \A_{q,n}=\F_q[C_n]
   =\left\{\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}a_rg^r:a_r\in\F_q\right\}
\]
consider the canonical inversion involution
\[
 \left(\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}a_rg^r\right)^*
   =\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}a_rg^{-r}
\]
and the self-map
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:T}
 T\colon\A_{q,n}\longrightarrow\A_{q,n},\qquad T(a)=aa^*.
\end{equation}
The phase space has $q^n$ elements.  The assumption $n\mid(q-1)$ is kept
throughout: it makes the cyclic algebra split over the stated ground field
and excludes modular nilpotents from $x^n-1$.

Group-algebra involutions and their symmetric and unitary units are classical;
see, for example, \citet{BovdiGrishkov2019}.  Finite Fourier decomposition is
standard \citep{Terras1999}, and scalar power-map functional graphs over
finite fields have an established literature
\citep{VasigaShallit2004,QureshiReis2023}.  We assign those ingredients no
novelty credit.  Our bounded target is their conjunction for the map
\eqref{eq:T} on the \emph{whole} group algebra, including zero divisors:
the paired-coordinate synchronization, all fixed and cycle counts, the
sharp transient depth, and a recovery statement.  A targeted source search
completed on 29 August 2026 did not locate this exact package, but that
database- and query-bounded observation is not a priority or novelty claim.
External circulation remains on \textbf{HOLD} pending a specialist review.

There are two proof routes.  A coefficient-free involution calculation first
collapses every orbit to ordinary powering inside the symmetric algebra.
Fourier idempotents then expose independent inversion-orbit blocks and make
the census exact.  The computational control reverses this order: it starts
from literal coefficient convolution and cyclic reversal, then compares the
result with the Fourier and arithmetic predictions.

\section{One-step collapse and Fourier blocks}

Write
\[
 \A_{q,n}^+=\{b\in\A_{q,n}:b^*=b\}.
\]
The first route requires neither a primitive root nor a choice of
coordinates.

\begin{proposition}[Algebraic collapse]
\label{prop:collapse}
For every $a\in\A_{q,n}$,
\[
 T(a)\in\A_{q,n}^+,
 \qquad
 T^k(a)=(aa^*)^{2^{k-1}}\quad(k\geq1).
\]
Moreover $T(b)=b^2$ for every $b\in\A_{q,n}^+$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The cyclic group algebra is commutative, so
$(aa^*)^*=a^*a=aa^*$.  Thus $T(a)$ is symmetric.  If $b=b^*$, then
$T(b)=bb^*=b^2$.  Starting with $b=aa^*$ and iterating squaring proves the
displayed formula.
\end{proof}

For the second route, choose a primitive $n$th root $\omega\in\F_q$.  The
Fourier transform
\[
 \what a(j)=\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}a_r\omega^{jr},\qquad j\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,
\]
is an algebra isomorphism from $\A_{q,n}$ to $\F_q^n$.  Indeed,
$p\nmid n$ and the usual Fourier matrix is invertible.  Multiplication becomes
coordinatewise, while
\[
 \what{a^*}(j)=\what a(-j).
\]
Let
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:so}
 s=\gcd(n,2),\qquad o=\frac{n+s}{2}.
\end{equation}
Here $s$ is the number of self-inverse characters and $o$ is the number of
orbits of $j\mapsto-j$.

\begin{theorem}[Fourier block normal form]
\label{thm:normal}
Under the Fourier isomorphism, a self-inverse character carries the block
\[
 z\longmapsto z^2,
\]
whereas a two-element orbit $\{j,-j\}$ carries
\[
 (u,v)\longmapsto(uv,uv).
\]
In particular, for every $k\geq1$ and every $j$,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:iterate}
 \what{T^k(a)}(j)
 =\bigl(\what a(j)\what a(-j)\bigr)^{2^{k-1}}.
\end{equation}
The fixed algebra $\A_{q,n}^+$ is isomorphic, as an algebra, to $\F_q^o$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Coordinatewise multiplication gives
\[
 \what{T(a)}(j)=\what a(j)\what a(-j).
\]
If $j=-j$, this is $z^2$.  Otherwise the two output coordinates coincide,
giving the paired block.  After the first step both kinds of blocks square,
which proves \eqref{eq:iterate}.  Finally, $a=a^*$ is equivalent to
$\what a(j)=\what a(-j)$ on every inversion orbit; choosing one common
coordinate on each orbit identifies the fixed algebra with $\F_q^o$.
\end{proof}

The paired block exhibits the first qualitative anomaly: two independent
input coordinates synchronize in one step and never separate.  This is the
only source of an extra transient level beyond scalar squaring.

\section{Fixed points and the complete recurrent census}

For $k\geq1$, put
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:Lk}
 L_k=1+\gcd(2^k-1,q-1).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact fixed sequence]
\label{thm:fixed}
For every $k\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:fixed}
 F_k:=\#\Fix(T^k)=L_k^o
 =\bigl(1+\gcd(2^k-1,q-1)\bigr)^o.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $T^k(a)=a$ precisely when $a\in\A_{q,n}^+$ and each of its
$o$ symmetric Fourier coordinates satisfies $z^{2^k}=z$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In a paired block, fixedness under the $k$th iterate and
\cref{eq:iterate} force
\[
 u=v=(uv)^{2^{k-1}}.
\]
Writing the common value as $z$ reduces the condition to $z^{2^k}=z$.
The same equation holds on a self-inverse block.  It has the root $0$ and
exactly $\gcd(2^k-1,q-1)$ roots in the cyclic group $\F_q^\times$.  The $o$
inversion orbits are independent, proving \eqref{eq:fixed}.  The equivalent
description also follows directly from \cref{prop:collapse}: a fixed point of
$T^k$ lies in the first image and hence is symmetric, where $T^k$ is
$2^k$th powering.
\end{proof}

Write
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:decomp}
 q-1=2^\alpha m,\qquad m\ \text{odd},
\end{equation}
and let $\mu_m\leq\F_q^\times$ be the subgroup of order $m$.  Set
\[
 R_q=\{0\}\cup\mu_m.
\]
For a point $x$ in a finite dynamical system, define
$\depth(x)=\min\{r\geq0:T^r(x)\text{ is periodic}\}$, and let $D$ be the
maximum of this depth over the phase space.

\begin{theorem}[Recurrent core and sharp depth]
\label{thm:depth}
The recurrent set is, in symmetric Fourier coordinates,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:recset}
 \Rec(T)=R_q^o\subseteq\A_{q,n}^+.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:recsize}
 \#\Rec(T)=(m+1)^o.
\end{equation}
The maximum transient depth is
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:depth}
 D=\alpha+\mathbf1_{\{n>s\}}.
\end{equation}
Both bounds are attained, including the cases $n=1,2$ and characteristic
two.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $z\neq0$, write $\ord(z)=2^u d$ with $d$ odd.  Repeated squaring removes
one factor of $2$ from the order at each step until $u=0$.  Thus $z$ is
periodic under squaring if and only if $z\in\mu_m$; zero is fixed.  Its scalar
depth is $u$, whose maximum is $\alpha$, attained by a primitive element of
$\F_q^\times$.

A periodic paired block must already be in the image of its update, hence
must be diagonal.  On the diagonal it is scalar squaring, so its recurrent
points are exactly $(z,z)$ with $z\in R_q$.  This proves \eqref{eq:recset}
and \eqref{eq:recsize}.

Every self-inverse block reaches its recurrent part in at most $\alpha$
steps.  Every paired block becomes diagonal after one step and then needs at
most $\alpha$ further squarings.  Hence the right side of \eqref{eq:depth} is
an upper bound.  More precisely, if $d_q(z)$ denotes scalar depth under
$z\mapsto z^2$, then a self block $z$ has depth $d_q(z)$, while a paired
block $(u,v)$ has depth
\[
 \depth(u,v)=
 \begin{cases}
  d_q(u),&u=v,\\
  1+d_q(uv),&u\ne v.
 \end{cases}
\]
Indeed, diagonal pairs already follow scalar squaring, whereas a nondiagonal
pair cannot be periodic and first synchronizes to $(uv,uv)$.  Thus the extra
level belongs only to genuinely nondiagonal pairs.  If no paired orbit
exists, then $n=s$, equivalently
$n\in\{1,2\}$, and a primitive scalar coordinate attains depth $\alpha$.
If a pair exists, put $(1,\gamma)$ on that block for a primitive
$\gamma\in\F_q^\times$ and put recurrent values on the other blocks.  The
first step gives $(\gamma,\gamma)$, which still has scalar depth $\alpha$;
the total depth is $\alpha+1$.  When $q$ has characteristic two,
$\alpha=0$; a pair can occur only when $n\geq3$, and the same construction
attains the remaining one synchronization step.
\end{proof}

\section{Cycles and the Artin--Mazur zeta function}

Let $P_k$ be the number of points of least period $k$ and $C_k=P_k/k$ the
number of $k$-cycles.  Let $\mu$ denote the M\"obius function.

\begin{theorem}[All cycles and zeta]
\label{thm:zeta}
For every $k\geq1$,
\begin{align}
 P_k&=\sum_{d\mid k}\mu(k/d)
       \bigl(1+\gcd(2^d-1,q-1)\bigr)^o,                         \label{eq:Pk}\\
 C_k&=\frac1k\sum_{d\mid k}\mu(k/d)
       \bigl(1+\gcd(2^d-1,q-1)\bigr)^o.                         \label{eq:Ck}
\end{align}
If $\ell=\ord_m(2)$ for $m>1$ and $\ell=1$ for $m=1$, then $C_k=0$ unless
$k\mid\ell$.  The Artin--Mazur zeta function \citep{ArtinMazur1965} is the
rational finite product
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:zeta}
 \zeta_T(z)
 :=\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{F_k}{k}z^k\right)
 =\prod_{k\mid\ell}(1-z^k)^{-C_k}.
\end{equation}
In particular, \eqref{eq:Ck} is a complete cycle inventory, not only an
asymptotic count.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In any finite map, $F_k=\sum_{d\mid k}P_d$.  M\"obius inversion and
\cref{thm:fixed} give \eqref{eq:Pk}; division by $k$ gives
\eqref{eq:Ck}.  On $R_q\setminus\{0\}=\mu_m$, squaring is an automorphism of
order $\ell$.  Coordinatewise squaring on $R_q^o$ also has order $\ell$, so
every recurrent period divides $\ell$.  Finally, a cycle of length $k$
contributes
\[
 \exp\left(\sum_{r\geq1}\frac{z^{rk}}r\right)=(1-z^k)^{-1}
\]
to the defining exponential.  Multiplying over all cycles proves
\eqref{eq:zeta}.
\end{proof}

\section{Rigidity from temporal data}

The exponent $o$ remembers only the number of inversion orbits, so by itself
it leaves an odd/even ambiguity in $n$.  Depth and phase size resolve it.

\begin{theorem}[Recovery of the field size and cycle length]
\label{thm:rigidity}
Within the family $n\mid(q-1)$, the data
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:data}
 \left(q^n,\ (F_k)_{k\geq1},\ D\right)
\end{equation}
determine the ordered pair $(q,n)$ uniquely.  More explicitly:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $o=\log_2F_1$;
\item $m=\max_{k\geq1}F_k^{1/o}-1$;
\item the only possible lengths are $n=2o-1$ and, when $o\geq2$,
      $n=2o-2$;
\item for a candidate $n$, put
\[
 \varepsilon_n=\mathbf1_{\{n>\gcd(n,2)\}},\qquad
 q_n=2^{D-\varepsilon_n}m+1.
\]
The unique candidate satisfying the phase-size identity $q_n^n=q^n$ (and
the family conditions) is the original pair.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $L_1=2$, \cref{thm:fixed} gives $F_1=2^o$.  Because $2^k-1$ is odd,
\[
 \gcd(2^k-1,q-1)=\gcd(2^k-1,m).
\]
If $m>1$, some $k$ satisfies $2^k\equiv1\pmod m$; for $m=1$ the claim is
immediate.  Thus the maximum in item 2 is exactly $m$.  Solving
$2o=n+\gcd(n,2)$ gives precisely the candidates in item 3, and
\cref{thm:depth} gives the formula for $q_n$.

It remains to show that the candidates cannot both survive.  If $o=1$, only
$n=1$ is positive.  If $o\geq3$, both candidate lengths have paired
characters, so both have $\varepsilon_n=1$ and hence the same candidate
field size.  Their distinct positive exponents cannot give the same phase
size.

The only delicate branch is $o=2$, where the candidates are $n=2$ and
$n=3$.  Write their reconstructed field sizes as $q_2$ and $q_3$.  Their
different synchronization corrections give
\[
 q_2-1=2(q_3-1).
\]
If both also matched the phase size, then $q_2^2=q_3^3$, so coprimality of
$2$ and $3$ would give integers $q_2=r^3$ and $q_3=r^2$ with $r\geq2$.
Substitution yields
\[
 r^3-1=2(r^2-1),\qquad
 (r-1)(r^2-r-1)=0,
\]
which has no integer solution with $r\geq2$.  Hence at most one candidate
survives in every branch, while the true one necessarily does.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of rigidity]
The phase-size datum in \eqref{eq:data} is the cardinality of the given
finite phase space, not an invariant inferred from the zeta function.  The
theorem is therefore a qualified recovery statement inside the split cyclic
family, not a claim that zeta functions classify arbitrary finite maps or
group algebras.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact controls and limitations}

The accompanying verifier uses no computer-algebra package.  For each stored
small lane it enumerates all coefficient vectors, computes cyclic convolution
and reversal literally, constructs the full functional graph, and checks:
the Fourier product rule, \cref{eq:iterate,eq:fixed,eq:recsize,eq:depth}, the
M\"obius cycle ledger, and the zeta exponents.  Prime-field lanes include
$(q,n)=(3,1),(3,2),(5,2),(5,4),(7,3),(11,2),(13,3)$.  Separate polynomial
basis implementations of
\[
 \F_4=\F_2[t]/(t^2+t+1),\qquad
 \F_{16}=\F_2[t]/(t^4+t+1)
\]
exercise genuine extension-field arithmetic at $n=3$.  A broader arithmetic
lane checks the recovery algorithm for all divisors $n\mid(q-1)$ over a
registered list of prime powers.  The stored output is a deterministic exact
certificate, not numerical evidence in place of the proofs.

The splitting hypothesis is essential to the present formulas.  When
$n\nmid(q-1)$, Frobenius acts nontrivially on character blocks; when
$p\mid n$, the group algebra is not semisimple.  Neither regime is covered
here.  Likewise, the cited unit literature focuses largely on units, whereas
the transient maximum here is often attained using a whole-algebra block;
this distinction motivates the calculation but does not establish priority.

There is also an internal firewall against P86.  That paper studies the
spatial two-block factor $Y_i=U_iU_{i+1}$ of an iid bi-infinite field process,
with support entropy and prediction memory as its observables.  Here a paired
product is only one Fourier block of a finite self-map; after one update it
synchronizes and enters scalar squaring, and the observables are temporal
fixed counts, cycles, and depth.  The shared multiplication primitive is
therefore disclosed rather than counted as a separate contribution.
