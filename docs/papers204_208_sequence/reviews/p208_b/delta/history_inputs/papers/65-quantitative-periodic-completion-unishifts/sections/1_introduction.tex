\section{Introduction}

Periodic completion asks for more than density: given a finite globally valid pattern, how large a period lattice is sufficient to realize it?  In multidimensional symbolic dynamics the answer can be nonexistent or noncomputable.  The uniform extension structure of unishifts changes the situation.  Salo introduced avoshifts and equal-extension-count systems in an algorithmic setting \citep{salo2024avoshifts}, then proved that every unishift on $\ZZ^d$ has dense totally periodic points \citep[Theorem~9]{salo2025iubs}.  The proof passes, up to a matrix in $\mathrm{GL}(d,\ZZ)$ and convex blocking, to an inductive uniform binondeterministic spacetime (IUBS), and at the periodic step chooses a sufficiently sparse lower-dimensional period lattice \citep[Lemma~25]{salo2025iubs}.

This note extracts an explicit two-dimensional completion bound once that presentation has been fixed.  Its elementary mechanism has two independent finite-state parts.  A radius-$R$ dependency cone expands the prescribed width from $W$ to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L}
 L=W+2R(H-1).
\end{equation}
The bottom word is a path in a balanced graph on $A$ and can be closed using at most $\card A-1$ additional symbols.  After the $H$ prescribed rows have been reconstructed on the resulting horizontal cylinder, they form a path in a balanced graph with at most $\card A^m$ vertices.  Closing this second path gives the vertical period.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
For the fixed presentation defined in \cref{sec:presentation}, we provide:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the explicit bounds
      $m\leq W+2R(H-1)+\card A-1$ and
      $T\leq H+\card A^m$, with a complete dependency-cone and balanced-digraph proof;
\item a finite construction with time and memory bounds in the presentation parameters;
\item an effective finite-quotient certificate for every nonidentity automorphism, quantitative in its block radius; and
\item controls showing that the horizontal additive constant can be attained, while balance and the coordinate firewall cannot be deleted.
\end{enumerate}

Nondeterministic cellular automata have an independent topological literature \citep{dilena2014}; here we use precisely the local Cartesian-product relation appearing in Salo's spacetime definition.  The qualitative implication from dense periodic points to residual finiteness of automorphism groups is classical in one-dimensional SFTs \citep{boyle1988}, was stated in a general group-subshift setting by Ceccherini-Silberstein and Coornaert \citep{ceccherini2012}, and is revisited sharply by Salo \citep{salo2026residual}.  Our automorphism statement does not claim that implication as new.  Its content is the presentation-local period and finite-set bound supplied by the completion theorem.

\paragraph{Scope and priority.}
The theorem is not a coordinate-free estimate for every unishift.  Salo's representation theorem changes basis and replaces symbols by convex blocks \citep[Theorem~1 and Lemma~27]{salo2025iubs}.  Those operations alter the alphabet, neighborhood radius, and rectangular geometry.  We therefore state all constants only after the presentation is fixed.  A bounded primary-source search found the qualitative IUBS argument but no printed instance of the two displayed bounds; this negative search is not a proof of priority.
