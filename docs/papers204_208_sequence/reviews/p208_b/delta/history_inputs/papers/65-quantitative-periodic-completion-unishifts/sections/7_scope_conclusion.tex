\section{Scope and conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The result is a quantitative lemma for the presentation $\Pi$, not an invariant of an abstractly conjugate subshift.  If an original unishift $U\subset B^{\ZZ^2}$ is represented using a basis matrix $M$, a convex blocking set $C$, and a blocked alphabet $A\subset B^C$, then at least three costs intervene:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ distorts an axis-aligned rectangle into a lattice parallelogram;
\item the $C$-blocking enlarges the support that must be protected; and
\item $a=\card A$ and the NCA radius $R$ may be much larger than the original alphabet and defining radius.
\end{enumerate}
Once $(M,C,\Pi)$ are specified, one may bound a transformed support by a presentation-coordinate rectangle and apply \cref{thm:completion}; translating the resulting periods back is then elementary linear algebra.  Without these data, printing \eqref{eq:m-bound} in the original coordinates would be unsupported.  This firewall is essential because Salo's structure theorem is explicitly only up to basis change and convex blocking \citep{salo2025iubs}.

Within that boundary, the progress is concrete.  A valid $W\times H$ rectangle needs only its radius-$R$ bottom dependency cone.  Balance closes that cone with the optimal worst-case alphabet additive term.  Uniform binondeterminism balances the finite graph of periodic rows and gives an explicit vertical state-count bound.  The same construction turns a local witness for a nonidentity automorphism into a bounded rectangular periodic orbit and hence a finite quotient that detects it.

Several refinements remain open.  Trace-specific estimates can replace $a$ by the size or directed diameter of the relevant component, and $a^m$ by $\card{Y_m}$ or a return-distance bound in $D_m$.  Obtaining useful subexponential vertical bounds requires dynamical information not present in the IUBS axioms.  Establishing a coordinate-free estimate would require quantitative control of the basis and blocking supplied by the unishift representation theorem.  None of these stronger statements is used here.
