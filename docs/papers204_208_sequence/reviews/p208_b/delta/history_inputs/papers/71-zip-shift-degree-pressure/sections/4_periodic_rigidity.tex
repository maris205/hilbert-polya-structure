\section{Weighted periodic data and profile rigidity}\label{sec:rigidity}

The same exponential sum in \eqref{eq:q} appears without a variational
argument when periodic points are weighted by their local degrees.

\begin{proposition}[Degree-weighted periodic identity]\label{prop:periodic}
For every $n\geq1$ and $t\in\R$,
\[
 \sum_{x\in\Fix(F_\tau^n)}
 \exp\!\left(t\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\phi_\tau(F_\tau^j x)\right)
 =Q_\tau(t)^n.
 \tag{4.1}\label{eq:periodic}
\]
In particular $|\Fix(F_\tau^n)|=|S|^n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each word $(s_0,\ldots,s_{n-1})\in S^n$ determines one fixed point of
$F_\tau^n$ by the unambiguous coordinate formula
\[
 x_i=s_{i\bmod n}\quad(i\geq0),
 \qquad
 x_{-j}=\tau(s_{(-j)\bmod n})\quad(j\geq1),
 \tag{4.2}\label{eq:periodic-coordinates}
\]
where residues lie in $\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$.  Every fixed point arises uniquely
in this way.  Its local degrees at times $0,\ldots,n-1$ are
$k_{\tau(s_{n-1})},k_{\tau(s_0)},\ldots,k_{\tau(s_{n-2})}$, a cyclic
permutation of the advertised list.  Summing the product of their $t$th
powers over $S^n$ factorizes as
$(\sum_s k_{\tau(s)}^t)^n=Q_\tau(t)^n$.
\end{proof}

The weighted orbit sums also give a closed zeta function.  For
$|u|<Q_\tau(t)^{-1}$, set
\[
 \zeta_\tau(t,u)=\exp\!\left(
 \sum_{n\geq1}\frac{u^n}{n}
 \sum_{x\in\Fix(F_\tau^n)}e^{tS_n\phi_\tau(x)}
 \right).
\]

\begin{corollary}[Degree-weighted zeta function]\label{cor:zeta}
For every real $t$,
\[
 \zeta_\tau(t,u)=\frac{1}{1-uQ_\tau(t)}.
 \tag{4.3}\label{eq:zeta}
\]
Its smallest positive pole is $e^{-P_\tau(t)}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Insert \eqref{eq:periodic} and use
$\sum_{n\geq1}(uQ)^n/n=-\log(1-uQ)$.
\end{proof}

We now compare two surjections $\tau:S\to Z$ and $\kappa:T\to W$.  A
topological conjugacy means a homeomorphism $H:X_\tau\to X_\kappa$ satisfying
$HF_\tau=F_\kappa H$.

\begin{theorem}[Profile, pressure, and conjugacy]\label{thm:rigidity}
The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $(X_\tau,F_\tau)$ and $(X_\kappa,F_\kappa)$ are topologically
conjugate.
\item The fibre-size multisets $\{k_z:z\in Z\}$ and
$\{|\kappa^{-1}(w)|:w\in W\}$ agree.
\item $P_\tau(t)=P_\kappa(t)$ for every $t\in\R$.
\end{enumerate}
Equal profiles admit a one-block conjugacy on the negative and nonnegative
coordinates separately.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Conjugacy preserves local degree pointwise because it bijects the preimage
sets of corresponding points.  It also bijects fixed points.  There is one
fixed point for each $s\in S$, and that point has degree $k_{\tau(s)}$.
Consequently the number of fixed points of degree $k$ is
\[
 N_k=k\,m_k.
 \tag{4.4}\label{eq:degree-hist}
\]
The conjugacy-invariant histogram $N_k$ recovers every multiplicity
$m_k=N_k/k$, proving $(1)\Rightarrow(2)$.

If the profiles agree, choose a bijection $\beta:Z\to W$ matching equal-size
fibres and, for each $z$, a bijection
$\alpha_z:\tau^{-1}(z)\to\kappa^{-1}(\beta z)$.  Put
$\alpha(s)=\alpha_{\tau(s)}(s)$ and define
\[
 H(x)_i=
 \begin{cases}
 \beta(x_i),&i<0,\\
 \alpha(x_i),&i\geq0.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{4.5}\label{eq:one-block-conj}
\]
The relation $\kappa\alpha=\beta\tau$ shows directly from
\eqref{eq:zip-map} that $HF_\tau=F_\kappa H$.  Coordinatewise inverse
bijections make $H$ a conjugacy, proving $(2)\Rightarrow(1)$.

Equal profiles give equal sums in \eqref{eq:q}, hence $(2)\Rightarrow(3)$.
Conversely suppose the pressure curves agree and set
\[
 R_\tau(u)=\exp P_\tau(u-1)=\sum_{k\geq1}m_k k^u.
 \tag{4.6}\label{eq:dirichlet}
\]
The largest fibre size is
$K=\lim_{u\to\infty}R_\tau(u)^{1/u}$, and its multiplicity is
$m_K=\lim_{u\to\infty}R_\tau(u)/K^u$.  Subtract $m_KK^u$ and repeat on the
finite remaining sum.  This recovers the whole profile from the curve and
proves $(3)\Rightarrow(2)$.
\end{proof}

The fixed-point argument already proves profile necessity.  The pressure
formulation is stronger as a thermodynamic representation: one convex
function simultaneously carries the profile, equilibrium degree averages,
and the spectrum proved next.
