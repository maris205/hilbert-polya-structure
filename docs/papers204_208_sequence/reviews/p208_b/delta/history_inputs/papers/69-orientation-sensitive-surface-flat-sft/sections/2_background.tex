\section{Conventions and surface formulas}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{The surface complex and its covers}

Let $N_3$ be represented by the standard one-vertex CW complex with three
oriented $1$-cells $x_1,x_2,x_3$ and one $2$-cell attached along
$x_1^2x_2^2x_3^2$.  Its fundamental group is
\eqref{eq:surface-presentation}.  The universal cover $\widetilde N_3$ has
vertices indexed by $\LambdaSurf$, with the positive $x_i$-edge starting at
$g$ and ending at $gx_i$.

For a subgroup $H\leq\LambdaSurf$, we use left cosets.  The corresponding
cover $H\backslash\widetilde N_3$ has vertex set $H\backslash\LambdaSurf$ and
an $x_i$-edge
\[
 Hg\longrightarrow Hgx_i.
\]
If $H$ has finite index $V$, the lifted CW structure has $V$ vertices, $3V$
edges, and $V$ faces.  In particular, its Euler characteristic is $-V$.

The orientation character is the epimorphism
$\omega\colon\LambdaSurf\to\ZZ/2\ZZ$ in \eqref{eq:f-and-omega}.  A connected
cover associated with $H$ is orientable if and only if
$H\leq\ker\omega$.  This criterion will be important because it is the same
finite-type shift, rather than two different systems, that is probed in both
topological directions.

\subsection{Shift convention}

For a finite alphabet $A$, the left shift of $\LambdaSurf$ on
$A^{\LambdaSurf}$ is
\[
 (h\cdot z)(g)=z(h^{-1}g).
\]
Therefore $z$ is fixed by $H$ exactly when it is constant on every left coset
$Hg$.  This convention matches the quotient cellulation above.  For a
$\LambdaSurf$-subshift $X$, write
\[
 \Fix_H(X)=\{z\in X:h\cdot z=z\text{ for all }h\in H\}.
\]

\subsection{Characters and Frobenius--Schur indicators}

Let $\K$ be a finite group and $\Irr(\K)$ its irreducible complex characters.
For $\chi\in\Irr(\K)$, set
\begin{equation}
 d_\chi=\chi(1),\qquad
 \nu_\chi=\frac1{|\K|}\sum_{g\in\K}\chi(g^2).
 \label{eq:fs-definition}
\end{equation}
The indicator $\nu_\chi$ is $+1$ for an irreducible admitting a nonzero
invariant symmetric bilinear form, $-1$ for an invariant skew-symmetric form,
and $0$ for a non-self-dual irreducible.  In particular,
$\nu_\chi\in\{-1,0,1\}$.  As our chosen modern normalization source, we use
Klug's account \cite[Corollary~1 and the closed-surface displays following
Theorem~3.1]{Klug2025}.  Snyder's lattice-TQFT argument gives a separate
topological and combinatorial derivation of both formulas \cite{Snyder2007}.
Historical ownership remains with Mednykh and Frobenius--Schur.

\begin{proposition}[Frobenius--Schur--Mednykh]
\label{prop:surface-hom}
Let $\Sigma_g$ be the closed orientable surface of genus $g$ and $N_\ell$ the
closed nonorientable surface of genus $\ell$.  Then
\begin{align}
 |\Hom(\pi_1(\Sigma_g),\K)|
 &=|\K|^{2g-1}\sum_{\chi\in\Irr(\K)}d_\chi^{2-2g},
 \label{eq:orientable-hom}\\
 |\Hom(\pi_1(N_\ell),\K)|
 &=|\K|^{\ell-1}\sum_{\chi\in\Irr(\K)}
      \nu_\chi^{\ell}d_\chi^{2-\ell}.
 \label{eq:nonorientable-hom}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

For completeness, the exponents can be read directly from the usual
presentations.  The orientable relation is a product of $g$ commutators; the
nonorientable relation is a product of $\ell$ squares.  Convolution of the
corresponding class functions diagonalizes in irreducible characters, giving
\eqref{eq:orientable-hom} and \eqref{eq:nonorientable-hom}.  We do not reprove
this character calculation, and we make no new claim about it.

The factors $\nu_\chi^\ell$ explain the orientation effect.  On orientable
surfaces, only the degree survives.  On nonorientable surfaces, a
non-self-dual character contributes zero, whereas an orthogonal or symplectic
character contributes with sign determined by the parity of $\ell$.
