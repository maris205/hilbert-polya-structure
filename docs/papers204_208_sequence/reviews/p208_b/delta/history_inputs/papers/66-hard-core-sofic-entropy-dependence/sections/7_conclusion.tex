\section{Conclusion}

The four-symbol hard-core shift turns approximation geometry into a transparent
weighted counting problem.  The safe symbol does two jobs: it makes the group
shift mixing, and it repairs locally bad finite models with vanishing entropy
cost.  Once this bridge is in place, a parity-compatible residual chain gives
many models on one side of a bipartition, whereas the uniform-permutation
model admits a strictly smaller annealed partition-function rate.  A
loop-aware degree bound keeps the latter entropy positive.

Thus the same natural nearest-neighbour SFT has two deterministic homomorphic
sofic approximations satisfying
\[
 0<\frac15\log4
 \le h_{\Sigma_{\unif}}(X_3)
 < h_{\Sigma_{\bip}}(X_3),
\]
with the stronger explicit interval in \Cref{thm:main}.  The result narrows a
known qualitative phenomenon to a small hard-core system; it does not replace
the existing priority theorem for positive approximation dependence, and it
does not yet compute either entropy exactly.
