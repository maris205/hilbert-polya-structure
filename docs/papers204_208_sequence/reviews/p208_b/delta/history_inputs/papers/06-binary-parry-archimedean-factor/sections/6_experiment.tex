The slow corner is reported rather than averaged away. At
$s=0.25+12i$, the last-cutoff Mellin relative error remains
$1.56\times10^4$. Negative moments emphasize the shrinking lattice points
near zero, while the high imaginary part causes severe oscillation. This is
consistent with pointwise and locally uniform convergence at fixed compact
sets but warns that the tested cutoff is far from asymptotic in the most
adversarial grid corner.

\subsection{Controls that prove too much}

Uniform $K_3$, uniform $K_4$, and a biased rank-one binary kernel all satisfy
$\tr K^r=1$. A reversible binary control with second eigenvalue $0.4$ instead
has $\tr K^r=1+0.4^r$ and fails immediately. Hence the Euler ledger selects
rank-one idempotence rather than the fair binary process.

After centering and variance normalization, the biased binary process and a
noncanonical scalar observable on $F_3$ also approach the one-dimensional
Gamma target. All nonconstant binary relabelings and rescalings become
identical after standardization. These are honest
\texttt{PROVES\_TOO\_MUCH} outcomes.

The canonical radial controls behave differently. At their final cutoffs,
the $K_3$ radial statistic has median error $1.5793$ against the binary target
but $0.00996$ against its native two-dimensional law. The corresponding
$K_4$ errors are $2.5506$ and $0.01009$. The dimension shift predicted by
\cref{prop:qcontrol} is therefore visible at finite cutoff.

An auxiliary Parry-inventory branch was also falsified. A pressure-normalized
Hellinger identity holds for shifted and random positive inventories, hence
proves too much; moreover, the identity-adjacency atom source of SD-C07 has no
native nontrivial transition probability. Adding full mixing would create the
mixed temporal cycles excluded by the source lock. This auxiliary branch is
not part of SD-C08.

The experiment therefore supports three exact conclusions: the stationary
binary fiber preserves the Euler trace ledger, the sign sector produces the
Gamma skeleton, and the frozen block-preserving chiral centering is
gauge-trivial. Theorem~\ref{thm:no-motion} strengthens the last numerical
certificate to every bounded $K$ in the same one-sided phase ansatz. The
experiment does not establish a global determinant or analytic continuation.
