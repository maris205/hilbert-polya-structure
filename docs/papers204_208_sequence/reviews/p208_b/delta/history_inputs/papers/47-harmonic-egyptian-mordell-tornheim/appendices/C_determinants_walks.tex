\section{Determinants and closed-walk typing}
\label{app:determinants}

We spell out the domain bookkeeping behind
\cref{cor:determinants}.  If $A\in\Schatten{2}$, the regularized product
$\det_2(I-zA)$ is entire in $z$.  On $|z|\|A\|<1$, expand the logarithm at
the identity.  Cancellation of the linear term gives
\[
 \log\det_2(I-zA)
 =-\sum_{r=2}^{\infty}\frac{z^r}{r}\Tr(A^r).
\]
If $A\in\Schatten{1}$, the uncancelled series begins at $r=1$, and
\[
 \det_2(I-zA)=\det(I-zA)e^{z\Tr A}.
\]
Applied to $A=E_s$, these facts give the exact permission table
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Domain & Legal object & First graph-specific trace term\\
\midrule
$\RePart s>\tfrac12$ & $\det_2(I-zE_s)$
  & $-z^2\Tr(E_s^2)/2$\\
$\RePart s>1$ & $\det(I-zE_s)$
  & $-z\Tr(E_s)$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The ordinary determinant is not used in the Hilbert--Schmidt-only strip.

For a finite compression $A_N=P_NE_sP_N$, multiplication gives
\begin{equation}
 \Tr(A_N^r)
 =\sum_{v_0,\ldots,v_{r-1}\leq N}
   \prod_{j=0}^{r-1}e_s(v_j,v_{j+1}),
 \qquad v_r=v_0.
 \label{eq:finite-closed-walks}
\end{equation}
Thus $z^r$ records $r$ traversed edges and the trace chooses a base point.
The division by $r$ in the logarithm is the cyclic-orbit normalization.
When a temporal orbit is a repetition, the usual stabilizer multiplicity
remains; none of this bookkeeping refers to $(a,b)=1$ on an individual
edge.

For $r=2$ in the Hilbert--Schmidt domain, the infinite counterpart of
\eqref{eq:finite-closed-walks} is absolutely summable and is precisely
\eqref{eq:ordered-edge-trace}.  We do not need to exchange an arbitrary
infinite collection of closed walks for a determinant product, and we make
no global logarithm claim across zeros of the determinant.
