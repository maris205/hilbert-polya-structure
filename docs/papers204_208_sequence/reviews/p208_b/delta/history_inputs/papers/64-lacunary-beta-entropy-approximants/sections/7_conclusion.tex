\section{Conclusion and scope}

For binary $\beta$-expansions whose zero gaps increase strictly, simple-Parry truncation has a sharp and certifiable entropy error.  The exact identity \eqref{eq:exact-tail-identity} converts the discarded tail into a nonlinear root displacement, and elementary exponential inequalities retain enough information to give a two-sided finite bound.  The leading term is the first omitted digit divided by the derivative mass $\beta D_\beta$.

The schedule theorem is intentionally asymmetric.  Increasing integer gaps impose rigidity on a logarithmic scale and exclude arbitrary exact schedules, while a recursive construction realizes any prescribed upper envelope.  We do not claim a classification of two-sided realizable schedules, uniform constants as $\beta\downarrow1$, or an extension to nonmonotone gaps and larger digits.  These boundaries are structural rather than presentational: removing them changes either the Parry comparison or the tail geometry used in the proof.
