\section{Clock dilution and first-return ownership}
\label{sec:clock-return}

The same exact source clock that gives the desired return weight forces a
large-edge obstruction.  The conclusion does not depend on uniform roofs.

\begin{theorem}[Whole recurrent operator is noncompact]
\label{thm:noncompact}
Suppose $\tau(e)\geq0$ on every edge and
$\sum_{e\in\Gamma_p}\tau(e)=\log p$.  For every $s$ with
$\sigma=\Ree s>0$, the primary recurrent adjacency $\Ls$ on
$\ell^2(\Vrec(\GW))$ is noncompact.  Consequently
$\Ls\notin\Schatten_q$ for every finite $q\geq1$, and it owns no ordinary
trace-class Fredholm determinant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose on each $\Gamma_p$ an edge $e_p$ of minimum roof.  Since the block has
$p-1$ edges,
\[
  \tau(e_p)\leq\frac{\log p}{p-1},\qquad
  |e^{-s\tau(e_p)}|
  \geq p^{-\sigma/(p-1)}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Let $\delta_p$ be the basis vector at the source of $e_p$.  The vectors
$\delta_p$ are orthonormal, hence weakly null, and their images lie in
mutually orthogonal prime blocks with norms that do not tend to zero.  A
compact operator must send a weakly null bounded sequence to a norm-null
sequence.  This contradiction proves noncompactness.  Every finite
Schatten-class operator is compact.
\end{proof}

The argument gives the exact design condition for the entire class of
vertex-disjoint cyclic successors.

\begin{corollary}[Disjoint-cycle length criterion]
\label{cor:length-criterion}
Consider finite directed cycles of lengths $\ell_n$ with nonnegative edge
roofs totaling $T_n$.  For fixed $\sigma>0$, there exists an allocation for
which the direct-sum weighted successor is compact if and only if
\[
  \frac{T_n}{\ell_n}\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus a prime-indexed disjoint successor with $T_p=\log p$ requires
$\ell(p)=o(\log p)$; an $O(\log p)$ bound is insufficient.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For necessity, compactness forces the largest edge modulus in the $n$th block
to tend to zero.  This modulus is determined by a minimum-roof edge, so its
roof tends to infinity; the average roof $T_n/\ell_n$ is at least that
minimum and also tends to infinity.  Conversely, allocate the roof uniformly.
Every block norm is then $e^{-\sigma T_n/\ell_n}\to0$.  A direct sum of
finite-dimensional operators whose norms tend to zero is compact.
\end{proof}

Uniform roofs make the spectral accumulation explicit.

\begin{corollary}[Unit-circle essential approximate spectrum]
\label{cor:essential-circle}
For the uniform Wilson allocation and every $s$ with $\Ree s>0$, every point
of the unit circle admits an orthonormal approximate-eigenvector sequence for
$\Ls$.  In particular, the unit circle lies in the essential approximate
point spectrum.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The eigenvalues in the $p$-block are
\[
  \lambda_{p,j}=p^{-s/(p-1)}
  \exp\!\left(\frac{2\pi i j}{p-1}\right),
  \qquad0\leq j<p-1.
\]
Their radii tend to one, their additional phase from $\Im s$ tends to zero,
and their angular mesh tends to zero.  Given $|\lambda|=1$, choose eigenvalues
from distinct prime blocks converging to $\lambda$.  The corresponding
normalized block eigenvectors are orthogonal and satisfy
$\|(\Ls-\lambda)u_p\|\to0$.
\end{proof}

This circle is a noncompactness witness, not a Hilbert--P\'olya spectrum.  It
has no asserted relation to the nontrivial zeros of $\zeta$.

\subsection{First return: an honest determinant of another clock}

Choose one base vertex $b_p$ on each prime cycle and induce to the base set.
The first-return operator acts on $\ell^2(\{b_p\})$.

\begin{theorem}[Return determinant and marker change]
\label{thm:first-return}
The induced operator is diagonal,
\[
  \Rs b_p=p^{-s}b_p.
\]
It is trace class exactly when $\Ree s>1$, and in that half-plane
\[
  \det(I-z\Rs)=\prod_p(1-zp^{-s}).
\]
The raw and induced factors are respectively
$1-z^{p-1}p^{-s}$ and $1-zp^{-s}$.  Hence the induced determinant does not
preserve the free graph-step marker.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
One first return multiplies every edge weight around $\Gamma_p$, giving
$e^{-s\log p}=p^{-s}$.  Its trace norm is $\sum_p p^{-\sigma}$, finite
exactly for $\sigma>1$.  The standard diagonal Fredholm product follows in
that region.  A return traverses $p-1$ original edges but is one induced step,
which proves the marker statement.
\end{proof}

At $z=1$ the products agree in the Euler half-plane.  That equality forgets
the coordinate which distinguishes the two time evolutions.  State expansion
and return-time changes are familiar in symbolic dynamics
\citep{ParrySullivan1975}; here the free marker turns the distinction into an
exact finite certificate, while trace-class theory fixes which object owns
the ordinary determinant \citep{Simon1977}.
