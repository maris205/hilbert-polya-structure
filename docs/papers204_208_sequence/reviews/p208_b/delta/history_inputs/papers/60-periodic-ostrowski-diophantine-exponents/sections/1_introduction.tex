\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Diophantine exponent of an infinite word records how efficiently
arbitrarily long prefixes can be built from a short initial part followed by a
fractional power.  It arose in the study of low-complexity digital expansions
and Diophantine approximation \cite{AdamczewskiBugeaud2007}.  For Sturmian
words, the repetition function gives a particularly effective interface with
this exponent \cite{BugeaudKim2019}.  Standard background on Sturmian words and
Ostrowski numeration may be found in \cite{Lothaire2002,AlloucheShallit2003}.

Wojcik's v1 preprint introduces formal intercepts and gives a complete
eight-case description of the repetition function of
$T^\rho(c_\alpha)$ \cite{Wojcik2025v1}.  The same source extracts four
quotients when the Ostrowski digits are eventually strictly inside their
allowed ranges.  Boundary tails are different: the digits $0$, $a_i-1$, and
$a_i$ activate several other rows, and substituting them into the four
interior expressions can create a zero or subscale denominator.  This note
asks whether an ultimately periodic boundary tail nevertheless gives a finite,
exact computation.

The result below uses the source's complete finite table as an input, with one
necessary correction.  In case~7 of Theorem~2.2.1 of the cited v1 source,
every occurrence of $\rho_{n-1}$ must be replaced by $\rho_n$.  The change is
not a silent normalization: \cref{sec:case-seven} derives it from the source's
preceding corollary and carry rule, explains an endpoint clipping, and gives a
finite instance in which the printed row violates $r(x,m)\leq m+1$.

The division of ownership is sharp.  Wojcik v1 owns the formal intercept
formalism, the repetition-function engine, the eight local cases, and their
connection with the Diophantine exponent.  The contribution here is the
documented case-7 correction and the effective periodic reduction of the full
boundary table: a three-scale exact calculation, a maximum of at most $4L$
quadratic-field elements, and certified algebraic output.  We do not claim a
new general repetition formula, a nonperiodic extension, or a runtime
complexity estimate.

Our answer is the following.  The terms ``legal'', ``formal intercept'', and
the matrix $P$ are defined in \cref{sec:repetition,sec:periodic}.

\Needspace{21\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}[Main theorem]
\label{thm:main}
Let
\[
  \alpha=[0;a_1,a_2,\ldots]
\]
be quadratic, and let $(b_i)_{i\geq1}$ be a legal ultimately periodic
Ostrowski digit sequence defining the formal intercept $\rho$ of the Sturmian
word $x=T^\rho(c_\alpha)$.  Then $\dio(x)$ is an effectively computable real
algebraic number of degree at most two.

More precisely, let $L$ be any common phase-aligned period of the tails of
$(a_i)$ and $(b_i)$, and let $\Lam>1$ be the Perron root of the associated
$2\times2$ continuant period matrix $P$.  Then
\[
  \dio(x)=1+\max \mathcal C,
  \qquad
  \mathcal C\subseteq\Q(\Lam),
  \qquad
  |\mathcal C|\leq4L,
\]
where $\mathcal C$ is explicitly constructible.  Empty plateau rows are
discarded by a terminating exact eventual-sign test.  There is a terminating
algorithm that validates the finite input, returns a primitive minimal
polynomial and a rational isolating interval for $\dio(x)$, and decides
equality and order for two such outputs.  No eventual-interior hypothesis is
imposed on the digits.
\end{theorem}

\Cref{sec:repetition} fixes notation and displays the corrected eight-case
interface.  \Cref{sec:case-seven} proves the correction.  The periodic matrix
and finite maximum are established in \cref{sec:periodic};
\cref{sec:algorithm} turns them into an exact terminating algorithm.
\Cref{sec:examples} gives two exact boundary-sensitive checks and records the
scope and release gate.
