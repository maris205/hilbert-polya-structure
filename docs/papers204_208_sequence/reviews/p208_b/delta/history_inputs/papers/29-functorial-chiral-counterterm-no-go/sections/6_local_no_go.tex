\section{Generic mixed-Gram residues and the local no-go}
\label{sec:no-go}

The mixed series is analytic surplus rather than analytic debt: it already
converges absolutely.  A diagonal subtraction cannot change it.

\begin{theorem}[Diagonal invariance]
\label{thm:diagonal-invariance}
Let $C_F^{\mathrm{diag}}$ be any diagonal quadratic counterterm.  For real
$t_1,t_2$,
\begin{equation}
 (Q_F-C_F^{\mathrm{diag}})(t_1)
 -(Q_F-C_F^{\mathrm{diag}})(t_2)
 =M_F(t_1)-M_F(t_2).
 \label{eq:diagonal-invariance}
\end{equation}
In particular, a diagonal scheme cannot make a nonconstant control ledger
constant or zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A diagonal term has no unordered-pair phase and is independent of the mixed
frequencies.  It cancels from the difference.
\end{proof}

The obstruction is already visible on two atoms.  For distinct positive
marks and $G_{xy}\ne0$, the mixed ledger contains
\begin{equation}
 \frac{4G_{xy}}{\sqrt{\nu(x)\nu(y)}}
 \cos\!\left(t\log\frac{\nu(y)}{\nu(x)}\right).
 \label{eq:two-atom-frequency}
\end{equation}
Nothing in \eqref{eq:two-atom-frequency} requires divisibility.  It is a
weighted overlap of two oblique source idempotents.

One might use a mixed counterterm to remove controls.  Because such a term is
not required for convergence, it must be justified by selectivity.  We test
the minimal class in which that justification could be local.

\begin{theorem}[Pair-local linear-Gram selectivity no-go]
\label{thm:pair-local-no-go}
Assume a counterterm is
\begin{enumerate}
\item quadratic in the active coefficient vector;
\item additive over diagonal atoms and unordered atom pairs;
\item local to the pointed induced one-/two-atom datum;
\item linear in the native Gram contraction on each pair;
\item invariant under transported relabeling and forbidden to branch on
printed numerical atom names.
\end{enumerate}
Then no such rule can preserve the divisibility mixed mechanism while
cancelling every matched nonzero mutated, composite-only, generic-DAG, and
random-inventory control mechanism.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On one local pair type, write the mixed counterterm as
$\beta G_{xy}$ times the native phase factor.  The residual multiplier is
$1-\beta$.  Exact preservation of a nonzero baseline pair forces
$1-\beta=1$, hence $\beta=0$.  Exact cancellation of a nonzero control pair
with the same transported local datum forces $1-\beta=0$, hence $\beta=1$.
Local naturality applies the same $\beta$ to both.  The requirements are
incompatible.
\end{proof}

The theorem is not a statistical claim.  The exact suite enumerates the
preregistered coefficient grid
\begin{equation}
 \{-2,-1,-\tfrac12,0,\tfrac12,1,2\}^2
 \label{eq:coefficient-grid}
\end{equation}
and finds zero selective solutions among 49 combinations.  The symbolic
$\beta=0$ versus $\beta=1$ contradiction proves the result beyond that grid.

\subsection{The open nonlocal case}

Bare naturality does not force a counterterm to be pair-local.  A global
coefficient may inspect an isomorphism invariant of the complete filtered
tower.  Formally, it may even multiply $M_P$ by the indicator of the tower
isomorphism class of standard divisibility.  This construction is label-blind
after the class has been hard-coded, yet it merely repackages the complete
inventory as an oracle.

We therefore distinguish three statements:
\begin{itemize}
\item diagonal-only selectivity is refuted by
\cref{thm:diagonal-invariance};
\item quadratic additive pair-local linear-Gram selectivity is refuted by
\cref{thm:pair-local-no-go};
\item the existence of a nonlocal, independently motivated,
cutoff-compatible divisibility-coherence functional is open.
\end{itemize}
Excluding the third class requires an axiom such as locality, descent,
continuity, or controlled additivity that is motivated before the control
outcomes.
