\begin{abstract}
Burban and Drozd explicitly record $3x^2-2x^3$ in a known
idempotent-lifting construction with quadratic error improvement; the cubic
and that mechanism receive no contribution credit here.  We instead determine
its complete temporal and one-step inverse atlas on
$\mathbb Z/2^n\mathbb Z$.  A
parity-selected endpoint error has a valuation that doubles at every epoch,
which gives every pointwise entry time, every temporal shell, the sharp
height $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$, and the two recurrent points.  The inverse
problem retains information discarded by this clock.  In output valuation
$2v$, its normalized odd unit lies in one prescribed class modulo eight,
with different classes for $v=1$ and $v\geq2$.  An exact branchwise bit lift
then gives every target fibre, including the separate quotient boundaries
$N=n-2v=1,2$, and yields a closed image-size formula.  A deterministic audit
checks every state and target through $n=17$ and the normalized-unit lanes
$1\leq v\leq6$, $1\leq N\leq11$, totaling $2{,}563{,}880$ exact assertions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Prior boundary and the scoped-atlas theorem}\label{sec:theorem}

Burban and Drozd's Lemma~A.4 recalls a known result, with a pointer to
classical background, giving integer polynomials that improve an approximate
idempotent modulo successive powers of an ideal; it explicitly lists
\[
 G_1(x)=3x^2-2x^3
\]
as an example~\cite{BurbanDrozd2004}.  We use this as a direct prior record,
not as an origination claim.  Thus neither the literal cubic, its
idempotent-lifting role, nor quadratic error improvement is new here.  We ask
the narrower finite question: what are all entry-time shells, image classes,
and one-step fibres of this fixed map modulo $2^n$?

For $n\geq1$, write
\[
 F_n:\Zn\longrightarrow\Zn,
 \qquad F_n(x)=3x^2-2x^3.
\]
Select the endpoint error by parity,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error}
 e(x)=\begin{cases}x,&x\equiv0\pmod2,\\1-x,&x\equiv1\pmod2,\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and use the truncated convention $\vtwo(0\bmod 2^n)=n$.  Let
$\tau_n(x)$ be the first entry time into $\{0,1\}$.

\begin{theorem}[temporal and one-step inverse atlas]\label{thm:atlas}
For every $n\geq1$, the following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}[(A)]
\item For all $x\in\Zn$ and $t\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:valuation-clock}
 \vtwo(e(F_n^t(x)))=\min\{n,2^t\vtwo(e(x))\}.
\end{equation}
The reached endpoint is $x\bmod2$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entry-time}
 \tau_n(x)=\min\{t\geq0:2^t\vtwo(e(x))\geq n\}.
\end{equation}
For $t\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cdf}
 A_{n,t}:=\#\{x:\tau_n(x)\leq t\}
 =2^{\,n-\lceil n/2^t\rceil+1}.
\end{equation}
Consequently the exact shell polynomial is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shell-polynomial}
 D_n(z)=A_{n,0}+\sum_{t=1}^{M_n}(A_{n,t}-A_{n,t-1})z^t,
 \qquad M_n=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil,
\end{equation}
and $0,1$ are the only recurrent states.

\item Let $y\notin\{0,1\}$ and reflect it into the even basin by
\[
 y^\flat=\begin{cases}y,&y\equiv0\pmod2,\\1-y,&y\equiv1\pmod2.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then $y\in\im F_n$ if and only if
$\vtwo(y^\flat)=2v<n$ for an integer $v\geq1$ and, on putting
$N=n-2v$ and $u=y^\flat/2^{2v}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:image-condition}
 u\equiv
 \begin{cases}
 1\pmod2,&N=1,\\
 3\pmod4,&N=2,\\
 7\pmod8,&N\geq3\text{ and }v=1,\\
 3\pmod8,&N\geq3\text{ and }v\geq2.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Every admissible target in this stratum has exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre}
 \#F_n^{-1}(y)=2^{\,v+\min(N-1,2)}
 =2^{\,v+\min(n-2v-1,2)}
\end{equation}
preimages; every inadmissible nonendpoint target has none.  The endpoint
fibres and total image size are
\begin{align}
 \#F_n^{-1}(0)=\#F_n^{-1}(1)&=2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor},
 \label{eq:endpoint-fibres}\\
 \#\im F_n&=2+2\sum_{1\leq v<n/2}
 2^{\max(0,n-2v-3)}.\label{eq:image-size}
\end{align}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The two axes in Theorem~\ref{thm:atlas} are not interchangeable.  Part (A)
keeps only the error valuation.  Part (B) needs the normalized odd unit and
even distinguishes the first positive valuation stratum from all later
ones.  Table~\ref{tab:subtraction} records the claim boundary.

\section{Exact error squaring and the temporal census}\label{sec:time}

The forward law follows from two identities, but we print the count so that
the clock convention and the $n=1$ boundary are explicit.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:atlas}(A)]
Direct expansion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factorizations}
 F_n(x)=x^2(3-2x),\qquad
 1-F_n(x)=(1-x)^2(1+2x),\qquad
 F_n(1-x)=1-F_n(x).
\end{equation}
If $x$ is even, then $3-2x$ is odd.  If $x$ is odd, then $1-x$ is even
and $1+2x$ is odd.  Parity is preserved, and hence
\[
 \vtwo(e(F_n(x)))=\min\{n,2\vtwo(e(x))\}.
\]
Induction proves \eqref{eq:valuation-clock}; the first value $n$ is reached
at the time in \eqref{eq:entry-time}.

Put $a_t=\lceil n/2^t\rceil$.  Entry by time $t$ is equivalent to
$2^{a_t}\mid e(x)$.  The even basin contains $2^{n-a_t}$ such residues,
and reflection $x\mapsto1-x$ gives the same count in the disjoint odd basin.
This proves \eqref{eq:cdf}.  Consecutive differences give
\eqref{eq:shell-polynomial}.  Every nonendpoint selected error has valuation
at least one, so $2^t\geq n$ suffices for absorption; errors of valuation one
show that the sharp maximum is $M_n$.  All states reach $0$ or $1$, and both
are fixed, proving the recurrence assertion.  When $n=1$, every state is
already an endpoint, $M_1=0$, and the same formulas give $A_{1,0}=2$.
\end{proof}

This section is an exact finite specialization of the zero-credit quadratic
improvement mechanism; it supplies context for the inverse atlas rather than
a claim to a new lifting iteration.

\section{The normalized-unit lifting law}\label{sec:lifting}

For a nonzero even source write $x=2^v w$, where $v\geq1$ and $w$ is odd.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hv-factor}
 F_n(x)=2^{2v}h_v(w),\qquad
 h_v(w)=w^2(3-2^{v+1}w).
\end{equation}
The odd factor $h_v(w)$ retains the information that the temporal valuation
forgets.

\begin{lemma}[branchwise bit lifting]\label{lem:unit}
Fix $v\geq1$ and let $h_v$ act on the odd residues modulo $2^N$.
For $N=1$, its image is the unique odd class and that target has one
preimage.  For $N=2$, its image is $3\pmod4$ and that target has two
preimages.  For $N\geq3$, the image is $7\pmod8$ if $v=1$ and
$3\pmod8$ if $v\geq2$; every target in the displayed class has four
preimages.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For odd $w$, $w^2\equiv1\pmod8$.  If $v=1$, the second factor in
\eqref{eq:hv-factor} is $3-4w\equiv7\pmod8$; if $v\geq2$, the cubic
term is divisible by eight and $h_v(w)\equiv3\pmod8$.  Reduction gives
the claimed images and direct fibre counts for $N=1,2$.

Suppose $N\geq3$.  Split the odd inputs as $w=r+4z$ with
$r\in\{1,3\}$ and define the integer-valued function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi}
 \Phi_{v,r}(z)=\frac{h_v(r+4z)-h_v(r)}8.
\end{equation}
We prove that $\Phi_{v,r}$ is a permutation modulo $2^k$ for every $k\geq0$.
For odd $w$,
\begin{align*}
 h_v'(w)&=6w(1-2^vw),\\
 h_v''(w)&=6-6\cdot2^{v+1}w,\\
 h_v'''(w)&=-6\cdot2^{v+1}.
\end{align*}
Thus $\vtwo(h_v'(w))=1$, $\vtwo(h_v''(w))=1$, and
$\vtwo(h_v'''(w))\geq3$.  Set $\delta=4\cdot2^j$.  The exact cubic
Taylor identity, divided by eight, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:taylor}
 \frac{h_v(w+\delta)-h_v(w)}8
 =\frac{\delta h_v'(w)}8
  +\frac{\delta^2h_v''(w)}{16}
  +\frac{\delta^3h_v'''(w)}{48}.
\end{equation}
The first term is $2^j$ times an odd integer.  The last two terms have
valuations at least $2j+1$ and $3j+5$, respectively.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bit-toggle}
 \Phi_{v,r}(z+2^j)-\Phi_{v,r}(z)
 \equiv2^j\pmod {2^{j+1}}.
\end{equation}
Starting with the sole residue modulo one, \eqref{eq:bit-toggle} says that
the two lifts of every input class hit the two lifts of its output class.
Induction proves the permutation assertion.

Take $k=N-3$.  Within either $r$-branch, $z$ ranges modulo $2^{N-2}$,
whereas the output beyond its fixed three low bits depends on
$\Phi_{v,r}(z)$ modulo $2^{N-3}$.  Each branch therefore covers the required
output class twice; the two branches together give four preimages.  This
proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

The separate statements for $N=1$ and $N=2$ are essential: the four-to-one
law begins only at $N=3$.

\section{Every-target fibres and image size}\label{sec:fibres}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:atlas}(B)]
Equation~\eqref{eq:hv-factor} shows that a nonzero even output has valuation
exactly $2v<n$.  Conversely, if $2v\geq n$, its output is zero.  For
$2v<n$, put $N=n-2v$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:unit} gives exactly the normalized
unit conditions in \eqref{eq:image-condition}.

The source unit $w$ is specified modulo $2^{n-v}$, but $h_v(w)$ modulo
$2^N$ depends only on $w$ modulo $2^N$.  Reduction therefore contributes
\[
 2^{(n-v)-N}=2^v
\]
high lifts.  Lemma~\ref{lem:unit} contributes
$2^{\min(N-1,2)}$ reduced solutions.  Their product proves
\eqref{eq:fibre}, including $N=1,2$.

The zero fibre consists precisely of residues divisible by
$2^{\lceil n/2\rceil}$, and so has $2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ elements.
The reflection in \eqref{eq:factorizations} transfers this entire even-side
description to the odd basin and gives the same fibre over one.

Finally, a fixed class modulo eight contains $2^{N-3}$ odd units when
$N\geq3$, while the $N=1,2$ image has one unit.  Thus a nonzero stratum in
one parity basin has $2^{\max(0,N-3)}$ targets.  Summing over
$1\leq v<n/2$, doubling by reflection, and adding the endpoints proves
\eqref{eq:image-size}.
\end{proof}

For orientation, the fibre spectrum is nonuniform even when all targets are
viewed at the same modulus.  At $n=17$, for example, the exact image contains
$10{,}926$ targets and the positive fibre sizes are
$8,16,32,64,128,256,512$.  This numerical row illustrates the theorem; it is
not used in its proof.

\section{Exact controls and scope}\label{sec:controls}

The paper-local verifier uses only Python integers.  It exhausts every state,
orbit, target fibre, and image through $n=17$.  Independently, it exhausts
the normalized odd-unit maps for $1\leq v\leq6$ and $1\leq N\leq11$,
including the one- and two-bit boundaries.  The frozen transcript contains
$2{,}563{,}880$ exact assertions and has SHA-256
\begin{center}
\small\texttt{f5f1884f809110ca8ec3a954af1783c774896708495d626f694bbfb23f7876f1}.

Enumeration supplies counterexample pressure only; Sections~\ref{sec:time}--
\ref{sec:fibres} prove the all-$n$ statements.

The result is confined to the single map $3x^2-2x^3$ on
$\mathbb Z/2^n\mathbb Z$.  It asserts neither an odd-prime analogue nor a
general finite-ring theorem.  The bounded source screen did not locate the
normalized-unit one-step inverse atlas, but that non-hit is not a novelty,
priority, ownership-completeness, or release certificate.  An earlier source
for the residual formulas would require further subtraction.
