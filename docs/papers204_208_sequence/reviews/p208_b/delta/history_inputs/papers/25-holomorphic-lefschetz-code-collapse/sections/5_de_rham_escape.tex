\section{The canonical de Rham escape}
\label{sec:escape}

The odd sector suggested by \cref{thm:tensor-rigidity} is already present in
the source geometry.  Pullback on one-forms contributes exactly one
derivative factor at every step.

\begin{theorem}[Local all-order Lefschetz cancellation]
\label{thm:local-cancellation}
For an affine branch \(\phi(z)=a+qz\), set
\[
        W_0=wU_{\phi,0},\qquad W_1=wU_{\phi,1}.
\]
Then for every \(r\ge1\),
\begin{equation}
        \Tr W_0^r-\Tr W_1^r=w^r.
 \label{eq:local-supertrace}
\end{equation}
The degreewise Fredholm determinants obey
\begin{equation}
 \det(I-zW_0)=(1-zw)\det(I-zW_1).
 \label{eq:local-det-identity}
\end{equation}
Consequently the graded ratio equals \(1-zw\) wherever it is defined.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since \(U_{\phi,1}=qU_{\phi,0}\), the one-form repetition trace is
\[
        \Tr W_1^r=\frac{w^rq^r}{1-q^r}.
\]
Subtracting it from \eqref{eq:zero-trace} proves
\eqref{eq:local-supertrace}.  Substitute the full power identity into the
two trace logarithms.  The resulting equality holds near \(z=0\), and both
sides of \eqref{eq:local-det-identity} are entire, so the identity theorem
extends it to every \(z\).
\end{proof}

The numerator is the one-dimensional exterior character
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{tr}\Lambda^0(q^r)-
 \operatorname{tr}\Lambda^1(q^r)=1-q^r.
 \label{eq:exterior-character}
\end{equation}
It depends on the repeated derivative \(q^r\), unlike a hand-assigned odd
scalar whose sign or value would be raised incorrectly under repetition.

\subsection{Exact polynomial certificate}

Write \(\phi(z)=a+qz\).  On the monomial bases of \(P_N\) and
\(P_{N-1}dz\), the pullback matrices are
\begin{align}
 (M_0)_{j,k}
 &=\binom{k}{j}a^{k-j}q^j,
 &&0\le j\le k\le N,
 \label{eq:M0}\\
 (M_1)_{j,k}
 &=q\binom{k}{j}a^{k-j}q^j,
 &&0\le j\le k\le N-1.
 \label{eq:M1}
\end{align}
If \(D_{k-1,k}=k\) is the differentiation matrix, direct binomial algebra
gives
\[
        DM_0=M_1D.
\]
The sequence \eqref{eq:polynomial-complex} is exact.  Constants carry the
scalar \(w\), while differentiation identifies the nonconstant zero-form
quotient with the one-form space.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \det(I-zwM_0)=(1-zw)\det(I-zwM_1)
 \label{eq:finite-characteristic}
\end{equation}
as a polynomial identity for every \(N\).  This is a full characteristic-
polynomial certificate, not a finite list of trace checks.

\subsection{Spectral telescoping}

Centering the fixed point makes the two ordinary spectra transparent:
\[
 \spec(W_0)=\{wq^m:m\ge0\},\qquad
 \spec(W_1)=\{wq^{m+1}:m\ge0\}.
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\det(I-zW_0)}{\det(I-zW_1)}
 =\frac{\prod_{m\ge0}(1-zwq^m)}
        {\prod_{m\ge0}(1-zwq^{m+1})}
 =1-zw.
 \label{eq:spectral-telescope}
\end{equation}
All positive-degree modes pair.  The constant zero-form is the sole
cohomological survivor.

\begin{proposition}[Ordinary/graded firewall]
\label{prop:ordinary-graded}
The identity \eqref{eq:local-det-identity} concerns the graded quotient.
The ordinary determinant on \(\cH^0\oplus\cH^1\) is
\[
 \det(I-z(W_0\oplus W_1))
 =\det(I-zW_0)\det(I-zW_1),
\]
and is not \(1-zw\) for \(0<|q|<1\) and \(w\ne0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fredholm determinants multiply under direct sums.  The product retains both
degree spectra, while \eqref{eq:spectral-telescope} cancels their paired
factors by division.
\end{proof}

The escape is therefore genuine but typed.  Each parity sector has a valid
trace-class operator; their ratio supplies the desired local factor.  The
next question is not local analytic legitimacy but what survives when many
code returns share one recurrent object.
