\section{Expanded proofs and exact certificate conventions}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Path-length mixing at a fixed base vertex}

The two cycles used in \cref{thm:mixing} are genuinely based at the same
vertex:
\[
 3\to2\to3
\]
has length two, while
\[
 3\to4\to5\to3
\]
has length three because \(3\mid6\).  If fixed paths from \(u\) to \(3\) and
from \(3\) to \(v\) have lengths \(a\) and \(b\), then every path length of
the form
\[
 a+2x+3y+b,\qquad x,y\ge0,
\]
is realized.  Since the numerical semigroup generated by \(2,3\) contains
every integer except \(1\), every length at least \(a+b+2\) is realized.
This is the stated path-sense mixing, with no compactness assumption on the
countable alphabet.

\subsection{Finite trace formula}

For a fixed \(r\), each source has finite outdegree, so every diagonal matrix
element
\[
 \langle e_n,L_s^re_n\rangle
\]
is a finite sum over rooted closed walks based at \(n\).  By
\cref{thm:confinement}, it vanishes for \(n>2r-1\).  Therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:finite-trace-app}
 \sum_{n\ge2}\langle e_n,L_s^re_n\rangle
 =
 \sum_{n=2}^{2r-1}\langle e_n,L_s^re_n\rangle
\end{equation}
is a finite exponential polynomial in \(s\) for every \(s\in\C\).
When \(\RePart s>1/2\), \(L_s^r\) is trace class and its trace equals the
basis-diagonal sum, so \cref{eq:finite-trace-app} is the operator trace.

This separates two assertions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item the fixed-order closed-walk expression is a finite entire function of
  \(s\);
  \item the Fredholm determinant formed from the whole operator is proved only
  where \(L_s\in\Sone\).
\end{itemize}

Finite coefficients alone do not continue the whole determinant through the
trace-class boundary.

\subsection{Primitive recurrence checks}

The unweighted recurrence
\[
 T_r=\sum_{d\mid r}dP_d
\]
keeps primitive rotations and temporal powers separate.  For example,
\[
 T_4=10=2P_2+4P_4=2+8,
\]
so \(P_4=2\).  Likewise,
\[
 T_6=29=2P_2+3P_3+6P_6=2+3+24,
\]
so \(P_6=4\).  Counting a repeated length-two orbit as four unrelated rooted
objects would violate both identities.

In the weighted setting, a primitive class \(\gamma\) of length \(d\mid r\)
contributes
\[
 d\,N(\gamma)^{-2sr/d}
\]
to \(\Tr L_s^r\): it has \(d\) rooted representatives and is traversed
\(r/d\) times.  Hence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weighted-necklace}
 \Tr L_s^r
 =
 \sum_{d\mid r}d
 \sum_{\substack{[\gamma]\ {\rm primitive}\\\ell(\gamma)=d}}
 N(\gamma)^{-2sr/d}.
\end{equation}
Substitution of \cref{eq:weighted-necklace} into \cref{eq:logdet} yields the
primitive product in \cref{thm:fredholm}.

\end{table}

For length \(r\le4\), \cref{thm:confinement} restricts the maximum to
\(2r-1\).  Choose a maximum \(M\) and its proper-divisor successor \(d\).
The remaining edges can increase by at most one.  At \(r=2\), the inequality
forces \(M=3,d=2\).  At \(r=3\), the primitive case forces
\(M=5,d=3\); the only other closed words would be powers of a length-one
loop, which does not exist.  At \(r=4\), the primitive maxima are \(5\) and
\(7\), yielding the two rows in \cref{tab:low-classes}; the remaining rooted
terms are the two rotations of the double traversal of \((2,3)\).  This
produces
\[
 T_1,T_2,T_3,T_4=0,2,3,10
\]
and \cref{prop:first-traces}.

\subsection{Trace-norm holomorphy in detail}

For \(m\ge0\), the \(m\)-th \(s\)-derivative of a row coefficient is
\[
 (-\log[d(kd-1)])^m[d(kd-1)]^{-s}.
\]
Fix \(\sigma_0>1/2\) and choose
\(\varepsilon\in(0,\sigma_0-1/2)\).  The bound
\[
 \log^m x\le C_{m,\varepsilon}x^\varepsilon,\qquad x\ge2,
\]
shows that the derivative row norm is bounded, up to a constant depending on
\(m,\varepsilon,\sigma_0\), by
\[
 d^{-2(\sigma_0-\varepsilon)}.
\]
The outer sum converges.  Uniform trace-norm convergence on compact subsets
justifies termwise differentiation of the row series and proves
\cref{prop:holomorphy}.  Standard local Lipschitz bounds for the Fredholm
determinant then give holomorphy of \(D_{\SD}\) in \(s\).

\subsection{Exact sparse certificate protocol}

For each desired order \(r\):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item construct only the induced sparse graph on
  \(2,\ldots,2r-1\);
  \item propagate integer path counts for \(r\) steps and sum the diagonal;
  \item recompute at larger cutoffs as a regression, while labeling
  \(2r-1\) as the theorem-certified cutoff;
  \item recover \(P_r\) by Möbius inversion and check the forward recurrence;
  \item require exactly \(r\) rooted walks using the top vertex;
  \item for integer \(s\), repeat with rational endpoint weights and compare
  determinant coefficients from \cref{eq:newton} with multiplication of
  primitive factors.
\end{enumerate}

Sparse dynamic propagation is polynomial in the finite edge inventory for a
fixed order.  Cartesian enumeration of all \(N^r\) vertex words is neither
needed nor authorized by the certificate design.

\subsection{Endpoint and near-boundary diagnostics}

Finite nuclear prefixes at \(\sigma=0.51\) converge very slowly because the
outer comparison series is close to \(\sum d^{-1.02}\).  Their initial
growth cannot falsify \cref{thm:traceclass}.  Conversely, apparent numerical
stability at \(\sigma=0.50\) cannot prove convergence.  The row
decomposition and Fourier extraction, not a finite plot, decide the exact
threshold.
