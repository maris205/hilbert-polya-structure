\title[Random push--pop stack cocycles]{Running-Maximum Normal Forms for Random Push--Pop Stack Cocycles:\\
Image Contraction, Fibre Growth, and Two Exact Thresholds}
\author{Anonymous}

\keywords{random dynamical system, one-sided full shift, bicyclic monoid, reflected random walk, synchronization, annealed exponent}

\begin{abstract}
Let $X=\A^{\N_0}$, where $|\A|=b\geq2$.  At each time, independently
apply the left shift $D$ with probability $p$, or prefix a uniformly chosen
letter with probability $1-p$.  If $S_n$ counts shifts minus prefixes and
$M_n=\max_{0\leq k\leq n}S_k$, every length-$n$ cocycle has the exact
normal form
\[
 \Phi_n=C_{u_n}D^{J_n},\qquad J_n=M_n,\qquad |u_n|=I_n=M_n-S_n.
\]
Consequently, $\Phi_n(X)$ is one cylinder of diameter $b^{-I_n}$ and every
point in that cylinder has exactly $b^{J_n}$ preimages.  We prove the
almost-sure rates $(1-2p)_+\log b$ for image contraction and
$(2p-1)_+\log b$ for fibre growth.  For
$A_n=\Exp b^{J_n}$ and $\lambda=bp+(1-p)/b$, a first-passage expansion and
an exponential change of measure give the full trichotomy: $A_n$ converges
if $p<1/(b+1)$, grows linearly at $p=1/(b+1)$, and is asymptotic to an
explicit constant times $\lambda^n$ above that threshold.  Thus the
annealed exponent turns on at $1/(b+1)$, while synchronization and quenched
fibre growth change at $1/2$.  At $p=1/2$, the typical logarithmic scales
are of order $\sqrt n$, although the annealed fibre moment is exponential.
The general monoid-walk, reflected-walk, and random-cocycle frameworks are
classical; this internal note makes no absolute novelty or priority claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A one-sided shift forgets one symbol, while a prefix map inserts one.  Their
composition is elementary, but a random sequence of the two operations has
two different records of information loss: unmatched shifts create
preimages, and unmatched prefixes shrink the image to a cylinder.  The two
records are not independent.  They are the running maximum and the
drawdown of the same nearest-neighbour walk.

This note turns that observation into an exact random dynamical calculation.
One-sided full shifts and their cylinder geometry are standard symbolic
systems \cite{LindMarcus1995}; random cocycles belong to the general
framework developed in, for example, Arnold's monograph
\cite{Arnold1998}.  The algebra also has a clear prior owner boundary.  If
one fixes a single prefix letter, the shift and prefix maps realize the
bicyclic relation $DC=\Id$.  Normal forms, drift, entropy, and harmonic
measures for random walks on suitable groups and monoids have an established
theory, including Mairesse's treatment of zero-automatic monoids and the
bicyclic example \cite{Mairesse2005}.  Ballot identities, first-passage
laws, and reflected random walks are classical probability
\cite{Feller1968,Asmussen2003}.

The residual purpose here is narrower: we couple that normal form to exact
symbolic image and fibre observables, then compute the exponential moment of
the fibre degree with its reflection correction intact.  Four conclusions
result.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every finite cocycle is one prefix followed by a power of the shift;
      the two exponents are exactly $I_n=M_n-S_n$ and $J_n=M_n$.
\item The pathwise image contraction and fibre-growth rates have the sharp
      drift threshold $p=1/2$, and uniform synchronization occurs exactly
      for $p<1/2$.
\item The annealed fibre degree has a different threshold,
      $p=1/(b+1)$.  We give its finite ballot sum, its subcritical limit,
      its critical linear coefficient, and its supercritical exponential
      prefactor.
\item At $p=1/2$, each of $I_n/\sqrt n$ and $J_n/\sqrt n$, marginally,
      converges in law to a half-normal variable, whereas
      $\Exp b^{J_n}$ grows exponentially.  No joint-limit claim is made.
\end{enumerate}

All logarithms are natural.  External release is \textbf{HOLD}: the
displayed conjunction is supported by complete proofs and exact controls,
but the note does not claim that no equivalent specialization appears in
the monoid, queueing, or random-dynamical-systems literature.

\section{The cocycle and its finite normal form}
\label{sec:model}

Fix a finite alphabet $\A$ with cardinality $b\geq2$, and put
$X=\A^{\N_0}$.  For distinct $x,y\in X$, let
\[
 \kappa(x,y)=\min\{k\geq0:x_k\neq y_k\},
 \qquad d_b(x,y)=b^{-\kappa(x,y)},
\]
with $d_b(x,x)=0$.  If $u=u_0\cdots u_{r-1}\in\A^r$, write
\[
 \cyl{u}=\{x\in X:x_0\cdots x_{r-1}=u\},
 \qquad C_u(x)=ux.
\]
The restriction $b\geq2$ is essential for this formulation.  If $b=1$,
$X$ is a singleton, every generator is the identity map, the stated normal
form is nonunique, and every image has diameter zero rather than the formal
quantity $b^{-I}=1$.
The empty word $\epsilon$ satisfies $C_\epsilon=\Id$.  For a letter
$a\in\A$, write $C_a$ for the corresponding one-letter prefix map, and let
\[
 D(x_0x_1x_2\ldots)=x_1x_2\ldots .
\]
The defining cancellation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cancellation}
 DC_a=\Id\qquad(a\in\A).
\end{equation}

Let $(\omega_t)_{t\geq1}$ be iid with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:environment-law}
 \Prob(\omega_t=D)=p,
 \qquad
 \Prob(\omega_t=C_a)=\frac{q}{b},
 \qquad q=1-p,
\end{equation}
and define the forward cocycle
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocycle}
 \Phi_0=\Id,
 \qquad
 \Phi_n=\omega_n\circ\cdots\circ\omega_1.
\end{equation}
The uniform law on prefix letters is convenient but inessential for all
length, image-diameter, and fibre-degree statements below.

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:walk}
 \xi_t=\begin{cases}+1,&\omega_t=D,\\-1,&\omega_t=C_a\text{ for some }a,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 S_n=\sum_{t=1}^n\xi_t,
 \qquad
 M_n=\max_{0\leq k\leq n}S_k.
\end{equation}
Thus $\Exp\xi_t=2p-1$.

\begin{theorem}[Running-maximum normal form]\label{thm:normal-form}
For every finite environment word, there is a unique word $u_n\in\A^*$
and a unique integer $J_n\geq0$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-form}
 \Phi_n=C_{u_n}D^{J_n}.
\end{equation}
Their lengths are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:IJ}
 \boxed{\quad J_n=M_n,\qquad I_n:=|u_n|=M_n-S_n.\quad}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:image-fibre}
 \Phi_n(X)=\cyl{u_n},
 \qquad
 \diam\Phi_n(X)=b^{-I_n},
 \qquad
 \#\Phi_n^{-1}(y)=b^{J_n}\quad(y\in\cyl{u_n}).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We induct on $n$.  The empty product has $u_0=\epsilon$ and $J_0=0$.
Suppose $\Phi_n=C_uD^J$.

If $\omega_{n+1}=C_a$, then
$\Phi_{n+1}=C_{au}D^J$.  The walk decreases by one and its running maximum
does not change, so $J=M_n=M_{n+1}$ and
$|au|=M_{n+1}-S_{n+1}$.

Suppose next that $\omega_{n+1}=D$.  If $u=av$ is nonempty, then
\eqref{eq:cancellation} gives
$\Phi_{n+1}=C_vD^J$.  Here $I_n=M_n-S_n>0$, so
$S_n+1\leq M_n$.  The maximum and $J$ stay fixed, while the prefix length
decreases by one.  If $u=\epsilon$, then $S_n=M_n$ and
\[
 \Phi_{n+1}=D^{J+1},\qquad
 S_{n+1}=M_{n+1}=M_n+1.
\]
This proves existence and \eqref{eq:IJ}.

The map $D^J$ is onto and every point of $X$ has exactly $b^J$ preimages
under it.  The map $C_u$ is injective with image $\cyl{u}$.  Hence
\eqref{eq:image-fibre} follows, including the exact cylinder diameter in
the metric $d_b$.  Finally, the image determines $u$ and $|u|$, while the
constant fibre cardinality determines $J$.  The normal form is unique.
\end{proof}

For later use, write $\degf(\Phi_n)=b^{J_n}$ for this common fibre
cardinality on $\Phi_n(X)$.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:bicyclic}
For a fixed prefix letter, \eqref{eq:cancellation} is the bicyclic relation.
The theorem does not claim ownership of that algebraic normal form.  Its
role here is to identify the two symbolic observables in
\eqref{eq:image-fibre} with $M_n$ and $M_n-S_n$ without a subadditive error.
\end{remark}

\section{Quenched geometry and the drift threshold}
\label{sec:quenched}

Define the quenched fibre-growth and image-contraction rates by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quenched-rates}
 g_{\mathrm q}(p)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log b^{J_n},
 \qquad
 c_{\mathrm q}(p)=\lim_{n\to\infty}
   -\frac1n\log\diam\Phi_n(X),
\end{equation}
whenever the limits exist.

\begin{theorem}[Quenched rates and synchronization]\label{thm:quenched}
For every $0\leq p\leq1$, both limits in \eqref{eq:quenched-rates} exist
almost surely and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quenched-formulas}
 \boxed{
 g_{\mathrm q}(p)=(2p-1)_+\log b,
 \qquad
 c_{\mathrm q}(p)=(1-2p)_+\log b.}
\end{equation}
The random cocycle synchronizes uniformly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:synchronization}
 \sup_{x,y\in X}d_b(\Phi_nx,\Phi_ny)\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
if and only if $p<1/2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The strong law gives $S_n/n\to\delta:=2p-1$ almost surely.  We first show
\begin{equation}\label{eq:max-rate}
 \frac{M_n}{n}\longrightarrow\delta_+.
\end{equation}
If $\delta<0$, then $S_n\to-\infty$, so the all-time maximum is finite and
$M_n/n\to0$.  If $\delta=0$, the strong law implies that, for every
$\varepsilon>0$, all sufficiently large $k$ satisfy
$|S_k|\leq\varepsilon k$.  After dividing by $n$, the maximum over the
finitely many earlier $k$ vanishes, and the later maximum is at most
$\varepsilon$.  If $\delta>0$, the same uniform tail estimate gives
$M_n\leq(\delta+\varepsilon)n+o(n)$, while $M_n\geq S_n$ gives the matching
lower bound.  This proves \eqref{eq:max-rate}.

Now use $J_n=M_n$, $I_n=M_n-S_n$, and \eqref{eq:image-fibre} to obtain
\eqref{eq:quenched-formulas}.  If $p<1/2$, then $I_n/n\to1-2p>0$, so the
diameter in \eqref{eq:image-fibre} tends to zero and
\eqref{eq:synchronization} holds.  If $p>1/2$, then $S_n\to+\infty$ and
there are infinitely many strict record times; at each record time
$S_n=M_n$, hence $I_n=0$ and the image diameter is one.  At $p=1/2$, the
simple symmetric walk hits every positive integer almost surely, so the
same record-time argument applies.  Uniform synchronization therefore fails
for every $p\geq1/2$.
\end{proof}

At the drift boundary the quenched exponential rates vanish, but the
normal-form exponents still have a precise diffusive scale.

\begin{proposition}[Half-normal critical scale]\label{prop:critical-scale}
If $p=1/2$ and $Z$ is standard normal, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-law}
 \frac{J_n}{\sqrt n}\xrightarrow{\mathrm d}|Z|,
 \qquad
 \frac{I_n}{\sqrt n}\xrightarrow{\mathrm d}|Z|,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-means}
 \Exp J_n\sim\Exp I_n\sim\sqrt{\frac{2n}{\pi}}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Donsker's invariance principle and the continuous-mapping theorem
\cite{Billingsley1999} give
$M_n/\sqrt n\Rightarrow\sup_{0\leq t\leq1}B_t$.  The reflection principle
identifies the last variable with $|Z|$.  Time reversal and sign reversal of
the symmetric increments show, for each fixed $n$, that
\[
 I_n=M_n-S_n
 =\max_{0\leq k\leq n}(S_k-S_n)
 \quad\text{has the same law as }M_n.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:critical-law}.  Doob's $L^2$ maximal inequality gives
$\Exp[M_n^2]\leq\Exp[\max_{k\leq n}|S_k|^2]\leq4n$.  Thus
$M_n/\sqrt n$ is uniformly integrable.  Taking expectations in the weak
limit yields $\Exp M_n/\sqrt n\to\Exp|Z|=\sqrt{2/\pi}$, and the equality in
law transfers the result to $I_n$.
\end{proof}

\section{Annealed fibre degree and the rare-event threshold}
\label{sec:annealed}

Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annealed-def}
 A_n(p)=\Exp\bigl[b^{J_n}\bigr],
 \qquad
 \lambda=bp+\frac qb.
\end{equation}
The number $\lambda$ is the moment-generating factor
$\Exp[b^{\xi_1}]$.  Reflection changes finite-time constants and even
creates a bounded phase, so $A_n$ is not simply $\lambda^n$.

For $k\geq1$, let $T_k=\inf\{n\geq0:S_n=k\}$.  Since
\[
 b^m=1+(b-1)\sum_{k=1}^m b^{k-1}\qquad(m\in\N_0),
\]
we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-passage-decomposition}
 A_n(p)=1+(b-1)\sum_{k=1}^n b^{k-1}\Prob(T_k\leq n).
\end{equation}
For $0<p<1$, the ballot theorem gives, for $m\geq k$ with
$m\equiv k\pmod2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ballot}
 \Prob(T_k=m)=\frac{k}{m}
 \binom{m}{(m+k)/2}
 p^{(m+k)/2}q^{(m-k)/2}.
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:first-passage-decomposition}--\eqref{eq:ballot} are an
exact finite-$n$ formula for $A_n$.

\begin{theorem}[Full annealed trichotomy]\label{thm:annealed}
Assume $0<p<1$, and let $A_n$ and $\lambda$ be as in
\eqref{eq:annealed-def}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $p<1/(b+1)$, put $r=p/q$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subcritical}
 A_n\longrightarrow\frac{1-r}{1-br}.
\end{equation}
\item If $p=1/(b+1)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-linear}
 A_n=\frac{(b-1)^2}{b(b+1)}n+O(1).
\end{equation}
More exactly, if $\widehat M_n$ is the maximum of a walk whose $+1$
probability is $b/(b+1)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-exact}
 A_n=1+\frac{b-1}{b}\,\widehat\Exp\widehat M_n.
\end{equation}
\item If $p>1/(b+1)$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
 \rho=\frac{q}{pb^2}<\frac1b.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supercritical}
 \frac{A_n}{\lambda^n}\longrightarrow
 \frac{1-\rho}{1-b\rho}.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
At the endpoints, $A_n(0)=1$ and $A_n(1)=b^n$.  Consequently the annealed
fibre-degree exponent is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annealed-exponent}
 \boxed{
 g_{\mathrm a}(p):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log A_n(p)
 =\log\max\left\{1,bp+\frac{1-p}{b}\right\}.}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The equivalence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:threshold-equivalence}
 \lambda-1=(b-1)\left(p-\frac qb\right)
\end{equation}
places the threshold at $p=q/b=1/(b+1)$.

Suppose first that $p<1/(b+1)$.  Then $p<q$, and the negatively drifting
walk has a finite all-time maximum $M_\infty$.  The classical gambler's-ruin
calculation gives
\[
 \Prob(M_\infty\geq k)=\Prob(T_k<\infty)=\left(\frac pq\right)^k=r^k.
\]
Thus $M_\infty$ is geometric on $\N_0$ with mass
$(1-r)r^m$.  Since $br<1$, monotone convergence yields
\[
 A_n\uparrow\Exp b^{M_\infty}
 =(1-r)\sum_{m\geq0}(br)^m=\frac{1-r}{1-br}.
\]

Now let $p=1/(b+1)$.  Tilt each first-hit path by $b^{S_m}$.  Because
$\lambda=1$, the tilted walk has $+1$ probability $bp=q=b/(b+1)$ and
$-1$ probability $q/b=p=1/(b+1)$.  Every path stopped upon first hitting
$k$ acquires the same likelihood factor $b^k$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-hit-tilt}
 \Prob(T_k\leq n)=b^{-k}\widehat\Prob(T_k\leq n).
\end{equation}
Substitution into \eqref{eq:first-passage-decomposition} gives
\[
 A_n=1+\frac{b-1}{b}\sum_{k\geq1}
 \widehat\Prob(\widehat M_n\geq k)
 =1+\frac{b-1}{b}\widehat\Exp\widehat M_n,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:critical-exact}.  Under the tilted law the drift is
$\widehat\delta=(b-1)/(b+1)$.  Set
$\widehat Y_n=\widehat M_n-\widehat S_n$.  This reflected birth--death
chain moves toward zero with probability $b/(b+1)$ and away from zero with
probability $1/(b+1)$; at zero those moves lead respectively to zero and
one.  Its stationary law is
\[
 \widehat\pi(y)=(1-b^{-1})b^{-y}\qquad(y\in\N_0).
\]
The monotone common-increment coupling of the chain started at zero with a
stationary copy gives
\[
 0\leq\widehat\Exp\widehat Y_n
 \leq\sum_{y\geq0}y\widehat\pi(y)=\frac1{b-1}.
\]
Since $\widehat M_n=\widehat S_n+\widehat Y_n$ and
$\widehat\Exp\widehat S_n=\widehat\delta n$, it follows that
\[
 \widehat\Exp\widehat M_n
 =\widehat\delta n+O(1),
\]
and \eqref{eq:critical-linear} follows.

Finally suppose $p>1/(b+1)$.  On paths through time $n$, define the tilted
law $\Prob_*$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exponential-tilt}
 \frac{d\Prob_*}{d\Prob}\bigg|_{\sigma(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)}
 =\frac{b^{S_n}}{\lambda^n}.
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\[
 \frac{b^{S_n}}{\lambda^n}
 =\prod_{t=1}^n\frac{b^{\xi_t}}{\lambda}
\]
is a mean-one product martingale.  Hence the finite-dimensional tilted laws
are consistent and define iid increments on the infinite path space.
Under $\Prob_*$, the $+1$ and $-1$ probabilities are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-probabilities}
 \alpha=\frac{bp}{\lambda},
 \qquad
 \beta=\frac{q}{b\lambda},
 \qquad \alpha>\beta.
\end{equation}
Writing $Y_n=M_n-S_n$, the change of measure gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tilted-moment}
 A_n=\lambda^n\Exp_*[b^{Y_n}].
\end{equation}
The process $Y_n$ moves from $y\geq1$ to $y-1$ with probability $\alpha$
and to $y+1$ with probability $\beta$; at zero,
$P_*(0,0)=\alpha$ and $P_*(0,1)=\beta$.  It is an irreducible aperiodic
reflected birth--death chain with
stationary law
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stationary-drawdown}
 \pi(y)=(1-\rho)\rho^y,
 \qquad
 \rho=\frac\beta\alpha=\frac{q}{pb^2}.
\end{equation}
Starting from zero, the monotone common-increment coupling bounds $Y_n$ by
a stationary copy.  Because $b\rho<1$, this domination makes
$(b^{Y_n})_n$ uniformly integrable.  Ergodic convergence of the chain and
\eqref{eq:stationary-drawdown} now give
\[
 \Exp_*b^{Y_n}\longrightarrow
 \sum_{y\geq0}(1-\rho)(b\rho)^y
 =\frac{1-\rho}{1-b\rho}.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:tilted-moment}, this proves
\eqref{eq:supercritical}.  The endpoint identities follow directly from
$M_n=0$ at $p=0$ and $M_n=n$ at $p=1$.  The three cases then imply
\eqref{eq:annealed-exponent}.
\end{proof}

The trichotomy exposes a rare-event regime that an almost-sure drift
calculation cannot see.

\begin{corollary}[Two thresholds and a strict annealed gap]
\label{cor:two-thresholds}
For $1/(b+1)<p<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strict-gap}
 g_{\mathrm a}(p)>g_{\mathrm q}(p).
\end{equation}
For $0\leq p\leq1/(b+1)$ and for $p=1$, equality holds.  In particular,
when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intermediate-window}
 \frac1{b+1}<p<\frac12,
\end{equation}
the fibre degree $b^{J_n}$ is eventually constant almost surely, but its
expectation grows exponentially.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $1/(b+1)<p\leq1/2$, then $g_{\mathrm a}>0=g_{\mathrm q}$.  If
$1/2<p<1$, strict Jensen applied to the nonconstant variable
$b^{\xi_1}$ gives
\[
 g_{\mathrm a}=\log\Exp b^{\xi_1}
 >\Exp\log b^{\xi_1}=(2p-1)\log b=g_{\mathrm q}.
\]
The equality cases follow from \eqref{eq:quenched-formulas} and
\eqref{eq:annealed-exponent}.  In the window
\eqref{eq:intermediate-window}, the walk has negative drift, so
$J_n=M_n$ increases to the finite all-time maximum almost surely, while
$\lambda>1$ gives exponential annealed growth.
\end{proof}

At the second threshold, \cref{prop:critical-scale,thm:annealed} combine to
give a particularly sharp separation.

\begin{corollary}[Diffusive typical scale, exponential mean]
\label{cor:symmetric-annealed}
At $p=1/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-degree-law}
 \frac{\log\degf(\Phi_n)}{\sqrt n}
 \xrightarrow{\mathrm d}(\log b)|Z|,
 \qquad
 \frac{-\log\diam\Phi_n(X)}{\sqrt n}
 \xrightarrow{\mathrm d}(\log b)|Z|,
\end{equation}
whereas
\begin{equation}\label{eq:symmetric-annealed-asymptotic}
 \Exp\degf(\Phi_n)
 \sim\frac{b+1}{b}
 \left(\frac{b+b^{-1}}2\right)^n.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:symmetric-degree-law} is
\cref{prop:critical-scale} combined with \eqref{eq:image-fibre}.  In
\eqref{eq:rho}, $p=q=1/2$ gives $\rho=b^{-2}$, so the prefactor in
\eqref{eq:supercritical} is
$(1-b^{-2})/(1-b^{-1})=(b+1)/b$.  The base is
$\lambda=(b+b^{-1})/2$, proving
\eqref{eq:symmetric-annealed-asymptotic}.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact controls and ownership boundary}
\label{sec:controls}

The accompanying standard-library program separates exact regression
checks from floating diagnostics.  Its integer and rational layer performs
265,861 assertions:
\begin{itemize}
\item all $65{,}535$ direction words through time $15$ satisfy
      $J=M$ and $I=M-S$, with three identities checked per word;
\item all $29{,}524$ labelled words over $\{D,C_0,C_1\}$ through time $9$
      agree with definition-level composition on symbolic tails;
\item exhaustive finite-input maps verify the exact image cylinders and
      constant fibre degrees through $(b,n)=(2,10)$ and $(3,7)$;
\item the ballot sum \eqref{eq:first-passage-decomposition}--\eqref{eq:ballot}
      agrees with direct rational walk propagation for $b=2,3,5$, five
      interior rational probabilities through $n=18$, and separately at
      $p=0,1$ through $n=20$;
\item the critical identity \eqref{eq:critical-exact}, the supercritical
      tilt \eqref{eq:tilted-moment}, the stationary prefactor, and the
      symmetric equality in law of $I_n$ and $J_n$ are checked independently.
\end{itemize}
Five rescaled floating values illustrate convergence but do not certify an
identity, almost-sure limit, or weak limit.

The scope boundary is equally explicit.  Arnold provides the cited broad random
cocycle framework \cite{Arnold1998}.  Mairesse provides the cited general normal-form,
drift, entropy, and harmonic-measure results for the relevant monoid-walk
setting \cite{Mairesse2005}.  Feller and Asmussen represent the classical
ballot, first-passage, exponential-tilting, and reflected-walk tools
\cite{Feller1968,Asmussen2003}.  The note does not claim any of those
ingredients.  Its residual theorem package is the exact coupling
\[
 \text{prefix--shift cocycle}
 \longleftrightarrow
 (\text{image cylinder},\text{fibre degree})
 \longleftrightarrow
 (M_n-S_n,M_n),
\]
together with the alphabet-dependent annealed trichotomy and its comparison
with pathwise synchronization.

This system is not a random subshift of finite type: there is no random
adjacency constraint, forbidden language, or path-count matrix.  It is also
not a rank-one reset or a renewal-reward model.  A shift can cancel any
currently unmatched prefix, and the surviving normal form depends on the
running maximum of the entire direction walk rather than on iid reset gaps.
Nor is it a hidden-output process: the assertions concern images and fibres
of actual random maps on the full shift.  These distinctions are an internal
collision firewall, not a novelty claim.  Public posting, submission, and
priority language remain unauthorized pending a specialist literature
review.
