\begin{abstract}
For a finite group $G$, the nearest-neighbour derivative
$\Delta_G(x)_i=x_i^{-1}x_{i+1}$ forgets exactly a global left translation.
We turn this elementary observation into an exact local statement: on a
subshift $X\subseteq G^{\Z}$ where the derivative is injective, the least
source-word length separating $X$ from all its nontrivial translates
determines every admissible inverse window, and hence the exact symmetric
inverse radius.  We then construct binary rank-one subshifts $X_N$ using
cutting numbers at most $4$ and spacer lengths at most $2$.  Each $X_N$ is
minimal, aperiodic, and uniquely ergodic, with exact one-density
\[
 \rho_N=\frac{3\cdot 2^N-1}{3\cdot 2^{N+1}-1}<\frac12.
\]
Consequently the XOR derivative is a conjugacy from $X_N$ onto its image.
An explicit complementary pair of length $3|v_N|+1$, together with a defect
coding of the spacer sequence, gives the exact separation length
$s_{\Ftwo}(X_N)=3|v_N|+2$.  Hence the inverse symmetric radius is exactly
$3\cdot2^N-1$.  Thus bounded alphabet, bounded cutting, bounded spacers, and
a fixed radius-one forward code do not give a uniform bound on inverse
radius.
\end{abstract}
