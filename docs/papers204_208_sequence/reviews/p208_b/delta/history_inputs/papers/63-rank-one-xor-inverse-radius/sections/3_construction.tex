\section{A bounded-cut, bounded-spacer family}

Fix $N\geq1$ and suppress the superscript $(N)$ when no confusion can arise.
Starting from $v_0=0$, use \eqref{eq:construction-intro}.  Each $v_n$ is a
prefix of $v_{n+1}$, so there is a one-sided limit
$V_N\in\{0,1\}^{\N}$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:XN-definition}
 X_N=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{\Z}:\Lang(x)\subseteq\Lang(V_N)\}.
\end{equation}
Choose positions $p_j\geq j$ in $V_N$ and center the factors
$V_N[p_j-j,p_j+j]$ at the origin.  Since the alphabet is finite, first pass to
a subsequence that stabilizes on $[-1,1]$, then to one that stabilizes on
$[-2,2]$, and continue.  The diagonal subsequence defines compatible blocks
on every finite centered interval and hence a point
$x\in\{0,1\}^{\Z}$.  Every finite block of $x$ is a factor of $V_N$; thus
$x\in X_N$ and $X_N$ is nonempty.  The defining condition is closed and shift
invariant, so $X_N$ is indeed a subshift.

In rank-one notation, the first $N$ stages have cutting number $2$ and one
spacer of length $1$.  Every later stage has cutting number $4$ and spacer
tuple
\[
 (0,0,2).
\]
Thus the cutting numbers are at most $4$ and all spacer lengths are at most
$2$, uniformly in $N$.  This is a rank-one word construction in the standard
symbolic sense~\cite{GaoHill2016,GaoEtAl2025}.

The last pre-tail word has a particularly rigid alternating form.

\begin{lemma}[terminal alternating block]\label{lem:alternating}
Put $U=v_N$, $L=|U|$, and $m=2^N-1$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:U-form}
 U=(01)^m0,\qquad L=2^{N+1}-1,\qquad |U|_1=2^N-1.
\end{equation}
In particular $U$ begins and ends in $0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $0\leq n\leq N$, induction in the first line of
\eqref{eq:construction-intro} gives
\[
 v_n=(01)^{2^n-1}0.
\]
Indeed, if $v_n=(01)^a0$, then
$v_n1v_n=(01)^{2a+1}0$, and $2(2^n-1)+1=2^{n+1}-1$.
The length and one-count follow from the displayed word.
\end{proof}

For every fixed $k\geq N$, recursively expanding higher words produces a
canonical decomposition into copies of $v_k$, with adjacent copies separated
by either no symbol or by $11$.  Consequently consecutive starts of canonical
$v_k$-copies are separated by either $|v_k|$ or $|v_k|+2$.  We use this
bounded-gap observation twice: first for minimality and later to control word
frequencies.
