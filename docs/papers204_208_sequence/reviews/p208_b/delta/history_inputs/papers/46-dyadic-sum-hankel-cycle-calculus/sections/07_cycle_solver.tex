\section{Complete labeled cyclic closure}
\label{sec:cycles}

Fix a length $r\ge1$ and an ordered tuple of dyadic labels
$q_i=2^{a_i}\ge2$.  We seek positive integers
$n_1,\ldots,n_r$, with $n_{r+1}=n_1$, satisfying
\begin{equation}
  n_i+n_{i+1}=q_i,\qquad 1\le i\le r.
  \label{eq:cycle-system}
\end{equation}
The length range here includes $r=1$ because the graph retains loops.  By
contrast, traces in the Hilbert--Schmidt-only domain use $r\ge2$.

Iterating $n_{i+1}=q_i-n_i$ gives, for $2\le i\le r+1$,
\begin{equation}
  n_i=(-1)^{i-1}n_1+
      \sum_{j=1}^{i-1}(-1)^{i-1-j}q_j.
  \label{eq:cycle-recurrence}
\end{equation}
Closing at $i=r+1$ yields the single equation
\begin{equation}
  (1-(-1)^r)n_1
  =\sum_{j=1}^r(-1)^{r-j}q_j.
  \label{eq:cycle-closing}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Odd/even labeled cycle solver]
\label{thm:cycle-solver}
For the ordered system \cref{eq:cycle-system}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If $r$ is odd, there is one algebraic candidate,
  \begin{equation}
    n_1=\frac12(q_1-q_2+q_3-\cdots+q_r).
    \label{eq:odd-candidate}
  \end{equation}
  It is a positive solution exactly when every vertex obtained from
  \cref{eq:cycle-recurrence} is positive.
  \item If $r$ is even, a solution exists only if
  \begin{equation}
    q_1-q_2+q_3-\cdots-q_r=0.
    \label{eq:even-compatibility}
  \end{equation}
  Under this condition, set
  \begin{equation}
    b_1=0,\qquad
    b_i=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}(-1)^{i-1-j}q_j\quad(2\le i\le r),
    \label{eq:bi-definition}
  \end{equation}
  and
  \begin{equation}
    L(q)=\max_{\substack{1\le i\le r\\i\ {\rm odd}}}(-b_i),
    \qquad
    U(q)=\min_{\substack{1\le i\le r\\i\ {\rm even}}}b_i.
    \label{eq:LU-definition}
  \end{equation}
  The positive solutions are exactly
  \begin{equation}
    n_1=x\in\Z\cap(L(q),U(q)),\qquad
    n_i=(-1)^{i-1}x+b_i.
    \label{eq:even-solutions}
  \end{equation}
  Thus their number is $\max\{0,U(q)-L(q)-1\}$.
\end{enumerate}
In the odd block $A_s$, retain precisely those solutions for which $n_1$
is odd.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{eq:cycle-recurrence,eq:cycle-closing} follow by induction.  If $r$ is
odd, the coefficient on the left of \cref{eq:cycle-closing} is $2$, which
gives \cref{eq:odd-candidate}; it is integral because every $q_i$ is even.
The recurrence fixes every remaining vertex, so positivity is the only
condition left.

If $r$ is even, the left side of \cref{eq:cycle-closing} vanishes, and its
right side vanishes exactly under \cref{eq:even-compatibility}.  Write
$x=n_1$.  Formula \cref{eq:cycle-recurrence} becomes
$n_i=(-1)^{i-1}x+b_i$.  For odd $i$, positivity says $x>-b_i$; for even
$i$, it says $x<b_i$.  Their intersection is exactly
$\Z\cap(L(q),U(q))$.  The endpoints are integers, giving the stated count.
Finally, subtracting a vertex from the even integer $q_i$ preserves parity
along every edge.  Hence all vertices are odd exactly when $n_1$ is odd.
\end{proof}

The frozen examples illustrate all branches.  Labels $(2,4,4)$ give the
unique odd-length solution $(1,1,3)$.  Labels $(4,8,8,4)$ satisfy
\cref{eq:even-compatibility}; here
\[
  (n_1,n_2,n_3,n_4)=(x,4-x,4+x,4-x),
\]
so positivity gives $x\in\{1,2,3\}$, with $x=1,3$ in the odd block.  Labels
$(4,4,8,4)$ have nonzero alternating sum and no solution.

The passage from matrices to an absolutely convergent infinite walk sum uses
the following ideal estimate.

\begin{lemma}[Powers of finite compressions]
\label{lem:compression-powers}
Let $T\in\Schatten{2}$, let $P_N$ be the coordinate projection onto the
first $N$ basis vectors, and set $T_N=P_NTP_N$.  Then
$\norm{T_N-T}_2\to0$ and, for every integer $r\ge2$,
\begin{equation}
  \norm{T_N^r-T^r}_1\longrightarrow0.
  \label{eq:compression-power-convergence}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Hilbert--Schmidt convergence follows first for finite-rank operators and
then for $T$ by finite-rank density, because left and right multiplication by
an orthogonal projection are contractions in $\Schatten{2}$.  Put
$\delta_N=\norm{T_N-T}_2$.  Schatten H\"older gives the explicit square
estimate
\begin{equation}
  \norm{T_N^2-T^2}_1
  \le \delta_N(\norm{T_N}_2+\norm{T}_2)
  \longrightarrow0.
  \label{eq:compression-square-estimate}
\end{equation}
For $r>2$, the exact identity
\[
  T_N^r-T^r
  =(T_N^2-T^2)T_N^{r-2}
   +T^2(T_N^{r-2}-T^{r-2})
\]
and the ideal inequality yield
\[
  \norm{T_N^r-T^r}_1
  \le \norm{T_N^2-T^2}_1\norm{T_N}^{r-2}
   +\norm{T^2}_1\norm{T_N^{r-2}-T^{r-2}}.
\]
Here $\norm{T_N}\le\norm{T}$ and $\norm{T_N-T}\le\delta_N$; the usual
telescoping formula for bounded powers therefore makes the last factor tend
to zero.  This proves \cref{eq:compression-power-convergence}.
\end{proof}

When $\sigma>1/2$ and $r\ge2$, the trace admits a closed-walk
interpretation.  The product of edge weights around a based closed walk is
\begin{equation}
  \prod_{i=1}^r h_s(n_i,n_{i+1})
  =\prod_{i=1}^r n_i^{-s}.
  \label{eq:walk-weight}
\end{equation}
Indeed, apply \cref{lem:compression-powers} first to $H_\sigma$.  For every
$N$, expansion of the finite matrix trace gives
\[
  \Tr\!\left((P_NH_\sigma P_N)^r\right)
  =\sum_{1\le n_1,\ldots,n_r\le N}
    \prod_{i=1}^r h_\sigma(n_i,n_{i+1}),
  \qquad n_{r+1}=n_1.
\]
Every summand is nonnegative.  These finite sums increase with $N$ and,
by \cref{eq:compression-power-convergence}, converge to the finite value
$\Tr(H_\sigma^r)$.  Moreover,
\[
  \left|\prod_{i=1}^r h_s(n_i,n_{i+1})\right|
  =\prod_{i=1}^r h_\sigma(n_i,n_{i+1}),
\]
so the complex closed-walk series is absolutely convergent by comparison.
A second application of the lemma to $H_s$ identifies its sum with
$\Tr(H_s^r)$.  Consequently the solver is a matrix-independent trace
evaluator: enumerate an ordered label tuple, solve it using
\cref{thm:cycle-solver}, and add \cref{eq:walk-weight} for the accepted based
vertex words.  Primitive-period reduction and quotienting by cyclic base
point are separate operations, as detailed in \cref{app:cycles}.
