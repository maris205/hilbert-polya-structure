\subsection{Scope limitations}

The collapse theorem is finite dimensional for general trace characters.  It
does not classify every infinite-dimensional nuclear representation,
unbounded complex, odd-letter supercategory, non-type-I trace, or nonlocal
completed-orbit weight.  The countable result classifies the explicit
coordinate-projector family by direct calculation; it is not an infinite
Brauer--Nesbitt theorem.  Radical extensions remain possible, aggregate
commuting products remain weaker than wordwise traces, and the empty-word
convention remains a separate recognition issue.

\subsection{Paper27 minimum obligation}

The next candidate must not install a projector for each desired atom.  It
must derive a countable factorization or divisibility incidence compiler from
the source object and meet all of the following admission gates:

\begin{enumerate}
 \item produce individual necklace-resolved coefficients before
 abelianization, at every repetition;
 \item retain the original digit marker \(u^{\ell(n)}\) until explicit
 first-return induction;
 \item prove an honest trace-class, nuclear, or source-owned relative
 determinant domain for the whole countable object;
 \item run arbitrary-inventory controls and show why the source distinguishes
 atoms rather than a supplied list;
 \item either escape finite recognizability with controlled infinite memory,
 or accept atom collapse and obtain new same-object analytic continuation.
\end{enumerate}

An aggregate Euler product alone cannot pass the first gate, by
\Cref{fig:wordwise-firewall}.  A bar complex whose semisimple quotient is
\(\C^m\) cannot pass the fourth.  These are now theorem-backed admission
tests rather than heuristic preferences.
