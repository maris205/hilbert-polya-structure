\section{Canonical complex audit}
\label{sec:complexes}

The support-Euler formula suggests that a canonical chain complex might kill
mixed necklaces without fixed color projectors.  Three objects must be kept
separate: a shared free bar complex, a word-indexed exterior Euler fiber, and
the bar complex of the separable color algebra.

\subsection{Shared free and polynomial sources}

In a one-object free-renewal algebra, every sequence of return letters is
composable.  Its cyclic bar construction therefore organizes mixed necklaces
rather than deleting them.  Passing to a commutative polynomial algebra does
not help: the Koszul and Hochschild descriptions retain mixed classes such as
\(e_i\wedge e_j\) or \(dx_i\wedge dx_j\).  The algebraic complex remembers
exactly the mixed support that the selector was meant to remove.

The reduced-support space \(Q_S\) from \eqref{eq:reduced-support} has the
correct Euler number, but its zero-differential exterior algebra has nonzero
classes in both parities for \(|S|>1\).  Their dimensions cancel in
supertrace.  Moreover, the support \(S\) is supplied only after the completed
word has been read.  Consequently this construction is an exact orbitwise
coefficient, not a stationary bar resolution and not a proof that mixed
classes are null-homologous.

\subsection{The separable color algebra}

There is a canonical stationary success after one changes the algebra.  Put
\begin{equation}
 B_m=\C^m=\bigoplus_{i=1}^m\C e_i,
 \qquad e_ie_j=\delta_{ij}e_i.
 \label{eq:color-algebra}
\end{equation}
Left multiplication by \(e_i\) is the coordinate projector \(P_i\).

\begin{theorem}[Hochschild atom collapse]
\label{thm:hochschild-collapse}
For \(B_m\) over \(\C\),
\begin{equation}
 \HH_0(B_m)\cong B_m,
 \qquad
 \HH_q(B_m)=0\quad(q>0).
 \label{eq:hochschild-color}
\end{equation}
The surviving basis consists of the \(m\) supplied primitive idempotents.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The element

\[
 E=\sum_{i=1}^m e_i\otimes e_i
\]

is a separability idempotent: multiplication sends it to the unit, and it
centralizes the left and right \(B_m\)-actions.  Hence \(B_m\) is projective as
a \(B_m^e\)-module, so

\[
 \HH_q(B_m)=\operatorname{Tor}^{B_m^e}_q(B_m,B_m)=0
\]

for \(q>0\).  Degree zero is \(B_m/[B_m,B_m]\), equal to \(B_m\) because the
algebra is commutative.
\end{proof}

The theorem is positive and limiting at once.  A one-object category with
\(m\) freely composable loop letters contains mixed cycles.  The successful
algebra \eqref{eq:color-algebra} has \(m\) orthogonal objects/idempotents, one
private recurrent sector per color.  Its separability contraction already
names those colors through \(E\).  Homology does not reveal a hidden shared
selector; it retracts to the supplied atom list.

The result also explains why adding chain-level decoration cannot by itself
escape \Cref{thm:graded-rigidity}.  Whenever the stationary complex has a
finite even trace character, its Euler trace descends to the virtual
semisimplification already classified.  A future escape must change the
finite-recognizable theorem class and justify the resulting infinite or
relative trace, not merely lengthen the bar resolution.
