We give an exact Myhill criterion for a subshift that is a finite disjoint
union of clopen invariant irreducible pieces.  The union is Myhill precisely
when every piece is Myhill and no preinjective cellular automaton routes one
piece into a different piece.  The proof separates homoclinicity classes by
compactness, pastes component maps across a finite clopen partition, and uses
irreducibility to force each component image into one target component.  For a
positive-entropy mixing shift of finite type $M$, let $H(M)$ be the positive
integers $n$ for which $M$ has no point fixed by the $n$th shift power.  This
set is a finite downward ideal under divisibility.  A disjoint extension of
$M$ by periodic orbits is Myhill exactly when its distinct period labels form
an antichain in $H(M)$.  Finally, two directed cycles of lengths $2s+1$ and
$2s+2$ sharing one vertex yield a mixing core whose fixed-period support is
the numerical semigroup generated by those lengths.  The periods
$s+1,\ldots,2s$ form an admissible antichain, producing a Myhill shift of
finite type with exactly $s+1$ clopen irreducible components for every
$s\geq 1$.
