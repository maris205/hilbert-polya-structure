\section{A renewal family with unbounded component count}
\label{sec:renewal}

The antichain criterion has content for arbitrarily large finite
decompositions.  Fix $s\geq1$ and set
\begin{equation}
  m=2s+1.
  \label{eq:m-def}
\end{equation}
Let $R_s$ be the directed graph formed from a simple $m$-cycle and a simple
$(m+1)$-cycle that share exactly one vertex $v$.  Apart from $v$, their
vertices and edges are disjoint.  Let $M_s$ be the edge shift of $R_s$.

\begin{proposition}[Mixing and Perron equation]
\label{prop:perron}
The graph $R_s$ is primitive, $M_s$ is a positive-entropy mixing SFT, and its
Perron root $\lambda_s$ is the unique solution above one of
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_s^{-m}+\lambda_s^{-(m+1)}=1.
  \label{eq:perron-equation}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every vertex reaches $v$, and $v$ reaches every vertex, so $R_s$ is strongly
connected.  It contains cycles of consecutive lengths $m$ and $m+1$; their
gcd is one.  Hence the adjacency matrix is primitive and the edge shift is
mixing.

Let $r$ be a positive right Perron eigenvector and normalize $r_v=1$.  Along
a deterministic return branch of length $\ell$, the eigenvector equations
give value $\lambda_s^{-(\ell-1)}$ at the vertex immediately after $v$.  The
equation at $v$ is therefore
\[
 \lambda_s
 =\lambda_s^{-(m-1)}+\lambda_s^{-m},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:perron-equation}.  For $0<\lambda\leq1$, the left side of
\eqref{eq:perron-equation} is at least $2$, so no such $\lambda$ solves the
equation.  For $\lambda\geq1$, that side is continuous and strictly decreasing
from $2$ to zero.  There is a unique solution above one, so
$\ent(M_s)=\log\lambda_s>0$.
\end{proof}

The fixed-period support has a particularly simple description.

\begin{proposition}[Exact fixed support]
\label{prop:renewal-support}
For every $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
 \Fix_{M_s}(\sigma^n)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 n\in\langle m,m+1\rangle
 :=\{am+b(m+1):a,b\in\N\}.
 \label{eq:renewal-support}
\end{equation}
Writing $n=qm+r$ with $0\leq r<m$ gives the equivalent membership test
\begin{equation}
  n\in\langle m,m+1\rangle
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q\geq r.
  \label{eq:qr-membership}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every positive-length closed walk in $R_s$ meets $v$.  Indeed, a walk that
starts inside either branch follows its deterministic edges to $v$ before it
can close.  Rotate a closed walk to begin at a visit to $v$.  Between
successive visits it traverses exactly one of the two return loops, so its
length has the form $am+b(m+1)$.  Conversely, every concatenation of those
return loops is a closed walk.  A length-$n$ closed walk exists precisely when
the edge shift has a point fixed by $\sigma^n$, which proves
\eqref{eq:renewal-support}.

If $q\geq r$, then
\[
 n=(q-r)m+r(m+1),
\]
so $n$ belongs to the semigroup.  Conversely, suppose
$n=am+b(m+1)$ and write $b=tm+r$ with $0\leq r<m$.  Then
\[
 n=(a+b+t)m+r.
\]
Uniqueness of Euclidean division gives $q=a+b+t\geq b\geq r$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:qr-membership}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Hole formulas]
\label{cor:hole-formulas}
The period-hole set of $M_s$ is
\begin{equation}
 H(M_s)=\{qm+r:q\in\N,\ 0\leq r<m,\ q<r\},
 \label{eq:renewal-holes}
\end{equation}
where only positive integers are retained.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \max H(M_s)=m^2-m-1,
 \qquad
 |H(M_s)|=\frac{m(m-1)}2,
 \label{eq:hole-count}
\end{equation}
and every integer at least $m^2-m$ is realized as a fixed period.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:renewal-holes} is the negation of
\eqref{eq:qr-membership}.  For each remainder $r\in\{1,\ldots,m-1\}$, the
holes have $q=0,\ldots,r-1$.  Summing their counts gives
\[
 \sum_{r=1}^{m-1}r=\frac{m(m-1)}2.
\]
The largest hole occurs at $r=m-1$ and $q=m-2$, with value
$(m-2)m+(m-1)=m^2-m-1$.  The next integer is therefore the conductor.
\end{proof}

We now choose a large antichain from the initial holes.

\begin{theorem}[Exactly $s+1$ clopen components]
\label{thm:many-components}
Let
\begin{equation}
 P_s=\{s+1,s+2,\ldots,2s\}.
 \label{eq:Ps}
\end{equation}
Then $P_s$ is an $s$-element divisibility antichain in $H(M_s)$, and
\begin{equation}
 X_s=M_s\sqcup\bigsqcup_{p=s+1}^{2s}O_p
 \label{eq:Xs}
\end{equation}
is a Myhill SFT with exactly $s+1$ clopen irreducible components.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every $p\in P_s$ satisfies $p<m=2s+1$, so it is a hole by
\eqref{eq:renewal-holes}.  If $p<q$ lie in $P_s$ and $p\mid q$, then
\[
 q\geq2p\geq2s+2,
\]
contrary to $q\leq2s$.  Hence $P_s$ is an antichain.  The
period-hole criterion, \cref{thm:antichain}, makes $X_s$ Myhill.

Apply \cref{lem:block-diagonal} to $M_s$ and the $s$ cycle shifts.  The
displayed pieces are exactly the clopen irreducible components of the SFT
$X_s$.  There is one mixing piece and $s$ cycle pieces, for a total of $s+1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The symbolic proof covers every $s\geq1$.  Finite trace computations in
\cref{sec:controls} are consistency checks, not the source of the
arbitrary-parameter conclusion.
\end{remark}
