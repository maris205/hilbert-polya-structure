\section{Primary literature and claim boundary}
\label{sec:literature}

The closest collision is the original Bost--Connes construction.  It gives a
Hamiltonian with zeta partition trace and also a bosonic determinant, but the
one-particle space of the latter is explicitly indexed by primes
\citep{BostConnes1995}.  The $ax+b$ relations and canonical time evolution are
standard in the associated semigroup algebra \citep{Cuntz2008}.  The affine
Toeplitz system has an exact KMS phase diagram and a low-temperature GNS
partition function $\zeta(\beta-1)$ \citep{LacaRaeburn2010}.  In that
representation the additive coordinate has finite residue-class
multiplicity; this is not the full Cayley representation used below.

KMS states for non-principal groupoids require quasi-invariant measures and
isotropy traces \citep{Neshveyev2013}.  Monoid growth and type-I partition
functions likewise depend on a frozen representation and degree map
\citep{BruceLacaRamaggeSims2019,BruceLacaTakeishi2021}.  Recent algebraic-action
groupoids faithfully encode arithmetic actions \citep{BruceLi2024}, and their
homology and $K$-theory now have systematic computations
\citep{BruceKubotaTakeishi2026}.  These advances do not by themselves identify
a KMS normalization with a primitive closed-path trace-log.

On the symbolic side, higher-rank graphs provide path categories with
factorization rules \citep{KumjianPask2000}.  Ihara theory gives genuine
Euler products and determinant formulas for reduced primitive graph cycles
\citep{StarkTerras1996}; periodic infinite graphs require a specified finite
trace and bounded geometry \citep{GuidoIsolaLapidus2008}.  Simple-cycle and
line-graph determinant formulas continue to develop
\citep{Watanabe2026}.  Their ledger is the set of \emph{all} reduced prime
cycles, including cycles induced by group or semigroup relations.

Graph $C^*$-partition functions furnish a particularly useful firewall:
their graph example is a fixed-target sum over finite open paths, not a
primitive closed-cycle product \citep{BruceTakeishi2024}.  Crystallization can
produce boundary or vacuum reductions \citep{LacaNeshveyevYamashita2025}, but
using such a reduction would change the support object that this paper keeps
unprojected.

Directed labelled Cayley conventions are standard \citep{Caucal2020}, and
trace-class determinant identities used below are classical
\citep{Simon2005}.  We claim no novelty for any of these components.  A
bounded primary-source search through 2026-08-15 located no direct instance
of the exact conjunction studied here: the all-integer affine source, its
original generator marker, the complete positive/symmetric/nonbacktracking
ledger, the natural whole operator, and a theorem identifying its Fredholm
trace-log with the Bost--Connes partition trace.  This is a scoped search
statement, not a priority claim.
