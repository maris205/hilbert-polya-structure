\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Periodic points provide finite witnesses for two different aspects of a
symbolic system.  Their multiplicity at large periods can reveal entropy,
whereas the set of their least periods records which orbit lengths actually
occur.  Standard asymptotic statements leave finite questions unanswered.
At which \emph{specified} period can one already certify a given entropy
accuracy?  From which \emph{computable} integer onward can one certify every
least period?  This note gives one explicit answer to each question.

The first answer concerns a nonempty shift of finite type
$X\subseteq\Sigma^{\ZZ^d}$ equipped with a nearest-neighbour presentation
whose allowed edges are closed under reversal in every coordinate direction.
(In particular, $X$ is invariant under every coordinate reflection.)
Write $C_n$ for the number of locally admissible patterns on $[1,n]^d$ and
$P_m$ for the number of points fixed by $m\ZZ^d$.  For $n\ge4$, we prove
\begin{equation}
\frac{\log C_n-[n^d-(n-3)^d]\log|\Sigma|}{(n-1)^d}
\le \frac{\log P_{2n-2}}{(2n-2)^d}
\le \frac{\log C_{2n-2}}{(2n-2)^d}.
\label{eq:intro-sandwich}
\end{equation}
Combining this injection estimate with Bui's explicit local-cube estimate
\cite{Bui2026} gives a completely specified two-sided $O_d(n^{-1})$
distance from entropy.  In particular, \cref{cor:target-accuracy} turns a
requested tolerance $\delta$ directly into a sufficient period using only
$(d,|\Sigma|,\delta)$.  The proof takes a largest fibre of local patterns
with common boundary data and places independently selected members in the
$2^d$ reflected orthants.  A two-layer lower shell controls the periodic
seam, and disjoint private interiors make the construction injective.  The
same coordinatewise construction gives an anisotropic rectangular form.

The second answer concerns the edge shift of a primitive nonnegative integral
$r\times r$ matrix $A$.  Let $\lambda>1$ be its Perron root and let $\theta$
be the largest modulus of a non-Perron eigenvalue.  If $L_n$ denotes the
number of \emph{points} of least period $n$, then
\begin{equation}
L_n\ge \lambda^n-(r-1)\theta^n
-\tau(n)\bigl(\lambda^{n/2}
 +(r-1)\max(1,\theta)^{n/2}\bigr).
\label{eq:intro-least}
\end{equation}
Positivity of the right-hand side certifies period $n$.  More globally, the
three normalized error bases are strictly below one, which yields an
algorithmically certified conductor $N(A)$ such that every $n\ge N(A)$ is a
least period.  This conclusion follows from the standard trace formula and
M\"obius inversion; the contribution here is the displayed directly testable
lower bound and its effective tail certificate, not a new qualitative
eventual-period theorem.  Recent qualitative context includes the work of de
Jong \cite{deJong2025}; standard edge-shift background is in
\cite{LindMarcus2021}.

These statements use different geometries and should not be conflated.
Their common feature is instead operational: each turns a finite datum into a
finite periodic certificate while accounting explicitly for the discarded
information.  In \cref{eq:intro-sandwich}, the loss is a boundary shell.  In
\cref{eq:intro-least}, it is the population of fixed points inherited from
proper divisors.  \Cref{tab:certificates} compares the hypotheses, outputs,
and principal failure modes.

\paragraph{Ownership and claim posture.}
The reflection-extension geometry and the explicit local-count estimate used
here belong to Bui \cite{Bui2026}; Friedland \cite{Friedland1997} provides
foundational entropy context for multidimensional shifts of finite type.
Trace counting, Perron separation, and M\"obius inversion are standard.  The
source records used for this internal draft were frozen at Bui v1 and de Jong
v3 on 25 August 2026.  We make no priority claim based on a search miss.  The
manuscript is a source-adjacent quantitative note and remains on hold for an
independent priority and standalone-mass review.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The reflection certificate is a nearest-neighbour theorem.  A simple
range-two rule, printed in \cref{ex:range-two}, invalidates the naive
extension.  The least-period certificate requires both primitivity and
$\lambda>1$; it does not cover arbitrary irreducible shifts or zero-entropy
cycles.  Moreover, $L_n$ counts points, so the number of least-period orbits
is $L_n/n$.  The conductor obtained below is sufficient and effective, but
not asserted to be minimal.
