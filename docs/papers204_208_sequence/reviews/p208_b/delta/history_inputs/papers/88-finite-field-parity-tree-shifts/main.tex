\title[Finite-field parity tree shifts]{Finite-Field Parity Tree Shifts:\\
Exact Boundary Complexity, IID Rays, and Coordinate-Deletion Reconstruction}

\begin{abstract}
Let $q$ be a prime power, let $d\geq2$, and fix nonzero coefficients
$c_1,\ldots,c_d\in\F_q$.  We study labelings of the full rooted $d$-ary
tree that satisfy the linear parity rule
\[
 x_w=\sum_{j=1}^d c_jx_{wj}
\]
at every vertex.  Every height-$h$ block is parametrized uniquely by its
$d^h$ terminal labels, so the exact block count is $q^{d^h}$.  This gives a
complete normalization ledger: boundary-normalized complexity is $\log q$,
site-normalized complexity tends to $(d-1)\log q/d$, and double-logarithmic
growth is $\log d$.

Uniform terminal data define compatible uniform block laws and hence a
shift-invariant probability measure.  This measure is a homogeneous
block-Markov chain with an affine-hyperplane offspring kernel.  Although
siblings are jointly constrained, the labels on every deterministic ray are
iid uniform on $\F_q$.  In the opposite direction, the full level at depth
$h$ reconstructs the root through an explicit nonzero linear functional.
Every proper subset of that level is independent of the root: its mutual
information with the root is exactly zero, while the complete level has
mutual information $\log q$.  Thus an iid process on each ray coexists with
an exact coordinate-deletion threshold on every complete level.  The
tree-shift, entropy-normalization, joint-offspring, reconstruction, and
secret-sharing frameworks are assigned to their prior owners; the residual
contribution is this finite-field formula package, with no absolute priority
claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Local constraints on a rooted tree can be invisible along every individual
lineage and still be perfectly visible across a complete generation.  The
example in this note makes that separation exact.  Labels take values in a
finite field, and every parent is a fixed nondegenerate linear combination
of its children.  A single ray therefore sees a new uniform field element at
every step, while the exponentially wide boundary carries one field symbol
of purely collective information about the root.

Tree shifts of finite type and their automata-theoretic foundations are due
to Aubrun and B\'eal \cite{AubrunBeal2012}.  Ban and Chang developed the
nonlinear-recurrence approach to finite-type tree-shift complexity and used
the double-logarithmic entropy normalization \cite{BanChang2017}.  Petersen
and Salama introduced and established the site-normalized tree-shift entropy
used below \cite{PetersenSalama2018}.  These frameworks are prior.  We keep
both normalizations visible because the same exact block count produces
different constants under them.

Tree-indexed Markov processes form another established line, including the
work of Benjamini and Peres \cite{BenjaminiPeres1994}.  In the binary case,
a parent-to-pair kernel allowing conditional dependence between siblings is
already the bifurcating Markov-chain framework used by Guyon
\cite{Guyon2007}.  Souissi later introduced block Markov chains on rooted
trees \cite{Souissi2022}.  Our children are not conditionally independent:
given their parent, they are uniform on one affine hyperplane.  We use the
displayed joint-offspring factorization and claim no new Markov framework.

Root reconstruction from noisy independent-edge broadcasts is likewise an
established tree problem \cite{EvansEtAl2000}.  The law below is instead
noiseless and sibling-coupled, and our result is a finite-depth statement
about deleting coordinates from a complete level.  We claim no general
broadcasting or reconstruction theorem.

The residual results are four exact statements.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Restriction to the terminal level is a bijection, giving
      $|\cB_h|=q^{d^h}$ and an explicit root functional.
\item Boundary, volume, and double-log normalizations are all evaluated
      from this one formula, without fitting a nonlinear recurrence.
\item The compatible uniform law factors through a homogeneous joint
      offspring kernel, and every deterministic ray is iid uniform.
\item The root has zero mutual information with every proper subset of a
      complete level but is determined by the complete level.
\end{enumerate}
All four statements hold over every finite field, not only over prime
fields.  The accompanying control program performs exact enumeration over
$\F_2,\F_3,\F_4$, and $\F_5$, together with modular row reduction over
$\F_2,\F_3,\F_5$, and $\F_7$.  The single $\F_4$ lane is an extension-field
regression test; the all-prime-power scope comes from the proofs, not from
experimental extrapolation.

\subsection*{Internal collision firewall}

Two earlier internal systems require an explicit separation.  P49 concerns
complete cyclic \emph{hom} tree-shifts defined by parent--child adjacency,
transient phase allocation, and Hausdorff dimension in a boundary-weighted
metric.  The present shift instead has a star constraint coupling all
siblings to their parent; it has no transient feeder, phase optimizer, graph
homomorphism constraint, or Hausdorff-dimension claim.  P77 concerns a
one-dimensional countable orbit closure of fixed digit-weight automatic
sets, its Cantor--Bendixson tower, and cellular-automaton endomorphisms.  The
present system is an uncountable free-semigroup tree shift and uses neither
automatic digit support nor orbit-closure topology.  Accordingly, no claim
or proof from either internal paper is reused.

\section{The parity tree shift}
\label{sec:setup}

Fix an integer $d\geq2$ and write $D=\{1,\ldots,d\}$.  The vertex set of
the full rooted ordered $d$-ary tree is the free monoid
\[
 \cT_d=D^*=\bigcup_{h\geq0}D^h,
\]
whose root is the empty word $\eps$.  Put
\[
 \Lev_h=D^h,
 \qquad
 \Vol_h=\bigcup_{r=0}^h\Lev_r.
\]
Thus $|\Lev_h|=d^h$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:volume-size}
 |\Vol_h|=1+d+\cdots+d^h=\frac{d^{h+1}-1}{d-1}.
\end{equation}
For a labeling $x\in\F_q^{\cT_d}$ and $j\in D$, define the subtree shift by
$(\sigma_jx)_w=x_{jw}$.

Let $q$ be a prime power and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonzero-coefficients}
 \boldsymbol c=(c_1,\ldots,c_d)\in(\F_q^\times)^d.
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Parity tree shift]\label{def:shift}
The finite-field parity tree shift is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shift}
 X_{q,d,\boldsymbol c}
 =\left\{x\in\F_q^{\cT_d}:
 x_w=\sum_{j=1}^dc_jx_{wj}\text{ for every }w\in\cT_d\right\}.
\end{equation}
Let $\cB_h(X_{q,d,\boldsymbol c})$ be the set of its restrictions to
$\Vol_h$.
\end{definition}

The rule is a finite list of allowed labeled stars, so
$X_{q,d,\boldsymbol c}$ is a tree shift of finite type.  It is preserved by
each $\sigma_j$.  When $q=2$ and $c_1=\cdots=c_d=1$, the rule says that the
parent is the parity, or xor, of its children.  The terminology ``parity''
will also be used for its finite-field linear extension.

For $u=u_1\cdots u_h\in\Lev_h$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-coefficient}
 c_u=\prod_{r=1}^h c_{u_r},
 \qquad c_\eps=1.
\end{equation}
Every $c_u$ is nonzero by \eqref{eq:nonzero-coefficients}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact boundary parametrization]\label{thm:boundary}
For every $h\geq0$, restriction to the terminal level is a bijection
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-bijection}
 \cB_h(X_{q,d,\boldsymbol c})\longrightarrow\F_q^{\Lev_h},
 \qquad b\longmapsto b|_{\Lev_h}.
\end{equation}
For the inverse block, every vertex is reconstructed upward.  In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-reconstruction}
 b_\eps=\sum_{u\in\Lev_h}c_ub_u.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-count}
 |\cB_h(X_{q,d,\boldsymbol c})|=q^{d^h},
\end{equation}
and exactly $q^{d^h-1}$ of these blocks have any prescribed root label
$a\in\F_q$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Starting with arbitrary labels on $\Lev_h$, apply
\eqref{eq:shift} successively at levels $h-1,h-2,\ldots,0$.  This constructs
one legal block and shows both existence and uniqueness on $\Vol_h$.
The block extends to an infinite tree: for each terminal vertex, choose
$d-1$ child labels arbitrarily and solve for the last one using
$c_d\ne0$; repeat at every new terminal level.  Thus every upward-constructed
block belongs to $\cB_h(X_{q,d,\boldsymbol c})$.

Iterating the local equation gives \eqref{eq:root-reconstruction}.  More
explicitly, substituting the children of the root once produces coefficients
$c_j$; each additional substitution multiplies the coefficient along the
new edge, yielding $c_u$ after $h$ levels.  The bijection now gives
\eqref{eq:block-count}.  Finally, the right side of
\eqref{eq:root-reconstruction} is a nonzero linear functional on the
$d^h$-dimensional space $\F_q^{\Lev_h}$.  Each fiber is an affine hyperplane
of cardinality $q^{d^h-1}$.
\end{proof}

The root-refined block count also closes the Ban--Chang nonlinear recurrence
in one line.  If $A_h(a)$ denotes the number of height-$h$ blocks rooted at
$a$, then $A_h(a)$ is independent of $a$; writing the common value as $A_h$
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:snre-collapse}
 A_0=1,
 \qquad A_{h+1}=q^{d-1}A_h^d,
 \qquad A_h=q^{d^h-1}.
\end{equation}
Indeed there are $q^{d-1}$ child tuples with a prescribed parent, and the
$d$ rooted subblocks can then be selected independently.  Equation
\eqref{eq:snre-collapse} is a specialization of the prior nonlinear-
recurrence framework, not a new recurrence formalism.

\section{A normalization ledger}
\label{sec:complexity}

Because tree volumes grow exponentially with height, the word ``entropy''
has been used for more than one normalization.  We therefore state the
unambiguous finite-height quantity first:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-complexity}
 C_h:=\log|\cB_h(X_{q,d,\boldsymbol c})|=d^h\log q.
\end{equation}
All logarithms are natural.

\begin{theorem}[Exact normalized complexity]\label{thm:complexity}
For every $h\geq0$, the boundary- and site-normalized complexities satisfy
the first two identities below.  For every $h\geq1$, the double-logarithmic
complexity satisfies the third:
\begin{align}
 \frac{C_h}{|\Lev_h|}
   &=\log q,\label{eq:boundary-rate}\\
 \frac{C_h}{|\Vol_h|}
   &=\frac{(d-1)d^h}{d^{h+1}-1}\log q
     \longrightarrow\frac{d-1}{d}\log q,\label{eq:site-rate}\\
 \frac{\log C_h}{h}
   &=\log d+\frac{\log\log q}{h}
     \longrightarrow\log d.\label{eq:double-log-rate}
\end{align}
In particular, the Petersen--Salama site-normalized entropy is
$(d-1)\log q/d$, while the Ban--Chang double-logarithmic entropy is
$\log d$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Insert \eqref{eq:finite-complexity}, $|\Lev_h|=d^h$, and
\eqref{eq:volume-size}.  The three displayed identities and their limits
follow directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{The same exact block count under three normalizations.  The first
row is retained as a boundary complexity rather than called a third standard
entropy.}
\label{tab:normalizations}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Normalization & Finite-height value & Limit \\
\midrule
terminal level & $C_h/d^h$ & $\log q$ \\
all sites & $C_h/|\Vol_h|$ & $(d-1)\log q/d$ \\
double logarithm ($h\geq1$) & $(\log C_h)/h$ & $\log d$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The coefficient $\log q$ in
$|\cB_h|=\exp((\log q)d^h)$ records one free field symbol per terminal site.
By contrast, the full tree shift on $q$ symbols has one free symbol at every
site and hence the larger site-normalized rate $\log q$.  The parity rule
removes precisely the internal-site degrees of freedom.

\section{The compatible uniform block-Markov law}
\label{sec:measure}

Let $\nu_h$ be the uniform probability on
$\cB_h(X_{q,d,\boldsymbol c})$.  The next proposition shows that these finite
laws define one infinite law without a boundary choice at infinity.

\begin{proposition}[Compatibility and invariance]\label{prop:compatible}
The restriction map from height $h+1$ to height $h$ has constant fiber size
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restriction-fiber}
 q^{(d-1)d^h}=q^{d^{h+1}-d^h}.
\end{equation}
Hence the laws $(\nu_h)_{h\geq0}$ are projectively compatible and determine
a probability measure $\mu_{q,d,\boldsymbol c}$ on
$X_{q,d,\boldsymbol c}$.  It satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-cylinder}
 \mu_{q,d,\boldsymbol c}([b])=q^{-d^h}
 \qquad(b\in\cB_h),
\end{equation}
and is invariant under every subtree shift $\sigma_j$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix a legal block on $\Vol_h$.  At each of its $d^h$ terminal vertices, a
child tuple must satisfy one nonzero linear equation in $d$ variables.  It
has $q^{d-1}$ solutions.  The choices at distinct terminal vertices are
independent, proving \eqref{eq:restriction-fiber}.  Uniform measures
therefore push forward to uniform measures.  The projective-limit measure
exists because the alphabet is finite, and \eqref{eq:uniform-cylinder}
follows from \eqref{eq:block-count}.

For shift invariance, fix a vertex $v$ and a height $h$.  The labels at the
$d^h$ descendants $vu$, $u\in\Lev_h$, are an iid uniform subcollection of
the uniform level $|v|+h$.  Upward reconstruction inside the subtree rooted
at $v$ therefore gives the uniform law on $\cB_h$.  Cylinder sets generate
the Borel sigma-field, so every $\sigma_j$ preserves $\mu_{q,d,\boldsymbol c}$.
\end{proof}

For $a\in\F_q$ and $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_d)\in\F_q^d$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:offspring-kernel}
 K(a;y_1,\ldots,y_d)
 =q^{-(d-1)}\mathbf 1
   \left\{a=\sum_{j=1}^dc_jy_j\right\}.
\end{equation}
This is a probability kernel from a parent to its ordered child block.  For
$h\geq1$ and a labeling $b$ of $\Vol_h$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-markov-factorization}
 \mu_{q,d,\boldsymbol c}([b])
 =q^{-1}\prod_{w\in\Vol_{h-1}}
 K(b_w;b_{w1},\ldots,b_{wd}),
\end{equation}
with the convention that the right side is zero when a local rule fails.
For $h=0$, the factorization consists only of the root factor $q^{-1}$.
For a legal block, the exponent is
$1+(d-1)|\Vol_{h-1}|=d^h$, so
\eqref{eq:block-markov-factorization} agrees with
\eqref{eq:uniform-cylinder}.  Thus the measure is a homogeneous block
Markov chain in the joint-offspring sense of \cite{Souissi2022}.

\begin{lemma}[Local proper-subset uniformity]\label{lem:local-uniform}
Fix a parent value $a\in\F_q$.  Under the kernel $K(a;\cdot)$, every proper
subset of the $d$ child coordinates is iid uniform on the corresponding
power of $\F_q$, and its law does not depend on $a$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $s<d$ child coordinates and prescribe their values.  At least one child
coordinate remains free, and its coefficient is nonzero.  The residual
linear equation in $d-s$ variables has exactly $q^{d-s-1}$ solutions.
There are $q^{d-1}$ offspring tuples in total.  The probability of each
prescribed $s$-tuple is therefore $q^{d-s-1}/q^{d-1}=q^{-s}$, independently
of the parent value.
\end{proof}

The lemma shows why a standard independent-offspring branching description
would be inaccurate: a complete sibling block lies on a codimension-one
hyperplane.  Every proper sibling subblock nevertheless looks exactly
independent.

\begin{theorem}[Every ray is iid]\label{thm:ray}
Let
\[
 w_0=\eps,
 \qquad w_{r+1}=w_rj_{r+1}
 \quad(r\geq0)
\]
be any deterministic ray.  Under $\mu_{q,d,\boldsymbol c}$, the process
$(X_{w_r})_{r\geq0}$ is iid with common law $\Unif(\F_q)$.  Equivalently,
for all $h\geq0$ and $a_0,\ldots,a_h\in\F_q$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ray-law}
 \mu_{q,d,\boldsymbol c}
 \{X_{w_0}=a_0,\ldots,X_{w_h}=a_h\}=q^{-(h+1)}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The root is uniform by \eqref{eq:uniform-cylinder} with $h=0$.  By
\eqref{eq:block-markov-factorization}, conditional on the labels through one
generation, the ordered offspring block at each exposed vertex has kernel
$K$.  Lemma \ref{lem:local-uniform} with a single selected child says that
the next label on the ray is uniform and its conditional law does not depend
on the parent or on the earlier ray labels.  Multiplying these $h+1$
conditional probabilities proves \eqref{eq:ray-law}.
\end{proof}

Ray iid should not be confused with independence of the whole tree.  The
next section gives the strongest possible obstruction: one complete level
determines the root exactly.

\section{Exact coordinate-deletion reconstruction}
\label{sec:reconstruction}

Let $R=X_\eps$ and let $Z_h=(X_u)_{u\in\Lev_h}$.  For a subset
$A\subseteq\Lev_h$, write $Z_A=(X_u)_{u\in A}$.  Shannon entropy and mutual
information are computed with natural logarithms.

For a fixed depth, the information condition ``all coordinates recover,
while every proper coordinate subset reveals nothing'' is the perfect
$(n,n)$ threshold criterion from classical secret sharing, introduced
independently by Blakley and Shamir \cite{Blakley1979,Shamir1979}.  We claim
neither that access structure nor its additive finite-field mechanism.  The
calculation below identifies its simultaneous realization at every depth by
one shift-invariant tree law.

\begin{theorem}[Exact reconstruction threshold]\label{thm:threshold}
For every $h\geq1$, the complete level is an iid uniform vector in
$\F_q^{d^h}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-from-level}
 R=\sum_{u\in\Lev_h}c_uX_u
 \quad\text{almost surely, and indeed pointwise on }X_{q,d,\boldsymbol c}.
\end{equation}
For every proper subset $A\subsetneq\Lev_h$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proper-independence}
 \mu(R=a,Z_A=z)=q^{-(|A|+1)}
 \quad(a\in\F_q,\ z\in\F_q^A).
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{align}
 \Hsh(R\mid Z_A)&=\log q,
 &\I(R;Z_A)&=0,\label{eq:proper-information}\\
 \Hsh(R\mid Z_h)&=0,
 &\I(R;Z_h)&=\log q.
 \label{eq:full-information}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The terminal-level bijection and the uniform block law show that $Z_h$ is
uniform on $\F_q^{\Lev_h}$, hence its coordinates are iid uniform.
Equation \eqref{eq:root-from-level} is
\eqref{eq:root-reconstruction}.

Fix $A\subsetneq\Lev_h$ and condition on $Z_A=z$.  Choose one omitted
vertex $v\in\Lev_h\setminus A$.  All coefficients $c_u$ are nonzero, so
$c_vX_v$ is uniform on $\F_q$ and independent of the conditioned
coordinates.  Even after conditioning on every other omitted coordinate,
the sum in \eqref{eq:root-from-level} is therefore uniform.  Removing that
extra conditioning leaves
$\mu(R=a\mid Z_A=z)=q^{-1}$.  Since
$\mu(Z_A=z)=q^{-|A|}$, this proves
\eqref{eq:proper-independence}.

The root is uniform, so $\Hsh(R)=\log q$.  Independence gives
\eqref{eq:proper-information}; deterministic reconstruction gives
\eqref{eq:full-information}.
\end{proof}

Thus deleting even one label from a complete level removes all information
about the root, not merely some fraction of it.  The phenomenon is
high-order: every finite ray is a product process by Theorem
\ref{thm:ray}, and the whole generation is also an unconditional product
vector, yet one linear combination of all generation coordinates equals the
root.

\begin{remark}[Role of the assumptions]\label{rem:assumptions}
The condition $d\geq2$ is needed for ray iid: with one child the local rule
determines that child from its parent.  Nonzero coefficients are needed for
the all-proper-subsets statement.  If $c_j=0$, then at level one the root is
already reconstructed after child $j$ is omitted, so
\eqref{eq:proper-information} fails.  Boundary parametrization alone is
less restrictive; the assumptions are frozen because the combined theorem
package requires all of them.
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}

The parity tree shift has one free field coordinate per terminal vertex and
no other finite-block degrees of freedom.  That leaf parametrization gives
the entire complexity ledger and a canonical compatible uniform measure.
The same measure has two opposing exact faces: every lineage is iid, while
every complete generation reconstructs the root and loses all root
information after any coordinate is deleted.  The coefficient of each
terminal label is simply the product of the edge coefficients along its
path, so nondegeneracy at one local star propagates to every depth.

The scope is intentionally narrow.  We do not classify general linear tree
shifts with several equations, spatially varying coefficients, missing
coefficients, noisy parity checks, or nonlinear local rules.  Those changes
can alter rank density, compatibility, or the sharp reconstruction
threshold.  A natural next problem is to replace the single parity equation
by a matrix-valued local code and determine which rank profiles preserve iid
rays while producing a nontrivial hierarchy of boundary information.
