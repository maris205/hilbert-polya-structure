\section{The Fredholm trilemma}
\label{sec:trilemma}

The exact phase diagram separates three natural attempts to exploit the
cofactor.

\subsection{Pure cofactor roof}

Set \(s=0\) in \cref{eq:Lsu} and write

\[
 Q_ue_n=\sum_{d\mid n+1,\ d\ge2}q(n,d)^{-u}e_d.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Pure cofactor obstruction]
\label{thm:pure-obstruction}
For every \(u\in\mathbb C\), \(Q_u\) is not trace class.  Whenever it has a
bounded extension, it is noncompact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The trace-class conclusion follows immediately from \cref{thm:traceclass},
which would require \(0>1/2\).  More directly, the Fourier component
\cref{eq:successor-extraction} is now the unweighted unilateral shift

\[
 e_n\longmapsto e_{n+1}.
\]

Fourier averaging maps compact operators to compact operators, while this
shift is not compact.  Thus a bounded \(Q_u\) cannot be compact.
\end{proof}

For example, Schur's test proves boundedness when \(\Re u>1\): the row and
column sums are controlled by the convergent series
\(\sum_{q\ge1}q^{-\Re u}\).  We do not need a complete boundedness
classification in the remaining strip.

\begin{theorem}[Pure cofactor class series]
\label{thm:pure-series}
As a formal or \(|z|<1\) connected series,

\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_2^{\mathrm{cof}}(u,z)
 =2^{-u}\sum_{k\ge2}z^k
 =2^{-u}\frac{z^2}{1-z}.
\label{eq:pure-H2}
\end{equation}

It diverges at \(z=1\) for every finite \(u\).  More generally, for an atom
\(p\),

\[
 \mathcal H_p^{\mathrm{cof}}(u,z)
 =p^{-u}\frac{z^{2(p-1)}}{1-z^{p-1}},
 \qquad |z|<1.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every \(C_k\) has cofactor weight \(2^{-u}\) and period \(k\); summing over
\(k\ge2\) gives \cref{eq:pure-H2}.  For \(C_{k,p}\), the period is
\((p-1)k\) and the cofactor weight is \(p^{-u}\), giving the second geometric
series.
\end{proof}

The formula \cref{eq:pure-H2} is not a Fredholm coefficient of a trace-class
whole operator.  Its attractive atomic weight comes with infinite
multiplicity at the unmarked specialization.

\subsection{Endpoint regularization}

In the honest domain \cref{eq:S1-domain}, the combined class is

\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_2(s,u;z)=
 2^{-u}\sum_{k\ge2}z^kM_k^{-2s}.
\label{eq:regularized-H2}
\end{equation}

It is entire in \(z\), because \(M_k\) grows factorially.  The same growth is
the arithmetic defect: the orbit indexed by \(k\) has roof
\(2\log M_k\), not an atomic roof \(\log2\).  For general \(p\), the
factor \(M_{k,p}^{-2s}\) remains.

\subsection{Unitary characters}

By \cref{cor:character-S1}, a unitary character has the base threshold
\(\Re s>1/2\).  By \cref{cor:character-persistence}, it multiplies every
canonical primitive by the same nonzero phase \(\chi(2)\).  It changes no
magnitude and deletes no \(C_k\).

\begin{theorem}[Fredholm trilemma]
\label{thm:trilemma}
For the frozen successor--divisor cofactor cocycle, the three natural choices
have the following mutually obstructing outcomes:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{0.96\textwidth}{L{0.24\textwidth}L{0.30\textwidth}Y}
\toprule
Choice & Analytic outcome & Arithmetic outcome \\
\midrule
\(s=0\), pure cofactor
& never trace class; noncompact whenever bounded
& weight \(Q^{-u}\), but infinitely many canonical representatives \\
endpoint regularized
& honest Fredholm determinant in the two-half-plane domain
& factorial \(M_{k,p}^{-2s}\) dependence \\
unitary character
& same \(\Re s>1/2\) threshold as the base
& phases only; every \(C_k\) survives \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

No choice gives both an honest same-object Fredholm determinant and the
one-atom/one-primitive target ledger.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The three rows are respectively
\cref{thm:pure-obstruction,thm:pure-series},
\cref{thm:traceclass,eq:regularized-H2}, and
\cref{cor:character-S1,cor:character-persistence}.  Their conclusions are
properties of the frozen graph and weights, so there is no remaining choice
among these three that changes the stated ledger.
\end{proof}

\subsection{First-return collapse}

One might attempt to collapse the long successor segments by inducing on
edges with \(q>1\).

\begin{proposition}[Return grammar preserves the obstruction]
\label{prop:return}
Every \(C_k\) becomes one fixed first-return branch indexed by \(k\ge2\).
Its diagonal entry is \(2^{-u}z^k\) for the pure cofactor-time marking and
\(2^{-u}z^kM_k^{-2s}\) for the endpoint-regularized marking.

At \(s=0,z=1\), the induced pure diagonal has the constant nonzero entry
\(2^{-u}\) with infinite multiplicity and is noncompact.  Endpoint
regularization restores summability only while retaining the factorial
\(k\)-ledger.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:q2}, every \(C_k\) has exactly one \(q>1\) edge.  It therefore
defines one return branch.  Multiplication of its time, cofactor, and endpoint
weights gives the two entries.  The compactness and summability claims are
the corresponding diagonal-operator criteria.
\end{proof}

Thus Poincaré collapse trades the original graph for a countable return
grammar; it does not turn the family indexed by \(k\) into a single atom.
