\begin{abstract}
Let \((V,Q)\) be a nonsingular quadratic space of dimension \(2m\) over
\(\F_2\), and iterate
\[
                 \Phi(x,y)=(y,x+Q(x)y)
\]
on \(V^2\).  We determine its complete functional graph for both Witt signs.
The polar bit is invariant, and a three-bit quotient shows that every
transient has depth at most two and every eventual period belongs to
\(\{1,2,3,4\}\), with all pointwise exceptions classified.  A two-candidate
inverse formula gives every fibre, the full image tower, and the exact three
depth layers.  Character sums then resolve the Witt dependence, while the
reverse fibres force precisely six decorated cycle shapes.  This yields
closed counts for every component and cycle, hence the finite-map zeta
function.  The map is nonbijective and fails both standard Yang--Baxter
relations; transvection actions, Yang--Baxter pair maps, static quadratic-form
counts, and zeta bookkeeping are therefore treated only as owner boundaries.
Exact exhaustive controls through dimension ten include literal image-set
equality and component traversal.  The owner search is bounded, and no
novelty, priority, or external-release claim is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{The map and the residual question}

Let \((V,Q)\) be a nonsingular quadratic space of dimension \(2m\) over
\(\F_2\), with polar form
\[
 B(x,y)=Q(x+y)+Q(x)+Q(y).
\]
On \(V^2\), define
\begin{equation}
                  \Phi(x,y)=(y,x+Q(x)y).                 \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
The update is basis-free and equivariant for the diagonal action of
\(O(V,Q)\).  It swaps the pair when the departing vector is singular and
adds that vector to its partner when it is nonsingular.

Put
\begin{equation}
 N=|V|=2^{2m},\qquad
 S=\sum_{x\in V}(-1)^{Q(x)}=\varepsilon2^m,\qquad
 \varepsilon\in\{+1,-1\}.                               \label{eq:NS}
\end{equation}
For \(m=0\), only the plus convention occurs and the unique state is fixed.
For \(m\ge1\), the two signs are the two Witt types.  Classical
characteristic-two quadratic counts and orthogonal geometry are background
\cite{Fulton1977,HallShpectorov2021}.  We prove the counts needed below, so
no static census receives contribution credit.

The word ``shear'' does not identify \eqref{eq:map} with a transvection.
A symplectic transvection has a fixed centre and coefficient supplied by the
polar pairing; its generated group acts bijectively
\cite{Sjostrand2025}.  Here the moving state supplies \(Q(x)\), the active
vector changes at every step, and the map is noninjective.  Set-theoretic
Yang--Baxter maps are another pair-map neighbor, including both bijective
\cite{EtingofSchedlerSoloviev1999} and nonbijective finite-order settings
\cite{CatinoColazzoStefanelli2021}.  We give explicit failures of both
Yang--Baxter relations in \cref{rem:ybe}.

After these deductions, the residual package is the conjunction of exact
pointwise dynamics, every fibre and image, Witt-sensitive temporal layers,
and all decorated components of the literal map.  The finite-map zeta is a
consequence, not a separate contribution.

There are also internal package collisions.  The following firewall records
them without claiming an external literature comparison.

\section{Pointwise temporal classification}

For a state \((x,y)\), set
\[
                  a=Q(x),\qquad b=Q(y),\qquad c=B(x,y).
\]

\begin{proposition}[three-bit quotient]\label{prop:quotient}
The polar bit \(c\) is invariant, and
\begin{equation}
          (a,b,c)\longmapsto\bigl(b,a(1+b+c),c\bigr).     \label{eq:quotient}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Writing \((u,v)=\Phi(x,y)\), polarization and alternation give
\[
 Q(u)=b,\quad Q(v)=Q(x+ay)=a+ab+ac=a(1+b+c),\quad
 B(u,v)=B(y,x+ay)=c.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[complete pointwise clock]\label{thm:clock}
Every transient has depth at most two, and every eventual period belongs to
\(\{1,2,3,4\}\).  More precisely:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ccl@{}}
\toprule
\(c\)&\((a,b)\)& pointwise fate\\
\midrule
0&00& recurrent; period 1 iff \(x=y\), otherwise 2\\
0&01& recurrent; period 2 iff \(x=0\), otherwise 4\\
0&10& recurrent; period 2 iff \(y=0\), otherwise 4\\
0&11& depth 1; eventual period 2 iff \(x=y\), otherwise 4\\
1&00& recurrent of exact period 2\\
1&01& depth 2, followed by exact period 2\\
1&10& depth 1, followed by exact period 2\\
1&11& recurrent of exact period 3\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Depth two occurs in the plus plane for \(m=1\), in both signs for \(m\ge2\),
and not in the minus plane for \(m=1\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:quotient} gives, for \(c=0\),
\[
00\to00,\qquad01\leftrightarrow10,\qquad11\to10,
\]
and, for \(c=1\),
\[
00\to00,\qquad01\to10\to00,
\qquad10\to00,\qquad11\to11.
\]
On the pair of vectors, a singular or nonsingular departing state acts by
\[
 A_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 A_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}
 \quad\text{over }\F_2.
\]
In particular,
\[
 A_0^2=I,\qquad A_1^3=I,\qquad
 A_1A_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A_0A_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
and both two-step products square to \(I\).  The matrix word or landing test
for every quotient row is therefore as follows.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ccp{0.68\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
\(c\)&start&word and pointwise consequence\\
\midrule
0&00&\(A_0^2=I\); one step fixes the pair exactly when \(x=y\).\\
0&01&\((A_1A_0)^2=I\); two steps fix the pair exactly when \(x=0\).\\
0&10&\((A_0A_1)^2=I\); two steps fix the pair exactly when \(y=0\).\\
0&11&\(A_1\) lands in type 10 with second coordinate \(x+y\); hence the
eventual period is two exactly when \(x=y\).\\
1&00&\(A_0^2=I\), while \(\Fix(A_0)\) would force \(x=y\), excluded by
\(B(x,y)=1\).\\
1&01&\(A_1A_0\) lands in type 00 after two steps, whose period is exactly two.\\
1&10&\(A_1\) lands in type 00 after one step, whose period is exactly two.\\
1&11&\(A_1^3=I\), and \(\Fix(A_1)=\{(0,0)\}\), excluded by this type.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
These products give all listed periods and the only possible shortenings
\(x=y\), \(x=0\), or \(y=0\).  Thus no further period or transient occurs.

The depth-two type has the cardinality computed as \(A\) in
\eqref{eq:HMaz}; the stated small-form boundary follows immediately.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[not a Yang--Baxter map]\label{rem:ybe}
In a plus hyperbolic plane choose \(e,f\) with
\(Q(e)=Q(f)=0\), \(B(e,f)=1\).  Directly,
\[
 \Phi_{12}\Phi_{23}\Phi_{12}(e+f,e,f)=(f,e,f),\qquad
 \Phi_{23}\Phi_{12}\Phi_{23}(e+f,e,f)=(f,e,e).
\]
Thus the braid relation fails.  Put \(R=\Phi\).  With products acting
right-to-left, the two sides of
\(R_{12}R_{13}R_{23}=R_{23}R_{13}R_{12}\), evaluated at
\((e+f,e+f,e)\), are \((e,f,e)\) and \((e,f,0)\).  Hence the quantum
Yang--Baxter convention fails as well.
\end{remark}

\section{Fibres, images, and Witt layers}

\begin{theorem}[every fibre]\label{thm:fibre}
For each target \((u,v)\in V^2\),
\begin{equation}
 \Phi^{-1}(u,v)=
 \{(v,u):Q(v)=0\}\ \cup\
 \{(u+v,u):Q(u+v)=1\}.                                  \label{eq:fibre}
\end{equation}
The two candidates are distinct whenever both occur.  Consequently the
numbers of targets of indegree \(0,1,2\) are
\begin{equation}
 d_0=d_2=\frac{N(N-1)}4,
 \qquad d_1=\frac{N(N+1)}2.                              \label{eq:fibrehist}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
An unknown preimage has the form \((x,u)\), with
\(v=x+Q(x)u\).  The alternatives \(Q(x)=0,1\) give exactly
\eqref{eq:fibre}, and substitution proves sufficiency.  Equality of the two
candidates would force \(u=0\), contradicting their simultaneous conditions
\(Q(v)=0\) and \(Q(v)=1\).

The change of variables \((u,v)\leftrightarrow(v,w=u+v)\) is a bijection.
A missing target has \(Q(v)=1,Q(w)=0\), while a double target has
\(Q(v)=0,Q(w)=1\).  Since the numbers of singular and nonsingular vectors are
\(N_0=(N+S)/2\) and \(N_1=(N-S)/2\), either class has size
\(N_0N_1=(N^2-S^2)/4=N(N-1)/4\).  Edge mass gives \(d_1\).
\end{proof}

To enumerate the temporal types, let
\[
 C_{abc}=\#\{(x,y):Q(x)=a,Q(y)=b,B(x,y)=c\}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[pair census]\label{lem:pairs}
The eight counts reduce to
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 H=C_{000}&=\frac{N(N+3S+4)}8,\\
 M=C_{010}=C_{100}=C_{110}&=\frac{N(N-S)}8,\\
 A=C_{001}=C_{011}=C_{101}&=\frac{N(N+S-2)}8,\\
 Z=C_{111}&=\frac{N(N-3S+2)}8.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:HMaz}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the three binary indicators gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 C_{abc}=\frac18[&N^2+(-1)^aNS+(-1)^bNS+(-1)^{a+b}S^2\\
 &+(-1)^cN\{1+(-1)^a+(-1)^b+(-1)^{a+b}S\}].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:walsh}
\end{equation}
Indeed, nondegeneracy gives the three mixed polar sums \(N,N,N\), while
\(Q(x)+Q(y)+B(x,y)=Q(x+y)\) gives the final sum \(NS\).  Now use \(S^2=N\).
This self-contained Walsh calculation is retained even though such static
quadratic counts belong to the classical owner region.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[image tower and exact layers]\label{thm:layers}
The second image is exactly the recurrent set, and
\begin{equation}
 |\im\Phi^0|=N^2,\qquad
 |\im\Phi|=\frac{N(3N+1)}4,\qquad
 |\im\Phi^t|=\frac{N(5N-S+4)}8\quad(t\ge2).              \label{eq:images}
\end{equation}
The numbers \(L_j\) of states of depth \(j\) are
\begin{equation}
 L_0=\frac{N(5N-S+4)}8,\qquad
 L_1=\frac{N(N-1)}4,\qquad
 L_2=\frac{N(N+S-2)}8.                                  \label{eq:layers}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Formula \eqref{eq:fibre} has values, on the four \((a,b)\)-columns,
\[
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
c&00&01&10&11\\\hline
0&1&1&2&0\\
1&2&0&1&1
\end{array}.
\]
Together with \cref{thm:clock}, this says that the first image is the
recurrent set plus type \((c,a,b)=(1,1,0)\).  That extra type maps to the
recurrent type \((1,0,0)\), while recurrent points have predecessors of every
iterate along their cycles.  Hence \(\im\Phi^2=\Rec(\Phi)\) setwise.
The first image size follows from \eqref{eq:fibrehist}; reading the transient
rows and substituting \eqref{eq:HMaz} gives
\(L_2=A\), \(L_1=M+A\), and the displayed formulas.
\end{proof}

\section{All components and the zeta function}

The reverse dynamics is complementary to the forward quotient: it determines
the trees attached to the cycles already classified in \cref{thm:clock}.

\begin{theorem}[six decorated component shapes]\label{thm:components}
Every connected component has exactly one of the following shapes.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.57\linewidth}r@{}}
\toprule
Shape & Number\\
\midrule
bare fixed point & \(N_0\)\\
bare 2-cycle & \((H-N_0)/2\)\\
2-cycle with one leaf at one cycle vertex & \(N_1\)\\
2-cycle with one length-two tail at each cycle vertex & \(A/2\)\\
bare 3-cycle & \(Z/3\)\\
4-cycle with one leaf at each of two alternating vertices & \((M-N_1)/2\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
There are no other reverse trees.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In \(c=0\), type \(00\) is the swap: its \(N_0\) diagonal states are fixed,
and the others form bare 2-cycles.  Types \(01\) and \(10\) form 2-cycles
when one coordinate is zero and 4-cycles otherwise.  The fibre table in the
proof of \cref{thm:layers} gives every type-10 cycle vertex one additional
type-11 leaf.  Thus the exceptional 2-cycles number \(N_1\); the remaining
mixed states give \((M-N_1)/2\) four-cycles, with leaves at alternating
vertices.

In \(c=1\), each type-00 state lies on a 2-cycle and has one extra type-10
predecessor; that predecessor has one type-01 predecessor.  This gives
\(A/2\) components with a length-two tail at both cycle vertices.  Type 11
gives \(Z/3\) bare 3-cycles.  The zero fibres of the leaf types and the global
indegree ceiling two exclude every deeper or branched tree.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[cycles and zeta]\label{cor:zeta}
If \(c_j\) is the number of cycles of exact length \(j\), then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 c_1&=\frac{N+S}{2},\\
 c_2&=\frac{N^2+2NS+3N-6S}{8},\\
 c_3&=\frac{N(N-3S+2)}{24},\\
 c_4&=\frac{N^2-NS-4N+4S}{16}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cycles}
\end{equation}
The finite-map zeta function is
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_\Phi(t)=
 (1-t)^{-c_1}(1-t^2)^{-c_2}
 (1-t^3)^{-c_3}(1-t^4)^{-c_4}.                          \label{eq:zeta}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Sum the corresponding rows of \cref{thm:components} and substitute
\eqref{eq:HMaz}.  The standard periodic-point definition and cycle product
of Artin--Mazur zeta functions \cite{ArtinMazur1965} give \eqref{eq:zeta}.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact controls and release boundary}

The paper-local standard-library verifier exhausts the zero-dimensional
boundary and both Witt signs for \(1\le m\le5\), hence every ordered pair
through dimension ten.  It checks the literal update, polarization, every
quotient transition, every pointwise depth and period, all target fibres,
all eight pair counts, the fibre histogram, image and layer formulas, and all
cycle counts.  It also constructs the second-image set and compares it
pointwise with \(\Rec(\Phi)\), then traverses every functional component,
canonically records the reverse rooted tree at each cycle vertex modulo
directed cyclic rotation only, and checks the six shapes and their counts.
An asymmetric sentinel prevents reflection identification.  The canonical
run makes \(27{,}405{,}887\) exact assertions.

Finite exhaustion proves none of the all-size arguments or owner statements.
The source search included the literal formula, state-dependent shear and
switch vocabulary, orthogonal and symplectic transvections, and bijective and
nonbijective Yang--Baxter pair maps.  No direct or conjugate-map owner was
located in this bounded pass.  Because \eqref{eq:map} is extremely short,
owner risk remains substantial: a direct prior classification would supersede
the residual claim.  There is no assertion of first occurrence, novelty, or
priority.  Public posting, submission, and external circulation remain on
hold.
