\begin{abstract}
Let a nonempty Dyck path have primitive factorisation
$P=C_1\cdots C_k$, with $C_1=UAD$.  We iterate the deterministic rule that
fixes $P$ when $k=1$ and otherwise replaces its first two components by
$UAC_2D$.  The complete orbit is explicit: for $0\le t\le k-1$, after $t$
updates the first component has absorbed $C_2,\ldots,C_{t+1}$, and the
endpoint is fixed thereafter.  Consequently the entry time is
exactly $k-1$, all recurrent paths are fixed and primitive, and the unique
path of maximum depth $n-1$ in semilength $n$ is $(UD)^n$.  The number of
paths at depth $k-1$ is
\[
                 \frac{k}{2n-k}\binom{2n-k}{n}.
\]
We also resolve every terminal basin by depth.  If a fixed target is $UQD$
and the interior $Q$ has $r$ primitive factors, then it has exactly one basin
state at each depth $0,\ldots,r$.  Hence its depth-fibre polynomial is
$1+u+\cdots+u^r$, and the unique largest terminal fibre has size $n$.
First-return decomposition, Catalan and ballot enumeration, ground-level
comb/Tamari covers, deterministic leftmost-rotation precedents, and the
ordered-tree graft/lift representation are treated as background inputs.  The
residual is only the exact temporal/target-fibre conjunction for this literal
selector.  No priority follows, and external circulation remains on hold.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

A local cover relation does not by itself determine a dynamical system: one
must also specify which available cover is chosen at each state.  This note
fixes a particularly rigid selector on Dyck paths.  At every nonfixed state,
we perform the reassociation at the leftmost return to ground level.  The
selected down-step crosses the next primitive Dyck component, and no other
part of the path changes.

The atomic reassociation belongs to the Tamari setting.  Associativity orders
and their lattice property go back to Huang and Tamari
\cite{HuangTamari1972Associativity}; path formulations of Tamari-type covers
also appear in the $m$-Tamari literature
\cite{BousquetMelouFusyPrevilleRatelle2012Intervals}.  Primitive decomposition
of Dyck words and enumeration by the number of primitive components are
likewise established topics \cite{PanayotopoulosSapounakis2002Prime}, while
Catalan and ballot enumeration are standard background
\cite{Stanley2015Catalan}.  We assign all of these ingredients zero
contribution credit.

Two closer rotation precedents further narrow the boundary.  Pallo studies a
uniquely selected leftmost left-rotation on binary trees and the resulting
rooted directed tree, rank, and distance
\cite[pp.~802--803]{Pallo2006Rotational}.  That deterministic map is not the
map below: after adjoining a loop at its terminal root it has one fixed tree,
whereas our map has $\Cat_{n-1}$ fixed paths, more than one for $n\geq3$.
Thus it is neither equal nor conjugate, by mirror or reversal, to our map.
Separately, Chapoton identifies the comb-order covers on Dyck paths as exactly
the Tamari covers whose moved subpath lies at height zero
\cite[\S1.2, p.~438]{Chapoton2020DyckOrder}, tracing the comb order to Pallo's
right-arm rotations \cite{Pallo2003RightArm}.  Every nonfixed update below is
one of those ground-level covers, chosen at the leftmost ground return.  The
idea of a deterministic leftmost scheduler and the ground-level cover
correspondence both receive zero contribution credit.

The purpose here is narrower: to record, for this particular selector, the
exact conjunction of its full temporal law with its target-indexed inverse
fibres.  None of the scheduler, its individual covers, its tree model, or its
component census is claimed separately.  The argument has four parts.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item We give a closed formula for every iterate.  It turns the number of
primitive factors into the pointwise clock $\tau(P)=\fac(P)-1$ and identifies
$(UD)^n$ as the unique depth-$n-1$ path.
\item We derive the complete temporal census: depth $k-1$ contains
$\frac{k}{2n-k}\binom{2n-k}{n}$ states.
\item For every fixed target and every feasible depth, we construct one basin
state and prove that no second state exists at that depth.
\item We derive the depth-fibre polynomial and classify the unique target of
largest fibre.
\end{enumerate}
The closed iterate in \cref{lem:closed-orbit} is the common mechanism: it
proves the clock and leaves only a suffix cut to invert an endpoint.  No figure
is needed because that formula exposes the whole state change.

This package is owner-thin.  The source boundary in
\cref{sec:ownership} is a conservative credit assignment, not a priority or
originality decision.  The record is maintained for internal theorem
evaluation under \textsc{hold\_external}.

\section{Dyck factors and the literal map}\label{sec:setup}

A \emph{Dyck path of semilength $n$} is a word in $U,D$ with $n$ occurrences
of each letter such that every prefix contains at least as many $U$'s as
$D$'s.  Let $\Dyck_n$ denote the set of such paths.  A nonempty Dyck path is
\emph{primitive} if its only return to height zero occurs after its final
step.  Cutting at all positive returns gives a unique factorisation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:primitive-factorisation}
                         P=C_1C_2\cdots C_k
\end{equation}
into primitive Dyck paths.  We write $\fac(P)=k$.

Every primitive first factor has a unique first-return form $C_1=UAD$ with
$A$ a possibly empty Dyck path.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:literal-map}
 \PhiMap_n(P)=
 \begin{cases}
 P,&k=1,\\
 UAC_2D\,C_3\cdots C_k,&k\geq2.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
We suppress the subscript $n$ when the semilength is fixed.  The word
$AC_2$ is a Dyck path, so $UAC_2D$ is primitive.  Thus
\eqref{eq:literal-map} lies in $\Dyck_n$ and is well defined.

There is a standard ordered-tree form of the same rule.  Under the contour
bijection between Dyck paths and rooted ordered plane trees
\cite[Theorem~1.5.1]{Stanley2015Catalan}, the primitive factors
$C_1,\ldots,C_k$ encode the ordered subtrees rooted at the $k$ children of the
tree root.  Write these subtrees as $T_1,\ldots,T_k$.  For $k\geq2$,
\eqref{eq:literal-map} is precisely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tree-graft}
 (T_1,T_2,T_3,\ldots,T_k)
 \longmapsto
 (T_1\mathbin{\triangleleft}T_2,T_3,\ldots,T_k),
\end{equation}
where $T_1\mathbin{\triangleleft}T_2$ appends $T_2$ as the rightmost child of
the root of $T_1$.  Thus the factor clock below is just root degree minus one.
This contour conjugacy and graft description are representation-level
background, not residual claims.

Let $\tau(P)$ be the least $t\geq0$ for which $\PhiMap^t(P)$ is fixed, and
put
\[
                         \End(P)=\PhiMap^{\tau(P)}(P).
\]
A path is \emph{recurrent} when it lies on a directed cycle of $\PhiMap_n$.
For a fixed target $T$, define its depth-fibre polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre-definition}
                 \Fib_T(u)=\sum_{P:\,\End(P)=T}u^{\tau(P)}.
\end{equation}

The complete claim package follows.  We use
$\Cat_m=\frac{1}{m+1}\binom{2m}{m}$.

\begin{theorem}[Clock, layers, and terminal depth fibres]\label{thm:main}
For every $n\geq1$, the map $\PhiMap_n$ has the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Every nonfixed update reduces $\fac$ by exactly one.  The recurrent
paths are exactly the fixed paths, these are exactly the primitive paths, and
there are $\Cat_{n-1}$ of them.
\item If $P$ has $k$ primitive factors, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pointwise-clock}
                             \tau(P)=k-1.
\end{equation}
The maximum entry time is $n-1$, attained uniquely by $(UD)^n$.
\item For $1\leq k\leq n$, the number of paths at depth $k-1$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ballot-layer}
 \bigl|\{P\in\Dyck_n:\tau(P)=k-1\}\bigr|
       =\frac{k}{2n-k}\binom{2n-k}{n}.
\end{equation}
\item Let $T=UQD$ be fixed, and factor its interior as
$Q=Q_1\cdots Q_r$ into primitive paths.  For every $0\leq d\leq r$, the
unique basin state of $T$ at depth $d$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-formula}
 P_d=\bigl(UQ_1\cdots Q_{r-d}D\bigr)
             Q_{r-d+1}\cdots Q_r,
\end{equation}
with the empty-prefix and empty-suffix conventions.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminal-polynomial}
                              \Fib_T(u)=1+u+\cdots+u^r.
\end{equation}
The unique terminal fibre of maximum size is the fibre over
\begin{equation}\label{eq:largest-target}
                         T_n^{\max}=U(UD)^{n-1}D,
\end{equation}
and it contains $n$ states.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section{The factor-count clock}\label{sec:clock}

The first primitive component absorbs exactly one following component per
round.  The next lemma makes that statement precise at every time.

\begin{lemma}[Closed orbit]\label{lem:closed-orbit}
Let $P=C_1\cdots C_k$, where $C_1=UAD$.  For every
$0\leq t\leq k-1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closed-orbit}
 \PhiMap^t(P)=UA\,C_2\cdots C_{t+1}D\,
                         C_{t+2}\cdots C_k.
\end{equation}
When $t=0$, the block $C_2\cdots C_{t+1}$ is empty; when $t=k-1$, the
suffix $C_{t+2}\cdots C_k$ is empty.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At $t=0$, \eqref{eq:closed-orbit} reads
$UADC_2\cdots C_k=P$.  Suppose the formula holds for some $t<k-1$.
The word
\[
                         UA\,C_2\cdots C_{t+1}D
\]
is primitive: after its initial $U$, the Dyck word
$AC_2\cdots C_{t+1}$ stays at or above height one, and the displayed $D$ is
its first return to height zero.  Hence the primitive factorisation of the
right-hand side of \eqref{eq:closed-orbit} consists of that first word,
followed by $C_{t+2},\ldots,C_k$.  Applying \eqref{eq:literal-map} moves the
closing $D$ across $C_{t+2}$ and gives
\[
          UA\,C_2\cdots C_{t+1}C_{t+2}D\,
                         C_{t+3}\cdots C_k,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:closed-orbit} at time $t+1$.  Induction proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Recurrent set and pointwise clock]\label{cor:clock}
The statements in \cref{thm:main}(i)--(ii) hold.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $t<k-1$, the factorisation displayed in the proof of
\cref{lem:closed-orbit} has one first component and
$k-t-1$ untouched components, for a total of $k-t$ primitive factors.  Thus
each nonfixed step decreases $\fac$ by one.  At $t=k-1$, the formula is the
single primitive path
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-formula}
                         \End(P)=UAC_2\cdots C_kD.
\end{equation}
It is fixed, so $\tau(P)=k-1$.

If $k=1$, the definition fixes $P$.  If $k>1$, the strict decrease of $\fac$
prevents $P$ from lying on a cycle.  Fixed and recurrent paths are therefore
exactly the primitive paths.  Deleting the first $U$ and last $D$ is a
bijection from primitive paths in $\Dyck_n$ to $\Dyck_{n-1}$, giving
$\Cat_{n-1}$ fixed paths.

Every primitive factor has semilength at least one, so $k\leq n$.  Equality
holds only when all $k$ factors have semilength one, and the only such factor
is $UD$.  In view of \eqref{eq:pointwise-clock}, the depth is at most $n-1$,
with equality exactly at $(UD)^n$.
\end{proof}

The proof gives more than a global bound: the component statistic is the
exact amount of time remaining at every state.  In particular, no transient
can merge with another transient of a different factor count before their
depths agree.

\section{Complete temporal layers}\label{sec:layers}

The clock reduces temporal enumeration to a component census.  We include the
coefficient calculation to fix the boundary case and normalisation, while
treating the enumerative machinery as background.

Let
\[
                 C(z)=\sum_{m\geq0}\Cat_m z^m
\]
be the Catalan series, which satisfies $C(z)=1+zC(z)^2$.  A primitive path is
$UAD$ with $A$ an arbitrary Dyck path, so the semilength generating function
for one primitive component is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:primitive-gf}
                              R(z)=zC(z).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Ballot layer formula]\label{prop:layers}
For $1\leq k\leq n$, the number of paths in $\Dyck_n$ with exactly $k$
primitive factors is the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:ballot-layer}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A path with $k$ primitive factors is an ordered sequence of $k$ primitive
paths.  By \eqref{eq:primitive-gf}, its number is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:power-coefficient}
                  [z^n]R(z)^k=[z^{n-k}]C(z)^k.
\end{equation}
Put $m=n-k$ and $W=C-1$.  Then $W=z(1+W)^2$.  If $m=0$, the coefficient in
\eqref{eq:power-coefficient} is $1$, which agrees with
$k(2n-k)^{-1}\binom{2n-k}{n}=1$ because $n=k$.

For $m\geq1$, Lagrange inversion gives
\begin{align*}
 [z^m]C(z)^k
  &=[z^m](1+W)^k\\
  &=\frac{1}{m}[w^{m-1}]k(1+w)^{k-1}(1+w)^{2m}\\
  &=\frac{k}{m}\binom{2m+k-1}{m-1}\\
  &=\frac{k}{2m+k}\binom{2m+k}{m}.
\end{align*}
Substituting $m=n-k$ and using binomial symmetry yields
\[
             \frac{k}{2n-k}\binom{2n-k}{n-k}
             =\frac{k}{2n-k}\binom{2n-k}{n},
\]
as required.
\end{proof}

Combining \cref{cor:clock,prop:layers} proves
\cref{thm:main}(iii).  It also gives the complete temporal polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:temporal-polynomial}
 \sum_{P\in\Dyck_n}u^{\tau(P)}
   =\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{k}{2n-k}\binom{2n-k}{n}u^{k-1}.
\end{equation}
Across all semilengths, the same sequence construction yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bivariate-gf}
 \sum_{n\geq1}\sum_{P\in\Dyck_n}u^{\tau(P)}z^n
                  =\frac{R(z)}{1-uR(z)}
                  =\frac{zC(z)}{1-uzC(z)}.
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:temporal-polynomial}--\eqref{eq:bivariate-gf} package the
clock after it has been proved; generic generating-function extraction is not
part of the residual theorem claim.

\section{The terminal depth-fibre atlas}\label{sec:fibres}

The endpoint formula \eqref{eq:endpoint-formula} absorbs every component after
the first into the interior of one primitive path.  Inverting it amounts to
choosing how many final primitive components of the target interior should be
moved back outside.

\begin{theorem}[One source at every feasible depth]\label{thm:fibres}
Let $T=UQD\in\Dyck_n$ be primitive, and let
$Q=Q_1\cdots Q_r$ be the primitive factorisation of its interior.  For each
$0\leq d\leq r$, the path $P_d$ in \eqref{eq:source-formula} is the unique
path satisfying
\[
                            \End(P_d)=T,
                    \qquad \tau(P_d)=d.
\]
There is no basin state of $T$ at any other depth.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $d\in\{0,\ldots,r\}$.  The word
\[
                         UQ_1\cdots Q_{r-d}D
\]
is primitive because its interior is a Dyck path.  The suffix
$Q_{r-d+1},\ldots,Q_r$ consists of $d$ primitive paths.  Hence $P_d$ has
exactly $d+1$ primitive factors.  By \cref{cor:clock}, its depth is $d$, and
the endpoint formula gives
\[
 \End(P_d)=UQ_1\cdots Q_{r-d}Q_{r-d+1}\cdots Q_rD=T.
\]
This proves existence.

For uniqueness, take any $P$ with $\End(P)=T$ and $\tau(P)=d$.  The clock
implies that $P$ has $d+1$ primitive factors, say
\[
                         P=B_1B_2\cdots B_{d+1},
                         \qquad B_1=UAD.
\]
Its endpoint is $UA B_2\cdots B_{d+1}D$.  Equality with $UQD$ implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:interior-split}
                         Q=A B_2\cdots B_{d+1}.
\end{equation}
Because $A$ is a Dyck path, the boundary immediately after $A$ is a return of
$Q$ unless $A$ is empty.  Each $B_i$ for $i\geq2$ is primitive.  Therefore
the unique factorisation of $Q$ into primitive paths forces
\[
 A=Q_1\cdots Q_{r-d},\qquad
 (B_2,\ldots,B_{d+1})=(Q_{r-d+1},\ldots,Q_r).
\]
Thus $P=P_d$.  The same argument shows that $d$ must lie between $0$ and $r$,
so no other depth occurs.
\end{proof}

The boundary cases are included in the construction.  If $r=0$, then $T=UD$
and only $d=0$ occurs.  For arbitrary $r$, the case $d=0$ gives $P_0=T$,
while $d=r$ gives $P_r=(UD)Q_1\cdots Q_r$.

In the ordered-tree language of \eqref{eq:tree-graft}, the fixed target has a
root with one child $S$, and $Q_1,\ldots,Q_r$ encode the ordered children of
$S$.  The source $P_d$ is obtained by lifting the last $d$ children of $S$,
in order, to become root-level siblings after $S$.  Hence the suffix-cut and
suffix-lift descriptions are the same elementary inverse operation; the
graft/lift representation itself receives zero contribution credit.

\begin{corollary}[Fibre polynomial and unique maximum]\label{cor:max-fibre}
Equations \eqref{eq:terminal-polynomial}--\eqref{eq:largest-target} hold.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:fibres}, the coefficient of $u^d$ in $\Fib_T(u)$ is one for
$0\leq d\leq r$ and zero otherwise.  This proves
\eqref{eq:terminal-polynomial}; in particular, the ordinary fibre size is
$\Fib_T(1)=r+1$.

The interior $Q$ has semilength $n-1$.  Since every $Q_i$ has positive
semilength, $r\leq n-1$, and hence $\Fib_T(1)\leq n$.  Equality forces all
$r=n-1$ factors to have semilength one.  The only primitive factor of
semilength one is $UD$, so equality occurs exactly when
$Q=(UD)^{n-1}$.  This gives the unique target \eqref{eq:largest-target}.
\end{proof}

The terminal atlas is pointwise: it identifies the source word, not merely the
number of sources.  It also records when each source enters the fixed set,
rather than collapsing all depths into one ordinary fibre count.

\section{Ownership boundary and conclusion}\label{sec:ownership}

The ownership subtraction is now explicit.  Huang--Tamari and the
$m$-Tamari path literature own the lattice and atomic cover background
\cite{HuangTamari1972Associativity,BousquetMelouFusyPrevilleRatelle2012Intervals}.
Pallo's right-arm rotations and Chapoton's Dyck-path comparison own the comb
order and its height-zero cover characterisation
\cite{Pallo2003RightArm,Chapoton2020DyckOrder}.  Pallo's later leftmost
left-rotation supplies a direct precedent for a unique deterministic
leftmost-rotation scheduler, a rooted rotation tree, a grading, and a distance
\cite{Pallo2006Rotational}.  Its unique terminal root separates that map from
$\PhiMap_n$, whose terminal set has size $\Cat_{n-1}$, but the general
leftmost-scheduling idea is not residual here.

Prime factorisation and component enumeration are also direct background
\cite{PanayotopoulosSapounakis2002Prime}.  The standard contour bijection
\cite{Stanley2015Catalan} turns the literal move into the root-child graft
\eqref{eq:tree-graft}, makes the clock root degree minus one, and turns the
inverse formula into a suffix lift.  We therefore assign zero separate credit
to the scheduler, atomic or ground-level rotations, primitive decomposition,
comb/height-zero correspondence, root-degree clock, graft/lift representation,
ballot layers, Catalan enumeration, and generic coefficient extraction.

After those deductions, the only retained internal residual is the exact
conjunction, for the particular map \eqref{eq:literal-map}, of the all-time
iterate formula with the targetwise assertion that every feasible depth has
one specified preimage.  Its polynomial and extremal consequences are kept
only as consequences of that conjunction.  This is a bookkeeping residual,
not a novelty or ownership claim: the bounded searches recorded in the source
ledger did not establish an owner, but a non-hit provides no priority evidence.
We do not classify other schedulers or arbitrary chains.  The manuscript
therefore remains owner-thin and anonymous under \textsc{hold\_external}.
