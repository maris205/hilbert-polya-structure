\section{Arithmetic prefixes}\label{sec:prefixes}

Let $[L]=\{1,\ldots,L\}$.  The global free-axis coordinates immediately
give the exact prefix law, including extension to a full point.

\begin{proposition}[Exact prefix count]\label{prop:prefix-count}
For every $L\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefix-count}
 \left|\proj_{[L]}(X_{a,b})\right|
 =q^{L-\lfloor L/(ab)\rfloor}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefix-dimension}
 \dim_{\F_q}\proj_{[L]}(X_{a,b})
 =L-\left\lfloor\frac{L}{ab}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The free indices in the prefix are
\[
 \cB\cap[L]=\{n\leq L:ab\nmid n\},
\]
whose cardinality is $L-\lfloor L/(ab)\rfloor$.  Every coordinate at most
$L$ is determined by these free values: if $n=r a^i b^j\leq L$, then the
three indices $r a^i$, $r b^j$, and $r$ in
\eqref{eq:inverse-map} are all at most $n$.

Conversely, every assignment on $\cB\cap[L]$ occurs in a global point.  Extend
it arbitrarily, for example by zero, to all of $\cB$ and apply
\Cref{prop:global-homeo}.  Restriction from the prefix pattern set to
$\cB\cap[L]$ is therefore a bijection.  Counting its assignments proves
\eqref{eq:prefix-count}.
\end{proof}

There is also an independent check of the rank of the internally visible
constraint matrix.  The identification of its kernel with the actual prefix
projection still uses the global extension established in
\Cref{prop:global-homeo}; the pivot computation below is not, by itself, an
extension theorem.  The constraints visible inside $[L]$ are indexed by
$1\leq n\leq\lfloor L/(ab)\rfloor$.  Each has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefix-row}
 x_n-x_{an}-x_{bn}+x_{abn}=0.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Triangular prefix pivots]\label{lem:prefix-pivots}
The rows \eqref{eq:prefix-row}, for
$n\leq\lfloor L/(ab)\rfloor$, are linearly independent over every field.
Their rank is $\lfloor L/(ab)\rfloor$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that a nontrivial linear combination vanishes and choose the largest
index $n$ with nonzero row coefficient.  The coordinate $abn$ appears in the
row indexed by $n$ with coefficient one.  If it appears in another row
indexed by $m$, then one of
\[
 m=abn,\qquad am=abn,\qquad bm=abn,\qquad abm=abn
\]
holds.  Apart from $m=n$ in the last equality, each possible $m$ is larger
than $n$.  All such row coefficients vanish by maximality.  The coefficient
of $x_{abn}$ in the linear combination is therefore the nonzero coefficient
of the $n$th row, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The pivot proof shows that the local constraint matrix has exactly one
independent row for each multiple of $ab$.  Together with the extension and
dimension results in \Cref{prop:global-homeo,prop:prefix-count}, this confirms
that there are no further prefix relations.  In the graph language it gives
the identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefix-cycle-rank}
 \beta([L])=\left\lfloor\frac{L}{ab}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Thus the same number is the codimension of the prefix projection and the total
root-wise cycle rank of its incidence graphs.

\begin{definition}[Arithmetic-prefix complexity]\label{def:prefix-complexity}
Define
\[
 h_{\mathrm{pref}}(X_{a,b})
 =\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac1L
 \log|\proj_{[L]}(X_{a,b})|.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:prefix-rate}
The limit in \Cref{def:prefix-complexity} exists and equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefix-rate}
 h_{\mathrm{pref}}(X_{a,b})
 =\left(1-\frac1{ab}\right)\log q.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Divide \eqref{eq:prefix-count} by $L$ on the logarithmic scale and let
$L\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Terminology firewall]\label{rem:prefix-not-entropy}
The sets $[L]$ are arithmetic intervals, not a declared F\o lner sequence for
the multiplicative semigroup acting by decimations.  Accordingly,
\eqref{eq:prefix-rate} is a prefix pattern-growth invariant.  No topological
or measure entropy for a multiplicative action is inferred from it.
\end{remark}
