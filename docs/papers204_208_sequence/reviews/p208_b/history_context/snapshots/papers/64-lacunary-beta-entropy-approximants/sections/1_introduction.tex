\section{Introduction}

The greedy $\beta$-expansion introduced by R\'enyi and characterized lexicographically by Parry turns the orbit of one into a complete symbolic boundary for the $\beta$-shift \cite{renyi_1957,parry_1960}.  When this expansion terminates, the boundary has a periodic quasi-greedy version and the associated shift is of finite type.  Truncating a nonterminating expansion therefore gives a natural route from a general $\beta$-shift to simple-Parry shifts.

Two parts of that route are not new.  Adamczewski and Bugeaud used binary expansions with separated ones in their study of self-lacunary bases, and their admissibility argument already exploits increasing gaps \cite[Section~4]{adamczewski_bugeaud_2007}.  Hichri and Smyth explicitly observed that truncating the expansion of one produces Parry numbers converging to a non-Parry limit \cite[Example~4.4]{hichri_smyth_2024}.  Thus neither the sparse construction, the truncation equation, nor qualitative convergence is claimed here.

The nearest verified sources above do not supply a finite quantitative account of the entropy lost at a truncation.  In the increasing-gap family, the perturbation has enough order to identify the contribution of the first discarded one while retaining rigorous control of the entire remaining tail.  This produces one compact principle: \emph{the first omitted digit fixes the exponential scale, and the later digits only perturb its coefficient at a controlled relative order}.

Our contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
    \item We derive an exact tail identity and a two-sided, non-asymptotic entropy-gap certificate; see \cref{thm:finite-certificate}.  A second certificate uses only rational inequalities involving finitely many positions and is directly machine-checkable; see \cref{prop:rational-certificate}.
    \item We prove the first-omitted-digit asymptotic in \cref{thm:asymptotic}.  In particular,
    \[
    \frac{-\log(\htop(\Xb)-\htop(\Xbj))}{n_{j+1}}\longrightarrow\log\beta.
    \]
    The derivative normalization is explicit: the denominator is $\sum_k n_k\beta^{-n_k}=\beta D_\beta$ when $D_\beta=-F'(\beta)$.
    \item We separate rigidity from flexibility.  Strict gap growth forces at least a quadratic exponent in $j$, ruling out arbitrary slow or arbitrary exact schedules.  Conversely, every positive upper envelope can be met by a recursive gap choice; see \cref{thm:schedules}.
\end{itemize}

The hypotheses are deliberately narrow.  We do not treat arbitrary digit alphabets, nonmonotone gaps, negative $\beta$, or perturbations that change earlier digits.  We also make no arithmetic claim about the limiting base: the present conclusions concern symbolic admissibility and entropy, not algebraicity or transcendence.

\paragraph{Organization.}
\Cref{sec:setup} separates the finite greedy expansion from its periodic ceiling.  \Cref{sec:certificates} proves the finite certificates, \cref{sec:asymptotics} proves the sharp asymptotic, and \cref{sec:schedules} records the exact scope of schedule control.  \Cref{sec:computation} gives a fully reproducible triangular-position control.
