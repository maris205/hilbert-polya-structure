\section{The map and the question}
\label{sec:setup}

Nilpotence forces many finite algebraic systems to lose information, but it
does not determine when both registers of a coupled recurrence vanish.
For the map studied here, a leading-term cancellation can erase one
register earlier than a linear model predicts. The exact full-state
deadline nevertheless agrees with that model. Our purpose is to prove this
agreement on the full carrier, including the cancellation locus, and to
distinguish it from a linearization of the dynamics.

Throughout, $q$ is a prime power, $m\geq2$, and
\begin{equation}
 R=\Fq[t]/(t^m),\qquad I=tR,\qquad
 F:I^2\longrightarrow I^2,\quad F(x,y)=(y,x(t+y)).
 \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
The symbol $t$ denotes its residue class in $R$. For a nonzero element
$z$, let $v(z)$ be the least degree of its nonzero coefficients, and put
$v(0)=m$. Coefficient multiplication and counting give
\begin{equation}
 v(zw)=\min\{m,v(z)+v(w)\},\qquad
 |t^rR|=q^{m-r}\quad(0\leq r\leq m).
 \label{eq:ideals}
\end{equation}
An element with nonzero constant coefficient is a unit: factoring out that
coefficient reduces its inverse to a finite geometric series. In particular,
$v(t+z)=1$ whenever $z\in t^2R$.

For $s\in I^2$, define its depth by
$\tau(s)=\min\{n\in\Nzero:F^n(s)=(0,0)\}$, initially allowing infinity.
A state is recurrent if some positive iterate returns to it. We prove that
all depths are finite and that
\begin{equation}
 \tau(x,y)=\max\{2(m-v(y)),\,2(m-v(x))-1\}.
 \label{eq:preview}
\end{equation}
The associated depth count and the full target-fibre count follow from
different calculations: the first uses parity chains, the second uses the
image and kernel of multiplication on $I$.

Endomorphism dynamics provide a useful comparison class: their finite state
graphs have structural restrictions coming from the group operation
\cite{Bors2017}. The present map is not assumed to be an endomorphism.
Multiplicative feedback also occurs in the complex Fibonacci maps
$(x,y)\mapsto(xy+c,x)$ \cite{ElAbdalaouiEtAl2016}. At $c=0$, interchanging
coordinates gives the polynomial $(x,y)\mapsto(y,xy)$. This is background
for the one-step multiplication relation, not a transfer of complex dynamics
to the nilpotent ideal in~\eqref{eq:map}.

The contribution is the cancellation-safe temporal theorem for this
explicit nonlinear map. Neither the resulting numerical clock shape nor
the general valuation-contraction method is asserted to be new. We evaluate
the one-step inverse completely while explicitly deducting its multiplication
mechanism in Section~\ref{sec:controls}. Equal depth distributions will not
be confused with conjugate state graphs.
