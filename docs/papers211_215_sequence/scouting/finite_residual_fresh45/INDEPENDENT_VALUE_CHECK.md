## Mathematical findings

1. **The carrier and nonbijectivity are genuine.** On
   `Av_n(321)`, decreasing runs have length at most two. Reversing them
   removes only those pairwise inversions, so it cannot create a decreasing
   triple. For `n >= 2`, both the identity and `213...n` map to the identity.
   For `n = 1` the singleton boundary is bijective, as expected.

2. **The threshold equality is a real strengthening of a comparison.**
   An adjacent threshold `10` must be a descent. Two permutation descents
   cannot overlap. Consequently each threshold projection evolves by
   simultaneous `10 -> 01`, with equality, throughout the orbit. Taking
   all thresholds detects the identity exactly. This part must not be
   dismissed by citing only an unrestricted sorting bound.

3. **The clock evaluation is consistent and complete.** For a binary
   threshold with zero positions `p_j`, let `a_j=p_j-j`. Crossing zero
   `j` with one `r` has predecessors `(j,r+1)` and `(j-1,r)` when those
   cells exist. Their longest-path recurrence yields
   `T(j,r)=max_{i<=j, a_i>=r}(a_i-r+1+j-i)`.
   In particular the final crossing time is
   `max_{i:a_i>0}(p_i+k-2i)`. Empty crossing sets give zero.
   Maximizing over thresholds yields A1, including its zero-time case;
   `p_{k,j} <= n-k+j` gives the bound, and `23...n1` attains it.
   This is more information than a worst-case bound, although its proof
   is a binary crossing-schedule calculation after the exact projection.

4. **The inverse encoding has no detected missing compatibility condition.**
   A predecessor consists of disjoint swaps of target ascents. A new
   decreasing triple must contain one newly inverted pair and an exterior
   value. The prefix-maximum/suffix-minimum test defining `E` excludes
   precisely this event; other disjoint swaps preserve those exterior
   value sets. At an old target descent, selecting a neighbouring eligible
   edge removes the unwanted descent. Selecting neither leaves it behind.
   This proves the independent-set plus descent-coverage characterization,
   including simultaneous choices on both sides of a descent.

5. **The fibre sum is evaluated, not just a decoder.** Inclusion–exclusion
   removes neighbours of each selected uncovered descent. The remaining
   induced path splits into components, each counted by a Fibonacci
   number. This yields every target count, including zero, with no hidden
   recurrence or unspecified matrix power. The identity admits every
   independent set of the full path. A nonidentity omits at least the
   singleton at one descent index, proving the unique maximum `F_(n+1)`.
   The `n=1` boundary gives one. The hand example `1324` has exactly the
   three listed predecessor encodings; no computation was run.

These are two distinct mathematical axes. It would be incorrect to reject
the desk for supplying only a potential, only a maximum fibre, or an
unevaluated inverse description. No substantive proof correction is
required by this check; a manuscript would benefit from displaying the
crossing recurrence explicitly before its solution.

These deductions are useful and correct. Standard methods can certainly
support a substantive theorem, so their names alone do not justify a

removes the difficult interaction in its axis, leaving a short adapter to
the already close mechanism. The two resulting formulas are juxtaposed;
no further structural relation between sorting layers and fibres, new
distribution, or obstruction resolved by their combination is established.
Such an additional theorem is not an absolute project requirement, but
its absence matters when the present residual is this small and the
literal sits inside a mature archived owner family.

The sharp worst time deserves no independent credit beyond the classical
bound plus its elementary witness. The maximum fibre likewise follows
immediately once the path encoding is available: the identity supplies
all disjoint singleton/pair blocks. Thus these extremal corollaries do not
enlarge the residual enough to change the value assessment.
