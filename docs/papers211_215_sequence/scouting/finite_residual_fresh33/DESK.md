## 1. Simultaneous simplicial-vertex deletion

For fixed n >= 1, the finite carrier consists of simple graphs on arbitrary
subsets V of [n], including the empty graph. Define

    S(G) = {v in V : the open neighborhood N_G(v) is a clique},
    T(G) = G[V minus S(G)].

Empty neighborhoods count as cliques. Labels survive unchanged; there is
no external schedule or varying ground-set parameter. The empty graph and
each singleton have the same image, so this map is nonbijective.

Collision first: the original S10 section at lines 524–560 of
`docs/papers147_151_sequence/scouting/stochastic/SCOUT.md` was read.
It already uses simplicial deletion on clique windmills, but randomly and
with stopping at a complete graph. This is NOT an exact literal duplicate:
the current update deletes all simplicial vertices and deletes cliques too.
Its perfect-elimination and pruning proof engines are nevertheless occupied.
The original lines 65–85 of
`docs/papers107_111_sequence/scouting/COMBINATORIAL_SCOUT.md` expressly
exclude repeated leaf/core/dominated-vertex stripping as a consumed mechanism.
Other simplicial-collapse, local-pruning and fresh27 hits were navigation
only, not full original reads or claimed exact adapters.

Temporal assessment: vertex count falls at each nonfixed step; recurrent
states are exactly graphs with no simplicial vertex. This is generic
deletion, not a residual theorem. On the chordal subcarrier, elimination
theory yields the unique empty recurrent state. Merely packaging its
parallel rounds, or restricting to paths where the rounds are leaf peeling,
does not clear the temporal contribution boundary.

Independent inverse assessment: the empty target is characterized by all
vertices being simplicial, equivalently a disjoint union of cliques. This
is standard partition/cluster-graph enumeration, not an all-target inverse
theorem. For nonempty targets the added vertices must be simplicial while
every surviving vertex must fail that predicate in the predecessor;
enumerating such attachments is an unevaluated constraint, not a fibre
formula or extremal result. No uniform evaluated all-target mechanism was
obtained. Stop without expanding to graph enumeration.

Direct primary source: Habib and Limouzy,
[On some simplicial elimination schemes for chordal graphs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0901.2645),
arXiv v2, 17 February 2009. Browser-decoded body lines 67–125 give the
simplicial/elimination definitions and chordal equivalences; lines 427–449
give clique-tree leaf pruning. These passages delimit the existing
elimination mechanism. They do not establish the exact synchronous map's
full fibres or its sharp clock. The PDF extraction has broken typography;
no whole-paper proof audit or source-byte capture is claimed.

Decision: `KILL_GENERIC_PRUNING / INVERSE_UNEVALUATED`, author assessment.

## 2. Fixed-element Engel map on finite nilpotent groups

For a fixed finite nilpotent group G of class c and fixed g in G, define

    E_g(x) = x^{-1} g^{-1} x g,       x in G.

For nontrivial G this is nonbijective: the nontrivial center lies in the
identity fibre. This is a genuine finite autonomous family, but not a fresh
literal mechanism. Local search immediately found P119 and fresh10.
P119 `papers/119-regular-engel-unitriangular-dynamics/main.tex`, lines
60–145 and 210–250, was read directly. Its introduction already subtracts
nilpotent descent and fixed-second-variable Engel iteration; its actual
proof shows that nonempty fibres are left fixed-point/centralizer cosets.
The complete fresh10 `SOURCES_AND_LIMITS.md` was also read; it closes an
involution specialization on exactly this inverse-mechanism issue.

Temporal assessment: iteration descends the lower central series and
reaches the identity within c steps. A sharp general clock or exact depth
distribution is not supplied by this upper bound. P119 already contains a
much more precise regular-unitriangular filtration result. Enlarging to
arbitrary nilpotent groups weakens that conclusion rather than creates one.

Inverse assessment, separately: every nonempty E_g fibre is a left coset
of C_G(g), hence has size |C_G(g)|. This is the same actual algebraic proof
read in P119, not a new second axis. No new image-membership classification
or restricted-layer fibre theorem is supplied here.

Primary-source boundary: Bier's 2013 *Linear Algebra and its Applications*
article, [On solvability of Engel equations in the group of triangular
matrices over a field](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2012.10.009), was located
in the author's university publication record (volume 438(5), 2320–2330).
The DOI body request returned Internal Error. Thus this desk does NOT claim
to have newly read Bier's theorem; the exact local P119 proof is the
collision evidence, and Bier remains a direct-source body gap in this desk.
No source theorem is extended from regular triangular elements to arbitrary G.

Decision: `KILL_OLD_ENGEL_AND_COSET_MECHANISMS`, author assessment.
