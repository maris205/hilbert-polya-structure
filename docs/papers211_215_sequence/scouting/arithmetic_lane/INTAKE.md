## Literal 1: minimum-pile repacking (MPR)

For each integer N >= 0, the carrier is the finite set of integer partitions
of N, represented as weakly increasing positive tuples. The empty partition
is fixed. For a nonempty tuple x of length k, let a=min(x). Replace it by
the sorted positive multiset consisting of all x_i-a>0 and one new part k*a.
The new parts have total N. A one-part partition is fixed.

The potential number of parts never increases. The actual question is the
all-N recurrent dynamics on constant-part-count strata, not this elementary
potential. A full inverse can select the new part k*a in the target, but a
bare selection-and-reversal formula will not count as a second substantive
axis. No scientific pilot is authorized for MPR unless it first survives
literal/internal and primary-source checks.

## Literal 2: second-positive-order-statistic remainders (SPR)

For n>=1 and M>=0 the carrier is {0,...,M}^n with fixed labelled coordinates.
If x has at most one positive entry, fix x. Otherwise let p be the second
smallest positive entry, counting multiplicity. Simultaneously replace every
coordinate by its ordinary nonnegative remainder modulo p. The output lies
in the same carrier. This is not a pairwise or cyclic-neighbour Euclidean
step; the selected divisor coordinate is discarded, not retained.

The positive support strictly shrinks until at most one coordinate remains.
That generic n-step potential alone earns no temporal credit. A qualifying
contract would require a sharp joint n,M clock or comparably rigid nontrivial
orbit structure and a separate all-target inverse/extremal mechanism.
