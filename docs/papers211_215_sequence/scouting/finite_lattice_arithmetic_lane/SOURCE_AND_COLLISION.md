## QRM is not FCSR

The actual old originals were read before using their claims:

- `docs/papers204_208_sequence/scouting/finite_algebra_ninth/INTAKE.md`,
  complete intake, including the QRM carrier and formula.
- `docs/papers204_208_sequence/scouting/finite_algebra_ninth/PROOF_AND_ADAPTER_NOTES.md`,
  Section 5, including both the range-loss proof and square-sum inverse
  encoding. Other proof sections are not claimed as completely read.
- `docs/papers204_208_sequence/scouting/finite_algebra_ninth/SOURCE_AND_COLLISION.md`,
  complete source-boundary document. Its cited external RMS article was
  not newly read here and supplies no new direct-owner assertion.

For QRM, the carrier is `{0,...,M}^n`. At `n>=2` each coordinate becomes
the floor of the square root of the mean of the **other coordinates'
squares**; `n=1` is identity. For `n=2`, this is exactly the coordinate
swap, not a complex root. In particular `(0,1)` lies on a two-cycle when
`M>=1`. FCSR instead sends `(0,1)` to `(0,0)` and has no two-cycle. These
same-carrier maps are not conjugate. No statement about arbitrary factors
or about a conjugacy to a different-dimensional QRM is made.

At `n>=3`, the old QRM proof gives generic extremal-mean contraction and
an unevaluated square-sum coefficient expression. FCSR uses squared-norm
contraction and a quantization-cell inverse. These are both familiar
contraction/counting primitives, but not literally the same dynamics.
The old source's negative disposition is preserved, not repurposed as an
exact ownership theorem for the new literal.

The three complete files are physically copied under
`evidence01/originals/` and compared as raw bytes. This is documentary
capture, not scientific-code reuse or a new old-pilot execution.

## Direct source bodies actually read

[How to compute the square root of a complex number](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2020/06/09/complex-square-root/),
dated 9 June 2020, explicitly displays the modulus and half-sum/half-
difference coordinate formulas used before rounding in FCSR. The entire
main article was read, including its floating-point warning. It confirms
only this elementary algebraic primitive. It is not a peer-reviewed FCSR
dynamics article, a priority certificate, or a source for our claimed
finite time, basin or inverse statements. No floating arithmetic was used.

The linked authored page,
[Square roots of Gaussian integers](https://www.johndcook.com/blog/2020/06/09/square-root-gaussian-integer/),
was also read as discovery context. It concerns exact integral roots and
Pythagorean examples, not rounding dynamics. Its prose contains the
displayed `50+130i`/`50+120i` example inconsistency, so that example and
its broader Pythagorean wording are not used as theorem premises. The
actual browser bodies of both pages are preserved separately; no silently
corrected source quotation is substituted.
