L37: ## Abstract
L38: We continue the study of permutations of a fi nite regular semigroup that map each element to one of its inverses, providing a complete description in the case of semigroups whose idempotent generated subsemigroup is a union of groups. We show, in two ways, how to construct an involution matching on the semigroup of all transformations which either preserve or reverse orientation of a cycle.
L39: Finally, as an application, we use involution matchings to prove that when the base set has at least four members, a fi nite full transformation semigroup has no cover by inverse subsemigroups that is closed under intersection.

L39:   * cite16†Explore all metrics L40: ## Abstract
L41: With every reduced E-Fountain semigroup S which satisfies the generalized right ample condition we associate a category with zero morphisms \(\mathcal {C}(S)\). Under some assumptions we prove an isomorphism of \(\Bbbk \)-algebras \(\Bbbk S\simeq \Bbbk _{0}\mathcal {C}(S)\) between the semigroup algebra and the contracted category algebra where \(\Bbbk \) is any commutative unital ring.
L42: This is a simultaneous generalization of a former result of the author on reduced E-Fountain semigroups which satisfy the congruence condition, a result of Junying Guo and Xiaojiang Guo on strict right ample semigroups and a result of Benjamin Steinberg on idempotent semigroups with central idempotents. The applicability of the new isomorphism is demonstrated with two well-known monoids which are not members of the above classes.
L43: The monoid of order-preserving functions on an n-set and the monoid of binary relations with demonic composition.

L63: ## 1 Introduction
L64: Given a semigroup S and a commutative ring \(\Bbbk \), it is of interest to study the semigroup algebra \(\Bbbk S\). One approach is to associate with S a category \(\mathcal {C}(S)\) such that the semigroup algebra \(\Bbbk S\) is isomorphic to the category algebra \(\Bbbk \mathcal {C}(S)\). It is usually easier to study the category algebra because many of the natural basis elements are orthogonal.
L65: A pioneering work in this direction was done by Solomon [cite23†20 ] who proved that the semigroup algebra of a finite semilattice is isomorphic to the algebra of a discrete category. This was generalized to finite inverse semigroups by Steinberg [cite24†28 ]. From the ESN theorem [cite25†16 , Theorem 4.1.8] we know that every inverse semigroup S is associated with an inductive groupoid G(S).
L66: Steinberg proved that if S is finite then \(\Bbbk S\simeq \Bbbk G(S)\) and this result is fundamental in the representation theory of finite inverse semigroups [cite26†29 , Chapter 9]. Guo and Chen [cite27†11 ] obtained a similar result for finite ample semigroups. Motivated by questions on self-injective and Frobenius semigroup algebras, Guo and Guo [cite28†10 ] generalized this result to a class of semigroup they call strict right ample semigroups.
L67: Taking a different direction, the author extended the result of Guo and Chen on finite ample semigroups to a class of right restriction E-Ehresmann semigroups [cite29†22 , cite30†23 ] (see also [cite31†35 ]) and later to a wider class of reduced E-Fountain semigroups [cite32†26 ]. Let S be a semigroup and let E be a distinguished subset of idempotents \(E\subseteq S\) with the property that \(ef=e\iff fe=e\) for \(e,f\in E\).
L68: The semigroup S is called a reduced E-Fountain semigroup if for every element \(a\in S\) the sets of its right and left identities from E have minimum elements (with respect to the natural partial order on idempotents). The minimal right identity of a from E is denoted \(a^{*}\) and the minimal left identity of a from E is denoted \(a^{+}\).
L69: Reduced E-Fountain semigroups form a wide class that contains: inverse semigroups, \({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {J}}\,}}\)-trivial semigroups and many other types of semigroups. If S is reduced E-Fountain then we can define a graph \(\mathcal {C_{\bullet }}(S)\) whose objects are the idempotents of E and every \(a\in S\) corresponds to a morphism C(a) from \(a^{*}\) to \(a^{+}\).
L70: If S satisfies the congruence condition (the identities \((ab)^{*}=(a^{*}b)^{*}\) and \((ab)^{+}=(ab^{+})^{+}\)) then \(\mathcal {\mathcal {C_{\bullet }}}(S)\) becomes a category when we define \(C(b)C(a)=C(ba)\) if \(b^{*}=a^{+}\). If, in addition, S satisfies some further conditions that will be described below then it is proved in [cite32†26 , Theorem 4.4] that \(\Bbbk S\simeq \Bbbk \mathcal {C_{\bullet }}(S)\) for any commutative unital ring \(\Bbbk \).
L71: This result has led to several applications regarding quivers [cite33†21 , cite34†25 ], global-dimension [cite35†24 ] and projective modules [cite36†18 ] for algebras of semigroups of partial functions. A special case of this result is the neat proof that the algebra of the Catalan monoid is an incidence algebra [cite37†19 ] and there are also applications to the study of certain partition monoids [cite38†4 ].
L72: Recently, as part of a detailed study of semigroup determinants [cite39†30 ], Steinberg proved a similar result for the class of finite idempotent semigroups with central idempotents. These are finite semigroups that satisfy \(S^{2}=S\) and \(ea=ae\) for every element \(a\in S\) and idempotent \(e\in S\). Such a semigroup S is reduced E-Fountain if we take E to be the set of all idempotents of S.
L73: In this case the associated graph \(\mathcal {C}_{\bullet }(S)\) contains only endomorphisms and despite not being a category it is possible to add zero morphisms and turn it into a category with zero morphisms. Then, the semigroup algebra is isomorphic to the contracted algebra of the category with zero morphisms [cite39†30 , Theorem 6.5].
L74: The goal of this paper is to achieve a simultaneous generalization for our result on reduced E-Fountain semigroups that satisfy the congruence condition, Guo and Guo’s result on strict right ample semigroups and Steinberg’s result on idempotent semigroups with central idempotents. In Sect. cite40†2 we give some required preliminaries. In Sect.
L75: cite41†3 we consider the generalized right ample condition, discussed in [cite32†26 , cite42†27 ] and show that it is equivalent to the condition \((ba)^{*}=a^{*}\implies (ba^{+})^{*}=a^{+}\) for every \(a,b\in S\). If a reduced E-Fountain semigroup S satisfies the generalized right ample condition, then we can add zero morphisms to \(\mathcal {C_{\bullet }}(S)\) and obtain a new graph \(\mathcal {C}(S)\).
L76: We define composition \(C(b)\cdot C(a)\) of two non-zero morphisms C(b), C(a) with \(b^{*}=a^{+}\) to be equal to C(ba) if \((ba)^{*}=b^{*},\quad (ba)^{+}=a^{+}\) and 0 otherwise. We prove that this endows \(\mathcal {C}(S)\) with a structure of a category with zero morphisms. We define a relation \(\trianglelefteq _{l}\) on S by the rule that \(a\trianglelefteq _{l}b\) if \(a=be\) for an \(e\in E\). The main theorem of Sect.
L77: cite41†3 shows that if a reduced E-Fountain semigroup S satisfies the generalized right ample condition and \(\trianglelefteq _{l}\) is contained in a principally finite partial order then \(\Bbbk S\simeq \Bbbk _{0}\mathcal {C}(S)\) for every commutative unital ring \(\Bbbk \) (where \(\Bbbk _{0}\mathcal {C}(S)\) is the contracted algebra of \(\mathcal {C}(S)\)). In Sect. cite43†4 we explain why this is a generalization of the three above-mentioned results of the author, Guo and Guo, and Steinberg.
L78: In Sect. cite44†5 we show that the above isomorphism applies to two additional examples which are not included in any of the three older results: The monoid \({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {O}}\,}}_{n}\) of all order-preserving functions on an n-element set (see [cite45†5 , Chapter 14] and references therein) and the monoid of binary relations with demonic composition, whose origins are in computer science [cite46†1 ].
L79: ## 2 Preliminaries
L80: ### 2.1 Semigroups and categories
L81: 
L82: Let S be a semigroup and let \(S^{1}=S\cup \{1\}\) be the monoid formed by adjoining a formal unit element. Recall that Green’s preorders \(\le _{{{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {R}}\,}}}\), \(\le _{{{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {L}}\,}}}\) and \(\le _{{{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {J}}\,}}}\) are defined by:
L83: $$\begin{aligned} a\,\le _{{{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {R}}\,}}}\,b\iff&aS^{1}\subseteq bS^{1}\\ a\,\le _{{{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {L}}\,}}}\,b\iff&S^{1}a\subseteq S^{1}b\\ a\,\le _{{{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {J}}\,}}}\,b\iff&S^{1}aS^{1}\subseteq S^{1}bS^{1}. \end{aligned}$$
L84: The associated Green’s equivalence relations on S are denoted by \({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {R}}\,}}\), \({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {L}}\,}}\) and \({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {J}}\,}}\). Additional basic notions and proofs of semigroup theory can be found in standard textbooks such as [cite47†12 ].
L85: We denote by E(S) the set of idempotents of the semigroup S. The natural partial order on E(S) is defined by \(e\le f\iff (ef=e=fe)\) and note that \(\le \,=\,\le _{{{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {R}}\,}}}\cap \le _{{{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {L}}\,}}}\). For every subset of idempotents \(E\subseteq E(S)\) and every \(a\in S\) we denote by \(a_{E}\) the set of right identities of a from E:
L86: 
L87: $$ a_{E}=\{e\in E\mid ae=a\} $$
L88: Dually \({}_{E}{a}\) is the set of left identities from E. We define two equivalence relations \(\widetilde{\mathcal {L}}_E\) and \(\widetilde{\mathcal {R}}_E\) on S by
L89: 
L90: $$ a\,\widetilde{\mathcal {L}}_E\,b\iff (a_{E}=b_{E}) $$
L91: 
L92: $$ a\,\widetilde{\mathcal {R}}_E\,b\iff ( _{E}{a}= _{E}{b}). $$
L93: These relations are one type of “generalized” Green’s relations (see [cite48†7 ]). Note that \({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {L}}\,}}\subseteq \widetilde{\mathcal {L}}_E\) and \({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {R}}\,}}\subseteq \widetilde{\mathcal {R}}_E\).
L94: ### Definition 2.1
L95: 
L96: The semigroup S is called E-Fountain if every \(\widetilde{\mathcal {L}}_E\)-class contains an idempotent from E and every \(\widetilde{\mathcal {R}}_E\)-class contains an idempotent from E.
L97: 
L98: We remark that this property is also called “E-semiabundant” in the literature.
L99: ### Definition 2.2
L100: 
L101: An E-Fountain semigroup S is called reduced if
L102: 
L103: $$ {ef=e\iff fe=e} $$
L104: 
L105: for every \(e,f\in E\). Equivalently, S is reduced if \(\le _{{{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {L}}\,}}}=\le _{{{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {R}}\,}}}=\le \) when restricted to the set E.
L106: 
L107: A reduced E-Fountain semigroup is called a “DR semigroup” in [cite49†31 ].
L108: Let S be a semigroup and let \(E\subseteq S\) be a subset of idempotents which satisfies \({ef=e\iff fe=e}\) for every \(e,f\in E\). Then every \(\widetilde{\mathcal {L}}_E\)-class (\(\widetilde{\mathcal {R}}_E\)-class) contains at most one idempotent from E. The \(\widetilde{\mathcal {L}}_E\)-class (\(\widetilde{\mathcal {R}}_E\)-class) of \(a\in S\) contains an element of E if and only if the set \(a_{E}\) (respectively, \({}_{E}{a}\)) contains a minimum element.
L109: If S is a reduced E-Fountain semigroup, the unique idempotent from E in the \(\widetilde{\mathcal {L}}_E\)-class (\(\widetilde{\mathcal {R}}_E\)-class) of \(a\in S\) is denoted \(a^{*}\) (respectively, \(a^{+}\)). We emphasize that if \(e\in E\) satisfies \(ae=a\) then \(ea^{*}=a^{*}e=a^{*}\) and dually if \(e\in E\) satisfies \(ea=a\) then \(ea^{+}=a^{+}e=a^{+}\). See [cite49†31 ] for proofs and additional details.
L110: 
L111: The following simple observation will be very useful.
L112: ### Lemma 2.3
L113: 
L114: Let S be a reduced E-Fountain semigroup and let \(a,b\in S\). Then \((ba)^{*}\le a^{*}\) and \((ba)^{+}\le b^{+}\).
L115: ### Proof
L116: 
L117: The equality \(baa^{*}=ba\) implies \((ba)^{*}a^{*}=(ba)^{*}\) so \((ba)^{*}\le a^{*}\). Proving \((ba)^{+}\le b^{+}\) is dual. \(\square \)
L118: 
L119: Green’s relation \({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {L}}\,}}\) (\({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {R}}\,}}\)) is always a right congruence (respectively, left congruence), but this is not the case with the generalized Green’s relations \(\widetilde{\mathcal {L}}_E\) and \(\widetilde{\mathcal {R}}_E\).
