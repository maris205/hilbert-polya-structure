## Concrete subtraction, not a new slate

### 1. Concurrent mutually best blocking edges are already source-native

The exact comparison carrier is all matchings of a fixed finite labelled
simple graph $G=(V,E)$, with a fixed injective positive benefit
$b:E\to\mathbb R_{>0}$. An absent edge is blocking if each endpoint is
unmatched or currently has a lower-benefit partner. Every vertex chooses
its highest-benefit incident blocking edge, if any. Let $Q(M)$ be the edges
chosen at both endpoints. The simultaneous update removes all old matching
edges incident with $Q(M)$ and inserts $Q(M)$; all other old edges remain.
The set $Q(M)$ is a matching, because each vertex chooses at most one edge.
There is no external clock or randomness under injective benefits.

This is a literal specialization of the concurrent best-response law in
Martin Hoefer, *Local Matching Dynamics in Social Networks*,
[author-hosted primary manuscript](https://algo.rwth-aachen.de/team/hoefer/papers/Hoefer11Matching.pdf),
printed pp. 4–5, Section 2 and the opening of Section 3; journal version:
*Information and Computation* 222 (2013), 20–35,
[publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ic.2012.10.005).
Set capacity $k=1$ and take all social links, so accessibility is automatic.
With distinct benefits, each local best-response choice is a singleton;
the source's random tie convention then vanishes. Its lexicographic
potential applies to the same concurrent moves. No exact full-carrier
sharp clock or all-target inverse is extracted from this source, and no
claim is made that its exponential lower-bound construction specializes
to complete social access. This is direct rule ownership, not merely a
shared phrase or a false identification with GBPR's perfect-state repair.

Result: a serial-to-parallel wording change does not open a new mechanism.
No independent residual clock plus inverse theorem was found here. The
known rule was therefore not nominated or given a fresh acronym.

### 2. Symmetric conflict veto is a NOR/polarity control, not matching dynamics

Let $H=(W,F)$ be a fixed finite loopless labelled conflict graph. Its exact
source-class carrier is $\{0,1\}^W$, equivalently $2^W$, and its update is

$$
R(S)=W\setminus N_H(S),\qquad
R_i(x)=\bigwedge_{j\in N_H(i)}\neg x_j.
$$

The empty conjunction is one. For matching conflicts, $W=E(G)$ and
$H=L(G)$ is the line graph, with distinct edges adjacent precisely when
they share a vertex. This is only a restriction of the existing NOR class.

Juan A. Aledo, Eric Goles, Marco Montalva-Medel, Pedro Montealegre and
Jose C. Valverde, *Symmetrizable Boolean networks*, *Information Sciences*
626 (2023), 787–804,
[primary published body](https://ruidera.uclm.es/server/api/core/bitstreams/d3daaf59-63c7-4a36-95f5-23aec3e15244/content),
printed pp. 787–792, explicitly permits absent self-loops and identifies
homogeneous NOR systems as symmetric threshold networks. The direct
embedding uses $a_{ij}=-1$ on conflict edges, zero elsewhere and threshold
zero; it supports only periods one and two. Its source definitions fit
the loopless line graph without inserting a self-input. We do not cite
the failed 2022 or 2020 opens as read proofs, nor claim this 2023 article
states an all-target line-graph fibre formula or the stronger identity below.

The [background deduction](PROOF_PACKAGE.md) proves $R^3=R$ from relation
symmetry alone. The sharp transient question therefore collapses to a
one-step polarity identity, not a new many-stage matching phenomenon.
Moreover $R$ does **not** preserve the matching subcarrier: for the
two-edge path $G$, $R(\varnothing)=E(G)$ is not a matching. On the full
Boolean carrier, fixed states are just maximal independent sets of $H$
(maximal matchings for $H=L(G)$), and a target fibre is a neighborhood
cover problem. No special evaluated census or genuinely independent
inverse extremum survives here. We do not assert this excludes every
possible graph packing dynamics.
