## Assumptions and notation

A numerical semigroup $S$ contains zero, is addition-closed, and has exactly
$g$ missing nonnegative integers. For $g>0$, $F$ is the largest gap and $m$ the
least positive nongap. Write $S_g=\{0,g+1,g+2,\ldots\}$ and
$r(S)=|S\cap[1,g]|$. At $g=0$, the only state is $\mathbb N_0$.

For Moore refinement, $Q$ and the alphabet $\Sigma$ are finite, $\delta$ is
complete and deterministic, and $c$ is a fixed colour function. $E$ is the
initial partition relation; $E_t$ is the relation after $t$ iterations.
The action convention is $\delta(q,aw)=\delta(\delta(q,a),w)$.

For Ret, $X$ is nonempty, finite and nondegenerate, its left translations
$\sigma_x(y)=x*y$ are bijections, and
$(x*y)*(x*z)=(y*x)*(y*z)$. The source congruence theorem is an imported premise.
The state carrier consists of isomorphism classes of such cycle sets of
cardinality at most a fixed $N\ge1$, not a changing labelled carrier.

## Proof

### 1. Ordinarization

Step 1. For each $s\in S\cap[0,F]$, $F-s$ is a gap, since otherwise addition
would put $F$ in $S$. The map is injective. Exactly $g$ points of $[0,F]$
are gaps, hence $F+1-g\le g$ and $F\le2g-1$. The fixed-genus carrier is
finite. The case $g=0$ does not use $F$.

Step 2. If $m>g$, the first $g$ positive integers are all the gaps, so
$S=S_g$. If $S\ne S_g$, a positive nongap at most $g$ forces at least one
gap above $g$; thus $m\le g<F$. Removing $m$ cannot destroy additive closure:
a sum of two positive remaining elements is larger than $m$, and sums with
zero retain their summands. After adjoining $F$, sums with another positive
element exceed $F$ and cannot equal the removed $m$; the sum with zero is
the newly adjoined element. Exactly one gap and one nongap are exchanged.

Step 3. The update removes exactly one member of $S\cap[1,g]$ and adds none,
so $r$ falls by exactly one until it becomes zero. Its zero state is $S_g$.
Consequently $r(S)$ is the exact entrance time, and $S_g$ is the only
recurrent state. This excludes longer cycles by strict descent.

Step 4. If $r>0$, enumerate the positive nongaps at most $g$ as
$\lambda_1<\cdots<\lambda_r$. Each $\lambda_r+\lambda_i$ is a nongap,
is larger than the largest nongap at most $g$, and is at most $2g$.
The $r$ sums are distinct. There are $g-r$ nongaps in $(g,2g]$, because
all $g$ gaps lie at most $2g-1$. Thus $r\le g-r$ and
$r\le\lfloor g/2\rfloor$. The even-nongap example $E_g$ in the frozen
proof has precisely the $g$ odd gaps below $2g$ and attains that bound.
For $r=0$ the inequality requires no sums.

Step 5. At $g=3$, $E_3$ has gaps $\{1,3,5\}$; $H=\langle3,4\rangle$
has gaps $\{1,2,5\}$. To check the second description without enumeration,
$6,7,8$ are in $H$ and adding $3$ yields every integer at least $6$.
Both sets are addition-closed, distinct, and have one positive nongap at
most $3$. Removing respectively $2$ or $3$ and adding $5$ gives $S_3$.
Thus both attain the maximum $1$. This directly refutes the all-$g>0$
uniqueness sentence in the archived v1 Lemma 4. Its displayed odd-genus
argument treats $g\notin S$; $H$ does not satisfy that condition. No statement
about journal corrections, historical priority, or every exceptional genus
follows. The valid preceding arguments use neither Lemma 3 nor Lemma 4.

Step 6. The source inverse tree already deletes an effective generator above
the target Frobenius number and inserts a gap below the target multiplicity,
subject to closure. This is an inverse construction, not an evaluated new
all-target fibre law. The ordinary fixed state's self-preimage is separate
from its nontrivial tree children. Lemma 8's first-depth census is source
material, not a new inverse mechanism. Old NSB only adjoins the Frobenius
number and reduces genus; X16 iterates blowup/multiplicity. Neither is
falsely equated with fixed-genus ordinarization.

### 2. Moore refinement

Step 7. One refinement requires $pE_tq$, $c(p)=c(q)$, and
$\delta(p,a)E_t\delta(q,a)$ for every $a\in\Sigma$.
For $t=0$ the claimed bounded-word formula is exactly $pEq$.
Assuming it at $t$, the retained constraint accounts for the empty word and
all old lengths. The successor constraints prepend each $a$ to all words of
length at most $t$, giving the initial-relation constraints through length
$t+1$; the explicit colour comparison supplies length zero and successor
colour constraints supply lengths $1$ through $t$. This proves exactly
the two ranges $|w|\le t+1$ and $|w|<t+1$.

Step 8. A nonfixed refinement strictly splits a block and never merges
blocks, hence at most $|Q|-|\Pi|$ changes occur. At the terminal relation,
one-step invariance implies invariance under every word. Any right-invariant
equivalence refining both $E$ and the colour kernel satisfies all the
bounded-word constraints, and so refines every iterate and the terminal
relation. The terminal relation is therefore the greatest such equivalence.

Step 9. Empty $Q$ has one partition and zero changes. If $\Sigma$ is empty,
only intersection with the colour kernel remains, so at most one change
occurs. The $n-2$ strict-refinement bound uses a genuine two-block
accepting/rejecting start; it is not the arbitrary-partition bound and is
not the source's full loop count, which includes an equality-detection
iteration. No $n-2$ assertion is imported for one state or trivial final sets.
The exact A01/D2 block key and proof already occur in the inspected originals.

### 3. Retraction

Step 10. The cited congruence theorem makes $X\mapsto X/{\sim}$ a defined
operator, where $x\sim y$ means $\sigma_x=\sigma_y$. Isomorphisms preserve
that relation, so the operator descends to the finite carrier of isomorphism
classes bounded by $N$. Any nontrivial equivalence class reduces cardinality.
At most $|X|-1$ nonfixed changes are possible. If no cardinality reduction
occurs, all classes are singletons and the quotient is isomorphic to $X$.
Conversely an isomorphic quotient has the same cardinality and therefore
all translations were distinct. This proves the fixed-point characterization,
not convergence of every orbit to a singleton.

Step 11. In the frozen eight-point formula all additions are modulo $2$.
Each translation preserves the block index and is an involution: on its own
block it adds the fixed quantity $a-c$ to the second coordinate, and on the
other block it adds $b$ to the first. Its diagonal fixes $(a,b,i)$.
BCJO's complete construction and Theorem 3.3 provide the Yang--Baxter law.
The source convention uses YBE left actions, whereas a cycle set uses their
inverses; they coincide here because these translations are involutions.
More explicitly, invert the source identity
$\sigma_x\sigma_{\sigma_x^{-1}y}=\sigma_y\sigma_{\sigma_y^{-1}x}$.
The result is
$\sigma_{x*y}\sigma_x=\sigma_{y*x}\sigma_y$, which is the cycle law.

Step 12. For two source points with different indices, test in the first
point's block and choose the first coordinate unequal to its $a$; its
translation changes the second coordinate, whereas the other point's
translation does not. If indices agree but $a$ differs, testing within
that block separates them. If index and $a$ agree but $b$ differs, testing
in the other block separates them. All eight translations are distinct,
so this square-free nondegenerate cycle set is irretractable and does not
reach a singleton. The construction is source-owned, not new to fresh18.

Step 13. Define the hitting time $h(X)=\min\{t\ge0:|\operatorname{Ret}^tX|=1\}$
when that set is nonempty. BCJO v2 §2 instead defines its named level with
the smallest positive integer. Thus at $|X|=1$, $h(X)=0$ while that source's
level is $1$. For $|X|>1$ which reaches one point, the two minima agree;
for a non-singleton irretractable endpoint there is no finite such level.
A zero-inclusive convention is possible, but it cannot silently be substituted
for the cited convention. Only the qualified identification is accepted.
