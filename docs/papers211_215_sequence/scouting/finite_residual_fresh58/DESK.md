## Literal

Fix $n$. The carrier consists of all labelled simple graphs whose vertex set is a subset of $[n]$, including the empty graph. For a current graph $G$, let

$$C(G)=\{v\in V(G):N_G[v]\subsetneq N_G[w]\text{ for some }w\ne v\},$$
$$T(G)=G[V(G)\setminus C(G)].$$

The inclusion is **strict**: equal-neighbourhood twins are retained unless their shared neighbourhood is itself strictly contained in another. The update uses the old graph throughout each simultaneous round. The carrier is finite and closed; there is no label compression or external schedule.

## Deductive feasibility boundary

Every nonempty graph retains a vertex whose closed neighbourhood is inclusion-maximal. Hence a nonfixed step strictly decreases vertex count but never empties a nonempty graph. All recurrent states are exactly graphs with no strict closed-neighbourhood containment; the entrance time is at most $|V(G)|-1$. The empty graph is fixed. This is only a deletion-budget argument, not an admission-worthy temporal theorem.

For a path on $s\ge2$ vertices, the rule repeatedly removes its two leaves while more than two vertices remain, stopping at one vertex or one edge. Its time is $\lfloor(s-1)/2\rfloor$; $s=1$ has time zero. For a path with at least three vertices, the leaf's closed neighbourhood is a proper subset of its neighbour's, while no internal vertex has a containing closed neighbourhood elsewhere; the claim then follows by induction on $s$. This family does not provide a separate inverse or enumeration mechanism. No evaluated all-target fibre formula was proved, and counting arbitrary graph extensions is not substituted for one.
