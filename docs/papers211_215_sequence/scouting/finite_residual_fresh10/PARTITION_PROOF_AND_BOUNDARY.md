# Partition adjustment: proof and precise boundary

## Claim

For every integer $n\ge1$, the autonomous map $B:\Pi_n^3\to\Pi_n^3$
defined below is increasing along each orbit and reaches its least balanced
upper triple. Its recurrent points are exactly its balanced fixed points,
and every pairwise coordinate join is invariant.

If a target $(A,B_0,C)$ has total join $J=A\vee B_0\vee C$ with all blocks
of size at most two, its complete fibre has the explicit formula in Step 5.
There are two-step trajectories for every $n\ge4$.

These are limited certificates. They do not state a sharp all-$n$ maximum
entry time, a general target fibre formula or global fibre extremizers.

## Status

**PROVABLE AS STATED** for the limited claim.

A new two-axis admission theorem is **NOT CURRENTLY JUSTIFIED**. The
one-step idempotence of the earlier subspace carrier is false on this
partition carrier; replacing it with a generic rank bound does not supply
the required sharp temporal residual.

## Assumptions

The ground set is $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ with $n\ge1$. Its labels are retained.
A partition is identified with its equivalence relation. Refinement
$\alpha\le\beta$ means that every $\alpha$-block lies in a $\beta$-block.
The meet is relation intersection. The join is the equivalence relation
generated by the union, so the carrier is closed under both operations.

## Notation

Let $\Pi_n$ denote this finite lattice, $o$ its discrete partition,
$j$ its one-block partition, and $\rho(\alpha)=n-\#\operatorname{blocks}(\alpha)$.
The literal simultaneous update is
$$
B(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=
\bigl(\alpha\vee(\beta\wedge\gamma),
      \beta\vee(\alpha\wedge\gamma),
      \gamma\vee(\alpha\wedge\beta)\bigr).
$$
The letter $B$ in the map is not the target's second coordinate $B_0$.
A triple is balanced when all three pairwise meets are equal. Its entry
time is the least $h\ge0$ for which its $h$-th iterate is fixed.

## Proof strategy

Use the refinement order for the universal statements, an explicit
five-element sublattice of $\Pi_4$ to reject idempotence transfer, and
the direct product decomposition below a matching partition for the
restricted inverse formula.

## Dependency map

1. Extensivity and finite rank give termination; isotonicity gives leastness.
2. The three fixed-point inequalities give balancedness; no cycle can
   survive strict monotone growth.
3. Pairwise-join invariance uses absorption, not modularity.
4. The labelled four-point witness is checked by relation intersections
   and generated equivalences.
5. Extensivity forces every predecessor below the target; when its total
   join has blocks of size at most two, the remaining lattice intervals
   have a three-bit truth table.

## Proof

### Step 1. Termination and the stated nonsharp bound

Every coordinate of $B(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ contains the corresponding
input coordinate. Meet and join are isotone, hence so is $B$ on the product
order. Proper coarsening of a partition strictly increases its rank $\rho$.
Consequently every nonfixed application increases
$\rho(\alpha)+\rho(\beta)+\rho(\gamma)$ by at least one. This sum is at most
$3(n-1)$, so the orbit is fixed after at most
$$
3(n-1)-\rho(\alpha)-\rho(\beta)-\rho(\gamma)
$$
nonfixed steps. This upper bound is not asserted to be sharp.

If $n=1$, the product carrier is a singleton and the time is zero.
For all $n$, a periodic orbit in a finite partially ordered set whose
successive terms only increase must be constant: an increase followed by
a return would contradict antisymmetry. Thus every recurrent point here
is fixed.

### Step 2. Fixed points and least balanced upper triple

A triple is fixed if and only if
$$
\beta\wedge\gamma\le\alpha,\qquad
\alpha\wedge\gamma\le\beta,\qquad
\alpha\wedge\beta\le\gamma.
$$
Under these inequalities each pairwise meet is at most all three
coordinates, and therefore equals
$\delta=\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\gamma$. Conversely, if the pairwise meets
are equal, their common value lies below each coordinate and the three
displayed inequalities follow. This proves the fixed/balanced equivalence.

Let $z$ be the terminal iterate of an input $x$. It is a balanced upper
triple. For any other balanced upper triple $w\ge x$, isotonicity and
$B(w)=w$ give $B^k(x)\le w$ for every $k\ge0$ by induction. At a terminal
time this gives $z\le w$. Hence $z$ is the least balanced upper triple.

### Step 3. Pairwise-join invariants

Write the output as $(\alpha',\beta',\gamma')$. Then
$$
\alpha'\vee\beta'
=\alpha\vee\beta\vee(\beta\wedge\gamma)\vee(\alpha\wedge\gamma)
=\alpha\vee\beta,
$$
because the two added meets are below $\beta$ and $\alpha$, respectively.
Relabelling the already-proved identity by the two coordinate permutations
gives $\alpha'\vee\gamma'=\alpha\vee\gamma$ and
$\beta'\vee\gamma'=\beta\vee\gamma$. These identities do not identify a
unique triple, so they are not a sharp clock or full inverse formula.

### Step 4. A deductive two-step witness

For $n=4$, use the labelled partitions
$$
o=1|2|3|4,\quad b=12|3|4,\quad a=12|34,\quad
c=13|24,\quad j=1234.
$$
Here $b<a$, $a\wedge c=b\wedge c=o$, $a\wedge b=b$, and
$b\vee c=j$. Therefore direct substitution gives
$$
(b,a,c)\ \longmapsto\ (b,a,j)\ \longmapsto\ (a,a,j).
$$
The two arrows are strict because $c\ne j$ and $b\ne a$.
The last triple is balanced and fixed by Step 2.
For $n>4$, adjoining the other labels as singleton blocks to every
partition preserves all meets and joins in this calculation. This proves
entry time exactly two for the displayed input for every $n\ge4$.

This is a symbolic construction, not a computed orbit. It disproves
$B^2=B$ on these partition carriers. It does not identify the maximum
entry time.

### Step 5. Exact fibres on matching-block targets

Fix an arbitrary target $(A,B_0,C)\in\Pi_n^3$, and put
$J=A\vee B_0\vee C$. Assume every block of $J$ has size one or two.
By Step 1, every predecessor $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ satisfies
$\alpha\le A$, $\beta\le B_0$, $\gamma\le C$, hence all coordinates refine
$J$. Operations among partitions refining $J$ are performed independently
inside each $J$-block: equivalences cannot join two different blocks of
$J$, and their intersection and generated union restrict blockwise.

A singleton block permits exactly one choice in each coordinate.
On a two-element block, each coordinate either separates the pair
(bit zero) or joins it (bit one). The induced three-bit map is
$$
(a_0,b_0,c_0)\longmapsto
\bigl(a_0\lor(b_0\land c_0),
b_0\lor(a_0\land c_0),
c_0\lor(a_0\land b_0)\bigr).
$$
Its complete truth table is:
the zero vector maps to itself; each of the three vectors of weight one
maps to itself; all three weight-two vectors and the weight-three vector
map to the weight-three vector. This exhausts the eight possibilities.

Thus a target bit triple of weight two has no predecessor; a target of
weight zero or one has one; a target of weight three has four. A
two-element block of the actual join $J$ cannot have target weight zero,
but including that case makes the local truth table explicit.

Let $r$ count the two-element $J$-blocks joined in all three target
coordinates. Independence over the blocks gives the full target fibre:
$$
\#B^{-1}(A,B_0,C)=
\begin{cases}
0,&\text{some two-element $J$-block has target weight two},\\
4^r,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$
No predecessor outside the product interval has been omitted because of
the extensivity argument at the start of this step.

In particular, if $P$ has $k$ pair blocks and all other blocks singleton,
then the target $(P,P,P)$ has exactly $4^k$ predecessors. This is a
restricted exact formula, not a claim that $4^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ is
the global maximum. The proof includes $n=1$, where $r=0$ and the
single target has one predecessor.

## Source and internal subtraction

The update is precisely the first adjustment polynomial in
[Grätzer–Wehrung, §2, Lemmas 2.3 and 2.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0501430).
That source already treats balancing in arbitrary lattices and distinguishes
modular one-step closure. The present partition specialization is not a
new literal at the generic lattice level; the order-theoretic closure
mechanism receives zero contribution credit.

The earlier
[subspace-balancing proof](../finite_incidence_geometry_residual_scout02/PROOF_PACKAGE.md)
uses this identical lattice polynomial with meet as intersection and join
as vector-space sum. Its one-step conclusion depends on that carrier,
not on the symbols alone. Step 4 is a precise counterexample to importing
that conclusion here. The different carrier is real but does not erase
the primary generic owner.

[P182's original proof package](../../../../papers/182-cyclic-subspace-lattice-comparator/PROOF_PACKAGE.md)
instead uses $(a,b,c)\mapsto(c,a\wedge b,a\vee b)$ and proves collapse
to a two-cycle mechanism. There is no asserted conjugacy between that
map and the present update. The shared lattice vocabulary is only a
navigation/subtraction boundary.

## Corrections or missing assumptions

The all-parameter termination statement survives, but it is not the
required sharp residual theorem. Still missing are an evaluated sharp
maximum time with all extremizers, and a complete arbitrary-target
inverse/image classification with evaluated extremal fibres on the full
partition carrier. The matching-block restriction in Step 5 cannot be
removed by the displayed truth table; larger join blocks are non-Boolean.
