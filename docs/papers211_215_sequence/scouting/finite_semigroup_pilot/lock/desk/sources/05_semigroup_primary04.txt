L403: ## 5 Additional examples
L404: ### 5.1 The monoid of order-preserving functions
L405: Let \({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {T}}\,}}_{n}\) be the monoid of all functions \(f:[n]\rightarrow [n]\) (where \([n]=\{1,\ldots ,n\}\)). A function \(f\in {{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {T}}\,}}_{n}\) is called order-preserving if \(i\le j\implies f(i)\le f(j)\) for every \(i,j\in [n]\). We denote by \({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {O}}\,}}_{n}\) the submonoid of \({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {T}}\,}}_{n}\) of all order-preserving functions.
L406: In this section we will see that \({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {O}}\,}}_{n}\) is a reduced E-Fountain semigroup that satisfies the generalized left ample condition so by the dual of Theorem cite60†3.12 there is an isomorphism \(\Bbbk {{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {O}}\,}}_{n}\simeq \Bbbk _{0}\mathcal {C}({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {O}}\,}}_{n})\) of \(\Bbbk \)-algebras for any commutative unital ring \(\Bbbk \). We will also describe more explicitly the composition in \(\mathcal {C}({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {O}}\,}}_{n})\).
L407: Elementary observations
L408: 
L409: Recall that the kernel of of a function \(f\in {{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {O}}\,}}_{n}\) — \(\ker (f)\) — is the equivalence relation on its domain defined by \((i_{1},i_{2})\in \ker (f)\) if and only if \(f(i_{1})=f(i_{2})\). Since f is order-preserving, the kernel classes of f are intervals — if \(i_{1}\le i_{2}\le i_{3}\) and \((i_{1},i_{3})\in \ker (f)\) then \((i_{1},i_{2})\in \ker f\) also.
L410: Let \(K_{1},\ldots ,K_{l}\) be the kernel classes of f. Let \(y_{i}=f(K_{i}\)) and assume that the indices are arranged such that \(y_{1}<y_{2}<\cdots <y_{l}\). Choose \(x_{i}\) to be the maximal element of \(K_{i}\) and note that it must be the case that \(x_{l}=n\). Now, set
L411: 
L412: $$ X=\{x_{1},\ldots ,x_{l}\},\quad Y=\{y_{1},\ldots ,y_{l}\} $$
L413: so from every \(f\in {{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {O}}\,}}_{n}\) we can extract two sets \(X,Y\subseteq [n]\) such that \(|X|=|Y|\) and \(n\in X\). In the other direction, from two such sets \(X=\{x_{1},\ldots ,x_{l}\}\) where \(x_{l}=n\) and \({Y=\{y_{1},\ldots ,y_{l}\}}\) we can retrieve an f by setting
L414: 
L415: $$ f(x)={\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} y_{1} & x\le x_{1}\\ y_{i} & x_{i-1}<x\le x_{i}\quad (2\le i\le l). \end{array}\right. } $$
L416: As a conclusion, we observe that there is a one-to-one correspondence between functions \(f\in {{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {O}}\,}}_{n}\) and pairs of sets \(X,Y\subseteq [n]\) such that \(|X|=|Y|\) and \(n\in X\). From now on we denote by \(f_{Y,X}\) the function associated with \(X,Y\subseteq [n]\) (in contrary to [cite42†27 , Section 4.2] where this function was denoted \(f_{X,Y})\).
L417: Note that \({{\,\mathrm{\textsf{im}}\,}}(f_{Y,X})=Y\) (where \({{\,\mathrm{\textsf{im}}\,}}(f)\) is the image of f) and X contains the maximal elements of the kernel classes of \(f_{Y,X}\). Another easy observation is that \(f_{Z,Y}f_{Y,X}=f_{Z,X}\).
L418: The monoid \({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {O}}\,}}_{n}\) is a regular submonoid of \({{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {T}}\,}}_{n}\) and its Green’s equivalence relations are well-known (see [cite65†6 , Section 2]). In particular:
L419: $$\begin{aligned} f_{Y_{1},X_{1}}\,{{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {L}}\,}}\,f_{Y_{2},X_{2}}&\iff \ker (f_{Y_{1},X_{1}})=\ker (f_{Y_{2},X_{2}})\iff X_{1}=X_{2}\\ f_{Y_{1},X_{1}}\,{{\,\mathrm{\mathcal {R}}\,}}\,f_{Y_{2},X_{2}}&\iff {{\,\mathrm{\textsf{im}}\,}}(f_{Y_{1},X_{1}})={{\,\mathrm{\textsf{im}}\,}}(f_{Y_{2},X_{2}})\iff Y_{1}=Y_{2} \end{aligned}$$
L420: We want to define our distinguished set of idempotents E. Note that for every \(X\subseteq [n]\) with \(n\in X\) the function \(f_{X,X}\) is an idempotent that can be described by \(f_{X,X}(i)=\min \{x\in X\mid i\le x\}\). It will be convenient to define \(e_{X}=f_{X,X}\) where \(X\subseteq [n]\) with \(n\in X\) and we set \(E=\{e_{X}\mid n\in X\}\) (this is precisely the set of idempotents of the Catalan monoid — see [cite26†29 , Section 17.5.2]).

L36: ###### Abstract.
L37: 
L38: We give a direct straightforward proof that there is an isomorphism between the algebra of the Catalan monoid $C_{n}$, that is, the monoid of all order-preserving, weakly increasing self-maps $f$ of $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, over any commutative ring with identity and the incidence algebra of a certain poset over the ring.
L39: 
L40: ###### 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
L41: 
L42: 20M25,16G10,05E99
L43: ## 1. Introduction
L44: The Catalan monoid $C_{n}$ is the monoid of all order-preserving, weakly increasing self-maps $f$ of $[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$. It is well known that the cardinality of $C_{n}$ is the $n$-th Catalan number. See for example, [cite11†5 , Ex.6.19(s)]. $C_{n}$ has appeared in many guises in combinatorics and combinatorial semigroup theory under different names.
L45: For example, it has also been called the monoid of non-decreasing parking functions or, simply, the monoid of order-decreasing and order-preserving functions [cite12†2 , cite13†4 , cite14†1 ].
L46: It was first observed by Hivert and Thiéry [cite15†3 ], via an indirect proof, that $\Bbbk C_{n}$ is isomorphic to the incidence algebra of a certain poset $P_{n-1}$ for any field $\Bbbk$; a different indirect proof using the representation theory of $\mathscr{J}$-trivial monoids can be found in the second author’s book [cite16†10 ].
L47: Grensig gave a direct isomorphism from the incidence algebra of $P_{n-1}$ to $\Bbbk C_{n}$ for any base commutative ring with unit $\Bbbk$, but her proof is quite involved and long because of the technical recursive construction of a complete set of orthogonal idempotents.
L48: Here we show that there is a straightforward direct isomorphism from $\Bbbk C_{n}$ to the incidence algebra of $P_{n-1}$ over any base commutative ring with unit whose details are trivial to check. The proof is similar to that used by the second author for inverse monoids [cite17†9 ] and Stein for more general monoids [cite18†6 , cite19†7 , cite20†8 ]. The complication in previous approaches is avoided here as we show that the isomorphism is given by a unipotent upper triangular $0/1$-matrix.
L49: ## 2. Combinatorics of the Catalan monoid
L50: 
L51: If $n\geq 0$ is an integer, let $P_{n}$ be the poset consisting of subsets of $[n]$ ordered by $X\leq Y$ if and only if $|X|=|Y|$ and if $X=\{x_{1}<\cdots<x_{k}\}$ and $Y=\{y_{1}<\cdots<y_{k}\}$, then $x_{i}\leq y_{i}$ for $i=1,\ldots,k$. We shall also need the following refinement of this order given by $X\preceq Y$ if $|X|<|Y|$ or if $|X|=|Y|$ and $X\leq Y$.
L52: There is a well-known bijection between the Catalan monoid $C_{n+1}$ and the set of ordered pairs $(X,Y)$ of elements of $P_{n}$ with $X\leq Y$ given as follows. If $X\leq Y$, define $f_{X,Y}\colon[n+1]\longrightarrow[n+1]$ in $C_{n+1}$ by
L53: 
L54:  | $$f_{X,Y}(i)=\begin{cases}y_{1},&\text{if}\ 1\leq i\leq x_{1}\\
L55: y_{j},&\text{if}\ x_{j-1}<i\leq x_{j}\\
L56: n+1,&\text{if}\ i>x_{k}\end{cases}$$  |
L57: where we have retained the notation of the previous paragraph for $X$ and $Y$. The condition that $X\leq Y$ guarantees that $f_{X,Y}$ is order preserving and weakly increasing. Conversely, if $f\in C_{n+1}$, let $Y=f([n+1])\setminus\{n+1\}$ and let $X=\{i\in f^{-1}(Y)\mid i=\max\{f^{-1}(f(i))\}$; so $X$ consists of the maximum element of each partition block of $f$ except the block of $n+1$.
L58: Then it is straightforward to check from $f$ being order-preserving and weakly increasing that $X\leq Y$ and that $f=f_{X,Y}$. See [cite16†10 , Ch. 17 Sec. 5] for details where we note that a slightly different convention was used.
L59: Let us say that $S\subseteq[n]$ is a partial cross-section for $f\in C_{n+1}$ if $n+1\notin f(S)$ and $f|_{S}$ is injective, i.e., $|S|=|f(S)|$. We denote by $\mathsf{PCS}(f)$ the set of partial cross-sections of $f$. The following proposition is straightforward from the definitions and so we omit the proof.
L60: ###### Proposition 2.1.
L61: 
L62: Let $S$ be a partial cross-section for $f=f_{X,Y}$ in $C_{n+1}$.
L63: 
L64:   1. (1)
L65: 
L66: $S\preceq X$.
L67: 
L68:   2. (2)
L69: 
L70: $S\leq f(S)$.
L71: 
L72:   3. (3)
L73: 
L74: $f(S)\subseteq Y$, hence $f(S)\preceq Y$.
L75: 
L76:   4. (4)
L77: 
L78: $X\in\mathsf{PCS}(f)$.

L80: The following lemma is key to our proof.
L81: 
L82: ###### Lemma 2.2.
L83: 
L84: Let $f,g\in C_{n+1}$. Then $S\in\mathsf{PCS}(fg)$ if and only if $S\in\mathsf{PCS}(g)$ and $g(S)\in\mathsf{PCS}(f)$.
