## Claim, status and proof

**PROVABLE AS STATED:** $F^2=F$. The maximum fixed-point entrance time is zero for $n\le2$ and one for $n\ge3$. For $n\ge1$, the fixed points are exactly permutations whose prefix ending at $n$ is increasing; their number is

$$\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}(n-1-k)!.$$

At $n=0$ there is one fixed point. These are elementary author statements, not independent review findings or a paper contribution.

Strategy/dependencies: extract the increasing record subsequence; use its final global maximum to exclude new suffix records; characterize fixed points; choose the increasing prefix labels and order the complement.

1. For $n\ge1$, the record subsequence is strictly increasing and ends in $n$. In $F(w)$, every element of that initial record block is a left-to-right maximum. No suffix element is a record because $n$ already precedes it. The extracted record set and its order are therefore unchanged on the next update. The suffix also retains its order. Hence $F^2(w)=F(w)$. The identity at $n=0$ holds directly.

2. If the prefix of $w$ through $n$ is increasing, those and only those positions are records, so $F(w)=w$. Conversely, if a nonrecord occurred before a later record, stable extraction would move that later record before it, changing their order. A fixed point has no such pair, so its records form the entire prefix through $n$, which is increasing. This proves the fixed-point characterization.

3. Both permutations of size two have increasing prefixes through their maximum, as does the sole size-one permutation. Thus the map is the identity for $n\le2$. For $n\ge3$, the permutation $2134\cdots n$ changes to $234\cdots n1$, then is fixed. Combined with idempotence, this gives the exact maximum entrance time. An idempotent map has no nonfixed recurrent points, because every image point is fixed.

4. To form a fixed permutation, choose $k$ labels from $[n-1]$ to precede $n$, put them in their unique increasing order, and order the remaining $n-1-k$ labels arbitrarily after $n$. The position of $n$ recovers $k$ and the chosen set, so the constructions are disjoint and exhaustive. Summation gives the displayed count. ∎
