## SDT comparison, without assumption-based repairs

For the K-state rules, activating all enabled normal processes gives exactly our unconditional coordinate copy: if a normal process is disabled, its old value already equals its predecessor, so copying changes nothing. The root equality rule also agrees exactly. EWD426's n=N+1 and q=K>N conventions align with our n>=3, q>=n. Dubois–Guerraoui's process count is not N, nor is its stated daemon a sequential sweep.

Under the natural K-state privilege predicate, all constant states have one root privilege. A nonconstant state has exactly one privilege precisely when it has two constant consecutive blocks with distinct values. These single-privilege states are forward invariant, but some are not recurrent for our simultaneous map: for example, (0,0,1) at n=3,q=3 has one privilege and maps to the recurrent constant state (0,0,0). Thus legitimacy and recurrence differ pointwise. This is our deduction from the literal, not a textual theorem attributed to either source.

However, the n=q=4 hand path in PROOF_PACKAGE.md has two privileges at time four. Therefore the earlier tentative explanation “n to legitimacy, 2n-3 to recurrence” is not valid for this literal and is explicitly withdrawn. The source's aside is retained as an UNRESOLVED_EXACT_VARIANT_COMPARISON and is not used as a theorem, negative-control benchmark or novelty motive. We do not claim to have refuted an identified formal theorem, nor silently strengthen the source to a full-state orbit assertion. The rule owner is clear; the aside's exact algorithm attribution remains unaligned.
