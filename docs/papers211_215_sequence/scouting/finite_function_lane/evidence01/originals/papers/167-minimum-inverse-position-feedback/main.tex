\title[Minimum inverse-position feedback]{Minimum Inverse-Position Feedback on Finite Endofunctions}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Let $[n]=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ and replace an endofunction $f$ by the vector
whose $i$th coordinate is the least position carrying $i$, using $i$
itself when $i$ is absent.  We determine the resulting feedback dynamics.
Every first image is a disjoint union of directed cycles and loop-rooted
paths; cycles invert, while a path reverses or loses an endpoint according
to an order comparison.  This gives sharp tail heights $2n-2$ on the full
carrier and $2n-3$ after one update.  Recurrent components admit an exact
labelled census, yielding a closed exponential generating function,
all fixed-iterate counts, and the finite dynamical zeta function.  Fixed
states are involutions.  Separately, a forced-first-position construction
gives the one-step fibre over every target, including unsupported targets,
and proves that the maximum fibre is the Bell number $B_n$.  The cases
$n=1,2,3$ are included explicitly.  All classical transversal,
first-occurrence, set-partition, functional-graph, and zeta ingredients are
treated as background; external circulation remains on hold.
\end{abstract}

\section{The literal feedback map and theorem ceiling}

For $n\geq1$, put $\X_n=[n]^{[n]}$ and regard
$f\in\X_n$ both as an endofunction and as the word
$f(0)\cdots f(n-1)$.  Define the self-map $\M_n:\X_n\to\X_n$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:literal}
 (\M_n f)(i)=
 \begin{cases}
   \min\{j\in[n]:f(j)=i\},&f^{-1}(i)\ne\varnothing,\\
   i,&f^{-1}(i)=\varnothing.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The second line is part of the map, not an arbitrary completion of a
partial section.  Powers $\M_n^k$ below mean repeated feedback under
\eqref{eq:literal}, not composition powers of the original $f$.

The first line chooses the least member of each kernel class.  Least kernel
transversals and inverse matchings in transformation semigroups are
established background \cite{FernandesEtAl2009,Higgins2019}.  Indeed,
$f\circ\M_n(f)\circ f=f$, and
$e_f=\M_n(f)\circ f$ is the idempotent retraction sending each position to
the least member of its $\ker f$ block.  This does not make $\M_n$ a mutual
inverse selection: for $f=(0,0)$, $\M_2(f)=\id$ but
$\M_2(f)\circ f\circ\M_2(f)=f\ne\M_2(f)$.  The identity-on-missing rule is
what controls subsequent path splitting.

First-occurrence encodings and their set-partition interpretation are also
classical \cite{BeanEtAl2026}, as are functional-digraph decomposition
\cite{FlajoletOdlyzko1989} and periodic-point zeta functions
\cite{ArtinMazur1965}.  These ingredients, involution and Bell numbers,
and labelled-set calculus receive no contribution credit.  The result
proved here is only the exact conjunction forced by the literal rule
\eqref{eq:literal}.  No novelty or priority conclusion is drawn from the
bounded owner search.

For $x\in\X_n$, let $\tail(x)$ be its least entrance time into the recurrent
set and let $\per(x)$ be its eventual period.  Write
$\Y_n=\M_n(\X_n)$, let $R_n$ be the number of recurrent points, and let
$I_n$ be the number of involutions of $[n]$.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:involution-egf}
 \sum_{n\geq0}I_n\frac{x^n}{n!}=\exp(x+x^2/2).
\end{equation}

We also prepare the target-local inverse notation.  Given $g\in\X_n$, put
\[
 U_g=\{i:g(i)\ne i\}.
\]
If the values $\{g(i):i\in U_g\}$ repeat, define $\Phi_n(g)=0$.  Otherwise
set
\[
 F_g=\{i:g(i)=i,\ i\notin g(U_g)\}.
\]
For $A\subseteq F_g$, let $P_A=U_g\cup A$ and
\[
 r_A(i)=\begin{cases}g(i),&i\in U_g,\\i,&i\in A,\end{cases}
 \qquad R_A=\{r_A(i):i\in P_A\},
\]
and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi}
 \Phi_n(g)=\sum_{A\subseteq F_g}
 \prod_{\substack{0\leq j<n\\j\notin R_A}}
 \bigl|\{i\in P_A:r_A(i)<j\}\bigr|.
\end{equation}
Empty products have value one; a zero factor rejects that choice of $A$.

\begin{theorem}[complete dynamics and one-step fibres]\label{thm:main}
For the literal map \eqref{eq:literal}, the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Every component of every $g\in\Y_n$ is either a directed cycle or a
loop-rooted directed path.  One update inverts each cycle.  If a path of
size $s>1$ in root-to-leaf order is $P=(p_0,\ldots,p_{s-1})$, one update
reverses $P$ when $p_0>p_1$, and otherwise splits off $p_0$ and reverses the
remaining path; a singleton is fixed.
For $n\geq2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heights}
 \max_{f\in\X_n}\tail(f)=2n-2,
 \qquad \max_{g\in\Y_n}\tail(g)=2n-3.
\end{equation}
Both maxima are zero at $n=1$.

\item A path of size $s>1$ is recurrent exactly when
$p_0>p_1$ and $p_{s-1}>p_{s-2}$; a singleton is fixed.  On a prescribed
$s$-set, the number $c_s$ of connected recurrent states is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:connected}
 c_1=1,\quad c_2=1,\quad c_3=4,\qquad
 c_s=(s-1)!+\frac{s!}{4}\quad(s\geq4).
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rec-egf}
 \sum_{n\geq0}R_n\frac{x^n}{n!}
 =\frac{1}{1-x}\exp\!\left(\frac{x^3}{3}
       +\frac{x^4}{4(1-x)}\right).
\end{equation}

\item The fixed states are precisely the involutions.  For every $k\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iterate-fix}
 |\Fix(\M_n^k)|=
 \begin{cases}I_n,&k\text{ odd},\\R_n,&k\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The finite-map dynamical zeta function, as a formal power series, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeta}
 \zeta_{\M_n}(z)=(1-z)^{-I_n}
 (1-z^2)^{-(R_n-I_n)/2}.
\end{equation}

\item For every target $g\in\X_n$, including targets outside $\Y_n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre}
 |\M_n^{-1}(g)|=\Phi_n(g).
\end{equation}
If $B_n$ is the $n$th Bell number, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bell}
 \max_{g\in\X_n}|\M_n^{-1}(g)|=B_n,
\end{equation}
and the identity target attains the maximum.

\item The first three nonempty carriers have the following boundary data;
``two-orbits'' counts dynamical orbits rather than points.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c@{\quad}rrrrrrrr}
\toprule
$n$&$|\X_n|$&$|\Y_n|$&$R_n$&$I_n$&two-orbits&full $H$&image $H$&max fibre\\
\midrule
1&1&1&1&1&0&0&0&1\\
2&4&3&2&2&0&2&1&2\\
3&27&14&8&4&2&4&3&5\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section{Component action and the two sharp clocks}

\begin{lemma}[first-image components]\label{lem:components}
If $g=\M_n(f)$, then the off-diagonal values
$\{g(i):g(i)\ne i\}$ are distinct.  Its functional digraph therefore has
only directed-cycle and loop-rooted-path components.  On these components
$\M_n$ acts exactly as in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(i), components never merge,
and the recurrent-path criterion in part~(ii) holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Whenever $g(i)\ne i$, its value is the first position of symbol $i$ in
$f$.  Distinct symbols have distinct first positions.  Hence every vertex
has at most one incoming nonloop edge.  A nonloop cycle can support no
attached vertex, while a loop can support at most one chain, proving the
component classification.

Write a path as $P=(p_0,\ldots,p_{s-1})$, so
$g(p_0)=p_0$ and $g(p_j)=p_{j-1}$ for $j\geq1$.  In the word $g$, symbol
$p_0$ occurs at positions $p_0,p_1$, symbol $p_j$ for
$1\leq j\leq s-2$ occurs at $p_{j+1}$, and $p_{s-1}$ is absent.  If
$p_0>p_1$, the least occurrence of $p_0$ is $p_1$ and the entire path
reverses.  If $p_0<p_1$, the root remains a singleton and the other labels
form the reversed path.  A cycle has exactly one occurrence of every
symbol, so its orientation is inverted.  All relevant positions and
symbols lie in the original component, including the identity default at
an absent leaf; no merge is possible.

For $s>1$, the path returns after reversal precisely when its new root also
has the descending comparison, namely $p_{s-1}>p_{s-2}$.  If the first
comparison fails, a split occurs immediately.  If only the last comparison
fails, one reversal is followed by a split.  Since splits cannot be undone,
these paths are not recurrent.  This proves the criterion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[sharp path clock]\label{lem:path-clock}
For a path $P$ of size $s$, its tail $D(P)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:path-bound}
 D(P)\leq2s-2.
\end{equation}
For $s\geq2$, equality holds exactly for the strictly decreasing order
$p_0>p_1>\cdots>p_{s-1}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Induct on $s$.  A recurrent path has tail zero.  If $p_0<p_1$, one update
splits off $p_0$ and leaves a path of size $s-1$, so induction gives
$D(P)\leq1+2(s-1)-2=2s-3$.  It remains to take $p_0>p_1$ with $P$
nonrecurrent.  Lemma~\ref{lem:components} then gives
$p_{s-1}<p_{s-2}$: one update reverses the path, the next splits off
$p_{s-1}$, and the surviving path is the original prefix
$P^-=(p_0,\ldots,p_{s-2})$.  Therefore
\[
 D(P)=2+D(P^-)\leq2s-2.
\]
Equality is equivalent by induction to strict decrease of $P^-$; the last
displayed endpoint inequality then extends the decrease through
$p_{s-1}$.  Conversely, the same recurrence proves equality for every
strictly decreasing path.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(i)]
Components update in parallel, so a state's tail is the maximum of its path
tails.  Lemma~\ref{lem:path-clock} gives the global component bound.
Every $g=\M_n(f)$ contains the coordinate value zero, because the symbol
$f(0)$ has first occurrence zero.  The unique full-label path attaining
$2n-2$ has order $(n-1,\ldots,0)$, and its coordinate values are
$\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$; it cannot belong to $\Y_n$.  Any other full-label path
has integer tail at most $2n-3$, while a path on at most $n-1$ labels has
tail at most $2n-4$.  Thus the second maximum in \eqref{eq:heights} is at
most $2n-3$.

For $n\geq2$, let $w_n(j)=j+1$ for $0\leq j\leq n-2$ and
$w_n(n-1)=1$; in word notation this is
$w_n=(1,2,\ldots,n-1,1)$, and it maps to the increasing path
$(0,1,\ldots,n-1)$.  The path recursion in the
previous proof gives tail $2n-3$ for this image.  Since it is transient,
$w_n$ has tail $2n-2$.  Conversely, every source enters $\Y_n$ after one
update, so its tail is at most $1+(2n-3)$.  At $n=1$ the sole state is
fixed.  The component assertions were proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:components}.
\end{proof}

\section{Recurrent species, fixed iterates, and zeta}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(ii)--(iii)]
On a prescribed $s$-set, directed cycles contribute $(s-1)!$ for $s\geq2$.
There is one singleton.  Recurrent paths contribute none at $s=2$ and two
at $s=3$, since the middle position must contain the smallest label.  For
$s\geq4$, the two endpoint inequalities concern disjoint pairs of
positions.  Swapping either pair is a free involution on the $s!$ linear
orders, so exactly $s!/4$ orders satisfy both.  This proves
\eqref{eq:connected}.

A recurrent state is an unordered labelled set of connected recurrent
components.  The labelled-set formula gives
\[
 \exp\!\left(\sum_{s\geq1}c_s\frac{x^s}{s!}\right)
 =\exp\!\left(-\log(1-x)+\frac{x^3}{3}
                   +\frac{x^4}{4(1-x)}\right),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:rec-egf}.

Every nonsingleton recurrent path is exchanged with its distinct reversal.
Cycle inversion fixes exactly the two-cycles.  Thus a fixed state is a set
of singleton loops and disjoint two-cycles, equivalently an involution, and
\eqref{eq:involution-egf} applies.  Every recurrent point has period one or
two, while any point fixed by a positive iterate is recurrent.  This proves
\eqref{eq:iterate-fix}.  Substitution into the Artin--Mazur definition
\[
 \zeta_{\M_n}(z)=\exp\!\left(
    \sum_{k\geq1}|\Fix(\M_n^k)|\frac{z^k}{k}\right)
\]
and separation of odd and even $k$ gives \eqref{eq:zeta}.
\end{proof}

The first coefficients of \eqref{eq:rec-egf} are
\[
 R_0,\ldots,R_7=1,1,2,8,38,220,1540,12460,
\]
whereas $I_1,\ldots,I_7=1,2,4,10,26,76,232$.

\section{The inverse atlas and its Bell ceiling}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(iv)]
Fix a target $g$.  For every $i\in U_g$, any source above $g$ must contain
symbol $i$ first at position $g(i)$.  These forced positions must be
distinct, proving the zero case in the definition of $\Phi_n$.  A fixed
coordinate $i$ either represents an absent symbol or a symbol first
appearing at $i$.  The latter choice is impossible when position $i$ is
already in $g(U_g)$, so the optional present symbols are exactly a subset
$A\subseteq F_g$.

Once $A$ is chosen, the positions $r_A(i)$ are forced to carry $i$.  At an
unforced position $j$, the legal letters are precisely the symbols already
opened before $j$, namely
$\{i\in P_A:r_A(i)<j\}$.  These choices are independent across unforced
positions.  Their product, summed over $A$, is \eqref{eq:phi}; conversely,
every word constructed this way has target $g$.  This proves the
every-target identity \eqref{eq:fibre}, including the support test
$g\in\Y_n\Longleftrightarrow\Phi_n(g)>0$.

Now fix the kernel partition of a possible source.  A block with minimum
position $j$ has a uniquely determined label: it is the unique
off-diagonal $i$ with $g(i)=j$ if such an $i$ exists, and otherwise it can
only be $j$.  Hence at most one source over $g$ realizes each kernel
partition, giving the upper bound $B_n$.  Over $g=\id$, every set partition
does occur: label each block by its least position.  Present labels then
first occur at themselves and every other label is absent.  Distinct
partitions give distinct sources, proving \eqref{eq:bell}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(v)]
The case $n=1$ is immediate.  At $n=2$ the word dynamics are
\[
 00\mapsto01,\qquad01\mapsto01,\qquad
 10\mapsto10,\qquad11\mapsto00,
\]
which gives the second row.  At $n=3$, the connected census gives
$R_3=1+3+4=8$, while the involutions give $I_3=4$ and hence two dynamical
two-orbits.  Direct substitution of the $27$ targets into
\eqref{eq:phi} gives the nonzero-fibre histogram
\[
 6u+5u^2+2u^3+u^5,
\]
so $|\Y_3|=14$ and the maximum is five.  The two height entries follow from
part~(i).  This proves the boundary table.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact controls and scope}

A standalone standard-library verifier reconstructs \eqref{eq:literal}
without importing the scouting implementation.  It exhausts every state
and target through $n=7$, every path order and canonical cycle through size
nine, the component EGF through order fourteen, positive iterates through
six, all target fibres, Bell injections, sharp witnesses, and the complete
graphs at $n=1,2,3$.  Its frozen run records $12{,}603{,}676$ exact
assertions.  Two fresh processes produced byte-identical transcripts.
These computations are falsification controls; the proofs above are
uniform and do not rely on enumeration.

The off-diagonal injection in Lemma~\ref{lem:components} is necessary but
not sufficient for first-image membership; the product
\eqref{eq:phi} is the exact test.  The Bell bound does not assert that the
identity is the unique maximizing target.  The paper gives a finite exact
theorem for the stated identity-default selector and makes no assertion
about other completions of a kernel section.
