## The decisive CFL distinction

Bonizzoni et al., *Numeric Lyndon-based feature embedding of sequencing reads
for machine learning approaches*, arXiv:2202.13884v2 (2022-06-02), §3.1,
defines the fingerprint from the lengths of a string's factorization.
The actual §3.3 workflow outputs fingerprints, k-fingers and superfingerprints;
the read portions do not specify feeding a numeric length word back into CFL.
This limited observation is not an absence or novelty proof.
[Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.13884v2).
The archived returns include the definition, CFL ordering, collision discussion,
and workflow (WEB05, WEB06, WEB08).

P139 was then read directly, not inferred from its filename:
[historical main.tex](../../../../papers/139-lyndon-factor-start-feedback/main.tex).
Its literal forward map on binary words is the bit mask of CFL factor starts.
It is **not** identical to replacing the word by its factor lengths, which
changes the carrier and later factorization alphabet.

However, P139 §5 maps every possible target mask beginning in one to its ordered
factor-length composition. Its complete inverse consists of all nonincreasing
chains of Lyndon words with those prescribed lengths; the following corollary
counts them through rectangular comparison matrices, with the one-factor
necklace census and all-singleton cell as special cases. These passages were
read to EOF in LOCAL03, together with the preceding literal and temporal
sections in LOCAL02.

Thus reusing prescribed-length Lyndon chains, comparison matrices or the
one-factor Möbius count does not supply an independent fresh second axis for
the length-feedback hint. Generalizing the alphabet alone was not a rescue.
This is a specific inverse-mechanism subtraction, not an asserted conjugacy
between the two forward systems. A decreasing word-length budget by itself
would not remedy the missing second axis or establish a sharp clock.

## Convex-profile boundary

Abramson and Pitman, *Concave Majorants of Random Walks and Related Poisson
Processes*, arXiv:1011.3262v2 (2011-07-04), introduces ordered face lengths.
Its assumption A excludes equal means of distinct increment subsets; §6 instead
distinguishes maximal faces, contact/excursion blocks and intermediate segments
when ties occur. Theorem 22 describes their nested composition law.
Section 7 / Theorem 23 conditions a walk on its full concave majorant.
[Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/1011.3262v2).

For a finite integer-word feedback hint, equal slopes cannot silently be
discarded, and a law conditioned on an entire profile cannot simply be relabeled
as an evaluated preimage count for an ordered length target. The desk obtained
neither that finite target-specific evaluation nor a sufficient temporal theorem.
This is a value-gate rejection, not a claim that the paper already studies the
same feedback map. Negating heights to switch convex/concave convention does
not create a second nomination. WEB07–WEB08 preserve the actual passages.

The HTML rendering prints an additional 2026 date inside the body; its arXiv
version header and metadata identify v2 as 2011-07-04. Both observations are
retained in the provider returns; the rendering date is not treated as a new
paper version. No formula requiring assumption A was imported into a
tie-containing finite carrier.
