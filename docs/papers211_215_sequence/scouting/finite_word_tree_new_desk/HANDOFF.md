### Binary-tree leaf erasure followed by chain suppression

Hackl, Heuberger and Prodinger define a partial map Phi on nontrivial full
binary plane trees: erase all leaves, merge unary parent-child pairs until
none remain, then regard childless survivors as leaves. Their full version,
arXiv:1612.07286v1, Proposition 2.1's proof gives the literal reduction and
reconstructs all predecessors of a given reduced tree by replacing its
nodes with chains. The chain series is C(z)=z/(1−2z). Proposition 2.2 and
its complete short proof give Reg(Phi(t))=Reg(t)−1; equation (3) identifies
the exact number of reductions to the leaf.

Thus the apparently promising clock and inverse-expansion mechanisms
already occur together in the direct source. Merely fixing the terminal
leaf and restricting to trees with at most N internal nodes would make a
finite total carrier; it would not supply a residual theorem. That
terminal convention/carrier restriction is not counted as a new candidate.

Important distinctions from actual old originals:

- P114, main.tex lines 62–82, deletes nonroot leaves from a labelled rooted
  forest and preserves its roots. It does not suppress the newly unary
  chains. No literal equality or conjugacy with Phi is asserted.
- P148, main.tex lines 60–105, retains even-depth vertices and promotes
  ordered grandchild blocks, resetting parity. This differs from Phi;
  P148 itself calls Horton-style pruning nearby but different.
- Old TUS, SCOUT.md lines 323–338, suppresses nonroot unary vertices only
  and leaves the root uncontracted. It is another distinct rule; its
  inverse-chain mechanism is already subtracted, not a new second axis.

Decision: direct source owns the reduction, exact register clock and full
inverse-expansion mechanism. No fresh labelled or scheduling modification
with a separate resolved residual was established; no weak variant was
manufactured to fill an attempt slot.
