## Direct primary sources

1. Riverbend Community Math Center, *Candy Sharing Game — Lesson Plan*,
   [primary five-page PDF](https://riverbendmath.org/modules/Candy_Sharing_Game/Lesson_Plan/Candy_Sharing_Lesson_Plan.pdf).
   The actual last page, headed “Taking it Farther – Changing the Rules”,
   states the floor-half transfer / ceiling-half retention variant. It
   discusses equal fixed amounts, unequal fixed amounts and circulating
   excess as possible eventual behavior. It does not supply an all-parameter
   proof or sharp time/fibre theorem. This primary educational document
   establishes a pre-existing rule, not peer-reviewed priority of the
   complete polygon lift. No publication year was inferred from crawl dates.

2. Deepak Bal and Joseph DeGaetani, *A note on a candy sharing game*,
   Art of Discrete and Applied Mathematics 4(1), P1.02 (2021),
   [author PDF](https://msuweb.montclair.edu/~bald/files/candy-sharing.pdf),
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.26493/2590-9770.1304.2cf).
   Actual introduction, generalized rule and Theorem 1.1 were opened.
   Their divisibility-restoring extra candy changes the rule and mass.
   Their logarithmic bounds are not transferred to CGM or $H$.

3. Edsger W. Dijkstra, *The solution to a cyclic relaxation problem*,
   EWD386, August 8, 1973,
   [primary archive transcription](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~EWD/transcriptions/EWD03xx/EWD386.html).
   The complete short transcription was opened. Its point moves to the
   midpoint between its predecessor and successor, with discussion of
   asynchronous updates and finite-precision rounding. That is not the
   current simultaneous midpoint toward one's successor. It supports a
   mature circle-gap/rounding setting, not exact literal ownership of CGM.
