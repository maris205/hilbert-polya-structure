## 2. Isbell conjugation: the antitone identity is already available

For a finite symmetric metric d and nonnegative real-valued f, write

    Qf(i) = max_j max(d(i,j)-f(j),0).

This is the symmetric coordinate form of the source's L/R adjunction.
It reverses pointwise numeric order. For each i,j,

    Qf(j) >= d(j,i)-f(i),

so d(i,j)-Qf(j) <= f(i); taking the positive maximum gives
Q^2 f <= f. Applying Q reverses this inequality and yields Q^3 f >= Qf.
Applying Q^2 g <= g to g=Qf yields the reverse. Therefore Q^3=Q.

Consequently the image is Fix(Q^2), with entry in at most one step and
period one or two. This is not necessarily Fix(Q): for a two-point
distance-one space, (0,0) and (1,1) form a strict two-cycle. The classical
tight-span fixed equation is Qf=f, whereas the Isbell completion uses
conjugate pairs. The source distinguishes these spaces.
[Willerton, sections 2.1, 3.1 and 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1302.4370v1)

## 3. Metric trimming: source-owned descent; finiteness must be proved

For at least three points, let

    p(x) = min_{y,z distinct from x and each other}
                 (d(x,y)+d(x,z)-d(y,z))/2.

For two points p is half their distance; for one point p=0. Subtract
p(x)+p(y) off the off-diagonal distances and quotient zero distances.
This is the named trimming t(X). Its iterates reach a trim core in at most
|X| steps; the source supplies the canonical metric forest connecting X
to that core, and the converse trim-base forest theorem.
[Turaev, sections 2–5](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.06760v1)

“Finite metric space” fixes the number of points, not a finite number of
possible metrics. Nor is the naive integer-distance carrier closed.
A direct hand calculation illustrates this; it is not a numerical run.
Take four points with

    d12=d13=d14=d34=2,    d23=d24=3.

Every triangle has side lengths (2,2,3), (2,2,2), or (3,3,2), so this is a
metric. The three Gromov products at points 1,2,3,4 have minima
1/2,3/2,1,1, respectively. After subtraction the pairs 12 and 34 have
distance zero and every cross distance is 1/2. The quotient is therefore
a two-point metric at distance 1/2, outside the integer-distance carrier.
Its next trim is a singleton. This refutes only naive closure, not every
possible finite discretization.

## 4. Whitehead minimization: named descent, not free cancellation

The read publisher introduction states the classical Whitehead reduction
criterion for cyclically reduced words: a word that is not length-minimal
in its automorphism orbit admits a length-decreasing Whitehead move.
Repeatedly apply such a move and cyclically reduce. Each nonterminal
invocation strictly decreases the nonnegative integer cyclic length, so
there are at most the initial length many decreasing invocations.
This is the standard minimization phase, not a claim about the entire
Whitehead decision algorithm's running time.
[Kapovich–Schupp–Shpilrain, Convention 1.1, Proposition 1.2 and following algorithm](https://msp.org/pjm/2006/223-1/pjm-v223-n1-p06-s.pdf)
