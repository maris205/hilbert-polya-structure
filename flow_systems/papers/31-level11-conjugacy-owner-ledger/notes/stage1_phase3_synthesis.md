## Key themes

### Theme 1 — A finite subgroup model is necessary but not an element-decision certificate

**Strength:** strong (6 sources, evidence level VI)

The modular-subgroup cluster converges on finite, exact ways to represent or recognize finite-index subgroups. Millington (1969) <!--ref:P31-S01--><!--anchor:none--> supplies classification context, while Kulkarni (1991) <!--ref:P31-S02--><!--anchor:none--> and Chan et al. (1993) <!--ref:P31-S03--><!--anchor:none--> supply special-polygon interfaces. Lang et al. (1995) <!--ref:P31-S04--><!--anchor:none--> and Hsu (1996) <!--ref:P31-S05--><!--anchor:none--> address congruence recognition, and Voight (2009) <!--ref:P31-S06--><!--anchor:none--> gives a modern fundamental-domain algorithm surface.

Taken together, this cluster licenses a deterministic `Gamma_0(11)` representation and reduction fixture as a first dependency. It does not license the next inference: recognizing or representing the subgroup is logically weaker than deciding whether two particular hyperbolic elements are conjugate inside it. The Phase-3 synthesis therefore separates `SUBGROUP_MODEL_BOUND` from `PAIR_DECISION_BOUND`; neither state implies the other.

### Theme 2 — Several complete-decision traditions exist, but each misses a frozen P31 axis

**Strength:** strong (12 sources, evidence level VI)

The arithmetic tradition relates matrices to ideal classes through Latimer and MacDuffee (1933) <!--ref:P31-S07--><!--anchor:none-->, Taussky (1949) <!--ref:P31-S08--><!--anchor:none-->, and Wallace (1984) <!--ref:P31-S09--><!--anchor:none-->. Continued-fraction reduction appears in Appelgate and Onishi (1981) <!--ref:P31-S10--><!--anchor:none-->, while Grunewald (1980) <!--ref:P31-S11--><!--anchor:none--> and Grunewald and Segal (1980) <!--ref:P31-S12--><!--anchor:none--> supply arithmetic-group algorithm frameworks. Eick et al. (2019) <!--ref:P31-S13--><!--anchor:none--> provide a modern ambient integral-conjugacy anchor.

A separate hyperbolic-group tradition supplies individual and finite-list interfaces through Epstein and Holt (2006) <!--ref:P31-S16--><!--anchor:none--> and Buckley and Holt (2013) <!--ref:P31-S17--><!--anchor:none-->. Traina (1985) <!--ref:P31-S22--><!--anchor:none--> connects ambient modular conjugacy with real-quadratic class numbers, and Series (1985) <!--ref:P31-S15--><!--anchor:none--> provides modular-geodesic reduction context.

The synthesis does not select a winning tradition. Instead it identifies the same missing bridge across them: an exact specialization must preserve the determinant-one positive-trace lift, membership in `Gamma_0(11)`, orientation rather than inverse collapse, maximal primitive roots, termination, and a replayable positive or negative payload. General solvability and ambient equivalence are useful premises; neither is the project conclusion.

### Theme 3 — Canonicalization requires an explicit orientation, centralizer, and arithmetic-subroutine contract

**Strength:** strong (5 sources, evidence level VI)

Sarnak (1982) <!--ref:P31-S14--><!--anchor:none--> supplies class-number structure useful for aggregate fixtures, while Series (1985) <!--ref:P31-S15--><!--anchor:none--> supplies reduction/coding context. Baake and Roberts (2001) <!--ref:P31-S18--><!--anchor:none--> make the distinction between ordinary and reversing symmetries visible. Lenstra (2002) <!--ref:P31-S19--><!--anchor:none--> and Cohen (1993) <!--ref:P31-S20--><!--anchor:none--> provide Pell-unit and computational-number-theory background that may enter a centralizer parameterization.

These sources are compatible as ingredients but do not choose the P31 convention. The project must still canonicalize `M` and `M^{-1}` independently, retain an inverse-pair link, and prove that any centralizer quotient or Pell cycle used for a negative decision exhausts the eligible conjugators. A class number, a reduction cycle, or a unit algorithm is not by itself the terminal certificate.

### Theme 4 — Census evidence is a control surface, not a pairwise proof

**Strength:** emerging (2 direct sources, evidence level VI)

Golovchanskii and Smotrov (2008) <!--ref:P31-S21--><!--anchor:none--> provide the corpus's closest direct `Gamma_0(N)` primitive-hyperbolic class-counting precedent. Traina (1985) <!--ref:P31-S22--><!--anchor:none--> provides an ambient modular conjugacy/class-number bridge. Their shared value is independent census structure; their shared boundary is that an aggregate count does not identify which frozen rows share an oriented primitive owner.

This distinction clarifies how a future class-count formula could be used: only as a consistency fixture after all 9,453 pair statuses close, never as a substitute for `CONJUGATE_WITNESS` or `NONCONJUGATE_CERTIFIED` rows. It also prevents census agreement from being misreported as validation of the global `G` table or the cell-local `C` estimand.

## Consensus areas

1. A finite exact subgroup representation is a plausible first layer, but it is not the fixed-pair decision layer. This consensus is supported across the subgroup and domain cluster represented by Kulkarni (1991) <!--ref:P31-S02--><!--anchor:none--> and Voight (2009) <!--ref:P31-S06--><!--anchor:none-->.
2. Ambient matrix or modular conjugacy needs an explicit `Gamma_0(11)` restriction before it can support P31. (2019) <!--ref:P31-S13--><!--anchor:none-->, and Traina (1985) <!--ref:P31-S22--><!--anchor:none-->.

Grunewald and Segal (1980) <!--ref:P31-S12--><!--anchor:none--> and Buckley and Holt (2013) <!--ref:P31-S17--><!--anchor:none--> support only the more general algorithm surfaces recorded upstream.
4. Class counts and class-number correspondences are controls rather than individual decisions. Sarnak (1982) <!--ref:P31-S14--><!--anchor:none--> and Golovchanskii and Smotrov (2008) <!--ref:P31-S21--><!--anchor:none--> remain bounded to that aggregate role.

## Research gaps

1. **Methodological gap — subgroup specialization:** no source in the closed corpus supplies the exact bridge from a general conjugacy or domain algorithm to determinant-one positive-trace `Gamma_0(11)` pair decisions with the frozen orientation convention.
2. **Certificate gap — negative decisions:** no source-level support closes the required serialized solution lattice, congruence system, centralizer quotient, exhaustive residue/cycle payload, termination measure, and independent replay for every negative pair.
3. **Root and canonicalization gap:** the corpus contains reduction, centralizer, and Pell-unit ingredients, but no bound P31 maximal-root plus canonical-owner byte contract.
4. **Estimand gap:** the literature does not establish the project-defined separation of global owner table `G`, incidence table `I`, and cell-local table `C`, nor the mapping from pair closure to the frozen moments.
5. **Verification-depth gap:** all substantive citations in this synthesis lack frozen claim-level source locators; COI and retraction status remain unexamined.

These are bounded corpus and implementation gaps. They are not novelty, impossibility, or exhaustive-literature claims.

## Theoretical implications

1. **Representation, decision, and certification are different mathematical layers.** The combination of congruence-recognition literature such as Hsu (1996) <!--ref:P31-S05--><!--anchor:none--> and conjugacy literature such as Eick et al. (2019) <!--ref:P31-S13--><!--anchor:none--> makes this separation explicit.
2. **Aggregate arithmetic structure does not determine owner identity.** Direct class-count information from Golovchanskii and Smotrov (2008) <!--ref:P31-S21--><!--anchor:none--> can constrain a completed census but cannot manufacture its equivalence classes.
3. **Orientation is part of the object, not an implementation flag.** The reversing-symmetry context of Baake and Roberts (2001) <!--ref:P31-S18--><!--anchor:none--> supports retaining a typed distinction, while the actual choice remains the frozen P31 definition.
4. **The `G/I/C` distinction is an estimand theorem that begins only after owner closure.** No literature source is promoted into support for the frozen 138-instance/55-group mapping; its proof remains internal and subsequent.
