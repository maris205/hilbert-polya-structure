## Abstract

This report organizes the evidence base for a finite ownership question arising from a positive time change of the `Gamma_0(11)` geodesic flow. The controlling question asks how a complete global partition of 138 frozen Hecke-output instances into oriented primitive owners would induce a separate, cell-local no-double-credit moment classification across 55 source-word/prime groups. A closed corpus of 22 sources was examined through bibliography construction, metadata- and abstract-level verification, a source-by-claim matrix, thematic synthesis, and an independent Devil's Advocate checkpoint. The synthesis identifies four ordered dependencies: an exact subgroup representation, terminal decisions for all 9,453 unordered instance pairs, deterministic positive and negative certificate serialization with explicit root and inverse handling, and only then construction of the distinct global-owner, incidence, and cell-local objects `G`, `I`, and `C`. The literature supplies credible ingredients for each early dependency, but no included source binds them into the required `Gamma_0(11)` solver or validates the project-specific estimand map. Aggregate class counts remain post-closure controls rather than pairwise proofs. The resulting contribution is a fail-closed research architecture, not an owner census. All scientific endpoints, formal Route-A evaluation, positive arithmetic A2 credit, and Route B remain unexecuted.

**Keywords:** modular geodesic flow; conjugacy ownership; `Gamma_0(11)`; exact certificates; primitive orbits; evidence synthesis

## 1. Introduction

Finite orbit ledgers become mathematically ambiguous when repeated rows can arise from several mechanisms. Two matrices may encode the same primitive conjugacy owner, one matrix may be a proper power of another primitive element, an inverse may represent the opposite orientation, and one owner may recur in several correspondence cells. P31 isolates these mechanisms before any global product or determinant is discussed. Its inherited dynamical object is the positive time change `X_geo/rho_epsilon` on `T^1Y_0(11)`, with the real level-11 newform differential, positivity interval, Hecke normalization, reciprocal log-zeta convention, and period coordinate `k=2y+z` held fixed.

The registered finite population contains exactly 138 output instances arranged in 55 source-word/prime groups. The inherited instance-level split `2/2/134` and the three 55-group diagnostic summaries remain comparison controls. They are not owner counts. The proposed owner is an oriented primitive conjugacy class in `Gamma_0(11)`, represented through a canonical positive-trace determinant-one lift. An exact subgroup witness is required for a positive equivalence decision; equality of trace, length, homology, or another filter is insufficient. Inversion remains separately oriented and linked, and a traversal exponent remains different from a Hecke branch-cycle degree.

This typing discipline matters because P31 asks two linked but nonidentical questions. First, can the finite population be partitioned globally into exact oriented primitive owners? Second, if that partition closes, how should owner identity be mapped into cell-local recurrence estimands without awarding duplicate credit inside a cell or multiplying global owners across cells? The literature review therefore had to distinguish evidence about subgroup representation, conjugacy algorithms, canonical reduction, arithmetic centralizers, aggregate class counts, and estimand construction.

### Research question

**How does a complete global oriented-owner partition of the frozen 138-instance Hecke-output population induce the frozen cell-local no-double-credit moment classification?**

The question is conditional. A global partition must close before the local map is evaluated. The report does not assume that a complete solver exists, that all 9,453 pair decisions can be certified, or that the resulting taxonomy will preserve any inherited percentage. Its purpose is to state what the frozen evidence supports, what it does not support, and which proof obligations must close before scientific execution.

## 2. Literature and Theoretical Framework

### 2.1 Finite subgroup representations

The first source family addresses exact descriptions of finite-index modular subgroups. Millington (1969)<!--ref:P31-S01--><!--anchor:none:--> supplies foundational subgroup-classification context. Kulkarni (1991)<!--ref:P31-S02--><!--anchor:none:--> and Chan et al. (1993)<!--ref:P31-S03--><!--anchor:none:--> develop arithmetic-geometric and special-polygon interfaces that can inform finite domain data. Lang et al. (1995)<!--ref:P31-S04--><!--anchor:none:--> and Hsu (1996)<!--ref:P31-S05--><!--anchor:none:--> contribute congruence-recognition and finite presentation machinery. Voight (2009)<!--ref:P31-S06--><!--anchor:none:--> provides a later exact computational surface for Fuchsian fundamental domains.

These works support a bounded conclusion: a finite, replayable subgroup model is a plausible component of the proposed program. They do not decide whether a supplied pair of hyperbolic elements is conjugate inside the fixed subgroup, and they do not determine P31's serialization or orientation rules. A representation layer and a pair-decision layer must therefore have separate contracts and separate terminal states.

### 2.2 Ambient conjugacy and arithmetic decision routes

The second family contains several traditions that could contribute to a complete decision procedure. Latimer and MacDuffee (1933)<!--ref:P31-S07--><!--anchor:none:-->, Taussky (1949)<!--ref:P31-S08--><!--anchor:none:-->, and Wallace (1984)<!--ref:P31-S09--><!--anchor:none:--> connect integral matrix conjugacy with ideal-class structure. Appelgate and Onishi (1981)<!--ref:P31-S10--><!--anchor:none:--> provide a continued-fraction route for ambient `SL_2(Z)` conjugacy. Grunewald (1980)<!--ref:P31-S11--><!--anchor:none:--> and Grunewald and Segal (1980)<!--ref:P31-S12--><!--anchor:none:--> provide arithmetic-group algorithm frameworks, while Eick et al. (2019)<!--ref:P31-S13--><!--anchor:none:--> offer a more modern treatment of conjugacy in `GL(n,Z)`.

General hyperbolic-group algorithms form another route. Epstein and Holt (2006)<!--ref:P31-S16--><!--anchor:none:--> address individual conjugacy in word-hyperbolic groups, and Buckley and Holt (2013)<!--ref:P31-S17--><!--anchor:none:--> address finite lists. These sources make termination and batch decision plausible as interfaces. They do not automatically impose the positive-trace determinant-one lift, the `c congruent to 0 mod 11` subgroup condition, the oriented owner convention, maximal primitive roots, or a field-complete negative-certificate payload.

No route is selected by the synthesis. A polygon reduction may provide transparent subgroup geometry; an arithmetic route may expose ideals, units, and integral equations; a hyperbolic-group route may provide general decision bounds. The relevant selection criterion is not prestige, date, or implementation convenience. It is whether a specialization can prove its preconditions and termination, emit exact positive witnesses, and serialize a complete obstruction for every negative decision.

### 2.3 Reduction, inversion, centralizers, and arithmetic subroutines

Canonical owner bytes require more than a yes-or-no conjugacy result. Sarnak (1982)<!--ref:P31-S14--><!--anchor:none:--> supplies class-number structure that may inform aggregate fixtures. Series (1985)<!--ref:P31-S15--><!--anchor:none:--> provides modular-geodesic coding and reduction context. Baake and Roberts (2001)<!--ref:P31-S18--><!--anchor:none:--> make centralizers and reversing symmetries visible as distinct structures. Lenstra (2002)<!--ref:P31-S19--><!--anchor:none:--> and Cohen (1993)<!--ref:P31-S20--><!--anchor:none:--> supply Pell-unit and computational-number-theory background that may be useful in a centralizer quotient or ideal-arithmetic implementation.

Their admissible role is preparatory. None of these sources chooses the P31 convention that `M` and `M^{-1}` are canonicalized independently and connected by an inverse-pair relation. A reduction cycle also does not establish that every eligible conjugator has been exhausted. Any eventual negative certificate must connect the integral solution lattice, determinant and congruence conditions, centralizer action, a finite reduction domain, and a replayable termination argument.

### 2.4 Direct census precedents

Golovchanskii and Smotrov (2008)<!--ref:P31-S21--><!--anchor:none:--> provide the closest included precedent for class counts of primitive hyperbolic elements directly in `Gamma_0(N)`. Traina (1985)<!--ref:P31-S22--><!--anchor:none:--> connects modular-group conjugacy with real-quadratic class numbers. These sources strengthen the available census context, but their level of aggregation is decisive. A total count cannot specify which two frozen rows share an owner, cannot return an oriented conjugator, and cannot prove that the all-pairs ledger has zero unresolved entries.

The theoretical framework therefore assigns class counts a narrow role. They may test a completed partition for arithmetic consistency. They cannot construct that partition and cannot override a contradictory pair certificate.

## 3. Executed Literature Methodology

### 3.1 Design and closed corpus

The executed method was an evidence-synthesis pipeline, not the scientific owner-census methodology described for future work. Phase 1 fixed the research question, object, equivalence relation, population, estimands, controls, and kill gates. Phase 2 then captured 44 bibliographic records, removed nine duplicate manifestations, screened 35 unique records, excluded 13, and retained 22 sources. The inclusion frame covered modular-subgroup models, integral and subgroup conjugacy, reduction and centralizers, primitive roots, and direct modular class-count precedents. Sources confined to global determinants, unrelated groups, heuristics without a completeness interface, or redundant manifestations were excluded.

The independent verification pass matched all 22 source identities to DOI, publisher, journal, institutional, or authoritative records within the recorded scope. Nineteen sources were classified as peer-reviewed. The P31-S16 page range was corrected under the authorized metadata process to 287–305. Source verification remained mostly at metadata, abstract, and authoritative landing-page level. It did not inspect every theorem hypothesis, run a complete retraction or conflict-of-interest screen, or generate claim-level quotation, page, or section locators.

### 3.2 Matrix construction and thematic synthesis

Every source was assigned one support class, an admitted contribution, an excluded stronger claim, and an applicability warning in a 22-row literature matrix. The synthesis used all 22 IDs and grouped them into four overlapping themes: finite subgroup/domain interfaces; ambient and hyperbolic conjugacy routes; orientation, reduction, and arithmetic subroutines; and direct census precedents. Overlap was allowed because one work can inform more than one dependency without receiving broader claim authority.

Potential tensions were treated as typed comparisons, not theorem disputes. Aggregate census evidence was compared with specified-pair algorithms; subgroup recognition was compared with finite-list conjugacy; reduction coding was compared with reversal structure; and general arithmetic algorithms were compared with direct subgroup counts. Two procedural reconciliations remain subject to scholar confirmation. No tension entry licenses a theorem-level resolution.

An independent Devil's Advocate checkpoint returned `PASS` with zero Critical and zero Major findings. Its two Minor findings constrain this report. The phrase that literature licenses a deterministic representation must mean only that it licenses an attempt to bind one. Likewise, any synthesis-level resolution remains proposed while scholar confirmation is pending.

### 3.3 Analytical rule

The synthesis used a strict nonpromotion rule. A source could support only the contribution recorded in its verification row and matrix. Ambient conjugacy could not become subgroup conjugacy, an aggregate count could not become a pair decision, and an algorithm-existence statement could not become project certificate bytes. Search non-detection was recorded as a bounded gap rather than novelty or impossibility. ## 4. Evidence-Synthesis Findings

### 4.1 The dependency graph has four noninterchangeable layers

The first finding is architectural. The evidence supports organizing future work into four ordered layers. Layer one binds an exact `Gamma_0(11)` presentation or domain, its membership rule, and a terminating reduction procedure. Layer two specializes one complete conjugacy decision route to the frozen positive-trace, determinant-one, oriented subgroup problem. Layer three defines primitive-root, inverse-link, canonical-byte, positive-witness, and negative-obstruction schemas with independent replay. Layer four, which begins only after all pair decisions close, constructs `G`, `I`, and `C` and applies the frozen estimand rules.

The order is substantive. A subgroup model cannot stand in for pair decisions. A pairwise decision library cannot stand in for canonical, independently replayable certificates. A completed partition cannot by itself determine how repeated incidences are weighted in separate cells. Each transition needs an explicit theorem or validation surface.

### 4.2 Pair closure is the scientific bottleneck

The future all-pairs ledger contains `binom(138,2)=9,453` unordered pairs after exact primitive-root replacement. Every pair must terminate as invariant-excluded, positively conjugate with a witness, or negatively nonconjugate with a completeness certificate. The frozen scientific endpoint requires zero unresolved rows. The literature supplies several candidate algorithm families, but the synthesis found no bound source-to-implementation contract that already meets every P31 field requirement.

This gap is not a negative result about the existence of such a solver. It is an evidence and implementation boundary. If the theorem contract, fixture manifest, or independent replay cannot close, the correct endpoint is `NOT_EVALUABLE_CONJUGACY_INCOMPLETE`. The owner partition and downstream taxonomies must then remain unconstructed.

### 4.3 Owner identity and correspondence incidence must remain separate

The project defines three outputs with different units. `G` has one row per global oriented primitive owner. `I` preserves all 138 original instances and their source, prime, branch, cycle, and Hecke-degree coordinates. `C` retains one unit per owner inside each `(source_word, prime, hecke_degree)` cell while preserving raw multiplicity in `I`. An owner may appear in several groups and contribute once to each distinct group equation, but it remains one row in `G`.

No reviewed source establishes this project-specific map. Its validity must be proved internally after owner closure. The distinction also explains why the inherited `2/2/134` instance split, the 55-group law counts, and a future `|G|` denominator cannot replace one another.

### 4.4 Aggregate arithmetic evidence is a control, not a shortcut

Direct `Gamma_0(N)` counts are valuable because they offer a near-neighbor census surface. Yet an aggregate agreement can coexist with an incorrect partition: different erroneous merges and splits can preserve a total. The evidence synthesis therefore places census checks after pair closure. This ordering is a concrete safeguard against validating a global owner ledger through a statistic that contains less information than the ledger itself.

### 4.5 The report advances readiness without advancing scientific state

The principal progress is sharper than a generic literature review. The source base is mapped to exact proof obligations and failure states. Competing algorithm families remain visible, the decisive missing bridge is identified, and the downstream estimands are typed. At the same time, no owner decision has been made. `G`, `I`, and `C` do not exist as scientific outputs, and the inherited population summaries have not been recomputed.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Interpretation

The literature makes a complete P31 program conceivable but does not establish feasibility. Its strength lies in complementary components: finite subgroup models, ambient and arithmetic conjugacy theories, hyperbolic-group algorithms, reduction and unit machinery, and direct subgroup census precedents. Its weakness lies precisely at their junctions. P31 requires a single specialization to preserve subgroup membership, determinant, orientation, primitivity, termination, canonical serialization, and negative-certificate replay at once.

The strongest counterargument is that metadata- and abstract-level evidence cannot show that any candidate tradition admits the needed specialization. That objection is correct. The report therefore does not choose an implementation or predict closure. It treats the four-layer graph as a disciplined way to discover whether the program is evaluable.

### 5.2 Theoretical implications

Three distinctions carry beyond this finite population. First, representing a group, deciding conjugacy, and certifying a decision are separate mathematical tasks. Second, orientation belongs to the object definition; it cannot be repaired later by a display convention. Third, global identity and local incidence define different estimands even when they use the same underlying rows.

These implications remain scoped to research design. They do not establish a new theorem about `Gamma_0(11)`, a complete primitive spectrum, or a global dynamical determinant. P31 remains an A1 ownership investigation.

### 5.3 Practical implications

The next scientific preparation should produce immutable contracts before reading pair outcomes. The subgroup model needs a versioned proof and hash. The chosen pair route needs exact preconditions and a termination theorem. Root, inverse, positive, and negative schemas need adversarial fixtures and a read-only verifier. Only after those surfaces close should the 9,453-row ledger be executed.

This sequence also improves failure reporting. An unbound theorem, incomplete fixture set, unresolved pair, inconsistent owner coordinate, or within-group degree conflict already has a distinct terminal label. A failed attempt can therefore yield a precise not-evaluable result without being misreported as scientific nonconjugacy or failure of a recurrence law.

## 6. Limitations and Future Work

The evidence base has five principal limitations. First, all claim-level locators are absent, so the report cannot attest that theorem statements and hypotheses have been inspected at the exact passage level. Second, conflict-of-interest and general retraction screens were not run. Third, the corpus is historically weighted, which is appropriate for foundational mathematics but does not establish implementation readiness or correction completeness. Fourth, the direct `Gamma_0(N)` sources concern aggregate structure rather than the exact frozen pair interface. Fifth, the global `G/I/C` mapping is a project definition and still needs a self-contained proof.

Future work should proceed in a fail-closed order:

1. bind one exact subgroup representation and reduction contract;
2. compare candidate conjugacy routes against every required P31 certificate field;
3. freeze root, inverse, canonical serialization, and positive/negative replay fixtures;
4. execute all 9,453 pair decisions only after independent validation reports zero unresolved preexecution fixtures; and
5. construct and separately validate `G`, `I`, `C`, the conflict matrix, the global kernel taxonomy, and the 55 group-law equations.

No recommendation authorizes that execution in this phase. It states the next research package that could be proposed for separate approval.

## 7. Conclusion and Bounded Recommendations

The 22-source evidence base supports a precise answer to the composition-stage question. A global owner partition can induce the cell-local classification only through an ordered proof chain: exact subgroup representation, complete specified-pair closure, canonical certificate serialization, and a separately proved mapping into `G`, `I`, and `C`. The literature informs each link but closes none of them for P31. Aggregate class counts are controls, inverse orientation remains explicit, Hecke degree remains distinct from traversal power, and cell recurrence does not multiply global owners.

The immediate recommendation is to prepare a theorem-to-certificate binding package rather than run the owner census. If that binding cannot prove complete negative decisions, the program should report `NOT_EVALUABLE_CONJUGACY_INCOMPLETE`. If it closes, the finite ledger may then be executed under a new authorization. Either outcome would be informative without overstating the evidence.
