## Major findings

### `P31-DA-001` — all 9,453 bespoke pair certificates are treated as logically necessary without ruling out a complete canonicalization proof

- **Severity:** Major.
- **Type:** Method; frame lock; possible false necessity.
- **Exact report evidence:** The Abstract makes terminal decisions for all
  9,453 pairs the second ordered dependency. Sections 4.1 and 4.2 define pair
  closure as the scientific bottleneck and require every pair to terminate.
  Section 5.3 again places the 9,453-row ledger before construction of `G`,
  `I`, and `C`; Section 6 repeats zero-unresolved all-pairs closure as a
  necessary future step. Claim-intent surfaces C-002 and C-005 freeze the same
  premise.
- **Finding:** A complete canonical-form theorem can, in principle, certify a
  representative for each rooted oriented class with a biconditional:
  canonical bytes agree if and only if the owners are conjugate. If that
  theorem and each of 138 per-instance certificates are independently
  replayable, all pair relations follow from equality or inequality of
  canonical bytes. Transitivity then derives the partition without a distinct
  exhaustive nonconjugacy payload for every pair. The report does not show why
  such a route is invalid; it elevates the all-pairs audit chosen in Phase 1
  into a mathematical necessity.
- **Impact:** The design can emit `NOT_EVALUABLE_CONJUGACY_INCOMPLETE` merely
  because 9,453 pair payloads were not materialized even when an equally
  rigorous global partition certificate exists. That affects feasibility,
  the claimed scientific bottleneck, and the four-layer contribution. It does
  not license aggregate counts or heuristic clustering; it requires the
  report to distinguish completeness of the equivalence relation from one
  particular certificate expansion.

## Observations

### `P31-DA-002` — the all-pairs table remains a useful adversarial audit even if not foundational

- **Severity:** Observation.
- **Exact report evidence:** Sections 2.4 and 4.4 explain why aggregate counts
  can conceal compensating false merges and splits.
- **Finding:** A derived all-pairs table is a strong regression and audit
  artifact. The objection is to making its independently serialized 9,453
  negative decisions the only admissible proof architecture, not to retaining
  it as a check.

## Strongest steel-manned counterargument

> The object to be proved is a partition, not a bag of unrelated pair
> decisions. A complete normal-form or canonical-cycle theorem with replayable
> per-instance witnesses can prove the whole equivalence relation at once.
> Requiring a separate exhaustive negative certificate for each of 9,453
> pairs may multiply one theorem consequence into thousands of artifacts and
> misidentify storage expansion as the scientific bottleneck.

This counterargument still demands exact subgroup applicability, orientation,
roots, termination, serialization, and independent checking. It rejects only
the claim that pairwise materialization is the unique route to those goals.

## Missing evidence and perspectives

- an argument that no complete canonicalization biconditional can discharge
  the frozen equivalence relation;
- a comparison of pair-ledger, per-instance normal-form, and class-level
  spanning-certificate architectures under the same exactness standard;
- a proof-level distinction between a required scientific certificate and a
  derived audit expansion;
- a complexity or failure-mode analysis showing what the 9,453 independent
  payloads detect that canonical-class certificates cannot; and
- a perspective from certified partition or union-find proof systems that
  preserves negative completeness without one bespoke proof per pair.
