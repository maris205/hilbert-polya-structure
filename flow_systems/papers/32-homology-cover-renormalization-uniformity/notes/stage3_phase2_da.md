Evidence Anchor: text: §Frozen candidate factors "The two normalizations must be visible at different levels."

### Strongest Counter-Argument

The strongest counter-case is that the manuscript constructs elaborate formal escape routes around a conditional comparison that appears available much earlier. Once the proposed positive-content factor, zero-content factor, and base factor are granted, their scalar-function forms can be compared directly on a simple real domain. For fixed higher content greater than one, the stabilized expression changes both the exponential scale and multiplicity; for zero content and modulus greater than one, the displayed expression likewise differs structurally from the base factor. The formulas are not proved, so no unconditional obstruction follows, but the paper could state a conditional lemma: if the displayed derivations close, then equality does or does not hold as scalar functions under explicit hypotheses. Instead, it postpones even that conditional conclusion until an inverse-limit object, localization, singleton projection, and separate Hahn-type object are constructed. That makes the formal apparatus look capable of insulating the proposal from an elementary adverse implication. The claimed falsification-first reordering is also selected from the visual shape of unproved factors, so its information advantage is asserted rather than demonstrated. A sharper program would first derive one ownerwise factor and immediately test the weakest sufficient equality notion; only a surviving scalar comparison should motivate richer formal topology. On this view, the paper correctly avoids claiming an executed obstruction, but it has not yet shown why the unresolved formal objects are logically prior to a conditional scalar no-go. The central architecture is repairable by separating factor derivation from the minimal comparison theorem and treating formal products as downstream only when scalar survival makes them necessary.
