## Key themes

### Theme 1 — Canonical owner enumeration is a composite interface, not a single algorithm call

**Strength:** strong (6 sources, evidence level VI)

Cannon (1984) <!--ref:P32-S01--><!--anchor:none--> supplies combinatorial structure for cocompact hyperbolic groups. Epstein and Holt (2006) <!--ref:P32-S02--><!--anchor:none-->, Bridson and Howie (2005) <!--ref:P32-S03--><!--anchor:none-->, and Buckley and Holt (2013) <!--ref:P32-S04--><!--anchor:none--> supply individual, finite-subset, and finite-list conjugacy interfaces. Lysenok (1990) <!--ref:P32-S05--><!--anchor:none--> supplies the strongest verified root-extraction surface in the corpus. Wang et al. (2025) <!--ref:P32-S06--><!--anchor:none--> supply a recent surface-specific normal-form and root-finding candidate, but only as a non-peer-reviewed preprint in a presentation not yet mapped to the frozen one.

The cluster supports decomposing `SG2OwnerCanonical-v1` into equality, oriented conjugacy, maximal root, deterministic serialization, and prefix-completeness subcontracts. It does not support collapsing those subcontracts. In particular, deciding conjugacy among a supplied finite list is weaker than proving that a requested prefix contains every earlier eligible owner and has `unresolved=0`.

### Theme 2 — Cover and homology literature supplies structure, not the frozen normalization theorem

**Strength:** strong (6 sources, evidence level VI)

Jassim (1984) <!--ref:P32-S07--><!--anchor:none--> and Sunada (1985) <!--ref:P32-S08--><!--anchor:none--> supply finite abelian or Riemannian-cover context. Phillips and Sarnak (1987) <!--ref:P32-S09--><!--anchor:none-->, Katsuda and Sunada (1988) <!--ref:P32-S10--><!--anchor:none-->, and Katsuda and Sunada (1990) <!--ref:P32-S11--><!--anchor:none--> supply homology-sensitive geodesic or closed-orbit context. Cornelissen and Peyerimhoff (2023) <!--ref:P32-S12--><!--anchor:none--> provide modern decomposition and prime-geodesic splitting language.

These sources make the order of a homology image, splitting into lifts, and homology-stratum abundance plausible theorem interfaces. They do not establish the P32 formula for every oriented primitive owner, do not choose the paired `1/N` and `1/N^3` scalings, and do not prove that primitive lifts remain primitive under the frozen construction. Those statements must be derived from the exact deck action and owner content before scalar or product language is used.

### Theme 3 — Product and continuation theory does not imply ownerwise renormalized recovery

**Strength:** strong (6 sources, evidence level VI; one `PLAUSIBLE`, one correction-bound)

Selberg (1956) <!--ref:P32-S13--><!--anchor:none--> supplies only `PLAUSIBLE` historical trace/zeta context under the Phase-2 existence label. Ruelle (1976) <!--ref:P32-S14--><!--anchor:none--> supplies primitive-orbit dynamical-zeta context. Parry and Pollicott (1983) <!--ref:P32-S15--><!--anchor:none--> supply prime-orbit asymptotic context, while Brenner and Spinu (2009) <!--ref:P32-S16--><!--anchor:none--> supply finite-cover Selberg-zeta Artin formalism.

Giulietti et al. The collective implication is bounded: product and continuation frameworks exist, and finite-cover factorization is a relevant neighbor. None of them identifies P32's independent owner variables with a scalar product, proves the frozen coefficientwise identity, or licenses passage through an infinite homology-cover schedule.

### Theme 4 — Asymptotic counting is upstream of, but weaker than, the required compact-uniform tail

**Strength:** moderate (4 direct sources, evidence level VI)

Lalley (1989) <!--ref:P32-S19--><!--anchor:none--> supplies renewal and geodesic-counting tools. Margulis (1969) <!--ref:P32-S20--><!--anchor:none--> supplies foundational growth context. Sharp (1993) <!--ref:P32-S21--><!--anchor:none--> refines closed-orbit asymptotics by homology class, and Pollicott and Sharp (1998) <!--ref:P32-S22--><!--anchor:none--> supply an exponential-error-term surface for negatively curved surfaces.

These sources are candidates for a tail theorem, not an already verified tail theorem for P32. The missing inference is explicit: a headline asymptotic or error term must be transformed into an absolute bound for the exact logarithmic summand, uniform on every `K(delta,T,R)`, stable across the owner strata used, and strong enough for each iterated and diagonal limit order. Until that extraction is proved, only finite specialization is licensed.

5. **A content-one analytic theorem, if later proved, remains a restricted subproduct.** It cannot erase higher- or zero-content ownerwise mismatches, relabel the product as full, or repair the residual inverse-limit object.
