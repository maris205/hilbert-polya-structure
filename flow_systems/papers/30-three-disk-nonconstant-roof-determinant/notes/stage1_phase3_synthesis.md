## Theme 3 — Internal calibration is roof-agnostic

Ruelle (1976) <!--ref:P30-S13--><!--anchor:none--> supports a periodic-zeta and

<!--ref:P30-S15--><!--anchor:none--> and Fried (1995)
<!--ref:P30-S16--><!--anchor:none--> extend the relevant zeta/operator context
under additional hyperbolic or analytic assumptions.

The synthesis consequence is methodological: once a roof and operator are
consistently specified within a licensed theorem class, Euler, periodic-trace,
and determinant expressions are organizations of that same typed object.
Thus internal agreement can be expected for a unit roof, an admissible
pointwise shuffled roof, or neighboring-geometry roof as well as for the
physical roof. Such agreement tests bookkeeping and operator consistency; it
does not distinguish which roof represents physical Euclidean flight length.
That distinction requires independent geometric fidelity and a separately
licensed cross-roof non-equivalence test.

This theme also fixes a type firewall: the classical algebra cannot acquire
support merely by importing the quantum trace-class status discussed by

construction of Gaspard and Rice (1989)
<!--ref:P30-S03--><!--anchor:none-->.

**Theme strength:** strong for the roof-agnostic calibration principle;
insufficient for physical specificity or the unconstructed project operator's
nuclearity.

The corpus therefore supports four separate questions rather than one

3. Is quadrature or determinant-evaluation error bounded for the selected

4. Is roundoff bounded under a frozen precision and stopping rule?

A full function or zero comparison requires all four answers plus a common
coefficient/projection map. No admitted source row jointly instantiates those

**Theme strength:** moderate for separable numerical components; insufficient
for the common comparison object or complete error identity.

## Theme 5 — Livsic logic is asymmetric

Livsic (1972) <!--ref:P30-S23--><!--anchor:none--> supplies the foundational
periodic-data obstruction to cohomological triviality under stated hyperbolic
or symbolic hypotheses. de la Llave et al. (1986)
<!--ref:P30-S24--><!--anchor:none--> supplies regularity and perturbative
foundations for the cohomology equation. Kalinin (2011)
<!--ref:P30-S25--><!--anchor:none--> is a stronger matrix-cocycle context than
the scalar roof question requires, and Sharp (2026)
<!--ref:P30-S26--><!--anchor:none--> concerns abelian extensions for
homologically full transitive Anosov flows under stated assumptions.

The compatible use is directional. After the exact project system satisfies a
relevant theorem's hypotheses, a certified periodic-data mismatch can serve
as a negative obstruction to one proposed global scale/coboundary relation.
Finite agreement cannot establish global equivalence, and the stronger
matrix/abelian results do not automatically apply to the open three-disk
repeller. The closed corpus itself contains no project-specific nontransfer
result.

**Theme strength:** strong for the asymmetry of negative obstruction versus
positive equivalence; insufficient for exact open-billiard applicability or
an executed witness.
