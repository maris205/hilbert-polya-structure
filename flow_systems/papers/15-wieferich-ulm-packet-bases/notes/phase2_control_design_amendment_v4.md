### 3.3 Exact FD-5 frames, nonce, and direct P decision

FD 5 carries only these four payload forms:

~~~text
requester -> P:
AUDIT_OPEN audit=DEC serial=DEC

P -> requester:
AUDIT_CHALLENGE audit=DEC serial=DEC nonce=LOWERHEX64

requester -> P:
AUDITED_SPAWN audit=DEC serial=DEC nonce=LOWERHEX64 digest=LOWERHEX64 trigger=TRIGGER core=LOWERHEX

P -> requester:
AUDIT_RECEIPT audit=DEC serial=DEC nonce=LOWERHEX64 digest=LOWERHEX64
~~~

Continuation above is presentational.  Each frame is one complete seqpacket
containing exactly the shown canonical ASCII payload, at most 4096 bytes,
with no NUL, LF, separate length prefix, rights, explicit credentials,
requester-supplied ancillary item, or trailing byte.  The sole ancillary
record observed by P is the kernel-supplied credential required below.
DEC is canonical nonnegative decimal.  Serial
starts at 0 per audit endpoint, increases by exactly one after each terminal
serial, and has at most one open value.

The requester sends its two outbound frames with plain send.  P receives
each with recvmsg and requires exactly one kernel-supplied SCM_CREDENTIALS,
no other cmsg, and neither MSG_TRUNC nor MSG_CTRUNC.  In P's initial
namespaces the credential must be the registered requester outer PID and
outer uid/gid 65534/65534.  P independently uses the retained uid/gid maps,
stable NSpid chain, pidfd/start time, and cgroup registration to normalize
that tuple to the same requester inner PID and inner uid/gid 0/0.  The
requester's existing seccomp filter continues to deny sendmsg and sendmmsg.
P replies with plain send and no ancillary item.

For a valid AUDIT_OPEN, P computes the sole nonce as lowercase SHA-256 of
these exact ASCII bytes, with canonical decimals and no NUL or LF:

~~~text
P15R-AUDIT-NONCE-v1 audit=<AUDIT_DEC> requester_session=<SESSION_DEC> requester_child=<CHILD_DEC> serial=<SERIAL_DEC>
~~~

The audit handle is never reused and serial is strict, so a nonce is unique.
A nonce is consumed by either one receipt or one failure and is never
reissued.

LOWERHEX is an even, nonempty sequence of lowercase hexadecimal digits with
no prefix.  LOWERHEX64 is exactly 64 lowercase hexadecimal digits.  The
decoded core is nonempty, at most 512 bytes, and exactly:

~~~text
SPAWN request=DEC session=DEC target=TARGET method=METHOD purpose=PURPOSE handle=DEC
~~~

Digest is the lowercase SHA-256 of those exact decoded core bytes.  The
complete requester-authored AUDITED_SPAWN payload is at most 1280 bytes.
P independently:

1. hex-decodes core canonically;
2. recomputes its digest;
3. parses the exact request, session, target, method, purpose, and handle;
4. obtains trigger only from the same requester-authored outer payload;
5. joins those values to P's registered audit endpoint, requester child,
   role, owner, session, credentials, and the existing closed
   method/session/target/purpose/trigger authorization; and
6. only after all checks stores the exact complete outer bytes as one
   immutable pending direct observation and sends AUDIT_RECEIPT.

No G record, expected value, admission string, copied endpoint map, or
reserialized request supplies P's decision.

P calls recvmsg with a buffer larger than 48 only so truncation can be
detected.  The sole successful result is exactly one 48-byte datagram,
no ancillary item, no MSG_TRUNC/MSG_CTRUNC, and a source sockaddr_nl whose

### 4.5 Independent inode sources and reciprocal owner matrix

P obtains a child endpoint inode independently from the exact authenticated
proc descriptor audit.  It reads exactly /proc/<child-outer-PID>/fd/<slot>,
requires the canonical complete text socket:[INODE_DEC], and requires the
no-follow actual-descriptor fstat socket inode from the same stable
pidfd/start-time proc identity to equal that decimal.  It never uses a
G-reported inode or application pathname as the source.

For a G-held peer, P enumerates G beneath its authenticated outer pidfd,
start time, one-thread, credential, and guardian-cgroup identity and requires
exactly one live G descriptor with the returned peer inode and the
registered holder role.  For FD 5, P fstats its own retained audit peer.
No local FD number or copied inode string has authority.

At every applicable registered/pre-admission audit, every SOURCE_READY
audit, and every later v3 freeze/reference audit while a pair remains live,
P requires both:

~~~text
diag(endpoint_inode)=expected_peer_inode
diag(expected_peer_inode)=endpoint_inode
~~~

The complete owner matrix is:

| Child slot | Exact child targets | Expected peer holder | Exact live phases |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 8, ADMISSION_BARRIER | every G child | G, exactly one barrier-peer FD | registered and SOURCE_READY; child closes before first target instruction and G closes its peer immediately after observing that close |
| 4, RPC | TOP_TEST_CONTROLS, COPIED_REPRODUCE | G, exactly one RPC-peer FD | registered, SOURCE_READY, and target running through terminal FD-4 reply |
| 5, REQUEST_AUDIT | TOP_TEST_CONTROLS, COPIED_REPRODUCE | P, exactly one retained audit-peer FD | registered, SOURCE_READY, and target running until the requester closes FD 5 after its terminal FD-4 reply |

No endpoint inode may appear in two rows, map to itself, occur in two live
holder FDs, or have a reciprocal peer outside the expected holder.  The

The query occurs only in a protocol state in which the endpoint close edge
is blocked: the child is waiting at FD 8 for pre-admission and SOURCE_READY
audits, or workers are frozen under the existing later reference audit.
After an authorized close, the pair leaves the live matrix; a retained
closed-end identity is a close/reap receipt, not a substitute reciprocal
query.

### 4.6 Close transitions and fail-closed behavior

The exact endpoint close transitions are:

~~~text
FD 8:
  reciprocal at registered and SOURCE_READY
  -> P START
  -> child receives START and closes FD 8
  -> G observes peer closure and closes its peer
  -> both identities leave the live matrix before any running audit

FD 4:
  reciprocal from registration through terminal RPC reply
  -> child closes FD 4 before exit
  -> G drains/observes EOF and closes its peer
  -> both identities leave the live matrix before CHILD_REAPED

FD 5:
  reciprocal from registration through the requester's terminal FD-4 reply
  -> requester closes FD 5 before exit
  -> P accepts clean EOF and retains its own peer identity
  -> requester reap
  -> P closes its retained peer
~~~

At FD-5 half-close, no text claims that the absent child endpoint remains a
live reciprocal peer.  P's clean serial ledger, EOF, retained-peer identity,
and requester reap are all required.

Before START, an unavailable or nonunique inode, holder, query, response,
reciprocal relation, or close observation is E_POSSESSION_UNAVAILABLE.
After START, the same defect is a fatal containment condition under the
existing v2 kill/reap/cleanup contract and cannot produce PASS or ABSENT.

SO_PEERCRED, SCM_CREDENTIALS, equal uid/pid, FD number, local socket inode,
copied text, packet delivery, fixed barrier bytes, or G's table is never a
fallback or tie-breaker.  /proc/net/unix scanning, an opaque helper, an
external service, another binary, and a seventh implementation path are
forbidden.

The preflight's simultaneous A/B pairs are the mandatory primitive
cross-wire falsifier.  The later fresh reviewer must also hold all local
number/type/CLOEXEC/credential facts fixed while crossing two FD-8 expected
G peers and, separately, two FD-4 expected G peers, and must verify the
P-held FD-5 reciprocal row.  Those are independent-review obligations, not
author-side finding closure.
