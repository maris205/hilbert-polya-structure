## 3. D-M1 structural blocker — no independent causal capability

### 3.1 Exact authorized grammar is observationally insufficient

The v5 gate fixes exactly eight new FD-5 forms and exactly four new P--G
forms.  Before P sends SESSION_AUTH_RECEIPT, the requester already possesses
every byte of the later amended SESSION_CREATE: auth, session, nonce, digest,
method, trigger, owner, and request all appear in the challenge or the
requester's direct SESSION_AUTH_REGISTERED precommit.  The receipt reveals
no new one-use capability.

The P--G control socket and requester--G FD 4 are independent seqpacket
queues.  The following two executions can therefore leave the same bytes
queued when G next runs:

~~~text
COMPLIANT_WORLD:
  P sends SESSION_AUTH_GRANTED to G
  P sends SESSION_AUTH_RECEIPT to requester
  requester sends the precommitted SESSION_CREATE on FD 4

EARLY_WORLD:
  requester sends the same precommitted SESSION_CREATE on FD 4
  P later sends SESSION_AUTH_GRANTED to G
  P later sends SESSION_AUTH_RECEIPT to requester
~~~

If G is not scheduled until both queues contain their packets, the exact
grant and exact FD-4 packet are byte-identical in both worlds.  Kernel
credentials, pidfds, request numbers, the registered tuple, and a later
SESSION_AUTH_ACCEPTED report are also identical.  P has no direct observation
of the FD-4 enqueue edge.  The authorized grammar contains no grant-consumed
acknowledgment, post-receipt secret, transferred one-use capability, or
kernel receipt that distinguishes the worlds.

Holding an early FD-4 packet until the grant is dequeued merges these worlds
and is not a proof.  Rejecting solely because G happened to dequeue FD 4
before its control queue is also not a proof: in the compliant world the
grant send is causally earlier, but independent-socket scheduling can expose
the later FD-4 readiness first.  Poll/dequeue order cannot be relabeled as
send order.

Consequently the gate's required exact edge

~~~text
SESSION_AUTH_GRANTED / SESSION_AUTH_RECEIPT
  -> actual SESSION_CREATE bytes
~~~

cannot be independently enforced with the authorized bytes and capabilities.
Trusting TOP_TEST_CONTROLS to wait is expressly outside the required
fail-closed model.

### 3.2 Activation and later-operation worlds also converge

The requester can compute the canonical
`SESSION_CREATED request=DEC session=DEC` bytes before receiving G's actual
reply because both decimals are already fixed.  An early
SESSION_AUTH_ACTIVATED packet can therefore wait in the P-facing FD-5 queue
until SESSION_AUTH_COMMITTED becomes visible.  Its bytes and credentials are
then identical to a packet sent after the actual reply.  Retaining the packet
until the other queue catches up again merges the required and forbidden
worlds.

SESSION_AUTH_ACTIVE_RECEIPT is P-to-requester only.  No authorized P-to-G
record or G-visible one-use capability tells G that P reached
REGISTERED_ACTIVE.  After G commits and returns SESSION_CREATED, a requester
can issue a non-child FD-4 operation before P's active receipt.  P can still
withhold a later v4 AUDIT_RECEIPT, but G can already create, validate, clean,
or otherwise mutate private session state.  That violates the gate's exact
ordering of ACTIVE_RECEIPT before zero or more session operations.

The four authorized P--G records are already assigned to grant, actual-byte
report, commit, and committed tuple.  Reusing one for activation or adding a
fifth record changes the gate's exact closed enum.  Adding a secret to an
FD-4 request, passing a new descriptor, enabling a timestamp oracle, or
adding another cmsg likewise exceeds the authorized delta.  V5 cannot invent
one of those mechanisms.

### 3.3 Error branches are not closed by the fixed records

6. SESSION_AUTH_CLOSED validates but SESSION_AUTH_CLOSE_RECEIPT fails.

held request, installed session, registered object, admission gate, child,

### 4.1 The gate requires and forbids the same FD-5 duplicate

Amendment v4 Section 3.1 states that requester FD 5 “is never duplicated.”
Its Section 4.5 live-holder matrix also treats the original endpoint holders
as exclusive.  V5 gate Section 4.4 nevertheless requires P to execute
pidfd_getfd on child slot 5, which necessarily creates a second P-local open
file-description reference while the child original remains live.

The v5 gate's exhaustive bounded-supersession list authorizes replacement of
v4 Section 4.5's quoted “no-follow actual-descriptor fstat” phrase and the v2
syscall/preflight inventory.  It does not authorize superseding v4 Section
3.1's absolute no-duplicate clause or the relevant holder cardinality.  The
later mandatory pidfd_getfd sequence therefore cannot coexist with every
omitted v4 clause remaining binding.

The same bounded-authority conflict appears in D-M1: its required close
exchange occurs after SESSION_CLOSED, while v4 Sections 3.6 and 4.6 require
the requester to close FD 5 immediately after its terminal FD-4 reply.  The
v5 supersession list names the v2 session close lifecycle but does not
expressly authorize replacement of those v4 FD-5 lifetime clauses.  A v5
author cannot silently widen an exhaustive supersession list to resolve
either contradiction.

### 4.2 Duplicate error unwind is not total

Once pidfd_getfd succeeds, every later path must dispose of every acquired
duplicate before any original endpoint closes, any barrier releases, or any
kill/reap path relies on peer closure.  It is insufficient to define disposal
only after two fstat values and both reciprocal queries succeed.  Failure can
occur after any individual acquisition, FD_CLOEXEC check, fstat, proc-text
comparison, candidate enumeration, Unix-diag query, or zero/two-match test.

A corrected contract must freeze one unconditional unwind entered after the
first local duplicate exists.  It must enumerate every successfully returned
FD, close each exact number once, prove immediate F_GETFD=-1/EBADF without
intervening allocation, close and prove absence of every per-audit proc
directory FD, and only then permit an original close, thaw, barrier release,
kill, or reap.  If absence cannot be proved, the branch may yield only
crash-teardown; it cannot claim successful cleanup, PASS, or ABSENT.

The same totality is required for pidfd_open.  A returned pidfd followed by a
CLOEXEC, live-poll, credential, start-time, namespace, or cgroup mismatch is
still an allocated P descriptor and requires an exact close/EBADF edge.
Normal child pidfds must retain their distinct lifetime through exit, reap,
and CHILD_REAPED reconciliation.  The v5 attempted contract did not provide
a complete normal-and-error table for both lifetimes.

### 4.3 Proc-directory cardinality and G quiescence are not exact

An allocation-free two-snapshot audit needs exact retained proc-directory
capabilities.  A complete contract must state, by child slot, the exact count
for child and G, open flags, CLOEXEC state, process/start-time/namespace
identity join, acquisition point before the first pidfd_getfd, allowed
getdents/readlinkat operations, reset rule, normal close, every error close,
and immediate EBADF proof.  Saying only “already opened” or “retained proc
FDs” leaves an unlisted holder and error branch.

Equal numeric descriptor sets before and after candidate fstats do not rule
out same-number ABA replacement while G can run.  G must be in an exact
quiescent control state for the entire first snapshot, every candidate
pidfd_getfd/fstat, the second snapshot, reciprocal comparison, duplicate
disposal, and restored-holder proof.  The state must bar descriptor close,
dup, reopen, SCM_RIGHTS receipt, fork, signal-handler allocation, and all
other FD-table mutation.  Quiescence must be independently witnessed before
the first snapshot and remain valid through the final proof.  The v5 gate
requires a corresponding frozen control state, but the discarded v5 repair
did not freeze or prove this full interval.  If no retained state supplies
that proof, a corrected gate must authorize an exact added state or record
rather than leave one implicit outside its closed delta.
