## Contract Paraphrase

For D1, the adversarial test is whether weaknesses in research design, evidence handling, quantitative disclosure, or reproducibility could prevent the work’s inferences from being independently trusted.

For D2, the challenge is to seek domain-level inaccuracies, misuse of specialized concepts, distorted accounts of earlier scholarship, or assertions that conflict with the relevant body of knowledge.

For D3, the stress test is to attack the complete reasoning chain: expose contradictions, hidden premises, unsupported transitions from evidence to claims, fallacious moves, and counterarguments capable of defeating the central thesis.

For D4, the challenge is whether readers outside the immediate specialty could follow the framing and definitions, and whether any bridge to neighboring disciplines rests on demonstrated connections rather than assertion.

For D5, the adversarial question is whether organization, exposition, and visual presentation obscure or weaken the argument, and whether formal presentation choices create obstacles to rigorous evaluation.

For D6, the challenge is whether claims about publication-context fit, originality, importance, and value to the intended readership could withstand a skeptical demand for clear and proportionate support.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Breaks between premises, evidence, intermediate inferences, and the central conclusion; hidden assumptions; internal contradictions; and unaddressed rival explanations.
what_triggers_block: A substantial contradiction or inferential gap leaves a central step unsupported but potentially repairable through major restructuring or additional justification.
what_triggers_warn: A localized ambiguity, missing qualification, or weakly defended subsidiary inference strains coherence without threatening the core thesis.
what_triggers_fatal: The central conclusion cannot follow from the stated premises and evidence, or a stronger counterargument collapses the core thesis with no plausible repair within the paper’s framework.
