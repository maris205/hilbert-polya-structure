## 5. Direct dependency reconstruction

Let `K=supp_R(f)`. The complete load-bearing chain for P13-8 is:

1. Paper 11 gives `supp_actual(Phi_actual(f))=X x K` and actual
   quasi-compactness.
2. Paper 12 gives `Std(X)=coprod_(q in Q) O_q`, with every
   `O_q~=R/H` nonempty compact Hausdorff, and the continuous identity
   `J:G_std(X)->G_actual(X)`.
3. Since `J` is the identity on the carrier and the function is time-only,
   its standard support is
   `coprod_(q in Q)(O_q x K)`.
4. If `f=0`, this support is empty. If `f!=0` and `Q` is finite, it is a
   finite union of compact sets. If `f!=0` and `Q` is infinite, its open
   component cover has no finite subcover.
5. Nonemptiness of `Std(X)` makes the time-function map injective, so the iff
   and image/intersection statements are equivalent.
6. Multiplication by a circle-valued gauge preserves the nonzero locus.
7. The fixed-prime statement replaces `H` and `Q` by their already registered
   symbols and leaves the finite/infinite predicate unresolved.

**Dependency-break determination.** No qualifying nonformal dependency break
exists on the frozen theorem. Steps 1 and 2 are companion premises; Steps 3--5
are elementary product/coproduct support bookkeeping; Step 6 is the nowhere-
zero observation; and Step 7 is conditional substitution. The main iff does
not use the P13-3 multiplier collapse, P13-4 twisted identities, or P13-5
completion theory. Those core results enter only the supplementary gauge and
finite-`Q` star-map statements. Thus P13-8 is syntactically cross-owner but is
mathematically obtainable by direct substitution at exactly the point the lock
requires more.

The Phase-2 convention/owner audit is corroborating evidence: before the
Phase-3 support proof existed, its Section 6 already rederived the complete
zero/finite/infinite iff, gauge invariance, and conditional packet branch from
the same inherited premises. That does not diminish correctness; it confirms
that the later proof closes a formalized corollary rather than discovering an
additional load-bearing theorem.
