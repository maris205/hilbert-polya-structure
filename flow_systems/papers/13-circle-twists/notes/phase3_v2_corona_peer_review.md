## 4. P13-8A: cardinality, owner retyping, and topology

### 4.1 Mandatory inherited lower bound and direct Paper-13 delta

The proof correctly imports, without rederiving, Paper 2 Proposition
`prop:uncountable`:

```text
|U_p/H_p| >= 2^aleph_0.
```

Paper 2's exact proof uses the fully embedded sign subgroup, fixes every
unused coordinate to `1`, intersects it with the procyclic `H_p`, and
transfers the resulting bare-set lower bound to fixed-prime packet orbits.
The reviewed proof assigns that entire argument zero Paper-13 novelty,
priority, author-delta, standalone, and Route credit.

The authorized Paper-13 proof begins only with the converse estimate.  Each
`Z_ell^x` injects into the countable-digit space `Z_ell`, hence has cardinal
at most `2^aleph_0`.  The prime index set is countable, so

```text
|U_p|
 <=(2^aleph_0)^aleph_0
 =2^(aleph_0*aleph_0)
 =2^aleph_0.
```

Surjectivity of `U_p -> U_p/H_p` gives the same upper bound for the quotient.
Combining it with the inherited lower bound gives exact equality, and the
Paper-9 set bijection retypes it as

```text
|Q_p^bare|=|U_p/H_p|=2^aleph_0.
```

No continuum hypothesis, measure, quotient topology, or orbit enumeration
is used.

### 4.2 Four-owner firewall

The proof explicitly records that `Q_p^bare` is the carrier underlying the
actual Paper-9 quotient, but it transports no topology.  The owner split is
maintained throughout:

- `Q_p^actual` retains its indiscrete topology and countable two-open base;
- `Q_p^bare` is used only as a set and cardinal index;
- `Std(Gamma_p)` retains the Paper-12 coproduct topology; and
- `Q_p^disc` is the discrete quotient of that standardization.

Thus the continuum carrier does not make the actual indiscrete quotient
non-second-countable.  The proof makes no such inference.

### 4.3 Direct topology consequences

For an arbitrary coproduct of nonempty components, a countable base would
assign to each component the least indexed nonempty basis member contained
in it.  Disjointness makes this an injection of the component index set into
`N`; hence an uncountable coproduct cannot be second countable.

The component opens cover every compact subset.  Compactness reduces that
cover to finitely many components, so a compact subset meets only finitely
many components.  A countable union of compact subsets consequently meets
only countably many components and cannot cover a continuum-indexed
coproduct.

These arguments apply exactly to `Q_p^disc`, `Std(Gamma_p)`, and the standard
arrow coproduct `coproduct_q(O_q x R)`.  They establish non-second-
countability and non-`sigma`-compactness only on those owners and are not
used as a universal obstruction to a groupoid C-star construction.

## 6. Arbitrary-index multiplier theorem

For each separately typed `epsilon`, the author record is the genuine
arbitrary-index sum

```text
A=direct_sum_(q in Q^bare)^c0 B_q,
```

where a family vanishes at infinity when only finitely many coordinates
exceed any fixed positive threshold.  This definition neither enumerates
`Q^bare` nor replaces the algebra by a product.

I independently checked both directions of the direct multiplier proof.
For a double centralizer `(L,R) in M(A)`, let `I_q` be the coordinate ideal.
If `a in I_q`, `c in I_r`, and `r!=q`, then

```text
L(a)c=L(ac)=0,
cR(a)=R(ca)=0.
```

An approximate identity in `B_r` forces the `r`-coordinates of `L(a)` and
`R(a)` to vanish.  Thus every coordinate ideal is preserved and restriction
gives a uniformly bounded family `m_q in M(B_q)`.  Finite-support families
are dense in an arbitrary `c0` sum, so the coordinate formula determines the
original multiplier on all of `A`.

Conversely, a uniformly bounded family `(m_q)` acts by

```text
[L_m(b)]_q=m_qb_q,
[R_m(b)]_q=b_qm_q.
```

The uniform bound preserves the finite-above-threshold condition, and the
double-centralizer identities hold coordinatewise.  Coordinate approximate
units recover each `||m_q||`, while the coordinate action gives the converse
norm bound.  Hence the canonical identity is isometric:

```text
M(A)=product_(q in Q^bare)^bounded M(B_q).
```

The argument assumes neither countability nor separability, and it does not
require the `B_q` to be unital or mutually identified.

### 7.2 Kernel and direct quotient-distance calculation

On the infinite branch, the corona kernel is exactly the preimage of `A`, so
the intersection theorem gives zero kernel.  The proof correctly goes
further and computes the quotient norm.

For arbitrary `b=(b_q) in A` and `eta>0`, only finitely many coordinates
have `||b_q||>=eta`.  Choose a coordinate outside that finite set.  At that
coordinate,

```text
||d_q(a)-b_q|| >= ||a||-eta.
```

The multiplier-product supremum norm therefore gives

```text
||D(a)-b||>=||a||-eta.
```

Letting `eta` decrease to zero and then infimizing over `b in A` proves
`dist(D(a),A)>=||a||`.  Taking `b=0` gives the reverse inequality.  Hence

```text
||pi_cor(D_sigma^epsilon(a))||=||a||.
```

This establishes an isometric faithful corona embedding directly.  It does
not rely only on the general fact that an injective C-star homomorphism is
isometric.  On the finite branch the entire diagonal lies in `A`, and its
corona image is exactly zero.
