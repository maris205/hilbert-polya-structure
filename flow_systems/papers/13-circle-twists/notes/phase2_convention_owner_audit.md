### 2.1 Sorkin-to-frozen-orientation translation

The accessible source manifestation supports continuous triviality, but not
which scalar is conjugated in the source's “remultiplication.” This does not
create a mathematical mismatch. If a source convention replaces projective
operators by `V_t=r(t)U_t`, then

    sigma'(t,u) = (delta r)(t,u) sigma(t,u).

Thus `sigma'=1` yields `sigma=delta(overline r)` in the frozen convention. If
the source instead uses `V_t=overline{r(t)}U_t`, then

    sigma'(t,u) = overline{(delta r)(t,u)} sigma(t,u),

and `sigma'=1` yields `sigma=delta r`. In either case the existence assertion
translates, but the exact name and conjugation of the trivializer do not.
Accordingly, Sorkin may be cited as prior art for continuous real-line
triviality, while Phase 3 must still produce a direct, sign-exact proof of
`sigma=delta alpha` with the frozen `delta`.

### 2.2 Austad-to-frozen-formula translation

Austad's multiplicative notation is

    (L_y^c xi)(x) = c(y,y^{-1}x) xi(y^{-1}x),
    (f natural_c g)(x)
      = integral f(y) g(y^{-1}x) c(y,y^{-1}x) dy,

with the usual modular factor in the involution. Under

    G=(R,+),  y=s or u,  x=t,  y^{-1}x=t-y,
    Delta_R=1,  c=sigma,

these become exactly

    (lambda_sigma(s)xi)(t)=sigma(s,t-s)xi(t-s),
    (f *_sigma g)(t)
      = integral_R f(u)g(t-u)sigma(u,t-u)du,
    f^{*sigma}(t)=overline{sigma(t,-t)}overline{f(-t)}.

The integrated representation is therefore exactly

    (Lambda_sigma(f)xi)(t)
      = integral_R f(u)sigma(u,t-u)xi(t-u)du.

The projective relation also translates without an inversion:

    lambda_sigma(s)lambda_sigma(u)
      = sigma(s,u)lambda_sigma(s+u).

This is an exact match for the one-object usual-`R` owner. It does not itself
define the Paper-13 actual author algebra.

### 3.1 Phase lift and the remaining P13-3 obligation

    sigma(t,u)=exp(i q(t,u)),    q(0,0)=0.

    q(t,0)=q(0,u)=0.

    D(t,u,v)

### 3.2 Coboundary and gauge-star direction

For normalized continuous `a:R->T`, the frozen convention is

    (delta a)(t,u)=a(t)a(u)overline{a(t+u)},
    sigma overline{tau}=delta a,
    U_a:A_sigma->A_tau,  (U_a f)(t)=a(t)f(t).

At the convolution variables `(u,t-u)`, the gauge relation is exactly

    a(t)sigma(u,t-u)=a(u)a(t-u)tau(u,t-u).

Consequently

    U_a(f *_sigma g)=(U_a f)*_tau(U_a g).

Normalization plus the cocycle equation at `(t,-t,t)` gives

    sigma(t,-t)=sigma(-t,t).

Also `delta a(t,-t)=a(t)a(-t)`. Taking conjugates in the gauge relation gives

    a(t)overline{sigma(t,-t)}
      = overline{tau(t,-t)}overline{a(-t)},

so `U_a(f^{*sigma})=(U_a f)^{*tau}`. In particular,

    sigma=delta alpha  =>  U_alpha:A_sigma->A_1.

For the author actual fibre formula the variables remain

    (F *_sigma G)(x,t)

      = overline{sigma(x;t,-t)}overline{F(x.t,-t)}.

## 6. Complete rederivation of P13-8

Let `X` be nonempty with common stabilizer `H=LZ`, `L>0`, and let the bare
orbit set be `Q=X/R`. For `f in C_c(R)`, put `K=supp_R(f)`. Paper 11 supplies

    Phi_actual(f)(x,t)=f(t),
    supp_actual(Phi_actual(f))=X x K,

whose actual support is quasi-compact by the locked actual-owner projection
criterion. Pullback along `J` is the same pointwise function, while its
standard support is

    supp_std(J^*Phi_actual(f))=Std(X) x K
      = coproduct_(q in Q) ((R/H)_q x K).

Each `R/H` is a nonempty compact Hausdorff circle torsor.

### Case 1: `f=0`

Then `K` is empty and the standard support is empty, hence compact for every
finite or infinite `Q`. Thus `J^*Phi_actual(0)` always belongs to
`C_c(G_std(X))`.

### Case 2: `f!=0` and `Q` is finite

Now `K` is nonempty compact. Every `(R/H)_q x K` is nonempty compact
Hausdorff, and the displayed support is a finite union of these compact
components. It is compact, so the pullback belongs to `C_c(G_std(X))`.

### Case 3: `f!=0` and `Q` is infinite

Again `K` is nonempty. The component subsets `(R/H)_q x K` are open in the
displayed coproduct and together form an open cover. Each component is
nonempty, so no finite subfamily covers components indexed outside that
finite set. The support is not quasi-compact and therefore not compact.
Hence the nonzero pullback does not belong to `C_c(G_std(X))`.

The three cases prove the exact equivalence

    J^*Phi_actual(f) in C_c(G_std(X))
      iff f=0 or Q is finite.

This argument uses no finite computation to infer the infinite case.

### Gauge-support invariance

Every allowed gauge is circle-valued, hence nowhere zero. Multiplication by
`alpha` or its inverse preserves the nonzero locus and therefore preserves
support exactly. The displayed equivalence is unchanged by every proved
gauge-star map. For infinite `Q`, cochain pullback remains continuous; what
fails for nonzero global-QC time functions is only membership in the
standard compact-support test space.

### Fixed-prime conditional

For the fixed-prime packet, substitute only

    H=(log p)Z,    Q=Q_p

with `Q_p` a nonempty bare set. Therefore

    J^*Phi_actual(f) in C_c(G_std(Gamma_p))
      iff f=0 or Q_p is finite,

and for nonzero `f` the support transfer holds iff `Q_p` is finite. This is a
conditional theorem. Nothing in the audited source or companion tuple says
that `Q_p` is finite or infinite, and this audit infers no cardinality,
enumeration, measure, or actual quotient topology for it.
