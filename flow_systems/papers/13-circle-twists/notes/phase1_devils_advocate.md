## Strongest Counter-Argument

The proposed collapse may be mathematically true while the advertised nonretention theorem is still ill-posed. A gauge class is not an untyped value: it lives in an owner-indexed quotient $H^2_{\mathrm{tw}}(G;\mathbb T)$. Likewise, an isotropy restriction lives in a quotient indexed by the literal subgroup $H_x$, and a completed record may retain an embedding or representation even when its abstract algebra is isomorphic to $C^*(\mathbb R)$. Across two nonisomorphic actions or two periods, the present locks provide neither a common codomain nor a declared forgetful comparison. If owner metadata counts, the typed output already carries the action and $H_x$; a global gauge cannot erase either. If owner metadata does not count, the locks must say exactly what is forgotten and how the remaining class, test algebra, completion, representation, or restriction map is compared. The period criterion currently makes this worse by using the changing literal subgroup both as the type of the invariant and as evidence of retention.

The one-object time group through which the proof is meant to pass is also not registered as a distinct owner with explicit pullback/reduction maps. Thus a correct theorem on $\mathbb R$ could be stated next to the actual non-Hausdorff owner without an auditable typed transfer. These are not cosmetic gaps: they determine what P13-2, P13-6, and P13-7 mean. The defensible target is narrower—after a proved canonical time reduction and after forgetting inherited owner/subgroup metadata, the scalar twist contributes no additional action or period invariant. Until that comparison layer is locked, the headline conclusion can be read as either false or tautological even if the $H^2$ computation succeeds.
