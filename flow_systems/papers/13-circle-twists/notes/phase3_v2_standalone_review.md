## 3. Binding Major finding

### M1: P13-8B/C are correct, but their proposed centre reduces to the standard constant-diagonal corona lemma

**Severity**: Major  
**Evidence Anchor**: `equation: phase3_v2_corona_proofs.md (4.7), (5.3), (6.1)--(6.5), (7.1)--(7.4), (8.2)--(8.4), (9.2)--(9.6), and (10.1)--(10.4)`  
**Confidence**: 5 --- the complete dependency was reconstructed for both the
maximal and reduced records after exact companion subtraction.

The new proof materially strengthens the old test-support statement: it
constructs completed component embeddings, identifies their exact norms,
assembles an arbitrary-index multiplier diagonal, computes its exact
algebra/corona location and quotient norm, connects the named actual-author
completion to that diagonal, and proves gauge covariance.  Every one of those
statements is mathematically sound.

The difficulty is that, after the mandated subtraction, the load-bearing
analytic result is the following owner-free lemma.

> Let `I` be a nonempty set, let `C` and `B_i` be C-star algebras, and let
> `phi_i:C->B_i` be isometric star homomorphisms.  Put
> `A=direct_sum_(i in I)^c0 B_i`.  Then
> `Delta(c)=(phi_i(c))_i` is an isometric star homomorphism
> `C->M(A)`.  It lies in `A` exactly when `c=0` or `I` is finite.  If `I` is
> infinite, the composite into `M(A)/A` is isometric and faithful, with
> `dist(Delta(c),A)=||c||`.

This lemma does not use a packet, orbit torsor, prime, cocycle, actual topology,
or common model for the components.  It also needs no enumeration of `I`.  Its
proof is exactly the constant-norm `c0` calculation:

1. boundedness and the coordinate multiplier action place `Delta(c)` in
   `M(A)`, with norm `sup_i ||phi_i(c)||=||c||`;
2. a nonzero diagonal has the same positive norm in every coordinate, so it
   vanishes at infinity exactly when `I` is finite;
3. for any `b in A` and `eta>0`, an infinite `I` has a coordinate outside the
   finite set on which `||b_i||>=eta`; hence
   `||Delta(c)-b||>=||c||-eta`; and
4. taking `b=0` supplies the reverse distance inequality.

In the submitted proof, Theorem 4.3 supplies the `phi_i` by the norm chain
(4.7), Theorem 5.1 supplies the standard multiplier-product realization,
Theorems 6.2--6.3 instantiate Steps 1--2, and Theorem 7.1 instantiates Steps
3--4.  The fixed-prime theorem simply invokes the infinite case.  Thus the
claimed centre is not merely *similar* to a constant-sequence observation; its
kernel, membership dichotomy, and exact quotient norm are logically the
abstract constant-diagonal lemma once the component isometries are available.

The Major is a standalone/publication finding, not a correctness defect.  The
proof and its independent proof review remain PASS.  The material is suitable
for a technical note or a merge, but the prior routine-reduction M1 is not
closed.

## 5. Stress test of the proposed conjunction

### 5.1 Exact maximal/reduced norm chain

Equation (4.7) is one of the strongest parts of the proof.  It separately
types maximal and reduced component records and closes

```text
time,max >= component,max >= component,reduced
         = time,reduced = time,max.
```

This establishes isometric component maps without claiming equality of the
whole component algebras.  But its ingredients are the constant-coefficient
test map, restriction of every unit-regular representation to the twisted
left-regular time representation, the universal maximal bound, and amenability
of `R` at the endpoint.  After the required standard crossed-product and
amenable-group subtraction, it is a rigorous verification of the input
`phi_q` for the abstract lemma, not an owner-specific analytic obstruction.
The chain is unchanged for an arbitrary compact `R`-torsor with the same time
action and does not use fixed-prime arithmetic.

### 5.2 Actual-author to same-carrier standard diagram

Equation (8.2) is exactly

```text
named actual-author completion
  --widehat(Phi)^(-1)--> time completion
  --D--> multiplier of the standard c0 sum.
```

The first arrow is the inherited actual time collapse; the second is the
generic diagonal.  The diagram correctly respects the one-way owner firewall
and does not fabricate a global twisted C-star algebra on the actual owner.
For the same reason, however, it adds no comparison theorem for a full standard
global analytic object: its target and image are chosen componentwise in
advance.  Composing two isomorphism/embedding records does not create a new
interaction invariant.

### 5.3 Origin-free arbitrary-index construction

The absence of origins is mathematically clean.  The functions are constant in
the torsor coordinate, so equivariant translations fix them; the index is the
bare set rather than an enumeration.  Yet the abstract lemma is already
origin-free in a stronger sense: it accepts wholly unrelated `B_i` and maps
`phi_i`.  Neither the dichotomy nor the corona norm sees a torsor origin,
period, topology, or relation between distinct components.  Origin freedom
therefore protects canonicity but does not make the diagonal theorem
owner-specific.

### 5.4 Exact algebra/corona dichotomy

The exact `A`-versus-corona statement is stronger than the previous
test-support theorem, and proving the quotient norm rather than only
nonvanishing is a real improvement.  Nonetheless, its entire quantitative
content is that every coordinate has norm `||a||`.  The quotient norm is the
standard tail supremum after subtracting a `c0` family.  Replacing all packet
components by arbitrary C-star algebras admitting isometric maps from a common
`C` leaves the theorem word-for-word true.  This counterfactual removes the
claimed owner-specific dependency break.

### 5.5 Gauge covariance

Equations (9.2)--(9.6) are valid naturality squares.  They show that no
trivializer or character choice contaminates the construction.  They do not
show that the twist creates, changes, or detects the corona obstruction.  Set
`sigma=tau=1` and the same algebra/corona dichotomy and quotient norm remain.
The gauge package is therefore coherence data for the generic lemma, not a
second load-bearing theorem.

### 5.6 Unconditional fixed-prime remnant

Theorem 10.1 now gives a faithful corona image for every fixed prime and both
completion types.  This is cleaner and logically stronger than the prior
conditional packet statement.  But the prime enters the proof only through the
inherited fact that `Q_p^bare` is infinite (indeed continuum-sized), together
with the already registered common period.  The corona conclusion does not
distinguish one prime from another, does not use the quotient group structure
of `U_p/H_p`, and would hold for every infinite index set.  It is consequently
an unconditional specialization, not a new prime-sensitive remnant.

### 5.7 Conjunction and centrality

Packaging exact norms, arbitrary indices, an owner diagram, a quotient norm,
and gauge squares into one theorem is valuable.  A conjunction does not gain
standalone weight merely from having many typed clauses when each clause is
either an input verification or a functorial consequence of the same generic
lemma.  Three deletion tests are decisive:

- remove the twist and every finite/infinite and corona statement survives;
- remove the actual owner and the standard component diagonal theorem survives;
- replace the fixed-prime packet by arbitrary components with isometric common
  source maps and the central theorem survives.

Conversely, the package does not classify the full standard multiplier or
corona algebra, characterize the diagonal intrinsically among all its elements,
compute an extension or ideal invariant, or derive a packet- or
prime-sensitive analytic consequence.  Those are not mandatory theorem forms;
their absence simply confirms that the submitted result remains a statement
about one preselected constant diagonal.
