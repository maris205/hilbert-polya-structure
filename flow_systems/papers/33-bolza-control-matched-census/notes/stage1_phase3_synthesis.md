## Synthesis question and evidence status

The evidence question is narrower than the eventual scientific question:
does the closed corpus support a defensible method path for full conjugacy,
inversion pairing, primitivity, and completeness closure on both frozen
presentations? Phase 2 established 20/20 source-record coverage: nine
`S2_VERIFIED`, ten `VERIFIED`, and one `PLAUSIBLE`, with 18/20 works
counted as peer reviewed. Those labels verify identity and bounded fitness;
they do not promote every annotation into a theorem-level claim.

The answer is **component support without interface closure**. The corpus
supports object definitions, general algorithmic ingredients, semantic
distinctions, and validation architecture. It does not instantiate the one
deterministic, complete, serialized, independently checkable interface needed
to close `P33-RC-1` for both frozen groups.

## Theme 1 — object-level octagon and length-spectrum foundations

Nazarenko (2013)<!--ref:S01--><!--anchor:none--> supplies the two-parameter
compact genus-two octagon construction, opposite-side pairing, presentation,
and explicit generator context underlying the frozen control family. It does
not name the project specialization or prove its nonarithmeticity, systole, or
owner closure.

Aigon-Dupuy et al. (2005)<!--ref:S02--><!--anchor:none--> independently
corroborate the peer-reviewed genus-two octagon-family setting and its relation
to Fuchsian generators and Teichmüller coordinates, but do not select the
frozen transcendental parameter.

Aurich et al. (1991)<!--ref:S05--><!--anchor:none--> provide regular-octagon
matrices, a length law, and periodic-orbit enumeration precedent; that
enumeration convention is not automatically the P33 quotient by full
conjugacy, inversion, and group primitivity. Katz et al.
(2016)<!--ref:S08--><!--anchor:none--> supply a modern arithmetic-Fuchsian and
quaternion-order description of the Bolza surface, while their
congruence-cover and sampled examples do not establish a complete P33 owner
manifest. Grácio and Sousa Ramos
(2010)<!--ref:S09--><!--anchor:none--> connect symbolic dynamics and group
words to genus-two length-spectrum computation, but do not supply the stronger
conjugacy, inversion, primitivity, serialization, and zero-unresolved
certificate required here.

**Theme strength: strong for object and method context; insufficient for owner
closure.** These five sources license exact octagon representations and
length-spectrum enumeration as starting layers, not the stronger conclusion
that an enumerated word or repeated length is already one unique primitive
inverse-paired owner.

## Theme 2 — arithmeticity and systolic context are multi-source and bounded

Takeuchi (1975)<!--ref:S03--><!--anchor:none--> provides the

its application to the frozen control remains a separate project derivation
and must retain the visible 2006 correction notice. Popescu
(2024)<!--ref:S04--><!--anchor:none--> provides a modern

`exp(-1/5)`, but supplies no Fuchsian-group or P33 conclusion. The eventual
support chain must therefore join the frozen trace formula, the corrected
criterion, and the transcendence ingredient without attributing the
project-specific application to either source.

Jenni (1984)<!--ref:S06--><!--anchor:none--> is retained only as authoritative

pinpointed in the frozen verification record; its status remains
`PLAUSIBLE`, not upgraded by this synthesis. Schmutz
(1993)<!--ref:S07--><!--anchor:none--> supplies broader primary systolic and
extremal context, not a P33 replay or owner-completeness certificate.

**Theme strength: moderate and correction-bound.** The exact cutoff
inequalities remain inherited project locks. S06 cannot independently carry
an exact systole statement without later page-level verification, and neither
systolic source removes the known confound at `Lambda=21/10`.

## Theme 3 — domain, conjugacy, and root algorithms do not yet compose

Voight (2009)<!--ref:S10--><!--anchor:none--> gives exact algorithms for
domains, presentations, reduction, and word problems for suitable cofinite
Fuchsian input. The declared algebraic input regime does not automatically
cover the frozen transcendental control, and the paper does not provide
P33's positive/negative conjugacy payload. Despré et al.
(2023)<!--ref:S11--><!--anchor:none--> give a terminating Dirichlet-domain
algorithm from a fundamental polygon with side pairings, but the real-RAM
model and explicit exclusion of algebraic-number analysis leave P33's exact
predicate and certificate layers open.

Epstein and Holt (2006)<!--ref:S12--><!--anchor:none--> establish general
linear-time conjugacy solvability in word-hyperbolic groups and a
positive-conjugator pathway. This does not by itself instantiate constants,
canonical forms, complete negative evidence, deterministic bytes, or an
independent validator for either frozen presentation; the corrected
bibliographic range is 287–305. Lysenok
(1990)<!--ref:S13--><!--anchor:none--> establishes root-extraction and related
algorithmic solvability in hyperbolic groups, but not the project-specific
maximal-root/no-root payload or serializer needed for primitive-owner
admission.

**Theme strength: moderate for general decidability; insufficient for
executable P33 certification.** These four sources establish separable domain,
conjugacy, and root components. They do not demonstrate one compatible exact
input model with required positive and negative fields for both frozen groups,
so they do not compose on the frozen evidence into one replayable P33
interface and `P33-RC-1` remains open.

## Theme 4 — owner semantics require two distinct quotient operations

Cherubini et al. (2022)<!--ref:S14--><!--anchor:none--> support the semantic
relation between primitive closed geodesics and primitive root-conjugacy
classes and preserve the distinction between primitive owners and
repetitions. Their arithmetic prime-geodesic formula is outside P33 and they
do not define the frozen serialization.

Erlandsson and Souto (2024)<!--ref:S15--><!--anchor:none--> analyze reciprocal
geodesics and elements conjugate to their inverses. That is the special case
in which inversion collapses within one conjugacy class; it does not implement
P33's external identification of `[g]` and `[g^-1]` for every owner.

**Theme strength: emerging for definitions; inadequate for a canonicalizer.**
The two distinct scopes require the synthesis to separate (i) full-group
conjugacy, (ii) external inversion-pair canonicalization, and (iii)
maximal-root/primitivity. Neither source licenses trace, length, matrix
equality, or homology as a substitute for the full quotient contract.

## Theme 5 — rigorous checking is an architecture, not a result

Strohmaier and Uski (2013, corrected
2018)<!--ref:S16--><!--anchor:none--> exemplify an architecture separating
bounded computation, certified error control, and interval completeness, but
their objects are Laplace spectra and determinants rather than primitive
geodesic owners; the 2018 correction remains bound. Johansson
(2017)<!--ref:S17--><!--anchor:none--> supplies a primary arbitrary-precision
ball-arithmetic method with explicit radii, but interval arithmetic alone
cannot decide group conjugacy, root status, or census completeness. Rump
(2010)<!--ref:S18--><!--anchor:none--> supplies general validated-numerics
principles supporting the separation of proof-bearing enclosures from display
decimals, not a P33 group certificate.

Hoffman et al. (2016)<!--ref:S19--><!--anchor:none--> provide a
domain-adjacent precedent for separating approximate hyperbolic-geometry
candidates from rigorous interval validation, but work on cusped hyperbolic
3-manifolds and do not validate any P33 output. Hales et al.
(2017)<!--ref:S20--><!--anchor:none--> provide a large-scale precedent for
deterministic artifacts and checking independent of production, without
mandating a proof assistant or closing the surface-group interface here.

**Theme strength: strong for verification design; analogy-only for P33.**
Together these five sources support separating producer, checker, and
proof-bearing exact or interval fields; none implies that P33 has built or run
such a system.

- **Exact input model.** Voight
  (2009)<!--ref:S10--><!--anchor:none--> works in a suitable algebraic-input
  setting, whereas Despré et al. (2023)<!--ref:S11--><!--anchor:none--> use a
  real-RAM polygon setting. Each covers part of the problem; neither resolves
  the exact transcendental-control interface required by the frozen P33
  representation.
- **Decision versus evidence object.** Epstein and Holt
  (2006)<!--ref:S12--><!--anchor:none--> establish a conjugacy algorithm,
  while Hales et al. (2017)<!--ref:S20--><!--anchor:none--> provide an
  independent-checking precedent. They expose complementary questions:
  deciding conjugacy and serializing checkable positive and negative evidence
  are not the same operation.
- **General roots versus a surface-specific shortcut.** Lysenok
  (1990)<!--ref:S13--><!--anchor:none--> supports root extraction in
  hyperbolic groups, whereas the frozen methodology also permits an exact
  systolic inequality to prove primitivity when applicable. The corpus does
  not choose between these branches or license either without an explicit
  theorem-to-field binding.
- **Checker strength.** Johansson
  (2017)<!--ref:S17--><!--anchor:none--> and Rump
  (2010)<!--ref:S18--><!--anchor:none--> support enclosure methods, while
  Hoffman et al. (2016)<!--ref:S19--><!--anchor:none--> and Hales et al.
  (2017)<!--ref:S20--><!--anchor:none--> supply different validation and
  checking precedents. None shows that a full proof assistant is necessary.

## Research gaps

### Primary gap — `P33-RC-1` remains open

No verified source or compatible source combination instantiates, for both
frozen presentations, all of the following in one deterministic interface:

1. a theorem/algorithm identifier and version plus a proof that its hypotheses
   apply to each exact representation;
2. a canonical full-conjugacy decision with explicit positive conjugator
   fields and complete negative or canonical evidence;
3. external inversion-pair canonicalization distinct from self-reciprocity;
4. maximal-root/primitivity decisions with positive root witnesses and
   checkable no-proper-power evidence;
5. termination and candidate-universe completeness payloads tied to the
   frozen cutoff and physical clock;
6. deterministic ordering, byte serialization, hashes, and schema versions;
   and
7. an independent validator with specified accepted and rejected inputs.

Epstein and Holt (2006)<!--ref:S12--><!--anchor:none--> and Lysenok
(1990)<!--ref:S13--><!--anchor:none--> are the central general-algorithm
sources, but their solvability results do not instantiate those seven project
fields. Voight (2009)<!--ref:S10--><!--anchor:none--> and Despré et al.
(2023)<!--ref:S11--><!--anchor:none--> inform the presentation/domain layer
without closing the shared exact-input problem. Strohmaier and Uski (2013,
corrected 2018)<!--ref:S16--><!--anchor:none-->, Johansson
(2017)<!--ref:S17--><!--anchor:none-->, Rump
(2010)<!--ref:S18--><!--anchor:none-->, Hoffman et al.
(2016)<!--ref:S19--><!--anchor:none-->, and Hales et al.

fill missing group-theoretic evidence by analogy.

## Methodology recommendations for a later separately authorized freeze

These are design obligations only; none is executed in Phase 3.

1. **Create an applicability map per frozen group.** Bind each selected
   theorem's hypotheses to exact P33 representation fields. Voight
   (2009)<!--ref:S10--><!--anchor:none-->, Despré et al.
   (2023)<!--ref:S11--><!--anchor:none-->, Epstein and Holt
   (2006)<!--ref:S12--><!--anchor:none-->, and Lysenok
   (1990)<!--ref:S13--><!--anchor:none--> define separate component
   boundaries; an unproved binding remains `NOT_EVALUABLE`.
2. **Freeze a typed certificate schema.** Separate candidate provenance,
   cutoff inclusion, conjugacy, inversion pairing, root/primitivity, and
   completeness. Positive and negative dispositions must each have explicit
   evidence fields; no capped search may silently become a negative theorem.
3. **Freeze deterministic serialization.** Specify canonical word/matrix
   representation, ordering, duplicate treatment, version identifiers, and
   hashes before any owner output is inspected.
4. **Specify an independent checker.** It should validate declared fields and
   reject malformed, incomplete, or contradictory payloads without trusting
   producer status. Hales et al. (2017)<!--ref:S20--><!--anchor:none-->
   support broad producer/checker separation, not a mandated proof-assistant
   implementation.
5. **Keep numerical predicates subordinate.** Rational outward intervals or
   ball arithmetic may certify sign and cutoff predicates, but must never
   substitute for conjugacy or primitivity. Johansson
   (2017)<!--ref:S17--><!--anchor:none--> and Rump
   (2010)<!--ref:S18--><!--anchor:none--> support the enclosure layer only at
   this bounded level.
6. **Preserve correction and locator receipts.** Bind Takeuchi
   (1975)<!--ref:S03--><!--anchor:none--> and Strohmaier and Uski (2013,
   corrected 2018)<!--ref:S16--><!--anchor:none--> to their corrections, and
   either obtain an exact Jenni (1984)<!--ref:S06--><!--anchor:none--> theorem
   pin or omit S06 from exact claim support.
7. **Retain the fail-closed taxonomy.** If the common exact interface cannot
   be frozen, the licensed future state remains
   `NOT_EVALUABLE_CONJUGACY_METHOD_UNAVAILABLE`; it is not an owner no-go and
   cannot produce a between-surface scientific readout.
