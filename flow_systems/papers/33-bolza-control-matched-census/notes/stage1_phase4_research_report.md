## Abstract

This report asks whether a closed literature corpus supports a defensible path to exact primitive-geodesic owner censuses for a source-locked Bolza surface and one frozen nonarithmetic genus-two control at the inherited cutoff \(\Lambda=21/10\). The executed method was a staged literature investigation: research-question and design freezing, screening of 20 sources, record-level verification, a source-effect matrix, thematic synthesis, and an independent adversarial recheck. The evidence supports separate components for octagon representations, length-spectrum enumeration, hyperbolic-group conjugacy and root decisions, owner semantics, validated numerical predicates, deterministic serialization, and independent checking. It does not establish that these components form one exact, terminating, byte-stable interface applicable to both frozen group presentations. The resulting finding is therefore component support without interface closure. Seven implementation and proof obligations remain open under the gate `P33-RC-1`, and no owner census has been executed. The inherited empty-versus-nonempty support direction cannot carry an arithmetic comparison because the common cutoff straddles the source-locked systoles and the control panel is incomplete. The report recommends a separately authorized, fail-closed certificate freeze before any computation. It makes no novelty, formal Route-A, determinant, magnetic, or spectral claim.

**Keywords:** Bolza surface; hyperbolic octagon; primitive closed geodesic; conjugacy certificate; inversion-paired owner; validated computation

## Introduction

Primitive closed-geodesic counting becomes a different problem when the required output is not merely a list of words or lengths but a complete, independently checkable owner census. In the present project, one owner is an unoriented primitive unit \(\{[g]_{\Gamma},[g^{-1}]_{\Gamma}\}\). Admission therefore depends on full-group conjugacy, external inversion pairing, a no-proper-power decision, exact cutoff inclusion, and a completeness argument for the candidate universe. A repeated length or a plausible word list cannot discharge those obligations.

The two frozen objects make the distinction concrete. Nazarenko (2013)<!--ref:S01--><!--anchor:none:--> gives the two-parameter compact genus-two octagon construction and explicit generator context from which the control family is drawn. Aigon-Dupuy et al. (2005)<!--ref:S02--><!--anchor:none:--> provide peer-reviewed family-level corroboration for hyperbolic octagons and associated Fuchsian descriptions. On the target side, Aurich et al. (1991)<!--ref:S05--><!--anchor:none:--> develop regular-octagon matrices, a length law, and a periodic-orbit enumeration precedent, while Katz et al. (2016)<!--ref:S08--><!--anchor:none:--> give a detailed arithmetic-Fuchsian and quaternion-order description of the Bolza surface. Grácio and Sousa Ramos (2010)<!--ref:S09--><!--anchor:none:--> further connect symbolic dynamics and fundamental-group words to genus-two length-spectrum computation. These sources establish a substantial object and enumeration background, but none is treated here as an exact P33 census under the project owner convention.

The frozen research question is:

> Can exact full-conjugacy, inversion-pair, primitivity, and completeness certificates close both frozen owner censuses at the inherited cutoff \(\Lambda=21/10\) beyond the already-known coarse support?

This report does not answer that question by running a census. It answers the prior methodological question: what does the verified literature support, and what must still be frozen before execution can begin? The distinction matters because the project already knows the direction of coarse support from accepted upstream locks. At \(\Lambda=21/10\), successful replay must return an empty Bolza candidate universe and at least one control primitive owner, including the inherited control witness. Those replay conditions are not new P33 findings. Moreover, their contrast is fixed as `A0_INCONCLUSIVE_SYSTOLE_CONFOUNDED`, and the qualifying panel is fixed as `A0_CONTROL_PANEL_INCOMPLETE`. A formal A0 verdict is prohibited.

The report's contribution is consequently bounded. It organizes the closed evidence into a certificate architecture, identifies the precise noncomposition gap, and retains a lawful not-evaluable state. It does not claim priority, impossibility, scientific execution, or Route credit.

## Literature and Theoretical Framework

### Frozen Geometry, Arithmeticity Ingredients, and Systolic Context

The control is a fixed specialization of a compact genus-two octagon family, not a generic representative of every nonarithmetic surface. Nazarenko (2013)<!--ref:S01--><!--anchor:none:--> supports the construction and generator layer, whereas Aigon-Dupuy et al. (2005)<!--ref:S02--><!--anchor:none:--> support the broader peer-reviewed octagon-family setting. Neither source names the project identifier, proves the chosen specialization nonarithmetic, establishes its systole, or closes its owner census.

The nonarithmeticity chain is also deliberately multi-source. Takeuchi (1975)<!--ref:S03--><!--anchor:none:--> supplies an arithmetic-Fuchsian trace-field criterion, subject to the visible 2006 correction record. Popescu (2024)<!--ref:S04--><!--anchor:none:--> supplies a modern Lindemann-Weierstrass theorem ingredient relevant to the frozen expression involving \(\exp(-1/5)\). The project-specific conclusion requires those ingredients to be bound to the frozen trace formula. It is not a statement that either source makes about the named control.

On the Bolza side, Aurich et al. (1991)<!--ref:S05--><!--anchor:none:--> and Katz et al. (2016)<!--ref:S08--><!--anchor:none:--> provide complementary regular-octagon and exact-group contexts. Jenni (1984)<!--ref:S06--><!--anchor:none:--> remains only `PLAUSIBLE`, historical, and context-level in the frozen verifier because the exact systole theorem location was not pinned. Schmutz (1993)<!--ref:S07--><!--anchor:none:--> supports broader systolic and extremal context but does not independently validate the P33 replay inequality. The exact cutoff relations used by the design remain inherited project locks, not claims reconstructed from these metadata-level citations.

This separation yields the first theoretical rule: object identity, arithmetic characterization, and cutoff support are different evidence surfaces. Combining them requires an explicit derivation and source-lock replay. Similarity of geometry or terminology cannot substitute for that binding.

### From Words to Primitive Inverse-Paired Owners

Length-spectrum work provides candidate-generation precedents, but ownership demands stronger equivalence decisions. Aurich et al. (1991)<!--ref:S05--><!--anchor:none:--> and Grácio and Sousa Ramos (2010)<!--ref:S09--><!--anchor:none:--> show how exact or symbolic group information can lead to length-spectrum enumeration. Their methods do not automatically identify which candidate words lie in the same full surface-group conjugacy class. Equal trace, equal length, equal homology, or literal equality in one representation may be useful filters, but none is admitted as a final conjugacy certificate under the frozen design.

The semantic layer separates three operations. Cherubini et al. (2022)<!--ref:S14--><!--anchor:none:--> connect primitive closed geodesics with primitive root-conjugacy classes and distinguish primitive classes from repetitions. Erlandsson and Souto (2024)<!--ref:S15--><!--anchor:none:--> study reciprocal geodesics, including the special case in which an element is conjugate to its inverse. The P33 convention is broader: every \([g]\) and \([g^{-1}]\) pair is externally collapsed to one unoriented owner, whether or not the two oriented classes are internally conjugate. Thus full conjugacy, root extraction, self-reciprocity, external inversion pairing, and repetition accounting must remain separate fields.

### Algorithmic Components and the Common-Interface Problem

The corpus contains strong general components. Voight (2009)<!--ref:S10--><!--anchor:none:--> develops exact fundamental-domain, presentation, reduction, and word-problem algorithms for suitable cofinite Fuchsian input. Despré et al. (2023)<!--ref:S11--><!--anchor:none:--> give a terminating method for constructing a Dirichlet domain from a hyperbolic surface polygon with side pairings. Their input regimes differ materially for P33: the former does not automatically accommodate the frozen transcendental control, and the latter works in a real-RAM setting while leaving exact algebraic-number issues outside its scope.

Epstein and Holt (2006)<!--ref:S12--><!--anchor:none:--> establish a general linear-time solution of the conjugacy problem in word-hyperbolic groups, including a positive-conjugator pathway. The corrected bibliographic range is 287–305. The result does not, on the frozen evidence, instantiate constants, canonical forms, negative evidence, serialization, or an independent validator for either P33 presentation. Lysenok (1990)<!--ref:S13--><!--anchor:none:--> establishes root-extraction and related algorithmic solvability in hyperbolic groups, but does not provide the project-specific maximal-root or no-root payload needed for admission.

General solvability therefore supports feasibility at the component level. It does not yet support an executable P33 contract. A single interface must prove that each algorithm's hypotheses hold for each exact representation and must expose evidence objects that another implementation can reject or accept without trusting the producer's status labels.

### Validated Computation and Independent Checking

The verification literature supplies an architectural pattern rather than a transferred result. Strohmaier and Uski (2013, corrected 2018)<!--ref:S16--><!--anchor:none:--> separate bounded computation, rigorous error control, and completeness reasoning for spectral objects on hyperbolic surfaces. The correction remains binding, and determinants are outside P33. Johansson (2017)<!--ref:S17--><!--anchor:none:--> presents arbitrary-precision midpoint-radius arithmetic with explicit error radii. Rump (2010)<!--ref:S18--><!--anchor:none:--> surveys rigorous validation using floating-point computation. Together, these sources justify keeping proof-bearing enclosures distinct from display decimals; they do not decide conjugacy or primitivity.

Hoffman et al. (2016)<!--ref:S19--><!--anchor:none:--> give a domain-adjacent example in which approximate hyperbolic-geometry candidates are separated from rigorously validated outputs. Their objects are cusped hyperbolic 3-manifolds, not closed genus-two owner classes. Hales et al. (2017)<!--ref:S20--><!--anchor:none:--> provide a large-scale precedent for separating production computation from independently checkable formal artifacts. That precedent neither mandates a proof assistant for P33 nor validates an unbuilt certificate system.

The resulting framework has five layers: exact object representation, candidate-universe completeness, group-theoretic decisions, exact numerical predicates, and independent validation of deterministic artifacts. Failure at one layer cannot be repaired by success at another.

## Methodology

### Executed Research Design

The executed work was a literature-based, positivist/formalist investigation. It did not include a dynamical simulation, geodesic enumeration, owner deduplication, magnetic calculation, determinant calculation, or statistical test. Phase 1 froze the research question, two mathematical objects, physical clock, field subtype, owner rule, cutoff, result taxonomy, and fail-closed states. Phase 2 searched authoritative or original records, screened 26 unique works, excluded six under prespecified criteria, and retained 20. Phase 3 aligned each retained source to one source-effect row, integrated five themes, recorded six bounded pairwise tensions, and passed an independent adversarial recheck after a citation-binding and tension-schema correction.

The corpus contains 18 works conservatively counted as peer reviewed and two items whose peer-review status was not claimed. Source-record outcomes are nine `S2_VERIFIED`, ten `VERIFIED`, and one `PLAUSIBLE`. These labels concern identity, metadata, and bounded claim fitness. In particular, `S2_VERIFIED` is not treated as theorem-level validation. No systematic Retraction Watch, PubPeer, institutional conflict-of-interest, Scopus, Web of Science, COPE, DOAJ, or Cabell audit was performed. Absence of a recorded problem is therefore not represented as a clean certificate.

### Evidence Extraction and Synthesis

Each source was constrained by four fields: admissible contribution, excluded stronger claim, compatibility role, and verification or locator limit. The synthesis asked whether the admissible contributions compose into the exact interface required by the frozen design. It did not ask whether the literature contains suggestive analogies or whether a competent future implementation seems possible. This distinction prevents general algorithmic existence from being rewritten as project-level implementation evidence.

Five themes were used: object and enumeration foundations; arithmeticity and systolic context; domain, conjugacy, and root algorithms; owner semantics; and verification architecture. Pairwise tensions were treated as conditional differences or nonconflicts, not as fabricated contradictions. The six recorded pairs are a scoped advisory sample, not an exhaustive all-pairs contradiction search. Low bibliographic coupling was not an exclusion rule, and missed cross-neighborhood tensions remain possible.

### Validity Protections

Construct validity is protected by retaining one owner as a primitive full-group conjugacy class modulo inversion. Internal validity is protected by the inherited historically target-blind cutoff, the disclosure that its coarse support implication is now known, the absence of fitting data, and identical acceptance criteria for both surfaces. Comparative validity remains limited because the two systoles are unmatched at the common cutoff. External validity is restricted to the two source-locked surfaces and \(\Lambda=21/10\).

## Evidence-Synthesis Findings

### Finding 1: The Object Layer Is Adequate for Design, Not for Census Closure

The literature supports a defensible representation and candidate-generation starting point for both octagon settings. Nazarenko (2013)<!--ref:S01--><!--anchor:none:--> and Aigon-Dupuy et al. (2005)<!--ref:S02--><!--anchor:none:--> establish the control-family context. Aurich et al. (1991)<!--ref:S05--><!--anchor:none:-->, Katz et al. (2016)<!--ref:S08--><!--anchor:none:-->, and Grácio and Sousa Ramos (2010)<!--ref:S09--><!--anchor:none:--> establish complementary target-group and enumeration contexts. The sources do not show that every candidate below the cutoff has been generated, assigned exactly once, proved primitive, paired with its inverse, and discharged from an unresolved ledger. Object-level support is therefore necessary but insufficient.

### Finding 2: Algorithmic Decidability Does Not Compose Automatically

Voight (2009)<!--ref:S10--><!--anchor:none:--> and Despré et al. (2023)<!--ref:S11--><!--anchor:none:--> support domain and presentation machinery under different input assumptions. Epstein and Holt (2006)<!--ref:S12--><!--anchor:none:--> support general conjugacy decision, while Lysenok (1990)<!--ref:S13--><!--anchor:none:--> supports root extraction. The corpus does not provide a proved adapter that places both frozen groups inside one exact input model and serializes complete positive and negative evidence. This is the central noncomposition result of the synthesis.

The lawful conclusion is `NOT_EVALUABLE_CONJUGACY_METHOD_UNAVAILABLE` if that binding cannot be frozen. It is not an owner no-go. It says that the present evidence cannot support evaluation under the required proof standard.

### Finding 3: Owner Semantics Require Independent Dispositions

Cherubini et al. (2022)<!--ref:S14--><!--anchor:none:--> and Erlandsson and Souto (2024)<!--ref:S15--><!--anchor:none:--> jointly show why primitive-root semantics and reciprocal-class semantics cannot be collapsed. A future manifest must retain separate dispositions for conjugacy, inversion pairing, primitivity, and repetition. A power can preserve an orbit relationship without creating a new primitive owner. A self-reciprocal class is a special case within, not a replacement for, the external inversion convention.

### Finding 4: Verification Architecture Cannot Substitute for Group Theory

Strohmaier and Uski (2013, corrected 2018)<!--ref:S16--><!--anchor:none:-->, Johansson (2017)<!--ref:S17--><!--anchor:none:-->, and Rump (2010)<!--ref:S18--><!--anchor:none:--> support rigorous enclosures and completeness-style separation. Hoffman et al. (2016)<!--ref:S19--><!--anchor:none:--> and Hales et al. (2017)<!--ref:S20--><!--anchor:none:--> support producer-validator separation in adjacent or formally checked settings. These sources can guide how evidence is packaged and checked. They cannot supply a missing conjugator, a no-root proof, or a complete class partition.

### Finding 5: `P33-RC-1` Is a Seven-Obligation Preexecution Gate

The synthesis turns the broad interface warning into seven concrete obligations. All remain unimplemented.

| Obligation | Literature-supported component | Missing project-specific evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Representation applicability | Exact group and domain contexts | Versioned theorem or algorithm identifier and a proof that its hypotheses apply to each frozen representation |
| 2. Full conjugacy | General hyperbolic-group decision and positive-conjugator pathway | Canonical positive fields plus complete negative or canonical evidence for both presentations |

## Discussion

### Answer to the Evidence Question

The closed corpus supports a defensible method path only at the level of components. It does not show that exact full-conjugacy, inversion-pair, primitivity, and completeness certificates can presently close both censuses. That stronger answer requires a frozen implementation contract and execution. The evidence-synthesis finding is therefore component support without interface closure.

The strongest alternative interpretation is practical: general conjugacy and root algorithms already exist, exact-domain methods exist, and rigorous checking patterns exist, so their composition may be mainly an engineering task. The literature makes that hypothesis plausible. It does not prove compatibility with the two frozen representations, provide negative-certificate semantics, bind external inversion to maximal roots, or specify deterministic validator bytes. Calling the remaining work routine would therefore exceed the evidence.

### Theoretical Implications

The study sharpens the distinction between an orbit-list computation and an ownership theorem. The latter is relational and global. Each admitted owner depends on local predicates, but completeness depends on the absence of unresolved classes across a theorem-bounded universe. This makes serialization and independent checking part of the mathematical warrant rather than administrative polish.

The synthesis also clarifies the role of numerical validation. Johansson (2017)<!--ref:S17--><!--anchor:none:--> and Rump (2010)<!--ref:S18--><!--anchor:none:--> support exact-enclosure discipline for cutoff or sign predicates. Such enclosures remain subordinate to group-theoretic equivalence. Higher precision cannot turn an incomplete conjugator search into a proof of nonconjugacy.

### Practical Implications

Future execution should begin with a certificate contract, not a census loop. The producer should emit exact representation identifiers, normalized candidates, proof-bearing cutoff fields, conjugacy and root dispositions, inversion provenance, completeness fields, and deterministic hashes. A separate checker should reject malformed schemas, unsupported status combinations, duplicate owners, unresolved dispositions, and hash drift. Any resource ceiling must produce a bounded-incomplete or not-evaluable state rather than a negative theorem.

The existing subtype remains fixed: unit-speed physical base-geodesic time, \(b=1/2\), the signed-field even subsequence, the source-locked target and control, inverse-paired primitive owners, and \(\Lambda=21/10\). The magnetic field is held as an inherited model lock but is not compared. No magnetic period, phase, stability, signed-\(k\), or field-sign result is available.

### Route-A Interpretation

The report supplies no Route credit. The only potential future contribution remains A1 exact ownership and completeness. The inherited count direction is unusable as formal A0 evidence because the cutoff straddles the frozen systoles, and only two qualifying control types are present where the roadmap requires at least three. Consequently, `A0_INCONCLUSIVE_SYSTOLE_CONFOUNDED` and `A0_CONTROL_PANEL_INCOMPLETE` remain active, and a formal A0 verdict is prohibited. A2–A4 are not run. Route B remains closed.

## Limitations and Future Work

The source corpus is bounded rather than exhaustive. Search interfaces did not expose auditable global hit totals, and MathSciNet and zbMATH were not used as systematic screening databases. Search non-detection cannot establish novelty, priority, or impossibility. The six pairwise tension records are similarly nonexhaustive.

Several evidence uses remain locator-limited. S06 is `PLAUSIBLE` and page-unpinned. Some publisher records were available only at metadata or abstract level. `S2_VERIFIED` confirms a record-level match under the frozen procedure, not that every theorem-level paraphrase has been checked against an exact passage. All citations therefore retain `anchor:none`.

`[MATERIAL GAP]` Exact quotation, page, section, or paragraph contexts safe for claim-level citation anchoring were not supplied to the report compiler. The draft cannot claim locator-level citation compliance until a separately authorized source-finalization pass resolves or explicitly adjudicates those warnings.

The study has not executed the proposed science. It has not replayed the upstream locks, enumerated a candidate universe, decided a conjugacy pair, certified a root, emitted an owner manifest, tested an independent validator, or refreshed a canonical result. The seven `P33-RC-1` obligations are all open. The eventual outcome may be a complete census, a bounded-incomplete census, or a not-evaluable state.

Future work should proceed only through a new freeze that names the exact theorem and implementation versions, proves applicability to both representations, defines positive and negative evidence fields, fixes deterministic serialization and correction provenance, supplies validator fixtures, and preserves the existing resource stops. A later execution should report execution status, each surface's completeness, scientific result, and Route interpretation on separate axes.

## Conclusion and Bounded Recommendations

The frozen literature gives P33 a credible architecture, not an executed theorem. Exact octagon representations, general conjugacy and root algorithms, owner semantics, validated enclosures, and independent-checking precedents cover the needed component classes. Their interfaces remain incompatible or unspecified at the level required for a two-presentation, zero-unresolved, independently checkable owner census.

Three bounded recommendations follow. First, freeze the seven-part `P33-RC-1` contract before any owner computation. Second, retain `NOT_EVALUABLE_CONJUGACY_METHOD_UNAVAILABLE` and bounded-incomplete states as lawful endpoints, so a capped search never becomes a false negative theorem. Third, preserve the scientific and roadmap firewalls: the common cutoff remains systole-confounded for A0, the control panel remains incomplete, magnetic comparison and determinants remain closed, and no A2 or Route conclusion is available.

This Phase-4 report is therefore ready only with warnings. It is an evidence-synthesis draft for later review, not a canonical manuscript, scientific result, novelty assessment, or promotion decision.
