### Strongest Counter-Argument

The strongest opposing argument is that the paper labels a schema interoperable before either producer, proof type, adapter, or validator exists, and it builds much of its two-surface narrative around an inherited asymmetry whose exact supporting chain remains unresolved. A list of common semantic fields is useful, but fields such as “negative proof payload,” “termination witness,” and “coverage digest” do not establish that heterogeneous proofs can be translated without loss or independently checked. Producer-specific adapters may simply re-encode the same assumptions and code paths unless the trust boundary, implementation provenance, and shared dependencies are frozen. The word independent therefore risks describing topology rather than substantive adversarial separation. At the same time, the target's expected empty status and the control's expected nonempty status are treated as a material design fact, even though exact passage support is inconclusive and the relevant systolic chain is not reconstructed in this report. The manuscript prudently forbids an arithmetic comparison, but that prohibition also removes the main scientific reason to place these two surfaces in one study. What remains is a generic certificate envelope demonstrated on no producer and an intentionally confounded pair. A stronger contribution would first validate the semantic schema against two small, genuinely heterogeneous proof fixtures, disclose the validator-adapter trust graph, and type the target/control support directions as assumptions until exact evidence closes. Alternatively, each surface could be reported separately, with the common schema introduced only after real proof payloads expose which semantics are genuinely shared.
