`T^1(Gamma((3))\H^3)`, with `Gamma((3))` the Gaussian principal congruence
group, produce an intrinsic prime-ideal local ledger for `zeta_{Q(i)}` that

primes while preserving the split/inert/ramified owners and multiplicities?

## `[PROVED]` background and the level-`(3)` lemma

`[PROVED]` from the cited background: Bianchi quotients are finite-volume
arithmetic hyperbolic 3-orbifolds; finite-volume odd-dimensional Selberg/Ruelle
zeta theory has explicit cusp and scattering terms; complex length combines
length and holonomy.

`[PROVED]` here: the projected level-`(3)` principal congruence group is neat and
torsion-free.  Let

```text
Gamma_tilde((3)) = {A in SL_2(Z[i]) : A = I mod 3}.
```

Write `A=I+3B`.  For a two-by-two matrix,

```text
1 = det(A) = 1 + 3 tr(B) + 9 det(B),
tr(A) = 2 + 3 tr(B) = 2 - 9 det(B).                 (1)
```

If the multiplicative group generated by an eigenvalue `lambda` of `A` contains
a nontrivial root of unity, then `lambda` itself is a root of unity.  Since the
other eigenvalue is `lambda^(-1)`, `tr(A)=lambda+lambda^(-1)` is real, lies in
`[-2,2]`, and belongs to `Z[i] intersect R = Z`.  Equation (1) then gives
`tr(A) = 2 mod 9` as an integer, so `tr(A)=2` and `lambda=1`.  Thus no eigenvalue
group contains a nontrivial root of unity, which is neatness.  If the projective
class of `A` had finite order, then `A^m=+/-I`; the preceding argument makes `A`
unipotent with eigenvalues `1`.  In characteristic zero a nonidentity unipotent
matrix has infinite order (`(I+N)^m=I+mN`), hence `A=I`.  Finally, `-I` is not
congruent to `I` modulo `(3)`, so projection introduces no kernel on
`Gamma_tilde((3))`.  The projected group is therefore torsion-free.

## Arithmetic owner and hypothesis

The primary calibration is

```text
zeta_{Q(i)}(s) = product_{pfrak} (1-Norm(pfrak)^(-s))^(-1).
```

Prime-ideal powers are repetitions before push-forward.  For the independent
secondary rational-prime target the frozen owner rules are:

```text
p=2:        ramified; one prime ideal of norm 2, ramification index 2;
p=1 mod 4:  split; two distinct prime ideals, each of norm p;
p=3 mod 4:  inert; one prime ideal of norm p^2.
```

Hence the local factors are `(1-2^(-s))^(-1)`, `(1-p^(-s))^(-2)`, and
`(1-p^(-2s))^(-1)`, respectively.  This owner table is `[PROVED]`; an orbit map to
it is not.

`[HEURISTIC]`: an arithmetic-to-orbit map strong enough for Gaussian prime ideals
may emerge from a Hecke decomposition of the primitive ledger.  A further
rational-prime push-forward must preserve the owner table above.  Arithmetic
provenance alone proves neither map, and no Riemann-`zeta` A0 credit is assigned.
