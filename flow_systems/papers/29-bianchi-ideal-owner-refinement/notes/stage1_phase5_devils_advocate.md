## Major findings

### `P29-DA-001` — the literal single-ideal codomain may manufacture the first kill gate

- **Severity:** Major.
- **Type:** Construct validity; framing effect; possible begging of the
  scientific significance question.
- **Exact report evidence:** Section 1 defines the output as one literal
  nonzero prime ideal while conjugate split ideals remain distinct and calls
  this a project lock. Section 2.3 makes representative-independent selection
  of one split branch the central formal compatibility question. Sections 4.3
  and 8 then present that obligation as one of the report's two principal kill
  gates. Claim-intent surface C-004 additionally prohibits weakening the
  codomain to a norm, rational prime, residue, composite ideal, or unordered
  conjugate pair.
- **Finding:** The report demonstrates that the frozen question requires a
  literal branch, but it does not establish why that non-Galois-stable output
  is the mathematically informative notion of ownership. An unoriented class
  and a conjugate split pair naturally invite an unordered ideal orbit, norm,
  or another equivariant object. Excluding those objects by definition makes
  a split-branch obstruction possible, but does not by itself show that the
  obstruction reveals a property of the Bianchi flow rather than a property
  of the chosen codomain.
- **Impact:** The independent quotient gate survives, but the advertised
  two-kill-gate contribution and the significance of a future
  `SPLIT_IDEAL_CODOMAIN_OBSTRUCTION` remain underjustified. The issue is
  revision-bearing because the report's main conceptual contribution depends
  on treating the literal branch requirement as scientifically substantive,
  not merely governance-fixed.

## Strongest steel-manned counterargument

> The most natural arithmetic object attached to an unoriented class may be a
> Galois-stable ideal orbit rather than one member of a conjugate split pair.
> If so, requiring a literal member forces the construction to choose an
> orientation-like datum that the owner definition has deliberately removed.
> A failure at that gate would diagnose an internally incompatible type
> choice, not a deep obstruction to intrinsic arithmetic ownership.

This counterargument does not erase the report's quotient-certification
contribution. It does challenge the status of the split-branch gate as a
scientifically meaningful coequal headline.
