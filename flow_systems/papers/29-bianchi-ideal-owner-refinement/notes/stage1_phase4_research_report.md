## Abstract

This report evaluates what a closed, verified literature corpus can presently support about an intrinsic Gaussian-prime-ideal owner for primitive loxodromic classes of a fixed level-(3) Bianchi geodesic flow. The executed method is literature investigation and evidence synthesis only: 22 frozen records were screened, independently source-verified, entered into a source-effect matrix, and integrated under a fail-closed research design. The synthesis supports the Picard/Bianchi geometric object, primitive and unoriented conjugacy vocabulary, several component algorithms for arithmetic-group computation, and exact ideal-arithmetic validation. It does not provide either of the two interfaces needed before finite owner performance can be evaluated. First, no source-bounded direct mechanism has yet been instantiated that selects one literal Gaussian prime ideal while satisfying conjugacy, inversion, repetition, and split-ideal compatibility. Second, no complete level-(3) primitive-root, conjugacy, inversion, and canonical-unoriented quotient with positive and negative certificates has been assembled. These are independent kill gates: performance cannot select or repair either one. The report therefore advances a bounded proof-and-certificate program with explicit obstruction and not-evaluable outcomes, while making no claim of novelty, nonexistence, executed computation, finite refinement, or Route promotion.

**Keywords:** Bianchi geodesic flow, Gaussian prime ideals, primitive conjugacy classes, owner quotient, split-prime obstruction, certificate design

## 1. Introduction and Research Question

The fixed study object is the inherited unit-speed geodesic flow on a torsion-free level-(3) Gaussian Bianchi manifold. Hyperbolic arclength is the only admitted clock. Primitive owners are primitive loxodromic conjugacy classes, with inversion identifying the primary unoriented owner and powers treated as repetitions. The intended arithmetic output is one literal nonzero prime ideal of `Z[i]`. Associates represent the same ideal, whereas conjugate split ideals remain distinct. These definitions are project locks rather than conclusions of the present report.

The literature gives a solid external frame for the geometric side. Balkanova and Frolenkov (2020)<!--ref:P29-S01--><!--anchor:none:--> and Kaneko (2022)<!--ref:P29-S02--><!--anchor:none:--> directly anchor prime-geodesic analysis on the Picard quotient associated with `PSL_2(Z[i])`. Balog et al. (2022)<!--ref:P29-S03--><!--anchor:none:--> shows that Gaussian arithmetic and ideals naturally enter analysis of this object, but that contribution does not define a unique class-to-ideal assignment. Dever and Milićević (2023)<!--ref:P29-S08--><!--anchor:none:--> provides the corpus's clearest bridge between primitive closed geodesics, primitive conjugacy classes, and inversion-aware or unoriented descriptions. Its compact-lattice setting, however, cannot be treated as an automatic decision procedure for the noncompact Picard case.

The research question is consequently dispositional rather than success-seeking: what A0-A1 disposition should a performance-independently selected Gaussian-prime-ideal owner mechanism receive after formal admissibility, literal split-ideal compatibility, primitive-owner quotient completeness, bounded finite refinement, and specificity gates are applied? A negative obstruction or `NOT_EVALUABLE` state is as legitimate as a later bounded refinement. The question does not assume that a mechanism exists.

This formulation matters because the project begins from a historically non-injective finite matrix descriptor `(D9,J3)`. A new label is not intrinsically meaningful merely because it separates some finite collisions. Ownership must be defined before performance access, and the finite matrix-row population must first be converted into a certified primitive-owner population. The literature synthesis therefore addresses prerequisite compatibility, not the numerical value of the prospective statistic `S_H`.

## 2. Literature and Theoretical Framework

### 2.1 Picard/Bianchi context without an owner law

The direct Picard literature consistently supports the object and its primitive-geodesic setting. Koyama (2001)<!--ref:P29-S04--><!--anchor:none:--> supplies historical Picard prime-geodesic context, while Nakasuji (2001)<!--ref:P29-S05--><!--anchor:none:--> places Bianchi groups within a wider hyperbolic-three-manifold explicit-formula setting. Qi (2024)<!--ref:P29-S09--><!--anchor:none:--> concerns the exact Gaussian quotient and supplies useful first-party preprint context, but it remains non-peer-reviewed and cannot carry mechanism or quotient-completeness claims.

The analytic record also illustrates why source boundaries must remain explicit. Avdispahić (2018)<!--ref:P29-S06--><!--anchor:none:--> is correction-bound for affected analytic claims to Avdispahić (2019)<!--ref:P29-S07--><!--anchor:none:-->. The pair may support corrected hyperbolic-three-manifold context, but neither record contributes a Gaussian ideal-owner construction. The correction binding is a provenance requirement, not an extra vote for the proposed mechanism and not a retraction-clearance finding.

Two foundational sources broaden the structural frame without closing the project interface. Maclachlan and Reid (2003)<!--ref:P29-S17--><!--anchor:none:--> supports arithmetic-Kleinian, trace-field, and hyperbolic-three-manifold background. Elstrodt et al. (1998)<!--ref:P29-S18--><!--anchor:none:--> supports Bianchi/Picard actions and imaginary-quadratic arithmetic. These monographs are valuable for definitions and control design, but neither was admitted as a direct formula from a primitive unoriented class to one literal prime ideal.

The theoretical lesson is a strict separation between context and ownership. A geometric orbit can live on an arithmetic quotient, and Gaussian ideals can be important in analytic formulas, without a canonical rule assigning one literal prime ideal to each primitive class. The report does not infer such a rule from shared arithmetic vocabulary.

### 2.2 Group algorithms as quotient components

A certified owner population requires more than a presentation or a generic decidability statement. Page (2015)<!--ref:P29-S10--><!--anchor:none:--> supplies explicit arithmetic-Kleinian computation and fundamental-domain algorithms. Grunewald and Segal (1980)<!--ref:P29-S11--><!--anchor:none:--> supplies a general effective framework for arithmetic groups, and Grunewald (1980)<!--ref:P29-S15--><!--anchor:none:--> supplies primary arithmetic-group conjugacy methodology. Swan (1971)<!--ref:P29-S16--><!--anchor:none:--> provides presentations for relevant special-linear and Bianchi cases and thus helps define a word/matrix interface.

These sources support the design of exact inputs, termination obligations, and witness formats. They do not jointly instantiate the precise equivalence relation required here. The relevant procedure must decide projective lifts, loxodromic status, maximal group-element roots, level-(3) conjugacy, inversion links, and canonical unoriented IDs. It must also serialize positive conjugators and complete negative certificates and verify population accounting and transitivity.

Adjacent algorithmic literatures sharpen the transfer boundary. Préaux (2006)<!--ref:P29-S12--><!--anchor:none:--> concerns conjugacy in oriented geometrizable three-manifold groups. Epstein and Holt (2006)<!--ref:P29-S13--><!--anchor:none:--> studies conjugacy under word-hyperbolic hypotheses. Eick et al. (2019)<!--ref:P29-S14--><!--anchor:none:--> supplies constructive integral-matrix conjugacy in `GL(n,Z)`. Each can inform a certificate interface, but the decided relations are differently typed. None can be substituted for conjugacy in the frozen level-(3) subgroup of `PSL_2(Z[i])` without an explicit hypothesis-preserving reduction.

The quotient is therefore constitutive of the study population. Before it is complete, 11,481 inherited matrix rows, including the loxodromic subset, are still rows rather than certified primitive owners. Bounded search failure cannot be interpreted as a negative conjugacy certificate. If an exact complete procedure cannot be frozen, the correct status is `QUOTIENT_NOT_EVALUABLE`. If a predeclared resource limit ends with unresolved relations, the correct status is `QUOTIENT_UNRESOLVED_STOP`.

### 2.3 Ideal arithmetic as a validation layer

The ideal-arithmetic side is stronger, but it answers a different question. Cohen (1993)<!--ref:P29-S19--><!--anchor:none:--> supports algorithms for number fields, ideal factorization, and prime splitting. Lenstra (1992)<!--ref:P29-S20--><!--anchor:none:--> supports an effective algebraic-number-theory and certificate perspective. Belabas (2004)<!--ref:P29-S21--><!--anchor:none:--> provides practical ideal-arithmetic interfaces and implementation context. Belabas and Simon (2024)<!--ref:P29-S22--><!--anchor:none:--> provides maximal-power detection and a witness ideal for an integral ideal.

These contributions can support later checks that an output is a nonzero Gaussian prime ideal, that associates are treated correctly, and that split, inert, and ramified types are not conflated. They do not identify which invariant of a primitive loxodromic class should own an ideal. In particular, the maximal power of an integral ideal and the maximal root of a group element are distinct mathematical objects. An ideal-power witness cannot certify group primitivity, and group repetition cannot be decided by applying an ideal routine to an unrelated descriptor.

The split-prime case exposes the most important formal compatibility question. The primary owner is unoriented, so inverse representatives must map consistently. Yet a split rational prime yields two distinct conjugate Gaussian prime ideals. A lawful mechanism must select one literal member by a source-defined, representative-independent rule that respects conjugacy, inversion, and repetition. Replacing that output with its norm, its underlying rational prime, a residue, a composite ideal, or an unordered pair would avoid the difficulty by changing the codomain. It would not answer the registered question.

## 4. Evidence-Synthesis Findings

### 4.1 Finding 1: Object support is strong but non-constructive

The evidence synthesis finds strong convergence on the Picard/Bianchi object and useful primitive-conjugacy vocabulary. It does not find a source-bounded construction that maps the fixed primitive unoriented owner to one literal Gaussian prime ideal. This distinction survives removal of any one contextual source because the direct-object records and background sources share the same support boundary: they identify the arena, not the owner law.

This is not evidence that no mechanism exists. The search was bounded and web-accessible, all report anchors lack theorem-level locators, and a later exact-locator pass could expose a formula within an admitted source or a separately authorized search could identify one outside the corpus. The present finding is simply that no candidate mechanism has been instantiated under the frozen source/formula/hash registry grammar.

### 4.2 Finding 2: Quotient completeness is independently unresolved

The evidence synthesis finds multiple useful algorithm components but no completed object-specific quotient. A presentation, a fundamental-domain algorithm, general arithmetic-group decidability, nearby three-manifold conjugacy, word-hyperbolic complexity, and integral-matrix conjugacy do not automatically compose. The required theorem-hypothesis bridges, termination argument, exact relation, and certificate serialization remain missing.

This gap does not depend on whether a promising ideal formula is eventually identified. Even a formally admissible owner law cannot be evaluated on owners until every relevant matrix row is assigned to a certified primitive root and canonical unoriented class. Conversely, a complete quotient would not invent an owner law. This logical independence is the central result of the literature synthesis.

### 4.3 Finding 3: Literal ownership creates a split-branch obligation

The corpus supports exact ideal arithmetic but not ownership selection. The conjunction of unoriented owner semantics and literal split-ideal outputs creates a formal obligation that must be met before any finite score is opened. A candidate's branch choice must be deterministic, source-defined, conjugacy invariant, inversion compatible, repetition compatible, and independent of `(D9,J3)` collision outcomes. Failure yields `SPLIT_IDEAL_CODOMAIN_OBSTRUCTION` or `FORMAL_MAP_REFUTED`. It does not justify weakening the output type.

This finding is narrower than a universal obstruction theorem. The synthesis does not assert that inversion necessarily exchanges split branches for every conceivable mechanism. It states that a source-defined invariant selection law has not been instantiated and that the design must fail closed until one is proved.

### 4.4 Finding 4: Performance is downstream of both kill gates

The prospective primary estimand is the exact number `S_H(M)` of baseline-collision owner pairs in a certified holdout that a fixed mechanism maps to different literal ideals. The literature synthesis has not computed it. `S_H` is evaluable only after a performance-independent mechanism registry, formal-law pass, complete quotient, prospective owner partition, nonempty baseline-collision set, and valid outputs.

A positive `S_H` would establish only bounded finite refinement within the inherited word-ball and prospective holdout. It would not prove a global orbit-to-prime-ideal correspondence. Specificity would remain a separate gate requiring three directionally sensitive, evaluator-accepted control types. Relabeling-invariant ideal permutations and one-to-one composite substitutions remain unit or type tests rather than specificity evidence.

## 5. Discussion

The report's main contribution is conceptual precision. The first kill gate concerns whether a direct owner mechanism is formally admissible. The second concerns whether the finite row population has been converted into the correct owner population. Treating these as one engineering task creates two predictable errors: choosing a mechanism because it separates observed rows, or evaluating separation on rows that represent repetitions, inverses, or conjugate representatives of the same owner.

The gate order also clarifies how to interpret failure. `NO_ADMISSIBLE_IDEAL_OWNER_MECHANISM` is a source-registry outcome, not an impossibility theorem. `SPLIT_IDEAL_CODOMAIN_OBSTRUCTION` and `FORMAL_MAP_REFUTED` are formal outcomes about a registered candidate. `QUOTIENT_NOT_EVALUABLE` and `QUOTIENT_UNRESOLVED_STOP` are population-construction outcomes. `REFINEMENT_NOT_EVALUABLE`, `FINITE_NO_REFINEMENT`, and `FINITE_REFINEMENT` are downstream finite-estimand outcomes. `SPECIFICITY_NOT_ESTABLISHED` remains possible even if finite refinement is positive.

This taxonomy protects the dynamical object from arithmetic relabeling. The clock remains hyperbolic arclength, and no word-length or fitted clock is introduced. Primitive loxodromic classes remain owners. Powers remain repetitions. The arithmetic target remains Gaussian prime ideals attached to the `Q(i)` setting, not Riemann primes and not a Riemann-zeta Euler product. Nothing in the corpus advances determinants, zeros, analytic continuation, operator lifts, A2-A4, or Route B.

## 6. Theoretical and Practical Implications

Theoretically, the bottleneck is not generic factorization in `Z[i]`. It is compatibility among ownership, conjugacy, inversion, repetition, and split-prime branching. A lawful mechanism must be intrinsic before it is discriminative. This reframes a potentially misleading finite classification exercise as a proof-first study of mathematical types.

The quotient requirement has a second theoretical implication. Quotient certification is not ordinary data cleaning. It defines the unit over which the estimand is meaningful. Positive and negative pair certificates, maximal-root witnesses, inverse links, transitivity witnesses, and deterministic serialization are therefore part of construct validity.

Practically, the next authorized scientific-design step should remain modular. One package should conduct an exact-locator pass for at most three direct formulas and freeze formula bytes and laws. A separate package should build a theorem-hypothesis map for projective lifts, group roots, level-(3) conjugacy, inversion, and serialization. Only after independent formal passes should a scholar-confirmed freeze allow either package to touch a Paper-29 row. This ordering prevents performance leakage and makes obstruction outcomes publishable rather than hidden as implementation failures.

## 7. Limitations and Future Work

The source search was bounded, English-language, and web-accessible rather than exhaustive across proprietary subscription databases. Seventeen of 22 sources predate 2021, reflecting foundational mathematical literature. This age distribution cannot support a novelty inference. Books and a research chapter were retained for definitions and methods but were not counted as peer-reviewed journal evidence.

Verification was primarily metadata-, abstract-, and bounded full-text-scope based. Exact theorem hypotheses and locators were not frozen for report compilation. Every citation consequently carries `anchor:none`, and the draft has not passed locator-level citation compliance. Source-level conflict declarations were not comprehensively audited, and no structured live retraction query was run.

The report also has a strict scientific limitation: no candidate formula was extracted, no formula law was proved, no quotient algorithm was instantiated, no certificate was generated, no inherited row was reclassified, no prospective partition was emitted, no control was run, and no `S_H` value was calculated. Future work requires fresh scholar authority and should stop on the first unresolved prerequisite.

## 8. Conclusion and Bounded Recommendations

The frozen literature is sufficient to organize a rigorous Paper-29 research program but insufficient to report an owner mechanism or owner-level finite result. Its strongest contribution is the separation of two independent pre-performance kill gates: literal Gaussian-prime-ideal mechanism admissibility and complete primitive/unoriented quotient certification. Ideal arithmetic, group algorithms, and Picard context supply components. They do not collapse either gate.

Three recommendations follow. First, preserve the literal codomain and treat split-branch incompatibility as a legitimate obstruction. Second, require an exact, terminating, certificate-producing quotient before any row-level performance statement. Third, keep the mechanism registry, candidate ordering, quotient plan, owner partition, estimand, and sensitivity-qualified controls frozen before target access. These recommendations describe a future proof-and-certificate program. None has been executed in this phase.
