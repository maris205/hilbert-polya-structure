## Reproducible bounded-search protocol

### Interfaces and authoritative endpoints

The bounded search used the following interfaces. A general web index was used only to discover authoritative records; no search-result page, blog, encyclopedia, citation aggregator, or AI summary was admitted as evidence.

1. Crossref REST metadata and DOI resolver for exact-title/DOI metadata resolution.
2. Publisher and journal interfaces: American Mathematical Society, SpringerLink, De Gruyter/Forum Mathematicum, Elsevier/ScienceDirect, Wiley Online Library, Oxford Academic, Mathematical Sciences Publishers, Annals of Mathematics, and Centre Mersenne/Numdam.
3. Primary repositories and institutional copies: arXiv, Project Euclid, university/institutional repositories, and author-hosted manuscripts when a version-of-record landing page was also identifiable.
4. Book publisher records for monographs whose chapters supply foundational definitions or algorithms.

Proprietary Scopus and Web of Science result sets were not available. Semantic Scholar identifiers were not used for a verification verdict in this bibliography pass; exact DOI, normalized title, author, and year were used for deduplication. That is a declared limitation, not evidence for or against novelty.

### Search limits

- Date coverage: database inception through **2026-09-02**.
- Search language: English. A source was not excluded merely because it also supplied a French abstract; the included work itself had to be usable in English.
- Eligible document types: original peer-reviewed articles, formal corrections, primary preprints where no version of record was located, scholarly monographs, and original research book chapters.
- Currency rule: direct 2021–2026 work was preferred when it covered the frozen interface. Older work was retained only when it was foundational, defined the relevant object, supplied the governing theorem/algorithm, or remained the directly applicable published source.
- Stopping rule: stop after every frozen coverage cell had at least two plausible sources where the literature permitted it, metadata for at least 15 unique sources had been resolved, the peer-reviewed share exceeded 60%, and two consecutive query refinements yielded only duplicates, secondary material, or sources outside the A0–A1 fence.

### A priori inclusion and exclusion criteria

Include a record if it satisfied at least one frozen coverage cell and supplied a citable primary or authoritative basis for:

1. primitive closed geodesics as primitive loxodromic/conjugacy classes in a Bianchi/Picard or closely matching hyperbolic 3-manifold setting;
2. `PSL_2(Z[i])`, Gaussian/Bianchi arithmetic, or prime-ideal splitting/factorization in `Q(i)`;
3. an effective group/number-field algorithm whose inputs, outputs, termination, or witness structure can inform the later fail-closed quotient/certificate interface; or
4. an arithmetic control family that preserves the type while changing the ring/field or ideal arithmetic.

Exclude a record if it was secondary commentary; lacked a stable primary record; addressed only surfaces or unrelated groups without a transferable interface; asserted only existential separability with no usable effective procedure; treated label permutations as arithmetic evidence; replaced prime ideals by rational-prime labels; or would require applying a candidate mechanism, opening the prospective holdout, evaluating `S_H`, or entering A2.

## Distributional-skew advisory

- **Time:** 17/22 (77.3%) sources predate 2021. This triggers an older-literature advisory. The retention is deliberate: the definitions of Bianchi/Picard geodesics, arithmetic-group algorithms, group presentations, and computational number-field interfaces are foundational. Five 2021–2026 records provide a current check. This skew is not a novelty argument.
- **Method:** the corpus is entirely mathematical/theoretical or algorithmic. That concentration is imposed by the frozen proof-plus-certificate question; it does not support an empirical performance claim.
- **Venue/geography:** no single journal supplies 70% of the corpus. Author affiliation geography was not inferred from names or used as a selection variable.
- **Object:** Picard-manifold prime-geodesic papers are overrepresented within the geodesic theme because Paper 29 fixes `Q(i)`. Generic hyperbolic 3-manifold sources are retained only to define transfer boundaries.

### Theme G: primitive geodesics, loxodromic conjugacy, and the Picard/Bianchi object

**P29-S01.** Balkanova, O., & Frolenkov, D. (2020). Prime geodesic theorem for the Picard manifold. *Advances in Mathematics, 375*, 107377. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2020.107377

- Coverage: directly fixes the Picard quotient of `H^3` by `PSL_2(Z[i])` and its prime-geodesic counting problem, with Gaussian-integer arithmetic in the analytic method.
- Claim-support boundary: supports object identity and primitive-geodesic terminology; it does not define Paper 29's owner map, quotient certificate, or collision statistic.

**P29-S02.** Kaneko, I. (2022). The prime geodesic theorem for `PSL_2(Z[i])` and spectral exponential sums. *Algebra & Number Theory, 16*(8), 1845–1887. https://doi.org/10.2140/ant.2022.16.1845

- Coverage: a direct, peer-reviewed `Z[i]` source connecting the Picard group, primitive geodesics, and spectral sums.
- Claim-support boundary: informs the registered group/geodesic domain, not the admissibility or performance of an ideal-owner mechanism.

**P29-S03.** Balog, A., Biró, A., Cherubini, G., & Laaksonen, N. (2022). Bykovskii-type theorem for the Picard manifold. *International Mathematics Research Notices, 2022*(3), 1893–1921. https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnaa128

- Coverage: treats prime geodesics on the Picard manifold using arithmetic over Gaussian integers and explicitly works with ideals/characters over `Z[i]`.
- Claim-support boundary: supports the relevance of Gaussian arithmetic to the same geometric object; it does not license a particular primitive-owner assignment.

**P29-S04.** Koyama, S. (2001). Prime geodesic theorem for the Picard manifold under the mean-Lindelöf hypothesis. *Forum Mathematicum, 13*(6), 781–793. https://doi.org/10.1515/form.2001.034

- Coverage: foundational Picard-manifold prime-geodesic source and a direct historical link between the Bianchi quotient and its arithmetic spectral formulation.
- Claim-support boundary: conditional analytic estimates are outside Paper 29's estimand; only the object and primitive-geodesic interface are in scope.

**P29-S05.** Nakasuji, M. (2001). Prime geodesic theorem via the explicit formula of Ψ for hyperbolic 3-manifolds. *Proceedings of the Japan Academy, Series A, Mathematical Sciences, 77*(7), 130–133. https://doi.org/10.3792/pjaa.77.130

- Coverage: identifies the hyperbolic 3-manifold prime-geodesic setting and includes Bianchi groups `PSL_2(O_K)` in the formulation.
- Claim-support boundary: does not provide a computable conjugacy representative or Gaussian prime-ideal owner certificate.

**P29-S06.** Avdispahić, M. (2018). On the prime geodesic theorem for hyperbolic 3-manifolds. *Mathematische Nachrichten, 291*(14–15), 2160–2167. https://doi.org/10.1002/mana.201700190

### Theme C: effective conjugacy, arithmetic-group computation, and certificate interfaces

**P29-S10.** Page, A. (2015). Computing arithmetic Kleinian groups. *Mathematics of Computation, 84*(295), 2361–2390. https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-2015-02939-1

- Coverage: gives explicit algorithms for arithmetic Kleinian groups and fundamental domains, providing a concrete basis for later input normalization and checkable geometric/group computations.
- Claim-support boundary: a fundamental-domain algorithm is not by itself a complete conjugacy, root-primitivity, or unoriented-owner certificate.

**P29-S11.** Grunewald, F., & Segal, D. (1980). Some general algorithms. I: Arithmetic groups. *Annals of Mathematics, 112*(3), 531–583. https://doi.org/10.2307/1971091

- Coverage: foundational decision-procedure framework for arithmetic groups, relevant to termination and effective witness design.
- Claim-support boundary: general decidability does not instantiate Paper 29's exact `PSL_2(Z[i])` quotient, canonical representative, or fail-closed certificate bytes.

**P29-S12.** Préaux, J.-P. (2006). Conjugacy problem in groups of oriented geometrizable 3-manifolds. *Topology, 45*(1), 171–208. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.top.2005.06.002

- Coverage: supplies an algorithmic conjugacy result in a closely related 3-manifold group setting and clarifies the difference between an existence theorem and a decision procedure.
- Claim-support boundary: its manifold-group algorithm cannot be assumed to provide the Bianchi matrix/ideal witness format; an explicit reduction would be required.

**P29-S13.** Epstein, D. B. A., & Holt, D. F. (2006). The linearity of the conjugacy problem in word-hyperbolic groups. *International Journal of Algebra and Computation, 16*(2), 287–305. https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218196706002986

- Coverage: provides complexity-aware conjugacy methods in word-hyperbolic groups and is useful for specifying what a checkable conjugacy subroutine must return.
- Claim-support boundary: the nonuniform lattice `PSL_2(Z[i])` is not admitted by citation alone as an instance of every hypothesis; no direct owner claim follows.

**P29-S14.** Eick, B., Hofmann, T., & O’Brien, E. A. (2019). The conjugacy problem in `GL(n,Z)`. *Journal of the London Mathematical Society, 100*(3), 731–756. https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.12246

- Coverage: an explicit integral-matrix conjugacy algorithm with concrete subproblems and witnesses, useful for certificate-schema design.
- Claim-support boundary: conjugacy in `GL(n,Z)` is not the same equivalence relation as conjugacy in `PSL_2(Z[i])`; a valid comparison map must be frozen later.

**P29-S15.** Grunewald, F. J. (1980). Solution of the conjugacy problem in certain arithmetic groups. In G. Higman (Ed.), *Word problems II* (pp. 101–139). North-Holland. https://doi.org/10.1016/S0049-237X(08)71335-1

- Coverage: primary research chapter on effective conjugacy in arithmetic groups and an additional foundation for later algorithm provenance.
- Claim-support boundary: not counted as peer reviewed here; applicability to the exact group and a complete primitive-root witness must be demonstrated, not presumed.

**P29-S16.** Swan, R. G. (1971). Generators and relations for certain special linear groups. *Advances in Mathematics, 6*(1), 1–77. https://doi.org/10.1016/0001-8708(71)90027-2

- Coverage: supplies presentations for special linear groups over relevant rings, including classical Bianchi-group cases, which can ground a word/matrix certificate interface.
- Claim-support boundary: a presentation does not alone solve conjugacy, maximal-root primitivity, inversion invariance, or owner selection.

## Phase-2 limitations

1. This was a bounded web-accessible search, not an exhaustive subscription-database review.
2. Full texts were inspected only where the authoritative interface exposed them; `full_text_status` in the inventory distinguishes available/inspected scope from metadata-only records. No source-verification verdict is encoded.
3. Books and the research chapter are foundational but are conservatively excluded from the peer-reviewed numerator.
4. The corpus is strong on component algorithms and definitions but sparse on a single constructive primitive/unoriented Bianchi owner quotient. Later source verification must test theorem hypotheses and exact interfaces source by source.
5. The Avdispahić 2018 article must never be cited for its corrected exponents without its 2019 erratum.
6. Search absence, coverage gaps, and the age distribution must not be used as evidence of novelty.
7. No source was used to inspect the prospective holdout, apply any candidate mechanism, compute `S_H`, or advance Route A beyond A1.
