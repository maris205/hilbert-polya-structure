## Review Body

The manuscript builds a genuine bridge among arithmetic owner semantics, closed-orbit classification, provenance-rich ledgers, and falsifiable experimental design. Its strongest cross-disciplinary move is to treat object identity, quotient closure, mechanism registration, and performance as separately typed nodes whose evidence cannot be exchanged. It also states unusually careful external-validity limits: the literal ideal codomain is a stress test, candidate failure is not universal nonexistence, and finite separation is not a global orbit-to-ideal theorem. D4 nevertheless warrants a warning because readers outside the originating fields must reconstruct the vocabulary and control logic from several prose sections. A compact dependency/glossary surface and a control-to-stop-state matrix would make the otherwise defensible bridge easier to audit. This assessment grants no scientific execution or Route credit. Fine ideal arithmetic and full-group conjugacy correctness remain outside this seat's remit.

### S1: Certificate dependencies are made explicit

The four-node sequence distinguishes stable objects, quotient formation, mechanism admission, and downstream performance, then assigns independent replay to the edges. This is a substantive conceptual bridge from mathematical equivalence relations to proof-carrying dataflow rather than a metaphorical use of provenance.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "The architecture is most naturally read as a directed certificate graph."

### S2: Provenance is tied to estimand identity

The performance ledger is allowed to consume only a certified quotient and a matching registered mechanism. This makes lineage a truth condition for the finite estimand and gives data-governance readers a concrete reason that hashes and serializers matter mathematically.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §4.3 "Thus provenance is part of the estimand, not merely administrative metadata."

### S3: Extrapolation boundaries are unusually disciplined

The discussion repeatedly separates a conditional obstruction under the strict literal codomain from broader Galois-stable or orientation-enriched ownership concepts. That restraint prevents cross-disciplinary language from silently inflating a design stress test into a universal mathematical claim.

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §7 "It must not be advertised as an obstruction to every Galois-stable or orientation-enriched ownership concept."

### W1: Adjacent readers lack a compact vocabulary and dependency map

**Problem**: Terms such as matrix row, primitive owner, oriented or unoriented class, Gate M, Gate Q, registry entry, quotient ledger, and performance estimand are defined across multiple sections but never consolidated into one reader-facing map.

**Evidence Anchor**: absence: Abstract, §§1 and 4.1–4.3 — expected a compact glossary or visual dependency map connecting rows, owners, equivalence classes, gates, ledgers, and the estimand; checked abstract, introduction, both gate subsections, certificate-graph subsection, discussion, and conclusion

**Why it matters**: A data-provenance or dynamical-systems reader can understand each local definition yet still misread which transformations are mathematical quotients, which are registered hypotheses, and which are merely prospective artifacts.

**Suggestion**: Add one table or directed diagram with each node's input type, output type, admission predicate, stop state, and prohibited downstream use. Keep every unresolved node explicitly marked prospective.

**Severity**: Minor

**Confidence**: 4 — core expertise in provenance-rich certificate and equivalence-class design; fine ideal arithmetic is outside scope

### W2: Controls are typed but not mapped to diagnostic conclusions

**Problem**: The manuscript distinguishes relabeling, composite-object, and directionally sensitive controls, but it does not show which control outcome diagnoses mechanism invalidity, quotient contamination, registration leakage, or lack of arithmetic specificity.

**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §§4.3–4.5 and 6 — expected a control-to-failure-state matrix distinguishing mechanism, quotient, registration, and specificity failures; checked certificate graph, performance firewall, prospective interfaces, discussion, and limitations

**Why it matters**: Without that mapping, an adjacent experimental-design reader cannot tell which negative control falsifies which part of the interdisciplinary claim or which outcomes are intentionally non-diagnostic.

**Suggestion**: Add a prospective control ledger with control type, invariant inputs, permitted perturbation, expected diagnostic, non-diagnostic interpretations, and the stop state produced by failure. Do not populate it with unexecuted results.

**Severity**: Minor

**Confidence**: 4 — adjacent expertise in falsifiable benchmarking and control semantics; no claim about unexecuted arithmetic behavior
