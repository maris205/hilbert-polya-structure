# P29 Devil's Advocate Report — Checkpoint 1

Date: **2026-09-01 UTC (date only; exact review time unavailable)**  
Reviewed artifacts: `stage1_phase1_rq_brief.md` and
`stage1_phase1_methodology_blueprint.md`  
Review boundary: **Phase 1 scoping only; no external search or Phase-2 work**

## Verdict: REVISE

The design has strong target-data firewalls, honest finite-census boundaries,
and appropriate willingness to accept an obstruction. It is not yet ready to
freeze, however: the admissible candidate family and primary success criterion
remain adaptive, two proposed controls can preserve the very collision metrics
they are supposed to challenge, and the owner-grouped split is not operational
until an exact quotient procedure and stop rule exist.

## Critical issues

No Critical issue is established from the Phase-1 materials alone. The core
mechanism-or-obstruction study remains salvageable, but the Major issues below
must be resolved before Phase 2 can feed a candidate into execution.

## Numbered issues

### DA29-1 — The candidate family and selection rule are not frozen

- **Severity:** Major
- **Type:** Method / researcher degrees of freedom
- **Location:** RQ, “source-derived … owner map”; Methodology, “Source-screen
  dependency,” Formal branch step 1, and Exact finite branch step 3.
- **Problem:** Phase 2 may supply an unspecified number of
  “source-authorized” constructions, after which a “surviving candidate” is
  selected on the construction partition. The admissible construction grammar,
  maximum candidate count, rejection order, tie rule, and treatment of multiple
  candidates are absent. A sealed test does not undo adaptive search on a broad
  construction family, especially when the historical `(D9,J3)` behavior is
  already known.
- **Impact:** A positive separation result could reflect mechanism search and
  selection flexibility rather than an intrinsic, prospectively defined map.
- **Recommendation:** Before any candidate-dependent computation, freeze either
  one construction or a closed candidate-family grammar with deterministic
  ordering, multiplicity treatment, selection rule, and all-fail outcome. If
  Phase 2 discovers candidates rather than merely verifies a frozen one, return
  them to a new author-approved design checkpoint instead of proceeding
  directly to the finite branch.

### DA29-2 — “Refine `(D9,J3)`” has no decision-bearing endpoint

- **Severity:** Major
- **Type:** RQ clarity / method alignment
- **Location:** Primary RQ; Exact finite branch steps 4–6; Kill gate 6.
- **Problem:** The blueprint lists several outputs—validity, mapped fraction,
  distinct ideals, collisions, largest bucket, type confusion, and control
  margin—but does not identify a primary endpoint, an ordered gate, a minimally
  meaningful improvement, or the meaning of “survives equally.” A map can be
  fully well-defined yet separate poorly, or separate well while failing
  specificity; those are different scientific outcomes.
- **Impact:** The primary RQ has no deterministic yes/no or typed disposition,
  so the same results could be framed as refinement, weak evidence, or failure
  after inspection.
- **Recommendation:** Freeze an ordered disposition contract: first
  well-definedness/codomain/repetition, then quotient availability, then one
  primary separation endpoint, then specificity controls. Define the exact
  result class for every gate combination and keep all other metrics secondary.

### DA29-3 — Two negative controls are invariant under relabeling

- **Severity:** Major
- **Type:** Control validity / proves-too-much test
- **Location:** Frozen controls 3–4; Exact finite branch step 6; Kill gate 6.
- **Problem:** A bijective permutation of ideal labels within strata preserves
  the number and sizes of collision buckets. Likewise, substituting
  norm-matched composite labels one-for-one can preserve the owner partition.
  If separation metrics are the endpoint, these controls may be mathematically
  unable to differ from the candidate even when the mechanism is meaningful.
  Conversely, Kill gate 6 would then reject every map for a property forced by
  control construction.
- **Impact:** The control margin can become either non-discriminating or an
  automatic false failure; it cannot support A0 specificity as currently
  defined.
- **Recommendation:** For each control, state which statistic it is expected
  to change and prove that the control is capable of changing it. Controls
  that merely rename output bins should be treated as label-invariance unit
  tests, not specificity tests. The canonical three-control minimum must be
  satisfied by genuinely mechanism-challenging controls.

### DA29-4 — The analysis split depends on an unresolved quotient with no finite stop contract

- **Severity:** Major
- **Type:** Feasibility / scope
- **Location:** Frozen primitive owner and equivalence; Frozen target-blind
  partition; Kill gates 2 and 4.
- **Problem:** The corpus consists of matrix rows in a finite word ball, not a
  certified population of primitive unoriented conjugacy classes. Yet the
  construction/validation/test split requires a canonical primitive-owner ID.
  The design provides a kill gate but no exact conjugacy/root method, proof
  obligation for negative equivalence decisions, finite resource boundary, or
  first unresolved condition.
- **Impact:** The empirical branch is not executable as frozen. An open-ended
  conjugator/root search could change the population or partition after the
  design checkpoint.
- **Recommendation:** Make quotient certification an explicit prerequisite
  artifact with exact positive and negative certificates plus a prospective
  stop rule. Until it passes, call the population “loxodromic matrix rows
  awaiting primitive-owner quotient,” and do not describe a sealed owner split
  as available.

### DA29-5 — The unoriented owner may be unable to select one split prime ideal

- **Severity:** Minor at scoping; potentially Major after source screening
- **Type:** Construct validity
- **Location:** Owner codomain and inversion rule.
- **Problem:** The design simultaneously requires
  `owner(gamma^-1)=owner(gamma)` and insists that conjugate prime ideals above a
  split rational prime remain distinct. A natural invariant of an unoriented
  class may determine only a norm or an unordered conjugate pair, not one
  distinguished split ideal, unless additional orientation/embedding data are
  intrinsic and inversion-invariant.
- **Impact:** The desired codomain may be structurally incompatible with the
  frozen equivalence relation.
- **Recommendation:** Treat this compatibility as the first formal obstruction
  test. Do not weaken the codomain or quietly reintroduce orientation after
  outcomes are known.

## Strongest counter-argument

> An unoriented primitive Bianchi conjugacy class has no intrinsic reason to
> choose one of two conjugate split Gaussian prime ideals. Any rule that does
> so may require an orientation, embedding, or arithmetic label not preserved
> by inversion; any rule that avoids that choice returns only a norm or an
> unordered pair and therefore fails the frozen codomain. The proposed map may
> thus be obstructed before collision refinement is meaningful.

This is an adversarial hypothesis, not a finding; Phase 2 must test it rather
than assume it away.

## What's missing

- A closed candidate-family/selection protocol.
- A single primary endpoint and exact typed disposition table.
- Proof that each planned specificity control can affect that endpoint.
- A quotient/primitivity algorithm with complete certificate semantics and a
  finite resource stop.
- A leakage analysis for algebraically related owners across hash partitions.
- An explicit resolution of inversion invariance versus split-ideal
  distinctness.

## Stress-test results

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Remove the inherited finite ledger—does the empirical claim remain testable? | **No.** Only a formal mechanism/obstruction question remains. |
| Flip the RQ: is “no intrinsic single-ideal owner exists” credible? | **Yes.** It is the strongest live alternative and is not prejudged by the design. |
| Permute output labels—can collision counts remain unchanged? | **Yes.** This exposes a non-discriminating proposed control. |
| Substitute one-to-one composite labels—can the owner partition remain unchanged? | **Yes.** Separation alone cannot validate prime-ideal semantics. |
| Quotient certification fails—can the owner-performance branch still run? | **No.** Only a scoped quotient obstruction may be reported. |
| Apply the mechanism to a neighboring or parent group—does a positive result remain Gaussian-specific automatically? | **No.** Specificity requires a directionally valid matched control. |
| “So what?”—is significance justified now? | **Partly.** An exact obstruction is valuable, but a positive refinement is uninterpretable until endpoints and controls are repaired. |
