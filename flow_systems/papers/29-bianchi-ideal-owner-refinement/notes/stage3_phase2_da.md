The literal one-prime-ideal codomain is expressly a project-selected stress test, not a canonical output suggested by the geometry or forced by the literature. A failure at Gate M would therefore diagnose the chosen type or one registered mechanism, while a failure at Gate Q would diagnose an unfinished quotient engine; neither would yet illuminate intrinsic arithmetic ownership. Their non-entailment is also close to a type-level observation: a labeling rule does not construct its observational units, and a quotient does not invent a labeling rule. The extensive certificate graph makes that observation auditable, but no mechanism, quotient, serializer, witness, or replay receipt demonstrates that the proposed separation resolves a difficult interaction rather than restating it. Because all citation-to-passage links remain inconclusive, even the adjacent algorithmic components cannot presently establish that either gate is feasible in the exact subgroup. On this view, the paper's strongest achievement is preventing invalid claims, not generating a new theorem, obstruction, computation, or empirically discriminating method. A more compelling contribution would instantiate one nontrivial certificate edge, prove a conditional no-go statement for the strict codomain, or show through a worked adversarial fixture that the two-gate architecture catches an error that a simpler typed pipeline would miss. Until then, the architecture is coherent but its scientific decision value remains prospective.
