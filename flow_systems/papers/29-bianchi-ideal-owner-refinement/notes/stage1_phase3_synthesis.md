## Theme 1 — Exact object and owner semantics

The strongest direct-object records consistently locate the analytic setting
at the Picard quotient associated with `PSL_2(Z[i])` and use primitive-geodesic
language: Balkanova and Frolenkov (2020)
<!--ref:P29-S01--><!--anchor:none--> and Kaneko (2022)
<!--ref:P29-S02--><!--anchor:none--> are the principal anchors. Balog et al.
(2022) <!--ref:P29-S03--><!--anchor:none--> adds bounded context that Gaussian
arithmetic and ideals naturally occur in analysis of the same object, but its
verified support boundary excludes a unique class-to-prime-ideal assignment.

Dever and Milicevic (2023) <!--ref:P29-S08--><!--anchor:none--> supplies the
most useful corpus-level bridge from primitive closed geodesics to primitive
conjugacy classes and inversion/unoriented concepts. Its compact-lattice scope
does not, by itself, deliver a decision procedure for the noncompact Picard
group. Swan (1971) <!--ref:P29-S16--><!--anchor:none--> and Elstrodt et al.
(1998) <!--ref:P29-S18--><!--anchor:none--> can ground a presentation and the
Bianchi/Picard action, but neither turns the matrix-row population into a
certified primitive-owner population.

The wider prime-geodesic group is contextual rather than owner-constructive.
Koyama (2001) <!--ref:P29-S04--><!--anchor:none-->, Nakasuji (2001)
<!--ref:P29-S05--><!--anchor:none-->, and Qi (2024)
<!--ref:P29-S09--><!--anchor:none--> help delimit the Picard/Bianchi setting;
Qi remains a non-peer-reviewed preprint. Avdispahic (2018)
<!--ref:P29-S06--><!--anchor:none--> must remain bound to the Avdispahic (2019)
correction <!--ref:P29-S07--><!--anchor:none--> for affected analytic use. None
of these contextual records is admitted as owner-map evidence.

**Theme strength:** strong for object identity and primitive/inversion
vocabulary; weak for an executable primitive-owner quotient or ideal owner.

## Theme 2 — Group decision procedures are components, not a quotient

Page (2015) <!--ref:P29-S10--><!--anchor:none--> provides an explicit
arithmetic-Kleinian computation interface, while Grunewald and Segal (1980)
<!--ref:P29-S11--><!--anchor:none--> and Grunewald (1980)
<!--ref:P29-S15--><!--anchor:none--> supply general arithmetic-group decision
methodology. These records support requirements such as exact inputs,
termination arguments, and witness-producing algorithms. The frozen claim-
fitness boundaries do not identify one procedure that simultaneously decides
level-(3) conjugacy, inversion, maximal roots, and canonical unoriented owners
with serialized positive and negative certificates.

The adjacent conjugacy literature further sharpens what cannot be silently
transferred. Preaux (2006) <!--ref:P29-S12--><!--anchor:none--> concerns
oriented geometrizable three-manifold groups; Epstein and Holt (2006)
<!--ref:P29-S13--><!--anchor:none--> assumes word-hyperbolic hypotheses; and
Eick et al. (2019) <!--ref:P29-S14--><!--anchor:none--> treats conjugacy in
`GL(n,Z)`. These are useful interface comparators, but the verified corpus does
not supply the reductions that would equate their decision relations with
conjugacy in the frozen level-(3) subgroup of `PSL_2(Z[i])`.

The synthesis therefore separates five group-side obligations that no one
source may be presumed to collapse:

1. exact projective lift and loxodromic-domain validation;
2. maximal **group-element** root detection with a termination/completeness
   argument;
3. level-(3) conjugacy with positive conjugators and complete negative
   certificates;
4. inversion linking and canonical oriented/unoriented serialization; and
5. population-accounting and transitivity checks over the frozen matrix rows.

**Theme strength:** moderate for component design; insufficient for exact
object-specific composition or execution.

## Theme 3 — Gaussian ideal arithmetic does not supply ownership

Cohen (1993) <!--ref:P29-S19--><!--anchor:none--> and Belabas (2004)
<!--ref:P29-S21--><!--anchor:none--> support exact ideal arithmetic,
factorization, and implementation-oriented validation in number fields.
Lenstra (1992) <!--ref:P29-S20--><!--anchor:none--> supports an effective
problem-and-certificate viewpoint. These sources make literal Gaussian ideal
type checks plausible as a later, separately authorized validation layer; they
do not specify which invariant of a primitive loxodromic class should own one
prime ideal.

Belabas and Simon (2024) <!--ref:P29-S22--><!--anchor:none--> is especially
useful because it supports maximal-power detection and a witness ideal for an
**integral ideal**. Its verified boundary explicitly excludes loxodromic
group-element root detection. Hence ideal-power primitivity cannot close the
group-side primitive-owner quotient, and a group power cannot be classified
by applying an ideal-power routine to an unrelated descriptor.

The literal codomain creates a further compatibility test. The unoriented
semantics supported at a conceptual level by Dever and Milicevic (2023)
<!--ref:P29-S08--><!--anchor:none--> must coexist with factorization into
literal ideals supported at a component level by Cohen (1993)
<!--ref:P29-S19--><!--anchor:none-->. When a rational prime splits into
distinct conjugate Gaussian prime ideals, an admissible mechanism must have a
source-defined and representative-independent rule selecting one member while
remaining inversion invariant. The corpus does not currently freeze such a
rule; replacing the output by a norm, rational prime, residue, or unordered
pair would change the registered question rather than solve it.

**Theme strength:** strong for the separate ideal-arithmetic layer; absent for
a direct primitive-class owner law and unresolved for split-branch invariance.

## Debates and interpretive forks

These are synthesis-level interface forks, not assertions that the cited
authors directly dispute one another.

- **Transfer versus instantiation.** General decidability can inform a
  certificate design, but whether it yields a terminating implementation for
  the exact level-(3) Picard subgroup remains unestablished by the frozen
  locators. Grunewald and Segal (1980)
  <!--ref:P29-S11--><!--anchor:none--> supports the general side; Page (2015)
  <!--ref:P29-S10--><!--anchor:none--> supplies a closer arithmetic-Kleinian
  component.
- **Owner type versus easier invariant.** Gaussian ideal arithmetic is
  tractable at a component level, but no cited record licenses weakening one
  literal prime ideal to a relabeling-invariant norm or unordered pair. Cohen
  (1993) <!--ref:P29-S19--><!--anchor:none--> and Belabas (2004)
  <!--ref:P29-S21--><!--anchor:none--> therefore support validation, not owner
  selection.
- **Finite refinement versus structural ownership.** Even a future positive
  `S_H` would be a bounded finite separation result. The present literature
  synthesis supplies no basis for extrapolating it to a global orbit-to-prime-
  ideal correspondence.
