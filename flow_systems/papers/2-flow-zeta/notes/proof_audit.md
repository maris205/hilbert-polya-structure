## 2. \(B_p\) is uncountable: source-compatible proof

### 2.1 Notation check

For the actual group in the source,

\[
\widehat{\mathbb Z}_{(p)}
  =\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell,
\qquad
G_p:=\widehat{\mathbb Z}_{(p)}^\times
  =\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell^\times .
\]

The diagonal element \(p\) is a unit in every factor with \(\ell\ne p\).
The homomorphism \(n\mapsto p^n\) from \(\mathbb Z\) to \(G_p\) therefore
extends continuously to \(\widehat{\mathbb Z}\). Its image is the subgroup

\[
H_p:=p^{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\subset G_p .
\]

Equivalently, \(H_p\) is the closed procyclic subgroup generated by the
diagonal element \(p\). It is compact, hence closed, so

\[
B_p
 =\widehat{\mathbb Z}_{(p)}^\times/p^{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}
 =G_p/H_p
\]

is precisely the compact Hausdorff quotient group used in the source: under
its identifications,
\(\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{\mathbb F}_p^\times)\cong G_p\), while the
restriction of the field-automorphism group
\(\operatorname{Aut}(\overline{\mathbb F}_p)\) has image \(H_p\). No
injectivity of the exponent map is needed for the argument below.

### 2.2 Safe proposition

**Proposition.** For every rational prime \(p\), the group \(B_p\) has
cardinality at least \(2^{\aleph_0}\), hence is uncountable.

**Proof.** Let

\[
I_p=\{\ell:\ell\text{ is an odd prime and }\ell\ne p\}.
\]

This is countably infinite for both \(p=2\) and odd \(p\). Define

\[
S_p
 =\left\{(\varepsilon_\ell)_{\ell\ne p}\in G_p:
   \varepsilon_\ell\in\{\pm1\}\ \text{for }\ell\in I_p,\quad
   \varepsilon_\ell=1\ \text{otherwise}\right\}.
\]

For every odd \(\ell\), the two elements \(1\) and \(-1\) are distinct units
of \(\mathbb Z_\ell\). Consequently

\[
S_p\cong\prod_{\ell\in I_p}C_2,
\qquad
|S_p|=2^{\aleph_0},
\]

and every element of \(S_p\) has order dividing \(2\).

The procyclic profinite group \(H_p\) has at most one nonidentity
involution. Indeed, if it contained two distinct nonidentity involutions,
say \(x\) and \(y\), choose an open normal subgroup \(U\) containing none of
\(x,y,xy\). Then \(H_p/U\) is a finite cyclic group in which the images of
\(x\) and \(y\) are distinct nonidentity involutions, impossible. Hence

\[
|S_p\cap H_p|\le 2.
\]

If \(q:G_p\to B_p\) is the quotient homomorphism, then

\[
\ker(q|_{S_p})=S_p\cap H_p.
\]

It follows that

\[
|q(S_p)|
 =|S_p/(S_p\cap H_p)|
 =2^{\aleph_0}.
\]

Thus \(B_p\) is uncountable. \(\square\)

This verifies the substance of Phase 3, Proposition 1, with three safety
improvements:

1. use “order dividing \(2\)” or “elementary abelian \(2\)-subgroup,” rather
   than suggesting every element has exact order \(2\);
2. identify \(p^{\widehat{\mathbb Z}}\) as the closed procyclic **image** of
   the exponent map; injectivity is irrelevant;
3. define the fixed coordinates explicitly, so the proof covers both \(p=2\)
   and odd \(p\).

Through the source-audited noncanonical set-level parametrization, the set of
primitive period-\(\log p\) orbits in \(\Gamma_p\) is therefore uncountable.
This conclusion uses no topology on \(\Gamma_p/\mathbb R\).

**Classification:** **PROVED.** The proof does **not** assert that
\(\Gamma_p/\mathbb R\cong B_p\) homeomorphically, that the orbit family is
continuous, or that the source's “fibration” is locally trivial. Those claims
remain **OPEN** in the source audit.

## 3. Conventional Ruelle product: exact divergence theorem

Let \(\mathcal P_p\) be the primitive orbits in one packet. The preceding
result makes \(\mathcal P_p\) uncountable, and every
\(\gamma\in\mathcal P_p\) has length \(L_\gamma=\log p\). The conventional
finite-subset net would be

\[
Z_F(s)
 =\prod_{\gamma\in F}(1-e^{-sL_\gamma})^{-1}
 =(1-p^{-s})^{-|F|},
\qquad F\Subset\mathcal P_p .
\]

For real \(\sigma>0\), put
\(a_\sigma=(1-p^{-\sigma})^{-1}>1\). Given \(M>0\), choose \(n\) with
\(a_\sigma^n>M\), and choose \(F_0\) containing \(n\) distinct orbits. Every
\(F\supseteq F_0\) then satisfies

\[
Z_F(\sigma)
 =a_\sigma^{|F|}
 \ge a_\sigma^n
 >M.
\]

Hence the finite-subset net diverges to \(+\infty\) at every real
\(\sigma>0\). Every right half-plane contains such a positive real point, so
these orbit factors cannot define a finite nonzero ordinary Euler product,
much less a normally convergent holomorphic Ruelle product, on any right
half-plane.

For completeness, at a complex point where the factor is defined, set
\(a(s)=(1-p^{-s})^{-1}\). Since \(a(s)\ne1\), convergence of the
finite-subset net to a nonzero limit \(L\) is impossible: adjoining a fresh
orbit would force both \(Z_F\to L\) and \(a(s)Z_F\to L\), hence
\(a(s)L=L\). Depending on \(|a(s)|\), the net can instead be unbounded,
oscillatory, or tend to zero; none gives the required nonzero ordinary Euler
product. At \(p^{-s}=1\), an individual factor is undefined.

**Classification:** the conventional one-factor-per-individual-orbit product
is **A2_FAIL (PROVED)**. The theorem is an obstruction to isolated-orbit
local finiteness. It is not a no-go theorem for a separately defined clean
fixed-set trace, groupoid trace, transverse integral, or packet-level
determinant.

## 4. Normalization ambiguity: what is actually proved

### 4.1 Global rescaling lemma

Let \(A\) be a \(*\)-algebra and suppose a nonzero functional
\(\tau:A\to\mathbb C\) satisfies only homogeneous conditions such as
linearity, traciality, positivity, flow invariance, restriction/locality, and
disjoint-union additivity. Then, for every \(c>0\),

\[
\tau_c=c\tau
\]

satisfies the same conditions. If \(c\ne1\), then \(\tau_c\ne\tau\).
Therefore those conditions cannot establish uniqueness without a
nonhomogeneous normalization.

This lemma does not preserve an axiom such as \(\tau(1)=1\); that is exactly
why the hypotheses must be stated. The source defines neither the relevant
\(A\), its trace domain or unit, nor such a normalization axiom.

### 4.2 Independent component-mass lemma

Conditionally let

\[
A_{\mathrm{alg}}=\bigoplus_p A_p
\]

be the algebraic direct sum, let each \(A_p\) carry a nonzero positive trace
\(\tau_p\), and let the flow preserve the components. For every positive
sequence \(m=(m_p)_p\),

\[
\tau_m((a_p)_p)=\sum_p m_p\tau_p(a_p)
\]

is well defined because each element has finite support. It remains positive,
tracial, additive, and invariant under a component-preserving action. If two
weight sequences differ at an index where \(\tau_p\ne0\), the resulting
traces are distinct. Thus componentwise homogeneous axioms do not determine
the relative prime masses \(m_p\).

On a completed algebra, boundedness, lower semicontinuity, semifiniteness, or
summability may restrict the allowed sequences. Because the frozen source
gives neither such a completion nor a trace domain, arbitrary weights must
not be asserted there unconditionally.

The unique normalized Haar probability on the abstract compact group \(B_p\)
fixes its base mass to one only after that base has been selected. It does not
by itself:

1. transport canonically to the source packet;
2. provide a lift or disintegration on \(\Gamma_p\);
3. identify a fixed-point coefficient;
4. fix a global trace scale or relative masses between primes.

A period-preserving flow equivalence cannot naively identify components whose
least periods are distinct numbers \(\log p\). Stronger arithmetic morphisms
could nevertheless impose cross-prime relations in an enriched category. No
such packet category is source-defined, so “no cross-prime constraints” is an
assumption of the component-mass lemma, not a source theorem.

**Classification:** the two nonuniqueness results are **PROVED
CONDITIONALLY** under their explicit algebra/domain/axiom hypotheses. For the
frozen Deninger object, existence and uniqueness of a packet trace remain
**NOT_TESTABLE**. Stipulating \(m_p=1\) recovers the desired formal
arithmetic factor but does not derive it dynamically; by the protocol this is
\(\mathrm{A2\_FAIL}\), not evidence of a canonical trace.
