## 3. Strongest counter-argument

The strongest case against standalone status remains serious.  Most v4
ingredients are elementary or have close generic precedents.  Each orbit is
the usual torsor `R/H`; a set-indexed topological coproduct is standard;
maps of action groupoids over `B R` are forced by their equivariant unit
maps; component rotations and permutations form the familiar unrestricted
wreath mechanism; and degree-one cohomology of a transitive component is
expected to reduce to isotropy.  Once one knows that a disjoint union has
one slope per component, the statements “the actual indiscrete complex has
one global slope,” “the comparison is diagonal,” and “full symmetric-group
invariants are constant” are short calculations.  Absence of a single
source containing the entire conjunction cannot convert an elementary
assembly into a major new theory.

That objection would control if v4 stopped at its topology or automorphism
formula.  It does not.  The final theorem compares two continuous cochain
complexes on the **same marked packet action and carrier**, with different
continuity structures, and proves exactly which new standardized classes do
and do not descend from the actual topology:

```text
R ~= H_cnv^1(G_actual;R)
  --J^*--> H_cnv^1(G_std;R) ~= R^Q,

image(J^*)=(R^Q)^(Aut_R(G_std))=the constant diagonal.
```

Paper 11 supplies only the left-hand time-factorization mechanism.  It
cannot yield the section-free standard topology, nonzero standardized
coboundaries, the zero-isotropy potential theorem, the full arbitrary-`Q`
automorphism extension, or the intrinsic characterization of the comparison
image.  Those require a different topology and new direct arguments.  The
proof is elementary in tools, but the topology-sensitive comparison is not
a restatement of Paper 11 or of Deninger's stabilizer.  This is the exact
distinction between “routine components” and a nonredundant central theorem.

### 4.2 Section-free topology and categorical equivalence

For each orbit `O` and `x in O`, the orbit map `q_x(t)=x dot t` has fibres
the `H=LZ` cosets.  If `x'=x dot u`, then

```text
q_(x')=q_x o T_u.
```

Because `T_u` is a real homeomorphism, the quotient topology on the same
orbit set is independent of `x`.  The quotient `R/H` is compact Hausdorff.
Taking the nonempty coproduct of these orbit topologies is Hausdorff, has
open orbits, and has jointly continuous action.  Conversely, compact-to-
Hausdorff rigidity forces every competing Hausdorff action-compatible open
orbit to carry the same quotient topology, and the open-orbit condition
forces the coproduct globally.  The uniqueness claim is therefore valid at
the locked cocompact-lattice domain and is not silently generalized to
`H={0}` or another noncocompact subgroup.

Strict preservation of `c=t` fixes every arrow's time coordinate, so a
strict arrow is uniquely

```text
F(x,t)=(F_0(x),t),
F_0(x dot t)=F_0(x) dot t.
```

This proves descent, uniqueness, and faithfulness.  Every equivariant
coproduct homeomorphism has the unique unchanged-time lift, proving
fullness.  `Indisc` replaces the **whole** carrier topology by one global
indiscrete topology; it does not leave a coproduct of componentwise
indiscrete spaces.  On the explicitly nonempty target category, the two
same-set composites are strict identities.  The v3 packet-domain and empty-
target defects are absent.

### 4.3 Automorphisms and choice

Every strict equivariant automorphism permutes the orbit set `Q`.  The
kernel consists canonically of one independent rotation in `R/H` on every
orbit, giving the full product rather than a direct sum:

```text
1 -> (R/H)^Q -> Aut_R(G_std) -> Sym(Q) -> 1.
```

The injection and kernel identification require no orbit section.  Under
the declared ZFC convention, choosing one origin per orbit lifts every
permutation and proves surjectivity.  Fixing such origins gives a split with
the correct inverse-index permutation action, but the splitting and wreath
coordinates are noncanonical.  No topology or continuous splitting is
claimed for the automorphism groups.  These choice and canonicity boundaries
are exact.

### 4.4 Standardized `H^1`, coboundaries, and the full product

For a standardized cocycle, the normalized isotropy slope

```text
rho([b])(q)=b(x,L)/L
```

is independent of the chosen unit: compare the cocycle identity for `u+L`
and `L+u`, using `x dot L=x`.  It is representative-independent because a
coboundary vanishes at isotropy time `L`.

Every algebraic function `lambda:Q->R`, including an unbounded one for
infinite `Q`, gives the continuous cocycle

```text
b_lambda(x,t)=lambda([x])t,
```

because standardized orbit components are open.  This proves surjectivity
onto the full Cartesian product `R^Q`.  If all slopes vanish, a ZFC-selected
origin on each orbit defines

```text
h(x_q dot t)=b_0(x_q,t).
```

Zero isotropy proves well-definedness modulo `LZ`; quotient descent proves
continuity on each orbit; coproduct gluing proves global continuity; and the
frozen sign gives `d^0h=b_0`.  Thus the kernel is exactly the coboundaries.
The sine-potential example supplies a genuinely nonzero standardized
coboundary with zero slope.  Consequently the theorem identifies
cohomology classes, not all cocycles:

```text
H_cnv^1(G_std;R) ~= R^Q,
B_cnv^1(G_std;R) generally != 0,
Z_cnv^1(G_std;R) generally != {b_lambda}.
```

### 4.5 `J`, variance, permutation action, and invariants

The same-set identity has the forced continuous direction from the finer
standard coproduct to the actual indiscrete topology:

```text
J:G_std -> G_actual.
```

Cochains are contravariant, hence

```text
J^*:H_cnv^1(G_actual;R)->H_cnv^1(G_std;R).
```

The stable actual theorem supplies the global line `R[c]` and zero actual
coboundaries.  Its pullback has slope `lambda` on every component, so the
image is the injective constant diagonal because `Q` is nonempty.

If `phi` has orbit permutation `sigma_phi`, raw pullback has slope

```text
rho(phi^*[b])=lambda o sigma_phi,
```

whereas the declared left action
`phi dot[b]=(phi^(-1))^*[b]` has slope
`lambda o sigma_phi^(-1)`.  The proof does not conflate these two formulas.
Kernel rotations act trivially, and every permutation lifts in ZFC, so the
invariant slopes are exactly the constant functions.  Scaled, unmarked,
orientation-reversing, and arbitrary abstract groupoid automorphisms are
correctly outside this statement.

No mathematical counterexample was found at the exact nonempty common-
cocompact-lattice domain.
