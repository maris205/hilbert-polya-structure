## 3. Strongest counter-argument

Most ingredients remain standard when isolated. A common-stabilizer orbit is
the usual homogeneous torsor `R/H`; open coproducts are elementary; the
automorphism group is the ordinary unrestricted wreath extension; and the
standardized `H^1` calculation is the componentwise transitive calculation.
The actual cohomology line is already the v2 consequence of global
indiscreteness. Thus v4 cannot earn standalone status merely by conjoining
quotient, coproduct, wreath, and bar-complex facts.

V4 nevertheless adds a materially different candidate centre. It compares
two continuous cochain complexes on the **same packet action with different
topologies**, obtains the enlargement

```text
R  --J^*-->  R^Q,
```

and characterizes its image intrinsically as the invariant subspace under all
strict equivariant automorphisms. This addresses both failures of v3: the
nontransitive packet is now in the common-`H` domain, and a new comparison
Route owner records the same-set packet construction while the old circle
owner remains only a one-orbit proxy.

That makes v4 a genuine **candidate** to close the prior standalone Major,
not a proof that it has closed. The current locks correctly require a new
targeted source/precedent audit, a direct proof, updated controls, and an
independent standalone adjudication. If those later lanes reduce the

### 4.2 Orbit topology, basepoint independence, and uniqueness

For an orbit `O` and `x in O`, the map

```text
q_x:R -> O,  q_x(t)=x dot t
```

is surjective by the definition of `O`, has fibre relation modulo
`H=LZ`, and has closed kernel because `LZ` is closed in `R`. If
`x'=x dot u`, then `q_(x')=q_x o T_u`; translation is a homeomorphism, so
the quotient topology is independent of `x`.

No design-level counterexample was found to the uniqueness clause. For any
competing Hausdorff topology with continuous action, the induced continuous
bijection `R/H -> O` is a homeomorphism because `R/H` is compact and `O` is
Hausdorff. Requiring all orbits to be open then forces the coproduct topology.
The compactness step correctly blocks an unauthorized extension to arbitrary
noncocompact stabilizers.

The resulting coproduct is Hausdorff, and the action is jointly continuous
componentwise. The identity direction is correctly

```text
Std_coprod(X) -> X_actual,
```

from the finer topology to the global indiscrete topology. Its reverse is
noncontinuous on these nontrivial lattice objects. Nothing here is the
Kolmogorov, Hausdorff, or completely regular reflection of `X_actual`: those
reflections collapse an indiscrete nonempty space to a point, whereas
standardization retains the action set and retopologizes each orbit.

### 4.3 Strict descent, unique lift, and inverse construction

The marked condition fixes the time coordinate. Range and source then force

```text
F(x,t)=(F_0(x),t),
F_0(x dot t)=F_0(x) dot t.
```

Thus a strict groupoid isomorphism descends to an equivariant orbit-bijection,
and quotient continuity on every open component makes it a standardized
homeomorphism. Conversely an equivariant coproduct homeomorphism has the
unique lift `(x,t) |-> (F_0(x),t)`; after global indiscretization it is an
arrow homeomorphism with the frozen range-first operations and mark.

On nonempty objects, the same-set convention makes the two composites strict
identities. If abstract representatives are used instead, the identity-set
maps give the required natural isomorphisms. There is no remaining
concrete-versus-up-to-natural-isomorphism ambiguity **apart from the empty
target-object omission recorded as `m1` below**.

### 4.4 Automorphism exact sequence, choice, and splitting

The map to `Sym(Q)` is canonical, as is its kernel: a function
`a:Q->R/H` rotates each orbit by `a(q)`. Hence the section-free statement is
the abstract-group extension

```text
1 -> Map(Q,R/H) -> Aut_R(Std_coprod(X)) -> Sym(Q) -> 1.
```

No topology on these groups is claimed. In ZFC, choosing one origin in every
orbit supplies lifts of all component permutations and a group-theoretic
splitting. The resulting semidirect product is noncanonical, and the locked
action `(sigma dot a)(q)=a(sigma^(-1)q)` is compatible with coordinates
indexed at the target component. The transitive case is recovered at
`Q={q}`: `Sym(Q)=1` and the automorphism group is `R/H`.

No canonical splitting, no continuous splitting, and no topological-group
classification is licensed.

### 4.5 Standardized `H^1`, coboundaries, and choice

The proposed slope map is correctly typed for the frozen unnormalized
continuous complex:

```text
rho([b])(q)=b(x,L)/L,  x in q.
```

The cocycle identity at commuting `u` and `L`, together with `x dot L=x`, is
the required basepoint-independence check. A degree-zero coboundary vanishes
on isotropy, so the slope is representative-independent.

The two directions of the proposed classification have the right continuity
requirements:

- for any algebraic function `lambda:Q->R`, the cocycle
  `b_lambda(x,t)=lambda([x])t` is continuous because `Q` is only the discrete
  component index of the constructed coproduct; and
- after subtracting these slopes, a choice of `x_q` defines
  `h(x_q dot t)=b_0(x_q,t)`. Zero isotropy makes this well-defined, the
  quotient topology gives continuity on each orbit, and the coproduct gives
  global continuity. With the frozen sign
  `d h(x,t)=h(x dot t)-h(x)`, the potential has the required direction.

This supports the algebraic product `R^Q`, not a direct sum and not a space of
continuous functions on actual `Q_p`. The amendment correctly requires the
proof to exhibit generally nonzero `B_cnv^1(G_std;R)` and to avoid the false
claim that all cocycles are literally the time-only representatives
`b_lambda`. No higher degree, cohomology topology, or Morita statement is
smuggled into the target.

### 4.6 `J`, the diagonal, and automorphism invariants

The continuous functor is the identity from finer to coarser topology:

```text
J:G_std -> G_actual.
```

Cohomology therefore pulls back in the opposite direction:

```text
J^*:H_cnv^1(G_actual;R) -> H_cnv^1(G_std;R).
```

The stable actual theorem gives the line `R[c]` and zero actual
coboundaries. Restriction to every standardized orbit sends `lambda[c]` to
the constant slope `lambda`, so the image is the diagonal. Strict component
rotations act trivially on slopes and component permutations permute the
coordinates. Because `Q` is nonempty and the full symmetric group is reached
in ZFC, the invariant functions are exactly the constants.

The locks consistently exclude scaled, unmarked, or arbitrary abstract
groupoid automorphisms from this invariant theorem. No pullback-direction or
automorphism-domain mismatch was found.

### m1 — the target coproduct category does not explicitly exclude the empty coproduct

**Severity:** Minor  
**Confidence:** 5/5 — direct comparison of both object classes and the claimed
inverse  
**Evidence Anchor:** `text: notes/phase3_standalone_amendment_v4.md:81-89 — "the category of topological coproducts of standard right R/H torsors ... The inverse Indisc ..."`

`C_common(H)` explicitly requires `X` to be nonempty. By the ordinary
categorical convention, however, “topological coproducts of standard torsors”
can include the coproduct of the empty family. The empty coproduct is then an
object of `Tor_R^coprod(H)`, but `Indisc(empty)` is not an object of
`C_common(H)`. Consequently the stated inverse/equivalence is not literally
typed on all target objects.

The surrounding intent is clear: `research_protocol.md:528` says the target
has “precisely those coproducts,” referring to the standardizations of the
preceding nonempty source objects, and every packet application has nonempty
`Q`. That makes this a bounded domain omission rather than a Major change to
the construction. Exact-byte category locks should not depend on excluding an
empty coproduct by implication, however.

**Mandatory repair:** define `Tor_R^coprod(H)` to have **nonempty**
topological coproducts of standard right `R/H` torsors, equivalently require
its component set `Q` to be nonempty. Repeat that guard in the active protocol
and candidate lock wherever the inverse/equivalence is summarized. Then the
same-set strict inverse (or displayed natural equivalence for abstract
representatives) is fully typed.
