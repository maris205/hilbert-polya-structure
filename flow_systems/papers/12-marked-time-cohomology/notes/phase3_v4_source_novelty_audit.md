## 2. Direct-precedent rule at the v4 strength

A record counts as a **direct v4 package precedent** only if one source, or
one explicitly connected theorem package, supplies every item below at the
audited strength:

1. **P1 — same actual owner:** a nonempty, globally indiscrete,
   non-necessarily-transitive right action of the usual topological group
   `R`, with every unit having the same lattice stabilizer `H=LZ`, `L>0`, and
   strict coordinate-time mark `c(x,t)=t`;
2. **P2 — section-free same-carrier standardization:** the basepoint-
   independent quotient topology on each orbit, followed by the topological
   coproduct over the bare set `Q=X/R`, together with the compact-Hausdorff
   uniqueness statement among Hausdorff action-compatible topologies having
   open orbits;
3. **P3 — exact categorical lift/descent:** full faithfulness and the inverse
   global-indiscretization construction for strict maps over `B R`, rather
   than a set-level orbit decomposition alone;
4. **P4 — exact automorphism boundary:** the canonical extension
   `1 -> (R/H)^Q -> Aut_R -> Sym(Q) -> 1`, including the distinction between
   that section-free extension and a choice-dependent noncanonical
   unrestricted wreath split for arbitrary `Q`;
5. **P5 — exact cohomology convention:** the Paper-12 globally continuous,
   unnormalized nerve complex with trivial real coefficients, including
   nonzero standardized coboundaries, and the calculation
   `H_cnv^1(G_std;R) ~= R^Q`;
6. **P6 — topology-change comparison:** the identity functor
   `J:G_std->G_actual`, the contravariant constant-diagonal `J^*`, and the
   equality of its image with invariants under all strict time-preserving
   equivariant automorphisms; and
7. **P7 — fixed-prime typing:** application to the Deninger/Paper-9 packet
   with `Q_p` used only as a bare orbit set and no topology transported from
   the actual quotient.

The test is conjunctive: `P1 AND ... AND P7`.  Standard sources for a
topological coproduct, `G/H`, action groupoids over `BG`, wreath products, or
vertex-group cohomology remain nearest components even when their conclusions
can be assembled.  Conversely, failure to locate the full conjunction is not
by itself a standalone-strength or priority result.

## 4. Included primary/authoritative sources and exact ceilings

### S1 — topological coproduct

The Stacks Project, [Section 5.29, Tag
`0B1W`](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0B1W), states that an arbitrary
set-indexed disjoint union of spaces receives the coproduct topology, with
component-open sets as a basis, and records the colimit property in Lemma
5.29.1.

**Permitted use:** this is authoritative support for the topology placed on
the disjoint union of orbit components and for componentwise continuity.

**Ceiling:** it contains no group action, common stabilizer, same-carrier
indiscrete topology, cohomology, automorphism extension, or fixed-prime
packet.  It does not prove the v4 uniqueness theorem; that proof still needs
the locked compact-to-Hausdorff argument on each orbit.

### S2 — standard homogeneous quotient

The Encyclopedia of Mathematics entries [*Topological
group*](https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Topological_group) and
[*Homogeneous
space*](https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Homogeneous_space) give the
authoritative generic facts used here: `G/H` carries the quotient topology,
is Hausdorff when `H` is closed, and represents a transitive orbit with
stabilizer `H`.  They explicitly include `R/Z` as the circle quotient.

For `H=LZ`, `L>0`, compactness also follows directly because the quotient is
the continuous image of the compact interval `[0,L]`.  Thus, if a Hausdorff
topology on the same orbit makes the orbit map continuous, the induced
continuous bijection `R/H->O` is a homeomorphism.  The active lock correctly
restricts the uniqueness assertion to the cocompact lattice case; it does
not generalize it to `H={0}`.

**Ceiling:** quotient topology, Hausdorffness, and compactness are standard
background.  These sources do not compare an actual indiscrete carrier with
a finer same-set coproduct or state the cohomological diagonal.

### S3 — action groupoids over `B G`

David Gepner and Lennart Meier, [*On equivariant topological modular
forms*](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X23007509), *Compositio
Mathematica* **159** (2023), 2638--2693, DOI
`10.1112/S0010437X23007509`, Proposition 2.15 at printed p. 2647, is the
mandatory nearest formal precedent.

Proposition 2.15 says that the action-groupoid functor for a topological group
`G` is fully faithful after passing to topological groupoids over the
one-object groupoid `B G`.  Its proof identifies a map over `B G` with the
equivariant object map and forces the arrow map to have identity `G`
coordinate.  In the Paper-12 convention, preserving `c(x,t)=t` is precisely
the relevant over-`B R` condition.

**Exact-domain stop:** the source convention at printed p. 2643 takes `Top`
to be compactly generated weak-Hausdorff spaces.  A nontrivial globally
indiscrete Paper-12 unit is not weak Hausdorff.  The proposition therefore
supplies the nearest formal mechanism and mandatory citation boundary, not
an imported theorem on the actual owner.  It does not construct the
orbitwise topology, global `Indisc`, automorphism extension, cohomology, or
`J` comparison.  The active v4 design correctly requires a direct proof in
its exact category.

### S4 — orbit decomposition and wreath automorphisms for finite `G`-sets

Bertrand J. Guillou and J. Peter May, [*Equivariant iterated loop space
theory and permutative G-categories*](https://doi.org/10.2140/agt.2017.17.3259),
*Algebraic & Geometric Topology* **17** (2017), 3259--3339, DOI
`10.2140/agt.2017.17.3259`, Proposition 5.19 at printed p. 3311, decompose a
finite `G`-set by orbit type.  For `k` copies of `G/H`, the equivariant
automorphism group is the wreath product of the symmetric group with
`W_H=N_G(H)/H`.  The following paragraph explicitly uses chosen
isomorphisms when passing to a skeleton.

**Exact-domain stop:** the paper fixes a finite group `G`, finite `G`-sets,
and finite `k`.  It does not cover the topological group `R`, arbitrary
possibly infinite `Q`, the unrestricted product `(R/H)^Q`, or the actual
indiscrete topology.  For abelian `R`, its generic orbit formula motivates
the component `R/H`, but the v4 extension, its choice accounting, and all
topological/cohomological assertions require direct proof.

### S5 — wreath automorphisms of finitely many groupoid components

Murat Alp and Christopher D. Wensley, [*Automorphisms and homotopies of
groupoids and crossed modules*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10485-008-9183-y),
*Applied Categorical Structures* **18** (2010), 473--504, DOI
`10.1007/s10485-008-9183-y`, Section 3.1, published p. 481, state that the
automorphism group of a disjoint union of `m` isomorphic connected groupoids
is the corresponding wreath product.  Their article begins with finite
groupoids and the displayed result has finite `m`.

**Exact-domain stop:** this is a strict algebraic-groupoid automorphism
result, not a theorem about arbitrary set-indexed topological coproducts or
automorphisms constrained over `B R`.  It supports the strong routine ceiling
for the permutation-plus-component-automorphism mechanism.  It does not
make the v4 split canonical and does not supply any cohomological invariant
statement.

### S6 — transitive vertex-group cohomology comparator

K. A. Mackenzie, [*Rigid cohomology of topological
groupoids*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1446788700011794), *Journal of the
Australian Mathematical Society* (Series A) **26** (1978), 277--301, DOI
`10.1017/S1446788700011794`, is the mandatory cohomological comparator.
Definition 1.3 at printed p. 279 builds transitivity and Hausdorffness into
its topological-groupoid convention; the subsequent theory assumes local
triviality, local compactness, a paracompact connected manifold base, and
continuous locally convex vector-bundle modules.  Theorem 3 at printed
pp. 298--299 identifies Mackenzie's derived rigid cohomology with the
continuous cohomology of a vertex group.

**Exact-domain stop:** Mackenzie's rigid cohomology is not the Paper-12
author-defined globally continuous unnormalized nerve complex.  His theorem
does not cover a nontransitive coproduct as one groupoid, the actual
non-Hausdorff owner, arbitrary functions on `Q`, or the map `J`.  It is a
nearest precedent for the general expectation that a transitive component's
cohomology is controlled by isotropy.  V4 therefore must prove its exact
degree-one result directly and may not invoke Mackenzie as an equality of
theories.

### S7 — fixed-prime packet inputs

Christopher Deninger, *Dynamical systems for arithmetic schemes*, arXiv
[`1807.06400v4`](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06400v4), physical/author
pp. 38--39, Section 6 and Theorem 6.1, supplies the fixed-prime packet action,
the common multiplicative stabilizer `p^Z`, and the additive logarithmic-time
conversion to `(log p)Z`.  The retained manifestation and exact page map are
already bound in `notes/sources/coh-source-manifest.md`.

Paper 9, not Deninger, supplies the actual globally indiscrete packet
topology and the orbit quotient identified with `Q_p`; Paper 11 supplies the
Paper-12 range-first action-groupoid predecessor.  The v4 lock keeps these
owners separate and uses only the underlying orbit set in the constructed
coproduct.

**Ceiling:** the source does not define the Paper-12 groupoid, either
topology comparison, the author complex, `R^Q`, or the invariant diagonal.
The packet application remains conditional on the already frozen every-unit
source gate and may not become a full-suspension or cross-prime theorem.

## 6. Source-side substantive-increment and proof-feasibility gate

The generic v4 components have a strong routine ceiling:

1. the quotient topology and Hausdorff circle `R/LZ` are standard;
2. a set-indexed topological coproduct is standard;
3. strict maps over `B R` use the exact formal mechanism isolated by
   Gepner--Meier Proposition 2.15;
4. orbit permutations plus component rotations are the ordinary wreath
   mechanism exhibited by Guillou--May and Alp--Wensley; and
5. reduction of a transitive cohomology theory to isotropy has Mackenzie as a
   close, although non-importable, comparator.

Thus topology plus an automorphism formula would still reproduce the v3
routine-reduction problem.  The active v4 bytes do not stop there.  They make
the proof-eligible centre the exact same-carrier comparison

```text
H_cnv^1(G_actual;R) ~= R
       --J^*--> H_cnv^1(G_std;R) ~= R^Q,

rho(J^*(lambda[c]))=(q |-> lambda),
image(J^*)=(R^Q)^(Aut_R(G_std)).
```

This conjunction compares two continuity regimes on the same marked
underlying action groupoid.  It also requires the full strict automorphism
group, including all component permutations, to characterize intrinsically
which independent standardized orbit slopes descend from the actual global
indiscrete topology.  None of the nearest component sources states that
comparison, and the active design includes direct proof obligations for
surjectivity, zero-isotropy potentials, nonzero standardized coboundaries,
the contravariant direction of `J^*`, and strict-automorphism invariants.

That is enough to clear the **source-feasibility/proof-eligibility** gate:
the exact design is not relying on a concealed direct theorem; all strong
generic precedents are named and delimited; and the component facts do not
already supply the full locked comparison.  The v3 Major is not
retrospectively erased: its conclusion remains correct for the v3
topology/category/automorphism package.  V4 supplies the additional
cohomological comparison that v3 lacked.

This audit deliberately does **not** grant `STANDALONE_PASS`.  Whether the
direct proof and controls deliver sufficient mathematical substance, rather
than only a short formal synthesis, is a post-proof judgment for the
independent standalone reviewer required by the active amendment.  If the
diagonal/invariant theorem fails, becomes vacuous, or collapses under proof
to a repackaging judged insufficient, the locked `NOTE_OR_MERGE` rule still
applies.  Reserving that later judgment is a gate boundary, not an open
source finding in the present exact design.

- claiming that endpoint unavailability is a zero result;
- importing Gepner--Meier to the non-weak-Hausdorff actual owner;
- importing Guillou--May or Alp--Wensley to arbitrary `Q` without a direct
  proof and explicit choice accounting;
- identifying Mackenzie's rigid cohomology with `H_cnv`;
- calling the wreath splitting canonical, or replacing `(R/H)^Q` by a direct
  sum;
- using all abstract, scaled, or unmarked groupoid automorphisms in the
  invariant theorem;
- placing a topology on `R^Q` or on cohomology;
- generalizing the uniqueness theorem to noncocompact `H`, especially
  `H={0}`;
