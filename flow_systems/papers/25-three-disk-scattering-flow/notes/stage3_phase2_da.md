## Review Body

**Strongest counter-argument.** The manuscript proves a clean but deliberately narrow impossibility: one constant substitution cannot preserve the physical periods of every symbolic owner. A skeptical reader can accept that proof and still dispute the paper's claimed increment. The unit-roof determinant follows by diagonalizing the standard no-repeat adjacency matrix and applying the classical finite-type determinant identity; the physical obstruction applies the elementary necessary periodic-sum test to two symmetric lengths. The manuscript does not establish whether this particular three-disk application, as opposed to its careful object typing, is new relative to the specialist literature. On that record, the defensible contribution may be a rigorous negative-control synthesis and reusable exposition rather than two independently novel theorems. The 2,241-row replay does not resolve that objection. It verifies the implementation and shows finite-cutoff prevalence, but the exact two-owner argument already settles the stated scalar-transfer question throughout the admissible equilateral family. A binary match count after calibrating on period two supplies neither a second proof nor a quantitative error profile for the remaining owners. Unless the paper differentiates its theorem from prior roof-cohomology practice and states a distinct empirical estimand for the replay, the positive theorem and large ledger risk adding apparent scale without increasing the central inference.

The scope-pressure tests do not support a stronger objection. The operative theorem is consistently restricted to an owner- and repetition-preserving scalar substitution, while the limitations explicitly leave a genuine nonconstant-roof transfer operator open. The half-density result is repeatedly typed as a local return-map identity and is not promoted to a global determinant, convergence result, or spectral correspondence. Accordingly, the symbolic tuple remains `(A0_FAIL,A1_PASS_ANALYTIC,A2_ANALYTIC_DETERMINANT,A3_FAIL,A4_FAIL)` with `ROUTE_A_REJECTED`; the physical tuple remains unassigned; no symbolic A1/A2 credit transfers to the physical flow; and Route B is not invoked.

The D3 warning reflects contribution-level inference gaps rather than a break in the scalar-clock proof. Both identified weaknesses can be repaired by sharper novelty positioning, a focused comparison with prior art, and either a separately motivated replay quantity or a clear demotion of the ledger to implementation verification.
