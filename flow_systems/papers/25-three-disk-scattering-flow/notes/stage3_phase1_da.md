## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor): The adversarial test is whether the study design, treatment of data, reporting of quantitative results, and reproducibility provisions can withstand scrutiny at the field's peer-review standard; weaknesses here threaten confidence in the evidentiary foundation.

D2 (domain_accuracy): The adversarial test is whether every substantive claim, account of prior work, technical term, and domain-specific result is factually faithful to the current evidence, with no misstatement capable of distorting the paper's conclusions.

D3 (argumentative_coherence): The adversarial test is whether the central thesis follows consistently from the stated premises and evidence, with hidden assumptions, logical leaps, contradictions, and stronger rival explanations exposed before they can undermine the core argument.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance): The adversarial test is whether readers in adjacent fields receive enough framing and definition to evaluate the work, and whether any interdisciplinary implication is supported rather than asserted beyond the demonstrated scope.

D5 (writing_and_structure): The adversarial test is whether organization, exposition, and presentation of figures or tables permit the argument to be audited without avoidable ambiguity, and whether the manuscript satisfies the structural conventions expected of a reviewable submission.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution): The adversarial test is whether the work's claimed originality and significance would constitute a meaningful contribution for the configured readership, rather than relying on an overstated novelty claim or a mismatch between contribution and venue expectations.

## Scoring Plan

### D3: argumentative_coherence
dimension_id: D3
what_to_look_for: Trace the central thesis from premises through evidence to conclusions, testing each inference for consistency, necessary assumptions, scope control, and stronger counter-explanations.
what_triggers_block: A material break in the central reasoning leaves a core conclusion unsupported, although substantial revision could restore a defensible thesis.
what_triggers_warn: A localized ambiguity, unstated assumption, or incompletely addressed alternative weakens an inference without defeating the central argument.
what_triggers_fatal: The core conclusion contradicts the stated premises or evidence, or depends on a demonstrably false indispensable premise, so revision cannot preserve the thesis.
