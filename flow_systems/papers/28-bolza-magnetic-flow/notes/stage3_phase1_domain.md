## Contract Paraphrase

D1 (methodology_rigor): The methodology seat must determine whether the study design, treatment of data, statistical presentation, and provisions for reproducibility satisfy the relevant field-level peer-review standard; this dimension is mandatory and is outside the domain seat's scoring remit.

D2 (domain_accuracy): The domain seat must assess whether substantive claims are consistent with current evidence in the field, earlier scholarship is characterized accurately, and domain-specific terms and reported results contain no factual mistakes; this dimension is mandatory and belongs solely to the domain seat.

D3 (argumentative_coherence): The methodology and devil's-advocate seats must judge whether the central thesis remains internally consistent, its evidence adequately supports its claims, and no fallacy defeats the main argument; this dimension is mandatory and is not scored by the domain seat.

D4 (cross_disciplinary_relevance): The perspective seat must examine whether readers from neighboring fields can follow the framing, definitions, and implications, and whether interdisciplinary assertions have adequate support; this is a high-priority dimension outside the domain seat's scoring remit.

D5 (writing_and_structure): The Journal-Fit seat must evaluate the manuscript's organization, expository clarity, figures and tables, and compliance with venue conventions; this normal-priority dimension is outside the domain seat's scoring remit.

D6 (venue_fit_and_contribution): The Journal-Fit seat must assess whether the manuscript suits the configured venue and offers an original, significant contribution appropriate for that venue's readership; this dimension is mandatory and is not scored by the domain seat.

## Scoring Plan

### D2: domain_accuracy
dimension_id: D2
what_to_look_for: Check substantive claims against current domain evidence, verify that prior work is represented accurately, and examine field-specific terminology and reported results for factual correctness.
what_triggers_block: A substantial but repairable domain error, material misrepresentation of prior work, or misuse of field-specific terminology or results that undermines an important claim.
what_triggers_warn: A localized domain imprecision, incomplete qualification of prior evidence, or minor terminology or result-description error that does not undermine an important claim.
what_triggers_fatal: A domain-specific factual contradiction or wholesale distortion of decisive prior evidence that, by itself, invalidates the central claim and makes acceptance impossible.
