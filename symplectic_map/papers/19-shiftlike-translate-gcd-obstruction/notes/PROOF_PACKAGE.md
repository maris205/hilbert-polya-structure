# Proof Package

## 0. Purpose, status, and dependency declaration

This document proves the complete corrected theorem package for Paper 19.
The status is

`PROVABLE AS STATED`.

The only indispensable external proof theorem is `EXT-L`, Laurent's
qualitative Mordell--Lang theorem for algebraic tori. Every recurrence-
specific character, lattice, translate, root-scheme, gcd, component-height,

## 1. Exact setup

Let `Omega` be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Fix

`k>=2`, `1<=nu<=k-1`, `a in Omega*`, and `P in Omega[X]`.

Define

`S(z_1,...,z_k)=(z_2,...,z_k,P(z_(k-nu+1))+a z_1)`.

The inverse is

`S^(-1)(w_1,...,w_k)`

` =(a^(-1)(w_k-P(w_(k-nu))),w_1,...,w_(k-1))`,

so `S` is a polynomial automorphism. In zero-based scalar coordinates its
recurrence is

`x_(n+k)=P(x_(n+k-nu))+a x_n`.                                    (1.1)

For `m>=0`, let `V_m subset (G_m)^(k+m)` be cut out by (1.1) for
`0<=n<m`. If `K` is a characteristic-zero field over which the data are
defined and `Gamma<=K*`, put

`T_m(S,Gamma)={z in Gamma^k:S^j(z) in Gamma^k for 0<=j<=m}`.        (1.2)

Every initial tuple determines all future coordinates uniquely. Projection
therefore gives a bijection

`V_m intersect Gamma^(k+m) -> T_m(S,Gamma)`.                       (1.3)

The symbol `m` counts equations or transitions.

## 2. Character and arithmetic preliminaries

### Lemma 2.1: group-algebra independence

Let `H` be a connected algebraic torus and `M=X*(H)`. Distinct characters
form an `Omega`-basis of the group algebra `Omega[M]`. Hence, in a vanishing
linear combination of characters, every character class has total
coefficient zero. In particular, no nonzero singleton term can occur.

On a translate `xi H`, every ambient coordinate restricts as

`x_i=xi_i chi^(u_i)`, `xi_i in Omega*`, `u_i in M`.                 (2.1)

The restriction map

`Z^N=X*((G_m)^N) -> X*(H)=M`                                      (2.2)

is surjective. Thus the ambient coordinate characters generate the actual
character lattice `M`. This final surjectivity, not merely torsion-freeness of
an abstract over-lattice, is used to determine `dim H` in Part B.

### Lemma 2.2: saturated relation lattices

A connected subtorus is determined by the saturated kernel of (2.2). If a
saturated lattice `L subset Z^N` is contained in that kernel and both have
the same rank, then they are equal.

Indeed, saturation gives `L=(L tensor Q) intersect Z^N`. An equal-rank
over-lattice inside `Z^N` lies in the same rational span and therefore equals
`L`.

### EXT-L and the finite-rank bridge

We use the following qualitative consequence of Laurent's torus theorem.

> If a closed subvariety of a complex algebraic torus contains no
> positive-dimensional torus translate, then its intersection with the
> division group of any finitely generated subgroup is finite.

Let `Gamma` have finite rank, with arbitrary torsion. Choose finitely many
elements whose classes form a `Q`-basis of `Gamma tensor_Z Q`, and let
`Gamma_0` be the subgroup they generate. For every `gamma in Gamma`, clearing
the rational relation and then killing the resulting torsion element gives a
positive power of `gamma` in `Gamma_0`. Hence

`Gamma subset Gamma_0^div`.                                       (2.3)

For an arbitrary characteristic-zero field, use the finitely generated field
generated by the coefficients and the generators of `Gamma_0`. It embeds in
`C`; the power relations place all points under consideration in an
algebraic closure to which the embedding extends. This proves the required
arbitrary-field corollary without embedding the entire ground field.

No effective bound is imported.

## 3. Part A setup and the predecessor lattice

Assume through Section 8 that

`P(X)=c+sum_(ell=1)^s b_ell X^(e_ell)`,                            (3.1)

where

`0<e_1<...<e_s`, `a,c,b_ell in Omega*`, and `s>=2`.

Fix `1<=m<k`, and suppose `xi H subset V_m` for a connected subtorus `H`.
Write the restricted characters additively as `u_i in M=X*(H)`.

For the equation with index `n`, put `t=k+n-nu`. Restriction of (1.1) gives

`xi_(n+k)[u_(n+k)]-a xi_n[u_n]-c[0]`

` -sum_ell b_ell xi_t^(e_ell)[e_ell u_t]=0` in `Omega[M]`.         (3.2)

If `u_t!=0`, the `s+1>=3` characters

`0,e_1u_t,...,e_su_t`

are distinct, because `M` is torsion-free. The two endpoint terms can cover
at most two of them, leaving a singleton. Hence

`u_(k+n-nu)=0`.                                                    (3.3)

After collecting the polynomial terms, (3.2) becomes

`xi_(n+k)[u_(n+k)]-a xi_n[u_n]-P(xi_t)[0]=0`.                      (3.4)

If `P(xi_t)!=0`, character independence forces `u_n=u_(n+k)=0`. If
`P(xi_t)=0`, it forces

`u_(n+k)=u_n` and `xi_(n+k)=a xi_n`.                               (3.5)

Both cases yield the relation vectors

`A_n=epsilon_(k+n-nu)`,

`B_n=epsilon_(k+n)-epsilon_n`, `0<=n<m`.                           (3.6)

Let `L_m` be their span. Define

`R_m={n-nu mod k:0<=n<m}`.                                        (3.7)

Modulo the `B_n`, every future coordinate is its corresponding initial
coordinate. The `A_n` then kill exactly the `m` distinct initial coordinates
in `R_m`. Therefore

`Z^(k+m)/L_m` is freely generated by the initial coordinates outside `R_m`.
                                                                        (3.8)

In particular, `L_m` is saturated of rank `2m`, and

`dim H<=k+m-2m=k-m`.                                               (3.9)

Equation (3.9) is the Paper 17 dimension theorem `BG17`.

## 4. TA1: rigidity of the maximum-dimensional subgroup

Assume now `dim H=k-m`. The kernel of the ambient character map has rank

`k+m-(k-m)=2m`.

It contains the saturated rank-`2m` lattice `L_m`; Lemma 2.2 gives

`ker(Z^(k+m)->M)=L_m`.                                             (4.1)

Let `H_m` be defined by

`x_r=1` for `r in R_m`,

`x_(k+n)=x_n` for `0<=n<m`.                                       (4.2)

Its character kernel is exactly `L_m`, by (3.8). Hence (4.1) implies

`H=H_m`.                                                          (4.3)

This proves `TA1`. It classifies only the subgroup underlying a translate of
the maximum possible dimension. It makes no assertion about lower-dimensional
or inclusion-maximal translates.

## 5. TA2: normalized scalar classification

### 5.1 Unique normalization

The free initial coordinates of `H_m` are the indices outside `R_m`. Given a
coset `xi H_m`, multiply `xi` by the unique element of `H_m` whose free
initial coordinates are the inverses of those of `xi`. The resulting
representative satisfies

`xi_i=1` for every initial `i notin R_m`.                           (5.1)

No nontrivial element of `H_m` preserves all values in (5.1), so the
representative is unique.

Define middle scalars

`y_n=xi_(k+n-nu)`, `0<=n<m`.                                      (5.2)

### 5.2 Active indices

The endpoint character in equation `n` is nontrivial precisely when the
initial index `n` is outside `R_m`. Thus the active indices are

`A_m={n in [0,m-1]:n notin R_m}`.                                 (5.3)

For `0<=n<m`, membership `n in R_m` is equivalent to the existence of
`j in [0,m-1]` with `j=n+nu` or `j=n+nu-k`. Taking complements gives

`A_m=[max(0,m-nu),min(m,k-nu))`.                                  (5.4)

The length of this intersection is

`alpha_m=min(m,k-m,nu,k-nu)`.                                     (5.5)

One may verify (5.5) by the four relative positions of the interval
endpoints, or by the identity

`min(m,k-nu)-max(0,m-nu)=min(m,k-m,nu,k-nu)`

whenever the left interval is nonempty; for `1<=m<k` it always is.

At an active index, (3.4) has nontrivial equal endpoint character and hence
requires

`P(y_n)=0`.                                                        (5.6)

At an inactive index, all three characters are zero and only the scalar
recurrence remains.

### 5.3 Reconstruction from the `y` variables

For `n<nu`, the middle coordinate `k+n-nu` is an initial killed coordinate,
so set

`xi_(k+n-nu)=y_n`.                                                 (5.7)

For `nu<=n<m`, the coordinate in (5.2) is the previous future coordinate
`xi_(k+n-nu)`. Solving the earlier recurrence gives the killed initial scalar

`xi_(n-nu)=a^(-1)(y_n-P(y_(n-nu)))`.                               (5.8)

Set the free initial scalars by (5.1), and then define every output by

`xi_(k+j)=P(y_j)+a xi_j`, `0<=j<m`.                                (5.9)

For `nu<=n<m`, substituting (5.8) into (5.9) with `j=n-nu` gives
`xi_(k+n-nu)=y_n`, so there is no consistency gap.

All initial scalars in (5.8) are nonzero exactly when

`y_n!=P(y_(n-nu))`, `nu<=n<m`.                                    (5.10)

An output with `j+nu<m` is already the nonzero variable `y_(j+nu)`. If
`j+nu>=m` and `j<k-nu`, then `j` is active, so (5.6) and (5.1) make the
output equal to `a`. The only remaining outputs have `k-nu<=j<m`; there
`j in R_m`, with `xi_j=y_(j+nu-k)`, and nonvanishing is exactly

`P(y_j)+a y_(j+nu-k)!=0`.                                         (5.11)

### 5.4 Necessity, sufficiency, and uniqueness

Define `E_m subset (G_m)^m` by equations (5.6) for `n in A_m` and open
conditions (5.10)--(5.11). Every normalized maximum-dimensional translate
gives a point of `E_m` by (5.2).

Conversely, a point of `E_m` reconstructs nonzero scalars through
(5.7)--(5.9). If `n` is active, (5.6) makes equation `n` hold as an identity
on `H_m`, with copied endpoint characters. If `n` is inactive, (5.9) is the
scalar recurrence and all characters are trivial. Thus the reconstructed
translate lies in `V_m`.

The normalization and (5.2) recover the original point, proving a bijection
on geometric points and the explicit locally closed scheme description. This
proves `TA2`.

## 6. TA3: nonemptiness and fixed-fiber geometry

Let `delta=deg P=e_s`. Since `P(0)=c!=0`, all `delta` roots counted with
multiplicity lie in `G_m`.

Fix independently one root of `P` at each active coordinate. The remaining
coordinates form an irreducible torus of dimension `m-alpha_m`.

For (5.10), the two indices differ by `nu`. The active interval (5.4) has
length at most `nu`, so they cannot both be active. Therefore the forbidden
function has at least one free variable. If `y_n` is free it is linear in that
variable; if `y_(n-nu)` is free, `P(y_(n-nu))` is nonconstant. Hence its zero
set is proper.

For (5.11), the two indices differ by `k-nu`. The active interval has length
at most `k-nu`, so again they cannot both be active. If the first is free,
`P(y_j)+constant` is nonconstant; if the second is free, the expression is
linear in it. If the active variable makes `P(y_j)=0`, the expression may be
a unit, whose forbidden zero set is empty and therefore still proper.

A finite union of proper closed subsets cannot cover an irreducible variety
over the infinite field `Omega`. Thus every root assignment gives a nonempty
open subset. If `alpha_m=m`, then `m<=nu` and `m<=k-nu`, so neither inequality
family has any index; the zero-dimensional root assignment itself survives.

If `P` is squarefree, it has `delta` distinct roots. The `delta^alpha_m`
root assignments are disjoint, and each surviving open torus is smooth and
irreducible of dimension `m-alpha_m`.

If `P` has `r` distinct roots, reduction replaces its root scheme by `r`
reduced points. Hence `E_m^red` has `r^alpha_m` irreducible components. A
multiple root at an active coordinate leaves the corresponding nilpotent
direction in the unreduced fiber. This proves `TA3`.

## 7. TA4: universal fixed-support geometry

Fix the actual exponent support and set

`B=Spec Q[a^(+-1),c^(+-1),b_1^(+-1),...,b_s^(+-1)]`.              (7.1)

Let

`P_univ(X)=c+sum b_ell X^(e_ell)`

and `delta=e_s`. Because the leading and constant coefficients are units, the
root scheme

`Z=V(P_univ) subset G_(m,B)`                                      (7.2)

is finite locally free of rank `delta` over `B`: after dividing by the
leading unit, its coordinate algebra has basis `1,X,...,X^(delta-1)`, and
the unit constant term makes `X` invertible in the quotient.

The active root equations identify the universal `E_m` before inequalities
with

`Z^(alpha_m) x_B G_(m,B)^(m-alpha_m)`.                             (7.3)

Conditions (5.10)--(5.11) remove relative closed subschemes. Hence
`mathcal E_m` is an open subscheme of (7.3). Each copy of `Z` is a relative
hypersurface in a smooth one-dimensional torus; their product is a relative
local complete intersection. Finite local freeness and the torus factors give
flatness, preserved by the open restriction.

Over the discriminant complement, every root is simple, so `Z -> B` is
etale. Thus `mathcal E_m -> B` is smooth there.

Fix a coefficient fiber and a geometric point with active roots
`rho_i` of multiplicities `mu_i`. In the completed local ring at `rho_i`,

`P(rho_i+z_i)=unit_i z_i^(mu_i)`.

The inequality functions are units at a point of `E_m`. Therefore the
completed **fiber** local ring is

`Omega[[t_1,...,t_(m-alpha_m),z_1,...,z_(alpha_m)]]`

` /(z_1^(mu_1),...,z_(alpha_m)^(mu_(alpha_m)))`.                   (7.4)

This proves `TA4`. It does not turn `E_m` into a Hilbert or Fano scheme and
does not classify the total-space singular locus.

## 8. CA1: coefficientwise finite-rank sharpness

Take `m=k-1`. Then `alpha_(k-1)=1`, and Section 6 proves that `E_(k-1)` is
nonempty for every permitted coefficient tuple. Choose one translating point
over the algebraic closure of the coefficient field. Its finitely many
coordinates generate a finite algebraic extension.

The subgroup `H_(k-1)` is one-dimensional and saturated. Let `t=2`, which has
infinite multiplicative order in characteristic zero, and let `Gamma` be
generated by `t` and the finitely many translation scalars. Varying the torus
parameter through `t^N` gives distinct points of the translate, all with
coordinates in `Gamma`. By (1.3), `T_(k-1)` is infinite.

At `m=k`, BG17 excludes positive-dimensional translates, so `EXT-L` gives
finiteness of `T_k` for every finite-rank group. This proves `CA1`, with the
quantifiers “after a compatible finite extension” and “for a compatible
finitely generated group.”

## 9. Part B setup and TB1: exclusive labels

Assume through Section 18 that

`P(X)=c+b X^d`, `a,b,c in Omega*`, `d>=2`.                         (9.1)

Let

`g=gcd(k,nu)`, `q=k/g`, `L=(k-nu)/g`.                             (9.2)

Then `q>=2`, `1<=L<q`, and `gcd(q,L)=1`.

Fix one residue class modulo `g`. With core coordinates indexed by `j`, the
recurrence is

`x_(j+q)=c+b x_(j+L)^d+a x_j`.                                   (9.3)

On a translate `xi H`, write restricted characters additively as `u_i in
M=X*(H)` and core translation scalars as `eta_i`. The group-algebra identity
is

`eta_(j+q)[u_(j+q)]-c[0]-b eta_(j+L)^d[d u_(j+L)]`

` -a eta_j[u_j]=0`.                                                (9.4)

All four coefficients are nonzero. Every character class in (9.4) must occur
at least twice. Apart from all characters being zero, the only partitions are
the three pairings of four terms:

| label | `u_j` | `u_(j+L)` | `u_(j+q)` | scalar conditions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `A` | `0` | `v` | `d v` | `eta_(j+q)=b eta_(j+L)^d`, `a eta_j=-c` |
| `B` | `v` | `0` | `v` | `eta_(j+q)=a eta_j`, `b eta_(j+L)^d=-c` |
| `C` | `d v` | `v` | `0` | `eta_(j+q)=c`, `a eta_j=-b eta_(j+L)^d` |
| `Z` | `0` | `0` | `0` | `eta_(j+q)=c+b eta_(j+L)^d+a eta_j` |

In `A`, `B`, and `C`, `v!=0`. Thus the labels are exclusive. A configuration
with all three variable characters equal and nonzero is impossible because
the constant character would be a singleton.

For support bits `s_i=1_(u_i!=0)`, every row gives

`s_(j+q)=s_j+s_(j+L)` in `F_2`.                                  (9.5)

This proves `TB1`.

## 10. TB2--TB3: gcd reduction and colored scaling components

Original equation indices differing by `k=gq` and `k-nu=gL` stay in one
residue class modulo `g`. Hence the full recurrence splits into `g` copies of
the core index pattern (9.3). This proves `TB2`.

For one core word, make a graph whose vertices are the indices with nonzero
character. In every active triple, connect its two nonzero vertices:

- `A`: direct `j+L -> j+q` with weight `1`;
- `B`: direct `j -> j+q` with weight `0`;
- `C`: direct `j+L -> j` with weight `1`.

Weight `e` means the target character equals `d^e` times the source.

Consider a connected component. Traverse an undirected closed walk with
signed total weight `s`. It gives `v=d^s v` in `M tensor Q` for a nonzero
character `v`. Since `d>=2`, this forces `s=0`. Therefore there are integer
heights `h_i`, unique up to a common additive constant on the component, such
that every directed edge has the displayed height difference.

Assign a separate formal color `e_C` to every component and define

`F_i=T^(h_i)e_C` if index `i` is in component `C`, and `F_i=0` otherwise.
                                                                        (10.1)

Then in the free module

`direct_sum_C F_2[T,T^(-1)] e_C`

every local label gives

`F_(j+q)=F_j+T F_(j+L)`.                                          (10.2)

For example, label `C` has `F_j=T F_(j+L)` and zero output, so the two equal
monomials cancel in characteristic two. Labels `A`, `B`, and `Z` are equally
direct.

Colors are essential: two graph components could accidentally carry equal
values in `M` without being joined by a local equation. They must remain
separate in (10.2). This proves `TB3`.

## 11. TB4: extinction after `q^2` core equations

Suppose a nonzero component occurs in a word satisfying equations
`j=0,...,q^2-1`. Choose a vertex of minimum height in that component.

If its index `i>=q`, inspect equation `i-q`, where it is the output. It cannot
have label `A`, because then the middle vertex has height one less. It cannot
have label `C`, whose output is zero. Hence it has label `B`, and the old
vertex `i-q` is in the same component with the same minimum height. Repeating
reaches a minimum-height index

`r in {0,...,q-1}`.                                                (11.1)

Now inspect equations with old indices `r+jq`. A minimum-height nonzero old
vertex cannot be `A`, whose old vertex is zero, or `C`, whose middle vertex
has lower height. Thus it is `B`. For `0<=j<=q-1`, all these equation indices
are at most `q^2-1`, so

`u_(r+jq)!=0`, `u_(r+L+jq)=0`.                                   (11.2)

From two consecutive zeros separated by `q`, exclusivity or (9.5) forces the
intermediate `L`-shifted support bit to vanish. Induction gives the triangular
zero set

`u_(r+tL+jq)=0`,

`1<=t<=q`, `0<=j<=q-t`.                                           (11.3)

At `t=q,j=0`, (11.3) says `u_(r+qL)=0`. But the `B` persistence chain in
(11.2), at its `L`th step, says `u_(r+Lq)!=0`. Since `qL=Lq`, this is a
contradiction. Every colored component is empty, so every involved character
is zero. This proves `TB4`.

## 12. TB5: the unique `q^2-1` core shadow

Now assume equations `j=0,...,q^2-2`.

If the minimum-height initial index from (11.1) satisfied `r<=q-2`, all `q`
equations at `r+jq`, `0<=j<=q-1`, would still lie in the window, reproducing
the contradiction above. Hence

`r=q-1`.                                                          (12.1)

There are now `q-1` persistence equations, giving

`u_(q-1+jq)!=0`, `0<=j<=q-1`,                                    (12.2)

and

`u_(q-1+tL+jq)=0`,

`1<=t<=q-1`, `0<=j<=q-1-t`.                                      (12.3)

Put

`A_*=Lq`, `N_*=(L+1)q-1`.                                        (12.4)

We prove that (12.3) fills `[A_*,N_*-1]`. For each
`s in {0,...,q-2}`, choose the unique `t in {1,...,q-1}` satisfying

`tL == s+1 mod q`.

Write `tL=hq+s+1` and set `j=L-1-h`. Then

`Lq+s=(q-1)+tL+jq`.                                               (12.5)

Since `tL<=t(q-1)`, one has `h<=L-1`. Also

`tL-q(t+L-q)=(q-t)(q-L)>=0`,

so `h>=t+L-q`. These two bounds give

`0<=j<=q-1-t`,

placing (12.5) in (12.3). Conversely the central interval has only `q-1`
positions before `N_*`, so the coverage is exact.

The remaining coordinate

`N_*=(q-1)+Lq`

is the `L`th point in the nonzero persistence chain (12.2). Thus every
nonzero colored component contains the same vertex `N_*`. Components are
disjoint sets of vertices, so there is exactly one nonzero component.

Normalize its height by

`F_(N_*)=1`.

Then

`(F_(A_*),...,F_(N_*))=(0,...,0,1)`.                              (12.6)

Recurrence (10.2) determines the word forward from any consecutive `q`
values. It also determines it backward, because

`F_j=F_(j+q)+T F_(j+L)`

and descending `j` makes both right-hand indices already known. Therefore
(12.6) gives at most one nonzero core word.

### 12.1 Backward generating function

Let `G_t=F_(N_*-t)`. Then

`G(z)=sum_(t>=0)G_t z^t`

` =(1+T z^L)/(1+T z^L+z^q)`

` =1+z^q/(1+T z^L+z^q)`.                                        (12.7)

Thus `G_0=1`, `G_1=...=G_(q-1)=0`, and for `t>=q`,

`G_t=sum_(alpha q+beta L=t-q)`

` binom(alpha+beta,beta)_(mod 2) T^beta`.                         (12.8)

### 12.2 Forward generating function

Put `p=q-L` and `H_t=F_(A_*+t)`. Then

`H(z)=sum_(t>=0)H_t z^t`

` =z^(q-1)/(1+T z^p+z^q)`,                                      (12.9)

so

`H_t=sum_(alpha q+beta p=t-q+1)`

` binom(alpha+beta,beta)_(mod 2) T^beta`.                        (12.10)

If `gcd(q,s)=1` and `D<qs`, the equation

`alpha q+beta s=D`, `alpha,beta>=0`,                             (12.11)

has at most one solution. Indeed, the difference of two solutions is an
integer multiple of `(s,-q)`, and two distinct nonnegative solutions would
force `D>=qs`.

In the backward range needed for indices down to zero, (12.8) has
`D<=Lq-1`. In the forward range through the last involved coordinate
`q^2+q-2`, (12.10) has `D<=pq-1`. Hence every coefficient in the involved
window is either zero or a single monomial `T^beta`. Equations (12.7)--(12.10)
therefore construct the unique valid shadow, not merely a formal polynomial
solution.

If one adds the next output coordinate, then `D=pq` in (12.10). The two
solutions `(alpha,beta)=(p,0)` and `(0,q)` give

`F_(q^2+q-1)=1+T^q`,                                              (12.12)

which is neither zero nor a monomial. This is the first collision and also
reconfirms TB4.

In character form, the unique shadow is

`u_i=d^beta w` if `F_i=T^beta`, and `u_i=0` if `F_i=0`,            (12.13)

where `w=u_(N_*)!=0`. This proves `TB5` for an arbitrary torsion-free
character target, with all involved characters lying in one cyclic subgroup.

## 13. Actual `X*(H)` and primitive rank one

For an actual full recurrence window in which TB4 has already killed every
other residue class, ambient coordinate characters generate `M=X*(H)` by
(2.2). Every remaining ambient coordinate character is zero or `d^beta w`,
and `w` itself occurs at `N_*`. Therefore

`M=Z w`.                                                          (13.1)

Thus `w` is a primitive generator and `dim H=1`.

Without the actual-surjectivity hypothesis, or before the other original
residue classes have been extinguished, an abstract torsion-free lattice
could have an unused direct summand. The correct core conclusion is only that
the **involved characters** span a cyclic subgroup. Equation (13.1) is
asserted for the actual torus coset after the original-window bookkeeping in
Section 14, where all other ambient characters are zero.

## 14. TB6: transfer to the original window

At `m=kq=gq^2`, original equation indices `0,...,gq^2-1` distribute as
exactly `q^2` equations in each residue class modulo `g`. TB4 makes every
character zero. The involved ambient coordinates are exactly

`u_0,...,u_(kq+k-1)`.                                             (14.1)

The endpoint `2kq-1` would be incorrect in general.

At `m=kq-1=gq^2-1`, residues `0,...,g-2` each receive `q^2` equations and
residue `g-1` receives `q^2-1`. TB4 kills all characters in the first
`g-1` residues. Any positive-dimensional actual torus must therefore use the
