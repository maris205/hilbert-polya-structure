# Citation Verification

## Verification policy

The literature check was bounded, primary-source-first, and closed for this
author package on **2026-08-19**. Publisher records, original journal texts,
and author-posted preprints are used for technical scope. Search-result pages
are not scientific evidence.

A bounded negative result means only that no direct collision was found in
the checked sources. It is never converted into a global or absolute priority
claim. The search must be refreshed at any future source-lock date.

`EXT-L`, Laurent's qualitative torus theorem, is the only indispensable
external proof input. Every other source supplies terminology, general
background, or a closest-scope comparison.

## EXT-L: Michel Laurent, 1984

- Michel Laurent, “Equations diophantiennes exponentielles,” *Inventiones
  mathematicae* 78 (1984), 299--327.
- DOI: `10.1007/BF01388597`.
- Publisher record:
  <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01388597>.
- EuDML journal record: <https://eudml.org/doc/143175>.

The exact specialization used is qualitative: for a closed subvariety `X` of
a complex torus and the division group of a finitely generated subgroup,
`X` meets that division group in a finite union of intersections with torus
translates contained in `X`. If `X` contains no positive-dimensional torus
translate, the intersection is finite.

The project does not attribute an effective count, a recurrence-specific
exceptional set, or an algorithm to Laurent.

The arbitrary-characteristic-zero-field and arbitrary-torsion bridge is
internal: a finite-rank group lies in the division hull of a finitely
generated subgroup, and the finitely generated field formed from coefficients
and group generators embeds into `C`. No source is cited for the false claim
that every large characteristic-zero field embeds in `C`.

## Why `E_m` is not called a Fano scheme

- Ilten--Zotine, “On Fano Schemes of Toric Varieties,”
  arXiv:1605.05745, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.05745>.
- Ilten--Kelly, “Fano Schemes of Complete Intersections in Toric Varieties,”
  arXiv:1910.05593, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05593>.

Those sources parameterize projective linear subspaces in toric varieties.
Paper 19 parameterizes normalized affine torus translates by explicit scalar
coordinates. No Hilbert/Fano functorial representability theorem has been
proved here; the sources therefore motivate the terminology restriction
rather than provide a theorem input.
