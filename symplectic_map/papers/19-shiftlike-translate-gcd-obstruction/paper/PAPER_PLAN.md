## Unified story and reader promise

**One-sentence story:** Character relations first isolate an extremal shadow, and the paper then decides whether translating scalars lift it: the anchored multi-support shadow lifts along the explicit normalized scheme `E_m`, whereas the unique support-one penultimate shadow has no lift for `q>=3` and lifts for `q=2` exactly on the resonant coefficient locus.

The first content page must answer all three reader questions immediately:

1. **What?** Classify every maximum-dimensional anchored translate, including its fixed-fiber and fixed-support coefficient geometry, and prove the support-one `q^2/q^2-1` character theorem followed by the `q>=3` scalar obstruction.
2. **Why?** Character constraints alone do not decide containment of a torus translate; compatible nonzero translation scalars are the missing layer in both regimes.
3. **So what?** Part A upgrades an equality example to a coefficientwise classification and family theorem; Part B shows that the last possible character shadow can be universally non-liftable, with the `q=2` resonance as the exact exception.

The abstract and introduction must distinguish exact **character** extinction from the non-optimal `q>=3` scalar conclusion. They must never call `kq-1` an exact, optimal, shortest, minimal, or sharp scalar threshold.

## Hypotheses and notation in mandatory order

The source must introduce assumptions in the following order, without silently strengthening or weakening them.

### Common geometric and arithmetic setup

1. Let `Omega` be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
2. Fix `k>=2`, `1<=nu<k`, and `a in Omega*`.
3. For `P in Omega[X]`, define the type-`nu` shift-like map
   `S(z_1,...,z_k)=(z_2,...,z_k,P(z_(k-nu+1))+a z_1)` and display its inverse before deriving the zero-based recurrence
   `x_(n+k)=P(x_(n+k-nu))+a x_n`.
4. For `m>=0`, define `V_m subset (G_m)^(k+m)` using exactly the equations `0<=n<m`; define `V_0=(G_m)^k`.
5. Only then, over a characteristic-zero field `K` and for `Gamma<=K*`, define `T_m(S,Gamma)` using states at times `0,...,m`, and prove the projection bijection
   `V_m intersect Gamma^(k+m) -> T_m(S,Gamma)`.
6. Define a torus translate as `xi H`, with `H` a connected closed subtorus. Put `M=X*(H)`, write ambient restrictions as `x_i=xi_i chi^(u_i)`, and record that ambient coordinate characters surject onto the actual `M`.
7. State character independence and saturated-lattice facts before either part. State `EXT-L` and prove the arbitrary-characteristic-zero-field, arbitrary-torsion finite-rank bridge, but use it only after geometric translate exclusion or construction is complete.

Geometric dimension belongs only to a contained translate `xi H`, never to `T_m`.

### Part A hypotheses and notation

After the common setup, assume

`P(X)=c+sum_(ell=1)^s b_ell X^(e_ell)`,

with actual collected support `0<e_1<...<e_s`, all of `a,c,b_ell` nonzero, `s>=2`, and `1<=m<k`. Introduce, in order:

- the predecessor relation lattice `L_m` generated by
  `A_n=epsilon_(k+n-nu)` and `B_n=epsilon_(k+n)-epsilon_n`, `0<=n<m`;
- `R_m={n-nu mod k:0<=n<m}` and the canonical subgroup `H_m`;
- the unique normalized representative whose free initial scalars are one;
- `y_n=xi_(k+n-nu)`;
- `A_m=[max(0,m-nu),min(m,k-nu))` and
  `alpha_m=min(m,k-m,nu,k-nu)`;
- the normalized locally closed scheme `E_m` with its root equations and both open nonvanishing families;
- `delta=deg P`, the number `r` of distinct roots, active multiplicities `mu_i`, the fixed-support coefficient torus `B`, the universal root scheme `Z(P_univ)`, and `mathcal E_m`.

Keep the subscripted lattice `L_m` typographically distinct from the unsubscripted Part B step `L`.

### Part B hypotheses and notation

After closing Part A, assume

`P(X)=c+bX^d`, with `a,b,c in Omega*` and `d>=2`.

Introduce, in order:

- `g=gcd(k,nu)`, `q=k/g`, and `L=(k-nu)/g`, so `q>=2`, `1<=L<q`, and `gcd(q,L)=1`;
- one core residue and its characters `u_j` and translation scalars `eta_j`;
- the exclusive labels `A`, `B`, `C`, `Z`, including all scalar signs;
- component colors `e_C`, integer heights `h_i`, and
  `F_i=T^(h_i)e_C` in the direct sum of colored Laurent modules;
- `p=q-L`, `A_*=Lq`, `N_*=(L+1)q-1`, and, for `q>=3`,
  `z_*=N_*-2L=(q-2)L+q-1`;
- the backward and forward generating functions `G(z)` and `H(z)`;
- for the `q=2` fill, `Phi(x,y)=(y,c+b y^d-x)` and its inverse.

The abstract core conclusion is only that the involved characters span one cyclic subgroup. The stronger equality `X*(H)=Z w` appears only after original-window bookkeeping kills the other residues and ambient-character surjectivity is invoked.

## Section-by-section proof blueprint

### 2. Recurrences, character lattices, Laurent bridge, and predecessor boundary

1. Display `S`, its inverse, and derive the zero-based recurrence by direct substitution.
2. Define `V_m` with exactly `m` equations and `T_m` with `m+1` states; prove the projection bijection.
3. Prove group-algebra character independence and record surjectivity of ambient coordinate restrictions onto actual `X*(H)`.
4. State the saturated-kernel lemma.
5. State `EXT-L` in precisely the qualitative torus form used later. Prove the finite-rank bridge with arbitrary torsion by division hulls, then pass through the finitely generated coefficient-and-generator field into `C`; never claim that an arbitrary large field embeds into `C`.
6. State the Paper 17 background theorem `dim H<=k-m`, its relation lattice, and terminal `T_k` finiteness as predecessor inputs. Do not count either as Paper 19 novelty.

### 3. Saturated equality rigidity

1. Restrict the anchored recurrence to `xi H`; because `s+1>=3` middle characters are distinct when the middle character is nonzero, the two endpoints cannot prevent a singleton.
2. Deduce `u_(k+n-nu)=0` and `u_(k+n)=u_n`, including the scalar alternative that still gives both character relations.
3. Mod out by copy relations; show the middle relations kill exactly the distinct residues `R_m` and the quotient is free on the other `k-m` initial coordinates.
4. Conclude that `L_m` is saturated of rank `2m`.
5. At equality, compare ranks of the actual kernel and `L_m`; identify the connected embedded subgroup exactly as `H_m`, not just up to isogeny. End with the lower-dimensional nonclaim.

### 4. Normalized translates and nonemptiness

1. Prove unique normalization from free initial coordinates.
2. Derive `A_m` directly from residue membership and prove the four-minimum formula for `alpha_m` without a gcd assumption.
3. Introduce `y_n`; derive the active root equations.
4. Reconstruct every scalar in order:
   - direct killed initial middles for `n<nu`;
   - solved killed initials `a^(-1)(y_n-P(y_(n-nu)))` for `n>=nu`;
   - free initials equal to one;
   - outputs `P(y_j)+a xi_j`.
5. Prove consistency when an output is a later `y`; classify every output as later `y`, automatic active tail `a`, or one of the second open conditions. This is the completeness checkpoint for `TA2`.
6. For nonemptiness, fix an arbitrary active root tuple. Show that indices separated by `nu` or `k-nu` cannot both lie in the active interval, so every forbidden equality retains a free variable and defines a proper closed set, possibly empty.
7. Treat `alpha_m=m` separately: both inequality ranges are empty. Conclude nonemptiness for every root tuple, not from a repeated-root shortcut.

### 5. Fixed-fiber and universal geometry

1. For squarefree `P`, separate the `delta^alpha_m` root assignments and prove that each surviving open torus is nonempty, smooth, irreducible, and of dimension `m-alpha_m`.
2. For arbitrary `P`, pass to reduction to obtain `r^alpha_m` geometric components and retain local nilpotent directions at multiple active roots.
3. Fix actual exponent support and unit coefficients over `B`. Divide by the leading unit, prove the root algebra is free on `1,X,...,X^(delta-1)`, and use the unit constant term to keep roots in `G_m`.
4. Identify `mathcal E_m` as the exact open in `Z(P_univ)^alpha_m x_B G_m^(m-alpha_m)`. Prove flatness and relative lci before taking the open; prove smoothness only over the discriminant complement.
5. At a fixed coefficient fiber, factor each active equation by a local unit and derive the completed fiber local ring with relations `z_i^(mu_i)`. Say “completed fiber” every time; make no total-space singular-locus claim.

### 6. Coefficientwise arithmetic sharpness

1. Substitute `m=k-1` and record `alpha_(k-1)=1`.
2. Use nonempty `E_(k-1)` to choose one translating point over a finite algebraic extension.
3. Use the saturated one-torus and the infinite-order element `2`; adjoin only finitely many translating scalars to obtain a compatible finitely generated group and infinitely many distinct points.
4. Invoke Paper 17 for the terminal geometric result at `k`, then `EXT-L` and the internal field bridge for finite `T_k` for every finite-rank group.
5. Repeat the compatible-extension/compatible-group qualifier in the theorem and conclusion.

### 7. Exclusive labels and gcd cores

1. Write the four-term group-algebra identity with its signs.
2. Prove that no character class is a singleton. List exactly the three pairings and the all-zero case, with `v!=0` in `A/B/C` so the labels are exclusive.
3. Derive the scalar rules and the support recurrence over `F_2`.
4. Split indices modulo `g`; verify `q`, `L`, and coprimality.
5. In each active triple connect exactly the two nonzero vertices with edge exponent zero or one. A signed cycle of nonzero weight would force `v=d^s v`; use torsion-freeness and `d>=2` to construct integer heights.
6. Give every connected component a distinct formal color even if actual lattice values coincide, and check the colored recurrence label by label.

### 8. Component-height extinction and structural uniqueness

1. For one arbitrary colored component, choose a minimum-height vertex and trace it backward as an output. Prove only label `B` is possible until an initial residue `r` is reached.
2. Prove forward minimum-height persistence through `B`; its middle character is a genuinely zero actual character by label exclusivity, not merely absent from the selected color.
3. Propagate the triangular zero set
   `u_(r+tL+jq)=0`, `1<=t<=q`, `0<=j<=q-t`, and contradict the nonzero persistence column using `qL=Lq`. Repeat componentwise to prove `TB4` for arbitrary rank.
4. With only `q^2-1` equations, prove `r=q-1`.
5. Prove central-block coverage explicitly: for each `s in [0,q-2]`, choose the unique `t in [1,q-1]` with `tL=s+1 mod q`, write `tL=hq+s+1`, take `j=L-1-h`, and establish both bounds `0<=j<=q-1-t`.
6. Show `N_*` lies on the nonzero persistence column. Because every nonzero component contains that same vertex, exactly one component remains. Normalize its central delta block.

### 9. Laurent shadow, original windows, and seven labels

1. Derive, rather than merely state,
   `G(z)=(1+Tz^L)/(1+Tz^L+z^q)` and
   `H(z)=z^(q-1)/(1+Tz^(q-L)+z^q)`.
2. Display both coefficient formulas. Prove the semigroup uniqueness lemma: if `D<qs` and `gcd(q,s)=1`, `D=alpha q+beta s` has at most one nonnegative representation.
3. Verify the complete backward range `D<=Lq-1` and complete involved forward range `D<=(q-L)q-1`; conclude every involved coefficient is zero or one monomial. At the next degree prove the only two representations yield `1+T^q`.
4. Transfer back to original windows. At `m=kq`, each residue has `q^2` equations and the ambient coordinates end at `u_(kq+k-1)`. At `m=kq-1`, residues `0,...,g-2` have `q^2` equations and residue `g-1` has `q^2-1`; verify full coordinate coverage.
5. Only now use ambient-character surjectivity to deduce actual `X*(H)=Z w`, with `w` present and primitive.
6. For `q>=3`, derive the seven triples at
   `L-1`, `q-1`, `z_*-q`, `z_*+L-q`, `z_*`, `z_*+L`, `z_*+q`.
7. Prove `F_(z_*)=0` separately for `2L<q` and `2L>q`; exclude `2L=q` by coprimality. Prove the lowest and highest index bounds, and note that any small-parameter collision is only between compatible `C` labels.

### 10. Scalar obstruction, `q=2` fill, and arithmetic consequences

1. Read the first `C/B` overlap to obtain `bc^(d-1)=-1`.
2. Read the later `C/A` overlap to obtain `a=-1`.
3. Use the neighboring `C/C/A` labels to make the old, middle, and future translation scalars at the `Z` equation all equal to `c`; substitute to obtain `2c=0`, contradicting characteristic zero and `c!=0`.
4. State `TB8` only as a universal penultimate obstruction and one-step improvement over character extinction; explicitly say that no shorter-window survivor has been proved.
5. For `q=2`, force `L=1`, derive the `CBA` word and the necessity of `a=-1`, `bc^(d-1)=-1`.
6. Verify the full active scalar tuple `(b s^d,s,c,-s,b(-s)^d)` and primitivity from the exponent vector containing one.
7. For every inactive residue when `g>1`, use the actual polynomial automorphism `Phi(x,y)=(y,c+b y^d-x)` with inverse `(c+b x^d-y,x)`. Prove each required finite iterate coordinate is a nonzero polynomial, avoid finitely many proper coordinate-zero hypersurfaces over the infinite field, and assemble the full original translate.
8. Derive `CB1` from `TB4`, `TB8`, `TB9`, the finite-rank bridge, and `EXT-L`, with no effective count.

### 11. Related work, ownership limits, and conclusion

Use quantifier-level comparisons, not a bibliography dump. Entire-translate containment in a finite recurrence window must be distinguished from intersections with a fixed translate, torsion-coset counting, one-orbit subgroup hitting, `S`-unit integrality, or semilinearity of iteration-exponent sets. Close by restating the common scalar-lift answer and the `q>=3` non-optimality boundary.
