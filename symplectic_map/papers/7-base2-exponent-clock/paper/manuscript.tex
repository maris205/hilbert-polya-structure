\title{Exact 2-Adic Valuation of Higher-Period Multipliers\\
for a Frozen PCF Quadratic}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{August 14, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
For the postcritically finite quadratic
\(g(z)=z^2-u\), where \(u^3-2u^2+2u-2=0\), we determine the
2-adic valuation of every multiplier of exact period at least two.  A standard
non-Archimedean contraction argument shows more generally that, in residue
characteristic two, every nontrivial cycle of \(z^2+c\) with
\(0<|c|<1\) consists of units and has multiplier norm exactly \(|2|^n\).
At the frozen cubic this yields
\(w(\Lambda_C)=n w(2)\) at every place over two; in particular, every
rational multiplier is \(2^n\) times an odd integer.  We then identify each
local cycle as a unique Hensel lift of a Frobenius orbit and its normalized
multiplier as an unramified norm; reduction modulo two gives a two-coefficient
obstruction that excludes \(\Lambda_C=\pm2^n\) at exact periods two and
three but already admits an exact degree-four Frobenius orbit.  A
source-locked audit records twelve sign--period no-hit decisions over periods
two through seven, each supported by separately implemented but algebraically
equivalent exact gcd and resultant/field-norm certificates;
all were development-seen, and a uniform exclusion valid for every
\(n\ge4\) remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Let \(C=(z_0,\ldots,z_{n-1})\) be an exact period-\(n\) cycle of a
quadratic polynomial.  Its multiplier factors as
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_C=(g^n)'(z_0)=2^n B_C,
  \qquad B_C=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}z_j.
  \label{eq:multiplier-factorization}
\end{equation}
The chain rule gives the visible factor \(2^n\), while global algebraic
integrality makes a rational normalized product integral; neither observation
determines whether \(B_C\) is a unit at two.
That distinction is decisive at the frozen map
\begin{equation}
  Q(U)=U^3-2U^2+2U-2,
  \qquad Q(u)=0,
  \qquad g(z)=z^2-u,
  \label{eq:frozen-map}
\end{equation}
where \(u\) denotes the unique real root of \(Q\).

The main result is an exact local statement in every period: for every
cycle-field place \(w\) above two and every exact \(n\ge2\),
\begin{equation}
  w(\Lambda_C)=n w(2).
  \label{eq:intro-main}
\end{equation}
If \(\Lambda_C\) is rational, its normalized product is therefore an odd
integer.  The theorem sharpens divisibility to exact valuation, but it leaves
the boundary values \(B_C=\pm1\) unresolved.  Figure~\ref{fig:boundary-map}
places that residual equality beside two predicates that must not be
conflated with it.

Our contributions are four concrete claims.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We give a complete elementary proof of a local sharp-boundary lemma
  for \(z^2+c\) in residue characteristic two and specialize it at the
  unique place over two of \(\Q(u)\).
  \item We prove the rational corollary
  \(\Lambda_C=2^n m\) with \(m\) odd by combining the local valuation with
  a self-contained integrality argument.
  \item We describe exact local cycles as Frobenius--Hensel lifts and identify
  \(B_C\) with their unramified norm.  Its first two residue coefficients
  rule out \(B_C=\pm1\) for \(n=2,3\), while an irreducible degree-four
  witness shows that this filter alone cannot exclude all higher-degree
  orbits.
  \item We preserve a one-shot, exact-symbolic ledger through period seven as
  an implementation-falsification record, with exact-period saturation,
  two separately implemented but algebraically equivalent exact target
  certificates (gcd and resultant/field norm), signed controls, and an
  independent result integrity audit.
\end{enumerate}

The scope boundary is part of the result.  This paper does not exclude
\(\Lambda_C=\pm2^n\) in all periods, does not decide
\(|\Lambda_C|=2^n\) without rationality, and makes no claim about prime
orbits, zeta zeros, quantization, or a general rigidity theorem for PCF
quadratics.  In particular, the 2-adic theorem does not imply the
Archimedean identity \(n^{-1}\log|\Lambda_C|=\log 2\), nor its negation.

Section~\ref{sec:object} fixes the object and its relation to nearby work.
Sections~\ref{sec:local}--\ref{sec:norm} prove the valuation and norm
statements.  Section~\ref{sec:equality} derives necessary equality
conditions, and Section~\ref{sec:audit} reports the bounded exact audit.
The final section isolates the remaining proof problem.  The finite ledger
is previewed in Figure~\ref{fig:registered-ledger}; the proof mechanism is
summarized later in Figure~\ref{fig:frobenius-filter}.

\section{Frozen object, genealogy, and nearby results}
\label{sec:object}

The derivative
\(Q'(U)=3U^2-4U+2\) is positive on \(\mathbb R\), so \(Q\) has a unique
real root.  Put \(a=u^2-u\).  Direct reduction by \(Q(u)=0\) gives
\begin{equation}
  0\longmapsto -u\longmapsto a\longmapsto -a\longmapsto -a.
  \label{eq:critical-orbit}
\end{equation}
Thus \(g\) is postcritically finite of type \((3,1)\).  The same algebraic
parameter appears in a recent study \citep{wang2026prime}, cited here only to
document the object's genealogy.  No empirical dataset, parameter search, or
conclusion from that work enters the present note.  Every mathematical claim
below is proved here from \eqref{eq:frozen-map}.

Arithmetic dynamics supplies standard terminology for periodic points,
local fields, and multipliers \citep{silverman2007arithmetic}.  Formal
dynatomic period and least period need not agree without additional checks
\citep{morton1994rational}; this distinction controls the finite audit in
Section~\ref{sec:audit}.  The closest literature divides into four axes.

\section{A local sharp-boundary lemma}
\label{sec:local}

We write absolute values multiplicatively and valuations additively.  No
normalization of the latter is needed, since all valuation identities are
homogeneous.

\begin{theorem}[Local sharp-boundary lemma]
\label{thm:local}
Let \(F\) be a complete non-Archimedean field of characteristic zero and
residue characteristic two.  For
\(f(z)=z^2+c\) with \(0<|c|<1\), every exact period-\(n\) point with
\(n\ge2\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
  |f^j(z)|=1\quad(0\le j<n),
  \qquad |(f^n)'(z)|=|2|^n.
  \label{eq:local-boundary}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If \(|z|>1\), then
\(|f(z)|=|z|^2>|z|\), and successive absolute values escape; hence every
periodic point belongs to the closed unit disk.  The open unit disk is
forward invariant because
\(|f(z)|\le\max\{|z|^2,|c|\}<1\) whenever \(|z|<1\).

Suppose a nontrivial cycle lies in that open disk.  For distinct cycle
points \(x,y\),
\begin{equation}
  |f(x)-f(y)|=|x-y|\,|x+y|<|x-y|.
  \label{eq:strict-contraction}
\end{equation}
Taking \(x=z_0\), \(y=z_1\), and applying \eqref{eq:strict-contraction}
around the cycle would strictly decrease their distance and return to the
same pair after \(n\) steps, a contradiction.  Every point of an exact
cycle of length at least two is therefore a unit.  Finally,
\begin{equation}
  (f^n)'(z_0)=2^n\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}f^j(z_0),
\end{equation}
and every factor in the product has norm one.  This proves
\eqref{eq:local-boundary}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exactness and comparison]
The displayed value is exact rather than merely a bound.  The power map
\(z\mapsto z^2\), whose parameter \(c=0\) lies just outside the strict
hypothesis \(0<|c|<1\), also attains the ambient multiplier threshold
\(2^n\) on every nonzero exact cycle.  Rivera--Letelier's independent
strict-threshold theorem also places the residue-characteristic-two boundary
at \(|2|^n\) \citep{riveraletelier2026critical}; the elementary proof above
is logically complete without that comparison.
\end{remark}

\section{The frozen 2-adic valuation theorem}
\label{sec:frozen-theorem}

Let \(K=\Q(u)\).  The polynomial \(Q\) is 2-Eisenstein.  It is therefore
irreducible over \(\Q_2\), the tensor product \(K\otimes_\Q\Q_2\) is a
field, and \(K\) has a unique place over two.  We denote its completion by
\(\Ku\).  This extension of \(\Q_2\) is totally ramified of degree three,
with uniformizer \(u\).  The defining equation also gives the useful exact
identity
\begin{equation}
  2=\frac{u^3}{u^2-u+1},
  \qquad v_u(2)=3,
  \label{eq:two-uniformizer}
\end{equation}
because the denominator is a \(u\)-adic unit.

\begin{theorem}[Exact frozen valuation]
\label{thm:frozen}
Let \(L/K\) be a finite extension containing the coordinates of an exact
period-\(n\) cycle \(C\) of \(g\), with \(n\ge2\), and let \(w\) be an
additive non-Archimedean valuation of \(L\) above the unique valuation of
\(K\) over two.  Every point of \(C\) is a \(w\)-unit
and
\begin{equation}
  w(\Lambda_C)=n w(2).
  \label{eq:frozen-valuation}
\end{equation}
If \(\Lambda_C\in\Q\), then
\begin{equation}
  v_2(\Lambda_C)=n,
  \qquad \Lambda_C=2^n m
  \quad\text{for some }m\in2\Z+1.
  \label{eq:rational-odd}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Complete \(L\) at \(w\).  Since the constant term of \(g\) is
\(-u\), Theorem~\ref{thm:local} applies and gives
\(w(z_j)=0\) for all \(j\).  Equation
\eqref{eq:multiplier-factorization} then gives
\eqref{eq:frozen-valuation}.

It remains to justify the ordinary integer in \eqref{eq:rational-odd}
without importing an unpublished result.  The polynomial
\(g^n(X)-X\) is monic over \(\OK\), so each periodic point is integral over
\(\OK\), hence is an algebraic integer over \(\Z\).  The product
\(B_C\) is therefore an algebraic integer.  If \(\Lambda_C\) is rational,
then \(B_C=\Lambda_C/2^n\) is rational as well, and
\(\Q\cap\overline{\Z}=\Z\) gives \(B_C\in\Z\).  Its valuation at every
place above two is zero, so this integer is odd.
\end{proof}

The conclusion ``odd'' is exact, but it is not an exclusion: the two
integers \(1\) and \(-1\) remain among the allowed normalized quotients.
The next section turns those two cases into a local norm problem.

\section{Frobenius cycles and the norm coordinate}
\label{sec:norm}

The residue field of \(\Ku\) is \(\F_2\).  Let \(\Kun/\Ku\) be the
unramified extension of degree \(n\), and let \(\sigma\) denote the lift of
the squaring Frobenius.  Reduction modulo \(u\) gives
\begin{equation}
  g^n(X)-X\equiv X^{2^n}-X\pmod u.
  \label{eq:frobenius-reduction}
\end{equation}
The derivative of the right-hand side is \(-1\), so all its roots are
simple.

\begin{proposition}[Frobenius--Hensel model]
\label{prop:hensel}
For each \(\alpha\in\F_{2^n}\), there is a unique root
\(z_\alpha\in\mathcal O_{\Kun}\) of \(g^n(X)-X\) reducing to
\(\alpha\).  If \(\alpha\) has exact Frobenius degree \(d\mid n\), then
\(z_\alpha\) has exact dynamical period \(d\), and
\begin{equation}
  \sigma(z_\alpha)=g(z_\alpha).
  \label{eq:frob-is-dynamics}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for exact degree \(n\),
\begin{equation}
  B_C=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}g^j(z_\alpha)
      =\Norm_{\Kun/\Ku}(z_\alpha).
  \label{eq:norm-coordinate}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Hensel's lemma applied to \eqref{eq:frobenius-reduction} supplies one lift in
each of the \(2^n\) residue classes.  These roots exhaust the degree-
\(2^n\) polynomial.  Suppose that \(\alpha\) has exact Frobenius degree
\(d\mid n\).  Let \(y\in\mathcal O_{K_{u,d}}\subset\mathcal O_{K_{u,n}}\)
be its unique Hensel lift satisfying \(g^d(y)=y\).  Because \(d\mid n\), the
same element also satisfies \(g^n(y)=y\); uniqueness among the roots of
\(g^n(X)-X\) reducing to \(\alpha\) therefore gives \(y=z_\alpha\).
Reduction shows that no smaller positive iterate fixes this lift, so its exact
period is \(d\).

Both \(\sigma(z_\alpha)\) and \(g(z_\alpha)\) are roots of
\(g^n(X)-X\) reducing to \(\alpha^2\): the coefficients of \(g\) lie in
\(\Ku\), and \(g\) commutes with its iterates.  Hensel uniqueness proves
\eqref{eq:frob-is-dynamics}.  Multiplying its \(n\) Frobenius conjugates
gives \eqref{eq:norm-coordinate}.
\end{proof}

The proposition distinguishes least period from formal period before any
dynatomic manipulation.  It also places every normalized cycle product in
the base local field \(\Ku\), a fact used below for repeated returns.

\section{What rational equality would force}
\label{sec:equality}

Relation \eqref{eq:two-uniformizer} gives \((2)=(u^3)\) in every unramified
extension under consideration and identifies the coefficient ring as
\[
  \mathcal O_{\Kun}/(2)
  \simeq \F_{2^n}[\bar u]/(\bar u^3).
\]
Thus \(1,\bar u,\bar u^2\) are a basis over \(\F_{2^n}\).  Use Teichmuller
representatives for residue-field elements and write a lift as
\(z_\alpha\equiv\alpha+b_1u+b_2u^2\pmod2\).  Substitution into
\(\sigma(z_\alpha)=z_\alpha^2-u\) may be performed in this quotient:
all cross terms in \((\alpha+b_1u+b_2u^2)^2\) contain a factor \(2\) and
vanish modulo \(u^3\).  Coefficient comparison gives
\(\sigma(b_1)=1\) and \(\sigma(b_2)=b_1^2=1\).  Hence
\begin{equation}
  z_\alpha\equiv\alpha+u+u^2\pmod2.
  \label{eq:lift-mod-two}
\end{equation}

Let \(\alpha_j=\alpha^{2^j}\), and let \(e_k\) be the elementary symmetric
function of degree \(k\) in \(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}\), with
\(e_0=1\).  Each \(e_k\) is Frobenius-fixed and hence lies in \(\F_2\); for
an exact orbit, \(e_n=\prod_j\alpha_j=1\).  Put \(t=u+u^2\).  In the
displayed quotient, \(t^2\equiv u^2\pmod{u^3}\) and \(t^3=0\), so
\[
  \prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(\alpha_j+t)
  =e_n+e_{n-1}t+e_{n-2}t^2
  =1+e_{n-1}u+(e_{n-1}+e_{n-2})u^2.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:lift-mod-two}, this expands the norm as
\begin{equation}
  B_C\equiv
  1+e_{n-1}u+(e_{n-1}+e_{n-2})u^2\pmod2.
  \label{eq:norm-expansion}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Two-coefficient obstruction]
\label{prop:two-coeff}
If \(B_C=\pm1\), then
\begin{equation}
  e_{n-1}=e_{n-2}=0.
  \label{eq:two-coeff}
\end{equation}
No exact period-two or period-three cycle satisfies this condition.  In
degree four the irreducible polynomial \(T^4+T^3+1\) does satisfy it, so
\eqref{eq:two-coeff} already fails to exclude every exact degree-four orbit.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The two signs reduce to the same element modulo two.  Comparing the
coefficients in the basis \(1,\bar u,\bar u^2\) in
\eqref{eq:norm-expansion} gives
\eqref{eq:two-coeff}.  The only irreducible quadratic is
\(T^2+T+1\), and the irreducible cubics are
\(T^3+T+1\) and \(T^3+T^2+1\).  In each case at least one of the
coefficients of \(T\) and \(T^2\), namely \(e_{n-1}\) and
\(e_{n-2}\), is nonzero.  Thus equality is impossible for \(n=2,3\).
The polynomial \(T^4+T^3+1\) is irreducible over \(\F_2\) and has zero
coefficients of \(T\) and \(T^2\).  Its Frobenius orbit therefore passes
the necessary filter.  Passing the filter does not certify equality; the
statement concerns only the two displayed residue coefficients, not the full
norm in \(\Ku\).
\end{proof}

Two further necessary conditions are useful because they retain information
discarded by reduction modulo two.  Order the points so that
\(g(z_j)=z_{j+1}\), with indices modulo \(n\), and define
\(P_C(X)=\prod_j(X-z_j)\).

\begin{proposition}[Cycle-polynomial conditions]
\label{prop:cycle-poly}
For every exact cycle,
\begin{equation}
  P_C(g(X))=(-1)^nP_C(X)P_C(-X).
  \label{eq:cycle-poly}
\end{equation}
If \(n\ge2\) and \(B_C=\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}\), then
\begin{equation}
  P_C(-u)=P_C(u)=P_C(a)=(-1)^n,
  \qquad P_C(0)=(-1)^n\varepsilon.
  \label{eq:special-values}
\end{equation}
These identities do not imply that the single-cycle polynomial belongs to
\(K[X]\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since \(u+z_j=z_{j-1}^2\),
\[
  P_C(g(X))=\prod_j(X^2-z_j^2)
           =(-1)^nP_C(X)P_C(-X),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:cycle-poly}.  The fixed point \(-a\) is not on an
exact cycle of length at least two.  Substitution of \(X=-a\) into
\eqref{eq:cycle-poly} and cancellation of \(P_C(-a)\) gives
\(P_C(a)=(-1)^n\).  Next,
\(P_C(0)=(-1)^nB_C\), and substitution at \(X=0\) gives
\(P_C(-u)=(-1)^n\).  Substitution at \(X=u\), using \(g(u)=a\), then
gives \(P_C(u)=(-1)^n\).
\end{proof}

Finally, repetition creates no hidden rational base-two equality.  The only
roots of unity in \(\Ku\) are \(\pm1\): reduction removes all nontrivial
odd-order torsion, while a nontrivial higher 2-power root of unity generates
an even-degree extension of \(\Q_2\), which cannot lie in the cubic field
\(\Ku\).  If the \(r\)-fold return of an exact period-\(n\) orbit is
rational with ordinary Archimedean absolute value \(2^{nr}\), equivalently
if it equals \(\pm2^{nr}\), then
\(B_C^r=\pm1\).  Since \(B_C\in\Ku\), it follows that already
\(B_C=\pm1\).  The point still has exact period \(n\), not \(nr\), and
the argument says nothing about a modulus-only return without rationality.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.88\textwidth}{\centering
\textbf{Open boundary.}  No argument above proves
\(B_C\ne\pm1\), equivalently \(\Lambda_C\ne\pm2^n\), uniformly for
every \(n\ge4\).  The all-period status is \openstatus; individual periods
four through seven are separately absent in the finite audit below.}}
\end{center}

\section{Registered finite exact audit}
\label{sec:audit}

The finite calculation is a bounded test of the exact-period implementation,
not evidence for extending Proposition~\ref{prop:two-coeff} to all periods.
The source lock fixed the candidate, the signs \(\{+1,-1\}\), and the
periods \(2,\ldots,7\).  Every one of those periods had been seen during
development before the lock; there is no blind or prospective split.

For each \(n\), the implementation forms a squarefree set-theoretic exact
component \(\Psi_n^{\mathrm{set}}\), verifies that its degree is divisible
by \(n\), and checks that the cycle product \(B_n(X)\) is invariant under
\(g\) modulo that component.  Each sign is checked by two separately
implemented but algebraically equivalent exact certificates: the gcd must be
the constant polynomial one, while the resultant must have nonzero exact
norm from \(K\) to \(\Q\).  All twelve pairs agreed.  The
complete numbers, including exact nanosecond timings and norm
factorizations, appear in Appendix~\ref{app:ledger}.

The controls guard the principal false-negative modes.  For \(z^2\) at
period two the positive equality is detected and a declared negative target
is rejected.  For \(z^2-2\), the exact component
\(X^2+X-1\) has \(B=-1\) and multiplier \(-4\), so the signed path is
exercised.  For \(z^2-3/4\), a nonempty formal period-two component is
removed completely by least-period saturation and produces no false hit.
An upstream regression independently preserves the earlier coordinate and
multiplier identities.  The reviewed code tree passed 38 distinct tests with
no failure, error, or skip.

\section{Discussion and open boundary}
\label{sec:discussion}

The valuation theorem removes one source of ambiguity: a rational
higher-period multiplier cannot have extra powers of two hidden in its
normalized cycle product.  The remaining equality question lies wholly
inside the unit group of \(\Ku\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:hensel} makes that
question a norm equation, while Proposition~\ref{prop:two-coeff} sees only
the first two nilpotent coefficients of the norm modulo \(u^3\).  The
degree-four witness pinpoints the failure: those two coefficients can vanish
without forcing any conclusion about whether the full norm is \(\pm1\).

subgroup together with the nonlinear Hensel equation, or impose Galois
constraints on the special values in \eqref{eq:special-values}.  Each route
would need a degree-independent contradiction; a longer finite cutoff would
not supply one.

\(\Lambda_C=\pm2^n\), Archimedean modulus equality

exponent equality are different predicates.  This note proves no uniform
equality exclusion for all \(n\ge4\).  In the surrounding project ledger,
