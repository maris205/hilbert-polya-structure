## Independent theorem and proof replay

### Exact assumptions, window, and bound

The manuscript states (K) to be an arbitrary field of characteristic zero,
(d\ge2), (a,b,c\in K^\ast), and
(Gamma\le K^\ast) to have finite rank (r), without finite generation or
coefficient membership. Its definition

\[
T_m(H,\Gamma)=\{P\in\Gamma^2:H^j(P)\in\Gamma^2
\text{ for every }0\le j\le m\}
\]

correctly makes (T_4) a five-state, four-transition window with recurrence
indices (0,1,2,3), and (T_3) a four-state, three-transition window. The
displayed upper bound is exactly

\[
4d\exp\!\bigl(18^9(3r+1)\bigr)+81d^2.
\]

### Nondegenerate contribution

Writing (H^j(P)=(x_j,x_{j-1})) gives

\[
x_{i+1}=b x_i^d+a x_{i-1}+c,
\qquad
H^{-1}(X,Y)=\left(Y,\frac{X-bY^d-c}{a}\right).
\]

Thus every (P\mapsto H^i(P)) is injective. The normalized equation is

\[
\frac{x_{i+1}}c-\frac{b x_i^d}c-\frac{a x_{i-1}}c=1.
\]

The variables ((x_{i+1},x_i^d,x_{i-1})) lie in the direct product
(Gamma^3), whose rank is (3r); (1/c,-b/c,-a/c) remain arbitrary fixed
nonzero coefficients. Published Evertse--Schlickewei--Schmidt Theorem 1.1
therefore gives at most
(exp(18^9(3r+1))) nondegenerate triples at one index. A triple fixes
(x_i^d), so (X^d-x_i^d) leaves at most (d) possible middle coordinates.
Injectivity pulls this local-state bound back to initial states. The union over
the four indices is consequently at most
(4d\exp(18^9(3r+1))).

exactly

\[
A_i:a x_{i-1}+c=0,\qquad
B_i:b x_i^d+c=0,\qquad
C_i:b x_i^d+a x_{i-1}=0.
\]

With (u=x_{i-1}), (v=x_i), and
(alpha=-c/a), independent substitution gives:

### Free-chain closure, words, overlaps, and short periods

On the compatible (BA) branch, (t=x_{i-1}) gives

\[
x_i=\alpha,\quad x_{i+1}=at,\quad x_{i+2}=ba^dt^d.
\]

The third letters (A,B,C) impose respectively (at=\alpha),
(b^{d+1}a^{d^2}t^{d^2}=-c), and
(b^{d+1}a^{d^2}t^{d^2-1}=-a^2). Their counts are at most
(1,d^2,d^2-1).

On the compatible (CB) branch, (bc^{d-1}=-1) and (t=x_i) give

\[
x_{i-1}=-bt^d/a,\quad x_{i+1}=c,\quad x_{i+2}=at.
\]

The (CBB) and (CBC) continuations impose degree-(d) equations. Only
(CBA) remains free, and only when (a=-1). Then

\[
x_{i-1}=bt^d,\quad x_{i+1}=c,\quad x_{i+2}=-t,
\quad x_{i+3}=b(-t)^d,
\]

and the fourth letters impose bounds (1,d^2,d^2-1) for
(CBAA,CBAB,CBAC). Every division is by a nonzero group element.

These cases prove that every word in ({A,B,C}^4) has at most (d^2)
initial states. If two labels hold at one index, the normalized summands are
one of ((-1,1,1),(1,-1,1),(1,1,-1)). Choosing any valid label at every
index covers the point; multiple choices only overcount. Thus the eighty-one
words contribute at most (81d^2). The argument nowhere assumes that the
five states are distinct, so periods (1,2,3,4) require no correction.

### Rank-one sharpness and periodic corollary

For every (d\ge2), choose (c^{d-1}=-1), set

\[
K=\mathbb Q(c),\quad b=1,\quad a=-1,\quad
\Gamma=\langle2,c,-1\rangle,
\]

and take (P_t=(t,t^d)) with (t=2^n). The elements (c) and (-1) are
torsion, while (2) has infinite order, so the group has rank one. Direct
substitution gives

\[
H(P_t)=(c,t),\qquad H^2(P_t)=(-t,c),\qquad
H^3(P_t)=((-t)^d,-t).
\]

All coordinates lie in (Gamma), and the initial points are pairwise
distinct. This proves the stated infinite (T_3) family and realizes the
forward word (CBA).

For the corollary, every point on an exact-period orbit wholly contained in
(Gamma^2) lies in (T_4(H,Gamma)). Distinct exact-period orbits are
disjoint and an exact-period-(n) orbit has (n) points. Consequently
(sum_{n\ge1}nC_n^\Gamma(H)\le\#T_4(H,\Gamma)). The manuscript explicitly
rejects the larger, unauthorized one-representative interpretation.
