## Independent theorem and proof replay

### Exact assumptions and nondegenerate contribution

The manuscript states the theorem for every characteristic-zero field
(K), every integer (d\ge2), arbitrary (a,b,c\in K^\ast), and every
finite-rank subgroup (\Gamma\le K^\ast) of rank (r), without finite
generation or coefficient membership. Its definition with (0\le j\le m)
makes (T_4) a five-state, four-transition window with local indices
(0,1,2,3), and makes (T_3) a four-state, three-transition window.

Writing (H^j(P)=(x_j,x_{j-1})) gives

\[
x_{i+1}=b x_i^d+a x_{i-1}+c,
\qquad
H^{-1}(X,Y)=\left(Y,\frac{X-bY^d-c}{a}\right).
\]

The normalized equation is

\[
\frac{x_{i+1}}c-\frac{b x_i^d}c-\frac{a x_{i-1}}c=1.
\]

The variable triple ((x_{i+1},x_i^d,x_{i-1})) lies in (\Gamma^3), whose
rank is exactly (3r); (1/c,-b/c,-a/c) remain arbitrary fixed nonzero
coefficients. Published Evertse--Schlickewei--Schmidt Theorem 1.1 therefore
gives at most
(\exp(18^9(3r+1))) nondegenerate triples at one index. A triple fixes
(x_i^d), so it has at most (d) local-state lifts, and invertibility pulls
the count back injectively. The union over four indices is at most
(4d\exp(18^9(3r+1))).

### Exhaustive degeneracy and chain closure

All three normalized summands are nonzero. A degenerate solution therefore
has a vanishing two-term proper subsum, exhausted by

\[
A_i:a x_{i-1}+c=0,\qquad
B_i:b x_i^d+c=0,\qquad
C_i:b x_i^d+a x_{i-1}=0.
\]

With (u=x_{i-1}), (v=x_i), and (\alpha=-c/a), direct substitution
reproduces all nine forward transitions:

| Word | Constraint | Bound |
|---|---|---:|
| (AA) | (u=\alpha, v=\alpha) | (1) |
| (AB) | (u=\alpha, b^{d+1}v^{d^2}=-c) | (d^2) |
| (AC) | (u=\alpha, b^{d+1}v^{d^2-1}=-a) | (d^2-1) |
| (BA) | (v=\alpha, b\alpha^d=-c), with (u) free if compatible | free |
| (BB) | (v^d=-c/b, u^d=-c/(ba^d)) | (d^2) |
| (BC) | (v^d=-c/b, u^d=-v/(ba^{d-1})) | (d^2) |
| (CA) | (v=\alpha, u=-b\alpha^d/a) | (1) |
| (CB) | (bc^{d-1}=-1, u=-bv^d/a), with (v) free | free |
| (CC) | (v=-bc^d/a, u=-bv^d/a) | (1) |

Thus only (BA) and (CB) can be free. On the compatible (BA) branch,
(t=x_{i-1}) gives

\[
x_i=\alpha,\quad x_{i+1}=at,\quad x_{i+2}=ba^dt^d,
\]

and (BAA,BAB,BAC) impose equations of degrees at most
(1,d^2,d^2-1). On the compatible (CB) branch,
(bc^{d-1}=-1) and (t=x_i) give

\[
x_{i-1}=-bt^d/a,\quad x_{i+1}=c,\quad x_{i+2}=at.
\]

The (CBB) and (CBC) continuations have degree (d); only (CBA) can
remain free, and only when (a=-1). Then

\[
x_{i-1}=bt^d,\quad x_{i+1}=c,\quad x_{i+2}=-t,\quad
x_{i+3}=b(-t)^d,
\]

and (CBAA,CBAB,CBAC) have bounds (1,d^2,d^2-1). Every division is by
a nonzero group element. Every four-letter word therefore has at most

If two labels hold at one index, the normalized summands are one of

### Sharpness and periodic corollary

For each (d\ge2), the manuscript chooses

\[
c^{d-1}=-1,\quad K=\mathbb Q(c),\quad b=1,\quad a=-1,\quad
\Gamma=\langle2,c,-1\rangle.
\]

The elements (c) and (-1) are torsion and (2) has infinite order, so
(\operatorname{rank}\Gamma=1). For (t=2^n), the pair
(P_t=(t,t^d)) satisfies

\[
H(P_t)=(c,t),\qquad H^2(P_t)=(-t,c),\qquad
H^3(P_t)=((-t)^d,-t).
\]

All coordinates lie in (\Gamma), the points are pairwise distinct, and
the forward degeneracy word is exactly (CBA). This proves the existential
infinite-(T_3) claim through precisely three transitions.

For the periodic corollary, every point on an exact-period orbit wholly

orbits are disjoint, and an exact-period-(n) orbit has (n) points. Hence
(\sum_{n\ge1}nC_n^\Gamma(H)\le\#T_4(H,\Gamma)). The manuscript expressly
rejects the larger one-representative interpretation.
