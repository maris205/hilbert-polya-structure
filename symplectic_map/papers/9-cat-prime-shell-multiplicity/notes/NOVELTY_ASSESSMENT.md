# Novelty Assessment

## Verdict

**GO_SCOPED_NEGATIVE_NOTE_LOW_NOVELTY**.

Safe title:

**A Multiplicity Audit for Prime-Torsion Euler Products of the Cat Map**.

The proposed note is viable as a precise negative audit, not as a claim that
prime-lattice orbit multiplicities, dynamical Euler products, or weighted
zeta functions are new.  Its defensible contribution is the explicit
collision of three already natural structures:

1. complete prime-shell orbit multiplicity for one frozen cat map;
2. the difference between a genuine point-potential return product and a
   globally relabeled orbit product; and
3. a sharp statement that fixed nonzero scalar denominator weights cannot
   remove all repeated multiplicities, while the exact all-orbit counting
   repair is a shell-global fractional normalization that is tautological and
   non-prime-specific.

Overall novelty score: **2.5--3/10**.  The paper should use “audit,” “record,”
“separate,” and “obstruction,” never “discover,” “first,” or “construct the
Riemann dynamics.”

Search cutoff: **2026-08-14 UTC**.  The search covered classical prime-lattice
cat-map dynamics, toral local/global zeta functions, weighted dynamical zeta
products, orbit-counting Dirichlet series, and 2024--2026 cat-map/torsion
work.  No claim of literature completeness is made.

## Primary-source lock

### Prime-lattice and rational-lattice orbit structure

1. **Gregory Gaspari**, “The Arnold cat map on prime lattices,” *Physica D:
   Nonlinear Phenomena* **73**(4), 352--372 (1994),
   DOI [`10.1016/0167-2789(94)90105-8`](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(94)90105-8).
   The publisher abstract states that for every prime $p\ne5$ all nonzero
   points on the prime lattice have a common period, whose form and orbit
   decomposition are determined.  This is the dominant novelty collision and
   must be cited beside the local multiplicity theorem.

2. **Ian Percival and Franco Vivaldi**, “Arithmetical properties of strongly
   chaotic motions,” *Physica D* **25**(1--3), 105--130 (1987),
   DOI [`10.1016/0167-2789(87)90096-0`](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(87)90096-0).
   This is foundational arithmetic cat-map orbit-counting context.  The
   present note claims no priority for connecting quadratic arithmetic to
   cat-map cycles.

3. **Freeman J. Dyson and Harold Falk**, “Period of a Discrete Cat Mapping,”
   *American Mathematical Monthly* **99**(7), 603--614 (1992),
   DOI [`10.1080/00029890.1992.11995900`](https://doi.org/10.1080/00029890.1992.11995900),
   stable JSTOR record [`2324989`](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2324989).
   This is classical discrete-cat period arithmetic, not a source of a new
   Euler-product claim.

4. **Michael Baake, John A. G. Roberts, and Alfred Weiss**, “Periodic orbits
   of linear endomorphisms on the $2$-torus and its lattices,” *Nonlinearity*
   **21**, 2427--2446 (2008),
   DOI [`10.1088/0951-7715/21/10/012`](https://doi.org/10.1088/0951-7715/21/10/012),
   arXiv:[`0808.3489`](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.3489).
   It explicitly compares global and rational-lattice orbit statistics and
   gives finite-lattice Euler products.  Paper 9's prime-shell product is a
   deliberately restricted Riemann-local-factor diagnostic, not a new
   finite-lattice Euler product or a replacement for that theory.

5. **Michael Baake, Natascha Neumärker, and John A. G. Roberts**, “Orbit
   structure and (reversing) symmetries of toral endomorphisms on rational
   lattices,” *Discrete and Continuous Dynamical Systems* **33**(2),
   527--553 (2013),
   DOI [`10.3934/dcds.2013.33.527`](https://doi.org/10.3934/dcds.2013.33.527).
   Appendix A.1 directly supplies cycle-generating polynomials for the
   standard cat map at $p=2$, at the ramified prime $p=5$, and in the other
   prime-power cases.  This is a strong collision, not merely background.
   Paper 9 may re-derive the prime-shell formulas for its audit but must not
   advertise a new cycle classification.  The symmetry analysis also makes a
   centralizer quotient a real later escape, not a mechanism excluded here.

### Dynamical zeta and weighted-orbit formalism

6. **Michael Artin and Barry Mazur**, “On periodic points,” *Annals of
   Mathematics* **81**(1), 82--99 (1965),
   DOI [`10.2307/1970384`](https://doi.org/10.2307/1970384).
   The fixed-point exponential and its primitive-orbit expansion are
   classical.  Paper 9 merely applies that ledger to selected finite shells.

7. **David Ruelle**, “Zeta-functions for expanding maps and Anosov flows,”
   *Inventiones Mathematicae* **34**, 231--242 (1976),
   DOI [`10.1007/BF01403069`](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01403069).
   Weighted dynamical zeta functions and analytic operator machinery are
   prior art.  No new transfer theorem or meromorphic continuation is claimed.

8. **William Parry and Mark Pollicott**, *Zeta Functions and the Periodic
   Orbit Structure of Hyperbolic Dynamics*, Astérisque **187--188** (1990),
   DOI [`10.24033/ast.28`](https://doi.org/10.24033/ast.28).
   This is the primary reference for primitive/repetition ledgers and Hölder
   weights.  The finite denominator-degree obstruction below does not extend
   or compete with their analytic theory.

9. **Michael Baake, Eike Lau, and Vytautas Paskunas**, “A note on the
   dynamical zeta function of general toral endomorphisms,” *Monatshefte für
   Mathematik* **161**(1), 33--42 (2010),
   DOI [`10.1007/s00605-009-0118-y`](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00605-009-0118-y),
   arXiv:[`0810.1855`](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.1855).
   It gives the ordinary toral Artin--Mazur zeta function by integer
   arithmetic.  Paper 9 must not call its prime-shell relabeling “the”
   dynamical zeta function of the cat map.

### 2024--2026 frontier check

10. **Kai Tan and Chengqing Li**, “The Graph Structure of a Class of
    Permutation Maps over Ring $\mathbb Z_{p^k}$,”
    arXiv:[`2506.20118`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20118) (2025 preprint).
    It develops exact cycle-length distributions and lifting over prime-power
    rings, with the cat map as a canonical example.  This makes broad claims
    of new finite-ring cycle analysis untenable.

11. **Aryaman Chandra**, “Arithmetic Landscape Functions of a Discrete Cat
    Map,” arXiv:[`2607.24857`](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24857) (2026
    preprint).  It expresses a finite-torus Green function and the exact
    finite-permutation identity $\det(I-\alpha P)$ as a product over cycles.
    This directly collides with generic orbit-product/determinant packaging.
    Paper 9 does not claim transfer, determinant, Green-function, or
    spectral-landscape novelty.

The 2024 torsion-equidistribution literature on algebraic tori was also
screened.  It concerns Galois distribution and singular test functions, not
the frozen finite-field orbit multiplicity or the scalar Euler-factor degree
obstruction, and is therefore background rather than a direct collision.

## Claim extraction used for the search

The novelty search tested five formulations separately:

1. exact prime-shell orbit counts for the trace-three cat map in
   split/inert/ramified cases;
2. a prime-indexed Euler product over all nonzero $p$-torsion primitive
   orbits;
3. removal of orbit multiplicity by scalar or Hölder orbit weights;
4. global shell normalization by fractional exponents;
5. a canonical one-orbit selection or symmetry quotient.

Search formulations included “cat map prime lattice orbit decomposition,”
“toral automorphism prime torsion Euler product,” “weighted dynamical zeta
Hölder multiplicity,” “finite lattice local zeta orbit count,” and 2024--2026
variants on arXiv and primary publisher records.  No primary source was found
that packaged exactly the proposed A0 decision, but Gaspari resolves most of
the local orbit theorem and the remainder is elementary product algebra.
Absence from a bounded search is not evidence of historical priority.

## Safe positioning and forbidden claims

Use this one-sentence position:

> Classical prime-lattice orbit structure forces a multiplicity exponent in
> the most direct prime-shell orbit product; ordinary nonzero scalar weights
> cannot remove it, and the exact fractional repair is shell-global and
> non-specific.

Mandatory wording boundaries:

- Say **pure scalar denominator orbit-label product with fixed nonzero
  coefficients independent of the local variable**, not every possible
  dynamical or cohomological determinant.
- Say **no canonical selector is supplied by the frozen construction**, not
  that no selector can exist in any enrichment.
- Say **absolute convergence for $\operatorname{Re}s>3$ and failure in the
  safe strip $1<\operatorname{Re}s\le2$**, not an exact abscissa.
- Say **fractional global normalization reproduces the Euler factor
  tautologically**, not that all normalization is impossible.
- State explicitly that matrix-valued factors, numerator or alternating
  cancellation, Fredholm/cohomological superdeterminants, and centralizer
  quotients are not ruled out.
- Say **low-novelty negative audit**, not a new prime-generating dynamical
  system, a Riemann model, a transfer/Fredholm theorem, or a quantization.
