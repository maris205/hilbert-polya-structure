\section{Elliptic jets and endpoint asymptotics}\label{sec:elliptic}

The circle shrinking to the elliptic centre requires a different estimate
from the hyperbolic circles.  Its period stays finite, but smoothness in
the original surface forces the nonzero Fourier amplitudes to vanish.
We construct a time angle in which this vanishing has a smooth expansion
in $\varepsilon=h_--h$.  Throughout this section only the shrinking circle
is integrated; the persistent circle at the same limiting energy is
disjoint from the local domain.

\subsection{A positive symmetric time angle}

Write $s_-=s_{w_-}$ and
$\omega_-=|w_-|\sqrt{3-4w_-}$.  The negative-definite Hessian at $s_-$
and the analytic Morse lemma give a real analytic chart $\mathcal K$ with
\begin{equation}\label{ell:morse-chart}
 h\circ\mathcal K(p,q)=h_--\frac{p^2+q^2}{2},\qquad
 \mathcal K^*\Omega=-b(p,q)\,\dd p\wedge\dd q,
 \quad b>0,\quad b(0,0)=\omega_-^{-1}.
\end{equation}
If necessary, reflecting $q$ gives the stated orientation without
changing the energy.  The value of $b(0,0)$ follows from the linear
Hamiltonian frequency.  For $p=\varrho\cos\varphi$ and
$q=\varrho\sin\varphi$, our convention $\iota_X\Omega=-\dd h$ gives
$X=b^{-1}\partial_\varphi$ and $\dd t=b\,\dd\varphi$.
Thus increasing $\varphi$, not its negative, is the positive time
direction, and $L_0=2\pi/\omega_-$.

Choose a sufficiently small $\varepsilon_0>0$ and put
$\varrho_0=\sqrt{2\varepsilon_0}$.  For the construction alone, allow
$-\varrho_0\leq\varrho\leq\varrho_0$; negative radius does not label an
additional physical circle.  Set
\[
 b_\varrho(\varphi)=b(\varrho\cos\varphi,\varrho\sin\varphi),\qquad
 \bar b(\varrho)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}b_\varrho(\varphi)\,\dd\varphi,
 \qquad \widetilde L(\varrho)=2\pi\bar b(\varrho).
\]
Let $B_\varrho$ be the unique zero-mean periodic primitive of
$b_\varrho-\bar b(\varrho)$.  It is real analytic in $(\varrho,\varphi)$:
substitution into the convergent local power series of $b$ gives finite
trigonometric polynomials in each homogeneous degree, and integration of
their nonzero frequencies preserves normal convergence, with every fixed
finite number of derivatives on a smaller cylinder.  Define the lift
\[
 \Psi_\varrho(\varphi)
   =\varphi+\frac{B_\varrho(\varphi)}{\bar b(\varrho)}.
\]
Its derivative is $b_\varrho/\bar b>0$, and it commutes with translation
by $2\pi$.  The analytic inverse function theorem on a finite cover of
the angle circle therefore gives an analytic inverse lift
$\varphi_\varrho(\psi)$.  The parametrization
\begin{equation}\label{ell:time-angle}
 z(\varrho,\theta)=\mathcal K\bigl(
 \varrho\cos\varphi_\varrho(2\pi\theta),
 \varrho\sin\varphi_\varrho(2\pi\theta)\bigr),\qquad \theta\in\TT,
\end{equation}
is analytic on the full signed-radius cylinder.  On the physical circle
$\varrho=\sqrt{2\varepsilon}$ it satisfies
$\dd t=L(\varepsilon)\,\dd\theta$ and
$\dd\mu=L(\varepsilon)\,\dd\varepsilon\,\dd\theta$, where
$L(\varepsilon)=\widetilde L(\sqrt{2\varepsilon})>0$.
Since $B_0=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{ell:angle-first-jet}
 z(0,\theta)=s_-,\qquad
 \partial_\varrho z(0,\theta)
   =D\mathcal K_0(\cos2\pi\theta,\sin2\pi\theta).
\end{equation}

The zero-mean normalization fixes the symmetry needed below.
Indeed, $b_{-\varrho}(\varphi)=b_\varrho(\varphi+\pi)$ implies
$\bar b(-\varrho)=\bar b(\varrho)$ and, by uniqueness,
$B_{-\varrho}(\varphi)=B_\varrho(\varphi+\pi)$.  Hence
\[
 \Psi_{-\varrho}(\varphi)=\Psi_\varrho(\varphi+\pi)-\pi,
 \qquad
 \varphi_{-\varrho}(\psi)=\varphi_\varrho(\psi+\pi)-\pi,
\]
which proves the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{ell:half-turn}
 z(-\varrho,\theta)=z(\varrho,\theta+1/2).
\end{equation}
An arbitrary energy-dependent choice of angle origin would not by itself
supply this identity.

\subsection{Ambient jets and summable remainders}

All norms below are ordinary finite-order norms in a fixed, slightly
larger compact Morse neighbourhood $K$.  They are equivalent to the
corresponding original-chart norms used in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
For $k\ne0$, write
\[
 \widehat f_k(\varepsilon)=\int_0^1
 f(z(\sqrt{2\varepsilon},\theta))e^{-2\pi\ii k\theta}\,\dd\theta,
 \qquad a_k(\varepsilon)=L(\varepsilon)
 \widehat f_k(\varepsilon)\overline{\widehat g_k(\varepsilon)}.
\]
These are the Fourier coefficients of the original functions; subtracting
$\Pi$ removes only $k=0$ and requires no assertion about the original
smoothness of $f-\Pi f$.

\begin{proposition}[Original centre jets]\label{prop:elliptic-jets}
For original smooth $f,g$, the amplitudes have expansions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jet-amplitudes}
 a_k(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon A_k+\varepsilon^2R_k(\varepsilon),
 \qquad
 A_k=2L_0d_{f,k}\overline{d_{g,k}},\qquad k\ne0,
\end{equation}
where $R_k$ is smooth on the closed half-interval and $A_k=0$ for
$|k|\ne1$.  If
$f\circ\mathcal K=f(s_-)+a_fp+b_fq+O(p^2+q^2)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{ell:first-mode-jets}
 d_{f,1}=\frac{a_f-\ii b_f}{2},\qquad
 d_{f,-1}=\frac{a_f+\ii b_f}{2},\qquad d_{f,k}=0\quad(|k|\ne1).
\end{equation}
For all integers $J,S\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elliptic-remainder-norm}
 \sup_{k\ne0}(1+|k|)^S
 \|R_k\|_{C^J([0,\varepsilon_0])}
 \leq C_{J,S}
 \|f\|_{C^{2J+4+S}(K)}\|g\|_{C^{2J+4+S}(K)}.
\end{equation}
The constants depend on the fixed parameter and chart, not on $k$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the signed radius to define
$f_k(\varrho)=\int_0^1f(z(\varrho,\theta))e^{-2\pi\ii k\theta}\,\dd\theta$.
Equation~\eqref{ell:half-turn} gives
\[
 f_k(-\varrho)=(-1)^kf_k(\varrho),\qquad f_k(0)=0\quad(k\ne0).
\]
The finite chain rule on the fixed cylinder and periodic integration by
parts give, for all integers $u,S\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{ell:radial-fourier-norm}
 \|f_k\|_{C^u([-\varrho_0,\varrho_0])}
 \leq C_{u,S}(1+|k|)^{-S}\|f\|_{C^{u+S}(K)}.
\end{equation}
For $S=0$ this follows directly from the integral; for $S>0$ move $S$
angle derivatives onto the composed function.  As $\widetilde L$ is
positive, even and analytic,
$\widetilde a_k(\varrho)=\widetilde L(\varrho)
f_k(\varrho)\overline{g_k(\varrho)}$ is even and vanishes to order at
least two at zero.

We use a finite-norm form of smooth even descent.  If $v$ is even and
$C^{2J}$, then $V(\varepsilon)=v(\sqrt{2\varepsilon})$ is $C^J$ up to
zero, with norm bounded by a constant times $\|v\|_{C^{2J}}$.
For the first derivative the identity
\begin{equation}\label{ell:even-descent}
 \frac1\varrho v'(\varrho)=\int_0^1v''(t\varrho)\,\dd t
\end{equation}
follows from $v'(0)=0$.  Repeated application of
$\varrho^{-1}\partial_\varrho$ expresses its $j$th iterate as multiple
integrals of $v^{(2j)}$ with bounded weights: at each step the next odd
derivative vanishes at zero and the same identity applies again.
These integrals extend continuously to zero.  Since
$\partial_\varepsilon=\varrho^{-1}\partial_\varrho$, they prove the
claim and its norm bound for $0\leq j\leq J$.

Let $d_{f,k}=f_k'(0)$.  Subtracting the quadratic Taylor coefficient
from $\widetilde a_k$ leaves an even function vanishing to order four.
The integral remainder formula therefore yields
\[
 \widetilde a_k(\varrho)
 =\varrho^2L_0d_{f,k}\overline{d_{g,k}}
      +\varrho^4q_k(\varrho),\qquad
 \|q_k\|_{C^{2J}}
 \leq C_J\|\widetilde a_k\|_{C^{2J+4}},
\]
where $q_k$ is smooth and even.  In particular, the apparent division by
$\varrho^4$ is justified at zero by the fourth-order integral remainder,
not merely by formal parity.  Applying~\eqref{ell:even-descent} to
$q_k$ gives~\eqref{eq:jet-amplitudes} with
$R_k(\varepsilon)=4q_k(\sqrt{2\varepsilon})$.
The product rule and~\eqref{ell:radial-fourier-norm} prove
\eqref{eq:elliptic-remainder-norm}; $S$ angle derivatives on one Fourier
factor suffice for the stated weight, so $2J+4+S$ original derivatives
on each function are sufficient.  Finally,
\eqref{ell:angle-first-jet} gives~\eqref{ell:first-mode-jets} and
$d_{f,k}=0$ for $|k|\ne1$.
\end{proof}

The two possible leading coefficients are consequently
\[
 A_1=\frac{L_0}{2}(a_f-\ii b_f)
                  (\overline{a_g}+\ii\overline{b_g}),\qquad
 A_{-1}=\frac{L_0}{2}(a_f+\ii b_f)
                  (\overline{a_g}-\ii\overline{b_g}).
\]
They determine only the first energy coefficient.  All higher modes and
the higher-order parts of the two first modes remain in $R_k$.
Replacing $f$ by $f_r=f\circ F_T^r$ gives the coefficients
$A_k^{(r)}$ in~\eqref{eq:center-coefficients}; the original parity
relation is treated together with the other circles in
Section~\ref{sec:global}.

\subsection{The nonstationary endpoint}

Let $\chi\in C_c^\infty([0,\varepsilon_0))$ equal one near zero; this
means smoothness on the closed half-axis and vanishing before the right
endpoint.  With $\Gamma_\varepsilon$ the shrinking circle, define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal E_m(f,g)
 &=\int_0^{\varepsilon_0}\chi(\varepsilon)
   \int_{\Gamma_\varepsilon}
   ((I-\Pi)f)(G_T^mz)\,
   \overline{((I-\Pi)g)(z)}\,\dd t\,\dd\varepsilon
   \notag\\
 &=\sum_{k\ne0}\int_0^{\varepsilon_0}
   \chi(\varepsilon)a_k(\varepsilon)
   e^{2\pi\ii km\sigma_E(\varepsilon)}\,\dd\varepsilon,
 \qquad m\geq1.\label{ell:fourier-integral}
\end{align}
The first equality uses positive time and the measure in
Lemma~\ref{lem:coarea-projection}.  For the second, $G_T$ translates this
angle by $\sigma_E(\varepsilon)=2\rho_-(h_--\varepsilon)$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:elliptic-jets} with $J=0,S=2$ gives an integrable
absolute majorant for the entire sum, justifying the Fourier identity
and Fubini without a finite-mode truncation.  Put
$\sigma_c=\sigma_E(0)=2\theta_T$.  The centre phase jets
\eqref{eq:center-phase-jets} give $\beta=\sigma_E'(0)=-2\rho_-'(h_-)$,
which is nonzero unless $T=3/16$; at that parameter
$\gamma=\sigma_E''(0)=2\rho_-''(h_-)<0$.

\begin{proposition}[The two elliptic endpoint terms]\label{prop:elliptic-endpoints}
For a sufficiently small fixed cutoff, if $T\ne3/16$ then
\begin{equation}\label{ell:ordinary-endpoint}
 \mathcal E_m(f,g)
 =-\frac1{m^2}\sum_{k=\pm1}
   \frac{A_ke^{2\pi\ii km\sigma_c}}{(2\pi k\beta)^2}
   +O\bigl(m^{-3}\|f\|_{C^{20}(K)}\|g\|_{C^{20}(K)}\bigr).
\end{equation}
If $T=3/16$, then
\begin{equation}\label{ell:quadratic-endpoint}
 \mathcal E_m(f,g)
 =\frac1m\sum_{k=\pm1}
   \frac{\ii A_ke^{2\pi\ii km\sigma_c}}{2\pi k\gamma}
   +O\bigl(m^{-3/2}\|f\|_{C^{20}(K)}\|g\|_{C^{20}(K)}\bigr).
\end{equation}
The constants may depend on $T,K,\chi$.  Both remainders include all
nonzero Fourier modes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of~\eqref{ell:ordinary-endpoint}]
Shrink the cutoff so that $\sigma_E'$ has no zero on its whole support.
Set $s_\beta=\sgn\beta$ and
$x=s_\beta(\sigma_E(\varepsilon)-\sigma_c)$.  This is an increasing
analytic coordinate with inverse $\varepsilon=E(x)$,
$E(0)=0$ and $E'(0)=|\beta|^{-1}$.  The transformed amplitude
\[
 b_k(x)=\chi(E(x))a_k(E(x))E'(x)
\]
is smooth and compactly supported on the closed half-axis, with
$b_k(0)=0$ and $b_k'(0)=A_k/\beta^2$.  For every real $\omega\ne0$,
three integrations by parts give
\begin{equation}\label{ell:three-ibp}
 \int_0^\infty b_k(x)e^{\ii\omega x}\,\dd x
 =-\frac{b_k'(0)}{\omega^2}+E_k(\omega),\qquad
 |E_k(\omega)|\leq
 \frac{|b_k''(0)|+\|b_k'''\|_{L^1}}{|\omega|^3}.
\end{equation}
All right-end boundary terms vanish by compact support.  The fixed
coordinate change gives
\[
 |b_k''(0)|+\|b_k'''\|_{L^1}
 \leq C\bigl(|A_k|+\|R_k\|_{C^3}\bigr).
\]
Taking $\omega=2\pi km s_\beta$ and restoring
$e^{2\pi\ii km\sigma_c}$ proves the stated negative coefficient,
independently of the sign of $\beta$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:elliptic-remainder-norm} with $J=3,S=2$ makes the
remainders summable over all $k$ and uses only original $C^{12}$ norms.
Thus the common $C^{20}$ bound in the statement is sufficient.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The quadratic endpoint at \texorpdfstring{$T=3/16$}{T=3/16}}

\begin{proof}[Proof of~\eqref{ell:quadratic-endpoint}]
Here $\sigma_E'(0)=0$ and $\gamma<0$.  The positive extension of
\[
 x=\sqrt{\frac{2(\sigma_E(\varepsilon)-\sigma_c)}{\gamma}}
   =\varepsilon(1+O(\varepsilon))
\]
is an increasing analytic coordinate after shrinking the fixed
neighbourhood.  Its inverse $E$ has $E'(0)=1$, and the phase is exactly
$\sigma_c+\gamma x^2/2$.  Write the transformed amplitude as
\[
 \chi(E(x))a_k(E(x))E'(x)=x c_k(x).
\]
The function $c_k$ is smooth and compactly supported,
$c_k(0)=A_k$, and finite-order division and the fixed change of variable
give
\begin{equation}\label{ell:quadratic-amplitude-bound}
 \|c_k'\|_\infty+\|c_k''\|_{L^1}
 \leq C\bigl(|A_k|+\|R_k\|_{C^2}\bigr).
\end{equation}
With $\omega=2\pi km\gamma$, an integration by parts with this actual
compactly supported amplitude gives
\begin{equation}\label{ell:quadratic-ibp}
 \int_0^\infty xc_k(x)e^{\ii\omega x^2/2}\,\dd x
 =\frac{\ii A_k}{\omega}
  -\frac1{\ii\omega}\int_0^\infty
        c_k'(x)e^{\ii\omega x^2/2}\,\dd x.
\end{equation}
Only the value $c_k(0)$ contributes at the boundary.

For completeness, let $d$ be compactly supported and $C^1$, and split
its oscillatory integral at $\delta=|\omega|^{-1/2}$.
The part on $[0,\delta]$ is at most
$\delta\|d\|_\infty$.  On $[\delta,\infty)$ use
$\partial_xe^{\ii\omega x^2/2}=\ii\omega x e^{\ii\omega x^2/2}$.
The boundary and derivative terms are bounded by
\[
 \frac{\|d\|_\infty}{|\omega|\delta}
 +\frac1{|\omega|}\int_\delta^\infty
      \left(\frac{|d'|}{x}+\frac{|d|}{x^2}\right)\,\dd x
 \leq |\omega|^{-1/2}
       \bigl(2\|d\|_\infty+\|d'\|_{L^1}\bigr).
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{ell:quadratic-remainder}
 \left|\int_0^\infty d(x)e^{\ii\omega x^2/2}\,\dd x\right|
 \leq |\omega|^{-1/2}
       \bigl(3\|d\|_\infty+\|d'\|_{L^1}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Apply this with $d=c_k'$ in~\eqref{ell:quadratic-ibp}.  The resulting
single-mode error is at most
$C|km|^{-3/2}(|A_k|+\|R_k\|_{C^2})$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:elliptic-remainder-norm} with $J=2,S=2$ sums these
errors absolutely using original $C^{10}$ norms, and hence also the
stated $C^{20}$ norms.  Restoring the constant phase gives
\eqref{ell:quadratic-endpoint}.  This argument never treats the
uncut integral of $xe^{\ii\omega x^2/2}$ as a convergent ordinary
improper integral.
\end{proof}

The $m^{-1}$ term comes from the shrinking circle alone.  At $T=3/16$
the persistent circle at $h_-$ is a separate, two-sided nondegenerate
stationary circle, and contributes at order $m^{-1/2}$.  The invariant
partition in Section~\ref{sec:global} includes it once and combines the
two contributions without changing the original observable class.
