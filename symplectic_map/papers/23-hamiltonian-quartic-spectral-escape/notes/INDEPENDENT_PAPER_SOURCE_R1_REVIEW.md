### 5.8 Perron pairing, modulo five, and pairwise distinctness

Every entry of \(C_g\) is strictly positive for \(g\geq10\), so it is

\[

Therefore \(\lambda_1(F_g)=\rho(C_g)\). This is not an entropy claim.

For \(g\equiv3\pmod5\), direct coefficient reduction gives

\[
\overline R_g(t)=f(t)=t^4-2t^3-t^2+1.
\]

Its values at \(0,1,2,3,4\) are \((1,4,2,4,3)\), so it has no linear
factor. If

\[

\]

coefficient comparison gives

\[
a+c=3,\quad ac+b+d=4,\quad ad+bc=0,\quad bd=1.
\]

The four ordered constant pairs are
\((1,1),(2,3),(3,2),(4,4)\). For equal constants,
\(ad+bc=b(a+c)=3b\neq0\). For either unequal inverse pair, the first and
third equations force \(a=c=4\), after which
\(ac+b+d=1\neq4\). Thus no quadratic factor exists, and the monic quartic
is irreducible over \(\mathbb F_5\).

\(-(4g+10)\), forcing the parameters equal. This proves precisely the
certified residue-class, pairwise-distinct quartic Perron result and no
all-parameter irreducibility claim.
