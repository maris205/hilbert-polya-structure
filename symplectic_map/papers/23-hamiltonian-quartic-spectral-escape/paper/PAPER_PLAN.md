## Frozen mathematical object

### Field, parameter, potentials, and maps

Let \(K\) be an arbitrary field of characteristic zero and let \(g\ge10\) be an integer. No algebraic-closedness assumption is used. For

\[
q=(q_1,q_2,q_3,q_4),\qquad p=(p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4),
\]

define

\[
V_g(q)=q_1^2q_2^2q_3^2q_4^2+q_1^g+q_2^{g-1},
\]

\[
W_g(p)=p_1^2p_2^2p_3^2p_4^2+p_3^{g-1}+p_4^g.
\]

The only theorem family is

\[
S_g^+(q,p)=(q,p+\nabla V_g(q)),\qquad
T_g^+(q,p)=(q+\nabla W_g(p),p),
\]

\[
F_g=T_g^+\circ S_g^+.
\]

The subtraction shears appear only as inverse formulas. They are not alternate forward families.

### Phase matrices

Literal gradient supports and the four strict pure-branch choices give

\[
A_g=
\begin{pmatrix}
g-1&0&0&0\\
0&g-2&0&0\\
2&2&1&2\\
2&2&2&1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B_g=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2&2&2\\
2&1&2&2\\
0&0&g-2&0\\
0&0&0&g-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Because \(S_g^+\) acts first, the complete-step matrix is

\[

\begin{pmatrix}

2g+6&g+6&6&6\\
2g-4&2g-4&g-2&2g-4\\
2g-2&2g-2&2g-2&g-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The phase order \(B_gA_g\) is immutable.

### Exact degree and spectral conclusions

With \(\mathbf1=(1,1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}\), the actual phase degrees are

\[
u_n=C_g^n\mathbf1\quad(n\ge0),\qquad
v_n=A_gC_g^{n-1}\mathbf1\quad(n\ge1).
\]

For every \(n\ge1\), the fourth \(q\)-coordinate is uniquely maximal among all eight coordinate degrees. At \(n=0\), all eight degrees tie. Therefore

\[
\deg(F_g^n)=e_4^{\mathsf T}C_g^n\mathbf1\quad(n\ge0),
\qquad
\lambda_1(F_g)=\rho(C_g).
\]

The exact characteristic polynomial is

\[
\begin{aligned}
R_g(t)={}&t^4-(4g+10)t^3+(-2g^2-26g+45)t^2\\
&+(12g^3-70g^2+126g-72)t
+9(g-1)^2(g-2)^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Its determinant term and value at one are

\[
\det C_g=9(g-1)^2(g-2)^2,
\]

\[
R_g(1)=3g(g-1)(3g^2-11g+4)>0.
\]

For \(d_n=e_4^{\mathsf T}C_g^n\mathbf1\), Cayley--Hamilton gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_{n+4}={}&(4g+10)d_{n+3}+(2g^2+26g-45)d_{n+2}\\
&-(12g^3-70g^2+126g-72)d_{n+1}\\
&-9(g-1)^2(g-2)^2d_n.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(g\equiv3\pmod5\),

\[
R_g(t)\bmod5=t^4-2t^3-t^2+1
\]

is irreducible over \(\mathbf F_5\). Thus \(g=13,18,23,\ldots\) gives pairwise distinct quartic Perron first dynamical degrees.

At \(g=9\), the seed selector margins are

\[
(1,0,-2,5),
\]

and \(y+z=2=(g-5)/2\). This proves failure only for the ordinary seed and the displayed four-face itinerary.
