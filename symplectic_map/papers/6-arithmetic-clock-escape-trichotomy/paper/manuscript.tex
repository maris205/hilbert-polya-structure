\begin{abstract}
We study a fixed additive periodic-orbit readout whose exact prime hits admit
representations
\[
 \log p=v_p+\log q_p+\alpha_p,
\]
where the $v_p$ lie in one finite-dimensional rational space $V$, each
$q_p>0$ is algebraic and $q_p^2$ is a unit away from one finite rational-prime
set $\SQ$, and each $\alpha_p$ is real algebraic.  We prove the rank-plus-support
capacity bound $|\Phit|\leq\dimQ V+|\SQ|$.  A rational dependence among outside-support
$v_p$ terms produces $\log R=\beta$ with $R>0$ algebraic and $\beta$
algebraic.  Hermite--Lindemann forces $R=1$; after squaring, a finite-place
valuation above each distinct outside prime forces its relation coefficient
to vanish.  The proof requires neither a prior finiteness assumption nor a
target search.

We give source certificates for fixed finite-memory locally constant
readouts, good-reduction generalized H\'enon multiplier moduli, and regularly
evaluated algebraic exact actions.  A selector architecture is a corollary of
the additive theorem.  A source-locked exact/static audit closes nine
proof, scope, control, isolation, review, and upstream gates; its single
registered run has 51 passing tests, zero numerical candidate runs, and zero
target matches.  The result is an exact capacity certificate for the declared
class, not a universal symplectic no-go theorem, a complete escape trichotomy,
or a Riemann-zero construction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Prime logarithms arise as desired periodic scales in several arithmetic and
spectral analogies.  Berry and Keating describe a hypothetical chaotic
``Riemann dynamics'' whose primitive periods are tied to logarithms of primes
\citep{berry_keating_1999}.  Deninger's foliated and arithmetic dynamical
programs assign length $\log Nx$ to closed-point packets
\citep{deninger_1998,deninger_2026}; the scaling-site and ad\`ele-class-space
program of Connes and Consani contains prime-indexed periodic orbits of length
$\log p$ \citep{connes_consani_2016,connes_consani_2024}.  These works motivate
the scale, but they also warn against a universal obstruction: arithmetic
spaces can carry the primes intrinsically.

We ask a narrower design question.  Suppose a finite architecture combines
three familiar sources of periodic observables: a finite-rank locally constant
term, logarithms of multiplier moduli with one fixed finite bad-prime support,
and an algebraically evaluated action term.  How many distinct rational-prime
logarithms can the resulting \emph{additive} readout equal exactly when all
maps, supports, coefficients, and normalizations are fixed before the target
is inspected?

The answer is a rank-plus-support bound.  If every hit prime satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \log p=v_p+\log q_p+\alpha_p,
 \qquad v_p\in V,\quad q_p^2\in\cO_{\SQ}^{\times},
 \quad \alpha_p\in\Qbar\cap\R,
 \label{eq:intro-normal-form}
\end{equation}
with $q_p\in\Qbar\cap\R_{>0}$, then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{|\Phit|\leq\dimQ V+|\SQ|.}
 \label{eq:intro-bound}
\end{equation}
Here $\cO_{\SQ}^{\times}$ is shorthand for zero valuation at all finite
places not above $\SQ$; the definition is made field-independent below.

Our contributions are four precise statements.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We prove the additive bound \eqref{eq:intro-bound}, including
  rational and negative multiplier powers, arbitrary valid certificate
  selection, repeated-hit set semantics, and the $q=1$ edge case.
  \item We supply complete source certificates: higher-block recoding for
  locally constant readouts; a projective, non-Archimedean, and monodromy
  argument for good-reduction generalized H\'enon maps; and regular algebraic
  action evaluation with a separate gauge-invariance ledger.
  \item We demote the earlier selector union to a corollary and state the
  escape map only as necessary failures of this certificate.  The exits are
  neither exclusive, exhaustive, nor sufficient for arithmetic provenance.
  \item We bind the theorem and scope to a registered exact/static audit.
  Nine gates pass, 20 proof IDs and 19 scope-operation IDs close, and 51
  official tests pass without reading target data or executing a numerical
  candidate.
\end{enumerate}

The scope restriction is substantive.  We do not treat an algebraic
irrational coefficient on a multiplier logarithm, an arbitrary nonlinear
mixer, a logarithm applied after an algebraic action, a general H\"older roof,
a complex logarithm branch, or approximate equality.  We make no historical
priority claim and no claim about zeta zeros, trace formulas, determinants,
quantization, or ``Route B.''

\section{Arithmetic and dynamical context}
\label{sec:context}

\paragraph{Symbolic periods.}
Shifts of finite type, higher-block presentations, suspension flows, and
periodic-orbit zeta functions provide the standard finite-state setting for
locally constant roofs \citep{parry_pollicott_1990,lind_marcus_1995}.  Our
Class-L statement uses only an elementary consequence: a fixed finite-memory
observable becomes an edge function on a finite higher-block graph.  We do
not assert finite rational rank for a general point-dependent H\"older roof.

\paragraph{Arithmetic H\'enon dynamics and multipliers.}
Plane polynomial automorphisms decompose into elementary and generalized
H\'enon pieces \citep{friedland_milnor_1989}.  Arithmetic studies develop
heights, periodic points, and local behavior for H\'enon maps
\citep{silverman_1994,ingram_2014,hsia_kawaguchi_2018}.  Recent multiplier
work studies complex rigidity and thermodynamic geometry
\citep{cantat_dujardin_2026,bianchi_he_2026}.  Our Class-M certificate does
not claim novelty for that broad literature.  It isolates the narrower fact
that a frozen good-reduction architecture puts squared return moduli in an
$S$-unit group with one fixed rational-prime support.

\paragraph{Exact action readouts.}
Generating potentials and action sums are standard tools for exact symplectic
maps and variational dynamics \citep{delshams_ramirezros_1997,
bialy_tsodikovich_2023}.  In the present certificate, the operative arithmetic
fact is regular evaluation: a rational potential over $\Qbar$ evaluated on an
algebraic finite orbit gives an algebraic sum.  A full endpoint ledger is
needed to distinguish preservation of algebraicity from canonical gauge
invariance.

\paragraph{Transcendence and arithmetic spaces.}
The only transcendence input is Hermite--Lindemann, in the form that
$e^\beta$ is transcendental for nonzero algebraic $\beta$
\citep{baker_2022}.  Standard valuation and extension facts are used in their
usual number-field form \citep{neukirch_1999}.  Conversely, Deninger's
infinite-dimensional systems and the Connes--Consani scaling/ad\`ele spaces
are positive architectures outside the finite L/M/A class.  Connes's
trace-formula program supplies wider arithmetic-spectral context
\citep{connes_1999}, but no trace-formula or zero-spectral conclusion is
drawn here.

\section{The additive certificate and its closure}
\label{sec:setup}

Fix a finite set $\SQ$ of rational primes.  For a nonzero algebraic number
$x$, call $x$ an \emph{$\SQ$-unit} if, in a number field containing $x$,
\begin{equation}
 v_w(x)=0
 \quad\text{for every finite place $w$ not lying above $\SQ$.}
 \label{eq:sunit}
\end{equation}
We saturate the exceptional places above $\SQ$.  The definition is unchanged
after finite extension.

\begin{lemma}[Extension invariance]
\label{lem:extension}
Let $E/K$ be a finite extension of number fields and $x\in K^\times$.
If $x$ has zero valuation at every finite place of $K$ away from $\SQ$, then
the same holds at every finite place of $E$ away from $\SQ$.  Multiplication
and inversion preserve the property.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $w$ lies above $v$, normalized additive valuations satisfy
$v_w(x)=e(w/v)v_v(x)$ up to the common positive normalization convention.
A place $w$ away from $\SQ$ lies above a place $v$ away from $\SQ$, so both
sides vanish.  The product and inverse statements follow from additivity.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Canonical additive readout]
\label{def:readout}
Fix a finite-dimensional $\Q$-subspace $V\subset\R$, the set $\SQ$, and all
maps, coefficients, supports, gauges, and assembly operations independently
of the eventual target.  An in-scope readout is
\begin{equation}
 L=v+\log q+\alpha,
 \qquad v\in V,\quad q\in\Qbar\cap\R_{>0},\quad
 q^2\text{ an $\SQ$-unit},\quad \alpha\in\Qbar\cap\R,
 \label{eq:canonical}
\end{equation}
where $\log$ is the real logarithm on positive inputs.
\end{definition}

The target-independence clause is a provenance condition excluding preloaded
lookup data.  Once valid representations are supplied, the proof below uses
only the common spaces $V$ and $\SQ$ and the stated algebraicity and unit
conditions; it does not use the mechanism by which provenance was certified.

\begin{lemma}[Rational multiplier-log closure]
\label{lem:rational-closure}
Let $q_1,\ldots,q_t>0$ be algebraic, with every $q_j^2$ an $\SQ$-unit, and
let $c_j\in\Q$.  Then
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^t c_j\log q_j=\log q
\]
for a positive algebraic $q$ whose square is an $\SQ$-unit.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $D\geq1$ with $m_j=Dc_j\in\Z$ and set
$q=\prod_j q_j^{m_j/D}$, using the unique positive real algebraic roots in a
finite extension.  The real logarithm gives the stated equality.  Moreover
$(q^2)^D=\prod_j(q_j^2)^{m_j}$.  At a place outside $\SQ$, the right side has
zero valuation, hence $D v_w(q^2)=0$.  Negative exponents are harmless because
the unit group is closed under inversion.
\end{proof}

Thus the class admits finite rational sums, differences, repetitions, and
rational scalings of L terms; rational sums of positive M logarithms; negative
and rational M powers; $q=1$; and finite algebraic sums/scales of real
algebraic A terms.  It excludes algebraic irrational M-log coefficients,
nonlinear post-assembly maps, target/orbit lookup tables, log-after-action,
general H\"older roofs without a common finite-rank proof, complex-log
branches, approximate matching, and target-dependent support or weights.

\section{Finite arithmetic capacity}
\label{sec:theorem}

Let $\Phit$ denote a set of \emph{distinct} positive rational primes.
Repeated orbit hits and repeated certificates do not change its cardinality.

\begin{theorem}[Additive finite arithmetic capacity]
\label{thm:capacity}
Suppose every $p\in\Phit$ has at least one representation
\begin{equation}
 \log p=v_p+\log q_p+\alpha_p
 \label{eq:hit}
\end{equation}
within \cref{def:readout}.  Select one arbitrary valid representation for
each distinct prime before testing any relation.  Then
\begin{equation}
 |\Phit|\leq \dimQ V+|\SQ|.
 \label{eq:main-bound}
\end{equation}
No prior finiteness assumption on $\Phit$ is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $\PP_0=\Phit\setminus\SQ$.  We prove that the selected family
$\{v_p:p\in\PP_0\}$ is linearly independent over $\Q$.

Take distinct $p_1,\ldots,p_k\in\PP_0$ and suppose
$\sum_i c_i v_{p_i}=0$ with $c_i\in\Q$.  Clearing denominators produces
integers $m_i$, not all zero, such that
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{i=1}^k m_i v_{p_i}=0.
 \label{eq:relation}
\end{equation}
Substitution of \eqref{eq:hit} into \eqref{eq:relation}, using positivity and
the real logarithm law even for negative powers, gives
\begin{equation}
 \log R=\beta,
 \quad
 R=\frac{\prod_i p_i^{m_i}}{\prod_iq_{p_i}^{m_i}}
   \in\Qbar\cap\R_{>0},
 \quad
 \beta=\sum_i m_i\alpha_{p_i}\in\Qbar\cap\R.
 \label{eq:logR}
\end{equation}
If $\beta\neq0$, Hermite--Lindemann makes $e^\beta$ transcendental, contrary
to $e^\beta=R\in\Qbar$.  Hence $\beta=0$, and injectivity of the real
exponential gives $R=1$.

Square this algebraic identity before taking valuations:
\begin{equation}
 \prod_{i=1}^k p_i^{2m_i}
 =\prod_{i=1}^k(q_{p_i}^2)^{m_i}.
 \label{eq:squared}
\end{equation}
Put the finitely many factors into one number field $E$.
\Cref{lem:extension} preserves the $\SQ$-unit condition.  Fix $i$ and take a
finite place $w$ of $E$ above $p_i$.  The right side of
\eqref{eq:squared} has zero $w$-valuation because $p_i\notin\SQ$.
For $j\neq i$, the distinct rational prime $p_j$ also has zero
$w$-valuation, while $v_w(p_i)>0$.  Therefore
$2m_i v_w(p_i)=0$ and $m_i=0$.  This holds for every $i$, contradicting a
nonzero relation.

Every finite subfamily of the outside $v_p$ terms is independent.  A
$\Q$-space of dimension $r$ cannot contain $r+1$ independent vectors, so
$|\PP_0|\leq r$.  At most $|\SQ|$ distinct hit primes lie inside $\SQ$,
which proves \eqref{eq:main-bound}.  If $\PP_0$ were infinite, choosing
$r+1$ elements would already give the same contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Edge cases and abstract attainability]
If $V=\{0\}$, no outside-support prime can be hit.  The case $q_p=1$ simply
adds zero.  The rank contribution is abstractly attainable: a graph with $r$
deliberately inserted independent labels realizes $r$ formal labels.  This
shows capacity of the bare rank hypothesis only.  It is target injection, not
arithmetic emergence or an intrinsic-provenance optimality result.  Likewise, the
fixed H\'enon control with $a=-15/16$ and fixed point $(5/4,5/4)$ has exact
multiplier moduli $2$ and $1/2$ only because the denominator has already put
the prime $2$ in the bad support.
\end{remark}

\section{Three source certificates}
\label{sec:sources}

\subsection{Class L: finite memory gives finite rank}

Let a fixed finite-memory observable depend on $m$ consecutive symbols of a
finite-state shift.  Pass to the higher-block graph whose vertices are
admissible $(m-1)$-blocks and whose edges are admissible $m$-blocks.  The
periodic sum becomes an integer combination of finitely many edge values.
A finite collection of such architectures therefore lies in one fixed
finite-dimensional rational span $V$.  The edge values need not be
algebraic.  This certificate does not cover arbitrary H\"older roofs.

\subsection{Class M: good reduction gives fixed support}
\label{subsec:M}

Consider a finite composition of monic area-preserving generalized H\'enon
factors of degrees at least two over an $S$-integer ring.  Expanding a finite
periodic orbit into the individual factors gives a cyclic scalar recurrence
\begin{equation}
 z_{j+1}+z_{j-1}=P_{i_j}(z_j).
 \label{eq:henon-recurrence}
\end{equation}
Cyclic neighbor occurrences retain multiplicity: for a one-step cycle, the
left side is $2z_0$.

Homogenize the $j$th equation to its own degree $d_{i_j}$.  On the common
hyperplane at infinity, monicity leaves $z_j^{d_{i_j}}=0$ for every $j$;
there is no projective point at infinity.  After base change to an algebraic
closure, the cyclic projective solution scheme therefore lies in an affine
chart and is both proper and affine.  A finite-type affine scheme proper over
a field is finite.  Finiteness descends through the field extension, so the
cyclic scheme is finite, with zero-dimensional support even when it is
nonreduced, and all periodic coordinates are algebraic.

At a non-Archimedean place of good reduction, suppose a cyclic coordinate has
maximum norm $R>1$.  At an index attaining the maximum, the monic leading
term is uniquely dominant, so
$|P_{i_j}(z_j)|=R^{d_{i_j}}>R$.  The recurrence gives the contrary bound
$|P_{i_j}(z_j)|\leq R$.  Hence every periodic coordinate is integral away
from the fixed bad places.

The derivative factors and their inverses lie in $\SL_2$ of the integral
closure there.  Return eigenvalues are therefore units.  Put the orbit and
eigenvalue in a common normal extension and saturate every place above
$\SQ$.  Complex conjugation preserves the complement, so for
$q=|\lambda|>0$,
\begin{equation}
 q^2=\lambda\overline\lambda
 \label{eq:modulus-unit}
\end{equation}
is an $\SQ$-unit.  No step identifies $\overline\lambda$ with
$\lambda^{-1}$, and no claim is made that $\log q$ is algebraic.

\subsection{Class A: algebraic action with a separate gauge ledger}

Let an algebraic exact-symplectic map have a single-valued
$\Qbar$-rational potential $G$, and let all evaluations on an algebraic
periodic orbit be regular.  Every value $G(P_j)$ and hence every finite
action $\sum_jG(P_j)$ is algebraic.  Declared algebraic sums, scales,
averages, repetitions, real parts, imaginary parts, and moduli remain real
algebraic.

Under a stepwise algebraic exact gauge,
\begin{equation}
 G'_j=G_j+\chi_{j+1}\circ F_j-\chi_j+C_j,
\end{equation}
the action changes by the full endpoint term
\begin{equation}
 \chi_n(P_n)-\chi_0(P_0)+\sum_jC_j.
 \label{eq:gauge}
\end{equation}
Algebraic endpoint shifts preserve algebraicity even when they do not cancel;
endpoint compatibility is separately required for canonical gauge
invariance.  Poles, multivalued logarithmic gauges, closed non-exact
primitive changes, transcendental constants, and log-after-action lie outside
this source certificate.

\subsection{Selector corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Selector L/M/A architecture]
\label{cor:selector}
If a realized target is supplied by one L, M, or A component, embed it in
\eqref{eq:canonical} as
\[
 (v,q,\alpha)=(\ell_L,1,0),\qquad
 (0,|\lambda|,0),\qquad
 (0,1,\cA),
\]
respectively.  Then \eqref{eq:main-bound} applies.
\end{corollary}

\section{Capacity boundary and positive outside architectures}
\label{sec:boundary}

\begin{corollary}[Necessary certificate escapes]
\label{cor:escapes}
An exact construction realizing more than $\dimQ V+|\SQ|$ distinct rational
primes cannot retain all hypotheses of \cref{def:readout}.  It must lose the
common finite-rank space, fixed finite support, real-algebraic action term, or
rational additive normal form.
\end{corollary}

This is contraposition within one declared certificate.  The four failures
may overlap, need not cover arbitrary dynamics, and do not establish an
arithmetic correspondence.  The identity map on $\mathbb A^2$ with constant
potential $G\equiv\log2$ is a useful symbolic control: it shows that a
transcendental normalization can inject a target, but it lies outside Class A
and evaluates no numerical logarithm.  Similarly, allowing $q=p$ with
prime-dependent support or inserting $\log p$ as an edge length abandons the
target-independence hypothesis rather than refuting the theorem.

Positive arithmetic architectures make the boundary concrete.  Deninger's
systems for arithmetic schemes are infinite-dimensional and attach periodic
packets to closed points \citep{deninger_2026}.  Connes--Consani's scaling
site and ad\`ele class space carry prime places intrinsically and exhibit
periodic orbits $C_p$ of length $\log p$
\citep{connes_consani_2016,connes_consani_2024}.  These constructions do not
fit a frozen finite L/M/A certificate.  They are outside examples, not
confirmation that one of our four exits is sufficient and not evidence for a
universal dimension claim.

The theorem is exact-only.  Hermite--Lindemann and valuations do not turn
small numerical error into equality, so no approximate separation bound is
claimed.  Algebraic irrational coefficients on multiplier logs also remain
open: the elementary rational-root closure in
\cref{lem:rational-closure} does not cover them.

\section{Conclusion and limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A finite additive L/M/A certificate has finite arithmetic capacity.  Once the
locally constant contribution occupies one rational space, multiplier
moduli have one finite bad support, and the action contribution is real
algebraic, Hermite--Lindemann and finite-place valuations force a
rank-plus-support bound on exact rational-prime logarithms.  The additive
coupling, rather than a selector union, is the central result.

The theorem has deliberate limits.  It says nothing quantitative about near
matches, does not cover nonlinear or algebraic-irrational multiplier-log
mixing, and does not classify nonalgebraic, multivalued, adelic, or
infinite-place observables.  Its escape conditions are not a complete
trichotomy and carry no sufficiency claim.  Two concrete next questions are a
quantitative analogue for approximate equality and a separately source-locked
analysis of an intrinsic outside-class clock.  Neither direction is opened by
the present static audit.

\section{Edge-case and scope ledger}
\label{app:scope}

\paragraph{Selection and repetitions.}
For each distinct prime, choose one valid certificate before examining any
finite relation.  The proof works for every such choice.  Multiple orbits or
representations for one prime contribute one set element.

\paragraph{Positive rational powers.}
The unique positive real $D$th root of a positive algebraic number is
algebraic.  After adjoining finitely many roots, extension invariance retains
the $\SQ$-unit condition.  Negative exponents use inversion.  No complex
logarithm branch occurs.

\paragraph{Excluded outputs.}
The certificate forbids output labels asserting a universal symplectic
no-go, complete escape trichotomy, infinite-dimensional necessity, historical
priority, Riemann-zero progress, or Route-B progress.  The accepted
classification means only that the locked implication and provenance gates
pass.

\section{Expanded source-class proofs}
\label{app:sources}

\subsection{The projective-affine step in Class M}

For a fixed itinerary, homogenize each equation in
\eqref{eq:henon-recurrence} to its own degree.  At the common homogenizing
coordinate $Z=0$, the monic term forces every cyclic coordinate to vanish;
the projective solution scheme has no geometric point at infinity.  After
base change to an algebraic closure, it is consequently a closed subscheme of
an affine chart while remaining proper.  A finite-type affine scheme proper
over a field is finite, equivalently its coordinate algebra is
finite-dimensional over that field.  This statement permits nilpotents and
does not assume the scheme reduced.  Finiteness descends along the field
extension.  Thus the cyclic scheme is finite.  This establishes algebraicity
at every finite period, not merely at audited sample periods.

\subsection{The non-Archimedean maximum and monodromy}

At a good place, all nonleading polynomial coefficients are integral and the
leading coefficient is a unit.  If $R>1$ is the maximal cyclic norm, the
ultrametric inequality and monicity give a unique leading magnitude
$R^{d}>R$, contradicting the two-neighbor recurrence bound.  Hence the orbit
is integral.  Derivative factors and their inverses have integral entries and
determinant one, so a monodromy eigenvalue and its inverse are integral over
the local ring.  Both valuations vanish.  In a normal extension saturated
above $\SQ$, the same holds for the conjugate, which proves
\eqref{eq:modulus-unit} without a reciprocal-conjugate shortcut.

\subsection{Class-A gauge distinction}

Summing the stepwise identity gives \eqref{eq:gauge} by ordinary
telescoping.  If every displayed term is algebraic, the shifted action is
algebraic even when $\chi_n(P_n)\neq\chi_0(P_0)$.  Endpoint compatibility
removes that term and is required only when the numerical closed action is
claimed to be gauge invariant.  A pole cannot be canceled after evaluation,
and monodromy of a multivalued gauge requires a different ledger.
