## 8. Face Hessian, Jacobian, and cancellation audit

including every multiple tie and every original coefficient sign.  It
chooses the unique face support point (x_0,y_0) with minimal first
coordinate.  In
P_{XX}P_{YY}-P_{XY}^2, the target monomial
X^{2x_0-2}Y^{2y_0-2} can arise only from the self-pair at that point:
any second support point contributing to the same exponent sum would have
the same minimal first coordinate and hence would be identical.  Its
coefficient is exactly

It is nonzero because c_{x_0,y_0}, x_0, and y_0 are nonzero and

## 11. Projective formulas and global contraction

With r=u_1/u_2, \Phi(r)=\max_E(xr+y), and
\kappa=e/f, the forward ratio map is

\phi(r)=\kappa\,\frac{\Phi(r)-r}{\Phi(r)-1}.

For the inverse ratio s=v_1/v_2,

\psi(s)=\frac{\Phi(\kappa s)-\kappa s}{\Phi(\kappa s)-1}.

The scaling L(s)=\kappa s satisfies L\circ\psi=\phi\circ L exactly.
Thus the inverse selector is evaluated at \kappa s and is not confused
with the forward selector at s.

On a chamber branch (x,y), the absolute logarithmic derivative is

\frac{r(x+y-1)}
 {((x-1)r+y)(xr+y-1)}.

The denominator-minus-numerator gap is

x(x-1)r^2+2(x-1)(y-1)r+y(y-1),

which is strictly positive for every positive r.  Each fixed branch tends
to zero at both compactified logarithmic ends.  The maximum derivative on
each branch is therefore strictly below one, and finiteness of E gives a
single q<1 for all branches.  At a Newton wall, continuity and splitting
an interval at its finitely many wall crossings patch the branchwise
estimates into one global logarithmic contraction.  The proof does not
differentiate through a nonsmooth maximum or omit a wall endpoint.

The contraction mapping theorem on the complete logarithmic line gives a
unique fixed ratio and geometric convergence.  Scaling conjugacy gives
the inverse fixed ratio s_\star=r_\star/\kappa.  Applying the strict
contraction to an iterate also excludes every nontrivial numerical
periodic orbit.

## 12. Selector classification and absence of a numerical cycle

If the fixed ratio lies in a chamber interior, its positive distance from
the finite wall set and convergence imply eventual confinement to that
single chamber.  If it lies on a wall, the orbit starting on the wall is
fixed and retains the full tied face.

For a strict orbit, the logarithmic map is strictly decreasing.  Points
below the fixed log-ratio map above it and vice versa.  Convergence then
forces eventual alternation between the two adjacent open chambers.
Delayed landing on the fixed point is impossible because strict
monotonicity makes the map injective: a later hit would pull back uniquely
to a previous hit and ultimately to the seed.  With a multiple tie, the
extreme adjacent exponents govern the two open sides, while the entire
tied face governs the fixed orbit.  Thus label alternation is a selector
word only; the numerical ratios remain distinct and converge, so there is
no numerical two-cycle.

## 13. Interior and wall spectral arithmetic

In a stationary interior chamber \xi, the state matrix is
C_\xi=BA_\xi, a positive integral two-by-two matrix.  The fixed ray is
its positive Perron ray, and transient matrices do not alter the
exponential rate.  Its characteristic polynomial is monic quadratic over
the integers, giving algebraic degree at most two.

On a fixed Newton wall, the adjacent integral selector matrices share the
same positive rational ray.  Dividing its integral coordinates by their
gcd gives a primitive positive integral vector w.  If an integral matrix
sends w to \mu w, one primitive coordinate shows \mu is rational; because
\mu is also an eigenvalue of an integral matrix, it is an algebraic
integer.  A rational algebraic integer is an integer.  The two adjacent
matrices have the same multiplier because both realize the projective
fixed ray and the same per-step norm scaling.  Hence wall growth is by a
positive integral multiplier.  This is distinct from, and consistent
with, the interior quadratic cap.

## 14. Separate scalar recurrence proofs

The scalar statements are upper bounds on eventual recurrence order, not
minimality claims.
