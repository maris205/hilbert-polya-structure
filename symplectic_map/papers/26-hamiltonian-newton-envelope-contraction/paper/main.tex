\begin{abstract}
Let \(K\) be a field of characteristic zero and consider the planar polynomial
symplectic map \(F=T\circ S\), where \(S\) is generated by a position
Hamiltonian
\[
V(q_1,q_2)=\sum_{(x,y)\in E}c_{x,y}q_1^xq_2^y
\]
with finite nonempty collected support \(E\subset\Z_{\geq2}^2\), and \(T\)
is generated by the separated momentum Hamiltonian
\[
W(p_1,p_2)=\alpha p_1^{e+1}+\beta p_2^{f+1},
\qquad \alpha\beta\neq0,\quad e,f\geq2.
\]
All collected coefficients \(c_{x,y}\) are arbitrary elements of
\(K^\times\); the proof uses only their nonvanishing.  A Newton envelope
predicts the leading degrees of the iterates, but such a prediction is not
an exact polynomial statement until cancellation on every tied exposed
face has been excluded.  We isolate in the full face-Hessian determinant
the coefficient
\[
c_{x_0,y_0}^2x_0y_0(1-x_0-y_0),
\]
where \((x_0,y_0)\) is the unique face point with minimal first coordinate.
This proves algebraic independence of the two face derivatives and makes
all forward and inverse leading-form transports cancellation-free.

The resulting degree states satisfy exact, oppositely ordered transports
\[
u^+_{n+1}=B\Acal(u^+_n),\qquad
v^-_{n+1}=\Acal(Bv^-_n),
\]
and the ordinary seed yields the shifted vector bridge
\[
u^+_{n+1}=c_\star Bv^-_n.
\]
Projectivizing the forward transport gives a finite-envelope map that is a
strict contraction in logarithmic distance, uniformly across all Newton
walls.  The inverse map is its scaling conjugate.  Hence there is one
positive fixed ray: an interior ray makes the selector eventually
stationary, while a wall ray either remains fixed or attracts a strict
adjacent-label alternation whose numerical ratios still converge.  The
interior growth rate is a Perron root of a positive integral
\(2\times2\) matrix and therefore has algebraic degree at most two; on a
wall a primitive integral ray forces a positive integer multiplier.
Forward and inverse ordinary degrees separately obey eventual recurrences
of order at most two or four, with geometric fixed-ray cases.  In
particular, the forward and inverse exponential rates agree.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction, local related work, and contributions}

\subsection{Degree growth as an exact polynomial problem}

For a polynomial automorphism, ordinary total degree records how the four
coordinate polynomials expand under iteration.  Its exponential scale is
the first dynamical degree, but the exact degree sequence contains more
information than the exponential limit alone.  Algebraic entropy packages
the degree growth of iterates as a complexity invariant for rational
dynamics~\cite{BellonViallet1999AlgebraicEntropy}.  In broader birational
dynamics, dynamical degrees also admit spectral
interpretations~\cite{DangFavre2021SpectralInterpretations}.

The maps studied here admit an elementary-looking max-plus description:
one takes a support function of the exponent set and alternates it with a
diagonal power map.  That description is initially only an upper bound.
Two leading coordinate polynomials can share a whole exposed face, and
arbitrary nonzero coefficients allow apparent leading terms to cancel.  The central
polynomial task is therefore to certify the entire exposed face before
using any tropical transport as an equality.

\subsection{The three coupled obstacles}

Three difficulties must be resolved in a fixed order.  First, on a Newton
wall several monomials tie, so selecting one maximizer cannot justify a
leading form.  Second, \(F=T\circ S\) and
\(F^{-1}=S^{-1}\circ T^{-1}\) have opposite phase chronologies: the
forward state is visible in the position block, whereas the inverse state
is visible in the momentum block.  Third, a selector may keep switching
even though the projective state converges, so a chamber calculation by
itself does not control all tails.

These obstacles are coupled.  Exact transport requires cancellation
control; projective contraction requires exact transport; and the scalar
recurrence statements require both contraction and a separate visibility
argument for each direction.  The shifted forward--inverse bridge compares
the two vector states, but it cannot replace the inverse recurrence proof
because a diagonal rescaling need not preserve which coordinate realizes
an ordinary maximum.

\subsection{Local related-work synthesis}

Cluster-generated maps provide a separate setting in which invariant
symplectic structures and tropical degree recurrences
coexist~\cite{FordyHone2011SymplecticMaps}.  For mutation loops, stable
tropical sign data can control algebraic
entropy~\cite{IshibashiKano2021AlgebraicEntropy}.  The literature on
polynomial symplectomorphisms supplies family-level structural background
for the ambient map class~\cite{JaneczkoJelonek2008PolynomialSymplectomorphisms}.
Hamiltonian dynamics may be approximated by compositions of nonlinear
shears depending only on position or only on
momentum~\cite{BergerTuraev2025GeneratorsHamiltonianMaps}.
Affine-triangular automorphisms form an adjacent structured class in which
dynamical degrees obey arithmetic
restrictions~\cite{BlancVanSanten2022DynamicalDegrees}.

These comparisons are contextual.  None is used to transfer a theorem or
a proof step to the present family.  All cancellation, phase-order,
contraction, selector, spectral, and recurrence statements below are
proved directly.

\subsection{Contributions and proof roadmap}

The article makes five assumption-bounded contributions.

\begin{itemize}
\item It proves a coefficient-uniform, full-face Hessian certificate,
including arbitrary multi-point ties, and converts it into algebraic
independence of every exposed gradient pair.
\item It derives cancellation-free forward and inverse half-step carries,
visible ordinary degrees, exact \(B\Acal\) and \(\Acal B\) transports, and
the shifted vector bridge with its precise scalar boundary.
\item It proves one global contraction constant in logarithmic projective
distance, including branch derivatives, endpoint control, finite-envelope
uniformity, wall patching, and the scaling conjugacy for the inverse map.
\item It classifies selector tails by the unique fixed ray and obtains an
interior quadratic-at-most Perron value or a wall multiplier that is a
positive integer.
\item It proves forward and inverse scalar recurrence upper bounds
separately and supplies two exact hand-checkable fixtures together with
the boundaries of every mechanism.
\end{itemize}

The dependency chain is consequently rigid: symplectic phase order,
full-face noncancellation, exact degree transport, projective contraction,
selector classification, spectral arithmetic, and only then scalar
recurrences.  The theorem is confined to finite collected support in
\(\Z_{\geq2}^2\), a separated \(W\), the ordinary coordinate seed, and
characteristic zero.  Global claim-level novelty remains unresolved.  We
make no genericity substitution, no extension to arbitrary Hamiltonian
shears, and no higher-dimensional classification.

\section{Setting, symplecticity, and inverse phases}

\subsection{Map class, collected support, and ordinary degree}

Fix a field \(K\) of characteristic zero.  Let
\[
\varnothing\neq E\subset\Z_{\geq2}^2
\]
be finite and let
\[
V(q_1,q_2)=\sum_{(x,y)\in E}c_{x,y}q_1^xq_2^y,
\qquad c_{x,y}\in K^\times.
\]
The polynomial is collected: each exponent occurs once, and \(E\) is the
support of the resulting polynomial.  The coefficients are otherwise
arbitrary elements of \(K^\times\).  Let
\[
W(p_1,p_2)=\alpha p_1^{e+1}+\beta p_2^{f+1},
\qquad \alpha,\beta\in K^\times,\qquad e,f\geq2.
\]
On coordinates ordered as \((q_1,q_2,p_1,p_2)\), define
\[
S(q,p)=(q,p+\nabla V(q)),\qquad
T(q,p)=(q+\nabla W(p),p),\qquad F=T\circ S.
\]
All degrees below are ordinary total degrees in the four initial
coordinates.  Thus each initial coordinate has degree one.
For any polynomial automorphism \(G\), degree under composition satisfies
\[
\deg(G^{m+n})\leq\deg(G^m)\deg(G^n).
\]
Thus \(\log\deg(G^n)\) is subadditive, and Fekete's lemma gives the
well-defined first dynamical degree
\[
\lambda_1(G)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\deg(G^n)^{1/n}
=\inf_{n\geq1}\deg(G^n)^{1/n}.
\]

\subsection{Symplectic block calculation}

Let
\[
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_2\\-I_2&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Symplecticity and inverse order]\label{prop:symplectic}
The maps \(S,T,F\), and \(F^{-1}\) are polynomial symplectomorphisms.  The
inverse is \(F^{-1}=S^{-1}\circ T^{-1}\), so \(T^{-1}\) acts first.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Writing \(H_V=\Hess V(q)\) and \(H_W=\Hess W(p)\), symmetry of the two
Hessians gives
\[
DS=\begin{pmatrix}I_2&0\\H_V&I_2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
DT=\begin{pmatrix}I_2&H_W\\0&I_2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Direct block multiplication yields
\[
(DS)^{\mathsf T}JDS=J,\qquad
(DT)^{\mathsf T}JDT=J.
\]
Hence \(S\) and \(T\) preserve the standard symplectic form, and so do
their composition and inverses.  The displayed inverse order follows from
reversing a composition.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Subtraction inverses and phase chronology}

The inverse shears are literal subtractions:
\[
S^{-1}(q,p)=(q,p-\nabla V(q)),\qquad
T^{-1}(q,p)=(q-\nabla W(p),p).
\]
Consequently,
\[
F^{-1}(q,p)
=S^{-1}\bigl(q-\nabla W(p),p\bigr).
\]
Every inverse iterate therefore applies the upper subtraction
\(T^{-1}\) before the lower subtraction \(S^{-1}\).  The signs change
nonzero scalar factors in leading forms but do not change their degrees;
the phase order, by contrast, changes the state transport and cannot be
ignored.

\subsection{Support function and theorem staging}

For \(u=(u_1,u_2)^{\mathsf T}\in\R_{>0}^2\), set
\[
H(u)=\max_{(x,y)\in E}(xu_1+yu_2),\qquad
\Acal(u)=
\begin{pmatrix}H(u)-u_1\\H(u)-u_2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=\diag(e,f).
\]
The map \(\Acal\) is continuous and positively homogeneous.  We shall use
the ordinary seeds
\[
u^+_0=v^-_0=\one=(1,1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
The symbols \(u_n^+\) and \(v_n^-\) will become the visible forward
position-degree and inverse momentum-degree states.  Before asserting
their transports, we must prove that the entire exposed face survives
substitution and that no carried coordinate can tie a fresh block.  The
precise main theorem is therefore stated only after the cancellation
certificate in the next section.

\section{Full exposed-face Hessian and cancellation}

\subsection{Positive exposed support and full face polynomial}

For \(u\in\R_{>0}^2\), define
\[
E_u=\{(x,y)\in E:xu_1+yu_2=H(u)\}
\]
and retain the entire exposed-face polynomial
\[
P_u(X,Y)=\sum_{(x,y)\in E_u}c_{x,y}X^xY^y.
\]
On a wall, \(E_u\) may contain arbitrarily many support points.  All of
them remain in \(P_u\); choosing one tied monomial would discard exactly
the cancellations that must be controlled.

\subsection{Unique minimal first coordinate}

\begin{lemma}[Unique minimal-\(x\) point]\label{lem:minimal-x}
Every positive exposed face \(E_u\) has a unique point with minimal first
coordinate.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Finiteness gives existence.  If \((x,y),(x,y')\in E_u\), then
\[
xu_1+yu_2=xu_1+y'u_2.
\]
Since \(u_2>0\), one has \(y=y'\).  Thus two distinct face points cannot
have the same first coordinate, and the minimizer is unique.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The isolated face-Hessian coefficient}

\begin{lemma}[Nonzero full-face Hessian determinant]\label{lem:hessian}
Let \((x_0,y_0)\) be the point from Lemma~\ref{lem:minimal-x}.  The
coefficient of
\[
X^{2x_0-2}Y^{2y_0-2}
\]
in \(\det\Hess P_u\) is
\[
c_{x_0,y_0}^2x_0y_0(1-x_0-y_0).
\]
In particular, \(\det\Hess P_u\neq0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand
\[
\det\Hess P_u=(P_u)_{XX}(P_u)_{YY}-(P_u)_{XY}^2.
\]
An ordered pair \((x_a,y_a),(x_b,y_b)\in E_u^2\) can contribute to the
target monomial only if
\[
x_a+x_b-2=2x_0-2.
\]
Minimality gives \(x_a,x_b\geq x_0\), so equality forces
\(x_a=x_b=x_0\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:minimal-x} then forces
\(y_a=y_b=y_0\).  Hence the self-pair is the only contributor, even when
the exposed face has many points.

Its coefficient in \((P_u)_{XX}(P_u)_{YY}\) is
\[
c_{x_0,y_0}^2x_0(x_0-1)y_0(y_0-1),
\]
and its coefficient in \((P_u)_{XY}^2\) is
\[
c_{x_0,y_0}^2x_0^2y_0^2.
\]
Their difference is
\[
c_{x_0,y_0}^2x_0y_0
\bigl((x_0-1)(y_0-1)-x_0y_0\bigr)
=c_{x_0,y_0}^2x_0y_0(1-x_0-y_0).
\]
The factors \(c_{x_0,y_0}\neq0\), \(x_0,y_0\geq2\), and characteristic
zero separately ensure that this coefficient is nonzero.  Only
nonvanishing of coefficients has been used.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Jacobian independence}

\begin{lemma}[Two-variable Jacobian criterion]\label{lem:jacobian}
If \(R,S\in K[X,Y]\) and
\[
\det\frac{\partial(R,S)}{\partial(X,Y)}\neq0,
\]
then \(R\) and \(S\) are algebraically independent over \(K\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(R,S\) were algebraically dependent, then \(K(R,S)\) would have
transcendence degree at most one.  In characteristic zero the Kähler
differentials \(dR,dS\) would be linearly dependent over \(K(X,Y)\).
Therefore
\[
dR\wedge dS
=\det\frac{\partial(R,S)}{\partial(X,Y)}\,dX\wedge dY
\]
would vanish, contradicting the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

Taking \(R=(P_u)_X\) and \(S=(P_u)_Y\), Lemmas~\ref{lem:hessian}
and~\ref{lem:jacobian} show that the two derivatives of every full exposed
face are algebraically independent.

\subsection{Injective substitution and pure-power survival}

\begin{lemma}[Injective substitution]\label{lem:substitution}
If \(L_1,L_2\) are algebraically independent elements of a polynomial
ring over \(K\), then the substitution
\[
K[X,Y]\longrightarrow K[L_1,L_2],\qquad X\longmapsto L_1,\quad
Y\longmapsto L_2
\]
is injective.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Its kernel consists precisely of the polynomial relations between
\(L_1\) and \(L_2\), and algebraic independence says that this kernel is
zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Separated powers, scalars, and signs]\label{lem:powers}
If \(L_1,L_2\) are algebraically independent and \(a,b\geq1\), then
\(L_1^a,L_2^b\) are algebraically independent.  Nonzero scalar multiples
and sign changes preserve this property.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The map \(K[U,V]\to K[X,Y]\) given by
\(U\mapsto X^a\), \(V\mapsto Y^b\) is injective because distinct
monomials have distinct images.  Compose it with the injective
substitution \(X\mapsto L_1\), \(Y\mapsto L_2\).  Nonzero coordinate
scalings are automorphisms of the generated polynomial algebra.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Full-face leading-pair survival]\label{prop:survival}
Suppose \(Q_1,Q_2\) have ordinary degrees \(u_1,u_2>0\) and algebraically
independent leading homogeneous forms \(L_1,L_2\).  Then
\[
\deg V_{q_1}(Q_1,Q_2)=H(u)-u_1,\qquad
\deg V_{q_2}(Q_1,Q_2)=H(u)-u_2,
\]
their leading forms are
\[
(P_u)_X(L_1,L_2),\qquad (P_u)_Y(L_1,L_2),
\]
and this pair is algebraically independent.  The conclusion remains true
for every multi-point tie, after nonzero scalar changes and signs, and
after applying the separated pure powers arising from \(W\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The top ordinary degree of \(Q_1^xQ_2^y\) is \(xu_1+yu_2\).
Injectivity from Lemma~\ref{lem:substitution} prevents distinct monomials
in \(L_1,L_2\) from canceling, so the top part of \(V(Q_1,Q_2)\) is the
whole \(P_u(L_1,L_2)\).  Differentiating before substitution gives the
displayed degrees and leading forms.  If a polynomial relation held
between those two forms, injectivity would pull it back to a relation
between \((P_u)_X\) and \((P_u)_Y\), contrary to
Lemmas~\ref{lem:hessian}--\ref{lem:jacobian}.  Lemma~\ref{lem:powers}
handles pure powers, nonzero scalars, and subtraction signs.  The proof
uses the whole tied face and is uniform in all permitted coefficients.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Dependency-ordered Main Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Main theorem]\label{thm:main}
Let \(K\) have characteristic zero.  Let
\(\varnothing\neq E\subset\Z_{\geq2}^2\) be finite and collected, let
every \(c_{x,y}\) be an arbitrary element of \(K^\times\), and let
\[
V=\sum_{(x,y)\in E}c_{x,y}q_1^xq_2^y,\qquad
W=\alpha p_1^{e+1}+\beta p_2^{f+1}
\]
with \(\alpha\beta\neq0\) and \(e,f\geq2\).  For \(F=T\circ S\) and
ordinary total degree from the coordinate seed \(\one\), the following
statements hold in the listed dependency order.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The shears and their composition are symplectic, and
\(F^{-1}=S^{-1}\circ T^{-1}\) acts through \(T^{-1}\) first.
\item Every positive exposed face has nonzero Hessian determinant; its
two gradient forms are algebraically independent, and injective
substitution plus separated powers makes all forward and inverse leading
pairs survive without a genericity assumption.
\item With \(B=\diag(e,f)\), the exact visible states satisfy
\[
u^+_{n+1}=B\Acal(u^+_n),\qquad
v^-_{n+1}=\Acal(Bv^-_n),\qquad u^+_0=v^-_0=\one,
\]
and
\[
\deg(F^n)=\norminf{u^+_n},\qquad
\deg(F^{-n})=\norminf{v^-_n}.
\]
\item For \(c_\star=H(\one)-1\),
\[
u^+_{n+1}=c_\star Bv^-_n\qquad(n\geq0).
\]
This shifted vector identity yields a constant comparison and equality of
forward and inverse exponential rates, but neither termwise equality of
the scalar degree sequences nor an inverse scalar recurrence.
\item The forward ratio map
\[
\phi(r)=\kappa\frac{\Phi(r)-r}{\Phi(r)-1},\qquad
\Phi(r)=\max_{(x,y)\in E}(xr+y),\qquad\kappa=e/f,
\]
is a global strict contraction in logarithmic distance.  The inverse
ratio map is scaling-conjugate to it and selects chambers at
\(\kappa s\).
\item There is a unique positive fixed ray.  If it is interior to a
Newton chamber, every selector is eventually stationary.  If it is on a
wall, the fixed trajectory uses the full tied face, while every strict
trajectory eventually alternates the adjacent labels and its ratios
converge to the wall.  Multiple ties are governed by the two extreme
adjacent exponents off the wall and by the full face on it.  There is no
nontrivial numerical periodic orbit.
\item In the interior case the common exponential rate is the Perron root
of a positive integral \(2\times2\) chamber matrix and has algebraic
degree at most two.  In the wall case the adjacent matrices share a
primitive positive integral ray with a positive integer multiplier, which
is the per-step rate.  Thus
\[
[\Q(\lambda_1(F)):\Q]\leq2.
\]
\item Forward and inverse scalar degree tails are proved separately.
Interior tails have recurrence order at most two, strict wall tails have
interleaved recurrence order at most four, and fixed-ray cases are
geometric.  These are upper bounds only.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The remaining sections prove the conclusions after item two.  In
particular, no exact degree transport has been used in establishing the
face certificate.

\section{Exact forward/inverse transports and bridge}

\subsection{Weighted gradient transform}

Let \(Q_1,Q_2\) have leading forms \(L_1,L_2\) and degree vector \(u\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:survival} identifies the full top forms of
\(\nabla V(Q_1,Q_2)\) and gives the exact transform
\[
u\longmapsto\Acal(u).
\]
Because the statement is made after differentiating \(V\) and then
substituting, its two entries are \(H(u)-u_1\) and \(H(u)-u_2\), not two
independently chosen maxima.  Their algebraic independence is what allows
the next separated power phase to remain injective.

\subsection{Lower carry inequalities}

\begin{lemma}[Exact gradient degrees and carries]\label{lem:carries}
For every \(u\in\R_{>0}^2\),
\[
H(u)-u_1\geq u_1+2u_2>\max(u_1,u_2),
\]
\[
H(u)-u_2\geq2u_1+u_2>\max(u_1,u_2).
\]
If \(A=\Acal(u)\), then
\[
2A_1-A_2\geq3u_2>0,\qquad
2A_2-A_1\geq3u_1>0.
\]
Consequently, both components of \(BA\) strictly dominate both components
of \(A\) and every component of \(u\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the lower-carry assertions in
Lemma~\ref{lem:carries}]
Every support point has \(x,y\geq2\), hence
\[
H(u)\geq2u_1+2u_2.
\]
Subtracting \(u_1\) or \(u_2\) proves the first two inequalities.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cross-coordinate upper carry}

\begin{proof}[Completion of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:carries}]
Writing \(A_1=H(u)-u_1\), \(A_2=H(u)-u_2\), direct subtraction gives
\[
2A_1-A_2=H(u)-2u_1+u_2\geq3u_2>0,
\]
\[
2A_2-A_1=H(u)+u_1-2u_2\geq3u_1>0.
\]
Thus \(2A_1>A_2\) and \(2A_2>A_1\).  Since \(e,f\geq2\),
\[
eA_1\geq2A_1>\max(A_1,A_2),\qquad
fA_2\geq2A_2>\max(A_1,A_2).
\]
The lower carries already make each \(A_i\) exceed every component of
\(u\), completing the claim.
\end{proof}

The cross-coordinate step is essential: multiplying each component by at
least two does not, by itself, compare a small component with the other,
possibly larger component.

\subsection{Forward half-step induction}

At full forward time \(n\), let \(u_n^+\) be the position-degree vector.
After the next \(S\)-phase, let \(w_{n+1}^+\) be the fresh
momentum-degree vector.

\begin{proposition}[Forward top forms and position visibility]
\label{prop:forward}
For every \(n\geq0\), the forward leading position pair is algebraically
independent and
\[
w^+_{n+1}=\Acal(u^+_n),\qquad
u^+_{n+1}=Bw^+_{n+1}=B\Acal(u^+_n).
\]
For every \(n\geq1\), after a full iterate, every position degree exceeds
every momentum degree.  For every \(n\geq0\),
\[
\deg(F^n)=\norminf{u^+_n}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At \(n=0\), the pair \((q_1,q_2)\) is algebraically independent and both
coordinate blocks have degree one.  Applying \(S\) produces
\(p+\nabla V(q)\).  The lower carries make both gradient degrees exceed
one, so the old momentum terms cannot alter the top forms.
Proposition~\ref{prop:survival} gives an algebraically independent fresh
pair and \(w_1^+=\Acal(\one)\).

Applying \(T\) adds the separated pair
\[
\bigl(\alpha(e+1)p_1^e,\ \beta(f+1)p_2^f\bigr).
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:powers} preserves algebraic independence, while
Lemma~\ref{lem:carries} makes both new position degrees dominate both
fresh momentum degrees and the old position degrees.  Thus
\(u_1^+=Bw_1^+\), and the position block is visible.

For the induction step, assume the position leading pair at time \(n\) is
independent and dominates the carried momentum block.  The next
\(S\)-phase has exact degree vector \(\Acal(u_n^+)\); its two components
exceed every old position degree, hence every old momentum degree.
The next \(T\)-phase has vector \(B\Acal(u_n^+)\), whose two components
exceed both components of \(\Acal(u_n^+)\) by the cross carries.
Algebraic independence survives both phases.  This proves the transport
and makes the position maximum the ordinary degree after every full
iterate.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Inverse half-step induction}

At full inverse time \(n\), let \(v_n^-\) be the momentum-degree vector.
The next phase is \(T^{-1}\), and its fresh position vector is denoted
\(z_{n+1}^-\).

\begin{proposition}[Inverse top forms and momentum visibility]
\label{prop:inverse}
For every \(n\geq0\), the inverse leading momentum pair is algebraically
independent and
\[
z^-_{n+1}=Bv^-_n,\qquad
v^-_{n+1}=\Acal(z^-_{n+1})=\Acal(Bv^-_n).
\]
For every \(n\geq1\), after a full inverse iterate, every momentum degree
exceeds every position degree.  For every \(n\geq0\),
\[
\deg(F^{-n})=\norminf{v^-_n}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At \(n=0\), \((p_1,p_2)\) is algebraically independent.  The first
phase is \(T^{-1}\), so subtraction of the two separated pure powers
produces
\[
z^-_1=B\one.
\]
The factors and signs are nonzero, and Lemma~\ref{lem:powers} preserves
independence.  Since \(e,f\geq2\), these fresh terms dominate the initial
position coordinates.  The following \(S^{-1}\)-phase subtracts
\(\nabla V\) evaluated at that fresh position pair.
Proposition~\ref{prop:survival} and the lower carries give
\[
v^-_1=\Acal(B\one),
\]
whose two components exceed both components of \(z^-_1\) and therefore
the old momentum degrees.  The final momentum block is visible.

Now assume the momentum block at time \(n\) is independent and each of
its degrees exceeds every current position degree.  The next
\(T^{-1}\)-phase gives \(Bv^-_n\).  Each old position degree is below
each component of \(v_n^-\), while each new component is at least twice
its corresponding positive momentum degree; hence the fresh position
block dominates the carried position block.  The following
\(S^{-1}\)-phase gives \(\Acal(Bv^-_n)\), and each of its entries
dominates both entries of \(Bv^-_n\).  In particular, it dominates the
old momentum block as well.  Full-face independence and harmless
subtraction signs complete the induction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact vector states and total degrees}

Propositions~\ref{prop:forward} and~\ref{prop:inverse} establish the
oppositely ordered transports
\[
u^+_{n+1}=B\Acal(u^+_n),\qquad
v^-_{n+1}=\Acal(Bv^-_n).
\]
The order \(B\Acal\) belongs to the forward position state, while
\(\Acal B\) belongs to the inverse momentum state.  Their visible scalar
degrees are
\[
d_n^+=\deg(F^n)=\max(u^+_{n,1},u^+_{n,2}),
\]
\[
d_n^-=\deg(F^{-n})=\max(v^-_{n,1},v^-_{n,2}).
\]
These are ordinary degrees of the full four-coordinate maps, not merely
degrees of selected intermediate pairs.

\subsection{Shifted bridge induction}

\begin{proposition}[Shifted forward--inverse vector bridge]
\label{prop:bridge}
Let \(c_\star=H(\one)-1\).  Then
\[
u^+_{n+1}=c_\star Bv^-_n\qquad(n\geq0).
\]
This is a shifted vector identity for the ordinary seed.  It is not an
equality between \(d_{n+1}^+\) and \(d_n^-\), and it does not prove an
inverse scalar recurrence.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since both entries of \(\one\) equal one,
\[
\Acal(\one)=
\begin{pmatrix}H(\one)-1\\H(\one)-1\end{pmatrix}
=c_\star\one.
\]
Thus the base case is
\[
u^+_1=B\Acal(\one)=c_\star B\one=c_\star Bv^-_0.
\]
If \(u^+_{n+1}=c_\star Bv^-_n\), positive homogeneity of \(\Acal\)
gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
u^+_{n+2}
&=B\Acal(u^+_{n+1})\\
&=B\Acal(c_\star Bv^-_n)\\
&=c_\star B\Acal(Bv^-_n)\\
&=c_\star Bv^-_{n+1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Induction proves the identity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Norm comparison and exact boundary}

\begin{corollary}[Equality of first dynamical degrees]
\label{cor:rates}
For every \(n\geq0\),
\[
c_\star\min(e,f)d_n^-
\leq d_{n+1}^+
\leq c_\star\max(e,f)d_n^-.
\]
Consequently the forward and inverse exponential degree-growth rates
exist and satisfy
\[
\lambda_1(F)=\lambda_1(F^{-1}).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For every positive \(v\),
\[
\min(e,f)\norminf{v}\leq\norminf{Bv}
\leq\max(e,f)\norminf{v}.
\]
Combine this inequality with Proposition~\ref{prop:bridge} and the two
visibility formulas.  The two limits exist by the
degree-submultiplicativity argument in Subsection~2.1.  Taking roots,
fixed positive constants and a one-step index shift do not alter those
limits, which proves the equality.
\end{proof}

The comparison is constant-factor only.  Since
\(\norminf{Bv}\) need not be a constant multiple of \(\norminf{v}\),
the bridge gives neither termwise scalar equality nor a recurrence for
\(d_n^-\).  Section~7 derives the two scalar directions independently.

\section{Uniform logarithmic contraction}

\subsection{Projective support function and ratio map}

Represent a positive ray by \(u=(r,1)^{\mathsf T}\) and define
\[
\Phi(r)=H(r,1)=\max_{(x,y)\in E}(xr+y),\qquad \kappa=e/f.
\]
Then
\[
\Acal(r,1)=
\begin{pmatrix}\Phi(r)-r\\\Phi(r)-1\end{pmatrix},
\]
so the forward projective map is
\[
\phi(r)=\kappa\frac{\Phi(r)-r}{\Phi(r)-1}.
\]
Both numerator and denominator are positive because
\(\Phi(r)-r\geq r+2\) and \(\Phi(r)-1\geq2r+1\).

\begin{proposition}[Forward and inverse projective formulas]
\label{prop:projective}
The ratio of the forward state evolves by \(\phi\).  If
\(s=v_1/v_2\) is an inverse momentum ratio, then
\[
\psi(s)=\frac{\Phi(\kappa s)-\kappa s}
{\Phi(\kappa s)-1}
\]
and \(L(s)=\kappa s\) satisfies \(L\circ\psi=\phi\circ L\).
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Chamber derivative and monotonicity}

On a chamber selected by \(\xi=(x,y)\),
\[
\phi_\xi(r)=\kappa
\frac{(x-1)r+y}{xr+y-1}.
\]
Differentiation gives
\[
\phi_\xi'(r)
=-\kappa\frac{x+y-1}{(xr+y-1)^2}<0.
\]
Thus every branch is strictly decreasing.  The envelope \(\Phi\) is
continuous, and branch formulas agree at every wall, so \(\phi\) is
continuous.  If an interval crosses walls, partitioning it into chambers
shows that \(\phi\) is strictly decreasing on all of \((0,\infty)\).

\subsection{Logarithmic derivative and positive gap}

Set
\[
t=\log r,\qquad h(t)=\log\phi(e^t).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Branch logarithmic derivative]\label{lem:logderivative}
On the branch \(\xi=(x,y)\),
\[
|h'(t)|=\eta_{x,y}(r)
=\frac{r(x+y-1)}
{((x-1)r+y)(xr+y-1)},
\qquad r=e^t,
\]
and \(0<\eta_{x,y}(r)<1\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The chain rule and the two chamber formulas give the displayed fraction.
The denominator minus its numerator expands as
\[
\begin{aligned}
&((x-1)r+y)(xr+y-1)-r(x+y-1)\\
&\qquad=x(x-1)r^2+2(x-1)(y-1)r+y(y-1).
\end{aligned}
\]
Every term is positive for \(x,y\geq2\) and \(r>0\), which proves the
strict bound.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Endpoint compactification for one branch}

For a fixed \((x,y)\), the rational function \(\eta_{x,y}\) is
continuous on \((0,\infty)\), and
\[
\lim_{r\to0}\eta_{x,y}(r)=0,\qquad
\lim_{r\to\infty}\eta_{x,y}(r)=0.
\]
Extend it by zero at the two endpoints of the compactified positive
interval.  It attains a maximum \(q_{x,y}\), and
Lemma~\ref{lem:logderivative} implies
\[
q_{x,y}<1.
\]
This endpoint step rules out a supremum equal to one escaping through a
noncompact end.

\subsection{Finite-support uniformity}

Since \(E\) is finite, the single number
\[
q=\max_{(x,y)\in E}q_{x,y}
\]
satisfies \(q<1\).  The estimate applies even on a chamber where its
support point is active only on a subinterval, because it was proved on
the whole positive ray.  Finiteness is used precisely here; a pointwise
strict estimate over an infinite branch family would not produce this
maximum.

\subsection{Wall patching}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-envelope uniform log contraction]\label{thm:contraction}
For all \(t,t'\in\R\),
\[
|h(t')-h(t)|\leq q|t'-t|
\]
with the single constant \(q<1\) above.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume \(t<t'\).  The compact interval \([t,t']\) meets only finitely
many Newton walls.  Partition it at those wall points.  On each open
piece, \(h\) is differentiable and
\(|h'|\leq q\), so the mean-value bound holds on the closure by
continuity.  Summing over the pieces gives
\[
|h(t')-h(t)|
\leq\sum_jq\,|I_j|
=q|t'-t|.
\]
The case \(t'<t\) is symmetric.  This interval argument, rather
than a derivative assigned at a tied point, patches all walls.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Inverse map and scaled selector}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:projective}]
The forward formula follows directly from \(B\Acal(r,1)\).  For the
inverse state \(v=(s,1)^{\mathsf T}\), the intermediate vector \(Bv\)
has ratio \(\kappa s\).  Applying \(\Acal\) gives the stated \(\psi\).
Finally,
\[
(L\circ\psi)(s)
=\kappa\frac{\Phi(\kappa s)-\kappa s}{\Phi(\kappa s)-1}
=\phi(\kappa s)
=(\phi\circ L)(s).
\]
Thus inverse chamber selection is made at \(\kappa s\), never at \(s\)
unless \(\kappa=1\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fixed point and numerical-cycle exclusion}

\begin{corollary}[Unique ray and global convergence]\label{cor:fixed}
The map \(\phi\) has a unique fixed ratio \(r_\star>0\), and every
positive forward ratio converges to it geometrically in logarithmic
distance.  The inverse map has the unique fixed ratio
\(s_\star=r_\star/\kappa\).  Neither map has a nontrivial numerical
periodic orbit.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The real line in logarithmic coordinate is complete, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:contraction} and the contraction mapping theorem give a
unique fixed point \(t_\star\) and convergence to it.  A periodic orbit of
length \(m\geq2\) would be a nonfixed pair of points fixed by the
\(q^m\)-contraction \(h^m\), which is impossible.  The inverse conclusions
follow through the scaling conjugacy.
\end{proof}

The proof uses finite support, a one-dimensional projective chart, and
diagonal \(B\).  It gives no infinite-support result, no mixed-\(W\)
result, and no analogue in projective dimension at least two.

\section{Selector and spectral rigidity}

\subsection{Interior fixed ray}

Newton walls are the finitely many positive ratios at which at least two
affine functions \(xr+y\) tie for the maximum.  Their complement consists
of open chambers.

\begin{proposition}[Interior and wall selector dichotomy]
\label{prop:selectors}
If \(r_\star\) is interior to a chamber, every positive orbit is eventually
confined to that chamber.  If \(r_\star\) is a wall ratio, the fixed orbit
remains on the full tied face, while every other orbit eventually
alternates the two adjacent open chambers, converges to \(r_\star\), and
never lands on it after a positive delay.  At a multiple tie, the extreme
adjacent exponents control the two open sides and the entire tied face
controls the fixed orbit.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the interior-selector assertion in
Proposition~\ref{prop:selectors}]
In logarithmic coordinate, an interior \(t_\star\) has positive distance
from the finite wall set.  Convergence places every orbit eventually in a
smaller wall-free neighborhood, proving stationarity.  The wall case is
proved in the next subsection.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Wall fixed and strict trajectories}

\begin{proof}[Proof of the wall-selector assertions in
Proposition~\ref{prop:selectors}]
The trajectory starting at \(t_\star\) is fixed.  Since \(h\) is strictly
decreasing,
\[
t<t_\star\Longrightarrow h(t)>h(t_\star)=t_\star,
\]
\[
t>t_\star\Longrightarrow h(t)<t_\star.
\]
Every strict trajectory therefore changes sides at every step.
Convergence and finiteness of the wall set imply that, after a finite
transient, only the two chambers adjacent to \(t_\star\) occur.  If
\(h(t)=t_\star=h(t_\star)\), strict monotonicity forces \(t=t_\star\);
there is no delayed landing.

When several exponents tie, the upper envelope immediately to either side
is determined by the extreme exposed exponents.  At the wall itself,
Proposition~\ref{prop:survival} retains every tied term in \(P_u\).
Thus arbitrary multiple ties do not alter the classification.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Selector alternation is not a numerical two-cycle}

\begin{proposition}[Label alternation versus state dynamics]
\label{prop:no-cycle}
On a strict wall tail, adjacent chamber labels alternate, but the
successive numerical ratios are distinct and converge to \(r_\star\).
They do not form a nontrivial two-cycle.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Side swapping supplies the alternating labels.  The contraction estimate
gives
\[
|\log r_n-\log r_\star|\leq q^n
|\log r_0-\log r_\star|.
\]
Delayed landing is impossible, so a strict orbit has distinct ratios on
the two sides until their common limit.  Corollary~\ref{cor:fixed} rules
out every nontrivial numerical period.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Interior selector matrix and spectrum}

For \(\xi=(x,y)\), define
\[
A_\xi=
\begin{pmatrix}x-1&y\\x&y-1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
C_\xi=BA_\xi=
\begin{pmatrix}e(x-1)&ey\\fx&f(y-1)\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Interior Perron spectrum]\label{prop:interior-spectrum}
If \(r_\star\) is in the chamber selected by \(\xi\), then
\[
\lambda_1(F)=\rho(C_\xi)
\]
and this number is a positive algebraic integer of degree at most two.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:selectors} gives a threshold after which
\(u^+_{n+1}=C_\xi u^+_n\).  The fixed ray is a positive eigenvector of
the positive integral matrix \(C_\xi\), hence its eigenvalue is the
Perron root.  Transient factors do not change the exponential rate.
The characteristic polynomial
\[
t^2-\tr(C_\xi)t+\det(C_\xi)
\]
is monic with integer coefficients, proving the assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Primitive wall ray and common multiplier}

\begin{proposition}[Common primitive wall ray]\label{prop:wall-ray}
Suppose \(r_\star\) is on a Newton wall.  Then
\(r_\star\in\Q_{>0}\).  If \(\wvec=(a,b)^{\mathsf T}\) is the primitive
positive integral direction of that ray, every exponent \(\xi\) on the
tied face satisfies
\[
C_\xi\wvec=\mu\wvec
\]
with the same \(\mu\in\Z_{>0}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose two distinct tied exponents
\(\xi_-=(x_-,y_-)\) and \(\xi_+=(x_+,y_+)\).  Their tie gives
\[
x_-r_\star+y_-=x_+r_\star+y_+.
\]
If \(x_-=x_+\), this equality would give \(y_-=y_+\), contradicting
the choice of distinct exponents.  Hence \(x_-\neq x_+\), and
\[
r_\star=\frac{y_+-y_-}{x_--x_+}\in\Q_{>0}.
\]
The positive rational ray therefore has the stated primitive integral
direction \(\wvec\).

For every tied \(\xi=(x,y)\),
\[
H(\wvec)=xa+yb
\]
and therefore
\[
A_\xi\wvec=
\begin{pmatrix}H(\wvec)-a\\H(\wvec)-b\end{pmatrix},
\]
independent of \(\xi\).  Multiplication by \(B\) gives the same vector for
all tied selectors.  Because \(\wvec\) is the fixed projective ray, this
common vector is \(\mu\wvec\) for a positive rational \(\mu\).

It remains to prove integrality.  Since \(\gcd(a,b)=1\), choose
\(m,n\in\Z\) with \(ma+nb=1\).  Both coordinates of
\(C_\xi\wvec\) are integral, and
\[
\mu=m(\mu a)+n(\mu b)\in\Z.
\]
Positivity gives \(\mu\in\Z_{>0}\).  This primitive-ray argument applies
to every exponent in a multiple tie.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Integrality and per-step growth}

\begin{corollary}[Uniform algebraic-degree bound]\label{cor:quadratic}
For every map in the stated family,
\[
[\Q(\lambda_1(F)):\Q]\leq2.
\]
On a fixed wall, \(\lambda_1(F)=\mu\in\Z_{>0}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The interior case is Proposition~\ref{prop:interior-spectrum}.  On a
strict wall tail, let \(C_-,C_+\) be the adjacent matrices in step order.
Both send \(\wvec\) to \(\mu\wvec\), so
\[
(C_+C_-)\wvec=\mu^2\wvec.
\]
The positive product has Perron root \(\mu^2\), and growth per single
iterate is its positive square root \(\mu\).  If the seed is already on
the wall, every tied selector gives the same one-step multiplier directly.
\end{proof}

This quadratic cap is confined to the two-dimensional separated family;
it is neither a statement about all Hamiltonian shears nor a statement
about higher dynamical degrees.

\section{Forward and inverse scalar recurrences}

\subsection{State-matrix and visibility notation}

Retain
\[
A_\xi=\begin{pmatrix}x-1&y\\x&y-1\end{pmatrix},
\quad C_\xi=BA_\xi,\quad D_\xi=A_\xi B,
\]
and write
\[
r_n=\frac{u^+_{n,1}}{u^+_{n,2}},\qquad
s_n=\frac{v^-_{n,1}}{v^-_{n,2}}.
\]
The fixed ratios satisfy \(s_\star=r_\star/\kappa\).  Forward selectors
are evaluated at \(r_n\); inverse selectors are evaluated at
\(\kappa s_n\), the ratio of \(Bv_n^-\).  The visible degrees remain
\[
d_n^+=\norminf{u_n^+},\qquad d_n^-=\norminf{v_n^-}.
\]
The maximum must stabilize before a coordinate recurrence can be promoted
to either scalar degree sequence.

\subsection{Forward interior tail}

\begin{proposition}[Forward interior scalar tails]
\label{prop:forward-interior-recurrence}
Suppose \(r_\star\) is interior to the chamber \(\xi\).  Then \(d_n^+\)
eventually satisfies
\[
d^+_{n+2}=\tr(C_\xi)d^+_{n+1}-\det(C_\xi)d^+_n.
\]
If \(r_\star=1\), the ordinary-seed sequence is geometric from time zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For some \(n_{\mathrm{int}}\),
\[
u^+_{n+1}=C_\xi u^+_n\qquad(n\geq n_{\mathrm{int}}).
\]
Cayley--Hamilton gives
\[
C_\xi^2-\tr(C_\xi)C_\xi+\det(C_\xi)I_2=0,
\]
so each coordinate obeys the displayed order-at-most-two relation.  If
\(r_\star\neq1\), convergence puts \(r_n-1\) eventually at the fixed
nonzero sign of \(r_\star-1\); the same coordinate then realizes
\(d_n^+\).  If \(r_\star=1\), the seed \(u_0^+=\one\) is the unique fixed
ray, so \(C_\xi\one=\nu\one\) and
\(d_n^+=\nu^n\).  This proves both seed cases.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Forward fixed-wall and strict-wall tails}

\begin{proposition}[Forward wall scalar tails]
\label{prop:forward-wall-recurrence}
If the ordinary seed lies on the fixed wall ray, \(d_n^+\) is geometric.
For a strict wall tail, let the adjacent selector matrices be ordered so
that
\[
M_+=C_+C_-,\qquad M_-=C_-C_+.
\]
With
\[
\tau=\tr(M_+)=\tr(M_-),\qquad
\Delta=\det(M_+)=\det(M_-),
\]
one has, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
d^+_{n+4}=\tau d^+_{n+2}-\Delta d^+_n.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the fixed wall, Proposition~\ref{prop:wall-ray} sends the seed ray to
itself with multiplier \(\mu\), so the degree is geometric.  On a strict
tail, Proposition~\ref{prop:selectors} gives the two alternating step
orders.  The even and odd state subsequences are advanced by \(M_+\) and
\(M_-\), respectively.  Cyclicity of trace and multiplicativity of
determinant give their common \(\tau,\Delta\).  Cayley--Hamilton applied
to each product gives the same stride-two coordinate law.

A strict ordinary-seed orbit cannot have \(r_\star=1\), because
\(r_0=1\) would then be fixed.  Hence convergence stabilizes the same
visible coordinate on both parities beyond some \(n_{\mathrm{wall}}\).
Applying the two coordinate laws to that maximum proves the displayed
eventual scalar relation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Inverse chamber matrices}

The inverse recurrence starts from its own state equation.  If
\(\kappa s_n\) belongs to the chamber \(\xi\), then
\[
v^-_{n+1}=A_\xi Bv^-_n=D_\xi v^-_n.
\]
Moreover,
\[
D_\xi=A_\xi B=B^{-1}C_\xi B.
\]
The similarity identifies trace, determinant, and spectrum, but the first
display, together with inverse visibility, is the recurrence proof.  The
bridge instead controls \(\norminf{Bv_n^-}\), which can have a different
maximizing coordinate from \(\norminf{v_n^-}\).

\subsection{Inverse interior cases}

\begin{proposition}[Inverse interior scalar tails]
\label{prop:inverse-interior-recurrence}
If \(r_\star\) lies in the chamber \(\xi\), then \(d_n^-\) eventually
satisfies
\[
d^-_{n+2}=\tr(C_\xi)d^-_{n+1}-\det(C_\xi)d^-_n.
\]
When \(s_\star=1\), the inverse ordinary-seed sequence is geometric from
time zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since \(\kappa s_n\to r_\star\), there is \(n_{\mathrm{int}}\) such that
\[
v^-_{n+1}=D_\xi v^-_n\qquad(n\geq n_{\mathrm{int}}).
\]
Similarity gives
\[
\tr(D_\xi)=\tr(C_\xi),\qquad
\det(D_\xi)=\det(C_\xi),
\]
and Cayley--Hamilton gives the stated relation for each inverse state
coordinate.  If \(s_\star\neq1\), convergence makes the sign of
\(s_n-1\) eventually constant, so the same coordinate realizes
\(d_n^-\).  If \(s_\star=1\), the seed \(v^-_0=\one\) already lies on
the unique inverse fixed ray.  Thus
\(D_\xi\one=\nu\one\), \(v_n^-=\nu^n\one\), and the degree sequence is
geometric.  This establishes the inverse result without using the vector
bridge.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Inverse fixed-wall and strict-wall cases}

\begin{proposition}[Inverse wall parity tails]
\label{prop:inverse-wall-recurrence}
Assume \(\kappa s_\star=r_\star\) is a Newton wall.  If \(s_\star=1\),
the ordinary inverse seed is fixed and \(d_n^-\) is geometric.  Otherwise
a strict tail alternates the adjacent matrices \(D_-,D_+\).  With
\[
N_+=D_+D_-,\qquad N_-=D_-D_+,
\]
the two parity products satisfy
\[
N_\pm=B^{-1}M_\pm B,
\]
and, for all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
d^-_{n+4}=\tau d^-_{n+2}-\Delta d^-_n.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If \(s_\star=1\), then \(v_0^-=\one\) is the fixed inverse ray from time
zero.  Its intermediate state \(Bv_n^-\) remains on the tied Newton ray
\(\kappa s_\star=r_\star\).  The full face therefore gives the same
homogeneous output at every step, so \(v_n^-=\nu^n\one\) and \(d_n^-\)
is geometric.

For a strict ordinary inverse orbit, necessarily \(s_\star\neq1\);
otherwise its seed would already be fixed.  Scaling conjugacy and
Proposition~\ref{prop:selectors} give eventual adjacent-label
alternation, evaluated at \(\kappa s_n\).  Since
\(D_\xi=B^{-1}C_\xi B\), multiplying in the actual step order gives
\[
D_+D_-=B^{-1}C_+C_-B,\qquad
D_-D_+=B^{-1}C_-C_+B.
\]
Thus the two inverse parity products are similar to the corresponding
forward products.  Cayley--Hamilton gives the common stride-two
coordinate law.  Finally \(s_n\to s_\star\neq1\), so both parities
eventually lie in a neighborhood disjoint from \(1\); the same coordinate
then realizes the inverse maximum on each parity.  The scalar law follows.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Common coefficients, parity visibility, and indices}

The four products have shared coefficients
\[
\tau=\tr(M_+)=\tr(M_-)=\tr(N_+)=\tr(N_-),
\]
\[
\Delta=\det(M_+)=\det(M_-)=\det(N_+)=\det(N_-).
\]
The middle trace equality is cyclicity, the determinant equality is
multiplicativity, and the forward--inverse equalities follow from
similarity.  These equalities do not say that the matrices themselves are
equal.

Choose \(n_0\) larger than the selector thresholds and the forward and
inverse visibility thresholds.  Then the two independently proved laws
are
\[
d^\pm_{n+4}=\tau d^\pm_{n+2}-\Delta d^\pm_n
\qquad(n\geq n_0)
\]
on strict wall tails.  Each parity uses the product matching its actual
selector word.  The condition \(n\geq n_0\) is essential because a
coordinate recurrence cannot pass through a changing maximum before
visibility stabilizes.

\subsection{Consequence and boundary}

\begin{corollary}[Bidirectional recurrence upper bounds]
\label{cor:recurrences}
Forward and inverse interior degree tails have recurrence order at most
two; strict wall tails have interleaved recurrence order at most four;
fixed-ray tails are geometric.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine Propositions~\ref{prop:forward-interior-recurrence},
\ref{prop:forward-wall-recurrence},
\ref{prop:inverse-interior-recurrence}, and

\subsection{Failure modes tied to assumptions}

Allowing coordinate-axis support can make one face derivative vanish and
destroy the Hessian and independence certificates.  Allowing an exponent
equal to one removes a full coordinate copy from the strict carry.
Permitting a zero coefficient, an uncollected duplicate, or a support list
different from the actual collected polynomial makes the envelope record
a monomial that need not exist.  Positive characteristic can annihilate
\(x_0y_0(1-x_0-y_0)\) or a pure-power derivative.

A mixed term in \(W\), a nonseparated momentum Hamiltonian, or an
arbitrary Hamiltonian-shear extension replaces the diagonal power
transform and can destroy the scaling conjugacy and decreasing
projective map.  Infinite support removes the finite maximum used for
\(q<1\).  In dimension at least three there is no ordered one-dimensional
wall chart on which the side-swapping proof applies.  An altered phase
order, a different ordinary seed, or an arbitrary bridge seed changes the
base identity \(\Acal(\one)=c_\star\one\) and requires a new argument.
None of these failures is repaired here by a generic-coefficient
assumption.

\subsection{Mechanism-level collision subtraction}

Special two-term wall criteria, a forced two-label selector word,
two-step monodromy and parity mechanics, and interleaved-recurrence
framing are not presented as the contribution center.  Here such products
appear only after the arbitrary finite-support full-face theorem, the
global wall-safe contraction, exclusion of numerical cycles, the exact
inverse transport, and the common primitive-wall multiplier have been
proved.

Likewise, the article claims no support-rank bound, stationary sharp
construction, unbounded higher-dimensional Perron degree, or scalar
minimality.  The quadratic cap is a rigidity consequence for the narrower
planar separated family, not a contradiction to behavior in larger
families.  These are assumption-and-mechanism boundaries, not
literature-coverage claims.

\subsection{Remaining anti-claims}

The bridge is not a termwise equality of forward and inverse degree
sequences and is not a transfer proof for scalar recurrences.  Strict wall
label alternation is not a nontrivial numerical two-cycle or any other
numerical periodic orbit.  The recurrence orders are only at most two,
at most four, or geometric; no recurrence-minimality statement is made.

The theorem concerns ordinary degree growth and the first dynamical
degree only.  It gives no higher-dynamical-degree result, no entropy
conclusion beyond the contextual literature discussion, no
compactification claim, no integrability or nonintegrability conclusion,
and no periodic, Diophantine, or other arithmetic conclusion for point
orbits.  It neither classifies polynomial symplectomorphisms nor proves
nonconjugacy.  Coefficient uniformity is proved directly and is not a
genericity substitute.

Finally, there is no claim of support optimality, support-rank novelty, or
global quadratic sharpness for all Hamiltonian shears.  The contextual
survey is not exhaustive.  Global claim-level novelty remains unresolved,
and no priority, firstness, literature-level uniqueness, or
no-related-theorem assertion is made.

\section{Conclusion}

\subsection{The theorem in one chain}

For finite collected \(E\subset\Z_{\geq2}^2\), the isolated coefficient in
the full face-Hessian determinant turns a Newton-envelope prediction into
a cancellation-safe polynomial transport.  Strict lower and
cross-coordinate upper carries then identify the forward position and
inverse momentum blocks, while the ordinary seed supplies the exact
shifted vector bridge.  The finite envelope contracts uniformly in
logarithmic projective distance, including across walls, and its inverse
is obtained by a scaling conjugacy.  One fixed ray therefore controls
every selector tail: stationary in a chamber, or fixed/adjacent-alternating
at a wall.  This yields the interior quadratic-at-most versus wall-integral
spectral dichotomy and, through separate visible-state arguments,
bidirectional recurrence upper bounds.

\subsection{Open verification, not new mathematics}

The result remains bounded by its explicit characteristic-zero,
finite-support, two-dimensional, separated-power, phase-order, and seed
hypotheses.  Global claim-level novelty remains unresolved.  No broader
extension or additional invariant is asserted.
