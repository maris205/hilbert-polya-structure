## Problem Anchor

> For the normalized two-parameter Hénon family
> \(H_{a,c}(x,y)=(ay+x^d+c,x)\), with fixed integers \(d,n\ge2\), construct
> the generic cover of actual exact-period-\(n\) cycles without confusing the
> full fixed scheme, formal-period points, or special fibers; determine its
> cycle monodromy; and decide whether the cycle observables
> \(\tau=\sum_i z_i\) and
> \(\rho=\operatorname{tr}(DH_{a,c}^{n})\) each generate the generic
> primitive-cycle field.

## Category, Notation, and Boundary

Fix the integers \(d,n\ge2\) throughout.  Work over

\[
A=\mathbb Q[a,c],\qquad K=\operatorname{Frac}(A)=\mathbb Q(a,c),
\]

with the normalized family

\[
H_{a,c}(x,y)=(ay+x^d+c,x).
\]

Writing a marked orbit as \((z_i,z_{i-1})\), with indices modulo \(n\), gives

\[
z_{i+1}=z_i^d+a z_{i-1}+c.
\]

Set

\[
\nu=\nu(d,n)=\sum_{e\mid n}\mu(n/e)d^e,
\qquad r=r(d,n)=\frac{\nu}{n}.
\]

Here \(\nu\) is the generic number of actual exact-period-\(n\) points and
\(r\) is the generic number of primitive cycles.  The proposal is about this
fixed normalized family and its affine parameter plane.  It does not claim a
classification for arbitrary polynomial automorphisms or arbitrary Hénon
normal forms.

## Technical Gap

The cyclic fixed-point equations are elementary, but five distinctions are
essential and are often erased by shorthand:

1. \(\operatorname{Fix}(H^n)\) contains lower actual periods.
2. A formal dynatomic point at a branch parameter can also have lower actual
   period.
3. The generic actual-exact-period algebra need not be represented by an
   everywhere embedded actual-period subscheme of the full cyclic algebra.
4. Relative normalization is not the same operation as taking a convenient
   embedded coordinate ring, and normalization need not commute with a
   special-fiber base change automatically.
5. A cycle function being nonconstant does not make it primitive until its
   stabilizer is controlled by monodromy.

The missing contribution is therefore not another orbit count.  It is a
single scheme/field/monodromy chain that makes the normalized primitive-cycle
cover precise and then certifies two intrinsic generators of its function
field.

## Direct Prior Art and Residual Delta

The scalar special fiber imports stronger primitive-generator results than the
v1 proposal disclosed.

- Morton (1996), *On certain algebraic curves related to polynomial maps*,
  proves on p. 336 for \(f_c(z)=z^d+c\) that the orbit-shift fixed field is
  generated by
  \[
  w=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}f_c'(f_c^i(z))
   =d^n\prod_i z_i^{d-1}
   =\rho|_{a=0}
  \]
  for every \(d\ge2\).  For \(d=2\), Corollary 3 and p. 336 also prove that
  the scalar orbit sum \(t=\sum_i f_c^i(z)=\tau|_{a=0}\) generates the same
  fixed field.  The 2011 corrigendum concerns a finite-characteristic lifting
  argument and does not retract these characteristic-zero fixed-field results.
- Therefore scalar \(\rho\)-primitivity for all \(d\), and scalar
  \(\tau\)-primitivity for \(d=2\), are direct prior art.  PC2's residual
  contribution is their passage through PC1's two-parameter relative
  normalization, exact scalar fiber, and global \(S_r\) cycle cover; uniform
  all-\(d\) \(\tau\)-generation, especially the separate \(d\ge3\)
  asymptotic; and the integral basis-free characteristic polynomials in
  \(A[T]\).
- Cantat--Dujardin (2026), Section 3.2 and Theorem A/Theorem 3.7, define
  formal-period derivative-trace multisets and prove finite determination of
  Hénon maps from finitely many trace spectra.  This is direct adjacency to
  \(\rho\), not a PC2 theorem collision: their result uses several-period
  formal trace spectra for finite map recovery, while PC2 treats one fixed
  actual-period cycle cover and primitive generation of its function field.

No novelty is claimed for scalar multiplier/orbit-sum generators, formal
trace-spectrum rigidity, Gröbner bases, normalization, Reynolds averaging,
wreath monodromy, or primitive-element methods themselves.

## Method Thesis and Contribution Focus

**One-sentence thesis.**  For fixed \(d,n\ge2\), the generic actual
exact-period factor of the normalized two-parameter Hénon recurrence has a
geometrically integral, finite locally free relative normalization whose
scalar special fiber is the affine dynatomic cover, whose cyclic quotient has
full \(S_r\) monodromy, and whose cycle field is generated separately by
either the coordinate sum \(\tau\) or the pointwise derivative trace
\(\rho\).

- **PC1, dominant contribution:** construct the normalized primitive-point
  cover and its cyclic cycle quotient, prove the exact \(a=0\) fiber, and
  determine full symmetric monodromy on primitive cycles.
- **PC2, supporting contribution:** prove independently that \(\tau\) and
  \(\rho\) are primitive generators and that their characteristic polynomials
  are irreducible of degree \(r\).
- **Explicit non-contributions:** no arbitrary-Hénon extension, no global
  embedded formal-period subscheme, no statement that all special fibers are
  smooth or reduced, no everywhere free torsor, no projective compactification,
  and no numerical, prime, modulus, or parameter-search theorem.

## PC1: The Normalized Primitive-Period Cover

### 1. Full cyclic algebra

Define

\[
B_n=A[z_0,\ldots,z_{n-1}]/(F_0,\ldots,F_{n-1}),
\qquad
F_i=z_i^d+a z_{i-1}+c-z_{i+1}.
\]

In a graded term order, \(\operatorname{LM}(F_i)=z_i^d\).  These leading
monomials are pairwise coprime, so the \(F_i\) form a monic Gröbner basis over
\(A\).  Consequently

\[
\left\{\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}z_i^{e_i}:0\le e_i<d\right\}
\]

is an \(A\)-basis and \(B_n\) is finite free of rank \(d^n\).  This algebra
represents the full cyclic fixed scheme, not the primitive cover.

### 2. Generic actual-exact-period field

On the generic étale locus of \(B_n\otimes_AK\), take the canonical
idempotent block whose geometric points have actual exact period \(n\); denote
it by \(E_n\).  Its \(K\)-dimension is \(\nu\).

On \(a=0\), the recurrence reduces to the scalar map \(f_c(z)=z^d+c\).  The
irreducibility of the scalar dynatomic algebra over \(\mathbb Q(c)\), combined
with Henselian idempotent lifting, rules out a nontrivial decomposition of
\(E_n\).  Thus \(E_n\) is a single field of degree \(\nu\) over \(K\).

This step is deliberately generic and actual-period.  It does not identify
formal and actual periods at branch parameters.

### 3. Relative normalization and exact scalar special fiber

Let

\[
S=\operatorname{Norm}_{A}(E_n)
\]

be the integral closure of \(A\) in \(E_n\).  Excellence of \(A\) makes
\(S\) finite.  A normal finite surface is Cohen--Macaulay; over the regular
surface \(\operatorname{Spec}A\), miracle flatness makes \(S\) locally free of
rank \(\nu\).  A separate constants argument proves that
\(\operatorname{Spec}S\) is geometrically integral over \(\mathbb Q\).

Let \(\Phi_{d,n}(z,c)\) denote the scalar dynatomic polynomial and put

\[
D_n=\mathbb Q[c,z]/(\Phi_{d,n}(z,c)).
\]

The special-fiber statement is a proof obligation, not a formal base-change
identity.  The proposed proof establishes the unique height-one prime over
\((a)\), ramification index \(e=1\), and the multiplicity-one divisor of
\(a\); it then constructs the finite birational comparison with the normal
scalar dynatomic algebra and uses flatness/Cohen--Macaulayness to exclude a
nilpotent kernel.  The result is

\[
S/aS\cong D_n.
\]

In particular, this named scalar fiber is reduced and has no hidden
nilpotents.  The equality does not say that every other fiber is reduced,
smooth, or étale.

### 4. Cyclic quotient

Time shift

\[
\sigma(z_i)=z_{i+1}
\]

acts faithfully with order \(n\) on \(E_n\).  Define

\[
S_0=S^{\langle\sigma\rangle},
\qquad
F=\operatorname{Frac}(S_0)=E_n^{\langle\sigma\rangle}.
\]

Since the characteristic is zero, Reynolds averaging is exact and gives the
required arbitrary base-change compatibility for this finite invariant
algebra.  Therefore \(S_0\) is locally free of rank
\(r\) and

\[
S_0/aS_0\cong D_n^{C_n}.
\]

The scalar special cycle object is the affine scheme

\[
X_0^{\mathrm{aff}}(n)=\operatorname{Spec}(D_n^{C_n}).
\]

It is not called a projective compactification.

### 5. Geometric monodromy

Over the good part of the scalar line, the scalar dynatomic point cover has
the full wreath action \(C_n\wr S_r\).  The comparison uses the direction

\[
G_{\mathrm{special\ line}}\ \subseteq\ G_{\mathrm{global}},
\]

up to conjugacy in the common permutation representation.  Conversely,
global point monodromy commutes with time shift and is bounded by the wreath
centralizer.  Passing through that marked-point comparison, the induced
action on the cyclic quotient is

\[
G_{\mathrm{cycles}}=S_r.
\]

When \(r=1\), this statement is trivial and supplies no evidence for a
nontrivial monodromy mechanism.

## PC2: Two Separate Primitive Cycle Coordinates

### 1. Definitions and cyclic invariance

Define the orbit-coordinate sum

\[
\tau=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}z_i
\]

and the pointwise derivative-return trace

\[
\rho=\operatorname{tr}
\left(
DH_{a,c}(z_{n-1},z_{n-2})\cdots
DH_{a,c}(z_0,z_{n-1})
\right),
\]

where

\[
DH_{a,c}(z_i,z_{i-1})=
\begin{pmatrix}
d z_i^{d-1}&a\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Cyclic relabeling fixes \(\tau\).  Cyclic invariance of matrix-product trace
fixes \(\rho\).  Both observables are integral polynomial functions on
\(S\), so in fact \(\tau,\rho\in S_0\subset F\).

The notation must remain categorical:

- \(\rho\) is not the field trace \(\operatorname{Tr}_{F/K}(\rho)\);
- \(\rho\) is not the base function
  \(\det(DH^n)=(-a)^n\);
- \(\tau\) is not an algebra trace.

### 2. Separate non-base lemmas

For \(r>1\), prove separately that \(\tau\notin K\) and \(\rho\notin K\).
Morton (1996) already supplies the scalar generator, hence non-base, statement
for \(\rho\) for every \(d\), and for \(\tau\) when \(d=2\).  The source proof
also retains bounded symbolic comparisons of primitive Puiseux words, including
the separate \(d\ge3\) input for \(\tau\):

- for \(d\ge3\), compare a primitive word ending in \(\zeta\) with the one
  ending in \(\zeta^2\);
- for \(d=2,n\ge3\), compare a primitive word with one \(-1\) with a
  primitive word having two adjacent \(-1\)'s.

The leading coordinate sums differ, and the leading derivative-trace products
differ.  These are symbolic word classes for the proof, not a \((d,n)\) scan
or stored exploratory table.

### 3. From non-base to primitive

In the Galois closure of \(F/K\), the stabilizer of a marked cycle is
\(S_{r-1}\).  For \(r>1\), this subgroup is maximal in \(S_r\).  Each cycle
observable is fixed by \(S_{r-1}\); the separate non-base lemma says it is not
fixed by all of \(S_r\).  Its stabilizer is therefore exactly
\(S_{r-1}\).  Hence

\[
K(\tau)=F=K(\rho).
\]

For \(s\in\{\tau,\rho\}\), define the basis-free multiplication
characteristic polynomial

\[
\chi_s(T)=\det\!\left(T\operatorname{id}_{S_0}-m_s\right)\in A[T],
\]

where the determinant is taken on \(\bigwedge_A^rS_0\), with no global basis
chosen.  After extension to \(K\),
\(\chi_s(T)=N_{F/K}(T-s)\).  For each observable, the minimal polynomial,
multiplication characteristic polynomial, and norm polynomial agree and are
irreducible of degree \(r\) over \(K\); monicity and primitivity keep
\(\chi_s\) in \(A[T]\).  A top exterior-power or pairwise-difference product
records that the generic roots are distinct.  Non-base behavior alone is never
presented as a substitute for the \(S_r\)-stabilizer step.

### 4. The degree-one boundary

For \((d,n)=(2,2)\),

\[
\nu=2,\qquad r=1,
\]

and the exact two-cycle equations give

\[
\tau=z_0+z_1=a-1,
\qquad
z_0z_1=(a-1)^2+c,
\]

\[
\rho=4z_0z_1+2a=4a^2-6a+4+4c.
\]

Thus

\[
\chi_\tau(T)=T-(a-1),
\qquad
\chi_\rho(T)=T-(4a^2-6a+4+4c).
\]

This is the correct degree-one interpretation of PC2.  It is not evidence for
nontrivial \(S_r\) monodromy and does not support a blanket claim that the
observables are non-base.

## Proof Architecture

The proposed source proof should close in this order:

1. monic Gröbner basis and rank \(d^n\) for \(B_n\);
2. generic separability, actual-period idempotent decomposition, and Möbius
   degree \(\nu\);
3. scalar dynatomic field plus Henselian idempotent lifting, proving \(E_n\) is
   one field;
4. finite normalization, Cohen--Macaulay/miracle-flatness, geometric
   integrality, and exact special fiber via the unique \(a\)-adic prime,
   \(e=1\), finite birational comparison, and nilpotent exclusion;
5. Reynolds invariants and the affine cyclic quotient;
6. good-special-line subgroup inclusion plus the time-shift centralizer bound,
   yielding full \(S_r\) cycle monodromy;
7. separate infinity-word non-base lemmas for \(\tau\) and \(\rho\);
8. maximal-stabilizer primitivity, basis-free irreducible multiplication
   characteristic polynomials, and the explicit \((2,2)\) boundary.

No step may be replaced by a computer-algebra output.

## Mandatory Falsifiers and Scope Guards

1. **Formal/lower-period:**
   \(f_{-3/4}(-1/2)=-1/2\), multiplier \(-1\), and
   \(\Phi_2(-1/2,-3/4)=0\).
2. **Nonreduced special fiber:**
   \(\mathbb Q[a,t]/(t^2-a)\) specializes at \(a=0\) to
   \(\mathbb Q[t]/(t^2)\).
3. **Normal overring:**
   \(\mathbb Q[t^2,t^3]\subsetneq\mathbb Q[t]\).
4. **Degree-one cycle boundary:** the exact \((2,2)\) formulas above.
5. **Base-function attack:** reject \(\tau\in K\) or \(\rho\in K\) for
   \(r>1\), but accept both in \(K\) when \(r=1\).
6. **Monodromy direction:** reject
   \(G_{\mathrm{global}}\subseteq G_{\mathrm{special}}\) as the proof step.
7. **Trace-category attack:** reject replacement of \(\rho\) by either
   \(\operatorname{Tr}_{F/K}(\rho)\) or \((-a)^n\).
