\begin{abstract}
We study a proof-certified degree mechanism for compositions of separated
Hamiltonian shears.  Let the coefficient field have characteristic zero, let
$r\geq 3$, and let the collected supports of the two potentials be finite,
nonempty subsets of $\mathbb Z_{\geq 2}^{\,r}$.  A strict edge records unique
exposed support selectors, positive fresh carries, and the target
certificates needed for the next phase.  For every such edge, a grouped
positive-face Hessian argument and the Jacobian criterion give
coefficient-uniform survival of the exposed gradients under injective
substitution.  The leading phase is therefore the typed linear action
$(u,w)\mapsto(B_\beta A_\alpha u,A_\alpha u)$, with
$A_\alpha=\ones\alpha^{\trans}-I$ and
$B_\beta=\ones\beta^{\trans}-I$.  The two-phase degree vector translates in
the all-ones direction.  On every infinite certified strict branch, the
total-degree envelopes have at most $d_V+d_W-2$ selector changes, where
$d_V,d_W$ count distinct support totals, and the stationary tail is the
global-origin affine recurrence
$t_{n+1}=\lambda t_n+\mu$ with
$\lambda=(|\alpha|-1)(|\beta|-1)$.  Coordinate reversal yields a
phase-resolved inverse reciprocity criterion on observable spans; full
matrices occur only for full spans and only with reflected certificates.  A
four-projection margin lemma is proved for one typed pair core and one
forward/reflected-inverse step.  An exact asymmetric three-dimensional
fixture and two failure fixtures delimit the assumptions.  All statements
are certified-branch results; no empirical, numerical, or computational
evidence is used.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and contribution boundary}

A pair of nonlinear shears can look deceptively simple when written in
canonical coordinates.  Let $q,p\in K^r$ and consider
\[
 S_V(q,p)=(q,p+\nabla V(q)),\qquad
 T_W(q,p)=(q+\nabla W(p),p).
\]
Each map is triangular, but the leading weighted degrees of an iterate are
not determined by one scalar degree in general.  A support exponent can win
at one phase, a different exponent can win after the fresh block is
substituted, and the inverse reads the phases in the opposite order.  The
resulting selector word is a finite-state object coupled to a growing
degree vector.  The purpose of this article is to isolate a regime in which
that coupling has a rigid, auditable form.

The useful hypothesis is positivity of every support coordinate together
with a strict certificate at the edge being followed.  Positivity makes all
support totals at least $2r$, but it does not by itself remove ties or
cancellation.  We therefore separate three logically different tasks.
First, a grouped Hessian determinant supplies noncancellation for every
nonzero coefficient choice, and a Jacobian argument transfers this
independence through a fresh substitution.  Second, strict carries make the
formal degree action an actual leading-form action.  Third, support
functions along the resulting diagonal ray reduce the selector problem to
upper envelopes of lines indexed by total degree.  Keeping these tasks
separate is what permits exact failure boundaries.

The main contributions are four bounded statements.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item For every certified strict edge, the exposed gradient blocks survive
  uniformly over the coefficient class and the forward phase is
  $(u,w)\mapsto(B_\beta A_\alpha u,A_\alpha u)$.  The inverse is obtained in
  W-first, V-second order, with subtraction signs.
\item Every certified two-phase edge translates $u$ by a positive multiple
  of $\ones$.  Along an infinite certified strict branch, equal-total walls
  are invariant and unequal-total walls are crossed at most once, giving the
  bound $d_V+d_W-2$.  Once the selector pair is stationary, the exact
  affine tail uses the original seed as its origin.
\item Coordinate reversal gives a necessary and sufficient phase-resolved
  reciprocity test on the spans actually observed by the certified integer
  cell.  A proper span is not silently promoted to a full matrix identity.
\item A compact, normalized four-projection margin condition preserves one
  forward and one reflected inverse leading step on one typed pair core.
  It deliberately stops short of target-core inclusion and all-iterate
  perturbation stability.
\end{enumerate}

The hypotheses are not presentation choices.  If an exponent has a zero or
unit coordinate, the determinant witness can vanish.  If a carry is
nonpositive, an old block can cancel a fresh block.  If a reflected support
certificate is missing, the first inverse phase can already disagree.  The
exact examples in Sections 7 and 8 make these boundaries visible.

The article is organized as follows.  Section 2 positions the mechanism
against polynomial automorphisms, Newton and tropical degree methods,
Hamiltonian shears, and dynamical-degree work.  Section 3 fixes typed cells
and states the omnibus theorem.  Sections 4 and 5 prove survival, transport,
translation, and the wall bound.  Section 6 treats reversal and observable
spans.  Section 7 proves the local margin lemma and gives an exact fixture.
Section 8 records limitations and a reproducibility statement.

\section{Related work and claim-level collision positioning}

The surrounding literature supplies several adjacent languages but not the
conjunction used here.  Reversors and polynomial symmetries in the plane
provide terminology for phase reversal and fixed sets
\cite{gomez_meiss_2004}; filtrations for polynomial automorphisms provide a
broad forward/backward escape viewpoint \cite{shafikov_wolf_2003}.  Neither
reference imposes positive collected Newton supports in dimension at least
three, nor do they give the typed phase action proved below.

A second family studies monomial degree growth and its linear recurrences.
The degree sequences and entropy questions for monomial maps
\cite{hasselblatt_propp_2007} and matrix-induced recurrences
\cite{bedford_kim_2008} are useful contrasts.  Polytope-algebra and mixed
volume methods sharpen this toric perspective
\cite{favre_wulcan_2012}.  Our envelope variable is instead a support
function along a diagonal ray, and the affine tail is conditional on a
certified stationary selector pair.  There is no claim that our matrices
compute a global dynamical degree.

Symplectic and Hamiltonian contexts form a third family.  Cluster-algebra
constructions exhibit symplectic birational maps with tropical recurrences
\cite{fordy_hone_2011}, while polynomial Hamiltonian flows with special
straight-line orbits are studied in \cite{koch_lomeli_2014}.  Structural
results on polynomial symplectomorphisms
\cite{janeczko_jelonek_2008} and shear generation for Hamiltonian maps
\cite{berger_turaev_2025} explain why separated shears are a natural
laboratory.  The present result is narrower: it is an exact leading-form
statement for collected positive supports, not a classification of
Hamiltonian maps or a generation theorem.

Dynamical-degree work for affine-triangular automorphisms
\cite{blanc_van_santen_2022}, spectral interpretations
\cite{dang_favre_2021}, and monotonicity for Hénon-like families
\cite{bianchi_dinh_rakhimov_2024} illustrates why degree growth can have
global geometric structure.  Newton and tropical containment tools
\cite{el_hilany_2024,grigoriev_containment} motivate our use of exposed
faces and strict polyhedral certificates.  Recent dimension-four
affine-triangular results \cite{shao_sun_2025} and tropical degree work
\cite{nisse_2026} are current adjacent contexts, but use different map
classes and do not supply a reflected phase state.

Orbit graphs and finite-window functoriality
\cite{takenawa_2026}, as well as relative dynamical degrees for twisted
rational maps \cite{abboud_xie_2026}, are useful negative controls: a finite
selector word here is certified edge by edge and does not automatically
close under an ambient graph or a rational fibration.  Higher-dimensional
degree-growth examples \cite{deserti_2018} and general inverse-degree bounds
\cite{cheng_wang_yu_1994} likewise do not imply the exact inverse
reciprocity in Section 6.

The public literature check underlying these comparisons is bounded through
2026-08-29 UTC.  It is a bounded public screen rather than an exhaustive
survey or a priority statement.  The closest mechanisms are summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:related}.

\section{Typed setup, strict cells, and omnibus theorem}

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero and let $r\geq3$.  Let
\[
 E_V,E_W\subset\mathbb Z_{\geq2}^{\,r}
\]
be finite, nonempty, collected supports.  Write
\[
 V(q)=\sum_{\alpha\in E_V}c_\alpha q^\alpha,\qquad
 W(p)=\sum_{\beta\in E_W}d_\beta p^\beta,
 \qquad c_\alpha,d_\beta\in K^\times .
\]
The variables and incoming leading tuples used for degree bookkeeping are
in disjoint blocks.  A degree vector $u\in\RR_{>0}^r$ assigns
$\deg_u(q^\alpha)=\alpha\cdot u$.  A seed consists of positive integer
vectors and algebraically independent incoming leading blocks; disjoint
fresh blocks realize every declared integer seed.

Define the shears and their exposed actions by
\[
 S_V(q,p)=(q,p+\nabla V(q)),\quad
 T_W(q,p)=(q+\nabla W(p),p),\quad F=T_W\circ S_V,
\]
\[
 A_\alpha=\ones\alpha^{\trans}-I,\qquad
 B_\beta=\ones\beta^{\trans}-I.
\]
The support functions are
\[
 \hV(u)=\max_{\alpha\in E_V}\alpha\cdot u,\qquad
 \hW(v)=\max_{\beta\in E_W}\beta\cdot v.
\]

For a label pair $e=(\alpha,\beta)$, define the u-only cone
\[
 \Kcone=\{u\in\RR_{>0}^r:
 \alpha\text{ is the unique V maximizer at }u,\ 
 \beta\text{ is the unique W maximizer at }A_\alpha u,
 \ B_\beta A_\alpha u-u>0,
 \text{ and all listed target/reflection score checks involving only }u
 \text{ hold}\}.
\]
The source fresh carry and the pair-dependent predicates are kept separate:
\[
 \Ccell=\{(u,w)\in\RR_{>0}^r\times\RR_{>0}^r:
 u\in\Kcone,\quad A_\alpha u-w>0,\quad
 \PairGaps(u,w)>0\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]
All vector inequalities are componentwise.  The integer cell and observable
spans are
\[
 \CZcell=\Ccell\cap(\mathbb Z_{>0}^r)^2,\qquad
 U_e=\SpanR\{u:(u,w)\in\CZcell\},\quad
 V_e=\SpanR\{A_\alpha u:(u,w)\in\CZcell\}.
\tag{3.2}
\]
The reflected cell is $\Rstate(\Ccell)$ and its pair predicates are checked
after the swap; they are not folded into $\Kcone$.

\begin{definition}[Certified strict branch]
A finite or infinite edge word is certified when every edge has unique
selectors, positive source and target carries, a nonempty integer cell, and
the printed reflected score, carry, and target certificates required for
the phases being asserted.  A finite word may stop at a tie, failed carry,
empty cell, or missing certificate.  The phrase "infinite certified strict
branch" always includes all these certificates at every edge.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Certified phase, translation, and reciprocity theorem]
\label{thm:main}
For the supports and shears above, the following assertions hold.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\Alph*)]
\item For every certified strict edge and every nonzero coefficient choice
  in the stated class, the grouped positive-face Hessian is nonzero and its
  face-gradient components are algebraically independent.  Injective
  substitution and strict carries give the forward leading action
  \[
  (u,w)\longmapsto(B_\beta A_\alpha u,A_\alpha u).
  \]
  The inverse uses W first and V second, with subtraction signs.
\item On every certified edge,
  \[
  v=\hV(u)\ones-u,\qquad
  u'=\hW(v)\ones-v=u+\delta(u)\ones,\qquad \delta(u)>0.
  \]
  On every infinite certified strict branch, the number of selector changes
  is at most $d_V+d_W-2$, where $d_V,d_W$ count distinct support totals.
  After a stationary selector pair is reached,
  \[
  t_{n+1}=\lambda t_n+\mu,\quad
  \lambda=(|\alpha|-1)(|\beta|-1),\quad
  \mu=((|\beta|-1)\alpha-\beta)\cdot u_0,
  \]
  with $u_n=u_0+t_n\ones$ and the global origin $t_0=0$.
\item With coordinate reversal $R$ and
  $\Rstate(u,w)=(Rw,Ru)$, reflected inverse reciprocity is necessary and
  sufficient edgewise on $U_e,V_e$, provided reflected selector, carry, and
  target certificates are present.  Full-matrix identities are asserted
  only when the corresponding spans are full.
\item If a single typed pair core has the four normalized projections and
  selected-minus-new margins of Lemma~\ref{lem:margin}, then exactly one
  forward and one reflected inverse leading step are preserved.  No
  target-core inclusion or all-iterate stability is asserted.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section{Positive-face survival and degree transport proofs}

We begin with the canonical geometry, because the phase order matters.
The Jacobians are
\[
 DS_V=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\ \Hess V&I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 DT_W=\begin{pmatrix}I&\Hess W\\0&I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The Hessians are symmetric.  With
$J=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&I\\-I&0\end{psmallmatrix}$, direct multiplication
gives
\[
 DS_V^{\trans}JDS_V=J,\qquad DT_W^{\trans}JDT_W=J.
\]
Thus $S_V,T_W$, and $F$ are polynomial symplectomorphisms.  Their inverses
are
\[
 S_V^{-1}(q,p)=(q,p-\nabla V(q)),\qquad
 T_W^{-1}(q,p)=(q-\nabla W(p),p),
\]
so
\[
 F^{-1}=S_V^{-1}\circ T_W^{-1}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
The inverse order is W first and V second; the minus signs are nonzero
scalars in $K$.

Let $E_0$ be an exposed face of either support and set
\[
 P_{E_0}(X)=\sum_{\alpha\in E_0}c_\alpha X^\alpha,\qquad
 h_{ij}(\alpha)=\alpha_i(\alpha_j-\delta_{ij}).
\]
Grouping the determinant by row-labelled tuples gives
\[
 \det\Hess P_{E_0}
 =
 \sum_{(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r)\in E_0^r}
 \left(\prod_i c_{\alpha_i}\right)
 D(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r)X^{\sum_i\alpha_i-2\ones},
\tag{4.2}
\]
where $D(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r)=
\det[h_{ij}(\alpha_i)]$.  Choose a generic secondary linear weight on
$E_0$ with a unique minimizer $\alpha_0$.  The tuple
$(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_0)$ is the unique lowest secondary-weight group.
Its matrix is
\[
 [h_{ij}(\alpha_0)]
 =\operatorname{diag}(\alpha_0)
   (\ones\alpha_0^{\trans}-I).
\]
The matrix determinant lemma therefore yields
\[
 D(\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_0)
 =(-1)^r(1-|\alpha_0|)\prod_i\alpha_{0i}\neq0.
\tag{4.3}
\]
The associated coefficient is
$c_{\alpha_0}^{\,r}(-1)^r(1-|\alpha_0|)\prod_i\alpha_{0i}$.
It cannot vanish: the field has characteristic zero, every coefficient is
nonzero, and every support coordinate is at least two.  Thus the grouped
determinant is nonzero for every coefficient choice in the class.  The
argument covers a one-point face and a tied face.  In the tied case it is
only an auxiliary wall certificate; it does not select a tied vertex.

The characteristic-zero Jacobian criterion now implies that the components
of $\nabla P_{E_0}$ are algebraically independent.  If
$Z_1,\ldots,Z_r$ are independent incoming leading forms, the substitution
$X_i\mapsto Z_i$ is injective on the polynomial algebra generated by the
face tuple.  A selected face gradient therefore cannot disappear through a
hidden coefficient cancellation.  For a strict singleton this is also
visible from
\[
 \det A_\alpha=(-1)^r(1-|\alpha|)\neq0.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Suppose $\alpha$ is the unique V selector at $u$ and $\beta$ is the unique
W selector at $A_\alpha u$.  Each component of $\nabla V$ has fresh
weighted degree $A_\alpha u$.  The first strict carry
\[
 A_\alpha u-w>0
\tag{4.5}
\]
places this block above the old $p$ block.  The W gradient then has degree
$B_\beta A_\alpha u$, and the second carry
\[
 B_\beta A_\alpha u-u>0
\tag{4.6}
\]
places it above the old $q$ block.  Algebraic independence prevents
cancellation inside a fresh block, while (4.5)--(4.6) prevent cancellation
with an old block.  Consequently
\[
 (u,w)\longmapsto (u',v)=(B_\beta A_\alpha u,A_\alpha u).
\tag{4.7}
\]
For the inverse, start at $\Rstate(u,w)$, use the reflected W label first,
then the reflected V label, and multiply the fresh blocks by $-1$.  No
nonzero scalar sign changes a strict leading degree.  If a selector ties, a
carry fails, or the integer cell is empty, the edge is not certified and no
implicit equality is claimed.

A tied-face Hessian certificate deserves one further warning.  It proves
independence of the whole face gradient after a generic secondary
specialization, but it does not choose one vertex on the primary wall.
Selector uniqueness remains a separate strict hypothesis.

\section{Translation, wall monotonicity, and affine tail proofs}

Let $s=\alpha\cdot u=\hV(u)$ on a certified edge.  The first phase gives
\[
 v=A_\alpha u=s\ones-u=\hV(u)\ones-u.
\tag{5.1}
\]
For the selected $\beta$,
\[
 \beta\cdot A_\alpha u=|\beta|s-\beta\cdot u.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
 u'&=B_\beta v=(\beta\cdot v)\ones-v\\
 &=u+\bigl((|\beta|-1)\alpha-\beta\bigr)\cdot u\;\ones.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]
The support-function form of the increment is
\[
 \delta(u)=\hW(v)-\hV(u)
 =\bigl((|\beta|-1)\alpha-\beta\bigr)\cdot u>0.
\tag{5.3}
\]
The final inequality is precisely the second strict carry.  For an integer
seed, all matrices are integral, so $\delta(u)\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1}$.
Iterating (5.1)--(5.3) gives
\[
 u_n=u_0+t_n\ones,\qquad t_0=0,\qquad
 t_{n+1}-t_n=\delta(u_n)>0.
\tag{5.4}
\]
Thus the diagonal direction is an actual weighted-degree identity, not only
a formal matrix property.

The selector count is controlled by total-degree classes.  Put
\[
 T_V=\{|\alpha|:\alpha\in E_V\},\quad
 T_W=\{|\beta|:\beta\in E_W\},\quad
 d_V=|T_V|,\quad d_W=|T_W|.
\]
Along $u(t)=u_0+t\ones$ define
\[
 M_s=\max_{\substack{\alpha\in E_V\\|\alpha|=s}}\alpha\cdot u_0,
 \qquad
 \hV(u(t))=\max_{s\in T_V}(M_s+st),
\]
and
\[
 g(t)=\hV(u(t))-t=\max_{s\in T_V}(M_s+(s-1)t).
\tag{5.5}
\]
Every slope $s-1$ is positive.  If $t_2>t_1$, an argmax line at $t_1$
has strictly increased by $(s-1)(t_2-t_1)$, so $g(t_2)>g(t_1)$.

Equation (5.1) becomes
\[
 v(t)=g(t)\ones-u_0.
\tag{5.6}
\]
For $\beta,\eta\in E_W$,
\[
 \beta\cdot v(t)-\eta\cdot v(t)
 =(|\beta|-|\eta|)g(t)-(\beta-\eta)\cdot u_0.
\tag{5.7}
\]
Thus W selectors form an upper envelope of $d_W$ lines in the increasing
variable $g$.  A wall between unequal totals is crossed at most once.  A
wall between equal totals is invariant along the ray, because the first
term in (5.7) vanishes.  The V envelope has at most $d_V-1$ changes and
the W envelope at most $d_W-1$, so an infinite strict phase word satisfies
\[
 \#\{\text{selector changes}\}\leq(d_V-1)+(d_W-1)
 =d_V+d_W-2.
\tag{5.8}
\]
A discrete branch can skip a wall and only lower this count.  A finite branch
may terminate at a tie, failed carry, empty cell, or missing certificate;
(5.8) is not applied beyond that stopping event.

After finitely many changes on an infinite branch, fix a stationary pair
$(\alpha,\beta)$ and write
\[
 c=(|\beta|-1)\alpha-\beta.
\]
Since $c\cdot\ones=(|\alpha|-1)(|\beta|-1)-1$, (5.3) and (5.4) give
\[
 t_{n+1}=\lambda t_n+\mu,\qquad
 \lambda=1+c\cdot\ones=(|\alpha|-1)(|\beta|-1),\qquad
 \mu=c\cdot u_0.
\tag{5.9}
\]
The origin is global: $t_0=0$ and $u_0$ is the original seed.  Resetting the
origin after a transient selector change produces a different intercept
and is not the claimed formula.  Positivity gives $|\alpha|,|\beta|\geq
2r$, hence $\lambda>1$.  No minimal scalar recurrence, Perron degree,
entropy, or higher dynamical-degree conclusion follows from (5.9).

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.92\linewidth}
\textbf{Envelope picture.}
The ray $u_0+t\ones$ is parameterized by $t$.  The V envelope is the maximum
of lines $M_s+st$; subtracting $t$ gives $g(t)$ with positive slopes
$s-1$.  The W envelope is the maximum of lines in the increasing variable
$g(t)$.  Equal-total walls are parallel to the ray and unequal-total walls
are single-crossing.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

\section{Reflected inverse reciprocity and its span criterion}

Let $R$ reverse coordinates, so $R\ones=\ones$, $R^2=I$, and
$R^{\trans}=R$.  Write $\bar\alpha=R\alpha$ and $\bar\beta=R\beta$.  A
direct calculation gives
\[
 B_{\bar\alpha}R
 =(\ones(R\alpha)^{\trans}-I)R
 =\ones\alpha^{\trans}-R
 =RA_\alpha,
\]
and similarly
\[
 A_{\bar\beta}R=RB_\beta.
\tag{6.1}
\]
For $v=A_\alpha u$ and $u'=B_\beta v$, starting at
$\Rstate(u,w)=(Rw,Ru)$, the reflected inverse phase is
\[
 B_{\bar\alpha}Ru=RA_\alpha u=Rv,\qquad
 A_{\bar\beta}Rv=RB_\beta v=Ru'.
\tag{6.2}
\]
The first step uses the reflected W label and the second the reflected V
label.  It is not the ordinary forward order with a permutation appended.

The observable spans in (3.2) encode exactly what integer seeds can test.
We state the equivalence as a proposition.

\begin{proposition}[Edgewise reciprocity]
\label{prop:reciprocity}
Fix a certified edge $e=(\alpha,\beta)$.  Assume the reflected score,
carry, and target certificates hold on the reflected cell.  The following
are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For every integer seed in $\CZcell$, the phase-resolved one-step
  forward vector and the reflected inverse vector agree, and the equality
  persists along every edge of a finite certified seed-indexed word.
\item The restrictions
\[
 \left.B_{\bar\alpha}R\right|_{U_e}
 =\left.RA_\alpha\right|_{U_e},\qquad
 \left.A_{\bar\beta}R\right|_{V_e}
 =\left.RB_\beta\right|_{V_e}
\tag{6.3}
\]
hold on every edge of that word.
\end{enumerate}
If $U_e=V_e=\RR^r$, (6.3) upgrades to full-matrix identities.  On a proper
span it does not.  Equality of scalar total degrees, or comparison of a
different same numerical seed that is not fixed by $\Rstate$, is insufficient.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For necessity, restrict the first phase equality to all integer seeds in
the cell.  Their real span is $U_e$, so linearity forces the first identity
in (6.3).  The second phase equality applied to $A_\alpha u$ spans $V_e$
and forces the second identity.  For sufficiency, apply the first
restriction to each edge seed and use the reflected carry and target
certificates to identify the next typed cell.  Apply the second restriction
there.  Induction over the finite edge-indexed word gives equality at every
phase.  The full-span statement is ordinary equality of linear maps; no
such upgrade is possible from a proper span.
\end{proof}

The proposition is deliberately edgewise.  It does not assert a global
support reflection, a global reversor, a conjugacy, or an equality of maps
outside the certified cells.  The missing-reflection example in Section 8
shows why the reflected certificate is part of the hypothesis.

\section{Local lower-ideal lemma and exact fixture}

We first formulate the one-step margin statement without pretending that a
cone is a pair cell.  Fix one strict typed pair core and choose a nonempty
compact set
\[
 \widehat\Sigma_e^+\subset
 \Ccell\cap\{\|(u,w)\|_1=1\}
\]
on which every coordinate of $u,w,A_\alpha u-w$ is at least
$\varepsilon>0$, and every selected score, carry, target, and reflected
pair gap is at least $\eta>0$.  Define the four normalized projections
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 X^{V,+}&=\pi_u(\Ccell),&
 X^{W,+}&=\{A_\alpha u:(u,w)\in\Ccell\},\\
 X^{W,-}&=\{Ru:(u,w)\in\Ccell\},&
 X^{V,-}&=\{RA_\alpha u:(u,w)\in\Ccell\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Let $\widehat X^{S,\pm}$ denote their closures after $\ell^1$ normalization
and the coordinate lower bound $\varepsilon$.  For each retained row
$\ell$ and lower/new row $\rho$ in the associated section assume
\[
 \min_{x\in\widehat X^{S,\pm}}
 (\ell-\rho)\cdot x\geq\Delta_{S,\pm}>0.
\tag{7.1}
\]
For an unnormalized $x=s\widehat x$, homogeneity makes the gap at least
$s\Delta_{S,\pm}$.  Typed pair margins supply the analogous inequalities
involving $w$ and $Rw$.

\begin{lemma}[One-edge four-projection margin lemma]
\label{lem:margin}
Under the preceding compactness and margin assumptions, the selected rows
$(\alpha,\beta,R\alpha,R\beta)$ remain selected, with positive carries, for
exactly one forward step and exactly one reflected inverse step.  The
conclusion is a leading-form statement on the one typed pair core.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each selected-minus-new row is positive on the normalized closure by (7.1).
Homogeneity transfers positivity to every scale in the core.  The source
and target pair margins keep the new vector in the certified inequalities
for the next phase, while the reflected pair margins do the same after the
state swap.  The survival argument of Section 4 then turns these strict
inequalities into leading-form selection for one step in each direction.
No assertion is made about a second edge, because no target-core inclusion
was assumed.  In particular, a forward margin does not imply the reflected
margin or any all-iterate perturbation stability.
\end{proof}

\section{Boundaries, limitations, reproducibility, and conclusion}

The hypotheses can fail in several independent ways.  If an exponent has a
zero or unit coordinate, the coefficient in (4.3) can vanish or the fresh
all-ones carry can lose positivity.  If a strict selector becomes a tie, the
face certificate does not choose a vertex.  If $A_\alpha u\leq w$ or
$\delta(u)\leq0$, an old block can remain visible and (5.4) is not an actual
degree law.  Empty integer cells and missing reflected target certificates
are stopping boundaries, not cases where an equality is silently extended.

A cancellation fixture makes the role of the class concrete.  Take
\[
 V=(q_1+q_2+q_3)^3,\qquad W=(p_1-p_2)^3.
\tag{8.1}
\]
The support contains zero and unit coordinates, and the leading V-gradient
components are equal.  At the W half-step of the first iterate, the leading
$p_1-p_2$ terms cancel, so a degree predicted by an unqualified fresh-block
rule is lost.  This example lies outside the headline class and is not a
claim about a second iterate.

The reflection hypothesis has its own failure.  If $R\gamma$ is absent from
the appropriate reflected support or action certificate, the identities
(6.2) cannot be invoked.  On an open integer seed cell this produces an
$n=1$ phase-vector mismatch.  Likewise, a proper observable span supports
only the restricted equalities (6.3), not a full matrix assertion.  Scalar
total-degree equality and a comparison of unrelated same numerical seeds
cannot repair either defect.

For clarity, the article makes no claim of arbitrary-support or
zero-coordinate extension, an unconditional selector algorithm, a global
map-level reversor, global conjugacy, classification, entropy, higher
dynamical degrees, a minimal scalar recurrence, a Perron algebraic degree,
same-seed reciprocity away from an $\Rstate$-fixed pair, multi-edge or
all-iterate perturbation stability, or priority.  The selector bound applies
only to infinite certified strict branches, and the lower-ideal lemma stops
after one step.

Reproducibility here means literal symbolic derivations and the exact
arithmetic in (7.2)--(7.5).  No code, data, numerical scan, computer
algebra certificate, fitted parameter, or hardware experiment is needed or
used.  Every displayed inequality is a finite certificate that can be
checked from the printed support vectors.  A routine appendix, if supplied,
may repeat arithmetic already explained but may not introduce a new
hypothesis or computational claim.

The central message is therefore modest but exact.  Positive collected
supports plus strict certificates make a high-dimensional selector process
translate along one diagonal ray.  The finite wall count controls its
transient selector word, the stationary phase has an affine recurrence, and
reversal is an observable-span statement rather than an automatic global
symmetry.  The local margin lemma and the fixtures show both how the
mechanism can be used and where it stops.  Extending it to larger support
actions or finite-fan compactifications would require new certificates, not
a change of notation.
