# Initial proposal: positive Newton translation and phase reciprocity

## Starting idea

Study the composition of a position shear generated by \(V(q)\) and a
momentum shear generated by \(W(p)\), using Newton-support exponents as
weighted-degree selectors.  The initial intuition was that a selected
exponent \(\alpha\) acts by
\(A_\alpha=\mathbf1\alpha^{\mathsf T}-I\), a selected \(\beta\) acts by
\(B_\beta=\mathbf1\beta^{\mathsf T}-I\), and a product of such matrices might
produce a finite selector automaton with a reciprocal inverse word.

The candidate target was a proof-first theory article with one exact
three-dimensional fixture, not a numerical study.  A bounded literature
screen was planned to distinguish this mechanism from monomial maps, planar
reversors, toric degree growth, and prior Hamiltonian-shear results.

## Initial hypotheses

The working class was quickly restricted to a characteristic-zero coefficient
field, finite nonempty collected supports, positive integer weighted seeds,
and strict exposed selectors.  The final positive-support choice
\(E_V,E_W\subset\mathbb Z_{\ge2}^{\,r}\), \(r\ge3\), was adopted because it
makes every derivative row active and gives a coefficient-uniform grouped
Hessian witness.  Degree vectors are explicitly real and are never confused
with coefficient-field elements.

The intended claims were:

* exact forward and inverse phase matrices and carry inequalities;
* an all-ones translation after a V/W pair;
* a finite selector-change bound and an affine stationary tail;
* a reflected inverse comparison on spans generated by integer seeds; and
* a perturbation statement for lower support terms.

## Risks identified before refinement

1. A normal fan is not automatically a target-invariant automaton.  Every
   edge therefore needs a typed source cell, a target inclusion, and a
   reflected certificate.
2. Face ties can create leading-form cancellation.  A grouped determinant and
   Jacobian argument is required; “generic coefficients” is not enough.
3. A single seed cannot justify a full matrix identity.  Observable spans must
   be declared and witnessed.
4. Inverse phase order is W then V, with subtraction signs.  Comparing only
   total degrees can hide an order error.
5. An early pair-wall count was too conservative and a proposed multi-edge
   perturbation claim was too broad.

## Planned falsification

The \(r=3\) support fixture was chosen asymmetrically: two reflected active
pairs plus an unpaired \(\gamma\).  It should realize a transient C1→C2→C2
path while showing that the active component is not a global map reversor.
The outside-class pair
\(V=(q_1+q_2+q_3)^3,\ W=(p_1-p_2)^3\) was retained as a sharp cancellation
boundary.  Failure of any determinant, carry, target, or reflection identity
would terminate the proposal rather than be patched by a numerical example.

## Refinement decisions recorded for the next stage

The candidate was refined to:

* the controlling global wall bound \(d_V+d_W-2\), replacing older
  conservative expressions;
* a global seed origin \(t_0=0\) for the \((\lambda,\mu)\) tail;
* reflected reciprocity only on declared real \(U_e,V_e\) spans, with literal
  face identities only at full rank; and
* an E0051 lower-ideal lemma on one strict pair core and one step only.

No multi-edge, target-core, C1→C2 perturbation, all-iterate, entropy,
minimal-recurrence, Perron, or map-level reversor statement is carried into
the final proposal.
