\title{Period-Three Trace Residues and a Minimal Separator\\
on an Exceptional Quartic H\'enon Fiber}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Let
\(
f_{m,a}(x,y)=(y+(x^m-a)^2,x)
\)
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.  We study trace
moments on its nonreduced formal periodic schemes, rather than replacing
those schemes by their reduced supports.  The period-one multiplication
spectrum is \(0^{\times 2m}\), the formal exact-period-two spectrum is
\(2^{\times((2m)^2-2m)}\), and normalized conjugacy is governed by the
coordinate \(a^{2m-1}\).  For the period-three cyclic complete intersection,
we prove the two-term identity
\[
S_m(a,\eps)=C_m\eps^{3m}+D_m a^{2m-1}\eps^{2m},
\]
with \(C_m=0\) for odd \(m\), and derive a finite nested-binomial certificate
for \(D_m\) from a terminating normal-form recurrence and an exact Laurent
fiber sum.  In degree four, every normalized monic-centered map with formal
fixed-point trace multiset \(0^4\) belongs to the complete fiber
\(p(x)=(x^2-L)^2\).  On this fiber the pointwise formal exact-period-three
second trace moment is
\[
-1296000-1572864L^3,
\]
the exact-period-three zero-cycle has length \(60\), and the cyclewise moment
is \(-432000-524288L^3\).  Since \(L^3\) is precisely the normalized
conjugacy coordinate while periods one and two are constant, period three is
the minimal separator on this fiber.  Universal nonvanishing of \(D_m\)
remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Finite collections of periodic multipliers can determine a polynomial
automorphism only after one understands their exceptional fibers.  In the
normalized Jacobian-minus-one H\'enon category, Cantat and Dujardin identified
the quartic family
\[
(x,y)\longmapsto \bigl(y+(x^2-\lambda^2)^2,x\bigr)
\]
whose period-one and period-two trace data are constant
\citep{CantatDujardin2026}.  Thus neither the family nor its
low-period blindness is new.  The concrete question left by this obstruction
is whether a later period supplies an effective coordinate on the whole
exceptional fiber.

This note answers that question at period three for the complete normalized
quartic fiber whose formal fixed-point trace multiset is \(0^4\).  Writing
\(L=\lambda^2\), we show that the pointwise formal exact-period-three second
trace moment equals
\[
-384(3375+4096L^3).
\]
The coefficient of \(L^3\) is nonzero, and \(L^3\) is exactly the residual
normalized conjugacy coordinate.  Since the two lower formal trace multisets
are constant, this gives a sharp minimal separator on that fiber.  It does
not give a global period cutoff for all quartic H\'enon maps.

The quartic identity is the endpoint of a uniform algebraic mechanism.  For
every \(m\ge2\), three cyclic orbit equations form a monic complete
intersection of rank \((2m)^3\).  Their Jacobian determinant is also the
trace of the third derivative iterate.  Classical quotient-trace and global
residue theory then converts the desired trace moment into a top normal-form
coefficient \citep{CattaniDickensteinSturmfels1996}.  Weighted
homogeneity and a local degeneration leave only two parameter monomials.  The
remaining slope is evaluated by an explicit recurrence-to-Laurent-to-binomial
argument.  The conclusion is a formula for \(D_m\), not a proof that
\(D_m\ne0\) in every degree.

Our contributions are therefore the following.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em,label=(\roman*)]
  \item We retain the nonreduced formal periodic algebras and compute the
  period-one and formal exact-period-two trace spectra, as well as the
  normalized quotient coordinate \(a^{2m-1}\).
  \item We prove the uniform period-three law
  \(S_m=C_m\eps^{3m}+D_ma^{2m-1}\eps^{2m}\), the odd-\(m\) identity
  \(C_m=0\), and a transparent finite certificate for \(D_m\).
  \item We classify the complete normalized quartic fiber with formal
  fixed-point trace multiset \(0^4\), compute its period-three affine moment
  by two source-level derivations, and prove its scoped minimality after a
  separate local-multiplicity argument.
\end{enumerate}

The proof has two distinctions that cannot be suppressed.  First, a trace
element can be nonzero and nilpotent on a nonreduced scheme even when its
formal multiplication spectrum contains only zero.  Second, vanishing of the
fixed \emph{moment} does not determine the fixed scheme's local
\emph{multiplicity}.  The former is handled in the finite quotient algebras;
the latter is proved by formal transverse elimination before any
exact-period length is subtracted.

\section{Prior work and claim boundary}
\label{sec:prior}

\paragraph{Normal forms and multiplier rigidity.}
Friedland--Milnor theory supplies the structural and conjugacy framework for
plane polynomial automorphisms and generalized H\'enon normal forms
\citep{FriedlandMilnor1989}.  Cantat--Dujardin place this
framework in a modern multiplier-rigidity setting and provide the direct
quartic period-one/two obstruction used here
\citep{CantatDujardin2026}.  Our equivalence
\(f_{m,a}\sim f_{m,b}\) if and only if
\(a^{2m-1}=b^{2m-1}\) is a proved specialization within the normalized
one-factor category, not a new general normal-form theorem.

\paragraph{Residues and exact H\'enon identities.}
Multidimensional residues, quotient-algebra traces, deformations to initial
forms, and coefficient extraction are established tools
\citep{CattaniDickensteinSturmfels1996}.  Exact periodic-orbit sum
rules for H\'enon dynamics also predate this work
\citep{CvitanovicHansenRolfVattay1998}.  Those contour and
Fredholm-type sums use different weights from the raw finite-algebra trace
power considered below.  The present contribution is the family-specific
complete intersection, the two-term reduction, its coefficient certificate,
and the quartic separator; it is not the residue formalism itself.

\paragraph{Low periods and adjacent multiplier invariants.}
Low-period equations and stability for generalized H\'enon maps have been
studied explicitly, for example in the cubic area-preserving setting
\citep{DullinMeiss2000}.  Small-cycle multiplier maps in
one-dimensional polynomial moduli provide a close comparison but do not
cover Jacobian-minus-one plane automorphisms
\citep{Huguin2024}.  Formal dynatomic cycles and
higher-dimensional projective multiplier invariants supply adjacent language
and background \citep{Hutz2010,Hutz2020}.
Index-derived fixed-point relations in projective and polynomial-automorphism
settings are further methodological neighbors
\citep{GuillotRamirez2019,Ueda2004}.  None of these
sources is used as a direct proof of the identities below.

\paragraph{Bounded novelty statement.}
The literature search underlying this note located no prior explicit formula
for the period-three moment, the all-\(m\) coefficient certificate, or the
scoped quartic minimal-separator theorem.  This is a bounded absence
statement, not a historical-priority proof.  The claim-safe delta is limited
to the source-proved period-three results.  In particular, we do not claim
discovery of the exceptional quartic family, novelty of formal cycles or
global residues, or a first exact H\'enon period-three calculation of any
kind.

\section{Formal periodic algebras and main results}
\label{sec:setup}

Let \(\kfield\) be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.  For
an integer \(m\ge2\) and \(a\in\kfield\), set
\begin{equation}
\pmam(x)=(x^m-a)^2,
\qquad
\qmam(x)=\pmam'(x)=2m x^{m-1}(x^m-a),
\label{eq:family-polynomials}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\fmam(x,y)=\bigl(y+\pmam(x),x\bigr).
\label{eq:family-map}
\end{equation}
The Jacobian determinant of \(\fmam\) is \(-1\).

\begin{definition}[Formal multiset]
Let \(B\) be a finite-dimensional \(\kfield\)-algebra and \(h\in B\).  The
formal multiset of values of \(h\) is the multiset of roots of
\(\det(TI-M_h)\), counted with algebra dimension.  Here \(M_h\) is
multiplication by \(h\).  This convention retains nilpotent scheme length
while attaching to each support point the residue-field value of \(h\).
\end{definition}

For period two, ``formal exact period two'' means the formal period-two
spectral zero-cycle minus the embedded formal fixed spectral zero-cycle.  For
prime period three, we use the analogous subtraction, but we will prove
separately that the local fixed length inside \(\Fix(f^3)\) is the expected
one.

Write
\begin{equation}
\vnu=2m-1,
\qquad
\xtop=x_0^\vnu x_1^\vnu x_2^\vnu.
\label{eq:nu-top}
\end{equation}
All cyclic indices below lie in \(\ZZ/3\ZZ\).

\begin{theorem}[Uniform formal period-three law]
\label{thm:uniform}
For the family \eqref{eq:family-map}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item the formal period-one trace multiset is \(0^{\times2m}\), and the
  formal exact-period-two trace multiset is
  \(2^{\times((2m)^2-2m)}\);
  \item within normalized monic-centered H\'enon moduli,
  \[
  f_{m,a}\sim f_{m,b}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  a^{2m-1}=b^{2m-1};
  \]
  \item the formal period-three \(m\)-th trace moment satisfies
  \begin{equation}
  S_m(a,\eps)=C_m\eps^{3m}+D_m a^{2m-1}\eps^{2m}
  \label{eq:two-term-main}
  \end{equation}
  for integers \(C_m,D_m\), and \(C_m=0\) whenever \(m\) is odd;
  \item defining
  \begin{align}
  H(r,k)
  &=\sum_{\substack{u+v=k\\2u\le r,\;2v\le r}}
    \binom{k}{u}\binom{r}{2u}\binom{r}{2v},
  \label{eq:H-definition}\\
  A_{m,r}
  &=\sum_{k=\lceil m/2\rceil}^{\min(m-1,r)}
    (-1)^{r+k}\binom{m-1}{2(m-k)-1}H(r,k),
  \label{eq:A-definition}
  \end{align}
  with an empty sum equal to zero, the slope has the exact certificate
  \begin{equation}
  D_m=3\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
  \binom{m+1}{j}(2m)^{3m+3-2j}A_{m,m-j}.
  \label{eq:D-certificate-main}
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
No assertion that \(D_m\ne0\) for all \(m\) is included.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Complete quartic fiber and minimal separator]
\label{thm:quartic}
Let \(p\) be any monic-centered quartic, and set
\(f_p(x,y)=(y+p(x),x)\).  Its formal fixed-point trace multiset is \(0^4\)
if and only if
\[
p(x)=(x^2-L)^2
\]
for a unique \(L\in\kfield\).  On this complete normalized fiber,
\begin{equation}
S_2^{(3)}(L)=-1296000-1572864L^3
=-384(3375+4096L^3).
\label{eq:quartic-moment-main}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\[
f_{2,L}\sim f_{2,M}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
L^3=M^3.
\]
The period-one and formal exact-period-two trace multisets are constant on
the fiber, whereas \eqref{eq:quartic-moment-main} is affine with nonzero
slope in \(L^3\).  Hence period three is the minimal separating period on
this fiber.  The formal exact-period-three zero-cycle has pointwise length
\(60\), and its cyclewise second trace moment is
\[
-432000-524288L^3.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{warning}
The minimality conclusion in \cref{thm:quartic} concerns only the complete
normalized quartic fiber with formal fixed-point trace multiset \(0^4\).  It
is neither a theorem for all quartic H\'enon maps nor a global equality for a
degree-four period cutoff.
\end{warning}

\section{Low periods and normalized conjugacy}
\label{sec:low-periods}

Write \(p=\pmam\) and \(q=\qmam\).  A fixed point satisfies \(x=y\) and
\(p(x)=0\), so its coordinate algebra is
\begin{equation}
B_1=\kfield[x]/(p)=\kfield[x]/((x^m-a)^2),
\qquad \dim_{\kfield}B_1=2m.
\label{eq:fixed-algebra}
\end{equation}
Within \(B_1\),
\[
q=2m x^{m-1}(x^m-a),
\qquad q^2=0.
\]
Since
\[
D\fmam(x,y)=
\begin{pmatrix}q(x)&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
the trace element on the fixed algebra is \(q\).  Multiplication by \(q\)
is nilpotent, hence its characteristic polynomial is \(T^{2m}\).  This gives
the formal multiset \(0^{\times2m}\).  The statement is spectral: when
\(a\ne0\), the class of \(q\) is generally not zero in the nonreduced
algebra.

A direct iteration gives
\[
f_{m,a}^2(x,y)=\bigl(x+p(y+p(x)),y+p(x)\bigr).
\]
The fixed equations for \(f^2\) reduce to \(p(x)=p(y)=0\), so
\begin{equation}
B_2=\kfield[x,y]/(p(x),p(y)),
\qquad \dim_{\kfield}B_2=(2m)^2.
\label{eq:period-two-algebra}
\end{equation}
The derivative trace is
\[
\Tr(Df_{m,a}^2)=2+q(x)q(y).
\]
Because \((q(x)q(y))^2=0\), its sole formal eigenvalue is \(2\), with
multiplicity \((2m)^2\).  On the embedded fixed algebra the same trace is
\(2+q^2=2\).  Subtracting the length-\(2m\) fixed spectral cycle yields
\(2^{\times((2m)^2-2m)}\).  Nothing in this argument reduces either
scheme, so it remains valid at \(a=0\).

We next identify the normalized moduli coordinate.  For
\(h_c(x,y)=(cx,cy)\),
\begin{align}
h_c^{-1}f_{m,a}h_c(x,y)
&=\left(y+c^{-1}(c^m x^m-a)^2,x\right)\notag\\
&=\left(y+c^{2m-1}(x^m-ac^{-m})^2,x\right).
\label{eq:diagonal-conjugacy}
\end{align}
The image stays monic exactly when \(c^{2m-1}=1\), and then the new
parameter is \(b=ac^{-m}\).  Thus conjugacy implies
\(b^{2m-1}=a^{2m-1}\).

Conversely, if \(a^{2m-1}=b^{2m-1}\) and \(a\ne0\), then
\(\zeta=b/a\) is a \((2m-1)\)-st root of unity.  Since
\(\gcd(m,2m-1)=1\), exponentiation by \(-m\) is an automorphism of this
root group; choose \(c\) with \(c^{2m-1}=1\) and
\(c^{-m}=\zeta\).  Equation \eqref{eq:diagonal-conjugacy} gives the desired
conjugacy.  The case \(a=0\) forces \(b=0\).

For necessity within the normalized category, normal-form uniqueness reduces
a polynomial conjugacy to a simultaneous affine change
\((x,y)\mapsto(cx+d,cy+d)\).  The coefficient of \(x^{2m-1}\) in the
transformed degree-\(2m\) polynomial is \(2md/c\).  Centering and
characteristic zero force \(d=0\), so the diagonal calculation exhausts the
normalized conjugacies.

\section{The cyclic complete intersection and trace residue}
\label{sec:cyclic}

Represent a length-three orbit by states \((x_i,x_{i-1})\).  Introduce the
deformation
\begin{equation}
F_i=(x_i^m-a)^2+\eps(x_{i-1}-x_{i+1}),
\qquad
q_i=2m x_i^{m-1}(x_i^m-a).
\label{eq:cyclic-system}
\end{equation}
At \(\eps=1\), the equations \(F_i=0\) are exactly the cyclic equations for
\(\Fix(f_{m,a}^3)\).  Their Jacobian matrix is
\[
\left(\frac{\partial F_i}{\partial x_j}\right)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
q_0&-\eps&\eps\\
\eps&q_1&-\eps\\
-\eps&\eps&q_2
\end{pmatrix},
\]
and its determinant is
\begin{equation}
t_\eps=q_0q_1q_2+\eps^2(q_0+q_1+q_2).
\label{eq:t-epsilon}
\end{equation}
If \(M(q)=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}q&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\), then
\(Df^3=M(q_2)M(q_1)M(q_0)\).  Multiplication of these three matrices shows
that \(\Tr(Df^3)=q_0q_1q_2+q_0+q_1+q_2=t_1\).

Let
\begin{equation}
\cA_m=
\kfield[a,\eps,x_0,x_1,x_2]/(F_0,F_1,F_2).
\label{eq:cyclic-algebra}
\end{equation}
For any degree-compatible monomial order, the leading monomials are
\(x_0^{2m},x_1^{2m},x_2^{2m}\).  They are pairwise coprime, so the three
relations form a Gr\"obner basis over \(\kfield[a,\eps]\).  Consequently
\[
x_0^{e_0}x_1^{e_1}x_2^{e_2},
\qquad 0\le e_i<2m,
\]
form a free basis of \(\cA_m\), of rank \((2m)^3\).  The leading homogeneous
forms have no common point in \(\PP^2\), hence no intersection length is lost
at infinity.

Define
\begin{equation}
S_m(a,\eps)=
\Tr_{\cA_m/\kfield[a,\eps]}\bigl(M_{t_\eps^m}\bigr).
\label{eq:S-definition}
\end{equation}
The Jacobian of the complete intersection is \(t_\eps\).  The standard
trace--residue identity, valid also for nonreduced complete intersections,
gives
\begin{equation}
S_m(a,\eps)=\Res_{F_0,F_1,F_2}(t_\eps^{m+1}).
\label{eq:trace-residue}
\end{equation}
For these monic leading forms, the residue is the top normal-form coefficient:
\begin{equation}
\Res(G)=\coeff{x_0^{2m-1}x_1^{2m-1}x_2^{2m-1}}{\NF(G)}.
\label{eq:top-residue}
\end{equation}
In particular, \(S_m\in\ZZ[a,\eps]\).

\section{The two-term law}
\label{sec:two-term}

Assign weights
\[
\wt(x_i)=1,
\qquad
\wt(a)=m,
\qquad
\wt(\eps)=2m-1=\vnu.
\]
Each \(F_i\) has weight \(2m\), every \(q_i\) has weight \(\vnu\), and
\(t_\eps\) has weight \(3\vnu\).  Therefore each monomial
\(a^r\eps^s\) in \(S_m\) satisfies
\[
mr+\vnu s=3m\vnu.
\]
Since \(\gcd(m,\vnu)=1\), the only nonnegative solutions are
\[
(r,s)=(0,3m),(\vnu,2m),(2\vnu,m),(3\vnu,0).
\]
Thus
\begin{equation}
S_m=c_{0,m}\eps^{3m}+c_{1,m}a^\vnu\eps^{2m}
+c_{2,m}a^{2\vnu}\eps^m+c_{3,m}a^{3\vnu}.
\label{eq:four-term}
\end{equation}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig2_weighted_two_term_law.pdf}
  \caption{Weighted support and the two-term law. Weighted homogeneity
  permits exactly four parameter monomials in $S_m(a,\varepsilon)$. The
  separated algebra at $\varepsilon=0$ removes $a^{3(2m-1)}$, and the three
  nonfixed degeneration patterns together with the exact diagonal branch
  remove $a^{2(2m-1)}\varepsilon^m$. The surviving terms are
  $C_m\varepsilon^{3m}$ and $D_ma^{2m-1}\varepsilon^{2m}$; cyclic reversal
  additionally gives $C_m=0$ for odd $m$. For $m=2$, source-level
  coefficient derivations give
  $S_2^{(3)}(L)=-1296000-1572864L^3$. The diagram makes no universal
  separation claim: $D_m\neq0$ for every $m\geq2$ remains open.}
  \label{fig:weighted-two-term-law}
\end{figure*}

At \(\eps=0\), the algebra is the tensor product of three copies of
\(\kfield[a,x]/((x^m-a)^2)\).  In each factor \(q_i^2=0\), whereas
\(t_0=q_0q_1q_2\).  Because \(m\ge2\), we have \(t_0^m=0\), and hence
\(S_m(a,0)=0\).  Therefore \(c_{3,m}=0\).

It remains to remove the term of \(\eps\)-order \(m\).  Fix \(a\ne0\) and
work over an algebraic closure of the Puiseux field in \(\eps\), normalized
by \(v(\eps)=1\).  Flatness clusters all \((2m)^3\) solutions at triples
\((\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\) of roots of \(x^m=a\).  Put
\(x_i=\alpha_i+\delta_i\).  Since the roots are nonzero and simple before
squaring,
\[
(x_i^m-a)^2=c_i\delta_i^2+O(\delta_i^3),
\qquad
q_i=d_i\delta_i+O(\delta_i^2),
\]
with \(c_i,d_i\ne0\).  Three root patterns exhaust the possibilities.

\paragraph{All roots distinct.}
Every constant coupling \(\alpha_{i-1}-\alpha_{i+1}\) is nonzero.  Balancing
its valuation \(1\) against \(2v(\delta_i)\) gives
\(v(q_i)=1/2\), hence
\begin{equation}
v(t_\eps)\ge\frac32.
\label{eq:valuation-distinct}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Exactly two roots equal.}
After cyclic relabeling take \(\alpha_0=\alpha_1\ne\alpha_2\).  The first
two equations give \(v(q_0)=v(q_1)=1/2\).  In the third equation the coupling
has valuation at least \(3/2\).  If \(v(\delta_2)<3/4\), its square would be
the unique least-valuation term.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
v(q_2)\ge\frac34,
\qquad
v(t_\eps)\ge\frac74.
\label{eq:valuation-twoequal}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{All roots equal.}
Let \(r_0\) be the minimum valuation of a nonzero \(\delta_i\).  Coupling
terms have valuation at least \(1+r_0\).  If \(r_0<1\), a coordinate with
valuation \(r_0\) would contribute an uncancelled square of valuation
\(2r_0<1+r_0\).  Hence every nontrivial branch has \(r_0\ge1\), and
\begin{equation}
v(t_\eps)\ge3.
\label{eq:valuation-allequal}
\end{equation}
The exact diagonal fixed branch is handled separately in
\cref{lem:fixed-moment}; its moment contribution vanishes identically.

The trace of multiplication by an element of a finite algebra is the sum of
its support values weighted by local length; nilpotent parts have trace zero.
The three lower bounds show that every nonfixed cluster contributes to
\(S_m\) with \(\eps\)-valuation strictly greater than \(m\): the bounds are
\(3m/2\), \(7m/4\), and \(3m\), respectively.  Summation cannot decrease
valuation.  In \eqref{eq:four-term}, the only surviving candidate of exact
order \(m\) is \(c_{2,m}a^{2\vnu}\eps^m\).  Since \(a\ne0\), it follows
that \(c_{2,m}=0\).  Setting \(C_m=c_{0,m}\) and \(D_m=c_{1,m}\) proves
\eqref{eq:two-term-main}.

Finally, the coordinate reversal \(x_i\mapsto x_{-i}\) sends the system with
\(\eps\) to the system with \(-\eps\), while \(t_\eps\) is unchanged.
Therefore \(S_m(a,\eps)=S_m(a,-\eps)\).  The \(D_m\)-term is always even in
\(\eps\); if \(m\) is odd, \(3m\) is odd, forcing \(C_m=0\).

\section{A transparent coefficient certificate for \texorpdfstring{\(D_m\)}{Dm}}
\label{sec:coefficient}

This section gives the full coefficient extraction.  It is an algebraic
proof for symbolic \(m\), not an interpolation from finitely many degrees.

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering

For every integer exponent \(e_i\ge2m\), the relation \(F_i=0\) yields
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
x_i^{e_i}\equiv{}&2a x_i^{e_i-m}-a^2x_i^{e_i-2m}
-\eps x_i^{e_i-2m}x_{i-1}
+\eps x_i^{e_i-2m}x_{i+1}
\pmod{(F_0,F_1,F_2)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{R}\label{eq:recurrence-R}
\end{equation*}

For \(\be=(e_0,e_1,e_2)\in\ZZ^3\) and
\(\alpha,\beta\in\ZZ\), define
\begin{equation}
\cR_m(\be;\alpha,\beta)=
\coeff{a^\alpha\eps^\beta\xtop}
{\NF(x_0^{e_0}x_1^{e_1}x_2^{e_2})}.
\label{eq:R-state}
\end{equation}
It is zero if some \(e_i<0\), or if \(\alpha<0\) or \(\beta<0\).  On the
standard box its complete base cases are
\begin{equation}
\cR_m(\be;\alpha,\beta)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\be=(\vnu,\vnu,\vnu),\ (\alpha,\beta)=(0,0),\\
0,&0\le e_i<2m\text{ for all }i\text{, otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:R-base}
\end{equation}
If \(e_i\ge2m\), \eqref{eq:recurrence-R} gives the four signed branches
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\cR_m(\be;\alpha,\beta)
={}&2\cR_m(\be-m\bdelta_i;\alpha-1,\beta)
-\cR_m(\be-2m\bdelta_i;\alpha-2,\beta)\\
&-\cR_m(\be-2m\bdelta_i+\bdelta_{i-1};\alpha,\beta-1)
+\cR_m(\be-2m\bdelta_i+\bdelta_{i+1};\alpha,\beta-1).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:R-branches}
\end{equation}
The total exponent decreases by \(m,2m,2m-1,2m-1\), respectively.  Thus
\eqref{eq:R-base}--\eqref{eq:R-branches}, under any fixed reducible-coordinate
choice, form a terminating induction certificate.

The same coefficient has the order-independent Laurent representation
\begin{equation}
\cR_m(\be;\alpha,\beta)=
\coeff{a^\alpha\eps^\beta x_0^{-1}x_1^{-1}x_2^{-1}}
{\frac{x_0^{e_0}x_1^{e_1}x_2^{e_2}}{F_0F_1F_2}},
\label{eq:R-laurent}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\frac1{F_i}=x_i^{-2m}\sum_{h\ge0}
\left(
\frac{2a x_i^m-a^2-\eps x_{i-1}+\eps x_{i+1}}{x_i^{2m}}
\right)^h.
\label{eq:reciprocal-expansion}
\end{equation}
Unique normal form proves reduction-order independence.  In the Laurent
picture, interleavings of reductions at distinct coordinates are merely
different orders for the same monomial; branch-count tuples, rather than
interleavings, index the summands.

Expand
\begin{equation}
t_\eps^{m+1}
=\sum_{j=0}^{m+1}\binom{m+1}{j}\eps^{2j}
(q_0q_1q_2)^{m+1-j}(q_0+q_1+q_2)^j.
\label{eq:t-power-expansion}
\end{equation}
Put \(\bar q_i=x_i^{m-1}(x_i^m-a)\).  Extracting every scalar \(2m\),
define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\cC_{m,j}:={}&
\coeff{a^\vnu\eps^{2m-2j}\xtop}
{\NF\!\left(
(\bar q_0\bar q_1\bar q_2)^{m+1-j}
(\bar q_0+\bar q_1+\bar q_2)^j
\right)}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:C-precollapse}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
D_m=\sum_{j=0}^{m+1}\binom{m+1}{j}
(2m)^{3m+3-2j}\cC_{m,j}.
\label{eq:D-precollapse}
\end{equation}

Set \(r=m-j\) for \(0\le j\le m\).  Choose
\(\bs=(s_0,s_1,s_2)\in\NN^3\) with \(|\bs|=j\) and put
\(N_i=r+1+s_i\).  Choosing the low term in exactly \(\ell_i\) of the
\(N_i\) factors of
\(\bar q_i=x_i^{2m-1}-a x_i^{m-1}\) gives
\[
e_i=(2m-1)N_i-m\ell_i,
\qquad 0\le\ell_i\le N_i.
\]
The terminating recurrence therefore gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\cC_{m,j}
={}&\sum_{|\bs|=j}\binom{j}{s_0,s_1,s_2}
\sum_{0\le\ell_i\le N_i}
(-1)^{|\bell|}\prod_{i=0}^2\binom{N_i}{\ell_i}\\
&\hspace{25mm}\cdot
\cR_m\bigl(\be;\vnu-|\bell|,2r\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:C-recurrence-certificate}
\end{equation}
For \(j=m+1\), the requested \(\eps\)-degree is negative, and
\(\cC_{m,m+1}=0\).

We now unfold \eqref{eq:reciprocal-expansion}.  At source coordinate \(i\),
let \(A_i,B_i,U_i,V_i\) count, respectively, the four branch choices
\[
2a x_i^m,\qquad -a^2,\qquad -\eps x_{i-1},\qquad +\eps x_{i+1},
\]
and set
\begin{equation}
h_i=A_i+B_i+U_i+V_i,
\qquad
T_i=U_{i+1}+V_{i-1}.
\label{eq:h-T}
\end{equation}
A tuple
\begin{equation}
(\bs,\bell,\bA,\bB,\bU,\bV)
\label{eq:admissible-tuple}
\end{equation}
is admissible when all entries are nonnegative integers,
\(|\bs|=j\), \(0\le\ell_i\le N_i\), and
\begin{align}
|\bell|+\sum_i(A_i+2B_i)&=\vnu,
\label{eq:admissible-a}\\
\sum_i(U_i+V_i)&=2r,
\label{eq:admissible-epsilon}\\
e_i+mA_i+T_i-2m(h_i+1)&=-1
\qquad(i=0,1,2).
\label{eq:admissible-laurent}
\end{align}
Its signed multiplicity is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
W={}&(-1)^{|\bell|+\sum_i(B_i+U_i)}
2^{\sum_iA_i}\binom{j}{s_0,s_1,s_2}\\
&\cdot\prod_{i=0}^2
\binom{N_i}{\ell_i}
\frac{h_i!}{A_i!B_i!U_i!V_i!}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:admissible-weight}
\end{equation}
Every expanded Laurent choice has exactly one branch-count tuple, and every
admissible tuple has the requested parameter degrees and three Laurent
exponents.  Thus
\begin{equation}
\cC_{m,j}=\sum_{\text{admissible tuples in }\eqref{eq:admissible-tuple}}W.
\label{eq:C-admissible-sum}
\end{equation}
The sum is finite by \eqref{eq:admissible-a}--\eqref{eq:admissible-epsilon},
and the description is exhaustive without double counting.

\subsection{The local signed fiber identity}
\label{subsec:fiber-identity}

We use generalized binomial coefficients
\[
\binom{z}{s}=\frac{z(z-1)\cdots(z-s+1)}{s!}
\quad(s\ge1),
\qquad
\binom{z}{0}=1,
\]
and set \(\binom{z}{s}=0\) for \(s<0\).  For a nonnegative integer upper
argument, the usual out-of-range values are zero.

\begin{lemma}[Local fiber identity]
\label{lem:local-fiber}
For \(N,n,\alpha\in\NN\),
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\substack{\ell,A,B\ge0\\\ell+A+2B=\alpha}}
(-1)^{\ell+B}2^A\binom{N}{\ell}
\frac{(n+A+B)!}{n!A!B!}
=(-1)^\alpha\binom{N-2n-2}{\alpha}.
\label{eq:local-fiber}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Take the coefficient of \(z^\alpha\) in
\begin{align*}
(1-z)^N
\sum_{A,B\ge0}
\frac{(n+A+B)!}{n!A!B!}(2z)^A(-z^2)^B
&=(1-z)^N(1-2z+z^2)^{-n-1}\\
&=(1-z)^{N-2n-2}.
\end{align*}
The left coefficient is the sum in \eqref{eq:local-fiber}, and the right
coefficient is \((-1)^\alpha\binom{N-2n-2}{\alpha}\).
\end{proof}

Fix \((\bs,\bU,\bV)\) and put
\begin{equation}
n_i=U_i+V_i,
\qquad
\lambda_i=N_i-2n_i-2,
\qquad
\alpha_i=\frac{(2m-1)(N_i-1)+T_i}{m}-2n_i.
\label{eq:local-exponents}
\end{equation}
Because \(\sum_i(N_i-1)=m+2r\) and
\(\sum_iT_i=\sum_i n_i=2r\),
\begin{equation}
\sum_i\alpha_i
=\frac{(2m-1)(m+2r)+2r}{m}-4r
=2m-1=\vnu.
\label{eq:alpha-sum}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:admissible-laurent} is precisely
\(\ell_i+A_i+2B_i=\alpha_i\).  Multiplying
\cref{lem:local-fiber} by the orientation factor
\((-1)^{U_i}\binom{n_i}{U_i}\) turns its factorial into
\[
\binom{n_i}{U_i}
\frac{(n_i+A_i+B_i)!}{n_i!A_i!B_i!}
=\frac{h_i!}{A_i!B_i!U_i!V_i!}.
\]
Thus the local identity sums exactly over
\((\ell_i,A_i,B_i)\) at fixed coupling orientations, including all signs and
powers of two.  Define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{B}(\lambda,\alpha)=
\begin{cases}
(-1)^\alpha\binom{\lambda}{\alpha},&\alpha\in\NN,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:B-local}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Distinguished coordinate and transfer flow}
\label{subsec:transfer}

The integrality condition in \eqref{eq:local-exponents} is
\begin{equation}
T_i\equiv N_i-1=r+s_i\pmod m.
\label{eq:transfer-congruence}
\end{equation}
Write \(T_i=r+s_i+mz_i\).  Since
\(\sum_iT_i=2r\) and \(\sum_i(r+s_i)=m+2r\),
\begin{equation}
z_0+z_1+z_2=-1.
\label{eq:z-sum}
\end{equation}
Also \(0\le r+s_i\le m\).  Nonnegativity of \(T_i\) forces
\(z_i\ge-1\), and \(z_i=-1\) is possible only if \(s_i=j\) and
\(T_i=0\).

Suppose first that \(j\ge1\).  Two coordinates cannot both have \(s_i=j\)
because \(\sum_i s_i=j\).  Equation \eqref{eq:z-sum} therefore forces a
unique distinguished coordinate \(d\) with
\begin{equation}
s_d=j,\quad T_d=0,
\qquad
s_i=0,\quad T_i=r\quad(i\ne d).
\label{eq:distinguished-coordinate}
\end{equation}
By cyclic symmetry take \(d=0\), and write
\[
u=U_0,
\qquad
v=V_0,
\qquad
k=n_0=u+v.
\]
The incoming-transfer equations give uniquely
\begin{equation}
U_1=0,\quad V_2=0,\quad
V_1=r-u,\quad U_2=r-v,\quad
n_1=r-u,\quad n_2=r-v.
\label{eq:incoming-flow}
\end{equation}
Conversely, every nonnegative \((k,u,v)\) satisfying the ranges below gives
one transfer configuration.

At the distinguished coordinate,
\[
(\lambda_0,\alpha_0)=(m-1-2k,2m-1-2k),
\]
whereas
\[
(\lambda_1,\alpha_1)=(2u-r-1,2u),
\qquad
(\lambda_2,\alpha_2)=(2v-r-1,2v).
\]
Using
\[
(-1)^\alpha\binom{\alpha-M}{\alpha}=\binom{M-1}{\alpha}
\qquad(M\ge1,\ \alpha\ge0),
\]
the local factors become
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{B}(\lambda_0,\alpha_0)
=\binom{m-1}{2(m-k)-1},
\quad
\mathcal{B}(\lambda_1,\alpha_1)=\binom{r}{2u},
\quad
\mathcal{B}(\lambda_2,\alpha_2)=\binom{r}{2v}.
\label{eq:three-local-factors}
\end{equation}
They are nonzero exactly when
\begin{equation}
\left\lceil\frac m2\right\rceil\le k\le m-1,
\qquad
2u\le r,
\qquad
2v\le r.
\label{eq:guarded-ranges}
\end{equation}
The orientation multiplicity is \(\binom{k}{u}\), and its sign is
\[
(-1)^{U_0+U_1+U_2}=(-1)^{u+r-v}=(-1)^{r+k}.
\]
Hence the exact signed fiber over \((d,k,u,v)\) is
\begin{equation}
(-1)^{r+k}
\binom{m-1}{2(m-k)-1}
\binom{k}{u}\binom{r}{2u}\binom{r}{2v}.
\label{eq:signed-fiber}
\end{equation}
The inequalities \(2u,2v\le r\) and \(u+v=k\) imply \(k\le r\).
Summing \eqref{eq:signed-fiber} and then the three choices of \(d\) yields
\begin{equation}
\cC_{m,j}=3A_{m,r}
\qquad(1\le j\le m).
\label{eq:C-collapse-positive-j}
\end{equation}
When \(j>\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), one has
\(r=m-j<\lceil m/2\rceil\), so the range is empty and
\begin{equation}
\cC_{m,j}=0.
\label{eq:C-empty-range}
\end{equation}

The case \(j=0\) is not obtained by silently reusing the preceding
classification.  Here \(r=m\), \(s_i=0\), and
\(T_i=m(1+z_i)\).  The nonnegative incoming-transfer patterns are exactly
the cyclic permutations of
\begin{equation}
(0,m,m)
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(0,0,2m).
\label{eq:jzero-patterns}
\end{equation}
For the first type, the unique zero coordinate plays the role of \(d\),
giving \(3A_{m,m}\).  For the second type, take
\(T_0=2m\), \(T_1=T_2=0\).  The flow equations force \(n_0=0\), and at
coordinate zero
\[
N_0=m+1,
\qquad
\lambda_0=m-1,
\qquad
\alpha_0=2m+1.
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{B}(\lambda_0,\alpha_0)
=-\binom{m-1}{2m+1}=0.
\label{eq:jzero-exceptional-vanishing}
\end{equation}
All three rotations of this exceptional type vanish, and hence
\(\cC_{m,0}=3A_{m,m}\).

Combining \eqref{eq:D-precollapse},
\eqref{eq:C-collapse-positive-j}--\eqref{eq:jzero-exceptional-vanishing},
and \(\cC_{m,m+1}=0\) proves
\begin{equation}
D_m=3\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
\binom{m+1}{j}(2m)^{3m+3-2j}A_{m,m-j},
\label{eq:D-certificate-proved}
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:D-certificate-main}.  If
\(q=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), define
\begin{equation}
E_m=\sum_{j=0}^{q}\binom{m+1}{j}
(2m)^{2(q-j)}A_{m,m-j}.
\label{eq:E-definition}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
D_m=3(2m)^{3m+3-2q}E_m.
\label{eq:D-factorization}
\end{equation}
The derivation proves the certificate for every symbolic \(m\ge2\).  It
does not determine whether \(E_m\), and therefore \(D_m\), is nonzero in
every degree.

\section{The complete quartic fiber and its exact moment}
\label{sec:quartic}

Let \(p\) be a monic-centered quartic and
\(B_p=\kfield[x]/(p)\).  At each distinct root \(\alpha\) of \(p\), the
formal fixed trace eigenvalue is \(p'(\alpha)\), repeated with local algebra
length.  Thus the formal fixed-point trace multiset is \(0^4\) exactly when
every root of \(p\) is multiple.  The only multiplicity partitions are
\(4\) and \(2+2\).

If \(p=(x-\alpha)^4\), centering forces \(-4\alpha=0\), so
\(p=x^4\).  If
\(p=(x-\alpha)^2(x-\beta)^2\), centering gives
\(2\alpha+2\beta=0\), hence \(\beta=-\alpha\) and
\[
p=(x^2-\alpha^2)^2.
\]
Writing \(L=\alpha^2\) includes the first case as \(L=0\).  The coefficient
of \(x^2\) is \(-2L\), so \(L\) is unique.  This proves the full-fiber
classification in \cref{thm:quartic}.

\subsection{Two source-level derivations of the quartic slope}
\label{subsec:quartic-slope}

As an internal source-level cross-check, specialize the all-\(m\) certificate:
\[
H(2,1)=2,
\qquad
A_{2,2}=-2,
\qquad
A_{2,1}=0,
\]
which gives
\begin{equation}
D_2=3\cdot4^9(-2)=-1572864.
\label{eq:D2-crosscheck}
\end{equation}
We now derive the same slope without the all-degree collapse.

Put
\[
P_a(x)=(x^2-a)^2,
\qquad
g_a(x)=x(x^2-a),
\qquad
L_i=x_{i-1}-x_{i+1}.
\]
Then \(F_i=P_a(x_i)+\eps L_i\), \(q_i=4g_a(x_i)\), and
\[
t_\eps=4^3g_0g_1g_2+4\eps^2(g_0+g_1+g_2).
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}
t_\eps^3=\sum_{j=0}^3\binom{3}{j}4^{9-2j}\eps^{2j}G_j,
\qquad
G_j=(g_0g_1g_2)^{3-j}(g_0+g_1+g_2)^j.
\label{eq:quartic-t-cube}
\end{equation}
Define
\[
\cL_j(a,\eps)=
\coeff{x_0^{-1}x_1^{-1}x_2^{-1}}
{\frac{G_j}{F_0F_1F_2}}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\cC_{2,j}=\coeff{a^3\eps^{4-2j}}{\cL_j(a,\eps)},
\qquad
D_2=\sum_{j=0}^3\binom3j4^{9-2j}\cC_{2,j}.
\label{eq:quartic-Cj}
\end{equation}
Weights show that the selected coefficient is a constant multiple of
\(a^3\), so set \(a=1\).  With
\(P(x)=(x^2-1)^2\) and \(g(x)=x(x^2-1)\), expand
\begin{equation}
\frac1{P_i+\eps L_i}
=\sum_{h_i\ge0}(-1)^{h_i}\eps^{h_i}
\frac{L_i^{h_i}}{P_i^{h_i+1}}.
\label{eq:quartic-reciprocal}
\end{equation}
Introduce
\[
\rho_{n,h}(d)=
\coeff{x^{-1}}{\frac{x^d g(x)^n}{P(x)^{h+1}}}
\qquad(d\ge0).
\]
The identities
\[
\frac{g^3}{P}=x^3(x^2-1),
\qquad
\frac{g^3}{P^2}=\frac{x^3}{x^2-1},
\qquad
\frac{g^3}{P^3}=\frac{x^3}{(x^2-1)^3}
\]
give
\begin{align*}
\rho_{3,0}(d)&=0 &&(d\ge0),\\
\rho_{3,1}(0)&=\rho_{3,1}(2)=1,
&\rho_{3,1}(1)&=0,\\
\rho_{3,2}(0)&=\rho_{3,2}(1)=0,
&\rho_{3,2}(2)&=1.
\end{align*}

For \(j=0\), the total internal denominator order is
\(h_0+h_1+h_2=4\).  The first vanishing forces every \(h_i\ge1\), leaving
only the three cyclic arrangements of \((2,1,1)\).  For
\((h_0,h_1,h_2)=(2,1,1)\), the tensor coefficient is
\[
(\rho_{3,2}\otimes\rho_{3,1}\otimes\rho_{3,1})(L_0^2L_1L_2).
\]
Since
\[
L_0=x_2-x_1,
\quad L_1=x_0-x_2,
\quad L_2=x_1-x_0,
\]
we have
\(L_1L_2=-x_0^2+x_0(x_1+x_2)-x_1x_2\).  Extracting the
\(x_0\)-coefficient leaves \(-(x_2-x_1)^2\), and the remaining two
coefficients give \(-2\).  The other cyclic arrangements agree, and the
total sign is \((-1)^4=1\).  Hence
\begin{equation}
\cC_{2,0}=-6.
\label{eq:C20}
\end{equation}

For \(j=1\), each monomial of \(G_1\) has coordinate powers
\(g^3,g^2,g^2\), but the total internal denominator order is only two.  A
coordinate with \(h_i=0\) contributes either
\(g^2/P=x^2\) or \(g^3/P=x^3(x^2-1)\), both polynomials, so no
\(x_i^{-1}\)-term can occur.  Nonvanishing would require all three
\(h_i\ge1\), a contradiction.  Thus \(\cC_{2,1}=0\).

For \(j=2\), the internal \(\eps\)-degree is zero.  If
\[
\mu_n=\coeff{x^{-1}}{\frac{g(x)^n}{P(x)}},
\]
then \(\mu_1=1\), \(\mu_2=\mu_3=0\).  The square terms of \(G_2\) have
type \((3,1,1)\), and its cross terms have type \((2,2,1)\); their tensor
coefficients vanish as \(\mu_3\mu_1^2\) and \(\mu_2^2\mu_1\).  Hence
\(\cC_{2,2}=0\).  The \(j=3\) summand already contains \(\eps^6\) and
cannot contribute to \(\eps^4\).  Equation \eqref{eq:quartic-Cj} now gives
\begin{equation}
D_2=4^9(-6)=-1572864
\label{eq:D2-direct}
\end{equation}
without using the all-degree binomial collapse.

\subsection{A direct normal-form ledger for the constant}
\label{subsec:quartic-constant}

Set \(a=0\) and \(\eps=1\).  Then
\[
F_i=x_i^4+x_{i-1}-x_{i+1},
\qquad
t=64x_0^3x_1^3x_2^3+4(x_0^3+x_1^3+x_2^3).
\]
Let \(R(e_0,e_1,e_2)\) be the coefficient of
\(x_0^3x_1^3x_2^3\) in the normal form of
\(x_0^{e_0}x_1^{e_1}x_2^{e_2}\).  Reduction in the three coordinates gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R(e_0,e_1,e_2)
&=R(e_0-4,e_1+1,e_2)-R(e_0-4,e_1,e_2+1),\\
R(e_0,e_1,e_2)
&=-R(e_0+1,e_1-4,e_2)+R(e_0,e_1-4,e_2+1),\\
R(e_0,e_1,e_2)
&=R(e_0+1,e_1,e_2-4)-R(e_0,e_1+1,e_2-4).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:quartic-R}
\end{equation}
The terminal value is \(R(3,3,3)=1\), and every other standard exponent
triple has value zero.  The complete ledger needed for \(t^3\) is
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
Exponent pattern & \(R\)-value\\
\midrule
\((9,9,9)\) & \(-6\)\\
cyclic permutation of \((9,6,6)\) & \(2\)\\
cyclic permutation of \((9,3,3)\) & \(0\)\\
cyclic permutation of \((6,6,3)\) & \(-1\)\\
permutation of \((9,0,0)\) & \(0\)\\
permutation of \((6,3,0)\) & \(0\)\\
\((3,3,3)\) & \(1\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For example,
\[
R(9,6,6)=R(5,7,6)-R(5,6,7)=1-(-1)=2,
\]
and
\[
R(9,9,9)=2R(5,10,9)=2(1-4)=-6.
\]
The remaining rows require at most two reductions or have total degree nine
without already being the top standard monomial.

Put \(Q=64x_0^3x_1^3x_2^3\) and
\(R_0=4(x_0^3+x_1^3+x_2^3)\).  Grouping
\((Q+R_0)^3\) by the preceding patterns gives

Source & Top-coefficient contribution\\
\midrule

\section{Fixed moment, local multiplicity, and minimality}
\label{sec:minimality}

The next two lemmas have different conclusions and are used in that order.

\begin{lemma}[Zero fixed moment]
\label{lem:fixed-moment}
On the fixed algebra \eqref{eq:fixed-algebra}, the fixed contribution to the
formal period-three \(m\)-th trace moment is zero for every \(m\ge2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All three cyclic coordinates agree on the fixed algebra, so
\[
t_\eps=q^3+3\eps^2q.
\]
Since \(q^2=0\),
\[
(q^3+3\eps^2q)^m=0
\qquad(m\ge2).
\]
At \(\eps=1\), this is the fixed contribution to the trace of
\((Df^3)^m\).  Hence the formal exact-period-three moment equals the raw
\(f^3\)-fixed moment \(S_m(a,1)\) after subtracting a zero fixed moment.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Local fixed multiplicity inside \texorpdfstring{\(\Fix(f^3)\)}{Fix(f3)}]
\label{lem:fixed-multiplicity}
Let \(\alpha\) be a root of \(p\) of multiplicity \(r\ge2\).  The local
length of \(\Fix(f^3)\) at the associated fixed point equals \(r\), the
fixed-scheme length at \(\alpha\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set
\[
\delta=x_0-\alpha,
\qquad
u=x_1-x_0,
\qquad
v=x_2-x_0.
\]
At \((\delta,u,v)=(0,0,0)\), the \((u,v)\)-Jacobian of \((F_1,F_2)\) is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
which is invertible.  Formal elimination therefore gives
\[
u=-p(\alpha+\delta)+O(\delta^{2r-1}),
\qquad
v=p(\alpha+\delta)+O(\delta^{2r-1}).
\]
The remaining equation is
\begin{equation}
F_0=p(\alpha+\delta)+v-u
=3p(\alpha+\delta)+O(\delta^{2r-1}).
\label{eq:local-fixed-equation}
\end{equation}
Because \(2r-1>r\) and the characteristic is zero, the order of
\eqref{eq:local-fixed-equation} is exactly \(r\).  This is the local length
of \(\Fix(f^3)\) at the fixed support.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\Cref{lem:fixed-moment} is a statement about a trace power, while
\cref{lem:fixed-multiplicity} is a statement about scheme length.  The first
does not imply the second.  In particular, zero fixed moment is never used as
a shortcut for subtracting reduced fixed support.
\end{remark}

For the quartic fiber, the \(f^3\)-fixed algebra has length \(4^3=64\).
By \cref{lem:fixed-multiplicity}, the fixed algebra contributes its full
length \(4\), so the formal exact-period-three zero-cycle has length
\begin{equation}
64-4=60.
\label{eq:exact-length-60}
\end{equation}
Every exact period-three orbit contains three points, and matrix trace is
cyclically invariant along the orbit.  Only after the pointwise exact-period
subtraction do we divide by three:
\begin{equation}
\frac{-1296000-1572864L^3}{3}
=-432000-524288L^3.
\label{eq:cyclewise-moment}
\end{equation}

The normalized conjugacy calculation from \cref{sec:low-periods}, with
\(m=2\), gives
\[
f_{2,L}\sim f_{2,M}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
L^3=M^3.
\]
The coefficient of \(L^3\) in \eqref{eq:quartic-moment-main} is nonzero.
Hence the period-three moment separates precisely the normalized conjugacy
classes on the complete quartic fiber.  Periods one and two are constant
there, so period three is minimal.  This proves \cref{thm:quartic}.

\section{Scope, open problem, and proof provenance}
\label{sec:scope}

\subsection{The open coefficient problem}

The formula \eqref{eq:D-certificate-proved} reduces all-degree period-three
recovery to a concrete combinatorial question:
\begin{equation}
D_m\stackrel{?}{\ne}0
\qquad\text{for every }m\ge2.
\label{eq:open-nonvanishing}
\end{equation}
No degree-independent sign, positivity, or nonvanishing proof is known in the
present argument.  Consequently \cref{thm:uniform} proves an affine
two-term law and an exact slope certificate, but it does not prove that
period three separates the family in every degree.

\subsection{Nonclaims}

following:

  separation;
  \item a theorem for all quartic H\'enon maps, a global degree-four period
  cutoff, a global conjugacy classification, or global multiplier rigidity;
  \item discovery of the exceptional quartic family or of its period-one/two
  blindness;
  \item novelty of global residues, quotient traces, formal/dynatomic cycles,

  \item a historical-priority conclusion from a bounded no-hit search;

\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The exceptional family \(f_{m,a}(x,y)=(y+(x^m-a)^2,x)\) is formally blind
at periods one and two, yet its period-three trace residue has a rigid
two-term dependence on the normalized coordinate \(a^{2m-1}\).  A complete
Laurent fiber calculation turns the slope into the finite certificate
\eqref{eq:D-certificate-proved}.  In degree four, the complete normalized
fiber with formal fixed-point trace multiset \(0^4\) is
\(p=(x^2-L)^2\), and its exact affine period-three moment recovers \(L^3\).
The separate local-multiplicity argument shows that the exact-period scheme
has length \(60\), so the pointwise and cyclewise normalizations are both
controlled.

The quartic theorem is complete within its stated fiber.  The uniform theorem
ends at a sharper frontier: proving or disproving
\(D_m\ne0\) in every degree is an explicit open combinatorial problem.
