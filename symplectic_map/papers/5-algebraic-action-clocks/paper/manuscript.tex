\begin{abstract}
Let an exact symplectic map, a Liouville primitive, and a single-valued
rational exact potential be defined over $\Qbar$.  If the potential is
regular on an algebraic period-$n$ orbit, then its one-traversal action is a
finite sum of algebraic values and hence algebraic.  Hermite--Lindemann
therefore prevents this normalized action from equalling any branch of
$\log\beta$ for nonzero algebraic $\beta\ne1$, in particular the positive
real value $\log p$ for a rational prime.  The conclusion persists under
fixed algebraic scales, averages, repetitions, and algebraic exact gauges,
provided the full endpoint term is retained.  It fails as a map-only
statement: for the identity map, the constant potential $G\equiv\log 2$ is a
symbolic exact countercontrol once transcendental normalization is allowed.

For $H_a(q,p)=(q^2-a-p,q)$ we verify
$H_a^*(p\,dq)-p\,dq=d(2q^3/3-pq)$ and show that the usual type-1 generating
function has the opposite sign on the graph.  Every finite periodic point is
algebraic for algebraic $a$.  If $a$ is $S$-integral in a base field, then in
the orbit field only $3\Action$ is certified $S$-integral in general; the
fixed-point action $-1/3$ makes this denominator sharp.  A source-locked
eight-stage static audit and 82 passing tests verify the implementation while
computing no candidate orbit or action.  This is a narrow normalization-aware
design certificate, not a universal no-go theorem for symplectic clocks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Periodic-orbit descriptions of spectral problems naturally suggest looking
for a dynamical quantity proportional to a prime logarithm.  The
Berry--Keating discussion is one familiar source of this heuristic scale
\citep{berry1999riemann}.  Here we ask a strictly prior design question:
can a closed generating-function action of an algebraic exact-symplectic map,
with its representative fixed before any orbit is evaluated, equal $\log p$
\emph{exactly}?  We import neither prime tables nor zero ordinates, and we do
not attempt a trace formula or an approximate fit.

This question contains a normalization trap.  If
$F^*\theta-\theta=dG$, then $G+C$ is another exact potential for every
constant $C$.  A period-$n$ action changes by $nC$.  Absolute action is
therefore not a map-only invariant; one must record the primitive, potential,
additive representative, domain, and any endpoint convention.  Exact
symplectic literature routinely uses generating actions, action differences,
and variational principles \citep{kook1989periodic,mackay1984transport,
meiss1992symplectic,delshams1997melnikov}, while action and average-action
spectra emphasize the importance of iteration conventions
\citep{ginzburg2009action,mazzucchelli2013degenerate}.  Our contribution is
not a new definition of action.  It is a provenance certificate for one
frozen arithmetic representative.

The certificate has four parts.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Regular evaluation of a $\Qbar$-rational potential on a finite
  algebraic orbit places the action in $\Qbar$ at every period.
  Hermite--Lindemann then excludes every logarithm branch of every
  nontrivial algebraic target.
  \item A complete stepwise gauge ledger shows exactly what telescopes and
  what remains.  Algebraic endpoint mismatches and constants alter the value
  but not its algebraicity.  A transcendental constant defeats the conclusion
  and is therefore an assumption boundary, not an inconvenient special case.
  \item The quadratic H\'enon automorphism gives an explicit specialization:
  the exact potential and type-1 generating function have opposite signs,
  all finite periodic points are algebraic, and the justified integral
  refinement is $3\Action\in\OKS$, not $\Action\in\OKS$.
  \item A source-locked static program audits identities, domain gates,
  normalization controls, and proof dependencies.  It performs no candidate
  periodic-point solve or candidate action evaluation, so its outputs are
  implementation evidence rather than empirical support for the theorem.
\end{enumerate}

Figure~\ref{fig:action-certificate} displays both the implication and its
normalization boundary.  The scope is intentionally narrow.  We do not
exclude $\log|\Action|$, multiplier or return-time clocks, multivalued
potentials, closed non-exact changes of primitive, transcendental
normalizations, or approximate matching.

\section{Exact action and arithmetic context}
\label{sec:context}

Let $X$ be an algebraic variety over $\Qbar$, let $U\subset X$ be an open
set, and let $F:U\dashrightarrow X$ be a rational map.  On the associated
complex phase space, suppose a single-valued one-form $\theta$ and a
single-valued rational function $G\in\Qbar(X)$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
  F^*\theta-\theta=dG
  \label{eq:exact-potential}
\end{equation}
where all terms are defined.  For an orbit
$P_{j+1}=F(P_j)$ with $P_n=P_0$, define the one-traversal potential action
\begin{equation}
  \Action_G(P_0)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}G(P_j).
  \label{eq:action-definition}
\end{equation}
The regularity clause is part of the definition used here: every map step is
defined, every $P_j$ lies in $X(\Qbar)$, and $G$ has no pole at any $P_j$.

The distinction between absolute actions and action differences is essential.
The latter often remove additive constants and carry geometric information
about transport \citep{mackay1984transport,meiss1992symplectic}; the present
proposal needs an absolute value.  Likewise, a discrete type-1 generating
function can define a variational sum \citep{kook1989periodic,
mazzucchelli2013degenerate,bialy2023locally}, but its sign and endpoint
convention must be matched to \eqref{eq:exact-potential} rather than assumed.
Exact potential conventions and their additive ambiguity are standard
\citep{delshams1997melnikov}.

The arithmetic input is elementary until the last step.  Rational functions
defined over $\Qbar$ take algebraic values at algebraic points whenever their
denominators do not vanish; $\Qbar$ is a field; and a finite sum remains
algebraic.  The transcendence input is the classical
Hermite--Lindemann theorem: if $0\ne\alpha\in\Qbar$, then $e^\alpha$ is
transcendental \citep{baker2022transcendental}.  We use this theorem, not any
stronger result on linear forms in logarithms.

The H\'enon example belongs to the standard class of polynomial plane
automorphisms \citep{friedland1989dynamical}.  Quadratic symplectic maps and
their normal forms have a long history \citep{moser1994quadratic}, and recent
work continues to study arithmetic periodic points and broader open problems
\citep{dehenon2024open,kim2024many}.  None of these sources is cited for the
paper-specific algebraic-action certificate or the denominator-three
calculation.

\section{The algebraic-action certificate}
\label{sec:certificate}

We first separate finite evaluation from transcendence.

\begin{theorem}[Finite algebraic evaluation]
\label{thm:evaluation}
Let $F$, $G$, and $(P_0,\ldots,P_{n-1})$ satisfy the hypotheses above.
Then
\begin{equation}
  \Action_G(P_0)\in\Qbar.
  \label{eq:algebraic-action}
\end{equation}
The conclusion holds for every finite $n$; it does not arise from a bounded
period search.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $P_j\in X(\Qbar)$ and $G\in\Qbar(X)$ is regular at $P_j$, evaluation
gives $G(P_j)\in\Qbar$ for each $j$.  The action is a sum of exactly $n$
such values.  Closure of $\Qbar$ under finite addition proves
\eqref{eq:algebraic-action}.
\end{proof}

The theorem also applies to step-dependent maps and potentials: if every
$F_j$ is defined over $\Qbar$, every step is defined at its current point,
and every $G_j(P_j)$ is pole-free, then $\sum_jG_j(P_j)$ is algebraic.  One
must check the terms before summation.  A formal cancellation of undefined
expressions cannot certify an action.

\begin{corollary}[Algebraic logarithmic targets]
\label{cor:log-target}
Under Theorem~\ref{thm:evaluation}, let
$\beta\in\Qbar$.  If $\beta\ne0,1$, then $\Action_G(P_0)$ is not any complex
logarithm of $\beta$.  If $\beta=0$, no complex logarithm exists.  For
$\beta=1$, equality with a branch is possible only in the algebraic case
$\Action_G(P_0)=0$, corresponding to the zero branch.  In particular,
\begin{equation}
  \Action_G(P_0)\ne\log p
  \qquad\text{for every rational prime }p,
  \label{eq:prime-log-exclusion}
\end{equation}
where $\log p$ denotes the positive real logarithm.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $\Action_G$ were a complex logarithm of a nonzero $\beta$, then
$e^{\Action_G}=\beta$.  When $\Action_G\ne0$, Hermite--Lindemann says that
the left side is transcendental, contradicting algebraicity of $\beta$.
Thus an algebraic logarithm must be zero, forcing $\beta=1$.  The zero target
has no logarithm in $\C$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:prime-log-exclusion} follows
because $p\in\Qbar\setminus\{0,1\}$.
\end{proof}

The same argument closes several nearby conventions, provided every new
coefficient is frozen and algebraic.

\begin{proposition}[Algebraic post-conventions]
\label{prop:extensions}
Let $A=\Action_G(P_0)\in\Qbar$, let $c\in\Qbar^*$, and let
$r,n\in\Z_{>0}$.  Then $cA$, $A/n$, and $rA$ are algebraic and cannot equal
$\log\beta$ for $\beta\in\Qbar\setminus\{0,1\}$.  Under the standard
embedding $\Qbar\hookrightarrow\C$, the numbers
$\operatorname{Re}A$, $\operatorname{Im}A$, and $|A|$ are algebraic and
obey the same exclusion.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The scaled, averaged, and repeated values are algebraic by field closure.  If
$A$ is algebraic, so is its complex conjugate $\bar A$; hence
$\operatorname{Re}A=(A+\bar A)/2$ and
$\operatorname{Im}A=(A-\bar A)/(2i)$ are algebraic.  Also
$|A|^2=A\bar A$ is algebraic, and any square root of an algebraic number is
algebraic.  Corollary~\ref{cor:log-target} applies to each resulting
algebraic value.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A required nonclaim]
Proposition~\ref{prop:extensions} proves that $|A|$ cannot itself equal
$\log p$.  It says nothing about $\log|A|$.  Indeed, an algebraic action with
$|A|=p$ would produce $\log|A|=\log p$ by post-processing.  The argument also
does not control $\arg A$ or approximate equality.
\end{remark}

\section{Gauge ledger and normalization countercontrols}
\label{sec:gauge}

Suppose first that $\theta'=\theta+d\chi$ for a single-valued rational
function $\chi$.  Then
\begin{align}
F^*\theta'-\theta'
 &=F^*\theta-\theta+d(\chi\circ F-\chi) \\
 &=d\bigl(G+\chi\circ F-\chi+C\bigr)
\end{align}
for an arbitrary constant $C$.  Thus one may take
\begin{equation}
  G'=G+\chi\circ F-\chi+C.
  \label{eq:autonomous-gauge}
\end{equation}
On a closed period-$n$ orbit, single-valuedness gives
\begin{equation}
  \Action_{G'}-\Action_G=nC.
  \label{eq:autonomous-shift}
\end{equation}
Consequently $C=0$ preserves the numerical action; any algebraic $C$
preserves its algebraicity; a transcendental $C$ may destroy the certificate.

For a stepwise sequence, the general endpoint formula is more informative.
Let $P_{j+1}=F_j(P_j)$ and take
\begin{equation}
 G'_j=G_j+\chi_{j+1}\circ F_j-\chi_j+C_j.
 \label{eq:step-gauge}
\end{equation}
Every transition, gauge value, and potential value is assumed separately
defined.  Direct summation telescopes to
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}G'_j(P_j)-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}G_j(P_j)
 =\chi_n(P_n)-\chi_0(P_0)+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}C_j.
 \label{eq:general-endpoint}
\end{equation}
Endpoint compatibility $\chi_n(P_n)=\chi_0(P_0)$ permits the shorter
constant-sum formula.  Without compatibility the endpoint mismatch remains;
if it and all $C_j$ are algebraic, the shifted action is still algebraic and
the prime-log exclusion still holds.  Compatibility is a value-simplifying
condition, not a prerequisite for algebraicity.

Figure~\ref{fig:gauge-scope} summarizes exact positive, negative, and edge
controls.  A pole or undefined step stops evaluation.  A multivalued gauge
with untracked monodromy lies outside the formula.  The $\beta=0$ and
$\beta=1$ rows record the two necessary logarithm edge cases.  The matrix is
  categorical: each cell is derived from named frozen-result predicates or
  marked as theorem-defined in an explicit scope ledger; it does not
  numerically evaluate a logarithm.

\begin{proposition}[Sharp normalization boundary]
\label{prop:identity-control}
Exactness of the map alone does not force algebraicity of its absolute
action.  On $X=\A^2$ take $F=\operatorname{id}$ and $\theta=p\,dq$.  The
constant potential $G\equiv\log 2$ satisfies
$F^*\theta-\theta=0=dG$, while every point is fixed and has one-step action
$\log 2$.
\end{proposition}

The proposition is a symbolic control: ``$\log 2$'' is a formal constant
label and no decimal logarithm or external target table is evaluated.  It
shows why the hypothesis $G\in\Qbar(X)$, including its additive
normalization, cannot be removed.  More generally, after an orbit and its
action $A$ are known, the post-hoc choice
\begin{equation}
  C=\frac{\log p-A}{n}
  \label{eq:target-injection}
\end{equation}
forces the shifted action to equal $\log p$.  This is target injection, not
an intrinsic clock.  The source lock forbids orbit-dependent or
target-dependent normalization.

Three nearby constructions require new ledgers rather than a silent reuse of
\eqref{eq:general-endpoint}: a multivalued $\chi$ may carry monodromy; a
closed but non-exact change of primitive need not be $d\chi$; and a local
potential may not extend single-valuedly around the orbit.  None is ruled out
by the present certificate.

\section{The algebraic H\'enon certificate}
\label{sec:henon}

For $a\in\Qbar$, consider
\begin{equation}
  H_a(q,p)=(q^2-a-p,q),
  \qquad H_a^{-1}(q,p)=(p,p^2-a-q).
  \label{eq:henon-map}
\end{equation}
Its Jacobian matrix is
\begin{equation}
  DH_a(q,p)=\begin{pmatrix}2q&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad \det DH_a=1.
\end{equation}
With $\theta=p\,dq$, direct pullback gives
\begin{align}
 H_a^*\theta-
 \theta
 &=q\,d(q^2-a-p)-p\,dq \\
 &=(2q^2-p)\,dq-q\,dp \\
 &=d\left(\frac23q^3-pq\right).
 \label{eq:henon-potential}
\end{align}
Thus our potential convention is
\begin{equation}
  G(q,p)=\frac23q^3-pq.
  \label{eq:G}
\end{equation}

The type-1 generating function
\begin{equation}
  L_a(q,Q)=\frac13q^3-aq-qQ
  \label{eq:L}
\end{equation}
satisfies $p=\partial_qL_a=q^2-a-Q$ and
$P=-\partial_QL_a=q$, so its graph is precisely
$Q=q^2-a-p$, $P=q$.  Substitution on the graph gives
\begin{equation}
  L_a(q,Q)=-\frac23q^3+pq=-G(q,p).
  \label{eq:sign}
\end{equation}
The two conventional closed sums therefore have opposite sign.  Both remain
algebraic and both are excluded from $\log p$ under the frozen algebraic
normalization; nevertheless, sign must not be hidden in a comparison of
numerical action values.

Writing a periodic orbit as
$P_j=(q_j,p_j)$ gives $p_j=q_{j-1}$ and the cyclic recurrence
\begin{equation}
  q_{j+1}+q_{j-1}=q_j^2-a,
  \qquad j\in\Z/n\Z.
  \label{eq:recurrence}
\end{equation}
For $n=1$ the two ordered neighbor slots both equal $q_0$; for $n=2$ they
both equal the other coordinate.  The multiplicity is structural and must
not be collapsed.

\begin{theorem}[All-period H\'enon action]
\label{thm:henon}
Let $a\in\Qbar$.  Every finite periodic point of $H_a$ has coordinates in
$\Qbar$.  For every such period-$n$ orbit,
\begin{equation}
  \Action_G
  =\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac23q_j^3-q_{j-1}q_j\right)
  \in\Qbar,
  \label{eq:henon-action}
\end{equation}
and $\sum_jL_a(q_j,q_{j+1})=-\Action_G$.  Neither action convention equals
$\log p$ for a rational prime.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Homogenize the $n$ equations \eqref{eq:recurrence} in
$[Q_0:\cdots:Q_{n-1}:Z]\in\mathbb P^n$.  At $Z=0$, every equation reduces
to $Q_j^2=0$, hence every $Q_j=0$, which is not a projective point.  Thus the
projective zero scheme has no point on the hyperplane at infinity.  A
  positive-dimensional projective component would intersect the fixed
  hyperplane $Z=0$, where we just proved that there is no point.  Hence the
  projective scheme is zero-dimensional.  Consequently the affine cyclic
system has finitely many points and is cut out over the number field
$\Q(a)$; all $q_j$ are algebraic over $\Q(a)$ and hence over $\Q$.  Since
$p_j=q_{j-1}$, all phase-space coordinates are algebraic.  Formula
\eqref{eq:henon-action} follows from \eqref{eq:G}, and each term is
algebraic.  The sign identity follows from \eqref{eq:sign}; the logarithmic
exclusion follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:log-target}.
\end{proof}

The elementary argument above proves algebraicity, not a count of exact
periodic points or a transversality statement.  Scheme multiplicities are
irrelevant to the evaluation conclusion.

\begin{corollary}[$S$-integral denominator certificate]
\label{cor:s-integral}
Let $K_0$ be a number field, let $S_0$ contain its archimedean places, and
assume $a\in\mathcal O_{K_0,S_0}$.  For a finite periodic orbit choose a
finite extension $K/K_0$ containing every orbit coordinate, and let $S$ be
the places of $K$ above $S_0$, together with all archimedean places.  Then
every $q_j,p_j$ lies in $\OKS$ and
\begin{equation}
  3\Action_G\in\OKS.
  \label{eq:three-action}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $\Action_G$ is integral away from $S$ and places above the
rational prime $3$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Fix a non-Archimedean place $v\notin S$.  Then $|a|_v\le1$.  If
$R=\max_j|q_j|_v>1$, choose $j$ with $|q_j|_v=R$.  By the ultrametric
inequality,
\begin{equation}
 |q_j^2-a|_v=R^2,
 \qquad
 |q_{j+1}+q_{j-1}|_v\le R,
\end{equation}
contradicting \eqref{eq:recurrence}.  Hence every $q_j$ and thus every
$p_j=q_{j-1}$ is $S$-integral.  Finally,
\begin{equation}
 3\Action_G
 =\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(2q_j^3-3q_{j-1}q_j\right)
\end{equation}
is an integer-coefficient polynomial in those coordinates.
\end{proof}

The factor $3$ is necessary in general.  At $a=-1$, the point $(q,p)=(1,1)$
is fixed because $1^2-(-1)-1=1$, and
\begin{equation}
  \Action_G=G(1,1)=\frac23-1=-\frac13,
  \qquad 3\Action_G=-1.
  \label{eq:sharp-third}
\end{equation}
Thus we claim only \eqref{eq:three-action}; we do not claim that
$\Action_G$ is integral at places above $3$.

Figure~\ref{fig:henon-static} records the exact identity and proof ledgers.
The no-infinity rows at periods $1,2,3,5$ audit the generic leading-system
construction; they are not a finite-period proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:henon}.  The theorem uses the displayed all-$n$ argument.

\section{Source-locked static implementation audit}
\label{sec:audit}

The mathematical conclusion is all-period and deductive.  The accompanying
software has a narrower role: it checks whether the implemented formulas,

protocol.  Before the formal static run, a versioned source lock fixed the
candidate ID, hypotheses, nonclaims, registered stages, forbidden data, and
zero-candidate-execution rule.  An independent pre-run Round-3 review issued

that implementation.  Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} gives hashes and
reproduction commands.

\section{Interpretation, route decision, and limitations}
\label{sec:interpretation}

The strongest justified decision is
\begin{quote}
\centering
\texttt{GO\_AS\_NARROW\_DESIGN\_CERTIFICATE}: close only the frozen,
normalized algebraic absolute action as an exact prime-logarithm clock.
\end{quote}
This is stronger than a finite non-observation because it holds at every
period under stated hypotheses.  It is weaker than a map-level obstruction
because Proposition~\ref{prop:identity-control} shows that normalization can
change the arithmetic class.  The certificate should therefore be used as a
pre-execution filter: before searching or fitting, ask whether the proposed
observable is already forced into $\Qbar$.

The precise surviving directions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Logarithmic post-processing.}  The theorem excludes
  $|\Action|=\log p$, not $\log|\Action|=\log p$.
  \item \textbf{Transcendental normalization.}  A frozen, target-independent
  transcendental constant is outside the theorem.  Its mathematical origin
  would need separate justification; post-hoc target injection is not
  admissible.
  \item \textbf{Multivalued or topological cocycles.}  Monodromy and closed
  non-exact primitive changes require a branch or cohomology ledger.
  \item \textbf{Different observables.}  Multiplier logs, return times,
  energy derivatives, infinite-place or adelic quantities are not actions of
  the form \eqref{eq:action-definition}.
  \item \textbf{Approximation.}  Algebraicity does not prevent algebraic
  numbers from approximating transcendental ones.  No quantitative separation
  or fit statistic is proved here.
\end{itemize}

The H\'enon result is equally scoped.  We prove algebraicity of finite
periodic points and $S$-integrality of $3\Action_G$ for the stated quadratic
map.  We do not classify periodic points, periods, branches at infinity,
multiplier clocks, or analytic continuations.  We do not assert a prime-orbit
correspondence, a dynamical zeta identity, a spectral determinant, a trace
formula, or a quantization.  In particular, the paper is not a universal
no-go theorem for symplectic or arithmetic dynamics.

The individual ingredients are classical or elementary.  The literature
audit located no basis for a historical-first claim, and none is made.  The
standalone contribution is the complete normalization-aware, source-locked
certificate and its exact H\'enon instantiation.  If a publication venue
requires substantially deeper standalone novelty, the planned disposition is
\path{MERGE_IF_STANDALONE_DEPTH_IS_REQUIRED}: merge this certificate into a
broader obstruction synthesis rather than claim greater standalone depth.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A regular finite sum of a frozen $\Qbar$-rational exact potential on an
algebraic periodic orbit is algebraic.  Hermite--Lindemann then rules out an
exact equality with $\log p$ at every period.  The statement survives
algebraic scales and algebraic exact gauges only when endpoints and constants
are tracked; the symbolic identity-map control shows why arbitrary
transcendental normalization cannot be ignored.  For the quadratic H\'enon
map the exact potential is opposite in sign to the type-1 generating function,
all finite periodic points are algebraic, and only $3\Action$ is generally
$S$-integral, sharply.

The result is best understood as an arithmetic provenance test, not as a new
transcendence theorem and not as a universal obstruction to dynamical clocks.
A natural next project is a separately source-locked intrinsic
nonalgebraic or multivalued clock with a complete branch, endpoint, and target
independence ledger.

\section{H\'enon geometry and valuation details}
\label{app:henon-details}

This appendix records the two degeneracies most likely to be lost in a
compressed implementation.  For period one, cyclic indexing in
\eqref{eq:recurrence} yields
\begin{equation}
  2q_0=q_0^2-a.
\end{equation}
The two neighbor \emph{slots} coincide but must both be retained.  For period
two the system is
\begin{equation}
  2q_1=q_0^2-a,
  \qquad
  2q_0=q_1^2-a.
\end{equation}
At period $n\ge3$, the neighbors are written separately even if a special
solution happens to make their values equal.

For arbitrary $n$, the homogenized $j$th equation has the form
\begin{equation}
  Q_j^2-Q_{j+1}Z-Q_{j-1}Z-aZ^2=0.
\end{equation}
At $Z=0$ it becomes $Q_j^2=0$.  Hence the entire projective zero scheme is
disjoint from infinity.  The argument uses the fact that a
positive-dimensional projective subvariety cannot be contained in the affine
chart $Z\ne0$.  It does not require the affine equations to be reduced, and
therefore proves finiteness even in the presence of multiplicity.

For Corollary~\ref{cor:s-integral}, the orbit field is explicit in the
quantifiers.  Starting from $(K_0,S_0)$, one first chooses a finite extension
$K$ containing every coordinate and then extends $S_0$ to all places of $K$
above it.  The maximum argument is applied only at non-Archimedean
$v\notin S$.  This ordering avoids silently treating an algebraic coordinate
as if it belonged to the base field.  The sharp example
\eqref{eq:sharp-third} shows that the denominator in $G$ cannot be discarded.
