## Theorem normalization

Work over $\mathbf C$, let $d\ge2$, set $r=d-1$, and choose arbitrary positive
periods $\mathbf n=(n_1,\ldots,n_r)$, allowing repetitions. The marked cycles
are labelled, exact, pairwise disjoint, and simple. Point markings are first
formed and then divided by the free cyclic-shift group
$$
G_{\mathbf n}=\prod_i\mathbf Z/n_i\mathbf Z.
$$
The cycle-marked incidence is $\mathcal X_{\mathbf n}^{\circ}$, its scalar
fiber is $\mathcal S_{\mathbf n}$, and $\mathcal C_{\mathbf n}$ is the unique
irreducible component containing the full scalar locus. The coordinate map is
$$
\Psi=(-b,\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_r),
\qquad
\rho_i=\operatorname{tr}(DH_{b,p}^{n_i}).
$$

The five theorem parts are conjunctive. None is treated as a heuristic or
inferred from experimental evidence.

## Finite-free loop lemma and its limits

For the ordered $n$-loop algebra, the cyclic relations have monic leading
monomials $x_j^d$. They are pairwise coprime for distinct variables, so monic
Buchberger reduction over the coefficient ring gives the displayed standard
monomial basis and rank $d^n$.

The cyclic edge cases are explicit and correct:

- for $n=1$, the relation is $p(x_0)+(b-1)x_0$ with leading monomial $x_0^d$;
- for $n=2$, the relations are $p(x_0)+(b-1)x_1$ and
  $p(x_1)+(b-1)x_0$, with leading monomials $x_0^d,x_1^d$;
- tensoring the separate marking algebras gives rank
  $d^{n_1+\cdots+n_r}$.

The functorial recurrence identifies the ordered-loop scheme with the full
$H^n$ fixed-point incidence. The package correctly records that this full
scheme includes lower-period loops, collisions, nonsimple points, and
possibly nonreduced fibers. Finite freeness is never promoted to
irreducibility, an exact-cycle count, finiteness of the deleted open, or a
uniform reconstruction theorem.

## Simple incidence, exact cycles, and quotient

The orbit-variable Jacobian for each marking is $DH^{n_i}-I$. Its invertibility
on the simple locus proves that the point-marked incidence is étale over the
smooth parameter base. Exact period is obtained by removing the finitely many
proper-divisor fixed loci, and pairwise orbit disjointness is obtained by
removing the finitely many iterate-collision loci.

On an exact cycle, no nontrivial cyclic shift fixes the point marking. The
product cyclic action is therefore free, and the characteristic-zero finite
quotient is finite étale. Traces are invariant under the shifts and descend to
the cycle-marked quotient. Repeated numerical period values cause no gap:
cycles remain labelled and are required to be distinct; no unannounced
symmetric-group quotient is taken.

The residual normal-form action by $\mu_{d-1}$ is separate. It preserves $b$
and all traces and generically obstructs injectivity when $d>2$. The theorem
does not pass to a naive coarse quotient near stabilizers and does not claim
birationality or reconstruction on the monic-centered cover.

## Scalar boundary and the unique component

At $b=0$, direct induction gives
$$
H_{0,p}^n(x,y)=\bigl(p^n(x),p^{n-1}(x)\bigr).
$$
Thus the scalar fixed-point incidence is scheme-identical to the polynomial
fixed-point incidence. Minimal periods and orbit disjointness agree. Moreover,
$$
DH_{0,p}^n=
\begin{pmatrix}
(p^n)'(x)&0\\
(p^{n-1})'(x)&0
\end{pmatrix},
$$
so the Hénon trace is the polynomial multiplier $\lambda$ and
$\det(DH_{0,p}^n-I)=1-\lambda$. Scalar simplicity is therefore exactly
polynomial simplicity.

Gorbovickis's irreducible marked polynomial space and its nonempty simple
exact disjoint open give irreducibility of the point-marked scalar locus; the
free finite cyclic quotient remains irreducible. The total simple incidence is
regular because it is étale over a smooth base. Distinct irreducible
components of a regular scheme cannot meet. Hence the full irreducible scalar
locus lies in one and only one component $\mathcal C_{\mathbf n}$. No other
component meets $b=0$, and étaleness makes the scalar fiber reduced. This gives
the exact scheme-theoretic scalar fiber equality on the simple incidence.

The restricted projection $\mathcal C_{\mathbf n}\to\mathcal B_d$ is étale.
Its image is a nonempty open subset of the irreducible parameter base, hence
dense, so the projection is dominant. This proves no irreducibility statement
about the full marked Hénon incidence.

The indispensable imported result is Gorbovickis's arbitrary-period
polynomial theorem. The degree-two claim is assigned to Theorem 1.6 and Lemma
2.1, which operate for $d\ge2$ and $k\le d-1$, not to the headline Corollary

At that scalar point, the differential of $\Psi$ is block triangular. The
first diagonal block is $-1$, and the lower-right block is the polynomial
multiplier Jacobian. Therefore $\Psi$ is étale at that point. Source and target
are irreducible of the same dimension, so $\Psi$ is dominant and generically
étale. Injectivity of the induced function-field map proves that the marked
traces are algebraically independent over $\mathbf C(b)$.

## Safe fixed-$b$ specialization

Let $E$ be the nonempty étale locus of $\Psi$. The restriction $E\to T$ is
étale and hence open, so $V=\Psi(E)$ is nonempty open in
$T=\mathbf A^1\times\mathbf A^r$. Projecting $V$ to the first coordinate and
removing $b=0$ produces a nonempty open $U\subset\mathbf G_m$. For every
$b_0\in U$, the slice $V_{-b_0}$ is nonempty open dense in $\mathbf A^r$ and
lies in the image of the whole fiber. Hence the whole-fiber trace map is
dominant.

A reduced finite-type fiber has finitely many irreducible components. Because
their image closures cover the irreducible target, at least one component
dominates. Choosing it from the components meeting the base-changed étale
locus gives generic étaleness on that component. This argument neither assumes
nor proves irreducibility of a specialized fiber, all-component dominance,
every-$b$ coordinates, or a result at a prescribed value such as $b=-1$.

## Completed local form and Fitting base change

Étaleness of $\mathcal C_{\mathbf n}\to\mathcal B_d$ and equality of residue
fields identify the completed local ring at every simple scalar point with
$$
\mathbf C[[b,u_0,\ldots,u_{d-2}]].
$$
The scalar fiber is scheme-theoretically cut out by $b$, so its completion is
$\mathbf C[[u_0,\ldots,u_{d-2}]]$. Since each trace reduces to the polynomial
multiplier, divisibility by the non-zero-divisor $b$ gives the unique expansion
$$
\rho_i=\lambda_i+bG_i.
$$
This statement remains on the simple normal-form cover and is not transferred
through a coarse quotient at a stabilizer.

The square obtained by setting the target coordinate $t=-b$ to zero is
Cartesian. Relative Kähler differentials commute with this base change, and
zeroth Fitting ideals of the resulting finitely presented modules commute with
base change. Stacks Tags 07Z6 and 0C3I have exactly these recorded roles. In
the compatible local coordinates, the block determinant gives
$$
J_H\bmod b=\pm J_{\mathrm{poly}}.
$$
Thus the Hénon critical Fitting scheme restricts exactly to the polynomial
critical Fitting scheme on the simple scalar locus.

The repaired multiplicity guard is complete. Either the polynomial critical
scheme is empty, in which case the component claim is vacuous, or each actual
component is an effective Cartier divisor in the smooth scalar locus. At its
generic point, reduction of $(b,J_H)$ to $(J_{\mathrm{poly}})$ gives equality
of the finite generic lengths. For $d>2$, this does not give a numerical length
at an arbitrary closed point; such a statement would require a separately
proved proper transverse slice.
