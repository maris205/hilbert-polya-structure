## Accepted contribution

The bounded Paper-24 contribution is the conjunction of:

1. one explicit two-mode Hamiltonian product-shear family with arbitrary
   nonzero coefficients in characteristic zero;
2. a genuine Newton wall at $r=2$ shared by both competitive first-phase
   rows;

   selector sketch;

No single ingredient in that list is claimed new in isolation.

## Why the project is standalone

The original $m=2$ singleton was too close to Paper 20's grammar to carry a
new paper by itself. The accepted expansion to the full $m$-family plus the
crossed-binomial wall-fixing lemma changes the paper from “one more computed
matrix” into a support-family theorem with a rigid switching mechanism.

Standalone value would fall below threshold if any of the following were
removed:

- the full $m$-family;
- the true carry/no-cancellation/visibility proof;
- the forced wall-fixing ratio inside the ansatz;
- the exact parity laws and two-step monodromy framing.

## Mandatory anti-claims

The source and any later manuscript must not claim:

1. a theorem on the wall $r=2$;
2. a classification beyond the two-binomial / two-pure-power ansatz;
3. a maximal or necessary selector fan;
4. arbitrary period words, period $>2$, or finite-automaton realization;
5. positive-characteristic validity;
6. inverse-degree, entropy-equality, integrability, genericity,
   periodic-point, or nonconjugacy theorems;
7. novelty of the abstract period-$k$ monodromy principle itself;
8. first Perron realization, first tropical switching, or absolute literature
   priority.

## STOP conditions

Source progression must stop if:

- the theorem is narrowed back to the lone $m=2$ example;
- the period-two exchange is asserted without carry, no-cancellation, or
  visibility;
- the wall-fixing ratio is claimed outside the crossed-binomial /
  diagonal-pure-power ansatz;
- the paper headlines the conditional period-$k$ lemma instead of the explicit
  family;
- a direct public or internal theorem collision is verified;
- the article can no longer stand as a 22--30 content-page proof-first paper.

The proper response to a STOP is correction, narrowing, or blocking—not
computational supplementation or novelty inflation.
