\begin{abstract}
Periodic multipliers of a symplectic map occur in reciprocal pairs, but
reciprocity alone does not prevent an eigenvalue from having a prescribed
rational modulus.  We isolate an arithmetic obstruction for monic
area-preserving generalized H\'enon maps.  For a finite composition defined
over an $S$-integer ring, every finite complex periodic point is
$S$-integral, every return multiplier is an algebraic $S$-unit, and an exact
rational modulus $q$ can involve only the rational primes declared bad by
$S$.  The modulus statement does not assume that the multiplier is rational.
Its proof passes to a Galois closure and all places above the rational bad
set, so complex conjugation preserves the relevant unit group; it never
identifies complex conjugation with reciprocal pairing.

For the frozen map
\[
 H_u(X,Y)=(X^2-u-Y,X),\qquad u^3-2u^2+2u-2=0,
\]
with $u$ the unique real root, the bad set is empty.  Hence every exactly
rational periodic multiplier modulus equals one at every period, excluding
an exact rational-prime modulus clock.  A source-locked exact-arithmetic
audit through periods $1,2,3$ removes lower-period branches, recovers five
cycles, verifies determinant-one monodromy and reciprocal unit
polynomials, and finds one unit-modulus cycle and four cycles with irrational
algebraic-unit moduli.  This finite ledger audits the software; it is not the
source of the all-period result.  The control $a=-15/16$ has fixed point
$(5/4,5/4)$ and multipliers $2,1/2$, showing that the bad-prime support bound
is sharp.  The conclusion is a narrow good-reduction certificate, not a
universal obstruction for symplectic dynamics or for irrational instability
rates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Polynomial automorphisms of the affine plane provide a particularly explicit
meeting point of complex, arithmetic, and conservative dynamics.  Generalized
H\'enon maps form the basic nonlinear class in the structural description of
plane polynomial automorphisms \citep{friedland_milnor_1989}.  Their arithmetic
has been studied through periodic points, local and canonical heights, and
reduction at non-Archimedean places
\citep{silverman_1994,marcello_2003,kawaguchi_2006,kawaguchi_2013,ingram_2014,allen_demark_petsche_2018}.
Recent work also emphasizes rational periodic points in families and arithmetic
or multiplier rigidity
\citep{hsia_kawaguchi_2018,kim_etal_2024,cantat_dujardin_2026_rigidity,cantat_dujardin_2026_multiplier}.

This paper asks a deliberately narrower question.  Let $\lambda$ be an
eigenvalue of the derivative of a return map along a finite complex periodic
orbit.  When can its exact complex modulus be a positive rational number---and
in particular a rational prime?  The qualifier \emph{exact} matters: a decimal
close to an integer is not an arithmetic equality.  So does the distinction
between $\lambda\in\Q$ and $|\lambda|\in\Q$.  An algebraic multiplier may be
nonreal and nonrational while its modulus is rational.

For determinant-one maps the two eigenvalues are reciprocal, yet this
geometric fact alone gives no rationality obstruction: the pair $(2,1/2)$ is
already reciprocal.  The additional input here is good reduction.  A cyclic
non-Archimedean maximum argument makes periodic coordinates integral; integral
special-linear monodromy then makes both multipliers units.  To pass from a
unit statement about $\lambda$ to a support statement about $|\lambda|$, one
must also handle complex conjugation correctly.  Figure~\ref{fig:certificate}
summarizes this dependency chain.

Our contribution has three parts.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We prove an all-period theorem for finite compositions of monic,
  area-preserving generalized H\'enon maps over $\OKS$: finite periodic
  coordinates are $S$-integral, return multipliers are algebraic $S$-units,
  and every exact rational modulus is supported on the rational primes below
  $S$.
  \item We specialize the theorem to one source-locked algebraic-integer
  parameter $u$.  The resulting global polynomial symplectic automorphism has
  no periodic multiplier of exact rational-prime modulus at any period,
  although irrational algebraic-unit moduli larger than one remain possible.
  \item We supply a reproducible exact-arithmetic audit through period three,
  together with a sharp positive control and boundary controls.  The audit
  tests the implementation of exact branches, monodromy, elimination, and
  modulus classification; it does not extrapolate a finite search to all
  periods.
\end{enumerate}

The result should not be read as a universal symplectic no-go theorem.
Symplectic maps with rational multiplier moduli other than one exist, including
the elementary control in Section~\ref{sec:audit}.  Nor do we constrain
irrational algebraic moduli, singular values, or Lyapunov exponents.  The
obstruction concerns one exact arithmetic target under explicitly declared
good-reduction hypotheses.

\section{Arithmetic and multiplier context}
\label{sec:context}

The literature surrounding H\'enon dynamics is much broader than the support
certificate proved here.  The classical plane-automorphism framework supplies
the geometric setting \citep{friedland_milnor_1989}; arithmetic studies use
heights and local filtrations to control or organize orbits
\citep{silverman_1994,marcello_2003,kawaguchi_2006,kawaguchi_2013,ingram_2014}.
Non-Archimedean H\'enon systems also exhibit their own attractors and
horseshoes \citep{allen_demark_petsche_2018}.  Family-level work studies
periodic parameters and rational cycles
\citep{hsia_kawaguchi_2018,kim_etal_2024}.  Separately, dynamical
Manin--Mumford and recent multiplier-rigidity results impose powerful global
constraints under hypotheses very different from ours
\citep{dujardin_favre_2017,cantat_dujardin_2026_rigidity,cantat_dujardin_2026_multiplier}.

Table~\ref{tab:boundary} locates the present statement.  We use a standard
good-reduction mechanism but package it around an exact rational
\emph{modulus}, rather than rationality of the multiplier itself.  The main
technical caution is that the chosen complex conjugate $\bar\lambda$ need not
equal the reciprocal return eigenvalue $\lambda^{-1}$.  Both operations occur
in the proof, but at different stages.

\section{A good-reduction support theorem}
\label{sec:theorem}

Let $K$ be a number field, let $S$ be a finite set of places containing the
archimedean places, and put $R=\OKS$.  Write $\Rbar$ for the integral closure
of $R$ in $\overline K$.  Let $\SQ$ be the finite set of rational primes below
the finite places in $S$.  For $1\le i\le m$, take a monic polynomial
$p_i\in R[X]$ of degree $d_i\ge2$ and define
\begin{equation}
 H_i(X,Y)=(p_i(X)-Y,X),
 \qquad F=H_m\circ\cdots\circ H_1.
 \label{eq:factors}
\end{equation}
Each factor has polynomial inverse
$H_i^{-1}(X,Y)=(Y,p_i(Y)-X)$ and derivative
\begin{equation}
 DH_i(X,Y)=
 \begin{pmatrix}p_i'(X)&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det DH_i=1.
 \label{eq:derivative}
\end{equation}
Thus $F$ is a global polynomial automorphism preserving $dX\wedge dY$.

\begin{theorem}[Rational-modulus support]
\label{thm:support}
Every finite complex periodic point of $F$ has coordinates in $\Rbar$.  If
$F^n(P)=P$, both eigenvalues of $D_PF^n$ are units of $\Rbar$.  Fix the given
embedding into $\C$.  If one eigenvalue $\lambda$ satisfies
$|\lambda|=q\in\Q_{>0}$, then
\begin{equation}
 q\in\Z[\SQ^{-1}]_{>0}^{\times}
 =\left\{\prod_{p\in\SQ}p^{e_p}:e_p\in\Z\right\}.
 \label{eq:rational-support}
\end{equation}
In particular, an exact rational-prime modulus forces that prime to belong to
$\SQ$; no rationality assumption on $\lambda$ is required.
\end{theorem}

The proof is split into four lemmas to make the logical dependencies explicit.

\begin{lemma}[Algebraicity]
\label{lem:algebraicity}
Every finite complex periodic point of $F$ is algebraic over $K$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $F^n(P)=P$ and expand the orbit through its $m$ individual factors during
each of the $n$ returns.  The successive first coordinates form a cyclic
sequence $(z_j)_{j\in\Z/N\Z}$, with $N=mn$, satisfying
\begin{equation}
 p_{i_j}(z_j)-z_{j+1}-z_{j-1}=0,
 \label{eq:cyclic-recurrence}
\end{equation}
where $i_j$ is periodic modulo $m$.  Homogenize these $N$ equations with one
coordinate $Z$.  On the hyperplane $Z=0$, monicity and $d_{i_j}\ge2$ reduce
the $j$th equation to $Z_j^{d_{i_j}}=0$.  Hence all $Z_j$ vanish, which is
impossible in projective space.  The projective zero set therefore misses the
hyperplane at infinity.

A positive-dimensional projective subvariety meets every hyperplane, since
the hyperplane class is ample.  Thus the projective zero set is
zero-dimensional, and the affine cyclic system has finitely many complex
solutions.  Because its equations have coefficients in $K$, every coordinate
of every solution is algebraic over $K$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Cyclic non-Archimedean maximum]
\label{lem:maximum}
The coordinates of every finite periodic point belong to $\Rbar$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a finite extension $L/K$ containing the orbit coordinates.  Fix a
finite place $w$ of $L$ above a place outside $S$.  The coefficients of each
$p_i$ have $w$-absolute value at most one.  Suppose
$M=\max_j|z_j|_w>1$ and choose $j$ attaining the maximum.  Since $p_{i_j}$ is
monic of degree at least two,
\begin{equation}
 |p_{i_j}(z_j)|_w=|z_j|_w^{d_{i_j}}=M^{d_{i_j}}>M.
 \label{eq:strict-maximum}
\end{equation}
But the recurrence \eqref{eq:cyclic-recurrence} gives
\[
 |p_{i_j}(z_j)|_w
 =|z_{j+1}+z_{j-1}|_w
 \le\max\{|z_{j+1}|_w,|z_{j-1}|_w\}\le M,
\]
a contradiction.  Therefore all orbit coordinates have absolute value at
most one at every finite place outside $S$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:algebraicity} and
the valuation criterion for integrality imply that they lie in $\Rbar$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Unit monodromy]
\label{lem:monodromy}
For every $P$ with $F^n(P)=P$, both eigenvalues of $D_PF^n$ are units of
$\Rbar$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:maximum}, every entry $p_i'(z_j)$ in
\eqref{eq:derivative} belongs to $\Rbar$.  The return monodromy is a product
of these determinant-one matrices, so
\[
 M_P=D_PF^n\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\Rbar),\qquad
 \chi_P(T)=T^2-\operatorname{tr}(M_P)T+1.
\]
Each root is integral over $\Rbar$ because $\chi_P$ is monic with coefficients
in $\Rbar$; since $\Rbar$ is integral over $R$, transitivity of integrality
makes each root integral over $R$, and hence an element of $\Rbar$.  The other
root is $\lambda^{-1}$ because $\det(M_P)=1$, so the same argument applied to
both roots gives $\lambda,\lambda^{-1}\in\Rbar$, which is exactly the required
unit statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Conjugation-stable rational support]
\label{lem:conjugation}
If the multiplier in Lemma~\ref{lem:monodromy} satisfies
$|\lambda|=q\in\Q_{>0}$, then \eqref{eq:rational-support} holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a finite Galois extension $M/\Q$ inside $\C$ that contains $K$, the
orbit, and $\lambda$.  Let $T$ be \emph{all} finite places of $M$ above the
rational primes in $\SQ$.  Every finite place $W$ of $M$ outside $T$ lies over
a rational prime outside $\SQ$, so its restriction to $K$ lies outside $S$;
applying Lemma~\ref{lem:monodromy} to both $\lambda$ and $\lambda^{-1}$ gives
$v_W(\lambda)\geq0$ and $v_W(\lambda^{-1})\geq0$, hence
$v_W(\lambda)=0$.  Thus $\lambda\in\mathcal O_{M,T}^{\times}$.  The set $T$
is stable under
$\operatorname{Gal}(M/\Q)$ because it is defined by underlying rational
primes.  Complex conjugation therefore sends $T$-units to $T$-units, so
$\bar\lambda\in\mathcal O_{M,T}^{\times}$ and
\begin{equation}
 q^2=|\lambda|^2=\lambda\bar\lambda
 \label{eq:modulus-product}
\end{equation}
is a rational $T$-unit.  For every rational prime
$\ell\notin\SQ$, valuation of \eqref{eq:modulus-product} gives
$2v_\ell(q)=0$.  Thus $v_\ell(q)=0$, proving
\eqref{eq:rational-support}.
\end{proof}

The four lemmas prove Theorem~\ref{thm:support}.  The passage to all places
above $\SQ$ is essential: the original field $K$ and an arbitrary chosen set
of its places need not be stable under complex conjugation.  Just as
importantly, equation~\eqref{eq:modulus-product} does \emph{not} identify
$\bar\lambda$ with the reciprocal eigenvalue.  Reciprocity proves that
$\lambda^{-1}$ is an $S$-unit in Lemma~\ref{lem:monodromy}; conjugation proves
that $\bar\lambda$ is a $T$-unit in Lemma~\ref{lem:conjugation}.  These are
separate facts.

\begin{corollary}[Integral case]
\label{cor:integral}
If $S$ has no finite places, every exact rational periodic multiplier modulus
equals $1$.  If the multiplier itself is rational, it equals $+1$ or $-1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The positive units of $\Z$ are just $1$.  The second statement follows from
$|\lambda|=1$.
\end{proof}

\section{The frozen H\'enon certificate}
\label{sec:frozen}

Let $u$ be the unique real root of
\begin{equation}
 P(U)=U^3-2U^2+2U-2.
 \label{eq:parameter-polynomial}
\end{equation}
The derivative $P'(U)=3U^2-4U+2$ is everywhere positive because its
discriminant is negative, so the real root is unique.  Since $P$ is monic,
$u$ is an algebraic integer.  We freeze
\begin{equation}
 \Hu(X,Y)=(X^2-u-Y,X),\qquad
 \Hu^{-1}(X,Y)=(Y,Y^2-u-X).
 \label{eq:frozen-map}
\end{equation}
The derivative has determinant one, so this is a global polynomial
symplectic automorphism of $\A^2$.

\begin{corollary}[Frozen all-period obstruction]
\label{cor:frozen}
For every multiplier $\lambda$ of every finite periodic orbit of $\Hu$,
\[
 |\lambda|\in\Q_{>0}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |\lambda|=1.
\]
Consequently no positive rational prime is a periodic multiplier modulus,
even when $\lambda$ is nonrational.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Corollary~\ref{cor:integral} over $K=\Q(u)$ to the monic polynomial
$X^2-u$.
\end{proof}

The conclusion is an obstruction to one exact target, not to instability.
An algebraic unit can have an irrational absolute value greater than one; for
example, the integer matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has determinant
one and spectral radius $(3+\sqrt5)/2$.  Corollary~\ref{cor:frozen} also does
not decide whether $+1$ or $-1$ occurs as a multiplier of $\Hu$.

\section{Exact audit and sharp controls}
\label{sec:audit}

The theorem above supplies the all-period result.  The computation described
here has a different role: it checks that the exact periodic-branch,
monodromy, elimination, and modulus-classification software behaves as
specified on the first nontrivial periods.  The parameter, cutoff, controls,
and pass/fail rules were source-locked before candidate execution.  No
external prime table or zero-ordinate data was accessed.

trace.  Table~\ref{tab:ledger} records the compact eliminants.  The symbol

After taking the norm to $\Q$, the rational-root theorem leaves only
$L=\pm1$ as possible rational roots; exact substitution rejects both at each
audited period.  To classify moduli without a floating-point rationality
test, the implementation uses, for each real-hyperbolic selected cycle, the
exact relation
\[
 M^2-(T^2-2)M+1=0,
 \qquad M=\lambda\bar\lambda,
\]
and declares $M$ rational only when its irreducible minimal polynomial over
$\Q$ has degree one.  A separate exact square test is then required before
$q=\sqrt M$ is called rational.  The real-elliptic case is certified directly
from conjugate reciprocal roots, which give $M=1$.  Display approximations
never enter either decision.

The selected real embedding contains five audited cycles: two fixed cycles,
one $2$-cycle, and two $3$-cycles.  One elliptic fixed cycle has exact modulus
$1$; the remaining four are hyperbolic and have irrational algebraic-unit
moduli.  Figure~\ref{fig:finite-audit} reports these counts and the exact
software checks.  The observed finite rational-modulus set is $\{1\}$, in
agreement with Corollary~\ref{cor:frozen}; agreement is a consistency check,
not an inductive proof.

\subsection{Sharp positive and boundary controls}

The support conclusion is sharp at the level of allowed primes.  Set
$a=-15/16$ and $r=5/4$.  The point $(r,r)$ is fixed by
$H_a(X,Y)=(X^2-a-Y,X)$ because $a=r^2-2r$.  At that point,
\begin{equation}
 DH_a(r,r)=\begin{pmatrix}5/2&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 L^2-\frac52L+1=(L-2)(L-1/2).
 \label{eq:sharp-control}
\end{equation}
The coefficient denominator declares bad support $\{2\}$ before multiplier
access, and the generic exact-modulus pipeline recovers $2$ and $1/2$.

Two additional controls delimit the theorem.  For
$J_{a,\delta}(X,Y)=(X^2-a-\delta Y,X)$, the derivative determinant is
$\delta$.  At $(a,\delta)=(0,2)$, the fixed point $(0,0)$ has characteristic
polynomial $L^2+2$ and multipliers $\pm i\sqrt2$, which are not algebraic
units; the unit conclusion is correctly refused unless the determinant's bad
prime is tracked.  Conversely, the cat-map example above is integral and
determinant one but has an irrational algebraic-unit spectral radius larger
than one.  It confirms that the rational-support theorem does not bound all
instability rates.

The official run executed eight controls and preflight checks before opening
the candidate gate, then seven candidate and scope checks.  All $15$ registered
runs passed, as did $39$ unit and protocol tests.  The computation used exact
symbolic arithmetic on a local CPU and no GPU.

\section{Route decision and scope}
\label{sec:scope}

The source-locked decision separated carrier geometry from the proposed
arithmetic target.  Geometry passed: equation~\eqref{eq:frozen-map} is a global
polynomial symplectic automorphism.  The first arithmetic gate, A0, failed:
Corollary~\ref{cor:frozen} excludes every exact rational-prime multiplier
modulus at every period.  Later Route-A gates were therefore stopped by scope,
and no alternative route was opened.  Figure~\ref{fig:route} places this
decision beside the controls.

  frozen map passes the geometric carrier gate but fails the exact
  rational-prime modulus gate by theorem.  The denominator-$2$ control shows

  irrational-unit controls show which nearby conclusions are outside scope.}
  \label{fig:route}

The negative result has precise boundaries.
\begin{itemize}
  \item It applies to finite complex periodic points of the monic generalized
  H\'enon compositions in Theorem~\ref{thm:support}.  Monicity and degree at
  least two are used both at infinity and in the strict maximum argument.
  \item It controls exact rational complex moduli.  Near-rational decimal
  values, irrational algebraic moduli, singular values, and Lyapunov exponents
  are not classified.
  \item It is not a universal symplectic obstruction.  Bad reduction can
  support rational moduli such as $2$ and $1/2$, and other symplectic maps need
  not satisfy the theorem's arithmetic hypotheses.
  \item It does not assert a prime--orbit correspondence, a spectral
  determinant identity, compactness, quantization, or the absence of all
  hyperbolicity.  No target prime/zero dataset was consulted.
\end{itemize}

There is also a controlled extension boundary.  If a generalized H\'enon
factor has determinant an $S$-unit rather than one, a related unit statement
may be recovered by tracking the determinant in the bad set.  If its leading
coefficient is a nonunit, its valuations must likewise be declared bad and
the projective argument must be redone.  Neither extension is claimed here.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The certificate illustrates why a modulus question can be stricter than a
multiplier question.  Integral monodromy gives a reciprocal algebraic-unit
pair, but an exact modulus uses the chosen Archimedean embedding.  Passing to
a conjugation-stable Galois closure connects that Archimedean equality to
non-Archimedean support without assuming the chosen field or place set is
itself stable under conjugation.  This repair is small algebraically but
decisive logically.

The result is intentionally elementary.  It does not compete with the deeper
height, equidistribution, or rigidity theories cited in
Section~\ref{sec:context}.  Its value is as a fail-fast arithmetic certificate:
before searching periodic orbits for a prescribed rational modulus, inspect
the good-reduction data.  When the rational bad set is empty, the search for a
rational-prime modulus is closed at every period.  When the bad set is
nonempty, the theorem supplies a finite support filter rather than an
existence theorem.

The sharp control also suggests the right interpretation of ``sharp.''  The
theorem predicts which rational primes are allowed to appear, not that every
allowed prime is realized for every map or period.  Realization is a separate
dynamical problem.  Likewise, the low-period audit is useful because it tests
branch saturation, exact norm calculations, reciprocal polynomials, and
modulus elimination; none of those finite checks strengthens the quantified
all-period theorem.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For monic area-preserving generalized H\'enon compositions over an
$S$-integer ring, finite periodic coordinates are $S$-integral and return
multipliers are algebraic $S$-units.  A conjugation-stable Galois-closure
argument then forces every exact rational modulus to be supported on the
predeclared rational bad primes.  At the frozen integral parameter
$u^3-2u^2+2u-2=0$, the support is empty and the only possible rational modulus
is one, so the exact rational-prime modulus route is ruled out at all periods.
The exact audit through period three and the $a=-15/16$ control verify the
implementation and the sharpness boundary.  The outcome is a reusable
good-reduction screen, with an explicit boundary between deductive arithmetic
obstruction and finite computational evidence.

period ledger, exact polynomial records, candidate and control audits, a scope

\begin{enumerate}
  \item validate the source lock, forbidden-data scanner, proof structure,
  exact parameter, symplectic identity, and all controls;
  \item open the candidate gate only after every prerequisite passed;
  \item run the exact-period candidate audit through the locked cutoff
  $n=3$;
  \item run scope guards and the full $39$-test protocol suite;
  \item close the artifact set with SHA-256 hashes.
\end{enumerate}
All result-bearing figures are regenerated from those frozen JSON artifacts.
The figure loader checks the official run status, candidate identity, failure
count, and source-lock hash before writing output.  PDF and SVG files are
vector masters; PNG files are review renderings only.

The evidence hierarchy is fixed.  Theorem~\ref{thm:support} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:frozen} come from proof.  The $n\le3$ ledger checks an
implementation against exact identities.  The five selected-embedding cycles
are finite observations.  None of the experimental artifacts is cited as a
proof of an all-period claim.

\section{Exact audit identities}
\label{app:identities}

At a cyclic orbit $(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1})$, the recurrence is
\[
 x_{j+1}+x_{j-1}=x_j^2-u
 \quad(j\bmod n),
\]
and the monodromy is the ordered product
\[
 M_n=\prod_{j=n-1}^{0}
 \begin{pmatrix}2x_j&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The determinant is identically one before any quotient-ring reduction.
Consequently every orbitwise characteristic polynomial has form
$L^2-(\operatorname{tr}M_n)L+1$.  The period-$1$ and period-$3$ eliminants in
Table~\ref{tab:ledger} combine two cycles and hence have degree four; the
period-$2$ eliminant represents its unique cycle and has degree two.

After taking field norms from $\Q(u)$, the multiplier polynomials over $\Q$
have degrees $12,6,12$ for $n=1,2,3$, respectively.  Each is monic,
reciprocal, and has constant term one.  Their exact evaluations at $L=\pm1$
are nonzero, which certifies the absence of rational multiplier roots through
the cutoff.  This rational-root audit is strictly weaker than the
rational-\emph{modulus} theorem: a nonrational eigenvalue can in principle
have rational modulus, which is why the separate $M=\lambda\bar\lambda$
elimination is required.
