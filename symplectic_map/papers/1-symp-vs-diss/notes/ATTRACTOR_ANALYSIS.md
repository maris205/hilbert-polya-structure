# Attractor Analysis

## Target

Explain, as a secondary post-validation analysis, whether the observed return-gap
parity response can be generated by ordinary dissipative attraction in

\[
H_{a,\rho}(x,y)=(1-a x^2-\rho y,x).
\]

The immediate mathematical target is the stability boundary of the positive fixed
point. The numerical target is an ensemble classification into escape, positive or
negative fixed point, period 2 through 32, and unresolved dynamics. This analysis
does not read the confirmatory test split and contains no primes or spectral data.

## Invariant Object

The organizing object is the Floquet characteristic polynomial at the positive fixed
point, followed by the least tail-recurrence period for each diagnostic trajectory.
Parity itself is not redefined or optimized here.

## Assumptions

- \(a>0\) and \(0\leq\rho\leq1\).
- Stability means both fixed-point multipliers lie strictly inside the unit disk.
- A numerical period label means only that a finite tail meets the frozen recurrence
  tolerance. It is not a proof of an attractor or a completeness certificate.
- Basin fractions refer only to the frozen, parent-derived diagnostic ensemble.
- The endpoint \(\rho=1\) is interpreted using area preservation, not by extrapolating
  dissipative basin labels.

## Notation

- \(x_+(a,\rho)\): positive solution of the fixed-point equation.
- \(J_+\): Jacobian evaluated at \((x_+,x_+)\).
- \(b=2a x_+\): positive coefficient in the characteristic polynomial.
- \(\rho_{\mathrm{PD}}\): parameter at which one multiplier is \(-1\).
- `unresolved`: survived the escape box but no period at most 32 passed the finite-tail
  recurrence test.

## Derivation Strategy

Solve the fixed-point equation, form the exact characteristic polynomial, apply the
three strict Jury inequalities, and eliminate \(x_+\) at the flip boundary. Then use
the same exact threshold as an oracle for a deterministic numerical basin diagnostic.

## Derivation Map

1. The fixed-point identity determines \(x_+\).
2. The trace and determinant of \(J_+\) determine its characteristic polynomial.
3. Jury's criterion isolates the only nontrivial dissipative stability boundary.
4. Combining that equality with the fixed-point identity eliminates \(x_+\).
5. Long-burn recurrence classification tests whether ensemble behavior follows this
   ordinary attraction mechanism.

## Main Derivation

### Step 1: fixed point (identity)

At a fixed point, \(x=y=x_*\), so

\[
a x_*^2+(1+\rho)x_*-1=0.
\]

For \(a>0\), the positive solution is

\[
x_+=\frac{-(1+\rho)+\sqrt{(1+\rho)^2+4a}}{2a}
    =\frac{2}{1+\rho+\sqrt{(1+\rho)^2+4a}}.
\]

### Step 2: characteristic polynomial (identity)

The Jacobian and its polynomial at the fixed point are

\[
J_+=\begin{pmatrix}-2a x_+&-\rho\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\chi_+(\lambda)=\lambda^2+2a x_+\lambda+\rho.
\]

Thus the multiplier product is exactly \(\rho\).

### Step 3: Jury conditions (proposition under the stated real-parameter regime)

For \(\lambda^2+b\lambda+\rho\), both roots lie strictly in the unit disk iff

\[
1+b+\rho>0,\qquad 1-b+\rho>0,\qquad 1-\rho>0.
\]

Here \(b=2a x_+>0\). The first condition is automatic, the third restricts the
argument to the dissipative interval, and the flip boundary is

\[
2a x_+=1+\rho.
\]

### Step 4: eliminate the fixed point (identity)

Substituting \(x_+=(1+\rho)/(2a)\) into the fixed-point equation yields

\[
\frac{(1+\rho)^2}{4a}+\frac{(1+\rho)^2}{2a}-1=0,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{\rho_{\mathrm{PD}}=\sqrt{\frac{4a}{3}}-1}.
\]

At the boundary the multipliers are \(-1\) and \(-\rho_{\mathrm{PD}}\). For
\(3/4<a<3\), the fixed point is a sink for
\(\rho_{\mathrm{PD}}<\rho<1\). At \(\rho=1\) strict attraction ends because the
multiplier product is one. For \(a<3\), the same positive fixed point is elliptic at
the endpoint rather than attracting.

For the frozen value \(a=u_c=1.5436890126920763\),

\[
\rho_{\mathrm{PD}}=0.434660941450198\ldots.
\]

The neighbor thresholds are 0.4142135624 at \(a=1.50\), 0.4236104336 at
\(a=1.52\), 0.4422205102 at \(a=1.56\), and 0.4514360705 at \(a=1.58\).

## Empirical Results

The frozen run used 256 trajectories per cell, parent burn-in 4096, diagnostic burn-in
16,384, tail length 2048, escape bound 100, candidate periods 1--32, and absolute and
relative recurrence tolerances \(10^{-9}\). Its complete machine-readable products are
`results/attractors/attractor_diagnostics_v1.json`, `_summary.csv`, and
`_trajectories.csv`.

At \(a=u_c\), the classification was:

|---|---|
| 0, 0.02, 0.05 | 256 unresolved (no period \(\le32\) under the frozen tolerance) |
| 0.10 | 256 period-8 |
| 0.15 | 256 period-4 |
| 0.20 | 256 period-2 |
| 0.25--0.43 | surviving trajectories were period-2; escapes rose from 5 to 53 |
| 0.44 | 202 positive fixed point, 54 escaped |
| 0.45--0.90 | every survivor was the positive fixed point; its sampled basin shrank as escape increased |
| 0.99 | 1 positive fixed point, 255 escaped |

The change from period 2 to the positive fixed point occurs between 0.43 and 0.44,
which brackets the analytic value
\(\rho_{\mathrm{PD}}=0.434660941450198\ldots\). This agreement is a mechanism check,
not a fitted estimate: the analytic threshold was derived before classification.

Thus the principal structure is not specific to \(u_c\). It is the expected
dissipative bifurcation skeleton. At \(u_c,\rho=0.5\), for example, 183 diagnostic
trajectories converged to the positive fixed point and 73 escaped. The primary
validation ensemble at that same parameter had parity polarity \(-0.8775\), exposure
0.7138, and survival 0.7114. This comparison is descriptive because the ensembles and
horizons differ, but its sign is dynamically intelligible: convergence to the positive
fixed point (symbol \(R\)) terminates \(L\)-return production rather than preserving
the parent parity grammar.

At the symplectic endpoint the diagnostic found 256/256 escapes from this
parent-derived ensemble. The primary validation run likewise had zero terminal
survivors, exposure only 0.01145, and conditional pre-escape parity polarity
\(-0.7074\). The endpoint result is therefore carrier unavailability in this ensemble,
not evidence for an attracting periodic alternative and not a reliable conditional
parity-transport success or failure among long-lived bounded trajectories.

## Remarks and Interpretation

- If a positive-measure portion of the parent-derived ensemble converges to a
  period-\(2^k\) sink for \(\rho<1\), its return-gap parity can be produced by the
  attractor itself. That is an ordinary dissipative explanation, not arithmetic
  transport.
- At \(\rho=1\), \(\det DH=1\), and every period-\(n\) monodromy has determinant one.
  A periodic sink (both multipliers inside the unit disk) is therefore impossible.
  In particular, a zero-area periodic orbit cannot have an ordinary positive-area
  attracting basin under this smooth area-preserving map.
- Elliptic islands, chaotic bounded sets, and escaping trajectories remain possible
  at \(\rho=1\). The classifier deliberately reports these as unresolved unless a
  finite recurrence is numerically present; it does not rename them attractors.

## Boundaries and Non-Claims

- This analysis cannot validate the primary endpoint statistic or rescue a failed
  availability gate.
- Period labels do not prove uniqueness, basin geometry, or completeness.
- A finite tail can miss a very long period or slowly converging orbit, especially
  near bifurcation boundaries.
- No result here is evidence for rational primes, zeta functions, or Riemann zeros.
- Neighbor similarities are evidence for generic dynamics, not frozen-parameter
  specificity.

## Open Risks

- Critical slowing near \(\rho_{\mathrm{PD}}\) can move trajectories from a resolved
  class to `unresolved` at fixed burn length.
- Coexisting attractors can make the reported fractions initialization-dependent.
- Escape on the noncompact plane may dominate before an attractor is sampled.
- Recurrence tolerance sensitivity must be checked before any quantitative basin
  boundary claim; the present task uses the classifier only as a mechanism audit.
