# Proof Package

## Claim

Let

$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad R_q=\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z,
\qquad q\ge2,
$$

and define

$$
\Delta_q(v)=\det[v,Av],
\qquad
\mathrm{CV}_q=\{v\in R_q^2:\Delta_q(v)\in R_q^\times\}.
$$

The package proves the following corrected and fully scoped claim.

1. The full matrix centralizer is $R_q[A]$, its invertible part is
   $C_q=R_q[A]^\times$, and $\mathrm{CV}_q$ is a $C_q$-torsor.
2. The $A$-orbit set on $\mathrm{CV}_q$ is $C_q/\langle A\rangle$;
   quotienting it by the residual centralizer gives one class, while the
   induced $A$-map on the one-point quotient is the identity.
3. If $C_q^1=C_q\cap\mathrm{SL}_2(R_q)$, then
   $\mathrm{CV}_q/C_q^1\simeq\operatorname{im}N_q$, where $N_q$ is the
   norm of the quadratic algebra $R_q[T]/(T^2-3T+1)$.  Its cardinality is
   $\varphi(q)$ when $5\nmid q$ and $\varphi(q)/2$ when $5\mid q$.
4. Over prime fields the full nonzero shell has one, three, or two
   full-centralizer strata in the inert, split, or ramified cases,
   respectively; the cyclic locus has the exact cardinalities and discarded
   fractions frozen in the research question.
5. The torsor and one-point quotient hold for composite moduli as well as
   primes.  Thus a formal factor $(1-q^{-s})^{-1}$ assigned to the one class
   uses an external modulus label and is not generated by nontrivial quotient
   dynamics.

## Status

**PROVABLE AS STATED**, with the semantic qualification that “one factor”
means a factor assigned to a set-theoretic quotient class.  It does not mean
that the quotient dynamics intrinsically produces the clock $\log q$.

Terminal classification supported by the proof:

## Assumptions

- $q\ge2$ is an integer fixed before any finite audit.
- All modules, matrices, determinants, and units are over $R_q$ unless a
  prime field is named explicitly.
- The exact-order shell is

  $$
  E_q=\{v\in R_q^2:\operatorname{ord}_{+}(v)=q\}.
  $$

- “Full centralizer” always means the centralizer in
  $\mathrm{GL}_2(R_q)$.
- “Symplectic centralizer” means the intersection with
  $\mathrm{Sp}_2(R_q)=\mathrm{SL}_2(R_q)$.
- A quotient zeta, stack, orbifold, groupoid, equivariant Lefschetz theory,
  or twisted-sector correction is not assumed.

## Notation

- $e_1=(1,0)^t$.
- $f(T)=T^2-3T+1$.
- $S_q=R_q[T]/(f)$.
- $C_q=\operatorname{Cent}_{\mathrm{GL}_2(R_q)}(A)$.
- $C_q^1=C_q\cap\mathrm{SL}_2(R_q)$.
- $H_q=\langle A\rangle\le C_q^1$.
- $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, with
  $JAJ^{-1}=A^{-1}$, and
  $\mathcal R_p(A)=\{G\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p):
  GAG^{-1}=A^{\pm1}\}$.
- $N_q:S_q^\times\to R_q^\times$ is the determinant/algebra norm.
- $J_2(q)=q^2\prod_{p\mid q}(1-p^{-2})=|E_q|$, with the product over
  prime divisors.

## Proof Strategy

Use the explicitly cyclic vector $e_1$ to identify the commutant and the
cyclic-vector locus without a field assumption.  Then identify the determinant
with the quadratic-algebra norm.  Chinese remaindering and local quadratic
algebra structure give the norm image and cyclic-locus cardinality.  Finally,
classify the zeros of the binary quadratic form
$x^2-xy-y^2$ over prime fields and distinguish a set quotient from the
induced quotient dynamics.

## Dependency Map

1. The centralizer theorem depends on the unit determinant of
   $[e_1,Ae_1]$ and Cayley--Hamilton.
2. The torsor theorem depends on the centralizer theorem and the identity
   $[Ue_1,AUe_1]=U[e_1,Ae_1]$.
3. The $A$-orbit quotient depends on the torsor theorem and $A\in C_q$.
4. The symplectic quotient depends on determinant covariance of $\Delta_q$
   and the norm-image lemma.
5. The prime-stratum theorem depends on factoring the discriminant-five
   quadratic form.
6. The composite formulas depend on Chinese remainder decomposition and
   reduction modulo prime divisors.
7. The Route-A conclusion depends on the quotient-dynamics lemma and the
   existence of the same torsor for every composite control.

## Proof

### Step 1. A universal cyclic basis

Directly,

$$
Ae_1=\binom21,
\qquad
P=[e_1,Ae_1]=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det P=1.
$$

Hence $P\in\mathrm{GL}_2(R_q)$ for every $q\ge2$.  Thus
$e_1,Ae_1$ is an $R_q$-basis of $R_q^2$ for every modulus, including
non-fields and non-prime powers.

The characteristic identity is

$$
A^2-3A+I=0.
$$

In the basis $(e_1,Ae_1)$, the matrix of $A$ is therefore

$$
\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&3\end{pmatrix}.
$$

### Step 2. Centralizer over an arbitrary residue ring

Let $X\in\operatorname{Mat}_2(R_q)$ commute with $A$.  There are unique
$a,b\in R_q$ such that

$$
Xe_1=ae_1+bAe_1.
$$

Commutation determines the other basis vector:

$$
X(Ae_1)=A(Xe_1)=aAe_1+bA^2e_1.
$$

Consequently $X$ and $aI+bA$ agree on the basis $e_1,Ae_1$, so

$$
X=aI+bA.
$$

The reverse inclusion is immediate.  Therefore

$$
\operatorname{Cent}_{\operatorname{Mat}_2(R_q)}(A)=R_q[A].
$$

Taking precisely the invertible elements gives

$$
C_q=R_q[A]^\times.
$$

This proof uses no field hypothesis and no quoted cyclic-matrix theorem.

The group $C_q$ is intrinsically a modulo-$q$ object.  Reduction gives a
homomorphism

$$
\operatorname{Cent}_{\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)}(A)\longrightarrow C_q,
$$

but the definition of $C_q$ does not require an element to lie in the image
of this map.  Nor does it require determinant one.  Thus quotienting by all of
$C_q$ explicitly permits $q$-dependent local pseudo-symmetries; this is a
construction cost, not a claim that no local element can lift globally.

### Step 3. The cyclic-vector locus is a torsor

Every $v\in R_q^2$ has a unique expression

$$
v=ae_1+bAe_1=(aI+bA)e_1.
$$

Write $U=aI+bA\in R_q[A]$.  Since $UA=AU$,

$$
[v,Av]=[Ue_1,AUe_1]=U[e_1,Ae_1]=UP.
$$

It follows that

$$
\Delta_q(v)=\det U\det P=\det U.
$$

Over the finite commutative ring $R_q$, a square matrix is invertible if and
only if its determinant is a unit.  Thus

$$
v\in\mathrm{CV}_q
\iff U\in R_q[A]^\times=C_q.
$$

The map

$$
C_q\to\mathrm{CV}_q,
\qquad U\mapsto Ue_1,
$$

is therefore bijective.  Left multiplication makes this a simply transitive
action.  This proves the torsor theorem.

If $v\in\mathrm{CV}_q$, then $v$ is a member of a basis of $R_q^2$ and is
therefore unimodular.  Equivalently, its reduction modulo every prime divisor
of $q$ is nonzero.  Such a vector has exact additive order $q$, hence

$$
\mathrm{CV}_q\subseteq E_q.
$$

### Step 4. $A$-orbits and the one-class quotient

Under the torsor identification $\mathrm{CV}_q\simeq C_q$, the map $A$ acts
by left multiplication by the element $A\in C_q$.  Because $C_q$ is the unit
group of the commutative algebra $R_q[A]$, it is abelian.  Hence the set of
$A$-orbits is

$$
\Gamma_q^{\mathrm{cyc}}
=H_q\backslash C_q=C_q/H_q,
\qquad H_q=\langle A\rangle.
$$

If $A^kv=v$ for one cyclic vector $v=Ue_1$, then

$$
A^kUe_1=Ue_1.
$$

Since $U$ is invertible and $e_1$ is cyclic, $A^k$ fixes both basis vectors
$e_1,Ae_1$, so $A^k=I$.  Every orbit in $\mathrm{CV}_q$ therefore has exact
length $\operatorname{ord}_q(A)$, and

$$
|\Gamma_q^{\mathrm{cyc}}|
=\frac{|C_q|}{\operatorname{ord}_q(A)}.
$$

The residual group $C_q/H_q$ acts simply transitively on this orbit set, so

$$
\Gamma_q^{\mathrm{cyc}}/(C_q/H_q)=\{*\}.
$$

Equivalently, $\mathrm{CV}_q/C_q$ is one point.  Since $A$ belongs to the
group being divided out, its induced map on this quotient is

$$
\bar A(*)=*.
$$

The ordinary Artin--Mazur zeta function of this finite quotient map is the
zeta of one fixed point,

$$
\zeta_{\bar A}(z)=(1-z)^{-1}.
$$

Its primitive period is $1$ for every $q$; no function of $q$ occurs in the
quotient dynamics.  Writing $z=q^{-s}$ gives
$(1-q^{-s})^{-1}$ only after an external substitution.  The one-class set
quotient is exact, but it supplies neither a native $\log q$ return time nor a
reason to restrict $q$ to primes.

### Step 5. Quadratic-algebra norm and the symplectic quotient

The map

$$
S_q=R_q[T]/(T^2-3T+1)\longrightarrow R_q[A],
\qquad T\longmapsto A,
$$

is an isomorphism: it is surjective by definition and both sides are free of
rank two with basis $(1,T)$ and $(I,A)$, respectively.  For
$U=aI+bA$, a direct determinant calculation gives

$$
\det U=a^2+3ab+b^2.
$$

This is precisely the algebra norm of $a+bT$ in $S_q$.  Consequently

$$
C_q^1=\ker(N_q:S_q^\times\to R_q^\times).
$$

For $D\in C_q$ and $v\in\mathrm{CV}_q$,

$$
\Delta_q(Dv)
=\det[Dv,ADv]
=\det D\,\det[v,Av]
=N_q(D)\Delta_q(v).
$$

In particular $C_q^1$ preserves $\Delta_q$.  Conversely, under the torsor
identification $v=Ue_1$, one has

$$
\Delta_q(v)=N_q(U).
$$

Two cyclic vectors $Ue_1$ and $Ve_1$ have the same $\Delta_q$ value if and
only if

$$
N_q(VU^{-1})=1,
$$

which is equivalent to $VU^{-1}\in C_q^1$.  Therefore the fibers of
$\Delta_q$ are exactly the $C_q^1$-orbits and

$$
\mathrm{CV}_q/C_q^1\simeq\operatorname{im}N_q.
$$

Again $A\in C_q^1$ because $\det A=1$, so the induced $A$ action on every
$C_q^1$-orbit class is the identity.  Passing from the full centralizer to the
symplectic centralizer restores multiplicity but does not restore a quotient
clock.

### Step 6. Norm-image size

Write $q=\prod p^{k_p}$.  Chinese remaindering gives compatible products

$$
R_q\simeq\prod_{p^{k_p}\parallel q}R_{p^{k_p}},
\qquad
S_q\simeq\prod_{p^{k_p}\parallel q}S_{p^{k_p}},
$$

and the global norm is the product of the local norms.  It suffices to
determine each local image.

The discriminant of $f$ is $5$.

- If $p\ne5$, the algebra $S_{p^k}$ is quadratic etale over
  $R_{p^k}$.  In the split case it is a product of two copies of
  $R_{p^k}$ and the norm $(u,v)\mapsto uv$ is onto.  In the inert case it
  is the unramified quadratic extension; the residue-field norm is onto and
  the norm on successive principal-unit quotients is the trace map of the
  residue extension, which is onto.  Induction through the unit filtration
  proves surjectivity on $R_{p^k}^\times$.  This includes $p=2$: the
  reduction $T^2+T+1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb F_2$, giving the
  unramified binary extension.
- If $p=5$, the discriminant-five quadratic order is tamely ramified.  The
  residue of a unit norm is a square in $\mathbb F_5^\times$, and every
  square residue occurs.  On principal units, tame ramification makes the
  norm onto $1+5\mathbb Z/5^k\mathbb Z$; equivalently this follows by
  induction from the surjective trace on each relevant principal-unit
  quotient.  Hence the local image consists exactly of units whose reduction
  modulo $5$ is a square, an index-two subgroup of
  $(\mathbb Z/5^k\mathbb Z)^\times$.

The Chinese remainder product yields

$$
|\operatorname{im}N_q|
=
\begin{cases}
\varphi(q),&5\nmid q,\\
\varphi(q)/2,&5\mid q.
\end{cases}
$$

This also proves

$$
|C_q^1|=\frac{|C_q|}{|\operatorname{im}N_q|}.
$$

### Step 7. Prime cyclic and noncyclic strata

For $v=(x,y)^t$,

$$
Av=(2x+y,x+y)^t
$$

and therefore

$$
\Delta_p(x,y)=x(x+y)-y(2x+y)=x^2-xy-y^2.
$$

This binary quadratic form has discriminant $5$.

#### Binary prime

Over $\mathbb F_2$, the form becomes $x^2+xy+y^2$ and is nonzero at all
three nonzero vectors.  Thus

$$
|\mathrm{CV}_2|=3=|E_2|.
$$

The algebra is $\mathbb F_4$, so $C_2\simeq\mathbb F_4^\times$ and is
transitive on the nonzero shell.

#### Odd inert primes

If $(5/p)=-1$, the quadratic form is anisotropic.  It has no nonzero zero,
so

$$
\mathrm{CV}_p=E_p,
\qquad |\mathrm{CV}_p|=p^2-1.
$$

Here $S_p\simeq\mathbb F_{p^2}$ and
$C_p\simeq\mathbb F_{p^2}^\times$ acts simply transitively on the shell.

#### Odd split primes

If $(5/p)=1$ and $p\ne5$, the form factors as two distinct linear forms.
Its nonzero zero set is the disjoint union of the two eigenlines with the
origin removed, of size $2(p-1)$.  Hence

$$
|\mathrm{CV}_p|
=p^2-1-2(p-1)=(p-1)^2.
$$

In an eigenbasis,

$$
C_p=\{\operatorname{diag}(a,b):a,b\in\mathbb F_p^\times\}.
$$

It is transitive separately on each punctured eigenline and on the complement
where both coordinates are nonzero.  Thus the full nonzero shell has exactly
three $C_p$-orbits.  The retained fraction is

$$
\frac{(p-1)^2}{p^2-1}=\frac{p-1}{p+1},
$$

and the discarded fraction is $2/(p+1)$.

The integer matrix

$$
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
$$

satisfies $JAJ^{-1}=A^{-1}$.  Its reduction swaps the two split eigenspaces,
so adjoining it to $C_p$ merges the two noncyclic eigenline orbits.  It
preserves the union of the eigenspaces and its complement, so it cannot merge
the noncyclic stratum with $\mathrm{CV}_p$.  The reversing-symmetry group
$\mathcal R_p(A)=C_p\cup JC_p$ therefore has two, not one, full-shell
orbits.

#### Ramified prime

At $p=5$, the form is the square of one nonzero linear form.  Its zero set is
the unique eigenline, so the nonzero zero set has $p-1=4$ vectors and

$$
|\mathrm{CV}_5|=25-5=20.
$$

In Jordan coordinates the centralizer consists of

$$
\{aI+bN:a\in\mathbb F_5^\times,\ b\in\mathbb F_5,\ N^2=0\}.
$$

It is transitive on the punctured kernel of $N$ and on its complement, giving
two full-shell orbits.  The retained fraction is $5/6$ and the discarded
fraction is $1/6$.  The reversor $J$ preserves the unique Jordan eigenline
because it conjugates $A$ to $A^{-1}$, which has the same generalized
eigenline.  Hence the reversing group cannot merge the two strata.

In the binary and inert cases $C_p$ is already transitive, so the reversing
group remains transitive.  Consequently the full-shell reversing-group orbit
counts at the frozen primes $2,3,5,7,11$ are respectively

$$
1,1,2,1,2.
$$

These three layers have different meanings: $C_p^1$ is symplectic and
commuting, $C_p$ is the full local linear centralizer, and
$\mathcal R_p(A)$ also admits time reversal.  None gives a nontrivial induced
$A$ clock after $A$ itself has been divided out.

These cases also establish the full-shell centralizer orbit counts claimed in
the source lock.

### Step 8. Cyclic-locus size for arbitrary $q$

Membership in $\mathrm{CV}_q$ is equivalent to nonvanishing of $\Delta_q$
modulo every prime divisor of $q$.  For $p^k\parallel q$, every admissible
pair modulo $p$ has $p^{2(k-1)}$ lifts modulo $p^k$.  Therefore

$$
|\mathrm{CV}_q|
=\prod_{p^k\parallel q}p^{2(k-1)}c_p,
$$

where

$$
c_p=
\begin{cases}
p^2-1,&p=2\text{ or }(5/p)=-1,\\
(p-1)^2,&(5/p)=1,\ p\ne5,\\
p(p-1),&p=5.
\end{cases}
$$

The exact-order shell has size

$$
|E_q|=J_2(q)=q^2\prod_{p\mid q}(1-p^{-2}),
$$

where the product is explicitly over prime divisors.  This proves the general
discard formula by subtraction.  It also shows that the one-class
full-centralizer quotient exists at every modulus, prime or composite.

For the four frozen composite controls:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
q&|E_q|&|\mathrm{CV}_q|&|E_q\setminus\mathrm{CV}_q|\\ \hline
4&12&12&0\\
6&24&24&0\\
9&72&72&0\\
10&72&60&12
\end{array}
$$

Chinese remaindering and the known prime orders give

$$
\operatorname{ord}_4(A)=3,
\quad
\operatorname{ord}_6(A)=12,
\quad
\operatorname{ord}_9(A)=12,
\quad
\operatorname{ord}_{10}(A)=30.
$$

Thus the cyclic $A$-orbit counts are $4,2,6,2$, respectively.  The norm-image
formula gives symplectic-centralizer quotient counts $2,2,6,2$.

### Step 9. Formal factor and Route-A conclusion

There are two exact statements:

1. the full-centralizer set quotient of the cyclic locus has one element;
2. the ordinary quotient dynamics is the identity on that element.

Its formal zeta in an abstract variable is $(1-z)^{-1}$.  The substitution
$z=q^{-s}$ is not a Birkhoff sum, return period, monodromy invariant, or
quotient-dynamical datum.  It is a shell label.  Moreover, the same assignment
works for every composite $q$ in exactly the same way.

The construction therefore fails the Route-A A0 entry gate for two separate
reasons:

- the arithmetic clock $\log q$ is attached after quotienting; and
- the mechanism proves the same multiplicity-one statement for all moduli,
  so restriction to primes is external.

The stronger symplectic restriction does not rescue the mechanism: it leaves
$|\operatorname{im}N_q|$ quotient classes and still kills the induced action
of $A$.  A2--A4 and Route B must remain closed.

Therefore the scoped certificate follows. $\square$

## Corrections or Missing Assumptions

- The phrase “centralizer quotient gives one Euler factor” is corrected to
  “the full-centralizer set quotient has one class, whose identity dynamics
  has formal zeta $(1-z)^{-1}$.”  The specialization $z=q^{-s}$ is external.
- The full $\mathrm{GL}_2(R_q)$ centralizer is separated from the symplectic
  $\mathrm{SL}_2(R_q)$ centralizer.  Treating them as interchangeable would
  be false.
- The full-shell theorem is separated from the cyclic-locus theorem.  At
  split and ramified primes, a noncyclic stratum is discarded.

## Open Risks

- The local norm-image lemma uses standard unit-filtration facts for quadratic
  etale and tamely ramified local algebras.  An independent reviewer must
  verify its $p=2$ and $p=5$ boundaries explicitly before source-lock PASS.
- A stacky, groupoid, equivariant, or orbifold quotient may retain stabilizer
  and twisted-sector information lost by the coarse set quotient.  This
  package neither constructs nor rules out such a theory.
- Hecke or quantum centralizer actions naturally use norm-one/symplectic
  groups and may carry representation-theoretic information absent from the
  coarse quotient.  They remain outside scope rather than being declared
  impossible.
- No historical-priority claim is supported.  The centralizer and finite-ring
  symmetry ingredients are classical or directly covered by prior work.
