\section{The original surface and primitive pencil}
\label{geom:section}

The geometry needed below belongs to the original space of initial values,
not to a surface chosen from its spectral equation.  We first construct it
over $R=\mathbb Z[q^{\pm1},\tau^{\pm1}]$.  Write $S/R$ for the following
successive blowups of $\mathbb P^1_R\times_R\mathbb P^1_R$; $x,y$ denote
the coordinates on its original torus.  In each row the centers are taken
in the indicated order.  The last column gives a neighborhood of the
terminal exceptional line in the complement of the polar boundary.
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{c|l|l}
 &\text{centers}&\text{terminal chart}\\ \hline
1&(x^{-1},y-1)=(0,0)&x=u^{-1},\quad y=1+uv\\
2&(x,y^{-1})=(0,0),\ \ xy=\tau
 &x=u(\tau+uv),\quad y=u^{-1}\\
3&(x,y)=(0,0),\ \ E_{3,1}\cap\{y=0\},\ \ x^2/y=\tau
 &x=u(\tau+uv),\quad y=u^2(\tau+uv)\\
4&(x^{-1},y^{-1})=(0,0),\ \ y/x=q
 &x=[u(q+uv)]^{-1},\quad y=u^{-1}
\end{array}
\label{geom:charts}
\end{equation}
Here a center specified by one displayed ratio is the point on the
preceding exceptional divisor where that ratio has the displayed value.
The distribution is $1+2+3+2=8$.  These are the initial-value charts of
\cite{JoshiRoffelsen2026}; the computations below use their actual
coordinates.  In particular, the two occurrences of $\tau$ lie on
different components and do not merge a pair of centers.

Let $E_{i,j}$ denote the final strict transform of the $j$th exceptional
curve in cluster $i$.  Order the boundary components as
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
(D_1,\ldots,D_8)=
(C_{x0},E_{3,1},E_{3,2},C_{y0},C_{x\infty},
 E_{4,1},C_{y\infty},E_{2,1}),\\
D=\sum_{i=1}^8D_i.
\end{gathered}
\label{geom:boundary-order}
\end{equation}
Subscripts on this cycle are read modulo eight.  Put
$\mathcal U=S\setminus D$, $L_j=\mathcal O_S(jD)$, and
$\mathcal N=\mathcal O_D(D)$.  Specialization $q=s,\tau=t$ will be
indicated by subscripts only when needed.

\begin{proposition}\label{geom:surface}
The surface $S/R$ is smooth and projective.  Its divisor $D$ is a
relative simple-normal-crossing cycle of eight rational curves,
$D=-K_{S/R}$, and, on every geometric fiber,
$D_i^2=-2$, $D\cdot D_i=0$, and $D^2=0$.  The divisor $D$ is nef.
The open surface $\mathcal U$ consists of the original torus and the
four complete terminal affine lines in \eqref{geom:charts}.  The form
$\omega_S=dx\wedge dy/(xy)$ extends to a nowhere-degenerate relative
two-form on $\mathcal U$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each blowup center is a section with two relative coordinates $a,b$;
its blowup charts have coordinates $(a,b/a)$ and $(a/b,b)$.  They are
relative affine-plane charts, proving smoothness at each stage, while
blowups preserve projectivity.  The center in cluster 2 is obtained by
writing $y^{-1}=u$, $x=u\xi$ and then $\xi=\tau+uv$.
For cluster 3, write $x=a,y=ab$, then $b=u,a=u\xi$, and finally
$\xi=\tau+uv$.  For cluster 4 write $y^{-1}=u,x^{-1}=u\xi$, then
$\xi=q+uv$.  Cluster 1 uses $x^{-1}=u,y-1=uv$.
This verifies every center and chart in \eqref{geom:charts}.

One may first perform the four node blowups and then the four
non-node blowups.  The latter occur at the unit coordinates
$1,\tau,\tau,q$, so remain away from the nodes after any base change.
At a boundary node a two-form with a simple pole is locally
$a_0\,da\wedge db/(ab)$, with $a_0$ a unit.  Substitution
$a=u,b=uv$ gives $a_0\,du\wedge dv/(uv)$: the new exceptional
curve remains in the polar boundary.  At a smooth boundary point the
same substitution sends $a_0\,da\wedge db/a$ to
$a_0\,du\wedge dv$, so the terminal exceptional curve is not polar.
No zero divisor is introduced.  Consequently
$\operatorname{div}(\omega_S)=-D$, giving $D=-K_{S/R}$.

The node blowups insert the four intermediate components into the
coordinate rectangle, in exactly the cyclic order
\eqref{geom:boundary-order}.  The vectors of orders of $x,y$ along
the intermediate boundary are
\[
(1,0),(1,1),(1,2),(0,1),(-1,0),(-1,-1),(0,-1),(1,-1).
\]
Before the last four blowups its self-intersections in this order are
$-2,-2,-1,-2,-1,-1,-2,-1$.  A node blowup lowers the self-intersection
of each incident curve by one and creates a $(-1)$-curve; these rules
give the displayed list directly from the original rectangle of
self-intersection-zero curves.  The four final blowups lower each
remaining $-1$ to $-2$.  Adjacent components meet transversely once
and other pairs do not meet.  Thus $D\cdot D_i=-2+1+1=0$.
An irreducible curve not in $D$ has nonnegative intersection with
each effective component of $D$, which also proves nefness.

Each terminal exceptional curve meets $D$ once, and its complement
is the whole line $u=0$, $v\in\mathbb A^1$.  Substitution in
$\omega_S$, in the order of \eqref{geom:charts}, gives
\[
-\frac{du\wedge dv}{1+uv},\qquad
\frac{du\wedge dv}{\tau+uv},\qquad
-\frac{du\wedge dv}{\tau+uv},\qquad
-\frac{du\wedge dv}{q+uv}.
\]
Their denominators restrict to $1,\tau,\tau,q$ on the terminal
lines.  These are units; the remaining points of $\mathcal U$ are
on the torus.  This proves regularity and nondegeneracy everywhere.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exact polar divisor}

For the geometric arguments let $k$ be a field, $s,t\in k^\times$,
and suppose $s$ has exact order $r$.  In positive characteristic this
implies that $r$ is prime to the characteristic.  Keep the matrix and
integral of \eqref{main:matrix} and \eqref{main:integral}.  The original
integral construction supplies the Laurent identity, invariance, and
leading coefficient
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
I_{r,s}\in k[x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}],\qquad
I_{r,s}(x,y;t)=I_{r,s}(F_t(x,y);st),\\
F_t(x,y)=\left(\frac{t}{x-y/s},\frac{sx}{y}\right),\qquad
I_{r,s}=-t^rx^{-r}+O(x^{-r+1}).
\end{gathered}
\label{geom:laurent-input}
\end{equation}
The last expansion is an $x$-adic Laurent valuation over $k(y)$, not
an analytic asymptotic statement.  These inputs are the original
matrix-integral identities of \cite{JoshiRoffelsen2026}, Theorem 3.1
and its proof (also in the author's version 2).  They hold over the
cyclotomic integer ring after clearing the displayed substitution
denominators, so specialize to every allowed characteristic.  In
particular the leading coefficient is always nonzero.

\begin{lemma}\label{geom:poles}
On the specified compact surface, $(I_{r,s})_\infty=rD$.
The sections $1,I_{r,s}$ of $L_r$ are independent and have no common
zero.  They define a morphism $f:S_{t,s}\to\mathbb P^1_c$ with
$c=I_{r,s}$ and $f^{-1}(\infty)=rD$ as a scheme fiber.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All divisor calculations may be checked over an algebraic closure.
For completeness, the rational map in \eqref{geom:laurent-input}
preserves the two-form used above.  With $a=x-y/s$ and
$(X,Y)=F_t(x,y)$,
\[
\begin{gathered}
\det\frac{\partial(X,Y)}{\partial(x,y)}=\frac{st}{y^2a},
\qquad XY=\frac{stx}{ay},\\
(x,y)=\left(\frac{tY}{X(Y-1)},\frac{st}{X(Y-1)}\right).
\end{gathered}
\]
Thus $F_t$ is birational and $F_t^*\omega_{S_{st,s}}=\omega_{S_{t,s}}$.
More particularly, it transports the normalized boundary valuations
without ramification.  The following calculation records all eight
transports.  The pair in the last column is a target normal parameter
and a target coordinate on its divisor; its leading terms are in the
source parameter $u$, with residue coordinate $v$.
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
\text{source }(x,y)&\text{target}&\text{target coordinates}\\ \hline
C_{x0}:(u,v)&C_{y0}&(Y,X)=((s/v)u,-st/v+O(u))\\
C_{y0}:(v,u)&C_{y\infty}&(1/Y,X)=(u/(sv),t/v+O(u))\\
C_{y\infty}:(v,u^{-1})&E_{3,1}
 &(X,Y/X)=(-st\,u+O(u^2),-v/t+O(u))\\
E_{3,1}:(u,uv)&C_{x\infty}
 &(1/X,Y)=((s-v)u/(st),s/v)\\
C_{x\infty}:(u^{-1},v)&E_{2,1}
 &(1/Y,XY)=((v/s)u,st/v+O(u))\\
E_{2,1}:(uv,u^{-1})&E_{3,2}
 &(Y/X,X^2/Y)=(-(v/t)u+O(u^3),st^2/v+O(u^2))\\
E_{3,2}:(uv,u^2v)&E_{4,1}
 &(1/Y,Y/X)=(u/s,sv/t+O(u))\\
E_{4,1}:((uv)^{-1},u^{-1})&C_{x0}
 &(X,Y)=(stv\,u/(s-v),s/v).
\end{array}
\]
Every leading normal coefficient is a unit in $k(v)$, and every
residue map is nonconstant.  For each row, therefore,
$v_{D_i,t}(F_t^*g)=v_{D_j,st}(g)$ for every nonzero target rational
function $g$.  The rows form an eight-cycle.  Invariance in
\eqref{geom:laurent-input} transports the pole order $r$ at $C_{x0}$
to every boundary component.  The time becomes $s^jt$ during this
transport, but its leading coefficient $-(s^jt)^r$ remains a unit.

It remains to exclude poles on the four terminal curves.  This
requires actual regular local maps, not only limits on those curves.
For chart 1 set $w=1+uv$, $a=1-uw/s$; the coordinates in target
chart 2, whose time is $st$, are
\[
u'=uw/s,\qquad v'=\frac{st(w^2-sv)}{aw^2}.
\]
For chart 2 set $b=t+uv$, $a=1-su^2b$; the target chart 3 is
\[
u'=-uba/t,\qquad
v'=\frac{st^2(v-2su b^2+s^2u^3b^3)}{b^2a^3}.
\]
Indeed $ba^2-t=u(v-2su b^2+s^2u^3b^3)$ cancels the apparent
denominator $u$ in that expression.  From chart 3 to target chart 4,
\[
u'=u/s,\qquad v'=s\{(s-u)v-t\}/t.
\]
Finally, on chart 4, writing $b=s+uv$ gives the target torus coordinates
$(X,Y)=(-stb/v,s/b)$ near its generic point.  All displayed
denominators are units at the respective generic points.  On the
terminal lines the first three maps have residue coordinates
$st(1-sv)$, $sv$, and $s(sv-t)/t$, respectively, and the fourth has
$(X,Y)=(-s^2t/v,1)$.  Hence every terminal generic point reaches the
torus in at most four regular steps.  The Laurent integral is regular
there, and invariance pulls this regularity back.  The torus, the eight
boundary divisors, and these four terminal divisors exhaust the
possible divisorial poles.  Normality now gives the asserted pole
divisor.

Let $Z_0=(I_{r,s})_0$.  It has no component in $D$, and
$Z_0\sim rD$, so $D\cdot Z_0=0$.  Distinct effective curves on a
smooth surface have positive local intersection at each meeting
point.  Thus $D\cap Z_0$ is empty.  The zero divisors of the sections
$1,I_{r,s}\in H^0(S,L_r)$ are $rD,Z_0$; they have no common point.
The pole proves their independence.  The denominator section is
exactly $1$, so the resulting morphism has the stated coordinate
and the stated scheme fiber at infinity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Integral node frames and genuine constants}

The general relation between a torsion normal bundle and a Halphen
pencil is classical \cite[Proposition 2.2]{CantatDolgachev2012}.
For the obstruction calculation we need the actual frames over $R$,
including their compatibility with nonreduced base change.

\begin{lemma}\label{geom:constants}
For every commutative $R$-algebra $B$, the natural constant map gives
$R\Gamma(S_B,\mathcal O_{S_B})\simeq B[0]$.
In fixed integral node frames the boundary complex is
\begin{equation}
R\Gamma(D_B,\mathcal N_B^j)
\simeq [B\xrightarrow{\,1-q_B^j\,}B],
\qquad j\geq1,
\label{geom:boundary-complex}
\end{equation}
in degrees zero and one.  The frame changes and the degree-one
scaling used in this description are fixed over $R$ before any base
change.  Thus the complexes are compatible with reduction and with
multiplication by a base parameter.  On a field with $q=s$ of exact
order $r$, $\mathcal N$ has exact order $r$ and
$h^0(D,\mathcal N^j)=1$ precisely when $r\mid j$, and is zero otherwise.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First consider the blowup of $\mathbb A^2_B$ in its zero section.
It is the total space of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1_B}(-1)$, whose
affine projection to $\mathbb P^1_B$ pushes its structure sheaf to
$\bigoplus_{d\geq0}\mathcal O(d)$.  The two standard affine charts
compute the higher cohomology of each summand as zero and the
degree-zero cohomology of the total space as $B[a,b]$.  Their finite
\v Cech complex commutes with this direct sum.  Thus a blowup
$b$ satisfies $b_*\mathcal O=\mathcal O$ and
$R^ib_*\mathcal O=0$ for $i>0$.  Coordinate changes and localization
give the same calculation at every center of our surface; off the
center the morphism is an isomorphism.  Eight applications of Leray,
followed by the four standard charts on
$\mathbb P^1_B\times_B\mathbb P^1_B$, give
$R\Gamma(S_B,\mathcal O)=B[0]$.  The map in degree zero is the actual
constant inclusion throughout, not an unspecified free summand.

To determine the node gluing, let $S'$ be the surface after just the
four node blowups, with boundary $D'$.  The last four blowups are at
points $p_i$ of $D'$, and $D\to D'$ is an isomorphism.  If
$\mathcal L=\mathcal O_{D'}(D')$, the divisor pullback gives
\[
\mathcal N\simeq\mathcal L(-p_1-p_2-p_3-p_4).
\]
In the ordering \eqref{geom:boundary-order}, $\mathcal L$ has degree
one on components $3,5,6,8$ and degree zero on the other four.
At the successive nodes use the monomials
\begin{equation}
x^{-1},\ x^{-1},\ x/y,\ x/y,\ x,\ y,\ y,\ x^{-1}.
\label{geom:node-frames}
\end{equation}
Each has valuation $-1$ on both adjacent boundary components.
The adjacent order vectors displayed in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{geom:surface} have determinant one, so their local
characters give relative coordinates $a,b$ at the node.  The
corresponding monomial in \eqref{geom:node-frames} is a unit times
$(ab)^{-1}$, an actual local frame of $\mathcal O_{S'}(D')$.
The points $p_i$ are away from the nodes, so the same node frames
apply after the four twists.

The zero-degree components have equal entering and leaving frames,
giving propagation ratio one.  On the remaining components a section
before the twist is, successively,
\[
a x^{-1}+b x/y,\qquad b x/y+d x,\qquad d x+e y,\qquad
e y+a' x^{-1}.
\]
Vanishing at $x^2/y=\tau$, $y=1$, $y/x=q$, and $xy=\tau$ gives
\[
b/a=-\tau^{-1},\qquad d/b=-1,\qquad e/d=-q^{-1},\qquad a'/e=-\tau.
\]
The last component is traversed from $xy=\infty$ to $xy=0$;
this explains its direction in the last ratio.  These sections
vanish exactly at the prescribed points and become nowhere-zero
frames of the twisted degree-zero bundles.  Their full product is
$q^{-1}$.  Tensoring the same frames $j$ times gives product $q^{-j}$;
no division by $j$ or by $1-q^j$ occurs.

The normalization sequence at a relative node is the exact
difference sequence for $B[a,b]/(ab)$.  Tensoring it by
$\mathcal N^j$ and using the eight trivialized components gives a
complex $[B^8\to B^8]$, with node differences twisted by the above
units.  Eliminate the first seven differences by unit row and column
operations fixed over $R$.  The remaining difference is
$1-q^{-j}$.  Multiplying its degree-one target by the unit $-q^j$
gives \eqref{geom:boundary-complex}.  Because every operation was
integral and invertible, the same description works for arbitrary,
including nonreduced, $B$ and commutes with the indicated base maps.

Over a field a degree-zero section on any component is either zero
or nowhere zero.  Node compatibility propagates a nonzero section
around the entire cycle, and it closes if and only if $s^{-j}=1$.
When it closes it trivializes $\mathcal N^j$, uniquely up to a
constant; otherwise no nonzero section exists.  This proves both
the exact order and the section formula.
In particular the same complexes apply to $B=\mathcal O_1/(\pi_1^2)$
and to $k$, with their original constant $1$, and commute with
$0\to k\xrightarrow{\pi_1}\mathcal O_1/(\pi_1^2)\to k\to0$.
When $p=2$ this first quotient has characteristic four; nothing in
the chart calculation replaces it by a characteristic-two
dual-number ring.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Completeness, connectedness, and generic smoothness}

\begin{theorem}\label{geom:pencil}
For $s$ of exact order $r$ and every $t\ne0$,
\[
h^0(S,L_j)=1\quad(0\leq j<r),\qquad
H^0(S,L_r)=k\langle1,I_{r,s}\rangle.
\]
The morphism $f=I_{r,s}$ is the complete primitive pencil,
$f_*\mathcal O_S=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1}$, and all its geometric
fibers are connected.  Its geometric generic fiber is smooth,
integral, and of genus one, in every allowed characteristic.
All finite fibers are complete curves contained in $\mathcal U$.
Moreover $k(I_{r,s})$ is relatively algebraically closed in $k(S)$,
and $k(S)/k(I_{r,s})$ is separably generated.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Work first over an algebraically closed field.  The constants
calculation gives $H^1(S,\mathcal O)=H^2(S,\mathcal O)=0$.
Surface Riemann--Roch and $K_S=-D$, $D^2=0$ give $\chi(L_j)=1$.
Serre duality gives
$h^2(L_j)=h^0(\mathcal O(-(j+1)D))=0$: an effective representative
of the latter class would have negative intersection with an ample
divisor.  Thus $h^1(L_j)=h^0(L_j)-1$.
The exact sequences
\begin{equation}
0\longrightarrow L_{j-1}\longrightarrow L_j
\longrightarrow\mathcal N^j\longrightarrow0
\label{geom:boundary-sequence}
\end{equation}
and Lemma~\ref{geom:constants} give $h^0(L_j)=1$ for $j<r$.
At $j=r$, $H^1(L_{r-1})=0$ and $h^0(\mathcal N^r)=1$, so
$h^0(L_r)=2$.  This includes $r=1$, when $L_{r-1}=\mathcal O$.
Lemma~\ref{geom:poles} identifies the basis and proves that the
complete system has no base point.

Take the Stein factorization $S\xrightarrow{h}B\xrightarrow{g}\mathbb P^1$
\cite[Tag 03H0]{StacksProject2026}.
The curve $B$ is normal, projective, and integral, hence smooth over
our algebraically closed field, and $h_*\mathcal O_S=\mathcal O_B$.
Leray injects $H^1(B,\mathcal O_B)$ into $H^1(S,\mathcal O_S)=0$,
so $B\simeq\mathbb P^1$.  If $e=\deg g$, including its inseparable
degree, the projection formula yields
\[
2=h^0(S,f^*\mathcal O(1))
 =h^0(B,g^*\mathcal O(1))=e+1.
\]
Thus $e=1$, and $g$ is an isomorphism.  The geometric connectedness
in Stein factorization gives connectedness of every geometric fiber,
not merely of a generic point of a fiber.

We next verify generic smoothness without excluding characteristics
two and three.  A dominant morphism from an integral smooth surface
to a smooth curve is flat: over every base DVR its local rings are
torsion-free modules.  Its fibers are Cartier divisors of pure
dimension one.  The relative Jacobian criterion therefore identifies
the nonsmooth locus with the zero locus of the map from the pulled-back
cotangent line of the base to $\Omega^1_{S/k}$.
Let $z$ be a parameter at infinity.  Since the fiber is exactly
$rD$, at a smooth point of $D$ one has $f^*z=u^ra$, where $a$ is a
unit.  With regular coordinates $u,v$,
\[
d(f^*z)=u^{r-1}\{(ra+ua_u)\,du+ua_v\,dv\}.
\]
The coefficient $ra+ua_u$ is a unit near $u=0$, since $r$ is
invertible.  At a node, $f^*z=u^rv^ra$ and
\[
d(f^*z)=u^{r-1}v^{r-1}
 \{v(ra+ua_u)\,du+u(ra+va_v)\,dv\}.
\]
Both parenthesized factors are units near the node.  These formulas
give an open neighborhood of $D$ in which all nonsmooth points lie
in $D$.  If a component of the closed nonsmooth locus dominated the
base, properness would make it meet $D$.  Its intersection with that
neighborhood would then be a nonempty open subset contained in $D$,
forcing the whole component into $D$, a contradiction.  Hence all
nonsmooth components are vertical.  There are finitely many, and
their proper images are finitely many base points.  Removing these
points gives a nonempty smooth locus on the base.

The smooth geometric fibers there are connected, so their distinct
irreducible components cannot occur: components of a smooth curve
are disjoint.  They are consequently geometrically integral.
For any fiber divisor $G\sim rD$, adjunction computes the integer
\[
p_a(G)=1+\frac{G\cdot(G+K_S)}2=1.
\]
There is no division by the characteristic in this intersection
formula.  The smooth fibers thus have genus one.  These statements
also show that the geometric generic fiber is integral and smooth;
its function field has no new algebraic constants over $k(c)$ and
is separably generated.  In particular $I_{r,s}$ cannot be a rational
function of degree greater than one of another nonconstant rational
function on $S$.

The complete support of the infinite fiber is $D$, so every finite
fiber is disjoint from $D$ and is still projective.  Each terminal
exceptional curve $E$ satisfies $D\cdot E=1$, hence
$\deg(f|_E)=r>0$.  No finite-fiber component can be hidden entirely
in a terminal curve.  Thus the torus equation has the specified
complete fiber as its closure, retaining every terminal point.

For an arbitrary original field, make the same constructions after
extension of constants to an algebraic closure.  The centers,
sections, and parameter $c$ are unchanged.  Smoothness and geometric
integrality are detected by faithful flat field extension, while
proper cohomology commutes with a field extension.  The computed
section dimensions, $f_*\mathcal O=\mathcal O$, and all the geometric
claims therefore hold over the original field as stated.
\end{proof}
