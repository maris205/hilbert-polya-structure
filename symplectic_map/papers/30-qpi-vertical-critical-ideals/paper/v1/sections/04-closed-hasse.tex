\section{Complete smooth fibers and the Hasse polynomial}
\label{hasse:section}

Return to the residue parameters of the cyclotomic setup.  Let $k$
be a perfect field of characteristic $p>0$, let $\eta,t_0\in k^\times$
with $\eta$ of exact order $m$ and $p\nmid m$, and put
\[
J=I_{m,\eta}(x,y;t_0),\qquad T=t_0^m,\qquad
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_m=(-1)^{m+1}.
\]
For a finite closed energy, pass to its finite residue field
extension $l/k$ and write that energy as $h\in l$.
The hypothesis throughout this section is that the \emph{complete
scheme fiber} $X_h=(J=h)$ of the original pencil is smooth over $l$.
Smoothness only of its torus open set or of its reduced support
would not be enough.

\begin{theorem}\label{hasse:closed}
The curve $X_h$ is smooth, projective, geometrically integral, and
of genus one.  It lies completely in the original open surface,
including its intersections with the four terminal lines.
Over an algebraic closure $\bar l$ it is isomorphic, at the same
energy $c=h$, to the complete cubic $W_h$ of
\eqref{spec:weierstrass}.  In particular this cubic and the
complete spectral quotient $E_h$ of \eqref{spec:quotient-charts}
are smooth.  No point or isomorphism is asserted to descend from
$\bar l$ to $l$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{geom:pencil}, the original morphism remains a
surjective complete pencil after extension to $\bar l$, with
geometrically connected fibers and infinite fiber exactly $mD$.
Thus $X_{h,\bar l}$ is nonempty and connected.  It is smooth by
hypothesis, hence reduced.  Two irreducible components of a smooth
curve cannot meet, since a regular local ring is a domain.  Its
finitely many components would consequently be disjoint open and
closed subsets.  Connectedness implies that there is just one,
proving geometric integrality.  The curve is a closed fiber of the
projective surface, is disjoint from $D$, and has divisor class
$mD$.  Adjunction gives
\[
p_a(X_{h,\bar l})
=1+\frac{mD\cdot(mD+K_S)}2=1,
\]
which equals its geometric genus because it is smooth and integral.
This proves the first statements for this selected fiber without
using a classification of other finite fibers.

To compare complete models, set
\begin{equation}
A_0=\bar l[c]_{(c-h)},\qquad A=A_0^h,\qquad
L=\operatorname{Frac}(A),\qquad \varpi=c-h.
\label{hasse:henselian-base}
\end{equation}
Here $A_0^h$ is the ordinary henselization.  It is a DVR with the
same residue field $\bar l$ and the same uniformizer $\varpi$.
This operation is in the geometric residue problem at the fixed
energy; it is not a ramified lift of an arithmetic state.  Let
\[
\mathcal X=S_{\bar l}\times_{\mathbb P^1_c}\operatorname{Spec}A,
\qquad
\mathcal W=W_{\bar l}\times_{\mathbb A^1_c}\operatorname{Spec}A.
\]
The first is proper and of finite presentation.  It is flat
because a nonzero base parameter is a non-zero-divisor in the local
rings of the integral surface, and torsion-free modules over a DVR
are flat.  Localizing the smooth surface and then making this
ind-\'{e}tale henselian base change preserves regularity.  Its
generic fiber is smooth, projective, and geometrically integral of
genus one by Theorem~\ref{geom:pencil}, and its special fiber is the
geometrically integral reduced curve just proved.  For $\mathcal W$,
projectivity, flatness, regularity, geometrically integral reduced
special fiber, and geometrically smooth genus-one generic fiber
are exactly Lemma~\ref{spec:finite-critical}, preserved by this
localization and henselization.  Its point $O_W$ gives a section.
In particular, regularity here is a property of these specified
base changes, not of arbitrary base change of a regular scheme.

Choose $q_0\in X_{h,\bar l}(\bar l)$.  The morphism $\mathcal X/A$
is smooth in a neighborhood of $q_0$, by flatness, finite
presentation, and smoothness of that fiber at $q_0$.  An \'{e}tale
coordinate to $\mathbb A^1_A$ exists there; translate its residue
value to zero.  Pulling back the zero section gives an \'{e}tale
$A$-scheme with the specified residue point.  Henselian lifting
then supplies a section $e_A$ through $q_0$
\cite[Tag 07LW]{StacksProject2026}.  The section is only local over
$A$; this procedure makes no assertion that the original curve over
$l(c)$ has a point.

Theorem~\ref{spec:jacobian} gives the actual $E_L$-torsor action on
$X_L$, and \eqref{spec:birational-change} gives the specified
origin-preserving isomorphism $\iota_L:E_L\simeq W_L$.
The map $N\mapsto e_L+N$ trivializes this local torsor.  Composing
its inverse with $\iota_L$ gives the unique equivariant generic
isomorphism $\phi_L:X_L\to W_L$ satisfying
$\phi_L(e_L)=O_W$.

Both models are relatively minimal regular proper models.  Indeed
in either one the special fiber $F$ is the only vertical integral
component, has multiplicity one, and is the principal Cartier
divisor $\operatorname{div}(\varpi)$.  Its normal line bundle is
therefore trivial, so $F^2=\deg_F\mathcal O_F(F)=0$.
A curve of the first kind that could be contracted over the DVR
would have to be a vertical component with negative normal degree.
The unique available component has degree zero, excluding such a
curve.  This is minimality of a regular proper model in the sense of
\cite[Definition 55.8.4, Tag 0C2V]{StacksProject2026}, not merely
minimality of a Weierstrass equation.

The positive-genus uniqueness and mapping theorem for minimal
regular models applies: the identified generic curve is smooth,
projective, geometrically integral, has $H^0(\mathcal O)=L$, and
has genus one; both models are regular, proper, flat, and minimal.
It extends the specified $\phi_L$ to an $A$-morphism $\phi_A$.
Apply the same theorem to $\phi_L^{-1}$ to obtain its extension in
the other direction
\cite[Lemmas 55.10.1--55.10.2, Tags 0C6B and 0C9Z]{StacksProject2026}.
The two composites are the identity on the generic fiber.  Flatness
makes that fiber scheme-theoretically dense, and the targets are
separated; hence both composites are the identity everywhere.
This proves the full model isomorphism
\begin{equation}
\phi_A:\mathcal X\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal W,
\qquad \phi_A(e_A)=O_W.
\label{hasse:complete-model-isomorphism}
\end{equation}
It is $A$-linear, so in particular it fixes $c$ and induces the
claimed complete isomorphism at $c=h$.  All terminal-fiber points
are retained.  Different choices of $e_A$ need not give the same
isomorphism.

The special cubic is now known to be smooth.  Use the two original
spectral charts with $c=h$.  On $Z\ne0,\infty$,
\eqref{spec:birational-change} identifies them with a smooth open
of $W_h$.  At $Z=0$ and infinity the four derivatives are again
$-T,T,-1,1$, so these endpoints are also smooth.
The finite pushdown in \eqref{spec:finite-pushdown} with degree two
shows that this complete $E_h$ is projective and geometrically
connected of genus one; thus it is geometrically integral.
The open isomorphism uniquely extends between the smooth
projective curves to $E_{h,\bar l}\simeq W_{h,\bar l}$.
Its geometric smoothness is detected over $\bar l/l$, completing
the assertion about $E_h$ as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{hasse:cartier}
On the complete smooth spectral curve $E_h$ the specified form
\begin{equation}
\omega=\frac{dZ}{2\lambda-(T+hZ+Z^2)}
\label{hasse:differential}
\end{equation}
is a nonzero regular differential.  With the $p^{-1}$-linear
Cartier convention its value is
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\mathcal C(\omega)=H_p(T,h;\varepsilon)^{1/p}\omega,\\
H_p(T,h;\varepsilon)=
\begin{cases}
[Z^{p-1}]\bigl((T+hZ+Z^2)^2-4\varepsilon Z^3\bigr)^{(p-1)/2},
 &p\ne2,\\
h,&p=2.
\end{cases}
\end{gathered}
\label{hasse:polynomial}
\end{equation}
The polynomial $H_p$ is monic of degree $p-1$ in $h$.
For the original complete smooth curve $X_h$ one has
\[
\operatorname{Jac}(X_h)\text{ is geometrically supersingular}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H_p(T,h;\varepsilon)=0\text{ in }l.
\]
No simplicity of a root of $H_p$ is assumed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Phi=\lambda^2-(T+hZ+Z^2)\lambda+\varepsilon Z^3$.
At a finite point where $\Phi_\lambda\ne0$, the expression
$dZ/\Phi_\lambda$ is a regular local generator of the cotangent
line.  Where that derivative vanishes, smoothness makes
$\Phi_Z$ a unit and the equation $d\Phi=0$ gives the equivalent
expression $-d\lambda/\Phi_Z$.  These expressions agree on their
overlap and include every finite branch point.
On the infinity chart $V=Z^{-1}$, $\mu=\lambda Z^{-2}$ the form is
\[
-\frac{dV}{2\mu-(1+hV+TV^2)}.
\]
At both endpoints $V=0$ the denominator is a unit.  This verifies
regularity on the entire complete curve, and any one of these local
expressions proves that it is nonzero.  Since the curve has genus
one, a nonzero regular differential has an effective zero divisor
of degree $2g-2=0$, so is nowhere zero.

We use the convention
$\mathcal C(g^p\nu)=g\mathcal C(\nu)$ and
\[
\mathcal C\left(\sum_j a_jZ^j\,dZ\right)
=\sum_{i\geq0}a_{pi+p-1}^{1/p}Z^i\,dZ.
\]
These conventions distinguish the Hasse coefficient from its
$p$th root in the Cartier--Manin matrix; the distinction and
semilinear convention are discussed in the corrected version of
\cite[Section 2.5]{AchterHowe2019}.
The projection variable $Z$ is separating: its quadratic equation
has nonzero derivative in $\lambda$ as a rational function, also
in characteristic two.  Thus the displayed Cartier rules apply
in the curve's function field.

For odd $p$ set $Y=2\lambda-(T+hZ+Z^2)$.  Then
\[
Y^2=(T+hZ+Z^2)^2-4\varepsilon Z^3,
\qquad\omega=Y^{-p}(Y^{p-1}dZ).
\]
The polynomial $Y^{p-1}$ in $Z$ has degree $2p-2$.
Among its exponents the only one congruent to $p-1$ modulo $p$
is $p-1$.  The Cartier rules therefore give
\[
\mathcal C(\omega)
=Y^{-1}\mathcal C(Y^{p-1}dZ)
=H_p(T,h;\varepsilon)^{1/p}\frac{dZ}{Y}.
\]
For $p=2$, put $S_0(Z)=T+hZ+Z^2$.  Formula
\eqref{hasse:differential} is $dZ/S_0(Z)$, so
\[
\mathcal C(\omega)
=S_0(Z)^{-1}\mathcal C(S_0(Z)dZ)
=h^{1/2}\frac{dZ}{S_0(Z)}.
\]
The constant and quadratic terms contribute nothing and the linear
term contributes $h^{1/2}dZ$.  These are identities of regular
differentials on the complete curve, by the regularity just proved.
For odd $p$, the term of degree $p-1$ in $h$ in the coefficient
formula can only come from
$(h^2Z^2)^{(p-1)/2}$, and has coefficient one.  Terms of smaller
$h$-degree cannot change it.  For $p=2$ monicity follows from
$H_2=h$.

Pull the specified differential back along the two complete
geometric isomorphisms in Theorem~\ref{hasse:closed}.
Cartier commutes with pullback by curve isomorphisms, so the pulled
back form on $X_{h,\bar l}$ has the same displayed coefficient.
The Cartier operator on a genus-one curve is zero precisely when
its geometric Jacobian is supersingular.  This criterion also
applies when $X_h(l)$ has no chosen point, by taking the Jacobian
after geometric base extension.  A different isomorphism or
differential basis multiplies the form by a nonzero scalar $a$;
$\mathcal C(a\omega)=a^{1/p}\mathcal C(\omega)$ shows that this
cannot change whether the operator is zero.  Finally
$H_p(T,h;\varepsilon)\in l$ and $l\hookrightarrow\bar l$ is
injective.  Its geometric zero condition is exactly its zero
condition in $l$.  Only that condition is being returned to the
original field, not a chosen geometric point, a canonical
isomorphism, or an arbitrarily normalized differential.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{hasse:two-directions}
The smooth-fiber hypothesis also supplies the two differential
directions used later.  Along $X_h$, the map $J$ is smooth, so
\[
0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{X_h}\,dJ
\longrightarrow\Omega^1_{\mathcal U_l/l}|_{X_h}
\longrightarrow\Omega^1_{X_h/l}\longrightarrow0
\]
is exact with locally free terms.  A nonzero regular tangent
differential is nowhere zero because $X_h$ has genus one.
Consequently a lift of such a differential and the normal
direction $dJ$ give a local basis at every point of the complete
fiber, including the four terminal charts.  This conclusion
does not concern only the torus and makes no assertion about a
singular fiber.
\end{remark}
