\section{The complete first-layer critical ideal}
\label{first:section}

Fix $a=1$ and write $\mathcal O=\mathcal O_1$, $\pi=\pi_1$,
$s=\widetilde\eta\zeta_p$, $t=t_1$, $I=I_{mp,s}$, and
$\alpha=p^{-1}dI$. The residue field $k$ is perfect. We first compute
the obstruction to lifting the original small-order integral $J$.
An integer trace congruence then identifies that obstruction with the
first tangent component of $\alpha$ on a supersingular fiber.
The connecting homomorphisms are the usual ones; the issue is their
value on this particular section and this particular trace.

\subsection{The actual boundary Bockstein}

Write $S,D$ for the eight-center model and its boundary over $\mathcal O$.
Let $A_2=\mathcal O/(\pi^2)$, $L_n=\mathcal O_S(nD)$, and
$\mathcal N=\mathcal O_D(D)$. Subscripts $A_2$ and $k$ denote the
actual base changes of these sheaves and of the eight-center surface.
The coefficient sequence $0\to k\xrightarrow{\pi}A_2\to k\to0$
defines a Bockstein
\[
 \beta_L:H^0(S_k,L_{m,k})\longrightarrow H^1(S_k,L_{m,k}).
\]
For $p=2$, $\pi=-2$ and $A_2$ has characteristic four; it is not
replaced by a characteristic-two dual-number ring.

\begin{proposition}[The specified obstruction class]
\label{first:obstruction}
The kernel of $\beta_L$ is the actual constant line $k\langle1\rangle$.
For every complete smooth finite fiber $X=(J=h)$, the original section
$1$ trivializes $L_m|_X$ and induces an isomorphism
\[
 \rho_X:H^1(S_k,L_{m,k})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^1(X,\mathcal O_X).
\]
If $j_i$ are local regular lifts of $J$, then
\begin{equation}
 0\ne\kappa_J:=\rho_X\beta_L(J)
 =\left[\overline{(j_j-j_i)/\pi}\big|_X\right].
 \label{first:kappa}
\end{equation}
The assertion holds after the allowed perfect residue-field extensions
needed to specify $h$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The actual blowup-chart calculation of Lemma~\ref{geom:constants} gives
$H^0(S_A,\mathcal O)=A\langle1\rangle$ and $H^{>0}(S_A,\mathcal O)=0$
for $A=A_2,k$, with the same constant section and maps of coefficients.
The node frames in \eqref{geom:boundary-complex}, fixed first over
$\mathcal O$, compute the boundary cohomology by
\begin{equation}
 K_{j,A}=[A\xrightarrow{1-s_A^j}A]
 \quad\hbox{in degrees zero and one}.
 \label{first:boundary-complex}
\end{equation}
In particular, the elimination of seven invertible node pairs and the
degree-one scaling by $-s^j$ are the same before both base changes.
They commute with reduction, multiplication by $\pi$, and connecting
homomorphisms.

For $1\le j<m$, $1-\eta^j\ne0$, so the arrow in
\eqref{first:boundary-complex} is a unit over $A_2$ and $k$.
The exact sequences
\[
 0\longrightarrow L_{j-1,A}\longrightarrow L_{j,A}
 \longrightarrow\mathcal N_A^j\longrightarrow0
\]
therefore give, by induction,
$H^0(S_A,L_{j,A})=A\langle1\rangle$ and $H^{>0}(S_A,L_{j,A})=0$
for $0\le j<m$. If $m=1$, the induction interval is empty and the
assertion is precisely the constants calculation.

At $j=m$ over $k$, the boundary differential vanishes. Its two
cohomology groups are $k$, and the same exact sequence gives
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 0\longrightarrow k\langle1\rangle\longrightarrow H^0(S_k,L_{m,k})
 \xrightarrow{r_0}H^0(D_k,\mathcal N_k^m)\longrightarrow0,\\
 r_1:H^1(S_k,L_{m,k})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^1(D_k,\mathcal N_k^m).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Naturality for restriction to the boundary gives
$r_1\beta_L=\beta_Dr_0$. In the fixed boundary coordinates,
\[
 1-s^m=1-(1+\pi)^m\equiv-m\pi\pmod{\pi^2}.
\]
Lifting the degree-zero element $1$, applying the differential, dividing
by $\pi$, and reducing shows that $\beta_D$ is multiplication by
$-\bar m$. It is invertible since $p\nmid m$. Thus
$\ker\beta_L=\ker r_0=k\langle1\rangle$. The original Laurent leading
term makes $J$ nonconstant, so $\beta_L(J)\ne0$. This computation does
not identify $r_0(J)$ with an arbitrarily normalized boundary basis:
if its coordinate is $b_J$, the image is exactly $-\bar m b_J$.

The complete finite fiber is disjoint from $D_k$. Its actual defining
section yields
\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{S_k}
 \xrightarrow{J-h\cdot1}L_{m,k}
 \longrightarrow L_{m,k}|_X\longrightarrow0.
\]
The section $1$ has divisor $mD_k$, so its restriction to $X$ is
nowhere zero and supplies the stated trivialization. The vanishing of
$H^1(S_k,\mathcal O)$ and $H^2(S_k,\mathcal O)$ proves that the actual
restriction $\rho_X$ is an isomorphism. Represent the local lifted
sections using this same trivialization. With the Cech convention
$c_j-c_i$, the Bockstein is represented by
$\overline{(j_j-j_i)/\pi}$; restriction gives \eqref{first:kappa}.
Changing lifts adds a coboundary. Hence the class is the specified
restriction of the specified Bockstein and is nonzero on each $X$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The original prime trace congruence}

The next calculation keeps both the ramification and the nonresonant
terms of the original $m$-block. Set
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 B_s(z)=A(s^{m-1}z;t)\cdots A(z;t),\qquad r_0=s^m,\\
 T_0=t^m,\quad C=s^{m(m-1)},\quad
 \delta=s^{3m(m-1)/2},\quad j_t=[z^m]\operatorname{tr}B_s(z),\\
 \lambda=1\ (p\ne2),\qquad \lambda=-1\ (p=2).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Here $r_0$ is a primitive $p$th root, not the length $mp$.
Define the integer trace polynomials
\[
 L_0(S,D)=2,\quad L_1(S,D)=S,\quad
 L_n=SL_{n-1}-DL_{n-2},
\]
and the polynomial with state-constant coefficients
\begin{equation}
 F(v)=\lambda[Z^p]L_p(T_0+vZ+CZ^2,\delta Z^3).
 \label{first:F}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Prime trace congruence]
\label{first:prime-congruence}
On the original torus,
\begin{equation}
 I-F(j_t)\in p\pi\mathcal O[x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}],\qquad
 F(v)=\lambda v^p+pQ(v),\quad Q\in\mathcal O[v].
 \label{first:congruence}
\end{equation}
Moreover, $F'(v)/p\in\mathcal O[v]$ and
\begin{equation}
 \overline{F'(v)/p}=H_p(T,v;\varepsilon_m),\qquad T=\bar t^m.
 \label{first:F-derivative}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The constant matrix $A_0$ has trace $t$ and determinant zero, hence
$A_0^m=t^{m-1}A_0$. The highest matrix coefficient is
$C\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$, and multiplication of determinants gives
$\det B_s=\delta z^{3m}$. Thus the constant and top trace coefficients
are $T_0,C$. After reduction, cyclic rotation gives
$\operatorname{tr}B_\eta(\eta z)=\operatorname{tr}B_\eta(z)$.
Since $\eta$ has exact order $m$, every nonresonant trace coefficient
is divisible by $\pi$. Writing
\[
 S_*(Z)=T_0+j_tZ+CZ^2,\qquad
 E_s(z)=\operatorname{tr}B_s(z)-S_*(z^m),
\]
we have $E_s\in\pi R[z]$ and $[z^{im}]E_s=0$, where
$R=\mathcal O[x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1}]$.

Write $B_s(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{2m}B_i z^i$. For an exponent word
$\boldsymbol i=(i_0,\ldots,i_{p-1})$ of total degree $mp$, put
\[
 w(\boldsymbol i)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}\ell i_\ell,\qquad
 \tau(\boldsymbol i)=\operatorname{tr}(B_{i_{p-1}}\cdots B_{i_0}).
\]
The contribution to the original descending block product is
$r_0^{w(\boldsymbol i)}\tau(\boldsymbol i)$. Under cyclic shift,
$\tau$ is unchanged and the weight changes by $p(m-i_{p-1})$.
Thus each cyclic orbit has constant phase. Its size is one or $p$.
The unique size-one word has all indices $m$, with phase
$r_0^{mp(p-1)/2}=\lambda$: the phase is one for odd $p$, and minus
one for $p=2$, when $m$ is odd. Its contribution cancels against
$\lambda[z^{mp}]\operatorname{tr}B_s(z)^p$. Every size-$p$ orbit
contributes
\[
 p\bigl(r_0^{w(\boldsymbol i)}-\lambda\bigr)\tau(\boldsymbol i)
 \in p\pi R.
\]
We have proved
$I-\lambda[z^{mp}]\operatorname{tr}B_s^p\in p\pi R$ without
commuting matrix factors.

Cayley--Hamilton gives $\operatorname{tr}B_s^p=L_p(\operatorname{tr}B_s,\det B_s)$.
In symmetric variables $L_p(u+v,uv)=u^p+v^p$, so
$L_p(S,D)=S^p+pV_p(S,D)$ for an integer polynomial $V_p$.
Replacing $S_*+E_s$ by $S_*$ in the $pV_p$ part changes it by a
multiple of $p\pi$. For the $p$th-power part, expand in the scalar
trace coefficients. The only pure $p$th power contributing to degree
$mp$ has index $m$ and is $j_t^p$ on both sides. Every other
multinomial coefficient is divisible by $p$. An uncancelled such term
contains a nonresonant coefficient, hence a further factor $\pi$.
This proves that the resonant coefficient changes by a multiple of
$p\pi$, even when several nonresonant factors return to resonant
degree. The first assertion of \eqref{first:congruence} follows.

The identity $L_p=S^p+pV_p$ also implies
$F(v)\equiv\lambda v^p\pmod p$, proving the second assertion.
For the derivative, use the companion matrix
$M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}S&-D\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Cyclic insertion in its trace gives
\[
 \partial_S L_p=p(M^{p-1})_{11}=pU_{p-1}(S,D).
\]
Consequently
$F'(v)/p=\lambda[Z^{p-1}]U_{p-1}(T_0+vZ+CZ^2,\delta Z^3)$.
Here $\bar C=1$, $\bar\delta=\varepsilon_m$, and $\bar\lambda=1$.
For the middle equality, if $m$ is odd the exponent of $\eta$ in
$\delta$ is a multiple of $m$; if $m$ is even then $p$ is odd and
$\eta^{m/2}=-1$. Applying Lemma~\ref{trace:residue} at $a=1$
now gives \eqref{first:F-derivative}, including $p=2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Ramified integer Taylor quotient]
\label{first:taylor}
The quotient
\[
 \mathcal T_F(v,f)=\frac{F(v+\pi f)-F(v)}{p\pi}
\]
is a polynomial in $\mathcal O[v,f]$, and
\begin{equation}
 \overline{\mathcal T_F(v,f)}
 =H_p(T,\bar v;\varepsilon_m)\bar f-\bar f^p.
 \label{first:taylor-residue}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The cyclotomic equation $\Phi_p(1+\pi)=0$ yields
\[
 \rho:=\frac{\pi^{p-1}}p
 =-1-\sum_{j=2}^{p-1}\frac{\binom pj}{p}\pi^{j-1}
 \in\mathcal O^\times,
 \qquad \bar\rho=-1.
\]
Therefore $\overline{\lambda\rho}=-1$ for all $p$.
At $p=2$, the integer value is $\lambda\rho=1$, whose residue is
indeed $-1$; this is not the characteristic-zero equality $1=-1$.
Using $F=\lambda v^p+pQ$, the quotient is
\[
 \lambda v^{p-1}f+
 \lambda\sum_{j=2}^{p-1}\frac{\binom pj}{p}\pi^{j-1}v^{p-j}f^j
 +\lambda\rho f^p+\frac{Q(v+\pi f)-Q(v)}\pi.
\]
Every term is integral. Reduction kills the middle sum and gives
\eqref{first:taylor-residue} by \eqref{first:F-derivative}.
The sum is empty for $p=2$. The quotient is formed as a polynomial
before any specialization; on a ring with torsion its value means this
polynomial value, not a newly attempted division by a zero divisor.
\end{proof}

For each local regular lift $j_i$ of $J$, Lemmas
\ref{first:prime-congruence} and \ref{first:taylor} give
$I-F(j_i)\in p\pi\mathcal O$ on its intersection with the torus.
The functions $I$ and $F(j_i)$ are regular on the full chart by
Proposition~\ref{trace:regular}. Apply Lemma~\ref{trace:chart-injection}
over $\mathcal O/(p\pi)$ to their difference. It follows that
\begin{equation}
 G_i:=\frac{I-F(j_i)}{p\pi}
 \quad\hbox{is regular on each full local chart.}
 \label{first:Gi}
\end{equation}
This proves the congruence at every terminal point. It does not assert
that the torus function $j_t$ has a global regular lift.

\subsection{The image-sheaf class and the two coefficients}

\begin{proposition}[The actual first tangent class]
\label{first:tangent}
Suppose $X=(J=h)$ is a complete smooth finite fiber with
$H_p(T,h;\varepsilon_m)=0$. The local forms
$d(\bar G_i|_X)$ glue to a regular differential $\nu$ on $X$, and
\begin{equation}
 \partial\nu=\operatorname{Fr}_*\kappa_J\ne0
 \quad\hbox{in }H^1(X,\mathcal O_X^p).
 \label{first:image-class}
\end{equation}
Here $\partial$ belongs to
$0\to\mathcal O_X^p\to\mathcal O_X\xrightarrow{d}B_X^1\to0$,
where $B_X^1=\operatorname{im}d$, and $\operatorname{Fr}$ maps the
additive sheaf $\mathcal O_X$ to its image sheaf $\mathcal O_X^p$.
The differential $\nu$ is nowhere zero on the complete curve.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Set $\widehat H(v)=F'(v)/p$. Differentiating \eqref{first:Gi} before
reduction gives the exact local identity
\begin{equation}
 \alpha=\widehat H(j_i)dj_i+\pi dG_i.
 \label{first:local-form}
\end{equation}
On an overlap write $j_j=j_i+\pi\widetilde f_{ij}$ and
$f_{ij}=\bar{\widetilde f}_{ij}|_X$. The sign convention fixed in
Proposition~\ref{first:obstruction} and the Taylor formula give
\[
 \bar G_j-\bar G_i
 =-H_p(T,J;\varepsilon_m)\bar{\widetilde f}_{ij}
   +\bar{\widetilde f}_{ij}^{p}.
\]
Restriction to the Hasse-zero fiber therefore gives the exact equality
$\bar G_j|_X-\bar G_i|_X=f_{ij}^p$. Its differential vanishes, proving
that the local exact forms glue. Equation~\eqref{first:local-form}
identifies their common value with the tangent class of the original
form after its normal term is subtracted and division by $\pi$ is
performed. Replacing $j_i$ by another lift changes the restricted
primitive by a $p$th power, so does not change $\nu$.

We justify the kernel sheaf used in the connecting sequence. The
function field $K$ of a smooth curve over a perfect field has a
separating transcendence element $z$. If $[K:k(z)]=n$, then
$[K^p:k(z^p)]=n$ and $[K:k(z^p)]=pn$, so $[K:K^p]=p$.
Since $dz\ne0$, the elements $1,z,\ldots,z^{p-1}$ form a basis over
$K^p$; differentiation proves $\ker(d:K\to\Omega^1_{K/k})=K^p$.
If a regular function $g=b^p$ has zero differential, then at each
local discrete valuation ring $pv(b)=v(g)\ge0$. Normality makes
$b$ regular on the same open set. Thus the sheaf kernel is exactly
$\mathcal O_X^p$. Frobenius is injective on the reduced structure
sheaf and surjective onto this image sheaf, hence is an additive
sheaf isomorphism.

The local primitives $\bar G_i|_X$ compute the connecting class as
\[
 \partial\nu=[(f_{ij}^p)]
 =\operatorname{Fr}_*[(f_{ij})]=\operatorname{Fr}_*\kappa_J.
\]
It is nonzero by Proposition~\ref{first:obstruction}. This target is
essential: the subsequent map to $H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)$ kills the
connecting image by exactness. We have not asserted that the
supersingular Frobenius operator on $H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)$ is invertible.
Finally, $X$ is a complete smooth geometrically integral genus-one
curve by Theorem~\ref{hasse:closed}. A nonzero regular differential
has an effective zero divisor of degree $2g-2=0$. Its zero divisor
is empty, proving the last assertion at every point of $X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete first-layer coefficient ideal]
\label{first:ideal}
At every point $P$ of every original complete smooth finite fiber,
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak c(\alpha_{mp})_P=(\pi,\widetilde H)_P,
 \label{first:full-ideal}
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde H$ is any local lift of $H_p(T,J;\varepsilon_m)$.
If $h$ is a root of multiplicity $e_h$, the completed ideal, after
an allowed unramified residue-field extension, is $(\pi,z^{e_h})$
with $\bar z=J-h$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On an ordinary smooth fiber, \eqref{trace:global-residue} at $a=1$
is $\bar\alpha=H\,dJ$, with nonzero $H$ and nonvanishing $dJ$.
The coefficient ideal is the unit ideal, which is
$(\pi,\widetilde H)$ there.

On a supersingular fiber, choose a local lift $j_i$ and extend $dj_i$
to a basis $(dj_i,\theta)$ of the relative cotangent module at $P$.
This is possible because $dJ$ is a nonzero normal differential.
Write \eqref{first:local-form} as
\[
 \alpha=(\widehat H(j_i)+\pi A)dj_i+\pi B\theta.
\]
Proposition~\ref{first:tangent} says that $B$ is a unit at $P$.
Consequently its two coefficients generate
$(\widehat H(j_i)+\pi A,\pi B)=(\pi,\widehat H(j_i))$.
All lifts of the same residue function differ by a multiple of $\pi$,
so this is \eqref{first:full-ideal} with any $\widetilde H$.
The proof includes the terminal points because both \eqref{first:Gi}
and the nonvanishing of $\nu$ hold on the complete fiber.

At a root of multiplicity $e_h$, write
$H_p(T,J;\varepsilon_m)=(J-h)^{e_h}V(J)$ with $V(h)\ne0$.
Formal smoothness supplies a parameter $z$ lifting $J-h$, together
with a parameter along the curve, after the stated unramified
extension and completion. The lift of $V$ is a unit, and adjoining
$\pi$ to the ideal removes the choice of lift. The completed ideal
is therefore exactly $(\pi,z^{e_h})$. Ideal equalities obtained
after these faithfully flat changes descend to the original local
rings; their quantifiers are not enlarged to extra ramified states.
\end{proof}

For an unramified lifted supersingular state, $z$ maps into $(\pi)$,
so the image of $(\pi,z^{e_h})$ is exactly $(\pi)$. The common order
of the two coefficients of $\alpha_{mp}$ is one; on an ordinary state
it is zero. Multiplication by $p$, whose uniformizer order is $p-1$,
gives respectively $p$ and $p-1$ for $dI_{mp}$. In particular, this
is an equality of the complete original ideals, not merely a statement
after adjoining $\pi^2$.
