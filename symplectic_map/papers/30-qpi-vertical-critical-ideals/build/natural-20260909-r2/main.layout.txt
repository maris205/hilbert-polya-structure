      Vertical critical ideals and cyclotomic first jets of q-Painlevé I
                                                Anonymous


                                                  Abstract
          We determine the complete first-layer critical ideal and the higher cyclotomic first jets of the
      divided state differential of the original q-Painlevé I invariant. Reduction of this differential is
      a Hasse power times the small-order invariant differential, but that residue does not determine
      its tangent component on a supersingular level. We identify this component through the actual
      Bockstein for the relevant anticanonical power, an integer prime-trace congruence, and the
      connecting class of locally exact differentials on the complete fiber. For every prime and every
      tame block size, near every complete smooth finite fiber, the full first-layer coefficient ideal is
      generated by the cyclotomic parameter and the Hasse polynomial. At higher odd-prime levels we
      obtain a complete first-jet factorization and its exact truncated ideal, retaining multiple Hasse
      roots and arbitrary unit-time jets. In characteristic two the comparison starts at height two: a
      mixed cubic relation gives a truncated transverse quotient of length five with a specified action of
      the base parameter. We prove the actual spectral Jacobian and closed-fiber Hasse identifications
      needed to interpret these statements on the original eight-center surface, including its four
      terminal affine lines. Higher full thickness and singular-level critical ideals are not determined.


1     Introduction and main results
An invariant can become a pure power after reduction even though its state differential, divided in-
tegrally before reduction, retains nonzero information. For the root-of-unity q-Painlevé I invariant,
that divided differential reduces to a Hasse power times the small-order invariant differential. This
residue describes ordinary levels but vanishes on supersingular levels. We determine the missing
vertical information: the complete coefficient ideal at the first cyclotomic layer and the full first jet
at every higher layer.
    The first-layer result is controlled by a specific obstruction. The original small-order invariant
is a section of an anticanonical power, and this section does not lift across the first infinitesimal
cyclotomic thickening. An integer congruence for the original matrix trace turns its Bockstein
into the connecting class of a locally exact differential on each complete supersingular fiber. This
differential is nowhere zero. It supplies the second state direction that is absent from the residue
formula and determines the full local ideal. Higher layers retain a common factor at odd primes,
whereas characteristic two has a different height-two base and a mixed cubic relation.

1.3   Relation to existing mechanisms and scope
The initial-value surface and its eight-point geometry originate in the q-Painlevé literature [10, 11].
Anticanonical-cycle periods and the relation between a normal-bundle torsion order and a Halphen
pencil are established tools [4, 8]. We need their values on the original integer charts, and must
separately exclude quasi-elliptic behavior in the allowed small characteristics. Spectral line-bundle
correspondences and cyclic Picard constructions are likewise classical [2, 9]. The proof here supplies
the actual state inverse, excludes a hidden inseparable degree or pullback kernel, and descends the
torsor action over the original field. Minimal-model uniqueness [15, Tags 0C6B and 0C9Z] is applied
only after both models over the same local energy ring have been shown minimal.
    Hasse powers and semilinear iteration are not new mechanisms. Cartier conventions require care
even for one-dimensional spaces [1]. Vlasenko’s higher Hasse–Witt congruences already include the
residue iteration, and the formal-group integrality statement in the cited author version is not
restricted to ordinary fibers [16, Theorems 1(i) and 2, author version v3]. Our comparison uses
that specified version; no theorem from it is a proof premise below. Dwork-crystal and higher-
Hasse constructions give finer congruences under their respective invertibility and Frobenius-lift
hypotheses [3, 17]. We prove the needed coefficients and all block deformations directly, including
the supersingular locus and characteristic two.
    The local division of Frobenius differences and the resulting obstruction classes have a broad
prior theory [5–7]. Recent Frobenius–Witt formulations also treat arithmetic Kodaira–Spencer
classes [14]. These theories do not by themselves identify the specific Lm section with the original
trace congruence used here. Nor is ramification alone a distinction from that literature. Root-
of-unity first nonzero ramified terms occur in other specified systems, for example in quantum
K-theoretic spectral relations [12]; a spectral relation is not an identity of the present two-direction
critical ideals. Lubin–Tate already give a characteristic-two height-two elliptic lifting family [13,
Section 3.5]. Accordingly, neither height two, the number five, nor a normalizable unit parameter

                                                    4
is counted as a separate general mechanism here. The calculation concerns the mixed ideal and the
original base-parameter action in this model.
    All topic-specific proofs needed for the three theorems are given in the body. Sections 2–4
establish the original geometric and Hasse identifications; Sections 5–6 prove the integral trace
and complete first-layer ideal; Sections 7 and 8 give the two higher branches. The paper does not
determine higher full thickness, singular-level critical ideals, or exact orders after extra ramified
state extensions. It makes no general crystalline or formal-group comparison and no claim about
Riemann zeros.

Corollary 8.6 (Orders along the allowed states). Consider a state over the stated cyclotomic
DVR, after at most an allowed finite unramified extension, whose residue is a point of a complete
smooth finite fiber. Let ordπa of a one-form along that state mean the minimum order of its two
coefficients in a local relative cotangent frame. The exact values and lower bounds are those stated
in Table 1. On a supersingular fiber the divided order is exactly one at height one for every prime,
and also at height two for p = 2. For odd p at height at least two, and for p = 2 at height at least
three, it is at least two. The corresponding original orders are exactly p, exactly five, or at least
aφ(pa ) + 2, respectively. On an ordinary smooth fiber the orders are exactly zero for the divided
form and aφ(pa ) for the original differential.

Proof. At height one the full ideal of Theorem 6.5, including its multiple-root form, has image (π1 )
along a supersingular lifted state: the lifted transverse parameter lies in (π1 ). Thus the divided
order is exactly one. For odd p and a ≥ 2, the ideal (7.19) has image contained in (πa2 ), which
proves only the stated lower bound.
   At p = 2, a = 2, evaluation of a lift j of J lies in (π2 ). The term j 3 + π2 Te has order exactly
one, whereas π2 j 2 has order at least three. The image of the original ideal after adding (π22 ) is
therefore (π2 ). The original ideal itself must have order one, since an ideal of order at least two
cannot acquire an order-one element by adding (π22 ). For a ≥ 3, N ≥ 8 and both nonconstant
generators in (8.18) evaluate to order at least two. The truncated information consequently gives
order at least two, not equality.
   On an ordinary fiber the residue multiplier is nonzero and dJ is a coordinate differential, so one
coefficient is a unit. Finally, vπa (p) = φ(pa ) and dImpa = pa αmpa . Adding aφ(pa ) to each divided
order gives the original orders, in particular p at height one and five at (p, a) = (2, 2).

    These pointwise lower bounds do not say that a higher-level form is globally divisible by πa2
on the surface: the residue form is nonzero on the ordinary locus. The full first-layer ideal and
the higher-level ideals modulo πa2 answer different thickness questions. The present results do not
determine full higher critical ideals, exact higher supersingular orders beyond the cases stated,
singular-energy ideals, or orders after additional ramified state extensions. All identities concern
the original eight-blowup model and its complete terminal charts.
