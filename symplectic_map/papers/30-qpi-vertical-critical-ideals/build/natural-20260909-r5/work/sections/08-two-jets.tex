\section{Characteristic-two jets and mixed critical ideals}
\label{two:section}

At the prime two, the first higher-level comparison starts at four
blocks. Its base term contains a mixed contribution that the odd-prime
formula cannot recover by substitution. We first calculate this term
on the original torus, then identify its tangent component through the
characteristic-four first layer. A local divisibility argument carries
that identification to every point of the complete smooth fiber.

\subsection{The four-block base and all higher block counts}

Assume that $m$ is odd, $a\ge2$, and $N=2^a$. The block calculations
in this subsection allow the perfect residue field $k$ of the main
coefficient-ring setup; the geometric ideal conclusions below retain
the finite-residue-field hypothesis. Set
\[
 B=\mathcal O_a/(\pi_a^2),\qquad \epsilon=\pi_a\bmod\pi_a^2,
 \qquad s=\eta(1+\epsilon),\qquad t=t_a\bmod\pi_a^2.
\]
Use the original blocks
\[
 \mathsf B_s(z)=A(s^{m-1}z;t)\cdots A(z;t),\qquad
 \mathsf B_0(z)=A(\eta^{m-1}z;\bar t)\cdots A(z;\bar t),
\]
and put
\[
 j_*=[z^m]\operatorname{tr}\mathsf B_s(z),\qquad
 J=I_{m,\eta}(x,y;\bar t),\qquad
 T=\bar t^m,\qquad S(Z)=T+JZ+Z^2.
\]
The function $j_*$ is the actual block coefficient on the torus,
with residue $J$; no global regular lift is asserted for it.
Using the projection \eqref{odd:projection-definition}, define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \Gamma_m(z)&=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(
 d\mathsf B_0[\mathsf B_0,z\partial_z\mathsf B_0]\bigr),\qquad
 \gamma_m=\mathcal P_m\Gamma_m,\\
 \beta_N&=[Z^N]\gamma_m(Z)S(Z)^{N-3},\\
 \chi_m&=J\,dJ+[z^{2m}]\operatorname{tr}
                  (d\mathsf B_0\,z\partial_z\mathsf B_0).
 \end{split}
 \label{two:ambient-data}
\end{equation}
In particular, $\chi_m$ is an ambient torus form defined by the
original matrix, not by specifying its restriction to a fiber.

\begin{theorem}[Characteristic-two first jets]
\label{two:factorisation}
Identify $\mathcal O_2/(\pi_2^2)$ and $\mathcal O_a/(\pi_a^2)$
with $B=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ by sending their parameters to
$\epsilon$, and choose $t_2$ with the same entire image as $t_a$.
On the common original torus,
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_{mN}^{[2]}=j_*^{N-1}dj_*+\epsilon\beta_N,
 \qquad \beta_N=J^{N-4}\beta_4,
 \qquad \beta_4=T\,dJ+J^2\chi_m.
 \label{two:torus-formula}
\end{equation}
Thus the four-block base is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_{4m}^{[2]}
 =(j_*^3+\epsilon T)dj_*+\epsilon J^2\chi_m.
 \label{two:base}
\end{equation}
On the entire common original open surface, including its four
complete terminal lines,
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_{mN}^{[2]}
 =J^{\langle N-4\rangle}\alpha_{4m}^{[2]}.
 \label{two:global-factorisation}
\end{equation}
The multiplier is the common $(N-4)$th power of local lifts of
$J$, and is one when $N=4$. These form identities require no
smoothness or ordinarity assumption on an energy fiber.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Step 1: the correct common quotient.}
The polynomial
\[
 \Phi_{2^a}(1+X)=(1+X)^{2^{a-1}}+1
\]
is Eisenstein at two: its constant term is two, all other
nonleading coefficients are divisible by two, and the constant
term is not divisible by four. Hence $\pi_a$ is a uniformizer,
and $v_{\pi_a}(2)=2^{a-1}\ge2$. The quotient $B$ therefore has
characteristic two. The image of $\mathcal O_0$ factors through
$\mathcal O_0/(2)=k$ and gives its coefficient field, and each
element of $B$ has a unique form $u+v\epsilon$. This proves the
stated dual-number description, also at $a=2$.

The time lift $t_2$ exists by surjectivity of the quotient map.
The identical parameters $1,t,t,s$ in the original eight blowup
charts give the common model, just as in
\eqref{odd:common-ring}. This is not the natural embedding
$i\mapsto\zeta_{2^a}^{2^{a-2}}$: when $a>2$ it sends $i-1$
to zero in $B$. Nor can this quotient be identified with that
of height one, where $\pi_1=-2$ and
$\mathcal O_1/(\pi_1^2)$ has characteristic four.

\emph{Step 2: integral insertion and the internal deformation.}
Lemma~\ref{odd:block-insertion} applies before reduction for
$N=2^a$ and yields the same integral block expression
\eqref{odd:insertion-equation}. Its normalization divides by
$N$, not by $mN$; all $m$ differentiated factors are inside
$d\mathsf B_s$. The full expansion
\eqref{odd:block-variation} remains valid over the present $B$.
In particular, every internal spectral perturbation and the
actual time jet are retained.

Specializing Lemma~\ref{odd:block-trace} gives
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 S_s=\operatorname{tr}\mathsf B_s
     =S_*(z^m)+\epsilon V(z),\qquad \mathcal P_mV=0,\\
 S_*(Z)=t^m+j_*Z+Z^2,\qquad
 D_s=s^{3m(m-1)/2}z^{3m},\qquad dD_s=0.
 \end{gathered}
 \label{two:internal-trace}
\end{equation}
The highest trace coefficient is exactly one in $B$, because
$s^{m(m-1)}=1$ and $m(m-1)$ is even. The determinant coefficient
may still have a nonzero first-order term; it has not been
deleted, but its state differential is zero. When $m=1$, the
sum of internal spectral perturbations is empty, while the time
dependence in $A(z;t)$ remains.

\emph{Step 3: the characteristic-two internal term.}
The rank-two recurrence \eqref{trace:rank-two} and the universal
identity $U_{N-1}(S,D)=S^{N-1}$ in characteristic two give
\[
 F_N:=[z^{mN}]\operatorname{tr}(d\mathsf B_s\mathsf B_s^{N-1})
     =[z^{mN}]S_s^{N-1}dS_s.
\]
This identity is polynomial, so it remains true with square-zero
coefficients. Lemma~\ref{odd:projection}, applied with
$\Phi(S,D)=S^{N-1}$, eliminates precisely the two nonresonant
error terms
\[
 \epsilon(N-1)S_*^{N-2}VdS_*,\qquad
 \epsilon S_*^{N-1}dV,
\]
and no resonant deformation. Thus
\[
 F_N=[Z^{N-1}]S_*(Z)^{N-1}dj_*.
\]
Now $S_*^{N-1}=\prod_{i=0}^{a-1}S_*^{2^i}$ and the allowed
index in each original quadratic factor is $0,1,2$. To contribute
to $2^a-1$, the lowest index must be odd, hence equal to one.
After subtraction and division by two, every remaining index
is again forced to one. Consequently
\begin{equation}
 F_N=j_*^{N-1}dj_*.
 \label{two:internal-term}
\end{equation}
The coefficient $dj_*$ includes the variation of the state
differential caused by any nonzero first-order part of $t$.

\emph{Step 4: the characteristic-two weighted commutator.}
For a two-by-two matrix $M$ in characteristic two, write
$S_M=\operatorname{tr}M$ and $D_M=\det M$. Cayley--Hamilton
gives $M^2=S_MM+D_M\,\mathrm{id}$, and hence
\[
 [M,[M,X]]=M^2X+XM^2=S_M[M,X].
\]
Combining this relation with the characteristic-independent
operator identity \eqref{odd:weighted-operator}, for $N\ge4$,
gives the polynomial equality
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j=1}^{N-1}jM^{N-1-j}XM^{j-1}
 =\operatorname{ad}_M^{N-2}(X)=S_M^{N-3}[M,X].
 \label{two:weighted-commutator}
\end{equation}
It does not divide by $S_M$ and remains valid when $S_M=0$.

Because $m$ is odd, $r=s^m=1+\epsilon$, so each external block
has expansion
\[
 \mathsf B_s(r^jz)=\mathsf B_s(z)
                  +\epsilon j\,z\partial_z\mathsf B_0(z).
\]
Expand the descending product in
\eqref{odd:insertion-equation}. In a term already multiplied by
$\epsilon$, the other factors and the differential may be reduced.
Equation~\eqref{two:weighted-commutator} therefore yields
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_{mN}^{[2]}=F_N+\epsilon[Z^N]\gamma_m S^{N-3}
                 =F_N+\epsilon\beta_N.
 \label{two:external-term}
\end{equation}
The external speed is the residue of $m$, namely one. This is
distinct from the integral normalization in Step 2.

\emph{Step 5: actual support and binary selection.}
Lemma~\ref{odd:support} applies to $\Gamma_m$, so $\gamma_m$
is supported in degrees one through five, with upper bound
four when $m=1$. The product $\gamma_m S$ is consequently
supported in $[1,7]$. In characteristic two,
\[
 \gamma_m S^{N-3}
 = (\gamma_m S)\prod_{i=2}^{a-1}S^{2^i}.
\]
To contribute to degree $N=2^a$, the first index must be
divisible by four. The only such index in $[1,7]$ is four.
Subtracting four and dividing by four leaves the target
$2^{a-2}-1$. Each remaining index lies in $\{0,1,2\}$, and
the same binary argument forces every one to equal one.
Its scalar coefficient is
$J^{4+8+\cdots+2^{a-1}}=J^{N-4}$. Thus
\begin{equation}
 \beta_N=J^{N-4}[Z^4](\gamma_m S)=J^{N-4}\beta_4.
 \label{two:binary-factorisation}
\end{equation}
For $a=2$ the subsequent product is empty, so this includes
the base case. No Frobenius power is taken of a one-form.

\emph{Step 6: the actual four-block coefficient.}
Put $M=\mathsf B_0(z)$, $R_z=z\partial_zM$,
$\mathcal S=\operatorname{tr}M=S(z^m)$, and
$\mathcal D=\det M=z^{3m}$. The determinant sign is one
because $m$ is odd. Differentiating
$M^2+\mathcal SM+\mathcal D\,\mathrm{id}=0$ in a state
direction, using $d\mathcal D=0$, gives
\[
 M\,dM+dM\,M=(d\mathcal S)M+\mathcal S\,dM.
\]
Multiplication by $R_z$ and cyclic trace, with just one
differential factor in every term, give
\[
 \Gamma_m=d\mathcal S\operatorname{tr}(MR_z)
                +\mathcal S\operatorname{tr}(dM\,R_z).
\]
The adjugate identity $\operatorname{adj}(M)=\mathcal S\,\mathrm{id}+M$
also implies
$z\partial_z\mathcal D=\mathcal S(z\partial_z\mathcal S)
 +\operatorname{tr}(MR_z)$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \Gamma_m&=(z\partial_z\mathcal D)d\mathcal S+\mathcal S K_m,\\
 K_m&=d\mathcal S(z\partial_z\mathcal S)
                         +\operatorname{tr}(dM\,R_z).
 \end{split}
 \label{two:gamma-identity}
\end{equation}
This calculation uses the actual state-constant determinant;
it is not an identity without extra terms for arbitrary
state-dependent determinants.

Since $m$ and $3m$ are odd,
\[
 d\mathcal S=z^m dJ,\qquad z\partial_z\mathcal S=Jz^m,
 \qquad z\partial_z\mathcal D=z^{3m}.
\]
It follows that
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_m=z^{4m}dJ+S(z^m)K_m,\qquad
 K_m=J\,dJ\,z^{2m}
        +\operatorname{tr}(d\mathsf B_0\,z\partial_z\mathsf B_0).
 \label{two:K-polynomial}
\end{equation}
Here $\deg d\mathsf B_0\le2m-1$. In characteristic two the
derivative of its leading term $z^{2m}E$ also vanishes, so
$\deg(z\partial_z\mathsf B_0)\le2m-1$. The trace term in
\eqref{two:K-polynomial} has degree at most $4m-2$ and zero
constant coefficient. Since $2m\le4m-2$ for all $m\ge1$,
the same assertions hold for $K_m$. Thus
$\kappa_m=\mathcal P_mK_m$ is supported in degrees one
through three, with upper bound two when $m=1$, and
$[Z^2]\kappa_m=\chi_m$. Projection of
\eqref{two:K-polynomial} yields
\[
 \gamma_m=Z^4dJ+S\kappa_m.
\]
Because $S^2=T^2+J^2Z^2+Z^4$ and
$[Z^0]\kappa_m=[Z^4]\kappa_m=0$, we obtain
\[
 \beta_4=[Z^4](\gamma_m S)
         =T\,dJ+J^2[Z^2]\kappa_m
         =T\,dJ+J^2\chi_m.
\]
Together with \eqref{two:internal-term},
\eqref{two:external-term}, and $\epsilon dJ=\epsilon dj_*$,
this proves \eqref{two:torus-formula} and \eqref{two:base}.

\emph{Step 7: gluing the multiplier across the terminal lines.}
If $N>4$, then $N-4$ is even. For any two local regular
lifts $j,j+\epsilon f$ of $J$,
\[
 (j+\epsilon f)^2=j^2,
 \qquad (j+\epsilon f)^{N-4}=j^{N-4}.
\]
The powers therefore glue to the regular function
$J^{\langle N-4\rangle}$. For $N=4$ use the function one.
On the common torus, \eqref{two:binary-factorisation} and
\eqref{two:internal-term} give
\[
 \alpha_{mN}^{[2]}
 =j_*^{N-4}\bigl(j_*^3dj_*+\epsilon\beta_4\bigr).
\]
The form in parentheses is the four-block form for the same
$s,t$ over $B$. Both sides of \eqref{two:global-factorisation}
are regular by Proposition~\ref{trace:regular}, and restriction
is injective on each of the four nonreduced terminal charts by
Lemma~\ref{trace:chart-injection}. The torus identity thus
extends across every terminal line. This proof does not assume
that $\chi_m$, $\beta_4$, or $j_*$ separately extends to the
whole surface.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Identification with the actual first-layer tangent form}

For the geometric conclusions, take the finite residue field
$k$ and the permitted finite unramified extensions in the main
setup. Assume first that the complete scheme fiber
$X=(J=0)$ is smooth. By Theorem~\ref{hasse:closed} and
Proposition~\ref{hasse:cartier}, it is a geometrically integral genus-one
curve and is precisely a smooth supersingular fiber, since
$H_2(T,h)=h$. Let $\nu$ be the actual nowhere-vanishing
first-layer form of Proposition~\ref{first:tangent}.

\begin{lemma}[Characteristic-four comparison]
\label{two:tangent}
On the torus intersection with $X$, the form defined in
\eqref{two:ambient-data} satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \chi_m|_{\Omega_X^1}=\nu.
 \label{two:tangent-equation}
\end{equation}
In particular, its tangent restriction has the specified regular
nonzero extension to the complete $X$. This does not assert
ambient regularity of $\chi_m$ on the entire residue surface.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use a separate first-layer coefficient ring with
$\pi_1=-2$, $s_1=-\widetilde\eta$, and a unit time $t_1$
whose residue is $\bar t_a$. Only this residue is being
matched; no characteristic-four quotient is identified with
the higher dual-number quotient. Write
\[
 B_s(z)=A(s_1^{m-1}z;t_1)\cdots A(z;t_1),\quad
 S_s=\operatorname{tr}B_s,\quad j_*=[z^m]S_s,
\]
using $j_*$ here for this first-layer block coefficient, and put
$\delta_1=s_1^{3m(m-1)/2}$.
As $s_1^m=-1$, integral block insertion gives
\[
 \alpha_{2m}=[z^{2m}]\operatorname{tr}(dB_s(z)B_s(-z)).
\]
In the characteristic-four ring $\mathcal O_1/(\pi_1^2)$,
the monomial expansion of $-1=1+\pi_1$ gives
\[
 B_s(-z)=B_s(z)+\pi_1z\partial_zB_s(z).
\]
The integer identity
$\operatorname{tr}(dM\,M)=(\operatorname{tr}M)d(\operatorname{tr}M)
 -d(\det M)$ now computes the internal contribution; it
does not require division by two. The determinant of $B_s$
is state-constant. Its highest trace coefficient is exactly
one, since $s_1^{m(m-1)}=1$. Its nonresonant trace coefficients
are divisible by $\pi_1$, by cyclic invariance after reduction.
In the coefficient $[z^{2m}]S_s dS_s$, a term with exactly
one nonresonant factor is still nonresonant, and a term with
two such factors has $\pi_1^2$. Thus this coefficient is
$j_*dj_*$ modulo $\pi_1^2$.

Put
\[
 K_0=[z^{2m}]\operatorname{tr}
               (d\mathsf B_0\,z\partial_z\mathsf B_0).
\]
Reducing only the external correction, already multiplied by
$\pi_1$, gives
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_{2m}\equiv j_*dj_*+\pi_1K_0\pmod{\pi_1^2}.
 \label{two:char-four-expansion}
\end{equation}
On the other hand, the original prime trace polynomial
\eqref{first:F}, with Lemma~\ref{first:prime-congruence},
has, at $p=2$ and odd $m$,
\[
 F(h)=-[Z^2]\bigl((t_1^m+hZ+Z^2)^2-2\delta_1Z^3\bigr)
     =-h^2-2t_1^m.
\]
Consequently the actual torus function
$G=(I_{2m}-F(j_*))/(2\pi_1)$ is regular and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_{2m}=-j_*dj_*+\pi_1dG.
 \label{two:char-four-prime}
\end{equation}
Compare \eqref{two:char-four-expansion} and
\eqref{two:char-four-prime} before reducing two to zero.
The intermediate term is
$2j_*dj_*=-\pi_1j_*dj_*$. The identification
$\pi_1\Omega/\pi_1^2\Omega\simeq\Omega\otimes k$
therefore gives
\[
 d\bar G=K_0-JdJ=K_0+JdJ=\chi_m.
\]
Only the last equality uses characteristic two. By the actual
first-layer construction in Proposition~\ref{first:tangent},
$d(\bar G|_X)=\nu$; changing the local lift adds a function
whose restriction differs by a square and hence does not
change this differential. This proves
\eqref{two:tangent-equation} with the original tangent class,
not an unspecified scalar multiple of it.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Local difference quotients and the two divisibility steps}

Return to height two and its characteristic-two quotient
$B=\mathcal O_2/(\pi_2^2)$. On local opens covering $X$, choose
regular lifts $j_i$ of $J$. The residue formula
\eqref{trace:global-residue} is
$\bar\alpha_{4m}=J^3dJ$. Since the relative cotangent module
is locally free over the smooth $B$-surface, multiplication
by $\epsilon$ identifies its residue module with its
$\epsilon$-multiple. It therefore defines a unique regular
residue one-form
\begin{equation}
 \beta_i=\frac{\alpha_{4m}^{[2]}-j_i^3dj_i}{\epsilon}\bmod\epsilon.
 \label{two:local-quotient}
\end{equation}
This denotes a unique residue class, not a unique numerical
division inside the dual-number ring.

If $j_j=j_i+\epsilon f$, the full two-direction expansion is
\[
 (j_i+\epsilon f)^3d(j_i+\epsilon f)
 =j_i^3dj_i+\epsilon(j_i^2f\,dj_i+j_i^3df),
\]
so
\begin{equation}
 \beta_j-\beta_i=-J^2\bar f\,dJ-J^3d\bar f.
 \label{two:lift-change}
\end{equation}
On the intersection of such an open with the torus, use
$j_*$ from \eqref{two:base}. Equations~\eqref{two:base}
and \eqref{two:lift-change} show that $\beta_i-TdJ$ is
divisible by $J^2$, and that its quotient differs from
$\chi_m$ by a form of type $\bar f\,dJ+Jd\bar f$.

\begin{lemma}[Divisibility at every complete-fiber point]
\label{two:division}
For every $P\in X$, including every point on the four terminal lines,
the form
\begin{equation}
 \rho_i=(\beta_i-TdJ)/J^2
 \label{two:rho}
\end{equation}
is regular near $P$, and its tangent restriction to $X$ equals
$\nu$. Its tangent coefficient is therefore a unit at $P$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The smooth geometrically integral scheme fiber $X=(J=0)$
is a reduced prime Cartier divisor in the smooth residue
surface. Thus $R_P=\mathcal O_{\mathcal U_k,P}$ is a domain,
$(J)$ is prime, and $dJ$ is a nonzero conormal parameter at
$P$. The generic point of $X$ lies on the original torus.
Indeed, otherwise the integral complete genus-one curve
would lie in one of the four terminal affine lines, which
cannot contain such a complete curve.

The torus calculation consequently proves $J^2$ divisibility
at the generic point of this prime divisor. In a local free
frame let $b\in R_P$ be any coefficient of $\beta_i-TdJ$.
There are $u\notin(J)$ and $g\in R_P$ with $ub=J^2g$.
Primality first gives $b=Jb_1$. Cancelling $J$ in the domain
gives $ub_1=Jg$, and primality a second time gives
$b_1\in(J)$. Hence $b\in J^2R_P$. Applying these two
divisions to each coefficient proves the asserted regularity.

On the torus intersection, \eqref{two:lift-change} shows that
the tangent restriction of $\rho_i$ is that of $\chi_m$,
since both $dJ$ and $J$ disappear in $\Omega_X^1$.
Lemma~\ref{two:tangent} identifies it with $\nu$. Both are
now regular along the complete integral $X$, so their equality
extends to every point. The first-layer nowhere-vanishing
property of $\nu$ proves the unit assertion. No global
regularity of the ambient $\chi_m$ has been assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The characteristic-two mixed ideals]
\label{two:ideal}
For every odd $m$, every $a\ge2$, and every point $P$ of an
original complete smooth finite energy fiber in the stated
finite-residue-field setting,
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak c(\alpha_{mN})_P+(\pi_a^2)
 =\bigl(\pi_a^2,
         j^{N-1}+\pi_a\widetilde Tj^{N-4},
         \pi_a j^{N-2}\bigr)_P.
 \label{two:ideal-equation}
\end{equation}
Here $j$ is any local lift of $J$, and $\widetilde T$ is any
constant unit lift of $T$. In particular, at height two the
ideal is
\begin{equation}
 (\pi_2^2,j^3+\pi_2\widetilde T,\pi_2j^2).
 \label{two:height-two-ideal}
\end{equation}
These are exact truncated ideals; all two state coefficients
are retained.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At a point of $X=(J=0)$, extend $dj_i$ to a local relative
cotangent basis $(dj_i,\theta)$. This is possible because
$dJ$ is a nonzero conormal coordinate. From
\eqref{two:local-quotient} and Lemma~\ref{two:division},
write a lift of $\rho_i$ as $A_i dj_i+B_i\theta$; its
tangent coefficient $B_i$ is a unit. The actual local form is
\[
 \alpha_{4m}^{[2]}=(j_i^3+\epsilon\widetilde T)dj_i
             +\epsilon j_i^2(A_i dj_i+B_i\theta).
\]
Its coefficient ideal is therefore
\begin{equation}
 (j_i^3+\epsilon\widetilde T+\epsilon j_i^2A_i,
                                      \epsilon j_i^2B_i)
 =(j_i^3+\epsilon\widetilde T,\epsilon j_i^2).
 \label{two:coefficient-elimination}
\end{equation}
Taking the inverse image from the square-zero quotient proves
\eqref{two:height-two-ideal} in the original local ring.
Changing $j_i$ by $\epsilon f$ changes its cube by
$\epsilon J^2\bar f$, already in the second generator;
changing $\widetilde T$ changes only a square-zero error.
This verifies the arbitrary-lift assertion at height two.

For height $a$, use the common quotient with exactly matched
time in Theorem~\ref{two:factorisation}. Multiplication by
$J^{\langle N-4\rangle}$ multiplies both coefficients in
\eqref{two:coefficient-elimination}, regardless of whether
that scalar is a unit. In a local lift it is $j_i^{N-4}$,
giving the two generators in \eqref{two:ideal-equation}
after taking the inverse image modulo $\pi_a^2$.
For another lift, $j^{N-1}$ changes by
$\epsilon J^{N-2}\bar f$, contained in the other generator,
and $\epsilon j^{N-4}$ is unchanged. Thus the formula has
the stated lift independence at every height.

On an ordinary smooth fiber $J=h\ne0$,
\eqref{trace:global-residue} reads
$\bar\alpha_{mN}=J^{N-1}dJ$ with a unit coefficient.
The actual coefficient ideal is then the unit ideal, and the
right side of \eqref{two:ideal-equation} is also a unit ideal.
This treats all smooth finite fibers, not just $X=(J=0)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The base parameter, transverse length, and state orders}

\begin{corollary}[The height-two truncated quotient]
\label{two:length}
At a closed point $P$ of a smooth supersingular fiber, after
the allowed finite unramified extension and completion, there
are coordinates $w,z$, with $\bar z=J$ and $w$ along $X$,
such that
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 \frac{\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\mathcal U_2,P}}
       {\mathfrak c(\alpha_{4m})+(\pi_2^2)}
 &\simeq
 \frac{k(P)[[w,z,\epsilon]]}
      {(\epsilon^2,z^3+\epsilon T,\epsilon z^2)}\\
 &\simeq k(P)[[w,z]]/(z^5),\qquad
 \pi_2\longmapsto z^3/T.
 \end{split}
 \label{two:completed-quotient}
\end{equation}
The transverse length of this truncated quotient is five.
It is not the length of the full critical quotient.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Relative smoothness and the nonzero conormal $dJ$ allow
$z$ to be a lift of $J$ in a completed coordinate system.
Modulo $\pi_2^2$, the complete local ring is
$k(P)[[w,z,\epsilon]]/(\epsilon^2)$. The ideal
\eqref{two:height-two-ideal} gives the first quotient in
\eqref{two:completed-quotient}. Since $T\ne0$, the mixed
relation eliminates $\epsilon=z^3/T$. The relation
$\epsilon z^2=0$ becomes $z^5=0$, while $\epsilon^2=0$
becomes $z^6=0$, already a consequence. Thus the quotient
is free of rank five over $k(P)[[w]]$, with basis
$1,z,z^2,z^3,z^4$, and has the displayed action of the
original base parameter.
\end{proof}

The remaining coordinate $w$ makes this a transverse length,
not a total Artin length at the closed point. Nor does the
calculation assert flatness or determine the untruncated
critical quotient. After a further permitted finite unramified
extension, choose a residue unit $c_0$ with $c_0^3=T$.
The coordinate change $z=c_0z'$ normalizes the coefficient
in the base action to $\pi_2=z'^3$. Thus $T$ is not a new
formal modulus. The calculation determines its original
mixed term and base action, which cannot be obtained merely
from the number five. Concrete characteristic-two elliptic
lifts and height-two formal groups already occur in
\cite[\S3.5]{LubinTate1966}; their existence does not identify
the two original state coefficients calculated here.

\begin{corollary}[Orders along the allowed states]
\label{two:states}
Consider a state over the stated cyclotomic DVR, after at most
an allowed finite unramified extension, whose residue is a
point of a complete smooth finite fiber. Let
$\operatorname{ord}_{\pi_a}$ of a one-form along that state
mean the minimum order of its two coefficients in a local
relative cotangent frame. The exact values and lower bounds are
those stated in Table~\ref{main:state-orders}.
On a supersingular fiber the divided order is exactly one at
height one for every prime, and also at height two for $p=2$.
For odd $p$ at height at least two, and for $p=2$ at height
at least three, it is at least two. The corresponding original
orders are exactly $p$, exactly five, or at least
$a\varphi(p^a)+2$, respectively. On an ordinary smooth fiber
the orders are exactly zero for the divided form and
$a\varphi(p^a)$ for the original differential.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
At height one the full ideal of Theorem~\ref{first:ideal}, including
its multiple-root form, has image $(\pi_1)$ along a
supersingular lifted state: the lifted transverse parameter
lies in $(\pi_1)$. Thus the divided order is exactly one.
For odd $p$ and $a\ge2$, the ideal
\eqref{odd:multiple-root} has image contained in
$(\pi_a^2)$, which proves only the stated lower bound.

At $p=2,a=2$, evaluation of a lift $j$ of $J$ lies in
$(\pi_2)$. The term $j^3+\pi_2\widetilde T$ has order
exactly one, whereas $\pi_2j^2$ has order at least three.
The image of the original ideal after adding $(\pi_2^2)$
is therefore $(\pi_2)$. The original ideal itself must have
order one, since an ideal of order at least two cannot
acquire an order-one element by adding $(\pi_2^2)$.
For $a\ge3$, $N\ge8$ and both nonconstant generators
in \eqref{two:ideal-equation} evaluate to order at least
two. The truncated information consequently gives order
at least two, not equality.

On an ordinary fiber the residue multiplier is nonzero
and $dJ$ is a coordinate differential, so one coefficient
is a unit. Finally,
$v_{\pi_a}(p)=\varphi(p^a)$ and
$dI_{mp^a}=p^a\alpha_{mp^a}$. Adding
$a\varphi(p^a)$ to each divided order gives the original orders,
in particular $p$ at height one and five at
$(p,a)=(2,2)$.
\end{proof}

These pointwise lower bounds do not say that a higher-level
form is globally divisible by $\pi_a^2$ on the surface:
the residue form is nonzero on the ordinary locus. The full
first-layer ideal and the higher-level ideals modulo
$\pi_a^2$ answer different thickness questions. The present
results do not determine full higher critical ideals,
exact higher supersingular orders beyond the cases stated,
singular-energy ideals, or orders after additional ramified
state extensions. All identities concern the original
eight-blowup model and its complete terminal charts.
