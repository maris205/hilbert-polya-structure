## Frozen theorem audited

Let \(K\) be algebraically closed of characteristic zero and \(g\ge8\). On
\(\mathbb A^6_K\), with \(q=(q_1,q_2,q_3)\), \(p=(p_1,p_2,p_3)\), set

\[
 V=q_1^2q_2^2q_3^2+q_1^g,
 \qquad W=p_1^2p_2^2p_3^2+p_3^g,
\]
\[
 S(q,p)=(q,p+\nabla V(q)),
 \qquad T(q,p)=(q+\nabla W(p),p),
 \qquad F=T\circ S.
\]

These are canonical Hamiltonian shears: the inverse of each is obtained by
negating its potential, and the Hessian-symmetry calculation preserves

\[
\omega=\sum_{i=1}^3dq_i\wedge dp_i.
\]

The selected degree-transfer matrices are exactly

\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}g-1&0&0\\2&1&2\\2&2&1\end{pmatrix},
 \quad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&2\\2&1&2\\0&0&g-1\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
 C=BA=\begin{pmatrix}g+7&6&6\\2g+4&5&4\\2(g-1)&2(g-1)&g-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For \(u_1>0\), write \(x=u_2/u_1\), \(y=u_3/u_1\), and use

\[
 \mathcal K_g=\{u_i>0:x\ge1,\ y\ge1,\ x+y<R\},
 \qquad R=(g-3)/2.
\]

The intended conclusion is the exact recurrence \(u_{n+1}=Cu_n\) from
\(u_0=(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}\), with total degree seen by the third coordinate,

\[
 \deg(F^n)=e_3^{\mathsf T}C^n(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad \lambda_1(F)=\rho(C),
\]

and the strict family-specific comparison \(\rho(C)<(g-1)^2\).

## Independent algebra audit

### First shear selector

The gradient degrees in the \(S\)-phase are selected from

\[
 (u_1+2u_2+2u_3,\ (g-1)u_1),\quad
 (2u_1+u_2+2u_3),\quad
 (2u_1+2u_2+u_3).
\]

For the only competition, the pure first term wins by

\[
 (g-1)u_1-(u_1+2u_2+2u_3)
 =u_1[(g-2)-2(x+y)]>u_1,
\]

because \(x+y<R=(g-3)/2\). Thus the selected vector is \(Au\). The carried
\(p\)-coordinates are handled separately: at the base vector their gaps are
\((g-2)u_1\), \(2u_1+2u_3\), and \(2u_1+2u_2\), and later gaps follow from
componentwise growth.

### Second shear selector

For \(v=Au\), the first two mixed gradient degrees are \(v_1+2v_2+2v_3\)
and \(2v_1+v_2+2v_3\); the third row compares the pure degree
\((g-1)v_3\) with the mixed degree \(2v_1+2v_2+v_3\). The exact pure-minus-mixed
gap is therefore

\[
 (g-1)v_3-(2v_1+2v_2+v_3)
 =(g-2)v_3-2v_1-2v_2
 =(2g-4)x+(g-5)y-4,
\]

after \(u_1=1\), \(v_1=g-1\), \(v_2=2+x+2y\), \(v_3=2+2x+y\). This is at
least \(11\) for \(g\ge8\), \(x,y\ge1\). An earlier draft displayed the
equivalent positivity check with incorrect coefficients
\((2g-6)x+(g-6)y-6\); that line must be replaced by the exact expansion above.
The discrepancy does not affect the selector or any downstream inequality.

The old \(q\)-terms in the \(T\)-phase are strictly dominated by
\((C-I)u>0\). Consequently \(u^+=Bv\) and \(C=BA\), not a half-step matrix.

### Cone invariance

With \(U=Cu\) and \(u_1=1\), direct multiplication gives

\[
 U_1=g+7+6x+6y,\quad U_2=2g+4+5x+4y,\quad
 U_3=(g-1)(2+2x+y).
\]

The strict margins are

\[
 U_2-U_1=g-3-x-2y>1,
\]
\[
 U_3-U_1=g-9+(2g-8)x+(g-7)y\ge8,
\]

and, with \(H=RU_1-U_2-U_3\),

\[
 H=\tfrac12[g^2+2gx+4gy-4g-24x-24y-25]>0.
\]

For \(8\le g\le11\), use \(x<R-y\) to obtain the stated positive lower bound;
for \(g\ge12\), the \(x,y\) coefficients are nonnegative and the lower
endpoint is positive. Hence \(\mathcal K_g\) is strictly \(C\)-invariant.

### Visibility and no cancellation

The exact difference is

\[
 C-A=\begin{pmatrix}8&6&6\\2g+2&4&2\\2g-4&2g-4&g-2\end{pmatrix}>0.
\]

Thus final \(q\)-degrees dominate the carried \(p\)-degrees. Also

\[
 U_3-U_2=(2g-7)x+(g-5)y-6\ge6,
\]

so visibility is by \(e_3\), not \(e_2\). This correction is substantive and
must be enforced in every theorem, abstract, and degree formula. Every iterate
coefficient lies in the nonnegative-integer semiring generated by the initial
coordinate coefficients and \(g\). In characteristic zero, selected positive
coefficients cannot vanish; strict face gaps then rule out leading cancellation.

### Perron and cubic checks

Direct expansion gives

\[
 P_g(t)=t^3-(2g+11)t^2+(g^2-16g+19)t-9(g-1)^2.
\]

The matrix \(C\) is strictly positive. Its row sums are \(g+19\), \(2g+13\),
and \(5(g-1)\), all strictly less than \((g-1)^2\) for \(g\ge8\), so

\[
 \rho(C)<(g-1)^2.
\]

The auxiliary evaluation

\[
 P_g((g-1)^2)=g^3(g-6)(g-1)^2>0
\]

is consistent but is not, by itself, the spectral bound; the row-sum argument
is the proof. Perron--Frobenius and the positive \(e_3\) observable give the
dynamical-degree limit. Optional reductions modulo \(5\) for
\(g\equiv2,3,4\pmod5\) are respectively
\(t^3+t+1\), \(t^3-2t^2-1\), and \(t^3+t^2+t-1\); absence of roots in
\(\mathbb F_5\) proves irreducibility only for that optional arithmetic lemma.
