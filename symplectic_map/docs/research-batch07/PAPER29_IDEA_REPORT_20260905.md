## The current leading candidate, exactly

Let
$$
V(x,y)=x^4/4+x^2y,\qquad
W_\delta(p,z)=p^3/3+\delta z^7/7,\qquad\delta\in\mathbb C^*.
$$
The fixed polynomial symplectomorphism is
$$
P=p+x^3+2xy,\quad Z=z+x^2,\quad
X=x+P^2,\quad Y=y+\delta Z^6.
$$
Its actual ordinary total-degree sequence is denoted by d_n, with d_0=1.
The leading coefficient ratio starts at r_0=0 and, away from its first
pole, follows r_{j+1}=δ/(1+2r_j)^4.

The author theorem states that no finite hit at r_m=−1/2 gives
d_n=2·6^n for n≥1. A first hit at time m gives L=m+2 and
$$
\sum_{n\ge0}d_nt^n=
\frac{1+6t-40t^L}{(1-6t)(1-16t^L)}.
$$
Every factor of this denominator survives over C. The minimum eventual
constant-coefficient recurrence has order L+1, while the first dynamical
degree remains6 for all allowed parameters. Each L≥3 has a nonzero
algebraic parameter, by the separate parameter proof.

The scalar subsystem becomes R_c(u)=1+c/u^4 under u=1+2r, c=2δ. Its
critical cycle is 0→∞→1→…→0. This is a statement about coefficient dynamics;
it must not be called a physical periodic orbit of the four-dimensional map.

The nontrivial comparison is not merely “recurrence order can be large”.
The degree series has all L strictly subdominant exponential modes with
modulus 16^(1/L)>1, whereas d_n/6^n has a limit. The full parameter
classification and the actual next-layer restart proof are essential.
These distinctions still require an honest external value judgment.
