# Paper 29 cancellation candidate: primary-literature collision audit

Date: 2026-09-05. Scope: literature/novelty assessment only; not a proof acceptance, Route A/B evaluation, or permission to replace a locked manuscript. This report does not modify the completed higher-degree audit.

## 1. Bottom line

**The broad headline is insufficient.** A fixed-support, one-parameter family of polynomial symplectic automorphisms of \(\mathbb A^4\), with constant \(\lambda_1=6\) and unbounded minimal eventual degree-recurrence order, already has a short elementary product-and-conjugacy construction. Section 4 gives that construction explicitly. It was derived during this audit; it is not attributed to a publication and is not proposed as a new paper.

**A narrower finding remains a plausible research target, not established novelty:** complete coefficient-resonance classification for the specified two-gradient-shear family, together with arbitrarily many *strictly growing subdominant* modes in its exact rational degree series. The difference is \(T^L-16\), rather than merely \(T^L-1\). It should be presented as an exact structural theorem in a restrictive polynomial-automorphism class, not as the discovery that degrees contain information beyond the dynamical degree.

Three important collisions must remain visible:

- Critical-orbit landing conditions and parameter-dependent degree reduction are classical in rational surface dynamics; Bedford–Kim and Truong are direct precedents.
- Favre–Jonsson already obtain rational degree series for all polynomial plane maps, with a valuation/periodic-boundary-orbit mechanism and examples whose minimal order exceeds the algebraic degree of \(\lambda_1\).
- Nguyen's 2026 result includes a simple leading pole and a strictly subdominant exponential natural boundary. Thus even the broad assertion “simple main growth coexists with complicated exponentially growing lower-order behavior” is not new for dominant rational maps.

Recommendation: **PROCEED WITH CAUTION only with the narrowed theorem and an independent proof audit.** No PASS is issued. A numerical novelty score is withheld: the current evidence supports a bounded collision assessment, not a calibrated probability of originality.

X=x+P^2,\quad Y=y+\delta Z^6,\qquad \delta\in\mathbb C^*.

It is generated by \(V=x^4/4+x^2y\) and \(W=p^3/3+\delta z^7/7\), with standard form \(dx\wedge dp+dy\wedge dz\). Write \(d_n=\deg(F_\delta^n)\), \(d_0=1\), in the fixed displayed affine coordinates.

r_1=\delta,\qquad r_{j+1}=H_\delta(r_j)
=\frac{\delta}{(1+2r_j)^4}.
\]

If the first hit of \(-1/2\) occurs at \(m\), put \(L=m+2\ge3\). The target formula is

D_\delta(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}d_nt^n

### 3.1 Bedford–Kim and Truong: orbit-length degree formulas are not new

For \(f_{a,b}(x,y)=(y,(y+a)/(x+b))\), Bedford–Kim define \(q=(-a,0)\), \(p=(-b,-a)\), and \(V_n\) by first landing \(f^n(q)=p\). Blowing up the relevant orbit gives a Picard group of rank \(n+4\), with characteristic polynomial

\[
\chi_n(T)=-1+T^2+T^3-T^{n+1}-T^{n+2}+T^{n+4}.
\]

For \(n\ge7\), its unique root outside the unit disk is \(\lambda_n>1\). This is already a fixed-dimensional family where an exceptional orbit length changes a linear algebra computation of degree growth. A growing Picard rank alone does **not** prove that every scalar degree sequence has the full minimal order; that distinction must also be retained when citing this example. [Bedford–Kim, introduction, equations (0.1)–(0.3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0611297).

The five-author predecessor computes generic rates for \(k_F=j_F\circ\iota\) and explains degree nonmultiplicativity by an exceptional curve reaching indeterminacy. Its special cubic family has quadratic growth. [Bedford et al., Theorems 1–2 and introduction](https://www.lptmc.jussieu.fr/files/maillard/PUB2/Bedford_MPAG_2008.pdf).

Truong's Theorem 1 is especially close in form: for fixed even \(N=\deg F\), the coefficient condition \(a_0=2/(1+m)\) produces the growth polynomial

\[
T^{2m+1}\bigl(T^2-(N+1)T-1\bigr)+T^2+N,
\]

whereas nonexceptional \(a_0\) gives \(T^2-(N+1)T-1\). Theorem 2 treats odd degree with further coefficient strata. These are complete exceptional-case calculations, not just numerical observations. They concern rational plane maps and generally changing \(\lambda_1\), not the fixed \(\lambda_1=6\) polynomial symplectic automorphism statement. [Truong, Theorems 1–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0801.0076).

### 3.2 Diller–Favre: why the strictly growing residual is a useful distinction

Diller–Favre's Theorem 0.1 supplies an analytically stable model for a bimeromorphic surface map. Theorem 0.3 says that, when \(\lambda_1>1\), the spectrum on \(H^{1,1}\) outside the unit disk is exactly the single simple eigenvalue \(\lambda_1\). [Diller–Favre, Theorems 0.1 and 0.3](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~jdiller/research/papers/bimeromorphic.pdf).

Consequently, a scalar degree sequence read from this finite-dimensional stable-surface pullback cannot have a second strictly expanding recurrence mode: Jordan blocks on the remaining spectrum give at most polynomially growing residual terms. This is an inference from the spectral theorem and the pullback representation. It explains why the candidate's \(L\) roots of \(T^L-16\) are not merely the usual growing-rank surface calculation. It does **not** establish global nonconjugacy to every four-dimensional product, skew product, or birational construction.

### 3.3 Favre–Jonsson: exact polynomial degree recurrences and boundary periodicity

Theorems A–B establish eventual functorial compactifications and integral degree recurrences for polynomial plane maps. Corollary 4.4 uses periodic boundary orbits to obtain order at most two on suitable eventual arithmetic subsequences, not necessarily on the full sequence. Example 4.5 gives

\[
F(x,y)=(x(x-y^2),x+y),\qquad
a_n=a_{n-1}+a_{n-2}+2a_{n-3},
\]

with minimal order three and \(\lambda_1=2\). Factoring the characteristic polynomial gives \((T-2)(T^2+T+1)\). [Favre–Jonsson, Theorems A–B, Corollary 4.4 and Example 4.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.2770); [published article](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2011/173-1/p06).

The method is a substantive precedent for reading exact growth from dynamics at infinity. It does not directly apply to a four-dimensional polynomial automorphism. No uniform full-sequence recurrence-order bound in the candidate class follows from this source.

### 3.4 Blanc–van Santen and Xie: do not confuse growth bounds with exact recurrences

Blanc–van Santen's Proposition A, for strictly positive weights \(\mu\), identifies equality \(\lambda(f)=\deg_\mu(f)>1\) with weighted algebraic stability, equivalently nonvanishing of all iterates of the weighted leading map. Proposition B extends the tool to certain nonnegative weights, with qualifications when some weights vanish. Their affine-triangular classification and weak-Perron realization concern possible growth rates. Neither proposition states a uniform bound on the minimal ordinary-degree recurrence order for arbitrary polynomial automorphisms of \(\mathbb A^4\). [Blanc–van Santen, Propositions A–D](https://arxiv.org/html/1912.01324v2).

Xie's Theorem 1.4 gives finite computation of dynamical degrees to arbitrary precision; Theorem 1.8 proves Zariski lower semicontinuity for families of dominant rational maps satisfying the stated projective-family hypotheses. The proofs use recursive **inequalities**, not universal exact finite-order equalities. Constant \(\lambda_1\) with varying degree-series denominators is compatible with these results. The numbering here is that of arXiv v2; the family theorem is 1.8, not 1.9. [Xie, Theorems 1.4, 1.8 and Sections 3–5](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.12678v2).

Call–Silverman's earlier one-parameter degeneration questions concern sets where the *dynamical degree itself* drops. That is a different locus from the proposed degree-sequence resonance locus with no \(\lambda_1\) drop. [Call–Silverman, abstract and revised version metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.02119).

### 3.5 Nguyen 2026: a strong subdominant-complexity precedent

Theorems A/D concern monomial natural boundaries. The closer Corollary B treats \(f_\mu=g\circ\varphi_\mu\), an involution composed with a Gaussian-integer monomial map. Its degree series is meromorphic on

\[
|t|<\lambda_2(f_\mu)^{-1/2}=|\mu|^{-1},
\]

with exactly one simple pole, at \(\lambda_1(f_\mu)^{-1}\), and an outer natural boundary, where \(\lambda_1(f_\mu)>|\mu|>1\). [Nguyen, Corollary B](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06142v2).

Subtracting the main pole leaves residual coefficients \(b_n\) with \(\limsup |b_n|^{1/n}=|\mu|>1\) (an inference from the radius of convergence, not a claim of an ordinary limit). The maps are nonbirational, their parameter is discrete, and their series are nonrational. These differences support only the restricted comparison proposed here. The 2026-08-01 version records Math. Ann. 396, article 10. [Primary metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06142).

### 3.6 Recent adjacent work and historical traps

Jiang's article, published 2026-03-30, studies degeneration of a one-dimensional rational-surface family to the identity on a special fiber; after blowing up an indeterminate curve, the exceptional divisor carries an induced birational map of dynamical degree 16. It is a verified recent degeneration result, but does not supply the claimed fixed-support polynomial family or unbounded exact-recurrence assertion. [Jiang, abstract and publication metadata](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40879-026-00888-3).

Déserti gives intermediate polynomial growth in higher-dimensional polynomial automorphisms and counterexamples to plane-specific stability properties. These are relevant warnings against extending plane rigidity to \(\mathbb A^4\), but do not by themselves assert the current all-parameter degree-series formula. [Déserti, abstract and introduction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.04642).

Older work already studies when monomial degree sequences fail to satisfy any constant-coefficient recurrence. Accordingly, rationality or recurrence complexity must not be presented as a newly recognized subject. [Bedford–Kim, *Linear recurrences in the degree sequences of monomial mappings*, ETDS 28 (2008), primary journal abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/ergodic-theory-and-dynamical-systems/article/abs/linear-recurrences-in-the-degree-sequences-of-monomial-mappings/42CCD5B314A3C789628DCA049BFE8370).

An earlier explicit concept precedent is Abarenkova et al., equation (29):

\[
\alpha(t)=\frac{3(1+t-2t^2+4t^3)}{(1+2t)(1-2t)(1-3t-2t^2)}.
\]

The authors identify the reciprocal poles \(\pm2\) as subdominant to approximately \(3.56155\). But \(\alpha_n\) is the degree of \(\det M_n\), not automatically the candidate's ordinary iterate degree; Section 1.2 explicitly assumes that a factorization pattern observed for thirty iterates continues for all iterates. The homogeneous-polynomial generalization also loses invertibility. This is a historical concept/conditional-computation precedent, **not** a rigorously established all-indices polynomial-symplectic-automorphism collision. [Abarenkova et al., Section 1.2, equation (29), and conclusion](https://www.lptmc.jussieu.fr/files/maillard/PUB2/GrowthSpectrumPhysicaD.pdf).

## 4. Elementary counterbaseline to the broad novelty headline

This section is an independent mathematical derivation, not a literature quotation. It is included because an elementary available baseline can defeat a novelty claim even when no paper has been located with exactly that headline.

On coordinates \((x,p,u,v)\), let

\[
H(x,p)=(p,p^6-x),\qquad
T_a(u,v)=(au+v^2,a^{-1}v),\qquad a\in\mathbb C^*,
\]

and define the parameter-independent shear

\[
\Psi(x,p,u,v)=(x+u^2,p,u,v-2up),\qquad
G_a=\Psi^{-1}(H\times T_a)\Psi.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Both planar factors have Jacobian determinant one. Directly,

\[
d(x+u^2)\wedge dp+du\wedge d(v-2up)
=dx\wedge dp+du\wedge dv,
\]

so \(G_a\) is a polynomial symplectic automorphism. Its support is identical for every \(a\ne0\): after expansion the four coordinates have respectively \(10,3,4,14\) monomials, each coefficient a nonzero constant times a power of \(a\). Thus even literal support constancy is available here.

Put \(w=v-2up\). Write

\[
H^n(x+u^2,p)=(Q_n,P_n),\qquad
C_n=a^{n-1}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a^{-3j},\qquad
U_n=a^nu+C_nw^2.
\]

Then

\[
G_a^n=(Q_n-U_n^2,\ P_n,\ U_n,\ a^{-n}w+2U_nP_n).
\tag{4.2}
\]

For \(n\ge1\), \(\deg P_n=6^n\) and \(\deg Q_n=6^{n-1}\). If \(a^3\) has exact order \(L\ge2\), the geometric sum gives \(C_n=0\) exactly when \(L\mid n\); hence \(\deg U_n=1\) on those indices and \(4\) otherwise. The last coordinate in (4.2) has degree \(6^n+\deg U_n\), because its product term exceeds the degree-two summand and the polynomial ring is a domain. It also exceeds the degrees of the other three coordinates, including at \(n=1\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{\deg G_a^n=6^n+4-3\mathbf1_{L\mid n}\quad(n\ge1).}
\tag{4.3}
\]

For a primitive \(L\)-th root \(\zeta_L\), this equals

\[
6^n+\left(4-\frac3L\right)
-\frac3L\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}\zeta_L^{jn}.
\]

Every coefficient is nonzero. Linear independence of distinct exponential sequences gives minimal eventual characteristic polynomial

\[
(T-6)(T^L-1),
\]

and minimal eventual order \(L+1\), while \(\lambda_1(G_a)=6\). The true \(d_0=1\) differs from the extrapolated formula's value two: the all-indices minimal annihilator acquires an additional factor \(T\). This is why the word **eventual** must be retained when comparing orders.

This construction is fixed-dimensional, fixed-support, algebraic in a single nonzero parameter, intrinsically coefficient-cancelling, and has constant \(\lambda_1\) with arbitrarily large eventual order. Thus none of those attributes alone separates the candidate. But its residual satisfies

\[
\deg G_a^n-6^n\in\{1,4\},
\]

so all its subdominant modes have modulus one. This precise counterbaseline has no strictly growing residual. No claim is made that *every* fixed polynomial conjugacy or four-dimensional product must have bounded residual; the usual conjugacy degree estimates provide multiplicative, not additive, control.

## 5. What the proposed denominator would actually add

Assume (2.1) has been rigorously established for the actual ordinary degrees. Put \(A=6^L\). Its main-pole coefficient is

\[
C_L=\frac{2(A-20)}{A-16}>0.
\]

There is no numerator/denominator cancellation: at \(t=1/6\) the numerator is \(2-40/6^L\ne0\); at \(16t^L=1\) it becomes \(6t-3/2\), whose only possible zero is \(t=1/4\), incompatible with \(L\ge3\). Consequently all \(L\) distinct roots of \(T^L=16\) occur, and

\[
d_n=C_L6^n+O(16^{n/L}),\qquad
1<16^{1/L}<6.
\]

The residual is genuinely unbounded, for instance

\[
d_{kL}-C_L6^{kL}
=-\frac{A-24}{A-16}\,16^k.
\]

This supplies the defensible comparison: \(L\) nonzero strictly growing subdominant modes, not just a periodic indicator added to exponential growth. Their common modulus tends to one as \(L\to\infty\), while their number grows. The normalized sequence \(d_n/6^n\) still converges; claiming its divergence would be incorrect.

The sharp theorem should include all four parts together:

1. An ordinary-degree proof of the finite-hit cancellation/restart law, including the first-hit and total-degree-drop indexing.
2. The exact rational generating function and its genuinely minimal eventual denominator.
3. Nonemptiness of exact resonance periods of arbitrary length, without assuming simple roots or transversality unless proved.
4. The no-hit complement and constancy of \(\lambda_1\) over the entire parameter domain.

Parameter-count formulas are not needed for this positioning. In particular, a provisional polynomial-degree count must not be elevated into an exact count of distinct exact-period parameters.

## 6. Suggested positioning and remaining risks

A suitable factual positioning, conditional on proof, is:

> In a single fixed-support family of two-gradient-shear polynomial symplectic automorphisms of affine four-space, we classify coefficient resonances through a rational critical orbit and determine every ordinary degree sequence. The first dynamical degree remains six, while resonance produces rational degree series with arbitrarily many strictly expanding subdominant recurrence modes.

Avoid “first” unless a further focused novelty assessment supports that word. Avoid presenting an application of a known boundary-orbit idea as a new general mechanism. Cite the rational-surface and plane-polynomial precedents directly, and make the exact four-dimensional cancellation/restart theorem carry the contribution.

Remaining risks are bounded and explicit:

- The inspected papers do not directly prove the candidate theorem; this is not a proof that no unpublished or unindexed construction exists.
- The nonlinear leading-coefficient orbit alone does not identify the full four-dimensional map up to birational conjugacy. No comprehensive rank-two-conjugacy classification was established in this audit.
- Favre–Jonsson's periodic-boundary method is a genuine methodological neighbor, even though its ambient theorem is two-dimensional and not restricted to invertible maps.
- Minimal recurrence order is coordinate/degree-function dependent; \(\lambda_1\) is conjugacy invariant. Do not call the former an intrinsic conjugacy invariant without a separate theorem.
- Paper 28's supplied internal description uses dimension growing with a prescribed selector word and noncancelling weighted dynamics. That does not coincide with the present fixed-four-dimensional coefficient-resonance theorem, but it also does not rescue the weak headline defeated by Section 4.
- This remains a pure structure result. Nothing here computes a Riemann determinant, verifies a Hilbert–Pólya operator, proves higher dynamical degrees, or establishes the algebraicity-degree conjecture for general maps.
