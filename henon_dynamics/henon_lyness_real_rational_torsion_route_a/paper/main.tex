\begin{abstract}
\ifcase\CRevisionRound
We give a complete positive-real orbit description of the autonomous Lyness
map for every positive parameter. Strict convexity of its logarithmic
integral proves that every noncentral two-sided orbit lies on one compact
regular oval, with no missing positive components or escape trajectories.
A specified clockwise section gives the map's rotation number as a ratio
of two Abel integrals with the same quartic square root. This fixes the
orientation and distinguishes auxiliary Hamiltonian time from one discrete
iterate. Rational rotations classify every least period, every repetition
and all primitive orbit families; irrational rotations give dense orbits
on their own oval. The return derivative is an exact unipotent shear,
without an unsupported claim that its shear coefficient never vanishes.
The unique center, the globally five-periodic parameter and the coordinate
boundary are treated separately. These are classical source-local results
reconstructed with explicit proof and independently checkable identities,
not a new-literature-priority claim or a target determinant construction.
\or
We reconstruct the elliptic and arithmetic boundary of positive Lyness
dynamics. Every noncentral positive orbit lies on a smooth projective
cubic; a quartic discriminant separates all singular energies from the
regular positive fibers. The map acts by translation by a specified
rational point when the parameter and energy are rational. Consequently
a rational periodic orbit forces that translation point, not its starting
point, to be torsion. Mazur's theorem is an explicit deep input, and the
sharper positive-coordinate period restriction to one, five and nine is
credited to the established Lyness classification. Five occurs away from
the center only at parameter one, while an exact parameter-seven example
supplies a complete nine-cycle. The full real Abel rotation and return
matrix description is retained. No rational point is presumed to exist
on an arbitrary rational-energy oval, and no classification is extrapolated
from a finite rational search. This source reconstruction does not supply
an unbounded rational-prime periodic carrier.
\else
We separate the real and rational periodic spectra of the positive Lyness
family. For each fixed positive parameter other than one, every sufficiently
large prime integer is the least period of an entire real oval. The proof
uses a complete Abel rotation formula and two explicitly credited classical
endpoint limits, not global monotonicity or a numerical orbit fit. When
the parameter is rational, the classical elliptic-torsion classification
restricts positive rational periodic points to periods one, five and nine;
the large-prime real ovals therefore contain no rational point. Exact
return shears and all singular and exceptional boundaries remain part of
the same orbit description. Continuous resonant families obstruct the
ordinary real point-cardinality zeta. On every finite regular annulus the
generic Koopman spectrum is the unit circle, its only eigenvalue is one,
and the operator is noncompact; the exceptional parameter instead has five
pure-point roots. The result is an explicitly owner-heavy source-local
reconstruction, with no target divisor or quantum-generator claim.
\fi

\section{One source, two periodicity questions}
For $a>0$ consider the autonomous map and its invariant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source}
 F_a(x,y)=\left(y,\frac{a+y}{x}\right),\quad
 Q=(0,\infty)^2,\quad
 H_a(x,y)=\frac{(x+1)(y+1)(x+y+a)}{xy}.
\end{equation}
A least period is a positive iterate count. In particular, the historical
term ``prime period'' can mean least period; here a \emph{prime integer}
always means an integer prime. The rational subset is considered only
when $a\in\Q_{>0}$; it is not interchangeable with the full real quadrant.
The complete-real question asks for every point's oval, rotation, repetitions
and return derivative. The arithmetic question asks which of those
periodic families actually contain positive rational points.

\paragraph{Ownership and local progress.}
The autonomous Lyness family has strong classical owners.
Bastien--Rogalski give its global real behavior and rotation endpoints
\cite{br}. Beukers--Cushman prove an autonomous monotonicity theorem
\cite{bc}; this paper does not need global monotonicity and does not turn
it into a claim of a nonzero derivative everywhere. Gasull--Mañosa--Xarles
study rational periods and the elliptic normal form \cite{gmx}.
The deep rational torsion input is Mazur's theorem \cite{mazur}.
The repository predecessor C173 treated only the identity $F_1^5=I$.
Here the actual step is the whole positive-real foliation and its
arithmetic boundary, not another finite prefix at the same parameter.
All literature-dependent conclusions below are labeled; no priority claim
or target spectral identification is made.

\section{Every positive orbit is accounted for}
Let $\ell=(1+\sqrt{1+4a})/2$, $c_a=(\ell,\ell)$ and
$h_*=(\ell+1)^3/\ell$. Put $\omega=dx\wedge dy/(xy)$ and $R(x,y)=(y,x)$.
Direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reversal}
 F_a^{-1}(x,y)=((a+x)/y,x),\quad RF_aR=F_a^{-1},
 \quad F_a^*\omega=\omega,\quad R^*\omega=-\omega.
\end{equation}
The Cartesian determinant is $(a+y)/x^2$, not one. Its ratio to the new
coordinate product is $1/(xy)$, which proves the weighted area identity.

\begin{theorem}[Global positive exhaustion]\label{thm:ovals}
The point $c_a$ is the unique fixed point and the unique critical point of
$H_a$ in $Q$. The positive fiber at $h=h_*$ is $\{c_a\}$. Every $h>h_*$
has precisely one compact analytic oval $C^+_{a,h}$. Every noncentral
two-sided orbit lies on its unique such oval, bounded away from both axes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In $u=\log x,v=\log y$, the invariant expands to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:convex}
 K_a=e^u+e^v+e^{u-v}+e^{v-u}
 +(a+1)(e^{-u}+e^{-v})+a e^{-u-v}+a+2.
\end{equation}
The Hessian is a sum of positive multiples of exponent-vector outer
products; the vectors $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ already span the plane.
Thus it is positive definite. The positive and negative coordinate
exponentials make $K_a$ proper. It has a unique nondegenerate minimum;
symmetry and
\[
 \frac{d}{dr}H_a(r,r)=\frac{2(r+1)(r^2-r-a)}{r^3}
\]
locate it at $c_a$. Along each ray from the minimum in log coordinates,
strict convexity gives a strictly increasing value after the minimum,
tending to infinity. Each higher level is therefore one smooth radial
graph. The inverse in \eqref{eq:reversal} preserves positivity, and the
invariant confines every forward and backward iterate to that compact
level. Solving $F_a(x,y)=(x,y)$ gives the same unique center.
\end{proof}

\section{A normalized Abel rotation and all return matrices}
Write the level equation as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quartic}
 (x+1)y^2+B(x)y+(x+1)(x+a)=0,\quad
 B(x)=(x+1)(x+a+1)-hx,\quad
 D(x)=B(x)^2-4(x+1)^2(x+a).
\end{equation}
Let $p_h=(r,r)$ be the unique diagonal intersection with $r>\ell$.
Let $\alpha,\beta$ be the minimum and maximum $x$ coordinates on the
positive oval; they are the two turning roots delimiting that oval, not
arbitrarily chosen roots of the quartic. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:abel}
 T(h)=2\int_\alpha^\beta\frac{dx}{\sqrt{D(x)}},\qquad
 \tau(h)=2\int_r^\beta\frac{dx}{\sqrt{D(x)}},\qquad
 \rho(h)=\frac{\tau(h)}{T(h)}.
\end{equation}
These are positive finite Abel integrals with their positive square root
on the oval interval.

\begin{theorem}[Complete oval dynamics]\label{thm:rotation}
The function $\rho:(h_*,\infty)\to(0,1)$ is real analytic.
There are smooth coordinates $(h,\theta)$ on every regular annulus such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:angle}
 F_a(h,\theta)=(h,\theta+\rho(h)),\qquad
 R(h,\theta)=(h,-\theta),\quad \theta\in\R/\Z.
\end{equation}
If $\rho(h)=m/n$ in lowest terms, every point of that oval has least
period $n$; otherwise every two-sided orbit is dense on the oval. For
every positive integer $N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed}
 \Fix(F_a^N)=\{c_a\}\cup
 \bigcup_{\substack{h>h_*\\N\rho(h)\in\Z}}C^+_{a,h}.
\end{equation}
At a period-$n$ point the return matrix, in these coordinates, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shear}
 D F_a^{kn}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\kn\rho'(h)&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad k\ge1.
\end{equation}
Its multipliers are both one. A nonzero shear is asserted only when
$\rho'(h)\ne0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The auxiliary Hamiltonian vector field
$X_H=xy(H_y\partial_x-H_x\partial_y)$ satisfies
$\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH$ and is nonzero on each compact regular oval.
The map commutes with its complete oval flow because it preserves both
$H$ and $\omega$. On the upper and lower quadratic roots in
\eqref{eq:quartic}, respectively, $\dot x=\pm\sqrt D$. The full flow
period is therefore $T$. At $x=r$, the two roots are $r$ and
$(a+r)/r<r$; the latter is exactly $F_a(p_h)$'s second coordinate.
The positive clockwise arc from $p_h$ through $\beta$ to that point
takes time $\tau$. Elapsed flow time from $p_h$, divided by $T$,
defines $\theta$ and proves \eqref{eq:angle}. The swap fixes the section
and reverses the flow. Removing the square-root turning singularities
by $x=\alpha+(\beta-\alpha)\sin^2t$ proves analytic dependence on
each compact regular energy interval. The turning points are simple;
equivalently their affine gradient is nonzero and the log-convex oval
has a nondegenerate vertical tangency. The rotation on the circle then
proves the period and density statements. Iteration and differentiation
of \eqref{eq:angle} give \eqref{eq:fixed} and \eqref{eq:shear}.
\end{proof}

The two-by-two Cartesian monodromy at a return point is conjugate to
\eqref{eq:shear}; its nilpotent part squares to zero. At the center the
one-step characteristic polynomial is
$\lambda^2-\ell^{-1}\lambda+1$, so the multipliers are
$\exp(\pm2\pi i\rho_0)$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:center}
 \rho_0(a)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\arccos\frac{1}{2\ell}.
\end{equation}
The center is Lyapunov stable by its invariant minimum, not asymptotically
stable. Periodic ovals are continuous families, not isolated hyperbolic
orbits. The auxiliary $T$ and $\tau$ have not altered the source's
unit-iterate clock.

\section{Smooth cubics, exceptional parameters and rational torsion}
The projective level curve is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cubic}
 P_{a,h}(X,Y,Z)=(X+Z)(Y+Z)(X+Y+aZ)-hXYZ=0.
\end{equation}
The exact quartic elimination gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discriminant}
 \operatorname{disc}_xD=256h^3(a-h-1)^2a(h-h_+)(h-h_-),\qquad
 h_\pm=\frac{2a^2+10a-1\pm(4a+1)\sqrt{4a+1}}{2a}.
\end{equation}
Here $h_+=h_*$. Since $a=\ell^2-\ell$,
$h_*-a-2=4\ell+1+1/\ell>0$. The possible singular energies
$0,a-1,h_\pm$ do not meet any regular positive oval. More explicitly,
for $x\ne-1$ an affine singular point would force a repeated root of $D$;
at $x=-1$ the curve forces $y=0$ and has $P_y=h\ne0$.
The three points at infinity each have a nonzero partial derivative.
Thus every $h>h_*$ gives a smooth complex genus-one cubic.
The singular center fiber is not subjected to an elliptic torsion theorem.

\begin{proposition}[Translation point, not starting-point torsion]
With identity $O=[1:-1:0]$, the smooth cubic's group law gives
$F_a(P)=P+Q_0$, where $Q_0=[1:0:0]$. If $a,x,y$ are positive rational
and $P=(x,y)$ is noncentral, its finite least period is the order of
$Q_0$ in this rational elliptic group.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The tangent at $O$ is $X+Y=(h-a)Z$; substitution gives
$h(h-a+1)Z^3$, so $O$ is a flex. The swap is negation. The involution
$J(x,y)=((a+y)/x,y)$ is the sheet exchange of the degree-two projection
to the $y$ line. It has branch fixed points; the genus-one involution
law therefore gives $J(P)=J(O)-P$. Its homogeneous expression
$[Z(Y+aZ):XY:XZ]$ gives $J(O)=[0:1:0]=-Q_0$.
Since $F_a=R\circ J$, the translation formula follows, in agreement
with \cite[Section 2.1]{gmx}. Rationality gives $h\in\Q$ and a smooth
curve over $\Q$, so $F_a^n(P)=P$ is equivalent to $nQ_0=O$.
No assertion that $P$ itself is torsion follows from this cancellation.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Deep and sharper external inputs.}
Mazur's torsion theorem \cite{mazur} limits finite point orders on a
rational elliptic curve to $1,\ldots,10,12$. It is not proved by this
package. The additional positive-coordinate exclusion, credited to the
Lyness classification discussed in \cite{gmx}, sharpens positive rational
least periods to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational}
 \{1,5,9\}.
\end{equation}
This sharper restriction is explicitly imported, not a consequence
of Mazur's list alone and not an inference from our finite grid.
A positive rational center exists exactly when $a=r(r-1)$ for a rational
$r>1$, with center $(r,r)$.

\paragraph{The five-periodic parameter and an exact nine-cycle.}
Direct iteration gives $F_1^5=I$; all noncentral positive points then have
least period five. Conversely, for $a\ne1$, the equations $F_a^5(x,y)=(x,y)$,
after positive denominators are cleared, reduce to
\begin{align*}
 p&=a x-a y+a-y^2+y=0,\\
 q&=-a^2x-a^2+a x^2y+a x^2-a xy+a x-2ay+xy-y^2=0.
\end{align*}
Substitute $x=(a+y)(y-1)/a$ from $p=0$ into $q$ to obtain
$q=y(a+y)^2(y^2-y-a)/a$. Positivity forces $x=y=\ell$.
There is therefore no noncentral period five at $a\ne1$.
At $a=7$, the successive scalar coordinates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nine}
 3/2,\quad5/7,\quad36/7,\quad17,\quad14/3,\quad35/51,
 \quad28/17,\quad63/5,\quad119/10
\end{equation}
form the GMX nine-cycle \cite{gmx}. Adjacent cyclic pairs satisfy the
recurrence exactly and are distinct, proving least period nine; the
energy is $258/7$. This example does not establish nine-period existence
for every rational parameter. Rational energy alone does not ensure a
rational point on the positive oval. The excluded parameter $a=0$ is
globally six-periodic, and is not covered by a generic $a>0,a\ne1$ argument.

\section{Real prime periods and the operator stopping result}
We invoke exactly the following two classical endpoint statements from
\cite[Propositions 6--7]{br}, with the clockwise normalization in
\eqref{eq:abel}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limits}
 \lim_{h\downarrow h_*}\rho(h)=\rho_0(a),\qquad
 \lim_{h\to\infty}\rho(h)=1/5.
\end{equation}
Their proofs are external dependencies, not replaced by quadrature.
Since $\ell>1$, $1/6<\rho_0<1/4$, and $\rho_0=1/5$ exactly when $a=1$.
For $a\ne1$, the analytic function $\rho$ is nonconstant and its image
contains the open interval $J_a$ between the two endpoint limits.
We need neither equality of its image with $J_a$ nor uniqueness of an
energy with a prescribed rotation.

\begin{theorem}[The real--rational prime-period contrast]\label{thm:contrast}
For each fixed $a>0$, $a\ne1$, every sufficiently large prime integer
is a real least period. If $a$ is rational, every such periodic oval
with prime-integer period at least eleven has no rational point.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a rational interval $(s,t)\subset J_a$. For a prime integer
$p>1/(t-s)$ the interval $(ps,pt)$ has length greater than one and
contains an integer $m$ with $1\le m\le p-1$. Hence $m/p$ is reduced.
The intermediate value theorem and \eqref{eq:limits} supply a finite
energy with rotation $m/p$. Theorem~\ref{thm:rotation} gives a whole
oval of least period $p$. A rational point on that oval would contradict
the explicitly imported classification \eqref{eq:rational}.
\end{proof}

Every rational-rotation oval contains uncountably many primitive
orbits: its quotient by a rotation of order $n$ is another circle.
For $a\ne1$, such ovals exist by Theorem~\ref{thm:contrast}; for $a=1$,
the whole quadrant is fixed by the fifth iterate. Consequently the
ordinary real point-cardinality expression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:undefined}
 \exp\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\#\Fix(F_a^n)\,\frac{z^n}{n}\right)
\end{equation}
has an infinite coefficient at a finite order for every $a>0$.
It is not an ordinary formal zeta series with finite coefficients.
This says nothing against separately defined measured or regularized
constructions; changing the definition changes the candidate.

\begin{proposition}[A native unitary, not a target determinant]
Choose $h_*<h_1<h_2<\infty$ and normalize the finite invariant measure
$T(h)\,dh\,d\theta$ on that annulus. Its Koopman operator $Uf=f\circ F_a$
is unitary and noncompact. If $a\ne1$, its spectrum is the unit circle
and its point spectrum is exactly $\{1\}$, of infinite multiplicity.
The remaining angular summands have absolutely continuous spectral
measures. If $a=1$, its spectrum is the five fifth roots of unity, each
with infinite multiplicity.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Hamiltonian coordinates give $\omega=T(h)d\theta\wedge dh$.
Fourier decomposition in the angle gives the orthogonal direct sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:koopman}
 L^2=\bigoplus_{k\in\Z}L^2([h_1,h_2],T(h)dh),\qquad
 U_k g(h)=e^{2\pi i k\rho(h)}g(h).
\end{equation}
These multipliers have modulus one. The $k=0$ radial space gives
infinitely many eigenvectors with eigenvalue one, proving noncompactness.
For $a\ne1$, analyticity and nonconstancy make the level sets of each
$k\ne0$ multiplier measure zero, so there are no eigenvectors there.
Away from the discrete critical set, the multiplier is a smooth local
change of variable; its pushforward spectral measures are absolutely
continuous. The critical set has measure zero. Finally $\rho([h_1,h_2])$
contains an interval of positive length. Multiplying it by a sufficiently
large integer $k$ wraps the whole circle, giving that circle as the
essential range of $U_k$. At $a=1$, the rotation is identically $1/5$,
and \eqref{eq:koopman} gives all five infinite-multiplicity roots.
\end{proof}
The quadrant measure itself is not declared finite, and this annular
construction does not produce a distinguished self-adjoint target
generator. Noncompactness also excludes every finite Schatten class.

\section{Evidence, dependency boundaries and conclusion}
The canonical finite evidence contains 24 upper-diagonal orbit controls
with 288 exact map steps, ten distinct seed-induced five-cycles and one
nine-cycle, totaling 59 cycle points. Five rational centers are checked
separately. Five certified intervals inside the gap between classical
rotation endpoints use a 128-bit rational enclosure of $\pi$ and 96-bit
cosine enclosures. Their 1,280 prime and composite denominator controls
give 690 reduced-fraction sufficient-period witnesses. Those witnesses
are rotation values in the guaranteed image, not claimed exact coordinates
of reconstructed periodic ovals.

An independent checker differentiates the scalar recurrence in two dual
directions, rather than importing the producer's matrix product.
Symbolic checks cover the invariant, weighted symplectic law, quartic
discriminant, flex, exceptional period and return matrices. The separate
90-digit quadratures are numerical controls, not rigorous interval
certificates for Abel integrals. Universal geometry and periodicity
follow from the proof and the named classical inputs; finite counts do
not prove them. Repaired-hash mutations test semantic rejection as well
as type and YAML closure.
