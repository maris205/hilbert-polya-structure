\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A transfer-operator proposal for Hilbert--P\'olya must eventually choose an
operator space.  Formal periodic weights do not decide whether the proposed
operator is compact, nuclear, or even bounded, and scalar spectral estimates
need not survive an infinite-dimensional quantum fibre.  This distinction is
decisive for a Rauzy--Veech proposal coupled to the oscillator representation
of its symplectic homology cocycle.

The predecessor calculation reconstructed a four-letter genus-two Rauzy
class and proved abstract noncompactness criteria for discrete metaplectic
extensions.  Its unresolved hypothesis was concrete: no published transfer space
had yet been shown to satisfy the branch-compression or norm-summability
hypotheses.  We verify it here using the precompact first-return model
of Avila, Gou\"ezel, and Yoccoz (AGY) \citep{agy2006}.  AGY constructs a
countable full-branch expanding map, a roof with exponential tails, and a
weighted operator on bounded \(C^1\) functions.  Once an inducing word is
fixed, this is an actual analytic operator family rather than an averaged
Rauzy graph.

Our one-sentence conclusion is the following.  For every AGY neat-return
model satisfying the source hypotheses below, adjoining the unsmoothed
oscillator fibre makes the raw vector-valued \(C_b^1\) operator noncompact
throughout its defining half-plane; the normalized \(L^2\) companion is
already noncompact at scalar level and remains so after the twist throughout
\(\Re s\ge0\); full-matrix Rauzy decoding also removes the proposed
same-matrix branch-cancellation escape.

The argument is elementary once the correct space and chronology are fixed,
but it is stronger than finding a noncompact formal summand.  On \(C_b^1\),
the branch series converges absolutely in operator norm.  We then localize
the \emph{full} sum with a source-provided bump and a bounded evaluation map.
All other branches vanish in the compression.  On the normalized Hilbert
space, a cylinder projection gives a second exact isolation, while a twisted
Koopman operator identifies the imaginary-axis transfer as a coisometry.

The paper makes four checkable contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item We freeze a project-chosen four-letter AGY-admissible section with
\(\gamma_*=t^{64}(tbttbtbb)^8\).  Eight complete blocks prove strong
positivity by the AGY \(3d-4\) criterion; a maximal-prefix and no-border
check proves neatness.

\item We prove an all-length decoder.  From a fixed labeled start, the true
first Rauzy arrow is the unique one whose winner row dominates its loser row
in \(B_\gamma^T\).  Row subtraction recovers the remaining path matrix and
strictly decreases its entry sum.

\item We prove absolute branch-norm convergence and an exact
bump/evaluation compression on
\(C_b^1(\Delta;L^2(\R^2))\).  The compressed operator is a nonzero
infinite-dimensional unitary, so the full operator is noncompact and not
Banach nuclear for every \(\Re s>-\sigma _0\).

\item We prove that the invariant-density-normalized operator on
\(L^2(\mu;L^2(\R^2))\) is contractive but noncompact for \(\Re s\ge0\).
For \(s=it\), its essential norm is exactly one.  In the farther-right
half-plane \(\Re s\ge d/2\), the explicit Hilbert branch atoms are absolutely
summable in operator norm.
\end{enumerate}

The result is deliberately space-specific.  Noncompactness removes an
ordinary nuclear or Schatten-class determinant on the tested realizations;
it does not say that \(I-z\cL_s\) is never Fredholm.  A holomorphic space has
no nonzero branch-supported bump, and a flat, distributional, or semifinite
trace need not arise from an ordinary compact operator.  These options are
not repairs of the present operator and require independent source
specifications.

\paragraph{Relation to existing transfer spaces.}
AGY's scalar \(C^1\) space is the main source object.  The quasi-H\"older
realization of Aimino, Nicol, and Todd \citep{aimino2017} confirms that other
regularity completions are useful for Rauzy--Veech--Zorich statistics, but it
does not state that every return-cylinder indicator belongs to its space.
We therefore do not use quasi-H\"older cylinder projectors.  Compactness
obstructions for a single operator-weighted composition map are already
standard: the point-evaluation factorization of
\citet{bonet2012} forces compact point weights.  In a dynamical setting,
\citet{magee-naud2020} allow arbitrary Hilbert-space unitary twists for norm
estimates but establish the ordinary Fredholm determinant only for
finite-dimensional twists.  Our contribution is consequently not the bare
observation that an infinite-dimensional unitary is noncompact.  It is the
exact isolation of that unitary from the convergent \emph{multi-branch} AGY
operator, with the stated full half-planes and the independent normalized
\(L^2\) conclusion.  The oscillator representation and its metaplectic
covariance are themselves standard \citep{folland1989}.
