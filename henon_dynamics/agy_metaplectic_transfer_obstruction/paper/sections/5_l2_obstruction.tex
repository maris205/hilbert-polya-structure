\section{The normalized Hilbert companion}
\label{sec:l2}

The invariant density in \eqref{eq:density} defines conditional
inverse-branch probabilities
\begin{equation}
 p_\gamma(x)=j_\gamma(x)\frac{\rho(h_\gamma x)}{\rho(x)},
 \qquad
 \sum_\gamma p_\gamma(x)=1
 \quad\hbox{a.e.}
 \label{eq:branch-probabilities}
\end{equation}
Let \(\cH=L^2(\Delta,\mu;\cF)\) and define
\begin{equation}
 (\cP_sF)(x)=\sum_\gamma p_\gamma(x)e^{-sr_\gamma(x)}
 U_\gamma F(h_\gamma x).
 \label{eq:normalized-transfer}
\end{equation}
This Hilbert companion is not AGY's claimed scalar spectral-gap space.  It
is a natural invariant-measure normalization used here as an independent
robustness test.

The countable Bochner sum in \eqref{eq:normalized-transfer} is well defined.
Indeed, for \(a=\Re s\ge0\), Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{equation}
 \sum_\gamma p_\gamma e^{-ar_\gamma}\|F(h_\gamma x)\|
 \le
 \left(\sum_\gamma p_\gamma\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\sum_\gamma p_\gamma\|F(h_\gamma x)\|^2\right)^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:bochner-bound}
\end{equation}
The square of the second factor has integral \(\|F\|_{\cH}^2\) by branch
disintegration, so the sum is absolutely convergent almost everywhere.
For any tail \(A\) of an enumeration, the same inequality yields
\[
 \left\|\sum_{\gamma\in A}p_\gamma e^{-sr_\gamma}
 U_\gamma F\circ h_\gamma\right\|_{\cH}^2
 \le \int_{\bigcup_{\gamma\in A}D_\gamma}\|F(y)\|^2\,d\mu(y),
\]
which tends to zero.  Hence the series converges unconditionally in
\(\cH\), independently of its enumeration.

\begin{theorem}[Contraction and branch norm]
\label{thm:l2-branch}
For \(\Re s\ge0\), \(\cP_s\) is a contraction on \(\cH\).  For one branch,
let
\[
 (K_{s,\gamma}f)(x)
 =p_\gamma(x)e^{-sr_\gamma(x)}f(h_\gamma x),
 \quad
 K_{s,\gamma}:L^2(D_\gamma,\mu)\to L^2(\Delta,\mu).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \|K_{s,\gamma}\|
 =\esssup_{x\in\Delta}
  \sqrt{p_\gamma(x)}e^{-\Re(s)r_\gamma(x)}>0.
 \label{eq:branch-norm}
\end{equation}
Because \(\mu\) is nonatomic,
\begin{equation}
 \|K_{s,\gamma}\|_{\ess}=\|K_{s,\gamma}\|.
 \label{eq:scalar-branch-essential}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \|\cP_s\|_{\ess}\ge\|K_{s,\gamma}\|>0,
 \label{eq:l2-essential}
\end{equation}
so \(\cP_s\) is noncompact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Convexity of the squared Hilbert norm and
\eqref{eq:branch-probabilities} give, for \(a=\Re s\ge0\),
\[
 \|\cP_sF(x)\|^2
 \le\sum_\gamma p_\gamma(x)e^{-2ar_\gamma(x)}
     \|F(h_\gamma x)\|^2
 \le\sum_\gamma p_\gamma(x)\|F(h_\gamma x)\|^2.
\]
The branch disintegration identity
\begin{equation}
 \int_{D_\gamma}q(y)\,d\mu(y)
 =\int_\Delta p_\gamma(x)q(h_\gamma x)\,d\mu(x)
 \label{eq:disintegration}
\end{equation}
then proves \(\|\cP_sF\|\le\|F\|\).

Applying \eqref{eq:disintegration} to one scalar branch yields
\[
 \|K_{s,\gamma}f\|^2
 =\int_{D_\gamma}p_\gamma(Ty)e^{-2ar(y)}|f(y)|^2\,d\mu(y),
\]
which gives \eqref{eq:branch-norm}.  Extension by zero from
\(L^2(D_\gamma,\mu)\) isolates this branch even for the scalar normalized
operator.

To prove \eqref{eq:scalar-branch-essential}, write
\[
 m(y)=\sqrt{p_\gamma(Ty)}e^{-ar(y)},\qquad
 M=\esssup_{D_\gamma}m=\|K_{s,\gamma}\|.
\]
For every \(\varepsilon>0\), the set
\(\{m>M-\varepsilon\}\) has positive measure.  Nonatomicity lets us split
it into pairwise disjoint positive-measure sets \(E_n\).  The normalized
indicators \(f_n=1_{E_n}/\sqrt{\mu(E_n)}\) are orthonormal, hence weakly
null, while \(\|K_{s,\gamma}f_n\|\ge M-\varepsilon\).  Every compact
operator sends \(f_n\) to zero in norm.  Thus
\(\|K_{s,\gamma}\|_{\ess}\ge M-\varepsilon\); let
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).  The reverse inequality is automatic.

Extension by zero from
\(L^2(D_\gamma,\mu;\cF)\) isolates the branch exactly:
\begin{equation}
 \cP_sJ_\gamma=K_{s,\gamma}\otimes U_\gamma.
 \label{eq:l2-compression}
\end{equation}
For a bounded scalar operator \(K\) and an infinite-dimensional unitary
\(U\), \(\|K\otimes U\|_{\ess}=\|K\|\).  Applying this identity to
\eqref{eq:l2-compression} proves \eqref{eq:l2-essential}.
With scalar fibre \(U_\gamma=1\), the same compression and
\eqref{eq:scalar-branch-essential} prove noncompactness throughout
\(\Re s\ge0\) as well.
\end{proof}

On the imaginary axis the obstruction is sharp.

\begin{theorem}[Coisometry]
\label{thm:coisometry}
For every \(t\in\R\),
\begin{equation}
 \cP_{it}\cP_{it}^*=I,
 \qquad
 \|\cP_{it}\|_{\ess}=1.
 \label{eq:coisometry}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take inner products linear in the first argument.  On \(y\in D_\gamma\),
define
\[
 (\mathcal U_tG)(y)=e^{itr(y)}U_\gamma^*G(Ty).
\]
Invariance of \(\mu\) and unitarity make \(\mathcal U_t\) an isometry.
Equations \eqref{eq:normalized-transfer} and \eqref{eq:disintegration} give
\(\cP_{it}=\mathcal U_t^*\), hence the first identity in
\eqref{eq:coisometry}.  In the Calkin algebra,
\([\cP_{it}][\cP_{it}]^*=[I]\), so
\(\|\cP_{it}\|_{\ess}\ge1\).  Contractivity gives the reverse inequality.
\end{proof}

The normalized \(L^2\) obstruction is not caused specifically by the
oscillator fibre: the scalar operator is noncompact throughout
\(\Re s\ge0\), and the Koopman-adjoint argument makes it a coisometry with
essential norm one on the imaginary axis.  This section therefore closes a
proposed Hilbert realization as a space-level robustness test; the
oscillator-specific multi-branch obstruction is
\Cref{thm:c1-noncompact}.

\subsection{The absolute atomic half-plane}

Let \(q_\gamma=\mu(D_\gamma)\) and
\(R_\gamma=\inf r_\gamma\).  Uniform distortion,
\eqref{eq:branch-formula}, and \eqref{eq:density} imply
\begin{equation}
 p_\gamma(x)\asymp e^{-d r_\gamma(x)},
 \qquad
 q_\gamma\asymp e^{-dR_\gamma}.
 \label{eq:q-comparison}
\end{equation}
Thus the branch operator extended by zero satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \|K_{s,\gamma}\|
 \le Cq_\gamma^{1/2+\Re(s)/d}.
 \label{eq:atomic-bound}
\end{equation}
When \(\Re s\ge d/2\), the exponent is at least one and
\(\sum_\gamma q_\gamma=1\), so
\begin{equation}
 \sum_\gamma\|K_{s,\gamma}\|<\infty.
 \label{eq:atomic-sum}
\end{equation}
For four labels the threshold is \(\Re s\ge2\).

Even if branch terms are grouped by their projected symplectic matrix, no
signed aggregate can vanish.  Choose an input supported in one
\(D_{\gamma_0}\).  Every other branch evaluates that input outside its
support, while the \(\gamma_0\) term remains nonzero.  This proof retains the
central sign rather than replacing it by an absolute value.  The decoder in
\Cref{sec:decoder} independently shows that distinct fixed-start branches
have distinct full matrices in the current full-rank model.
