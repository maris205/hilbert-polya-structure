# C279 proof package

## Claim

For every finite unweighted path `P_n`, every `n>=1`, and every initial vector
`x^0 in R^n`, the Euclidean gradient flow of path total variation has a unique
global solution.  Its maximal constant blocks move by the explicit boundary
sign rule below, never split, merge jointly at simultaneous collisions, and
reach the initial mean after at most `n-1` event times.  The trajectory is
piecewise affine, obeys exact dissipation identities, reaches consensus in
finite time, and equals the path ROF minimizer at every time.

## Status

**PROVABLE AS STATED.**

The statement is restricted to finite unweighted paths with Euclidean vertex
norm.  General finite graphs are not included.

## Assumptions

- `n` is a positive integer.
- The graph has vertices `1,...,n` and only edges `(i,i+1)`.
- Vertex space is `R^n` with its ordinary Euclidean inner product.
- All edges have unit weight.
- The flow clock is maximal-monotone semigroup time, not a time discretization.

## Notation

Define `D:R^n -> R^{n-1}` by `(Dx)_i=x_{i+1}-x_i` and

```text
J(x)=||Dx||_1=sum_i |x_{i+1}-x_i|.
```

For a scalar `a`, `Sgn(a)={sign(a)}` when `a!=0` and
`Sgn(0)=[-1,1]`.  Then

```text
partial J(x)={D^T z : z_i in Sgn((Dx)_i)}.
```

A maximal constant block is an interval `B=[l,r]` on which all coordinates
have one value and which cannot be enlarged.  Put `m=|B|=r-l+1`,

```text
s_{l-1}=sign(x_l-x_{l-1}) if l>1, and 0 if l=1,
s_r    =sign(x_{r+1}-x_r) if r<n, and 0 if r=n.
```

The initial mean is `bar x=(1/n)sum_i x_i^0`.

## Exact theorem

1. There is a unique global absolutely continuous solution of

   ```text
   x'(t) in -partial J(x(t)),  x(0)=x^0.
   ```

2. Until the next collision, every coordinate in `B=[l,r]` has the common
   constant velocity

   ```text
   v_B=(s_r-s_{l-1})/m.
   ```

   Thus every current block height is affine.  The next event is the least
   positive equality time of two adjacent affine block heights.

3. Blocks never split.  At an event, every connected string of equal adjacent
   block heights is replaced by its union.  This includes multiple disjoint or
   chained simultaneous mergers.  Each event reduces the block count, so
   there are at most `n-1` distinct event times.

4. The mean is preserved and, outside the finite event set,

   ```text
   d[||x-bar x 1||_2^2/2]/dt=-J(x),
   dJ(x)/dt=-||x'(t)||_2^2.
   ```

   If `T` is the first consensus time, then

   ```text
   T <= sqrt(n)||x^0-bar x 1||_2,
   x(t)=bar x 1 for all t>=T.
   ```

5. For every `t>=0`, including event and post-consensus times,

   ```text
   x(t)=argmin_y {(1/2)||y-x^0||_2^2+tJ(y)}.
   ```

## Proof strategy

Use maximal-monotone theory only for existence and uniqueness.  Then solve the
finite polyhedral flow directly: interpolate the edge flux across each
plateau, show that this gives a common velocity and a valid subgradient, and
restart the unique solution after each joint collision.  One-homogeneity and
a path Poincare estimate force finite consensus.  Finally average the
time-dependent subgradient; no splitting makes that average a valid
subgradient at the final state, which is precisely the ROF optimality
condition.

## Dependency map

1. Convexity and continuity of `J` imply maximal monotonicity of `partial J`.
2. The incidence representation of `partial J` implies the flux interpolation
   lemma.
3. The interpolation lemma plus uniqueness implies piecewise affine motion and
   no splitting.
4. No splitting implies a finite merger ledger and preserves the sign of every
   edge that is nonzero at a later time.
5. One-homogeneity plus the path Poincare bound implies finite consensus.
6. Preserved final-edge signs imply the averaged-subgradient ROF KKT condition.

## Proof

### Step 1: well-posedness and the edge-flux form

The function `J` is finite, convex, continuous, and proper on the finite
Hilbert space `R^n`.  Its subdifferential is therefore maximal monotone, so the
Cauchy problem generated by `-partial J` has a unique global contraction
semigroup solution.  The chain rule for `J(x)=||Dx||_1` gives exactly

```text
partial J(x)={D^T z:z_i in Sgn((Dx)_i)}.
```

Every vector in the range of `D^T` has coordinate sum zero.

### Step 2: block-flux interpolation and velocity

Fix a maximal constant block `B=[l,r]` of size `m`.  The flux immediately to
its left is the fixed exterior sign `s_{l-1}` and the flux immediately to its
right is `s_r`; a missing endpoint flux is zero.  On its internal zero-jump
edges define

```text
z_{l+k-1}=s_{l-1}+(k/m)(s_r-s_{l-1}),  k=1,...,m-1.
```

Both endpoint values belong to `[-1,1]`, hence every interpolated value also
belongs to `[-1,1]=Sgn(0)`.  The flux is therefore admissible.  On every
coordinate of the block,

```text
(D^T z)_i=(s_{l-1}-s_r)/m.
```

Consequently `-D^T z` gives the common velocity
`(s_r-s_{l-1})/m`.  Equivalently, this interpolation uniquely minimizes the
sum of squared consecutive flux differences with its two boundary fluxes.
After the exterior nonzero-edge signs are fixed, `||D^T z||_2^2` is the sum
of these objectives over the maximal plateaus.  The global minimization
therefore separates blockwise, so the local discrete Dirichlet minimizer is
exactly the minimal-norm subgradient selected by the right derivative of the
convex flow.

### Step 3: exact events and no splitting

As long as adjacent block heights are unequal, their signs and sizes are
fixed.  Step 2 therefore supplies constant block velocities, and the affine
curve obtained from them satisfies the differential inclusion.  Uniqueness
makes it the semigroup solution on that interval.

Every pre-existing plateau has one velocity, so it cannot split before an
adjacent collision.  When adjacent heights become equal, regard their union,
or an entire connected string that becomes equal simultaneously, as one new
block.  Step 2 again assigns one common velocity to that union.  Uniqueness
therefore forbids later separation.  Each event strictly decreases the number
of maximal blocks.  There are at most `n-1` event times, and the same argument
handles several disjoint collisions at one time without imposing an order on
them.

### Step 4: dissipation and finite consensus

Mean preservation follows from `sum_i(D^Tz)_i=0`.  For a one-homogeneous
convex functional, every `g in partial J(x)` satisfies
`<g,x>=J(x)`.  Since `g` is orthogonal to constants and `x'=-g`, the convex
chain rule gives, at every non-event time (and almost everywhere globally),

```text
(1/2)d||x-bar x 1||_2^2/dt=<x-bar x 1,x'>=-J(x),
dJ(x)/dt=<g,x'>=-||x'||_2^2.
```

For each coordinate,

```text
|x_i-bar x| <= (1/n)sum_j |x_i-x_j| <= J(x),
```

because the variation along the path between `i` and `j` is at most `J(x)`.
Thus, with `rho=||x-bar x 1||_2`,

```text
rho <= sqrt(n)J(x).
```

Whenever `rho>0`, the first dissipation identity yields
`rho'=-J(x)/rho<=-1/sqrt(n)`.  Therefore `rho` reaches zero by
`sqrt(n)rho(0)`.  A constant state has zero subgradient of minimal norm and
remains constant.  This also shows that a non-consensus affine block phase
cannot persist forever, so Step 3 always has a next collision before
consensus.

### Step 5: ROF equals the flow

Fix `t>0`.  Write `-x'(s)=D^Tz(s)` with
`z_i(s) in Sgn((Dx(s))_i)` for almost every `s`.  If `(Dx(t))_i!=0`, that edge
has never entered a merged plateau: otherwise no splitting would keep it zero
thereafter.  By continuity its sign has consequently stayed equal to its
sign at time `t`, and `z_i(s)` equals that sign almost everywhere on `[0,t]`.
If `(Dx(t))_i=0`, every `z_i(s)` still lies in `[-1,1]`.

Set `zbar=(1/t)integral_0^t z(s) ds`.  The preceding dichotomy gives
`zbar_i in Sgn((Dx(t))_i)`.  Integration of the evolution equation now gives

```text
(x^0-x(t))/t=D^T zbar in partial J(x(t)).
```

Equivalently,

```text
0 in x(t)-x^0+t partial J(x(t)),
```

which is the necessary and sufficient optimality condition for the ROF
functional.  Its quadratic term is strictly convex, so the minimizer is
unique.  At `t=0` the identity is immediate.  This completes the proof.  QED.

## Corrections or missing assumptions

None inside the frozen model.  The path and Euclidean-normalization hypotheses
are essential scope restrictions, not removable decoration.

## Open risks

- ROF/flow equivalence is not promoted to a theorem on general graphs; the
  literature explicitly distinguishes the one-dimensional path situation
  from general graph behavior.
- The finite exact enumeration does not prove any of the all-real statements.
- No primitive-orbit, arithmetic determinant, quantum lift, or Route-B claim
  follows from convex-flow solvability.

## Route-A result

The only periodic solutions are the constant fixed points because every datum
reaches consensus in finite forward time.  There are no intrinsic prime
carriers, prime-power repetitions, dynamical Euler product, target divisor,
functional equation, or same-clock unitary lift.  The strict result is
`(A0_FAIL,A1_FAIL,A2_FAIL,A3_FAIL,A4_FAIL)`, overall
