\section{The finite Weil character}
\label{sec:weil}

\subsection{Convention and honest representation}

Fix an odd prime \(p\).  In standard coordinates let
\[
V=\F_p^2\oplus(\F_p^2)^\vee,
\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_2\\-I_2&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\psi_p(a)=e^{2\pi i\widetilde a/p}.
\]
For \(v=(x,\xi)\), use the Weyl operator
\[
[\pi(x,\xi)f](t)
=\psi_p\!\left(\xi^T(t+x/2)\right)f(t+x)
\quad\text{on }\C^{\F_p^2}.
\]
The canonical intertwining construction gives an honest representation
\[
\rho_p:\Sp(4,\F_p)\longrightarrow U(p^2)
\]
satisfying
\[
\rho_p(g)\pi(v)\rho_p(g)^{-1}=\pi(gv).
\]
This is a representation of \(\Sp(4,\F_p)\), not a choice of signs on a
double cover \citep{gurevich2009}.  The central element \(-I\) acts by
parity and cannot be discarded by passing to the projective symplectic group.

More generally, let \(J_*\) be any frozen unimodular alternating form and
choose an integral Darboux matrix \(T_*\) satisfying
\(T_*^T J_*T_*=J\).  We pull the representation back by
\begin{equation}
\rho_{p,J_*}(g):=\rho_p(T_*^{-1}gT_*),
\qquad g\in\Sp(J_*,\F_p).
\label{eq:rho-pullback}
\end{equation}
Changing the Darboux basis conjugates all fibre operators and leaves their
characters and fibre polynomials unchanged.  For the C25/C26 cocycle we take
\(J_*=J_0\) and the fixed matrix \(T\) from \cref{sec:source}; the C24
controls use their displayed form \(J_{24}\).  Whenever the source frame is
clear, we abbreviate
\[
\Theta_p(g):=\tr\rho_{p,J_*}(g).
\]

\subsection{Thomas's quotient discriminant}

Let \(g\in\Sp(J_*,\F_p)\), set \(H_g=g-I\), and write
\[
k_g=\dim\ker H_g.
\]
On \(V/\ker H_g\), define
\[
\sigma_g(\bar v,\bar w)
=\langle H_gv,w\rangle_{J_*}.
\]
If the columns of \(C\) represent a basis of the quotient, then
\[
d_g=\det(C^TH_g^TJ_*C)\in\F_p^\times
\]
is defined up to a square.  Let \(\eta_p\) be the Legendre character and
\[
G_p=\sum_{x\in\F_p}\psi_p(x^2/2),
\qquad G_p^2=\eta_p(-1)p.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Thomas character formula \citealp{thomas2008}]
\label{thm:thomas}
With the preceding convention,
\[
\Theta_p(g):=\tr\rho_{p,J_*}(g)
=p^{k_g/2}\gamma_p(1)^{4-k_g}\eta_p(d_g),
\qquad
\gamma_p(1)=p^{-1/2}G_p.
\]
Equivalently, every character lies in the exact quadratic span
\(\Z+\Z G_p\) and is given by
\begin{equation}
\Theta_p(g)=
\begin{cases}
\eta_p(d_g),&k_g=0,\\
\eta_p(d_g)\eta_p(-1)G_p,&k_g=1,\\
\eta_p(d_g)\eta_p(-1)p,&k_g=2,\\
\eta_p(d_g)pG_p,&k_g=3,\\
p^2,&k_g=4.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:character-table}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The table yields three strong implementation identities:
\begin{equation}
\Theta_p(I)=p^2,
\qquad
|\Theta_p(g)|^2=p^{k_g}=|\ker(g-I)|,
\qquad
\Theta_p(g^{-1})=\overline{\Theta_p(g)}.
\label{eq:character-checks}
\end{equation}
In particular, the full Weil character never vanishes.  Character values are
conjugacy invariant but not multiplicative.  An orbit weight must therefore
be \(\Theta_p(g_w)\), never a product of edge or branch characters.

\subsection{Good primes and quadratic fields}

The character simplifies strongly away from the fixed-vector locus.

\begin{proposition}[Good-prime quadratic law]
\label{prop:goodprime}
Let \(J_*\) be a unimodular integral alternating form, let
\(g\in\Sp(J_*,\Z)\), and let
\(D_g=\det(g-I)\ne0\).  For every odd prime \(p\nmid D_g\),
\[
\boxed{\Theta_p(g\bmod p)=\Leg{D_g}p.}
\]
The same statement holds for \(g^r\) whenever
\(p\nmid\det(g^r-I)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The condition \(p\nmid D_g\) gives \(k_g=0\).  In full quotient coordinates,
the determinant of \(\sigma_g\) differs from \(\det(g-I)\) by the determinant
of the unimodular alternating form, which is a square.  The \(k=0\) line of
\cref{eq:character-table} gives the claim.
\end{proof}

Thus a fixed orbit determines a genuine quadratic character through the
squarefree part of \(D_g\).  The primes dividing \(D_g\) are precisely where
the fixed-space dimension and quotient discriminant can add Gauss-sum data.
The simplicity is also a warning: at every good prime a
\(p^2\)-dimensional character has collapsed to one bit, \(+1\) or \(-1\).

\subsection{Complete finite-fibre polynomial}

The first character alone does not encode repetitions.  Define
\[
P_{p,g}(T)=\det(I-T\rho_{p,J_*}(g)).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
-\log P_{p,g}(T)
=\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{\Theta_p(g^r)}rT^r.
\label{eq:local-log}
\end{equation}
Because the degree is \(p^2\), the first \(p^2\) traces determine the
polynomial.  Writing \(P_{p,g}(T)=\sum_{m=0}^{p^2}c_mT^m\), Newton's recursion
is
\begin{equation}
c_0=1,
\qquad
c_m=-\frac1m\sum_{j=1}^m c_{m-j}\Theta_p(g^j).
\label{eq:newton}
\end{equation}
All computations can be performed exactly in \(\Q(G_p)\).  The determinant
character of \(\rho_p\) is trivial in genus two, so \(c_{p^2}=-1\); unitarity
also gives
\[
c_m=-\overline{c_{p^2-m}}.
\]
These identities are used as release tests.  The polynomial
\(P_{p,g}\) is the complete local invariant of the finite fibre.  It is not,
by itself, the full AGY orbit factor, which also contains the scalar Perron
and stability terms in \cref{eq:scalaratom}.
