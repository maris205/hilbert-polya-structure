\section{Route-A implications and claim boundary}
\label{sec:routea}

infinite-oscillator realization: the target operator itself now has an
ordinary determinant.  It is nevertheless not a global Riemann dynamical
determinant.  \Cref{tab:routea} separates the four evaluation layers.

\paragraph{Why the arithmetic is real.}
No prime table is used to choose a path, clock, or parameter.  Once a word is
fixed, \cref{prop:goodprime} produces a bona fide quadratic character, and
singular primes add exact Gauss phases.  These weights are intrinsic to the
finite reduction of the cocycle.

\paragraph{Why the arithmetic is insufficient.}
The good-prime character depends only on \(D_w\) up to square class.  The
observed words generate different square classes rather than a shared
conductor.  The complete \(p=43\) fibre collision and
\cref{thm:integral-conjugacy} show that even all repetitions can lose order
information.  More computation at neighboring primes would refine this
description but would not create a global assembly law.

\paragraph{The next large gate.}
A continuation of this route is justified only if the dynamics itself
selects an adelic measure or trace for the family \(\{\rho_p\}\), proves the
convergence of the resulting determinant, and explains how orbit-dependent
quadratic characters combine without changing the AGY clock.  Multiplying
the \(D_p\) by hand, taking an uncontrolled direct sum, or fitting prime
weights would violate the source lock.  If no intrinsic assembly appears,
the appropriate next move is to change the dynamical form rather than extend
the finite scan.

No statement in this paper matches Riemann zeros, proves a functional
equation, constructs a self-adjoint operator with the zeta ordinates, or
advances the Riemann hypothesis.  The contribution is a fixed-prime
determinant theorem together with exact sensitivity and collapse mechanisms.
