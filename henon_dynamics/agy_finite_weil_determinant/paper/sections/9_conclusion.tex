\section{Conclusion}

Finite Weil fibres solve the immediate operator-theoretic problem left by the
infinite oscillator: for each fixed odd prime, the source-locked AGY transfer
operator has a genuine trace-class twist and an ordinary Fredholm
determinant.  Its periodic weights combine the scalar Perron atom with an
exact Legendre--Gauss character, and complete finite-fibre polynomials detect
the frozen three-return chronology at \(p=3,5,7\).

The same experiment also identifies the boundary of that success.  A
complete period at \(p=43\) makes the finite fibre blind to the C26 order
change even though the scalar matrices remain spectrally distinct.  An
explicit integral symplectic conjugacy produces a stronger all-prime,
all-repetition collapse for two distinct C24 symbolic cycles.  Meanwhile,
150 tested AGY branches yield 150 quadratic signatures rather than a common
arithmetic conductor.

The fixed-prime family is therefore a natural arithmetic quantization, not a
global Hilbert--P\'olya operator.  The next credible step must derive a
canonical global prime assembly from the dynamics and prove its analytic
control.  Without that mechanism, extending the same small-prime census is a
small door; the research programme should move to a different dynamical
architecture.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The release includes the exact producer, an independent implementation that
does not import it, all coefficient arrays, mutation tests, source hashes,
and a one-command replay.  All mathematical decisions use integer, rational,
or finite-field arithmetic.
