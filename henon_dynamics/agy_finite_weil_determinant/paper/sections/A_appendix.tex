\section{Exact quotient computation}
\label{app:quotient}

Let \(H=g-I\) over \(\F_p\), and choose pivot columns
\(i_1,\ldots,i_r\) of \(H\).  The corresponding standard vectors
\(e_{i_1},\ldots,e_{i_r}\) project to a basis of
\(V/\ker H\): a linear combination lies in \(\ker H\) exactly when the same
combination of the independent columns of \(H\) vanishes.  If
\(C=(e_{i_1},\ldots,e_{i_r})\), the quotient matrix is
\[
Q_g=C^TH^TJC.
\]
Thomas's form is nondegenerate, so \(\det Q_g\ne0\).  Replacing \(C\) by
another quotient basis sends \(Q_g\) to \(R^TQ_gR\), multiplying the
determinant by \((\det R)^2\).  Its Legendre sign is therefore well defined.

The independent checker reimplements the pivot-column quotient construction
with immutable tuple matrices and its own modular elimination.  Independence
here means a separate arithmetic engine and source replay, not a claim that a
different quotient-basis theorem is being used.

\section{Arithmetic in the \((1,G_p)\) basis}

Write an exact value as \(a+bG_p\), where
\(G_p^2=\eta_p(-1)p\).  Multiplication and complex conjugation are
\[
(a,b)(c,d)
=(ac+bd\,\eta_p(-1)p,\ ad+bc),
\]
\[
\overline{(a,b)}=(a,\eta_p(-1)b).
\]
Newton's recursion \cref{eq:newton} may introduce rational pairs in this
basis even though the resulting coefficients are algebraic integers; the
quadratic basis need not be an integral basis.  The release therefore stores
reduced rational numerators and denominators rather than coercing each
coefficient to an integer.

\section{Frozen chronology witnesses}

The positive prefix is
\[
\gamma_*=t^{64}(tbttbtbb)^8,
\qquad |\gamma_*|=128.
\]
Let \(\gamma_2=\gamma_*\,bttbtbb\,\gamma_*\) and
\(\gamma_3=\gamma_*\,bbb\,\gamma_*\).  The forward three-return product is
\[
g_{\rm fwd}=g_{\gamma_3}g_{\gamma_2}g_{\gamma_*},
\]
whereas the tested noncyclic reverse is
\[
g_{\rm rev}=g_{\gamma_*}g_{\gamma_2}g_{\gamma_3}.
\]
The two-return products
\(g_{\gamma_2}g_{\gamma_*}\) and
\(g_{\gamma_*}g_{\gamma_2}\) are the mandatory trace-cyclicity control.
All matrices are reconstructed from labeled Rauzy edges; the release does not
hand-enter the large three-return matrices.

\section{Complete-period logic}

Suppose \(a,b\) in a finite group both satisfy \(a^N=b^N=I\).  If
\(\Theta(a^r)=\Theta(b^r)\) for \(1\le r\le N\), then equality holds for
all integer \(r\) by reduction modulo \(N\).  At \(p=43\), exact modular
multiplication gives \(N=925\) and no character difference in the full
period.  This converts a finite computation into an all-power theorem.  By
contrast, the absence of a difference for \(p=83\) and \(p=89\) through
\(r=24\) has no such status; differences appear later at 41 and 30.

\section{Independent replay and release artifacts}

From the project directory, the complete replay command is
\begin{verbatim}
bash code/run_c27.sh
\end{verbatim}
It performs the following steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item regenerate the exact certificate from the C24--C26 source locks;
\item run the independent checker;
\item execute chronology and scope mutation tests;
\item verify the regenerated artifacts against the frozen SHA-256 release
manifest.
\end{enumerate}

The primary certificate contains all local-polynomial coefficients, every
power-pattern class for odd \(p\le97\), the complete \(p=43\) period record,
the integral symplectic conjugator, and all 150 arithmetic-scan rows.  Finite
enumerations carry explicit flags preventing their promotion to all-length
theorems.

\section{Claim firewall}

The release excludes \(p=2\), edgewise metaplectic signs over finite fields,
branchwise products of characters, absolute-value replacements, parity-sector
substitution for the full fibre, chronological averaging, and use of
\(B^T\) as the forward homology cocycle.  It also excludes an unproved direct
sum or product over primes.  No Riemann zeros or prime tables are read,
trained on, or scored.
