\section{What a genuine H\"older test must prove}

Liv\v{s}ic's theorem characterizes cohomological nullity through periodic
orbit sums for hyperbolic and topological Markov systems
\citep{Livshits1972}.  Its use here requires a logical distinction: periodic
sums determine the cohomology class of an observable that already exists;
they do not assert that an arbitrary assignment \(\gamma\mapsto\Egal_\gamma\)
comes from a H\"older observable.

For the quantitative statement, pass explicitly to the one-sided H6
presentation \(\Sigma_A^+\).  Let
\(d_\vartheta(x,y)=\vartheta^{N_+(x,y)}\), with \(0<\vartheta<1\), where
\(N_+(x,y)\) is the length of the common initial word.  If \(\varphi\) is
H\"older with exponent \(\alpha\), its variation on a forward
\(m\)-cylinder is at most \(C\vartheta^{\alpha m}\).  This convention is
exactly the one measured by the forward cyclic block counts \(\Nblk_m\).

\begin{theorem}[quantitative higher-block gate]\label{thm:holder-gate}
Suppose \(\Egal_\gamma=\Birk_\gamma\varphi\) for every primitive cycle and
some \(\alpha\)-H\"older potential \(\varphi:\Sigma_A^+\to\mathbb R\).  If cycles \(\gamma_i\) and
integers \(c_i\) satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:m-relation}
 \sum_i c_i\Nblk_m(\gamma_i)=0,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:holder-discrepancy}
 \left|\sum_i c_i\Egal_{\gamma_i}\right|
 \leq C\vartheta^{\alpha m}
 \sum_i |c_i|\,|\gamma_i|.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose one representative in every nonempty \(m\)-cylinder and let
\(\varphi_m\) be the locally constant function taking the representative
value there.  Then
\(\|\varphi-\varphi_m\|_\infty\leq C\vartheta^{\alpha m}\).
The incidence relation kills
\(\sum_i c_i\Birk_{\gamma_i}\varphi_m\) by Lemma
\ref{lem:incidence}.  The remaining error on \(\gamma_i\) is at most
\(|\gamma_i|\|\varphi-\varphi_m\|_\infty\), and summation gives
\eqref{eq:holder-discrepancy}.
\end{proof}

Thus a general one-sided H\"older no-go cannot come from one finite relation at fixed
\(m\).  It requires a sequence of exact higher-block relations for which the
normalized excess discrepancy does not admit any exponential decay.  A
positive result would instead establish a compatible exponent and constant,
then still need an extension theorem producing one observable on all of
\(\SigmaA\).  Equation \eqref{eq:holder-discrepancy} is the correct
all-period gate for either direction in this presentation.  No claim about
an arbitrary two-sided H\"older assignment is obtained without an additional
cohomological reduction to a future-dependent representative.
