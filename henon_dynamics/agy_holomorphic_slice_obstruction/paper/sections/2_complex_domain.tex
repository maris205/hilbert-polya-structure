\section{Positive-prefix complexification}
\label{sec:complex-domain}

The common complex domain is our algebraic consequence of the published AGY
branch grammar and Rauzy chronology, not a formal complexification of real
contraction.  We keep the alphabet
size
\[
 d=4
\]
separate from the projective complex dimension \(q=d-1=3\).  Put
\[
 \ell(z)=\one^Tz,
 \qquad
 H_{\C}=\{z\in\C^d:\ell(z)=1\}\simeq\C^q,
 \qquad
 p_M(z)=\frac{Mz}{\ell(Mz)}.
\]
For the fixed neat strongly positive return path \(\gamma_*\), let
\[
 P=B_{\gamma_*}^T>0.
\]
AGY's Section~4.1.3 and Lemma~4.4 make each return either \(\gamma_*\) or a
minimal path \(\gamma_*\gamma_0\gamma_*\)\citep[pp.~162, 165]{agy2006}.
Later Rauzy arrows multiply on the left, so transposition places
\(B_{\gamma_*}^T\) on the left.  We therefore deduce
\begin{equation}
 A_\gamma:=B_\gamma^T=P C_\gamma,
 \qquad C_\gamma\geq0,
 \qquad h_\gamma=p_{A_\gamma}=p_P\circ p_{C_\gamma}.
 \label{eq:agy-factorization}
\end{equation}
Every \(C_\gamma\) is invertible and therefore has no zero row or column.
The direct real formulas are
\begin{equation}
 q_\gamma(x)=\ell(A_\gamma x),\qquad
 r_\gamma(x)=\log q_\gamma(x),\qquad
 j_\gamma(x)=q_\gamma(x)^{-d}.
 \label{eq:agy-roof-jacobian}
\end{equation}
The sign and exponent in \eqref{eq:agy-roof-jacobian} are fixed by the
published inverse-Jacobian formula~\citep{agy2006}.

Define the canonical complex section of the positive cone by
\begin{equation}
 \cD=
 \left\{z\in H_\C:
 \Re(z_i\overline{z_j})>0\text{ for every }i,j\right\}.
 \label{eq:complex-cone}
\end{equation}
Unlike a coordinatewise right half-plane alone, \(\cD\) is stable under
every nonnegative matrix relevant to \eqref{eq:agy-factorization}.

\begin{lemma}[Complex-cone geometry]
\label{lem:complex-cone}
The set \(\cD\) is a bounded connected domain containing the real positive
simplex.  If \(C\geq0\) is invertible, then
\[
 p_C(\cD)\subset\cD.
\]
If \(P>0\), then
\[
 p_P(\overline{\cD})\Subset\cD.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(z\in\cD\), the affine constraint gives
\[
 \Re z_i
 =\Re\!\left(z_i\overline{\sum_jz_j}\right)
 =\sum_j\Re(z_i\overline{z_j})>0.
\]
On \(\overline{\cD}\), all summands below are nonnegative and
\[
 1=|\ell(z)|^2
  =\sum_{i,j}\Re(z_i\overline{z_j})
  \geq |z_k|^2.
\]
Thus \(\overline{\cD}\) is bounded.  If
\(b=d^{-1}\one\), the segment \((1-t)z+tb\) remains in \(\cD\): expanding
each pairwise real product gives a positive combination of
\(\Re(z_i\bar z_j)\), \(\Re z_i\), \(\Re z_j\), and \(1\).  Hence \(\cD\)
is connected.

For \(C\geq0\), the absence of zero rows implies
\[
 \Re\bigl((Cz)_i\overline{(Cz)_j}\bigr)
 =\sum_{k,l}C_{ik}C_{jl}\Re(z_k\overline{z_l})>0.
\]
The coordinate sum of \(Cz\) cannot vanish, and division by that common
complex scalar preserves all signs.  This proves \(p_C(\cD)\subset\cD\).

Let now \(P>0\) and \(z\in\overline{\cD}\).  The same double sum is
nonnegative, while its diagonal part
\[
 \sum_kP_{ik}P_{jk}|z_k|^2
\]
is strictly positive.  Therefore \(p_P(z)\in\cD\).  The denominator stays
nonzero on the compact set \(\overline{\cD}\), so continuity yields the
compact containment \(p_P(\overline{\cD})\Subset\cD\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[One domain for the countable AGY family]
\label{thm:common-domain}
There is a bounded connected domain \(\Omega\subset H_\C\) such that
\begin{equation}
 p_P(\overline{\cD})\Subset\Omega\Subset\cD
 \label{eq:omega-gap}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \bigcup_{\gamma\in\Gamma}h_\gamma(\Omega)
 \subset p_P(\cD)\Subset\Omega.
 \label{eq:common-image}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The compact set \(K_P=p_P(\overline{\cD})\) is connected.  Choose a small
connected Euclidean neighborhood \(\Omega\) of \(K_P\) whose closure lies
in \(\cD\).  By \eqref{eq:agy-factorization} and
\Cref{lem:complex-cone},
\[
 h_\gamma(\Omega)
 =p_Pp_{C_\gamma}(\Omega)
 \subset p_P(\cD)
 \subset K_P\Subset\Omega
\]
for every branch.  The gap is independent of \(\gamma\), including limits
of the countable branch family.
\end{proof}

The enlargement in \eqref{eq:omega-gap} is essential.  The natural domain
\(p_P(\cD)\) contains the real AGY base, but the real branch images partition
that base modulo null sets and approach its boundary.  They cannot all lie
in one compact subset of the same natural domain.  The intermediate
\(\Omega\) contains the closure of the natural image and places that closure
strictly inside a larger holomorphic domain.

Real Hilbert contraction alone would not prove
\Cref{thm:common-domain}: a family of rational functions can contract one
real interval uniformly while poles approach it from imaginary directions.
Our proof avoids that failure because the matrices have the fixed positive
left factor deduced above and no branch-dependent poles on \(\cD\).  An explicit
one-dimensional counterexample and a matrix-normalization audit appear in
\Cref{app:complex-proofs}.
