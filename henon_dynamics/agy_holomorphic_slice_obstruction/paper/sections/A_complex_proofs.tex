\section{Complex-domain audits}
\label{app:complex-proofs}

\subsection{Why the natural image domain is too small}

Let \(\Delta\) be the real AGY base inside \(p_P(\cD)\).  The inverse
branch images \(h_\gamma(\Delta)\) form the full-branch partition of
\(\Delta\) modulo a null set.  Suppose that one compact set
\(K\Subset p_P(\cD)\) contained every complex branch image of that same
domain.  Its real intersection would contain every
\(h_\gamma(\Delta)\).  Since \(K\cap\Delta\) stays a positive distance from
the relative boundary, it cannot cover the positive-measure open region of
\(\Delta\) near that boundary.  This contradicts the full-branch
partition.  Therefore one must enlarge past
\(\overline{p_P(\cD)}\), as in \eqref{eq:omega-gap}; uniform compact
containment on the natural domain itself is false.

\subsection{Real contraction need not complexify uniformly}

The fixed positive prefix cannot be replaced by an abstract appeal to real
Hilbert contraction.  Let \(\eps_n\downarrow0\) and consider
\[
 f_n(z)=\frac12+\frac14\left(z-\frac12\right)
 +\frac{\eps_n^4}{(z-\frac12)^2+\eps_n^2}.
\]
For real \(z\in(0,1)\), the last term lies in \((0,\eps_n^2]\), and its
derivative has magnitude \(O(\eps_n)\).  After discarding finitely many
indices, all \(f_n\) map \((0,1)\) into one fixed compact subinterval and
have derivative bounded by a constant strictly below one.  They are
therefore uniformly contractive for both Euclidean and Hilbert metrics on a
fixed real subinterval.  Nevertheless, \(f_n\) has poles at
\(1/2\pm i\eps_n\).  No common complex neighborhood of \((0,1)\) supports
the full family.  In \Cref{thm:common-domain}, the nonnegative matrix
factorization prevents this pole degeneration.

\subsection{Boundary-degenerate normalized matrices}

For numerical estimates one may divide a branch matrix by its total entry
sum,
\[
 \widehat A_\gamma
 =\frac{A_\gamma}{\sum_{i,j}(A_\gamma)_{ij}}.
\]
The normalized family lies in the compact simplex of nonnegative matrices.
Its limits may lose columns or rank.  This does not threaten the common
denominator: if \(x_j\geq\delta>0\) on a real compact set, then
\[
 \ell(\widehat A_\gamma x)
 =\sum_j\left(\sum_i\widehat A_{\gamma,ij}\right)x_j
 \geq\delta.
\]
The fixed factor \(P>0\) prevents projective outputs from approaching the
boundary of the full positive cone.  This calculation explains why the
exact coordinate margin \eqref{eq:delta-prefix} is a useful mutation
sentinel.  It does not authorize replacing the raw integral matrix in the
roof or trace formula: scaling \(A_\gamma\) changes
\(q_\gamma^{-(s+d)}\).

\subsection{Complex powers and word telescoping}

The algebraic identity
\[
 q_A(z)q_B(p_Az)=q_{BA}(z)
\]
does not by itself justify multiplication of nonintegral complex powers on
an arbitrary domain.  On \(\Omega\Subset\cD\), every partial composite has
positive real denominator, so the principal logarithms are compatible.
Equivalently, the two holomorphic logarithmic expressions agree on the real
positive simplex and hence throughout the connected domain.  The proof of
\Cref{thm:perron-trace} uses the still safer periodic-point argument: every
intermediate normalizer is positive real, and its product is exactly the
Perron eigenvalue.

\subsection{An equivalent tubular construction}

The complex-cone domain is the cleanest global construction.  A local audit
can instead complexify the Hilbert Finsler norm on
\(K=\overline\Delta\Subset\{x_i>0\}\).  Uniform real contraction and the
total-entry normalization above give a Taylor estimate
\[
 \rho(h_\gamma z)
 \leq q\rho(z)+C\rho(z)^2,
 \qquad q<1,
\]
where \(\rho\) is the distance to \(K\) in a uniformly equivalent family of
complexified Hilbert norms.  A sufficiently small sublevel set then maps
into a strictly smaller one.  This alternative proves existence but hides
the stronger algebraic fact in \eqref{eq:common-image}: the entire common
image is forced by \(P\), independently of branchwise Taylor constants.
