\section{The infinite oscillator obstruction}
\label{sec:oscillator}

Let \(\cF=L^2(\R^2)\), and let
\(U_\gamma=\mu(\widetilde g_\gamma)\) be the oscillator representation of
the pathwise metaplectic lift.  Edge lifts are composed in true time order;
the central sign is never averaged.  The Weyl representation, metaplectic
covariance, and the Schr\"odinger--Bargmann equivalence used below are
standard~\citep{folland1989}.  Define the literal vector-valued transfer
operator by
\begin{equation}
 (\cL_s^{\rm Mp}F)(z)
 =\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
 w_{s,\gamma}(z)U_\gamma F(h_\gamma z).
 \label{eq:twisted-transfer}
\end{equation}

The noncompactness mechanism applies beyond Bergman space, provided the
candidate has two bounded slices.

\begin{theorem}[Evaluation-slice obstruction]
\label{thm:evaluation-slice}
Let \(X\) be a Banach space of \(\cF\)-valued functions on a set containing
a real interior point \(x_0\).  Assume that
\begin{enumerate}
 \item the constant embedding
 \(J:\cF\to X\), \((Jv)(x)=v\), is bounded;
 \item evaluation \(E_{x_0}:X\to\cF\) is bounded; and
 \item \eqref{eq:twisted-transfer} defines a bounded operator on \(X\).
\end{enumerate}
Suppose also that distinct AGY return branches have distinct projected
symplectic matrices.  Then, for every \(s\in\halfplane\),
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \|\cL_s^{\rm Mp}\|_{\ess}
 \geq
 \frac{\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
 |w_{s,\gamma}(x_0)|^2\right)^{1/2}}
 {\|E_{x_0}\|\,\|J\|}>0.}
 \label{eq:evaluation-bound}
\end{equation}
In particular, \(\cL_s^{\rm Mp}\) is noncompact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The real branch estimate \eqref{eq:real-branch-sum} gives absolute
coefficient summability, and evaluation on constants yields the exact
full-family compression
\begin{equation}
 E_{x_0}\cL_s^{\rm Mp}J
 =\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}w_{s,\gamma}(x_0)U_\gamma.
 \label{eq:fibre-compression}
\end{equation}
An inherited all-length decoder proves that the fixed-start projected
matrices are pairwise distinct.  The discrete metaplectic atom theorem,
restated and proved in the form needed here in
\Cref{lem:discrete-atoms}, gives
\[
 \|E_{x_0}\cL_s^{\rm Mp}J\|_{\ess}
 \geq
 \left(\sum_\gamma|w_{s,\gamma}(x_0)|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
If \(K:X\to X\) is compact, then \(E_{x_0}KJ\) is compact and
\[
 \|E_{x_0}(\cL_s^{\rm Mp}-K)J\|
 \leq\|E_{x_0}\|\,\|J\|\,
 \|\cL_s^{\rm Mp}-K\|.
\]
Taking the infimum over compact \(K\) proves
\eqref{eq:evaluation-bound}.  Every coefficient is nonzero, so the lower
bound is strictly positive.
\end{proof}

The theorem uses no holomorphic branch localizer.  It applies to a literal
bounded realization on a point-evaluative Hardy, Bergman,
\(H^\infty\), or reproducing-kernel space whenever constants belong
continuously.  It does not cover a non-tensor anisotropic distribution space
for which evaluation or every fibre slice is unbounded.  This explicit
hypothesis boundary is consistent with general vector-valued composition
theory, where compactness depends on the operator weight and not only on the
base map~\citep{bonet2012}.

\begin{theorem}[Same-domain dichotomy]
\label{thm:same-domain}
On
\[
 \cH_\cF=A^2(\Omega;\cF)
 \simeq A^2(\Omega)\widehat\otimes\cF,
\]
the series in \eqref{eq:twisted-transfer} converges absolutely in operator
norm for every \(s\in\halfplane\), but its sum is noncompact.  Thus
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 L_s:A^2(\Omega)\to A^2(\Omega)
 \text{ is trace class},\qquad
 \cL_s^{\rm Mp}:\cH_\cF\to\cH_\cF
 \text{ is noncompact}.}
 \label{eq:same-domain-dichotomy}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(K_P=p_P(\overline\cD)\Subset\Omega\).  Point evaluations on scalar
and vector-valued Bergman space are uniformly bounded on \(K_P\).  Since
every branch image lies in \(K_P\),
\[
 \|w_{s,\gamma}U_\gamma(F\circ h_\gamma)\|_{A^2}
 \leq C_{\Omega,P}\|w_{s,\gamma}\|_\infty
 \|F\|_{A^2}.
\]
Unitarity removes \(U_\gamma\) from this norm estimate, and
\Cref{lem:complex-weight-sum} makes the operator series absolutely
convergent.  Constants and interior evaluation are bounded on Bergman space,
so \Cref{thm:evaluation-slice} proves noncompactness.  The scalar assertion
is \Cref{thm:scalar-trace-class}.
\end{proof}

For the usual volume normalization,
\[
 \|J\|=\vol(\Omega)^{1/2},
 \qquad
 \|E_{x_0}\|=K_\Omega(x_0,x_0)^{1/2},
\]
where \(K_\Omega\) is the scalar Bergman kernel.  The obstruction is
therefore quantitative on the domain that supports the scalar determinant.

The conclusion concerns ordinary compact and nuclear operator theory.
The character of the infinite-dimensional Weil representation is a
distributional representation character, with a regular-locus expression,
not the Hilbert trace of an isolated unitary~\citep{thomas2008}.  Likewise,
the oscillator semigroup studied by \citet{hilgert1989} imposes analytic
semigroup geometry in the fibre.  Complexifying the AGY base does not place
its real symplectic cocycle in that semigroup.  A Bargmann transform only
unitarily changes the oscillator model and cannot remove
\eqref{eq:evaluation-bound}.  Heat damping would change the candidate unless
it were forced by a separately proved cocycle law.

Noncompactness excludes trace class, every finite Schatten class, and the
ordinary nuclear Fredholm determinant for \(\cL_s^{\rm Mp}\).  It does not
assert that \(I-u\cL_s^{\rm Mp}\) is never Fredholm, and it does not exclude
a separately constructed flat, distributional, or semifinite determinant.
Most importantly, no factor such as \(\tr U_{\boldsymbol\beta}\) may be
inserted into \eqref{eq:perron-atom}: that ordinary trace does not exist, and
the metaplectic lift sign is invisible to \(\chi_{\boldsymbol\beta}\).
