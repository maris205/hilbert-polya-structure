\begin{abstract}
For the two-frequency Pais--Uhlenbeck oscillator we prove an exact
Ostrogradsky normal form and classify every classical orbit closure.  A
rational frequency ratio closes every orbit; an irrational ratio leaves only
the equilibrium and single-mode circles periodic, while each genuine
double-mode orbit is dense on a two-torus.  We then resolve the
equal-frequency Jordan collision and all real sign degenerations.
\ifnum\CRevisionRound>1
Finally, the natural distinct-positive-frequency quantization is defined on
its maximal Hermite diagonal domain.  Rational ratios give an eigenvalue
lattice of infinite multiplicity, whereas irrational ratios give simple,
dense eigenvalues, pure-point spectral measures, and spectrum \(\R\).
\fi
Finite exact receipts audit conventions; the continuum statements are proved
analytically.
\end{abstract}

\section{Frozen model and canonical sign}
Consider
\begin{equation}
 L=\frac12\{\ddot x^2-(\omega _1^2+\omega _2^2)\dot x^2
 +\omega _1^2\omega _2^2x^2\},\qquad 0<\omega _1<\omega _2.
\end{equation}
The higher-order Euler--Lagrange equation factors as
\begin{equation}
 (D^2+\omega _1^2)(D^2+\omega _2^2)x=0.
\end{equation}
With \(q_0=x,q_1=\dot x,p_1=\ddot x\) and
\(p_0=-(\omega _1^2+\omega _2^2)\dot x-x^{(3)}\), the Ostrogradsky
Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
 H=p_0q_1+\frac12p_1^2+\frac12(\omega _1^2+\omega _2^2)q_1^2
 -\frac12\omega _1^2\omega _2^2q_0^2.
\end{equation}
Put \(\Delta=\omega _2^2-\omega _1^2\) and define
\begin{align}
 Q_1&=(p_1+\omega _2^2q_0)/\sqrt\Delta,&
 P_1&=(p_0+\omega _1^2q_1)/\sqrt\Delta,\nonumber\\
 Q_2&=(p_1+\omega _1^2q_0)/\sqrt\Delta,&
 P_2&=-(p_0+\omega _2^2q_1)/\sqrt\Delta.
\end{align}
Direct Poisson-bracket calculation gives
\(\{Q_j,P_k\}=\delta_{jk}\) and zero cross brackets.  Substitution gives
\begin{equation}
 H=-\frac12(P_1^2+\omega _1^2Q_1^2)
 +\frac12(P_2^2+\omega _2^2Q_2^2).
\end{equation}
Thus our convention has a negative low-frequency mode and a positive
high-frequency mode.  This explicit check is the \emph{canonical-sign owner};
changing either sign changes the theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Complete classical closure]
In the chamber above, the linear Hamiltonian flow is complete.  If
\(\omega _1/\omega _2\in\mathbb Q\), every trajectory is periodic.  If the
ratio is irrational, exactly the equilibrium and single-mode trajectories
are periodic, and each double-mode trajectory is dense on its invariant
two-torus.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation (2) has the general solution
\begin{equation}
x= a_1\cos\omega _1t+b_1\sin\omega _1t
  +a_2\cos\omega _2t+b_2\sin\omega _2t.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, (5) preserves the two modal radii
\(R_j^2=Q_j^2+P_j^2/\omega_j^2\).  If
\(\omega _1=gm,\omega _2=gn\) with coprime positive \(m,n\), then
\(T=2\pi/g\) advances the two phases by \(2\pi m,2\pi n\).  It is a
common period; a collapsed one-mode circle can have a smaller least period.

For irrational ratio, a periodic double-mode point would require both
\(\omega_jT\in2\pi\mathbb Z\), contradicting irrationality.  Single-mode
circles retain period \(2\pi/\omega_j\).  Kronecker's theorem makes the
two-frequency phase orbit dense in \(S^1\times S^1\), and the invertible map
(4) carries that closure to the Ostrogradsky torus.  Constant coefficients
give completeness for all real time.
\end{proof}

\section{Collision and sign boundaries}

The normal map (4) is singular at collision, so the boundary must be solved
from (2), not inferred by continuity.

\begin{proposition}[Complete real factor atlas]
For the real-factor equation \((D^2+\alpha)(D^2+\beta)x=0\), the distinct
positive chamber is supplemented by the following exhaustive faces, up to
interchange of \(\alpha,\beta\).
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(\alpha=\beta=\omega^2>0\), then
\begin{equation}
x=(a+bt)\cos\omega t+(c+dt)\sin\omega t.
\end{equation}
The first-order matrix has one size-two Jordan block at each of
\(\pm i\omega\); periodicity holds exactly when \(b=d=0\).
\item If \(\alpha=0<\beta=\omega^2\), the basis is
\(1,t,\cos\omega t,\sin\omega t\); boundedness and periodicity hold exactly
when the coefficient of \(t\) vanishes.  If \(\alpha=\beta=0\), the basis is
\(1,t,t^2,t^3\), and only its constant subspace is bounded or periodic.
\item A negative factor \(-\nu^2\) contributes
\(e^{\nu t},e^{-\nu t}\).  Pairing it with a positive factor, a zero factor,
or a distinct negative factor supplies the corresponding direct union of
bases.  At the repeated negative face, the basis is
\(e^{\nu t},te^{\nu t},e^{-\nu t},te^{-\nu t}\).
Every nonzero hyperbolic component is unbounded in at least one time
direction.  Hence the bounded/periodic subspace is the oscillatory plane for
positive/negative factors, the constant line for zero/negative factors, and
zero for two negative factors, repeated or distinct.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each item is the kernel basis of its factored constant-coefficient
polynomial.  At the positive collision the characteristic polynomial is
\((\lambda^2+\omega^2)^2\), while the nullity of the first-order matrix at
each root \(\pm i\omega\) is one.  Algebraic multiplicity two and geometric
multiplicity one force the asserted length-two Jordan blocks.  The listed
positive, zero, and negative cases exhaust both real factors.
\end{proof}

This direct calculation is the \emph{Jordan-boundary owner}.  It prevents the
imaginary repeated roots from being misreported as a diagonalizable stable
oscillator.

\section{Natural quantum difference operator}
Set \(\hbar=1\).  Let \(h_\omega=(-\partial_Q^2+\omega^2Q^2)/2\), and let
\(\phi_n^{(\omega)}\) be its normalized Hermite basis.  On
\(e_{n_1,n_2}=\phi_{n_1}^{(\omega_1)}\otimes\phi_{n_2}^{(\omega_2)}\), set
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_{n_1,n_2}=\omega _2(n_2+\tfrac12)-\omega _1(n_1+\tfrac12).
\end{equation}
Define \(\widehat H\) on the maximal diagonal domain
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D(\widehat H)=\left\{\sum c_ne_n:
 (c_n)\in\ell^2(\mathbb N_0^2),\quad
 \sum |\lambda_n|^2|c_n|^2<\infty\right\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Difference-spectrum dichotomy]
The operator \(\widehat H=-h_{\omega_1}\otimes I+I\otimes h_{\omega_2}\)
with domain (9) is self-adjoint and unbounded in both directions.  If
\(\omega_1=gm,\omega_2=gn\) with \(\gcd(m,n)=1\), then
\begin{equation}
 \sigma(\widehat H)=g(\mathbb Z+(n-m)/2),
\end{equation}
and every eigenvalue has infinite multiplicity.  If the ratio is irrational,
all eigenvalues are simple and dense, \(\sigma(\widehat H)=\R\), and all
spectral measures are pure point.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Hermite transform identifies (9) with the maximal domain of multiplication
by a real sequence on \(\ell^2(\N_0^2)\), hence the operator is self-adjoint.
Finite coefficient arrays are a core.  Sending either level to infinity gives
the two unbounded directions.

In the rational case,
\(\lambda/g=nn_2-mn_1+(n-m)/2\).  Bézout gives an integer solution to
\(nv-mu=k\) for each \(k\in\mathbb Z\); adding \((n,m)L\) makes both indices
nonnegative and produces infinitely many solutions.  This proves (10) and
its multiplicities.

In the irrational case, equality of two eigenvalues would make the frequency
ratio rational unless both index differences vanish.  Irrational-rotation
density lets \(\omega_2n_2-\omega_1n_1\) approximate every real number, with
nonnegative indices chosen arbitrarily large.  Thus the diagonal values are
simple and dense and their closure, the operator spectrum, is \(\R\).  For
\(f=\sum c_ne_n\), the spectral measure is
\(\sum |c_n|^2\delta_{\lambda_n}\), proving pure-point type.  This is the
\emph{dense pure-point owner}: dense eigenvalues are not discrete spectrum.
\end{proof}
