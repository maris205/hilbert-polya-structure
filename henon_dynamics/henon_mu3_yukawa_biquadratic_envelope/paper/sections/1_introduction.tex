\section{Introduction and the integrated theorem}
\label{sec:introduction}

An equality of permutation characters can make two number fields
arithmetically equivalent without explaining how they sit inside their common
normal closure.  In particular, a Gassmann collision does not by itself
produce a common intermediate field, a third quadratic companion, or usable
primitive elements.  Those are embedding-sensitive questions: they depend on
the exact subgroups in the exact permutation action, not merely on abstract
group isomorphism types or equal character rows.  This paper resolves those
questions for one normal $\WE$-extension attached to the $27$ lines of a
cubic surface.

The construction begins with a fixed degree-$320$ Gassmann pair
$K^{\Hplus}$ and $K^{\Hminus}$.  We transport the second subgroup into a
common normalizer and obtain three index-two subgroups
$\Hplus,\Hzero,\Hthree$ of a group $N$ of order $324$.  Their common
intersection $J$ has order $81$, and
\[
  N/J\cong \Vfour.
\]
Reversing inclusions by Galois correspondence gives a field diamond
\[
 M=K^N\subset \Fplus,\Fzero,\Fthree\subset L=K^J.
\]
What turns this abstract diamond into an explicit construction is a set of
three integral line-coordinate carriers.  Quadratic carriers generate $M$
and $L$, while the new companion $\Fzero$ is generated by a cubic orbit sum.
The need for degree three is formal and exact: $\Hzero$ and $N$ have the same
orbits on points and unordered pairs, so their invariant spaces coincide in
total degree at most two.

Arithmetic equivalence and its relation to induced trivial characters go
back to the classical criterion of Perlis
\citep[Theorem~1, printed p.~351]{perlis1977equation}; Bosma and de Smit give
computational context and discuss invariants forced by the equality
\citep[printed p.~68]{bosmadesmit2002small}.  James's complete $\WE$ subgroup
calculation supplies the ambient figures of $350$ conjugacy classes, $339$
permutation-character profiles, and eleven collisions
\citep[\S3.1, printed p.~22]{james2022bayesian}.  The present claim is not a
new collision criterion and not a globally unique $\Vfour$ construction.  It
is the following exact conjunction for the fixed released action: among
those eleven collision buckets, the pair $301/303$ alone has normalizers
conjugate in $\WE$ with index two over both members, and that embedded
configuration supports the explicit field and arithmetic package below.
The classical resolvent framework used to prove primitivity is due to
Stauduhar and its computational invariant formulation to Fieker and Kl\"uners
\citep[pp.~981--984]{stauduhar1973determination}
\citep[\S\S1--2 and Remark~2.1]{fiekerklueners2014computation}.  Explicit
cubic-surface realizations of $\WE$ provide the broader geometric context
\citep{elsenhansjahnel2015moduli}.

We now state the result as one theorem.  The notation in its statement is
defined without software locators in \cref{sec:released-input}; all finite
tables needed to check it appear in the appendices.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit biquadratic fixed-field envelope]
\label{thm:integrated}
Let $K/\Q$ be the fixed normal extension of \cref{sec:released-input}, with
$G=\Gal(K/\Q)=\WE$ and $|G|=51840$.  Let $\Hplus$ and $\Hminus$ be the
displayed embedded Gassmann subgroups, let
$\Hthree=x\Hminus x^{-1}$ under the left label-map convention, and let
$N,\Hzero,J$ be the exact subgroups in \cref{sec:envelope}.  Put
\[
 M=K^N,\qquad F_i=K^{H_i}\quad(i\in\{+,0,3\}),\qquad L=K^J.
\]
Then the following assertions hold together.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.8em]
\item \emph{Lattice.}  The orders of $N,H_i,J$ are $324,162,81$;
$J=[N,N]$, $N/J\cong\Vfour$, every pair of the $H_i$ intersects in $J$,
and every pair generates $N$.  Consequently
\[
 [M:\Q]=160,\qquad [F_i:\Q]=320,\qquad [L:\Q]=640,
\]
$L/M$ is biquadratic, and $\Fplus,\Fzero,\Fthree$ are its three quadratic
subfields.

\item \emph{Closures and automorphisms.}  Every field in the diamond has
normal closure $K$.  Moreover
\[
 \Aut_{\Q}(M)=1,\qquad \Aut_{\Q}(F_i)\cong C_2,
 \qquad \Aut_{\Q}(L)\cong\Vfour.
\]
The field $\Fthree$ is a $\Q$-conjugate of the original embedded minus
field $K^{\Hminus}$.

\item \emph{Primitive generators.}  For the integral scaled line roots
$\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{27}$, the explicitly defined carriers
$\mu,\xi_0,\lambda$ satisfy
\[
 M=\Q(\mu),\qquad \Fzero=\Q(\xi_0),\qquad L=\Q(\lambda).
\]
Their formal stabilizer orders are $324$, $162$, and $81$, and the
corresponding orbit sizes are $160$, $320$, and $640$.  All orbit values are distinct after
reduction at the unramified completely split prime $p=692717$.

\item \emph{Formal invariant gap.}  The $\Hzero$- and $N$-orbit partitions
on points and unordered pairs agree: the point orbit has size $27$, and the
pair-orbit sizes are
\[
 27,27,54,81,162.
\]
Hence no commutative $\Q$-coefficient formal polynomial of total degree at
most two in the $27$ labelled variables has stabilizer exactly $\Hzero$.
The displayed $27$-term cubic carrier $\xi_0$ does.

\item \emph{Restricted uniqueness.}  Among the eleven frozen collision
buckets, $301/303$ is the only pair for which the two normalizers are
conjugate in $G$ and both normalizer indices over the corresponding
subgroups are equal to two.  No broader uniqueness of a $\Vfour$
configuration is asserted.

\item \emph{Characters and zeta functions.}  On all $25$ conjugacy
classes, the following equality holds in the rational permutation
representation ring:
\[
 [G/J]+2[G/N]=[G/\Hplus]+[G/\Hzero]+[G/\Hthree].
\]
It follows that
\[
 \zeta_L(s)\zeta_M(s)^2
 =\zeta_{\Fplus}(s)\zeta_{\Fzero}(s)\zeta_{\Fthree}(s)
 =\zeta_{\Fplus}(s)^2\zeta_{\Fzero}(s).
\]
The bracket equality is not an isomorphism of finite $G$-sets.

\item \emph{Global and relative arithmetic.}  With $A,B$ defined in
\cref{eq:A-definition,eq:B-definition}, the signatures and signed
discriminants are those in \cref{tab:theorem-dashboard}.  The exact finite
support is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \{&3,5,181,283,997,1801,2346241,\\
   &14932047182473291995860108491583652133938007263719\}.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The absolute norms of the relative discriminants over $M$ are
\[
 3^8,\quad3^{16},\quad3^8,\quad3^{32}
\]
for $\Fplus,\Fzero,\Fthree,L$, respectively.

\item \emph{Both retained local towers.}  The complete relative tables hold
for both retained possibilities $D_3=\Tom{140}$ and $D_3=\Tom{206}$, with
relative factor totals $(36,28,36,56)$ and $(18,14,18,28)$.  All relative
ramification lies above $3$; every ramified relative row has $e=2$ and
different exponent $d=1$, hence is tame.  Neither branch is selected.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The proof has four conceptual moves.  First, exact subgroup intersections,
normalizers, and cores give the field lattice, normal closures, and
automorphisms.  Second, stabilizers of three integral formal carriers are
upgraded to stabilizers of evaluated elements by split-prime noncollision.
Third, the elementary character relation for $\Vfour$ is inflated and
induced to $G$.  Finally, conductor and double-coset formulas compute global
and relative arithmetic independently in both local branches.  Finite
exhaustions discharge bounded lemmas; they do not replace these mathematical
implications.

Our contributions are therefore specific and falsifiable:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item we identify and prove the common-normalizer envelope under the exact
eleven-bucket predicate, including its $112/120$ near-miss;
\item we construct primitive integral generators of the three key levels and
prove the quadratic-to-cubic formal invariant gap;
\item we derive the rational character and zeta identities and compute the
complete signed global and relative discriminants; and
\item we prove the same tame relative-ramification conclusion from two
separate exhaustive $3$-adic towers without selecting between them.
\end{enumerate}
