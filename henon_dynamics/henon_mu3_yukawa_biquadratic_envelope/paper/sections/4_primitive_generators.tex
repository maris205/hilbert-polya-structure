\section{Primitive generators and the formal invariant gap}
\label{sec:primitive}

The Galois correspondence in \cref{sec:envelope} identifies the fields but
does not exhibit elements that generate them.  We now construct such elements
from the integral line roots.  The method is in the classical
stabilizer--resolvent tradition
\citep{stauduhar1973determination,fiekerklueners2014computation}; the exact
supports, the cubic obstruction, and the split witness are specific to the
present labelled action.

\subsection{Three formal carriers}

Retain $\eta_+$ and $\eta_3=x\eta_-$ from
\cref{eq:eta-pm,eq:left-transport}.  Let $\tau$ be the exact element of
$N\setminus\Hplus$ whose one-line array is displayed in
\cref{app:carrier-supports}.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \mu=\eta_++\tau(\eta_+),
 \qquad
 \lambda=\eta_++2\eta_3.
 \label{eq:mu-lambda}
\end{equation}
The coefficient $2$ in $\lambda$ records the two support colors and prevents
an element from interchanging them.  The first carrier has $81$ distinct
quadratic monomials with coefficient histogram $54$ of weight one and $27$
of weight two.  The second has $135$ monomials, $54$ of weight one and $81$
of weight two.

For the companion field, let $\cT_0$ be the following orbit of triples,
written without compressed decimal notation:
\begin{multline}
\cT_0=\{\{1,2,9\},\{1,13,18\},\{1,14,25\},\{2,10,14\},
\{2,18,26\},\{3,6,12\},\{3,11,23\},\\
\{3,15,19\},\{4,5,21\},\{4,7,24\},\{4,16,17\},
\{5,7,20\},\{5,16,27\},\{6,8,11\},\\
\{6,19,22\},\{7,17,27\},\{8,12,15\},\{8,19,23\},
\{9,10,13\},\{9,25,26\},\{10,18,25\},\\
\{11,15,22\},\{12,22,23\},\{13,14,26\},\{16,20,24\},
\{17,20,21\},\{21,24,27\}\}.
\label{eq:T0-expanded}
\end{multline}
The cubic carrier is
\begin{equation}
 \xi_0=\sum_{\{i,j,k\}\in\cT_0}\alpha_i\alpha_j\alpha_k.
 \label{eq:xi0}
\end{equation}
The complete set in \eqref{eq:T0-expanded} is the definition.  We retain the redundant
support hashes in \cref{tab:carrier-certificate} to catch transcription or
index-origin errors.

\subsection{Integrality and split-prime noncollision}

\begin{lemma}[Integral scaling]
\label{lem:integral-scaling}
Each $\alpha_i=L_0d_i$ is an algebraic integer.  Hence
$\mu,\xi_0,\lambda$ are algebraic integers.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substitute $d=y/L_0$ into
$g(d)=L_0d^{27}+b_{26}d^{26}+\cdots+b_0$ and multiply by $L_0^{26}$.  The
result is the monic integral equation
\[
 y^{27}+b_{26}y^{26}+L_0b_{25}y^{25}+\cdots
   +L_0^{25}b_1y+L_0^{26}b_0=0.
\]
It is satisfied by $y=L_0d_i$.  Integer polynomials in algebraic integers
are integral, which applies to all three finite sums.
\end{proof}

The weighted-carrier stabilizers are exact formal statements:
\begin{equation}
 \Stab_G(\mu)=N,\qquad \Stab_G(\xi_0)=\Hzero,\qquad
 \Stab_G(\lambda)=J
 \label{eq:formal-stabilizers}
\end{equation}
when the symbols $\alpha_i$ are treated as independently labelled variables.
This is an exhaustive assertion for all three carriers: for each of the
$51840$ elements of the durable labelled action, the canonical weighted
monomial dictionary is transported and compared with the original.  The
elements passing that equality test form exactly $N$ for $\mu$, exactly
$\Hzero$ for $\xi_0$, and exactly $J$ for $\lambda$, of orders
$324,162,81$.  Thus the calculation excludes a larger ambient stabilizer; it
does not merely show that the named subgroup fixes the carrier.  Structurally,
the two $\Hplus$-support translates in $\mu$ are exchanged by
$N/\Hplus$; unequal weights in $\lambda$ recover $\cS_+$ and $\cS_3$
separately; and the $27$ triples in $\xi_0$ form one $\Hzero$-orbit.  Formal
stabilizers still do not exclude equalities introduced by evaluation at the
actual roots.  The next lemma supplies that bridge.

\begin{lemma}[Split-prime noncollision]
\label{lem:split-noncollision}
Let $\theta$ be an integral polynomial in the labelled roots, fixed formally
by $H\le G$.  Suppose that, at a common unramified prime where all labelled
roots lie in the residue field, the $[G:H]$ values obtained from the formal
$G/H$ orbit of $\theta$ are pairwise distinct.  Then
\[
 \Stab_G(\theta)=H,\qquad \Q(\theta)=K^H.
\]
Its orbit product is the monic integral irreducible separable minimal
polynomial of degree $[G:H]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If two characteristic-zero integral conjugates were equal, their difference
would be the zero algebraic integer and would reduce to zero at every common
integral specialization.  This contradicts distinctness of the stated
residues.  Thus the evaluated orbit has at least $[G:H]$ elements.  Since
$H$ fixes $\theta$, it has at most that many elements.  Orbit--stabilizer
therefore gives equality and the exact stabilizer $H$.  Galois
correspondence gives $\Q(\theta)=K^H$.  The orbit product is fixed by $G$,
has rational integral coefficients, and contains each conjugate once; hence
it lies in $\Z[T]$ and is the minimal polynomial.  Characteristic zero gives
separability.
\end{proof}

The common witness is
\begin{equation}
 p=692717.
 \label{eq:split-prime}
\end{equation}
It lies outside \eqref{eq:released-support}.  The normalized eliminant is
squarefree and factors as $1^{27}$ modulo $p$; the $27$ roots are distinct,
the line equations vanish, and the reconstructed line graph carries the full
$51840$-element faithful action.  The unramified Frobenius fixes all $27$
labels, so faithfulness makes it the identity in $G$; hence $p$ splits
completely in $K$ and all orbit values reduce in the same residue field.
Complete evaluation gives respectively
$160$, $320$, and $640$ distinct values.  The exact product fingerprints are
as follows.

Applying \cref{lem:split-noncollision} to the three rows proves
\begin{equation}
 M=\Q(\mu),\qquad \Fzero=\Q(\xi_0),\qquad L=\Q(\lambda).

expanded characteristic-zero coefficient vectors or hashes for them.

\subsection{Why the companion first appears in degree three}

The cubic in \eqref{eq:xi0} is not merely the first successful item in a
coefficient search.  Its degree is forced by a complete invariant-space
argument in the formal coordinate ring.

\begin{proposition}[Coefficient-orbit lemma]
\label{prop:coefficient-orbit}
Let $R=\Q[X_1,\ldots,X_{27}]$ with $G$ permuting the variables.  If two
subgroups $H\le N\le G$ have the same orbit partitions on points and
unordered pairs, then
\[
 R_{\le2}^{H}=R_{\le2}^{N}.
\]
In particular, no formal polynomial of total degree at most two can have
stabilizer exactly $H$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every element of $R_{\le2}$ has a unique expansion in the monomial basis
\[
 1,\qquad X_i,\qquad X_i^2,\qquad X_iX_j\quad(i<j).
\]
An $H$-fixed polynomial has coefficients constant on $H$-orbits.  The linear
and square coefficients are indexed by points; the mixed coefficients are
indexed by unordered pairs.  If the two partitions agree with those for
$N$, each coefficient function is also constant on $N$-orbits.  Therefore
every $H$-fixed polynomial of degree at most two is $N$-fixed.  The reverse
inclusion follows from $H\le N$, proving equality.  An element whose
stabilizer is exactly $H$ would be $H$-fixed but not $N$-fixed, so none
exists.
\end{proof}

For $H=\Hzero$, exact orbit enumeration gives the data in
\cref{tab:invariant-gap}.

The two partitions agree as sets, not merely as multisets of sizes.  By
\cref{prop:coefficient-orbit}, no commutative $\Q$-coefficient formal
polynomial in the labelled variables of total degree at most two has exact
stabilizer $\Hzero$.  In contrast, the orbit sum \eqref{eq:xi0} has exact
stabilizer $\Hzero$ and is primitive by the split witness.

This conclusion is deliberately formal.  Evaluating $X_i\mapsto\alpha_i$
may impose algebraic relations among the roots, and we do not classify
expressions in the resulting quotient ring.  The theorem says precisely that
degree two cannot distinguish $\Hzero$ from $N$ at the level of commutative
formal coefficient orbits, while the displayed cubic does.
