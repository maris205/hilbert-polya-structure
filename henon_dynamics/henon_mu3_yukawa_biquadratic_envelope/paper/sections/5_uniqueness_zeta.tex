\section{Exhaustive uniqueness, characters, and zeta functions}
\label{sec:uniqueness-zeta}

The common-normalizer construction sits inside a known finite collision
census.  We first delimit exactly what is unique, and then use the resulting
$\Vfour$ lattice to obtain a representation-ring identity.

\subsection{The exact eleven-bucket predicate}

For an unordered collision bucket $\{a,b\}$ represented by embedded
subgroups $H_a,H_b\le G$, define the predicate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:uniqueness-predicate}
\begin{split}
\mathcal P(a,b):\quad &N_G(H_a)\text{ and }N_G(H_b)
     \text{ are conjugate in }G,\\
&[N_G(H_a):H_a]=[N_G(H_b):H_b]=2.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
No degree or target locator is included in $\mathcal P$.  The computation
starts from all $350$ subgroup conjugacy classes and their $339$ distinct
permutation-character profiles; it then evaluates \eqref{eq:uniqueness-predicate}
on every one of the eleven collision buckets recorded by James
\citep[\S3.1, printed p.~22]{james2022bayesian}.

\begin{proposition}[Restricted exhaustive uniqueness]
\label{prop:restricted-uniqueness}
Among the eleven collision buckets, $\mathcal P(a,b)$ holds exactly for
$\{a,b\}=\{301,303\}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each bucket, generate both exact subgroups, compute their normalizers in
the full $51840$-element group, test normalizer conjugacy in $G$, and divide
the two normalizer orders by the subgroup order.  The complete rows are in
\cref{tab:collision-census}.  Only the final row has conjugate normalizers
and index pair $[2,2]$.  The calculation contains no target-filtering step:
all eleven Boolean leaves are computed before the unique qualifying list is
formed.
\end{proof}

This count does not say that the envelope is a globally unique $\Vfour$
configuration.  The bucket $112/120$ is the instructive near-miss: its
normalizers are conjugate, and after transport the two order-$16$ groups
generate a group of order $32$ with intersection of order $8$.  Nevertheless
each normalizer has order $64$, so the normalizer indices are $[4,4]$ and
$\mathcal P(112,120)$ is false.  Dropping the index-two clause would therefore
support a false uniqueness sentence.

\subsection{The rational \texorpdfstring{$\Vfour$}{V4} relation}

Let $V=N/J\cong\Vfour$ and let $A_+,A_0,A_3$ be its three order-two
subgroups, the images of $\Hplus,\Hzero,\Hthree$.  In the rational
representation ring of $V$,
\begin{equation}
 [V/1]+2[V/V]=[V/A_+]+[V/A_0]+[V/A_3].
 \label{eq:V4-relation}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the regular character is the sum of the trivial character and the
three nontrivial one-dimensional characters.  Each two-point permutation
character $\Q[V/A_i]$ is the sum of the trivial character and the unique
nontrivial character with kernel $A_i$.  Both sides of
\eqref{eq:V4-relation} therefore contain the trivial character three times
and each nontrivial character once.

Inflate \eqref{eq:V4-relation} along $N\twoheadrightarrow N/J$ and induce
from $N$ to $G$.  Transitivity of induction yields
\begin{equation}
 [G/J]+2[G/N]=[G/\Hplus]+[G/\Hzero]+[G/\Hthree].
 \label{eq:G-character-relation}
\end{equation}
The independent computation in \cref{tab:character-vectors} verifies
\eqref{eq:G-character-relation} on all $25$ conjugacy classes, with
coefficient order $(\Hplus,\Hzero,\Hthree,J,N)=(-1,-1,-1,1,2)$.
Checking all classes prevents a matching-dimensions argument from being
mistaken for character equality.

\begin{remark}[Characters, not finite sets]
The two sides of \eqref{eq:G-character-relation} are equal after passing to
rational permutation representations.  They are not isomorphic finite
$G$-sets: the orbit decompositions have different numbers and stabilizer
types.  Every use below is additive in characters.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Dedekind zeta consequence}

For $H\le G$, Artin formalism identifies the Dedekind zeta function of
$K^H$ with the Artin $L$-function attached to
$\Ind_H^G\one$.  Multiplicativity on character sums, as in Perlis's
criterion \citep{perlis1977equation}, converts
\eqref{eq:G-character-relation} into
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_L(s)\zeta_M(s)^2
 =\zeta_{\Fplus}(s)\zeta_{\Fzero}(s)\zeta_{\Fthree}(s).
 \label{eq:zeta-first}
\end{equation}
The original $\Hplus,\Hminus$ pair has equal permutation characters, and
conjugation by $x$ preserves the character.  Hence
$\zeta_{\Fthree}=\zeta_{\Fplus}$ and
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_L(s)\zeta_M(s)^2
 =\zeta_{\Fplus}(s)^2\zeta_{\Fzero}(s).
 \label{eq:zeta-second}
\end{equation}
The generic $\Vfour$ identity is classical; the contribution here is its
realization by the exact embedded fields together with their explicit
generators and arithmetic.

Equations \eqref{eq:zeta-first}--\eqref{eq:zeta-second} are the entire
analytic conclusion.  We compute no new decomposition Frobenius, bad Artin
Euler factor, epsilon factor, or root number, and infer no holomorphy or
automorphy statement.  The next section uses conductor characters only to
compute discriminants, a logically separate finite invariant.
