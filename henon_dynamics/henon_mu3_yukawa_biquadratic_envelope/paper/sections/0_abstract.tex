We construct an explicit biquadratic fixed-field envelope inside a normal
$\WE$-extension arising from the $27$ lines on a cubic surface.  Starting
from a degree-$320$ Gassmann pair, an exhaustive eleven-bucket computation
finds that its transported members are the only collision whose normalizers
are conjugate in $\WE$ and have index two over both subgroups.  Their common
normalizer produces fields of degrees $160$, $320$, and $640$: the top is
biquadratic over the degree-$160$ field, its three quadratic subfields have a
common normal closure, and primitive integral line-coordinate generators are
given for the base, a third quadratic companion, and the top.  The companion
requires a cubic formal invariant: its point and unordered-pair orbit
partitions agree with those of the normalizer, ruling out an exact stabilizer
in total degree at most two.  We also prove a rational permutation-character
relation and the resulting zeta identity, compute all signatures and signed
discriminants, and obtain relative discriminant norms
$3^8,3^{16},3^8,3^{32}$.  Complete double-coset tables for both retained
$3$-adic decomposition-group branches show that all relative ramification is
tame above $3$, with $e=2$ and different exponent $1$ in every ramified row.
The proof combines Galois correspondence, exact stabilizers, noncollision at
the completely split prime $692717$, and independent finite certificates;
neither local branch is selected.
