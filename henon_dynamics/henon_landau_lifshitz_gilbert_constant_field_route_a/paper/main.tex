\begin{abstract}
We close the complete source-local dynamics of the constant-field
Landau--Lifshitz--Gilbert equation on the unit sphere.  In the stereographic
coordinate \(z=(m_1+i m_2)/(1+m_3)\), the nonlinear vector field becomes
\(\dot z=(-\alpha\omega+i\omega)z\), while \(m_3\) is an exact tanh
trajectory.  The energy law, north/south stability, pure-precession latitude
periods, identity face, and every sampled-time fixed set follow in one
boundary atlas.  The periodic face is a continuum and therefore does not
provide an isolated primitive-orbit or arithmetic determinant.  Independent
exact reconstruction and symbolic checks accompany the paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and exact chart}
Let \(e_3=(0,0,1)\), \(m(t)\in\Sph\), and \(\alpha,\omega\geq0\).  We freeze
\begin{equation}
 \dot m=-\omega m\times e_3-\alpha\omega m\times(m\times e_3).
 \tag{1}
\end{equation}
This is the normalized Landau--Lifshitz/Gilbert convention: the usual
factor \(1+\alpha^2\) in the unreduced Gilbert equation has been absorbed
into the declared frequency \(\omega\) (equivalently, into the time unit).
The vector field is tangent to the sphere.  On \(m_3>-1\) put
\(z=(m_1+i m_2)/(1+m_3)\).  The inverse formulas are
\begin{equation}
 m_1+i m_2=\frac{2z}{1+|z|^2},\qquad
 m_3=\frac{1-|z|^2}{1+|z|^2}.
 \tag{2}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact flow and pole atlas]\label{thm:flow}
For \(-1<m_3(0)<1\), equation (1) has
\begin{equation}
 z(t)=z(0)e^{(-\alpha\omega+i\omega)t},\qquad
 m_3(t)=\tanh\!\left(\alpha\omega t+\operatorname{artanh}m_3(0)\right).
 \tag{3}
\end{equation}
The two poles are equilibria.  With \(E=1-m_3\),
\begin{equation}
 \dot E=-\alpha\omega(1-m_3^2)\leq0. \tag{4}
\end{equation}
If \(\alpha\omega>0\), the north pole is asymptotically stable and the south
pole is unstable.  Their transverse eigenvalues have real parts
\(-\alpha\omega\) and \(+\alpha\omega\), respectively, and precession
frequency \(\omega\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Writing (1) in components gives
\(\dot m_1=-\omega m_2-\alpha\omega m_1m_3\),
\(\dot m_2=\omega m_1-\alpha\omega m_2m_3\), and
\(\dot m_3=\alpha\omega(m_1^2+m_2^2)\).  The complex combination and the
unit-sphere identity yield the scalar linear equation and the logistic
equation in (3).  Substitution in (2) proves reconstruction and (4).
Linearizing the complex equation at the two chart ends gives the stated
real parts; the pole cases themselves are immediate from (1).
\end{proof}

\section{Periodic and sampled-time faces}
\begin{proposition}[All boundary cases]\label{prop:faces}
If \(\alpha=0\) and \(\omega>0\), latitude is conserved and every nonpolar
point runs on a circle of period \(2\pi/\omega\).  If \(\omega=0\), the flow
is the identity for every \(\alpha\).  For a sampled time \(\tau>0\), when
\(\alpha\omega>0\) the fixed set is exactly the two poles.  On the pure
precession face it is all of \(\Sph\) iff \(\omega\tau\in2\pi\mathbb Z\),
and otherwise it is the two poles; the \(\omega=0\) and \(\tau=0\) faces are
identity maps.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation (3) preserves \(|z|\) exactly when \(\alpha=0\), and its phase is
\omega t.  If \(\alpha>0\), the modulus can return to its initial value only
at a pole.  The listed resonance and identity alternatives then follow
directly from the exponential flow.
\end{proof}
