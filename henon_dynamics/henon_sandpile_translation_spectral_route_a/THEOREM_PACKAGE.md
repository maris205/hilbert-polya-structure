# C176 theorem package

## Assumptions and conventions

Let `G` be a finite connected undirected loopless multigraph with designated sink `s`. Write `V°=V(G)\{s}`, `r=|V°|`, and let the reduced Laplacian be
\[
\Delta_{vv}=\deg(v),\qquad \Delta_{vw}=-a_{vw}\quad(v\ne w),
\]
where multiplicities are retained. A configuration `eta` is stable when `0<=eta_v<deg(v)` for every nonsink vertex. Toppling `v` subtracts the `v`-th column of `Delta` from nonsink heights; chips sent to the sink disappear. For `b in N^r`, define
\[
T_b(\eta)=\operatorname{stab}(\eta+b).
\]
One addition followed by complete stabilization is the clock.

## Recurrent representative and torsor bridge

The sink makes legal stabilization terminate, and the Abelian/least-action argument makes its result independent of toppling order. The critical group is
\[
K(G,s)=\mathbb Z^r/\Delta\mathbb Z^r,
\qquad D=|K(G,s)|=\det\Delta.
\]

### Burning-representative lemma

Start with the sink burned. A stable configuration is recurrent exactly when vertices can be burned successively under the rule
\[
\eta_v\ge \#\{\text{edges from }v\text{ to currently unburned vertices}\}.
\]
Every class modulo `Delta Z^r` contains exactly one such recurrent stable configuration.

For completeness, let `beta_v` be the number of edges from `v` to the sink. Then `beta=Delta*1`. A complete burning order is equivalent to a legal sequence in which every nonsink topples once after adding `beta`, returning to the same stable state. Conversely, an unburnable set is a forbidden subconfiguration and prevents recurrence. Fixing local edge orders, the first burned predecessor edge at every vertex gives a spanning tree oriented to the sink; the height records the number and local rank of earlier burned edges, and this construction is invertible. Thus burning configurations are counted by sink-rooted spanning trees, namely `det Delta` by the cofactor expansion of the graph Laplacian. The least-action principle shows that two burning configurations in one toppling class coincide. Since both the quotient and burning set have `D` elements, every quotient class has one recurrent representative.

It follows that the recurrent set `R(G,s)` is canonically a `K(G,s)`-torsor, and its recurrent representative of the zero class supplies the sandpile-group identity. For recurrent `eta`, stabilization preserves recurrence and
\[
[T_b(\eta)]=[\eta]+[b].
\]
Hence `T_b` is exactly translation by `[b]` on the recurrent set.

## Two exact translation-order formulas

Let
\[
L=\operatorname{ord}_{K(G,s)}([b])
=\min\{\ell\ge1:\ell b\in\Delta\mathbb Z^r\}.
\]
If `U Delta V=diag(d_1,...,d_r)` is a Smith decomposition with unimodular `U,V`, then
\[
L=\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le i\le r}
\frac{d_i}{\gcd(d_i,(Ub)_i)}.
\]
Indeed `ell[b]=0` exactly when every `d_i` divides `ell(Ub)_i`.

Alternatively put `w=adj(Delta)b`. Since `Delta^{-1}=adj(Delta)/D`,
\[
\ell b\in\Delta\mathbb Z^r
\Longleftrightarrow D\mid \ell w_i\ \text{for every }i.
\]
Therefore
\[
L=\operatorname{lcm}_i\frac{D}{\gcd(D,w_i)}
=\frac{D}{\gcd(D,w_1,\ldots,w_r)}.
\]
For `b=0`, both formulas give `L=1`. If `r=0`, use the empty determinant convention `D=1`; the empty recurrent configuration is a singleton and `L=1`.

## Orbit, zeta, determinant, spectrum, and reversal theorem

Translation by an element of order `L` has no return before `L` at any point. Thus every recurrent orbit has exact length `L`, and the `D` recurrent states form `D/L` primitive cycles. For every `n>=1`,
\[
#\operatorname{Fix}(T_b^n|\mathcal R)=
\begin{cases}D,&L\mid n,\\0,&L\nmid n.\end{cases}
\]
Consequently
\[
\zeta_{T_b}(z)=(1-z^L)^{-D/L},\qquad
\det(I-zU_b)=(1-z^L)^{D/L},
\]
where `U_b f=f after T_b` on uniform `ell^2(R)`.

Each length-`L` permutation cycle contributes every `L`-th root of unity once. Equivalently, critical-group characters diagonalize translation; restriction to the cyclic subgroup generated by `[b]` is onto, with kernel of size `D/L`. Hence every `L`-th root occurs with multiplicity `D/L`.

Let `R(eta)=eta^{-1}` in the recurrent sandpile group. Then `R` is involutive and
\[
RT_bR=T_b^{-1}.
\]
Thus `Theta f=conjugate(f after R)` is antiunitary and `Theta U_b Theta=U_b^{-1}`. Since a finite permutation unitary is self-adjoint exactly when it equals its inverse,
\[
U_b=U_b^*\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 2[b]=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L\le2.
\]

## Strict all-stable-state boundary

The theorem is not a statement about every stable configuration. On the path `0-1-2` with sink `2` and `b=(1,0)`, the stable states are `(0,0)` and `(0,1)`, and both stabilize after addition to `(0,1)`. The full stable-state map is noninjective. Its recurrent subset is the singleton `{(0,1)}`, where the translation theorem gives `D=L=1`.

For arbitrary `b`, no all-stable-state cycle classification or eventual-recurrence theorem is asserted. In particular `b=0` makes the full stable-state map the identity, so blanket transient claims would also be false.
