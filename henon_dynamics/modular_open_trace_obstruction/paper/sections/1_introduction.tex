\section{Introduction}

The arithmetic in cusp scattering is naturally open: a trajectory enters
from infinity, crosses a compact core, and leaves toward an endpoint.  A
dynamical zeta or Fredholm determinant is naturally closed: its trace expansion
identifies the final state with the initial state and organizes repetitions of
primitive loops.  The distinction matters in Hilbert--P\'olya searches.  A
completed zeta function can occur in a scattering coefficient without the
scattering channels providing the primitive periods or discrete self-adjoint
spectrum required by the proposed construction.

The deterministic area-preserving H\'enon program that motivates this project
asks for a source-derived route from nonlinear dynamics to an arithmetic
spectral object \cite{Wang2026Henon}.  Earlier tests showed that the final
denominator of a modular matrix gives exact open-channel arithmetic but cannot
serve as a nonzero homogeneous clock on closed hyperbolic classes.  That result
did not address two large repairs: retain the endpoint so that denominator
increments compose locally, or replace the one-cusp scalar by a multi-cusp
matrix and retain all channels.  We test both repairs here without assuming
that either is a dynamical zeta.

Scattering geodesics and their sojourn times are classical
\cite{Guillemin1976,JiZworski2001,JiZworski2002}; a recent modular count gives
particularly explicit endpoint data \cite{PujahariSatpathy2025}.  We begin
with that open object rather than forcing it into a closed-orbit format.  Its
unoriented Laplace series is
\begin{equation}
Z_{\rm sc}(s;T_0)=\frac{T_0^{-2s}}2\left[
\frac{\zeta(2s-1)}{\zeta(2s)}+
\frac{\zeta(2s)L(2s,\chi_{-4})}{\zeta(4s)}\right].
\label{eq:intro-open-series}
\end{equation}
This formula is a direct corollary of the known count and is not presented as
a new scattering theory.  Its purpose is sharper: open arithmetic survives
before diagonal closure.

Our first main result identifies what endpoint retention actually changes.
The double-coset space \(P\backslash\GammaSL/P\) does not compose
representative-independently.  The boundary action groupoid does.  A
projective section defines an exact automorphy cocycle, but on
\(\mathbb P^1(\Q)\) the primitive integral section makes the cocycle
identically zero.  Every other section differs by an algebraic coboundary.  In
particular, \(2\log|c|\) in the affine section is a difference of endpoint
denominator gauges rather than an intrinsic loop period.

The second main result describes the cost of creating nonzero trace periods.
On the full real boundary, nonidentity loops are parabolic rational fixed
points or hyperbolic quadratic fixed points.  The former have zero automorphy
period.  At the attracting and repelling hyperbolic fixed points the periods
are respectively plus and minus the translation length.  Therefore the
automorphy-period support of a standard loop trace is zero on the rational
cusp orbit and otherwise lies in the signed Selberg length spectrum.  This
conclusion agrees with the established
modular transfer-operator endpoint \cite{Mayer1991,PohlWabnitz2026}, but the
elementary groupoid dichotomy makes the open-to-closed loss explicit.

The multi-cusp test reaches the same boundary by a different mechanism.  The
standard scattering matrix of squarefree \(\Gamma_0(N)\) has the classical
tensor form of Huxley and Hejhal \cite{Huxley1984,CakoniChanillo2019,
LevitinStrohmaier2021}.  A fixed Walsh transform diagonalizes every spectral
parameter, producing only the modular completed-zeta quotient times finite
Euler factors.  Consequently, the source matrices commute.  We do not average
their entries: we form a frozen product at distinct spectral parameters first
and prove that it is invariant under reordering.  This is a diagnostic for a
proposal that elects to encode successive steps by such bare products;
scattering theory itself does not identify the spectral parameter with time.

The obstruction has a necessary boundary.  Inserting rank-one cusp projectors
between scattering matrices leaves the commutative scattering algebra.  Such
projector-resolved amplitudes can distinguish parameter-to-edge assignments
and endpoint paths.  Related
off-diagonal and twisted zeta functions already have a substantial literature
\cite{Kiral2016,DiamantisFriedbergStromberg2026}; a finite witness alone does
not define another Fredholm determinant.

The contributions are therefore the following.

\begin{enumerate}
\item We derive \cref{eq:intro-open-series} as a genuinely open positive
control and audit its coefficients exactly through \(q=2000\).
\item We prove that the rational-endpoint automorphy cocycle is an algebraic
coboundary,
while its nonzero full-boundary loop periods are exactly signed Selberg
lengths.
\item We prove simultaneous Walsh diagonalization and permutation invariance
for frozen spectral-parameter products in the standard squarefree scattering
algebra, verifying the identities through \(N=210\) at 80-digit precision.
\item We exhibit projector-resolved assignment and path sensitivity and state
it as an explicit exclusion from the no-go rather than suppressing the
positive signal.
\end{enumerate}

All headline claims are analytic.  Computation audits normalization, matrix
identities, and implementation; no claim is fitted to Riemann-zero data.  The
result is a scoped Route-A obstruction, not a proof of the Riemann hypothesis
or a universal theorem about open groupoid kernels.
