\section{Full-boundary loops are Selberg periods}

The rational cusp orbit is too small to carry nonzero automorphy periods.  One
might therefore enlarge the unit space to \(\mathbb P^1(\R)\) before taking a
trace.  The resulting loops are completely rigid.

\begin{theorem}[cusp-trivial/full-boundary-Selberg dichotomy]
Let \((g,x)\) be a nonidentity loop in
\(\GammaSL\ltimes\mathbb P^1(\R)\).  If \(x\) is rational, then \(g\) is
parabolic and \(\sigma_v(g,x)=0\).  Otherwise \(x\) is a real quadratic
irrational, \(g\) is hyperbolic, and its two fixed points satisfy
\begin{equation}
\sigma_v(g,x_+)=\ell(g),\qquad
\sigma_v(g,x_-)=-\ell(g),
\quad
\ell(g)=2\operatorname{arcosh}\frac{|\tr g|}{2}.
\label{eq:signed-selberg}
\end{equation}
The loop period is independent of the projective section.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A loop at \(x=\infty\) has \(c=0\); after excluding the identity in
\(\GammaSL\), its stabilizer is parabolic and its automorphy period is zero.
A finite fixed point satisfies
\[
cx^2+(d-a)x-b=0
\]
for an integral lift
\(g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
Nonidentity elliptic elements have no real fixed point.  A parabolic element
has one rational eigenline with eigenvalue of modulus one, hence zero
automorphy period.

A hyperbolic element has two real eigenlines.  Neither can be rational: a
primitive integral eigenvector would give a rational algebraic-unit
eigenvalue, hence \(\pm1\), contradicting hyperbolicity.  The fixed points are
therefore quadratic irrationals.  If \(\lambda>1\) is the modulus of the
expanding eigenvalue, the two automorphy factors have moduli \(\lambda\) and
\(\lambda^{-1}\).  Their periods are \(\pm2\log\lambda\), which equal the
translation lengths in \cref{eq:signed-selberg}.  A section change is a
coboundary, and that coboundary vanishes on a loop.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[period-support consequence]
Any standard trace expansion supported on loops of the frozen endpoint action
groupoid has no nonzero rational-cusp period.  After extension to the full
real boundary, its nonzero automorphy-period set is the signed Selberg length
spectrum.
\end{corollary}

The word ``standard'' limits the corollary to a diagonal trace whose fixed
points are action-groupoid loops.  It does not cover off-diagonal matrix
coefficients, extra fiber state, or inserted projectors.  Within that scope,
the period dichotomy is consistent with established nuclear modular transfer
operators that close on Selberg zeta \cite{Mayer1991,PohlWabnitz2026}:
nonzero trace periods arise only after the endpoints form a hyperbolic closed
orbit.  The dichotomy alone does not determine coding multiplicities,
fixed-point Jacobians, nuclearity, or a Fredholm determinant.
