\section{Squarefree cusp channels form a commutative scattering algebra}

Endpoint retention is not the only possible repair.  For squarefree
\(N\), the surface \(\Gamma_0(N)\backslash\mathbb H\) has
\(2^{\omega(N)}\) cusps.  Its full standard scattering matrix retains every
incoming and outgoing cusp label.  The classical computation, first obtained
in the congruence scattering theory of Huxley and Hejhal and printed in modern
sources \cite{Huxley1984,CakoniChanillo2019,LevitinStrohmaier2021}, is
\begin{equation}
\Phi_N(s)=\phi(s)\bigotimes_{p\mid N}M_p(s),
\qquad
\phi(s)=\frac{\Lambda(2s-1)}{\Lambda(2s)},
\label{eq:tensor-scattering}
\end{equation}
where \(\Lambda(w)=\pi^{-w/2}\Gamma(w/2)\zeta(w)\) and
\begin{equation}
M_p(s)=\frac1{p^{2s}-1}
\begin{pmatrix}
p-1&p^s-p^{1-s}\\
p^s-p^{1-s}&p-1
\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:local-block}
\end{equation}
Young's explicit cusp/character basis changes place this factorization in the
broader Eisenstein newform theory \cite{Young2019}.

\begin{theorem}[fixed Walsh diagonalization]
Let \(N\) be squarefree and \(r=\omega(N)\).  Fix the Huxley--Hejhal
width/Atkin--Lehner cusp scaling matrices, order the divisor-labelled cusps by
their prime-incidence bits, and use the tensor identification in
\cref{eq:tensor-scattering}.  The matrix
\[
H_N=\bigotimes_{p\mid N}\frac1{\sqrt2}
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}
\]
is independent of \(s\) and diagonalizes \(\Phi_N(s)\) for every regular
\(s\).  For \(\eps\in\{\pm1\}^r\), the eigenchannel is
\begin{equation}
\phi_\eps(s)=\phi(s)
\prod_{p\mid N}
\frac{1+\eps_p p^{1-s}}{1+\eps_p p^s}.
\label{eq:eigenchannel}
\end{equation}
Furthermore,
\begin{equation}
\det\Phi_N(s)=\phi(s)^{2^r}
\prod_{p\mid N}
\left(\frac{1-p^{2-2s}}{1-p^{2s}}\right)^{2^{r-1}}.
\label{eq:scattering-determinant}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each local block is \(a_p(s)I+b_p(s)X\), where \(X\) swaps the two local
cusp bits.  Its fixed eigenvectors are \((1,\pm1)^T\), and direct
factorization gives
\[
a_p(s)+\eps b_p(s)
=\frac{1+\eps p^{1-s}}{1+\eps p^s}.
\]
Tensoring proves \cref{eq:eigenchannel}.  Multiplying the two local
eigenvalues gives \((1-p^{2-2s})/(1-p^{2s})\).  Each local determinant occurs
\(2^{r-1}\) times in the full tensor determinant, while \(\phi(s)\) occurs in
all \(2^r\) channels, proving \cref{eq:scattering-determinant}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[permutation invariance of spectral-parameter products]
For regular \(s_1,\ldots,s_m\) and every permutation \(\pi\),
\begin{equation}
\Phi_N(s_m)\cdots\Phi_N(s_1)
=\Phi_N(s_{\pi(m)})\cdots\Phi_N(s_{\pi(1)}).
\label{eq:order-blind}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In the fixed Walsh basis the two products have diagonal entries
\(\prod_j\phi_\eps(s_j)\).  Scalar multiplication is permutation-invariant.
\end{proof}

The proof preserves the frozen product and discovers that it is
permutation-invariant.  It does not replace the matrices by a mean transition
matrix.  The fixed basis change is also invertible and therefore does not by
itself discard a single matrix.  The obstruction is that the entire frozen
family lies in one commutative Atkin--Lehner algebra.  Here \(s\) remains a
spectral parameter, not a source-derived evolution time.  The result rules
out chronology only conditionally: a model that encodes its successive steps
by bare factors \(\Phi_N(s_j)\) cannot recover their order from the product.
An \(s\)-dependent cusp renormalization is outside the fixed normalization
and is not claimed to preserve simultaneous diagonalization.

The completed-zeta functional equation and the local ratios imply
\[
\Phi_N(s)\Phi_N(1-s)=I.
\]
On \(\Re s=1/2\), every ratio in \cref{eq:eigenchannel} has modulus one, so
the standard scattering matrix is unitary.  These are strong analytic
properties, but they do not create a primitive orbit or evolution law.

Finally, let \(\rho\) be a nontrivial zeta zero.  A local numerator in
\cref{eq:eigenchannel} can vanish only on \(\Re s=1\), and a local denominator
only on \(\Re s=0\).  Thus no local factor cancels the pole of \(\phi\) at
\(s=\rho/2\) or its zero at \(s=(1+\rho)/2\).  Every standard eigenchannel
retains the same shifted quotient divisor.
