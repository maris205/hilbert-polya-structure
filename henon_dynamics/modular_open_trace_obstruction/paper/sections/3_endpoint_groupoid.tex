\section{Endpoint retention and rational gauge triviality}

Let \(P\) be the stabilizer of infinity in \(\GammaSL\).  The open channels
are naturally indexed by \(P\backslash\GammaSL/P\), but a double-coset set is
not automatically a category of composable paths.

\begin{proposition}[double-coset composition is not intrinsic]
Multiplication of representatives does not define a product on
\(P\backslash\GammaSL/P\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With
\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
the matrices \(S\) and \(ST^n\) represent the same right \(P\)-coset.  Yet
\[
SS=-I,\qquad
ST^nS=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\n&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For \(n\ne0\), the first product is in the identity cell and the second has
nonzero lower-left entry, so they lie in different double cosets.
\end{proof}

The endpoint action groupoid repairs this type error.  For a
\(\GammaSL\)-invariant subset \(X\subseteq\mathbb P^1(\R)\), an arrow
\((g,x)\) runs from \(x\) to \(gx\), and
\((g,hx)\circ(h,x)=(gh,x)\).  Choose a nonzero vector \(v_x\) on each
projective line.  Define
\begin{equation}
g v_x=j_v(g,x)v_{gx},\qquad
\sigma_v(g,x)=2\log|j_v(g,x)|.
\label{eq:automorphy-cocycle}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\sigma_v(gh,x)=\sigma_v(g,hx)+\sigma_v(h,x).
\label{eq:cocycle-law}
\end{equation}
This is the elementary projective form of a Busemann-type groupoid cocycle;
the general framework is developed in \cite{Nekrashevych2014}.

\begin{theorem}[rational cusp algebraic coboundary]
Every section-induced absolute projective cocycle of the form
\cref{eq:automorphy-cocycle} on
\(\GammaSL\ltimes\mathbb P^1(\Q)\) is a set-theoretic groupoid coboundary.  In the affine section,
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\rm aff}(g,x)=2\log q(gx)-2\log q(x),
\label{eq:denominator-coboundary}
\end{equation}
where \(q(p/q)=q\) in lowest terms and \(q(\infty)=1\).  In particular, if
\(g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and
\(c\ne0\),
\[
\sigma_{\rm aff}(g,\infty)=2\log|c|.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a reduced rational \(x=p/q\), choose a primitive vector
\(\widehat v_x=(p,q)^T\), and take
\(\widehat v_\infty=(1,0)^T\).  An integral unimodular matrix maps a
primitive integral vector to another primitive integral vector.  After the
fixed sign convention is restored,
\[
g\widehat v_x=\pm\widehat v_{gx}.
\]
Hence the associated absolute automorphy factor is one and
\(\sigma_{\widehat v}\equiv0\).

Every other projective section has the form \(v'_x=r(x)\widehat v_x\).  Its
cocycle differs from zero by
\[
2\log|r(x)|-2\log|r(gx)|.
\]
For the affine section, \(r(x)=q(x)^{-1}\), which proves
\cref{eq:denominator-coboundary} and the specialization at infinity.
\end{proof}

Thus endpoint retention makes the denominator height exactly additive, but
only as an endpoint gauge difference.  It creates no nonzero intrinsic loop
period on the rational cusp orbit.

The qualifier ``set-theoretic'' is essential.  The primitive section and the
denominator gauge are neither continuous nor bounded for the topology
inherited from \(\mathbb P^1(\R)\).  The theorem does not assert a continuous,
H\"older, or Liv\v{s}ic coboundary and supplies no bounded conjugacy of
analytic transfer operators.  Its trace-level consequence is only that this
coboundary has zero period on rational loops.

There is also a parity obstruction.  The inverse of \((g,x)\) is
\((g^{-1},gx)\), so every real cocycle obeys
\[
\sigma(g^{-1},gx)=-\sigma(g,x).
\]
A physical sojourn time is positive and unchanged by reversing the geodesic.
It therefore cannot itself be a nonzero real groupoid cocycle; additivity
requires a signed lift.
