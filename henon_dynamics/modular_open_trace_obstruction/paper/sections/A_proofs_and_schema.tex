\section{Supplementary identities and result schema}

\subsection{Local factor identities}

Writing \(M_p(s)=a_p(s)I+b_p(s)X\), direct algebra gives
\[
a_p(s)+b_p(s)=\frac{1+p^{1-s}}{1+p^s},\qquad
a_p(s)-b_p(s)=\frac{1-p^{1-s}}{1-p^s}.
\]
Each ratio is inverted by \(s\mapsto1-s\).  Its numerator zeros have real
part one and its denominator zeros have real part zero.  These observations
prove both the local functional equation and the noncancellation assertion in
the open critical strips.

\subsection{Projected trace identity}

For \(P_a=e_ae_a^T\) and arbitrary matrices \(A,B,C\),
\[
P_aAP_b=A_{ab}e_ae_b^T.
\]
Multiplying three such factors and taking the trace yields
\[
\tr(P_aAP_bBP_cC)=A_{ab}B_{bc}C_{ca}.
\]
This identity explains why projectors can retain an itinerary even when
\(A,B,C\) commute.

\subsection{Machine-readable outputs}

The release stores the following compact artifacts.

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{arithmetic\_counts.csv}: exact \(q,\varphi(q),s_q,n_q\)
rows through the frozen cutoff.
\item \texttt{open\_series.csv}: high-precision partial sums, closed forms,
and elementary tail bounds.
\item \texttt{endpoint\_ledger.csv}: exact primitive and affine rational
section checks.
\item \texttt{exact\_certificates.json}: symbolic identities, source locks,
and exact representative witnesses.
\item \texttt{scattering\_checks.json}: Walsh channels, matrix identities,
spectral-parameter product permutations, and projector-resolved controls.
\item \texttt{summary.json}: frozen cutoffs, maximum residuals, and the scoped
decision.
\item \texttt{independent\_check.json}: higher-precision independent verdict.
\end{itemize}

All complex numbers are serialized as decimal real/imaginary pairs.  The
checker rejects duplicate JSON keys, unexpected CSV columns, missing rows,
and altered certificate values.
