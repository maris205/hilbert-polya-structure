\begin{abstract}
We refine a symbol-count suspension clock to a directed-edge roof on the full
binary shift.  An exact four-variable matrix determinant gives the rooted
trace law and primitive-orbit product at every period.  The nonlattice roof
$\bigl(1,\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt6\bigr)$ separates every distinct directed
edge-count sector, including the requested words $000111$ and $001011$.
It remains noninjective on primitive orbits: $001011$ and $001101$ collide,
and a flow-conservation theorem proves that all periodic data are blind to
off-diagonal orientation.  The construction is source-side and dynamical,
not an arithmetic or target-facing factorization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Formal determinant and all-period product}
Let $\Sigma$ be the two-sided full binary shift and assign the ordered edge
roof
\[
\tau=\begin{pmatrix}1&\sqrt2\\ \sqrt3&\sqrt6\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The suspension identifies $(x,t+\tau_{x_0x_1})$ with $(\sigma x,t)$.  For
formal edge variables put
\[
M(x)=\begin{pmatrix}x_{00}&x_{01}\\x_{10}&x_{11}\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\Delta(x)=\det(I-M(x)).
\]
Direct calculation gives
\begin{equation}
\Delta(x)=1-x_{00}-x_{11}+x_{00}x_{11}-x_{01}x_{10}. \tag{1}
\end{equation}
For every $n\geq1$, matrix multiplication yields the exact rooted-word law
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Tr}M(x)^n=\sum_{\substack{w\ {\rm rooted\ closed}\\|w|=n}}
\prod_{i,j}x_{ij}^{N_{ij}(w)}. \tag{2}
\end{equation}
Expanding $-\log\det(I-M)=\sum_{n\geq1}\operatorname{Tr}(M^n)/n$ and
grouping each rooted word by its unique primitive root and repetition proves,
in the total-edge-degree completion,
\begin{equation}
\Delta(x)=\prod_{[\gamma]\ {\rm primitive}}
\left(1-\prod_{i,j}x_{ij}^{N_{ij}(\gamma)}\right). \tag{3}
\end{equation}
There are only finitely many primitive necklaces of any fixed total degree,
so every coefficient of (3) receives finitely many contributions; the formal
product is therefore well defined.  Equations (2)--(3) hold at every period.

\section{Nonlattice specialization}
Substitute $x_{ij}=e^{-s\tau_{ij}}$.  Then
\begin{equation}
d_\tau(s)=1-e^{-s}-e^{-\sqrt6s}
+e^{-(1+\sqrt6)s}-e^{-(\sqrt2+\sqrt3)s}, \tag{4}
\end{equation}
and (3) specializes to
\[
d_\tau(s)=\prod_{[\gamma]}
(1-e^{-s\ell(\gamma)}),\qquad
\ell=N_{00}+\sqrt2N_{01}+\sqrt3N_{10}+\sqrt6N_{11}.
\]
If $h>0$ is the unique solution of
$\rho((e^{-h\tau_{ij}})_{ij})=1$, the logarithmic trace and primitive
expansions converge absolutely for $\operatorname{Re}s>h$.  Indeed, for
$\sigma>h$ the positive matrix $M_\tau(\sigma)=(e^{-\sigma\tau_{ij}})$ has
spectral radius below one, while absolute values at $s$ are bounded entrywise
by $M_\tau(\sigma)$.  Hence
$\sum_n\operatorname{Tr}(M_\tau(\sigma)^n)/n$ converges and dominates both
regroupings.  The explicit finite exponential polynomial (4) is entire, so
its reciprocal is meromorphic.  The fixed cycles $[0]$ and $[1]$ have lengths
$1$ and $\sqrt6$; their irrational ratio rules out a common clock lattice and
proves that the roof is nonlattice.  It also rules out an imaginary period of
the full exponential polynomial: $d_\tau(s+iT)=d_\tau(s)$ for all $s$ would
force both $e^{-iT}=1$ and $e^{-iT\sqrt6}=1$, hence $T=0$.

\section{Exact edge-sector separation}
The numbers $1,\sqrt2,\sqrt3,\sqrt6$ form the rational basis of
$\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$.  For completeness, applying the four independent
sign changes $\sqrt2\mapsto\pm\sqrt2$ and
$\sqrt3\mapsto\pm\sqrt3$ to a rational linear relation and adding with signs
isolates each coefficient.  Equality of two roof lengths therefore forces
equality of their entire directed-edge-count vectors.  At period six,
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
word & $(N_{00},N_{01},N_{10},N_{11})$ & rooted multiplicity & length\\
\midrule
$000111$ & $(2,1,1,2)$ & $6$ & $2+\sqrt2+\sqrt3+2\sqrt6$\\
$001011$ & $(1,2,2,1)$ & $12$ & $1+2\sqrt2+2\sqrt3+\sqrt6$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Their difference is
$1-\sqrt2-\sqrt3+\sqrt6\ne0$.  Thus the two words, which have the same
symbol population, occupy different edge sectors.  Under C130's
destination-symbol control $\tau_{ij}=\rho_j$ with
$\rho=(1,\sqrt2)$, both instead have length $3+3\sqrt2$.

The trace multiplicities also have a direct orbit explanation.  A primitive
period-six necklace contributes its six rooted rotations.  There is one
necklace in the $(2,1,1,2)$ sector, giving coefficient $6$, and two necklaces
in the $(1,2,2,1)$ sector, giving coefficient $12$.

\section{The residual collision is structural}
The primitive words $001011$ and $001101$ are not cyclic rotations, but both
have edge vector $(1,2,2,1)$ and hence the same roof time.  They are the first
same-edge-count primitive collision, at period six.

This obstruction holds at every period.  In any closed binary word, every
crossing from $0$ to $1$ must be balanced by a crossing from $1$ to $0$;
hence
\begin{equation}
N_{01}=N_{10}. \tag{5}
\end{equation}
Algebraically, write the symbols as integers.  Each transition contributes
$x_{r+1}-x_r$, which is $1$ on $01$, $-1$ on $10$, and zero otherwise.
Summing around a closed word telescopes to zero and proves (5) without a
period cutoff.
Every periodic length and determinant coefficient consequently depends on the
off-diagonal roof only through $\tau_{01}+\tau_{10}$, never through
$\tau_{01}-\tau_{10}$.  Ordered formal variables preserve the accounting,
but closed-orbit data themselves cannot recover orientation asymmetry.  The
same quotient is visible directly in (1), where the off-diagonal variables
occur only as $x_{01}x_{10}$.  Thus replacing
$(\tau_{01},\tau_{10})$ by $(\tau_{01}+a,\tau_{10}-a)$, while retaining
positive roof values, leaves every periodic length and determinant unchanged.

\paragraph{Progress over C130.}
C130 separated symbol-population clock sectors but aggregated $000111$ and
$001011$.  C135 refines the same exact all-period determinant to complete
directed-edge-count sensitivity and separates that pair.  It deliberately
retains collisions inside one edge sector and proves the exact observable
quotient rather than claiming orbit injectivity.
