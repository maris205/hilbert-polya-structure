\section{The source-locked H6 survivor}
\label{sec:survivor}

Consider
\[
  \Had(q,p)=(1-6q^2-p,q),
  \qquad \det D\Had=1.
\]
Let
\[
  X_\pm=\pm[1/3,5/8],
  \qquad Y_\pm=\pm[5/16,81/128],
  \qquad N_{st}=X_s\times Y_t.
\]
In the state order \((--,-+,+-,++)\), the frozen adjacency matrix is
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&0&1&0\\
 1&0&0&0\\
 0&1&0&1\\
 0&1&0&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The inherited exact contraction theorem identifies
\[
 \Lam=\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb Z}\Had^{-k}
      \left(\bigcup_{s,t}N_{st}\right)
\]
with the two-sided subshift \(\Sig\).  A state at time \(i\) is
\((\varepsilon_i,\varepsilon_{i-1})\).  Every admissible sign sequence has a
unique coordinate sequence satisfying
\[
 q_i=\varepsilon_i
 \sqrt{\frac{1-q_{i-1}-q_{i+1}}6}.
 \tag{3.1}\label{eq:recurrence}
\]
The square-root transform contracts in the sup norm by at most
\(2/\sqrt{17}\).

The same radicand audit gives the sharper pointwise range
\[
  \frac{\sqrt{17}}{12}\le |q_i|\le \sqrt{\frac38}.
  \tag{3.2}\label{eq:q-range}
\]
In particular every realized coordinate lies strictly inside its \(X\)-box,
with uniform margin at least \((\sqrt{17}-4)/12\).  Since
\(X_\pm\Subset Y_\pm\), the whole survivor lies in the interior of
\(N=\bigcup N_{st}\).

\begin{proposition}[Local basic-set gate]
\label{prop:basic}
The set \(\Lam\) is a compact, mixing, locally maximal uniformly hyperbolic
set for \(\Had\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Compactness, invariance, conjugacy, and uniform hyperbolicity are the
source-locked R058--R059 conclusions.  Put \(U=\operatorname{int}N\).
Equation~\eqref{eq:q-range} and the strict \(X\Subset Y\) inclusion give
\(\Lam\subset U\).  Because \(U\subset N\),
\[
 \Inv(U)\subset\Inv(N)=\Lam\subset\Inv(U),
\]
so \(\Lam=\Inv(U)\) is locally maximal.  Finally, a direct integer
calculation gives \(A^4>0\) entrywise.  Thus \(\Sig\), and hence \(\Lam\),
is mixing.
\end{proof}

The characteristic polynomial
\[
 \det(\lambda I-A)
 =(\lambda^2-\lambda-1)(\lambda^2+1)
\]
gives \(h_{\mathrm{top}}(\Had|_{\Lam})=\log\varphi\), where
\(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).
