\section{The canonical Weil-height repair}

For $\alpha=\eps^m\pi^n$, define the eigenvalue ratio
\[
r_{\gamma_{m,n}}=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha'},
\]
which is well defined up to inversion by the projective class.  Its two real
local logarithms are $x,-x$.  With the standard normalized absolute values,
its finite divisor is $n(\mathfrak p-\mathfrak p')$ at the two degree-one
primes over $13$, so the corresponding local logarithms are
$n\log13,-n\log13$.  All other finite local contributions vanish for the
$S$-units under consideration.

\begin{theorem}[height identity]\label{thm:height}
For every $\gamma_{m,n}$,
\[
2h(r_{\gamma_{m,n}})
=\ellinf(\gamma_{m,n})+(\log13)\ellp(\gamma_{m,n}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The absolute logarithmic Weil height is the sum of positive local logarithms
divided by $[K:\Q]=2$.  Exactly one logarithm in each conjugate pair is
positive.  Therefore
\[
h(r_{\gamma_{m,n}})
=\frac12\left(|m\cA+n\cC|+|n|\log13\right).
\]
\end{proof}

Define
\[
H(m,n)=|m\cA+n\cC|+(\log13)|n|.
\]
The roof is homogeneous under repetition.  It is also proper: bounded $H$
first bounds $|n|$ and then bounds $|m|$.  Thus the product formula does not
collapse the joint vector, but the height construction does select a
canonical scalarization.

\begin{proposition}[primitive height count]\label{prop:height-count}
Let $N_H(R)$ count primitive pairs modulo simultaneous sign with
$H(m,n)\le R$.  Then, as $R\to\infty$,
\[
N_H(R)\sim\frac{6}{\pi^2\cA\log13}R^2.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The map $(m,n)\mapsto(x,y)=(\cA m+\cC n,n)$ has determinant $\cA$.  In the
$(x,y)$ plane the height ball is the diamond
\[
|x|+(\log13)|y|\le R,
\]
of area $2R^2/\log13$.  Pullback divides this by $\cA$.  Visible lattice
points have density $6/\pi^2$, and quotienting by simultaneous sign divides
the result by two.
\end{proof}

The height identity is a positive arithmetic result, but not a
Hilbert--P\'olya determinant.  It forgets the joint Weyl-chamber direction,
and it does not remove the clean periodic-flat multiplicity in
\cref{prop:flat-torus}.  A correct height zeta must still be placed in a
regulator-weighted higher-rank trace framework.
