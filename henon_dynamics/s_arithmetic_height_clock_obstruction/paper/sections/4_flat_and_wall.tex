\section{Periodic flats and near-wall accumulation}

\subsection{The primitive object is not an isolated orbit}

For a bi-hyperbolic class, the minimal set in the product geometry is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minset}
\Min(\gamma)=\Ax_\infty(\gamma)\times\Ax_{13}(\gamma)
\cong\R^2.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:flat-torus}
The centralizer lattice generated by $\eps,\pi$ acts on
\eqref{eq:minset} with translation basis $(\cA,0),(\cC,1)$.  Its quotient is
a compact flat torus of area $\cA$ in the chosen normalization.  It maps to
the arithmetic quotient as an immersed periodic flat; the full finite Weyl
normalizer can further produce an orbifold quotient, such as the torus
modulo inversion.  In either description, a primitive direction labels a
parallel family of closed geodesics rather than an isolated periodic orbit.
\end{proposition}

This is not a defect of the example.  It is the standard higher-rank
geometry behind flat-volume and centralizer-covolume terms in Lefschetz and
prime-geodesic formulae, including modern periodic-torus counting and
equidistribution~\cite{Deitmar2004,Deitmar2007,DangLi2022}.  An unweighted product
over group classes may have the correct algebraic repetition law, but it is
not automatically a Ruelle determinant for the geometric dynamics.

\subsection{The real projection is nonproper}

\begin{theorem}[near-wall sequence]\label{thm:near-wall}
There are coprime integer pairs $(m_k,n_k)$ such that
\[
|n_k|\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
|m_k\cA+n_k\cC|\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We first show $\cC/\cA\notin\Q$.  If $q\cC=r\cA$ for nonzero integers, then
exponentiation gives
\[
\left(\frac{\pi}{\pi'}\right)^q
=\left(\frac{\eps}{\eps'}\right)^r.
\]
At a prime of $K$ above $13$, the left side has valuation $\pm q$, whereas
the right side has valuation zero.  This is impossible.  If $p_k/q_k$ is a
continued-fraction convergent to $\cC/\cA$, set
$(m_k,n_k)=(-p_k,q_k)$.  These pairs are coprime and have the desired error
with unbounded denominator.
\end{proof}

There is no contradiction with compactness: the joint displacement escapes
in the tree direction.  Only its archimedean projection approaches zero.

\subsection{Euler-product consequence}

Let $\mathcal P_F$ be the primitive directions of this flat modulo
simultaneous sign.  Consider the class product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:naive-product}
Z_F(s,w)=\prod_{(m,n)\in\mathcal P_F}
\left(1-e^{-s|m\cA+n\cC|-w|n|}\right)^{-1}.
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:divergence}
For every $s\in\mathbb C$ with $\Re s>0$, the specialization $Z_F(s,0)$ has
no ordinary infinite-product convergence.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Along \cref{thm:near-wall}, the quantity inside the exponential approaches
zero.  Hence the inverse local factors in \eqref{eq:naive-product} do not
approach one.  This violates a necessary condition for a nonzero convergent
infinite product.
\end{proof}

For real $s,w>0$, \eqref{eq:naive-product} does converge absolutely.  Indeed,
\[
\sup_{t\in\R}\sum_{m\in\Z}e^{-s\cA|m-t|}<\infty,
\]
and the remaining sum over canonical representatives $n\ge0$ is bounded by
a geometric series in $e^{-w}$.  The same bounds are locally uniform on the
positive quadrant, so logarithmic differentiation is valid there and
\[
\partial_s\partial_w\log Z_F(s,w)
=\sum_{\mathcal P_F}\sum_{r\ge1}
r\ellinf\ellp e^{-r(s\ellinf+w\ellp)}>0.
\]
Thus the joint class product is not $F(s)G(w)$.  The obstruction is not a
trivial factorization into real and $p$-adic marginals; it is the loss of
properness when the second clock is discarded.
