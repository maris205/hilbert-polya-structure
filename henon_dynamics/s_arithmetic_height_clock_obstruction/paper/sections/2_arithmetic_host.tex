\section{The arithmetic host}

Let
\[
B=\Q\langle i,j:i^2=-1,\ j^2=3,\ ij=-ji\rangle
\]
and $\mathcal O=\Z[1,i,j,ij]$.  Write $G=\operatorname{PGL}_1(B)$.
The reduced norm is
\[
\nrd(a+bi+cj+dij)=a^2+b^2-3c^2-3d^2.
\]
Standard facts about quaternion algebras and their orders used below may be
found in~\cite{Voight2021}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ramification}
The algebra $B$ is ramified exactly at the finite places $2$ and $3$.  It is
split at infinity and at $13$.  Consequently
\[
\Gamma=P(\mathcal O[1/13]^\times)
<G(\R)\times G(\Q_{13})
\simeq\Gp(\R)\times\Gp(\Q_{13})
\]
is a uniform arithmetic lattice.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the Hilbert symbol $(-1,3)_v$, the local values at $2$ and $3$ are $-1$.
At an odd prime away from $6$, both entries are units and the symbol is $+1$.
At the real place the second entry is positive, so the symbol is $+1$.
In particular it is $+1$ at $13$; explicitly,
$3\equiv4^2\pmod {13}$.  Thus the ramification set is $\{2,3\}$ and $B$ is
a division algebra over $\Q$ but split at the two selected local places.
The $S$-arithmetic lattice theorem applied to the localized order gives the
lattice assertion.  Uniformity follows from the $\Q$-anisotropy of $G$.
\end{proof}

Put $K=\Q(j)=\Q(\sqrt3)$.  Its integer ring is
$\mathcal O_K=\Z[\sqrt3]$.  Define
\[
\eps=2+j,\qquad \pi=4+j.
\]
Then
\[
N(\eps)=1,\quad N(\pi)=13,
\qquad
\eps^{-1}=2-j,\quad
\pi^{-1}=\frac{4-j}{13}.
\]
Thus both projective classes lie in $\Gamma$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:centralizer}
The projective centralizer of $[\pi]$ in $\Gamma$ is
\[
C_\Gamma([\pi])
=\mathcal O_K[1/13]^\times/\Z[1/13]^\times
\cong\Z^2,
\]
with basis $[\eps],[\pi]$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $[x]$ centralizes $[\pi]$, then $x\pi x^{-1}=q\pi$ for some
$q\in\Q^\times$.  Reduced-trace invariance and $\trd(\pi)=8$ give $8=8q$,
hence $q=1$.  The ordinary centralizer of the regular noncentral element
$\pi$ in $B$ is $K$, and coefficient comparison gives
$\mathcal O[1/13]\cap K=\mathcal O_K[1/13]$.  The field $\Q(\sqrt3)$ has
class number one and unit group generated by $-1$ and $\eps$.  Since
\[
13=(4+\sqrt3)(4-\sqrt3)=\pi\pi',
\]
every $13$-unit is $\pm\eps^m\pi^a(\pi')^b$.  Modulo rational $13$-units,
$\pi'\equiv\pi^{-1}$ and $-1$ disappears.  The two displayed classes are
therefore a free basis.
\end{proof}

Write $\gamma_{m,n}=[\eps^m\pi^n]$.  The only rational class in this torus
is $\gamma_{0,0}$: equality of the valuations at the two primes over $13$
first forces $n=0$, after which $\eps^m\in\Q$ forces $m=0$.  Hence every
nonzero pair is regular.  Its two real conjugates are positive, so its
reduced trace is nonzero; the same trace argument used above shows that its
projective centralizer is the displayed $K$-torus.  Projective torsion in the
centralizer has been removed, and
\[
\gamma_{m,n}^k=\gamma_{km,kn}.
\]
Any projective root centralizes the element and therefore belongs to this
same projective centralizer.  Hence $\gamma_{m,n}$ is
primitive in $\Gamma$, for $(m,n)\ne(0,0)$, precisely when
$\gcd(m,n)=1$.  Finally,
\[
N_{B^\times}(K)/K^\times\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(K/\Q),
\]
and conjugation by $i$ realizes the nontrivial automorphism.  It sends both
generators to their projective inverses, so simultaneous sign is the only
additional conjugacy identification among these directions.
