# Research narrative

The project began with a bounded question: whether one compact quaternionic
$S$-arithmetic quotient could combine a compact automorphic spectral baseline
with an independent $p$-adic clock.

The first arithmetic calculation is positive. The projective classes of
$2+\sqrt3$ and $4+\sqrt3$ generate a rank-two centralizer lattice, and the
joint real/tree clock has nonzero determinant. This establishes a concrete
two-place clock for the selected example.

The same lattice exposes the first scoped obstruction. Continued fractions
produce primitive directions arbitrarily close to the real Weyl wall while
their tree length grows. The resulting theorem, rather than the finite record
table, shows that the real-only one-flat class product has no ordinary
convergence half-plane.

Arithmetic also supplies a proper scalarization:

\[
\ell_\infty+(\log13)\ell_{13}=2h(r).
\]

The height identity and the quadratic primitive-direction asymptotic are
proved. The reported finite direction counts are high-precision reproducible
numerical checks, not exact symbolic counts and not a global prime-geodesic
theorem.

The scalar clock does not turn the system into rank-one isolated-orbit
dynamics. The minimum flat modulo its centralizer is a compact flat torus, its
orbifold image may carry a finite Weyl quotient, and higher-rank trace formulas
require flat and centralizer weights. Dang--Li's work on periodic flat tori,
alongside the established higher-rank and Hecke literature, makes this
prior-art boundary explicit.

The compact spectral baseline is also limited in a precise way. Adding a
bounded function of one fixed Hecke operator to $\sqrt{\Delta+1}$ preserves the
surface $T^2$ Weyl order, so that full baseline cannot acquire a $T\log T$
count through fixed affine rescaling.

The resulting position is deliberately modest: HCS-C16 is an explicit
arithmetic example with a scoped Route-A assessment. It neither constructs a
Riemann dynamical determinant nor rules out other $S$-arithmetic, scattering,
conductor-growing, sparse, or unbounded-operator mechanisms.
