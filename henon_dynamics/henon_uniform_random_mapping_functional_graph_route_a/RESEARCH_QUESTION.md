# Research question

For a uniformly sampled labelled mapping `f:[n]->[n]`, can one close in one
theorem the exact joint law of cyclic vertices and connected components, the
expected number of cycles of every length, the full tail/cycle law of a marked
orbit, and the resulting one- and two-dimensional square-root limits?

The answer proved here is yes.  A functional graph is a permutation on its
cyclic labels with rooted in-trees attached.  An all-minors matrix-tree
determinant counts the forest, while a collision-free prefix counts the marked
orbit.  These two finite decompositions also reveal an exact distributional
identity: the number of cyclic vertices and the marked collision length have
the same law.  The collision product then yields the Rayleigh and joint limits.

This is a source-local random-combinatorial problem.  It asks for no arithmetic
local datum, Euler factor, root number, automorphy statement, target divisor,
functional equation, or Hilbert–Pólya operator.
