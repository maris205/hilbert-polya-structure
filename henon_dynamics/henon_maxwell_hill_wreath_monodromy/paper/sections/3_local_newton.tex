\section{The local weight-two parity certificate}

The prime \(19\) divides both the discriminant of \(P_9\) and the norm in
\eqref{eq:norm}.  A repeated residue factor alone would be insufficient:
the power-basis order may have nontrivial index at this prime.  We therefore
use the roots in \(\overline{\Q}_{19}\) and the Newton polygon directly,
without invoking Dedekind factorization.

Set
\begin{equation}
 c=-3+5\cdot19^2=1802,
 \qquad A=c+T.                                              \label{eq:shift}
\end{equation}
Write
\[
 P_9(c+T)=\sum_{i=0}^{9}a_iT^i,
 \qquad B_8(c+T)=\sum_{i=0}^{8}b_iT^i.
\]
Exact integer division gives the data in Table~\ref{tab:newton}.  A unit
residue means the residue after dividing the coefficient by the displayed
power of \(19\).

\begin{lemma}[The degree-two cluster]\label{lem:cluster}
The polynomial \(P_9\) has a degree-two local factor whose two roots satisfy
\(v_{19}(T)=5/2\).  Its splitting field is totally ramified of degree two.
The other seven roots lie in unramified extensions and satisfy
\(v_{19}(T)=0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The lower Newton polygon has one negative-slope edge
\[
 (0,5)\longrightarrow(2,0),\qquad \text{slope}=-\frac52.
\]
Indeed, \((1,3)\) lies strictly above that segment; every subsequent
coefficient is a unit.  The edge has horizontal length two and slope
denominator two.  Its residual polynomial is
\[
 18+5Y\in\F_{19}[Y],
\]
which is linear and separable.  The Newton polygon theorem therefore gives
one degree-two, ramification-index-two cluster and two roots of valuation
\(5/2\) \citep{serre1979localfields}.

For completeness, exact factorization of the residue polynomial is
\begin{align*}
 P_9(A)\equiv -7(A+3)^2
 &(A^2+A-7)\\
 &\cdot(A^5+3A^4+2A^3+A^2+4A-2)\pmod {19}.
\end{align*}
The quadratic and quintic factors are distinct and separable.  Their roots
therefore generate only unramified extensions.  The local splitting field
has ramification index two, contributed by the displayed Newton cluster,
and the remaining Newton edge is horizontal.
\end{proof}

We normalize the valuation \(v\) on this local splitting field by
\(v(19)=2\).  Thus the two cluster parameters satisfy \(v(T)=5\).

\begin{proposition}[Odd Hill valuation]\label{prop:odd-hill}
After labelling the two cluster roots first, one valuation of \(L\) above
\(19\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \bigl(v(\beta_1),\ldots,v(\beta_9)\bigr)\bmod2
   =(1,1,0,0,0,0,0,0,0).                                  \label{eq:parity}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For either cluster root, Table~\ref{tab:newton} gives
\[
 v(b_0)=6,\qquad v(b_1T)=5,\qquad v(b_iT^i)\ge10\quad(i\ge2).
\]
The minimum is unique, so no cancellation is possible and
\(v(B_8(c+T))=5\).  The denominator in \eqref{eq:beta} satisfies
\(4827099043\equiv7\pmod {19}\), hence it is a unit.  Both cluster values
of \(\beta\) therefore have valuation five.

It remains to rule out positive valuation at another root.  Exact residue
factorization gives
\[
 B_8(A)\equiv9(A+1)(A+3)
 (A^6+4A^5-7A^4+A^3-4A^2-6A+6)\pmod {19},
\]
and consequently
\begin{equation}
 \gcd(P_9,B_8)\bmod19=A+3.                                  \label{eq:gcd19}
\end{equation}
Every noncluster root reduces to one of the other, coprime residue factors,
so its \(B_8\)-value is a unit.  The valuation row is exactly
\eqref{eq:parity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The translation by \(5\cdot19^2\) is not a numerical fit.  It separates
the two-adic-depth structure of the repeated residue root sufficiently to
exhibit the primitive edge \(-5/2\).  Every coefficient and residue in
Table~\ref{tab:newton} is reconstructed from \eqref{eq:p9} and
Appendix~\ref{app:ledgers} by exact integer arithmetic.
\end{remark}
