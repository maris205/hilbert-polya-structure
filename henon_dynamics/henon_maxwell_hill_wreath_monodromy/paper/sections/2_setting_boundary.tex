\section{The locked Maxwell--Hill object and the claim boundary}

\subsection{Dynamical source}

The foundational local manuscript studies the area-preserving recurrence
\begin{equation}
 x_{n+1}=1-Ax_n^2-x_{n-1},                                 \label{eq:recurrence}
\end{equation}
equivalently the map
\begin{equation}
 H_A(q,p)=(1-Aq^2-p,q),                                    \label{eq:henon}
\end{equation}
whose Jacobian determinant is one \citep{wang2026henonmodel}.  That source
motivates the H\'enon direction through numerical spectral comparisons.  We
retain only the exact conservative recurrence.  None of its numerical
Riemann-zero fits, continuum approximations, or near-critical parameter
claims is an assumption in the present theorem.

C33 applied the cyclic period-five generating action to
\eqref{eq:recurrence}, isolated a transverse equal-action node of two exact
period-five points, and descended the product of their chronological Hill
determinants \citep{hcs2026c33note}.  The resulting collision polynomial is
\begin{align}
P_9(A)={}&110592A^9-294912A^8+159744A^7+225792A^6\notag\\
&-162816A^5-51520A^4+50672A^3+736A^2-6032A+1037. \label{eq:p9}
\end{align}
It is irreducible, and C33 certifies
\begin{equation}
 \Gal(P_9/\Q)=S_9.                                         \label{eq:s9}
\end{equation}

Put \(K=\Q[A]/(P_9)\).  The two node branches have Hill values
\(h_1,h_2\) in a quadratic algebra over \(K\).  Their product
\begin{equation}
 \beta=N_H=h_1h_2=\frac{B_8(A)}{4827099043}\in K^\times    \label{eq:beta}
\end{equation}
is fixed by branch exchange.  A common Hill normalization
\(h_i\mapsto\mu(A)h_i\) multiplies \(\beta\) by \(\mu(A)^2\), so
\([\beta]\) is intrinsic under the permitted gauge.  The polynomial
\(B_8\) is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:ledgers}.  Exact resultant
arithmetic gives
\begin{equation}
N:=\Norm_{K/\Q}(\beta)
=\frac{2^6\,13\,19^5\,41\,59^5\,5653^2}{3^5}.             \label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}
C33 used the nonsquare rational norm to prove \(\beta\notin K^{\times2}\),
and explicitly left the all-conjugate rank and full wreath group unclaimed.

Let \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_9\) be the roots of \(P_9\) in its
splitting field \(L\), and write \(\beta_i=\beta(\alpha_i)\).  We study
the relation kernel
\begin{equation}
 R=\left\{r\in\F_2^9:
       \prod_{i=1}^{9}\beta_i^{r_i}\in L^{\times2}\right\}. \label{eq:R}
\end{equation}
The Kummer rank is \(9-\dim R\).

\subsection{Two different meanings of monodromy}

Arai constructs monodromies of loops in the complex H\'enon hyperbolic
horseshoe locus and studies their action on symbolic dynamics
\citep{arai2007loops}.  That is topological continuation in a complex
parameter locus.  Here ``monodromy'' means the arithmetic Galois group of
the normal closure of a quadratic extension over the number field cut out
by \(P_9\).  The base points, acting objects, and representations are
different; the present result is not an extension of Arai's symbolic
monodromy theorem.

For a tower \(\Q\subset K\subset K(\sqrt\beta)\), embedding the Galois
group of the normal closure in a permutation wreath product is standard.
The quadratic and Kummer cases are made explicit, for example, by
\citet[Theorem~1.11 and Corollary~1.14]{barquero2023wreath}.  Newton
polygons and valuation parity in square-class questions are likewise
standard local number theory \citep{serre1979localfields}.  We use these
results without a novelty claim.
