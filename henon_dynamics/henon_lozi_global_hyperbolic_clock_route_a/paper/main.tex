\begin{abstract}
For the area-preserving Lozi family
$F_a(x,y)=(1-a|x|-y,x)$ with $a>4$, we reconstruct the entire set of
bounded two-sided orbits by a uniform contraction on sequence space.
Every binary sequence occurs once, and the whole bounded set stays a
positive distance from the nonsmooth switching line. This resolves
global completeness, not merely a finite periodic prefix.
\ifnum\CRevisionRound>0
Projective cone contraction gives the invariant stable and unstable lines.
Every period, primitive cycle, signed reciprocal multiplier and reversal
then follows with an exact affine-return formula.
\fi
\ifnum\CRevisionRound>1
We distinguish the elementary unweighted zeta from a stability-weighted
holomorphic germ with a controlled tail. The derivative also defines a
separate positive instability roof. At integral parameters its periodic
lengths cannot, after any positive rational rescaling, equal the logarithm
of an integer greater than one. Exact rational and independent symbolic
checks audit the normalization. These results establish source dynamics
and an arithmetic obstruction, not a target spectral identity.
\fi
\end{abstract}

\section{A global question beyond finite word enumeration}
Finite sign words can be solved exactly without showing that every infinite
itinerary exists or that every bounded geometric orbit has been found.
For a nonsmooth map, a coding must also resolve derivatives at its branch
boundary. We close these questions in one explicit sufficient
chamber of the globally defined map
\begin{equation}\label{map}
 F_a(x,y)=(1-a|x|-y,x),\qquad a>4.
\end{equation}
Its inverse is $(x,y)\mapsto(y,1-a|y|-x)$. Let $K_a$ be the set of points
whose full two-sided orbit is bounded. The source clock is one application
of \eqref{map}. We do not identify that clock with any later suspension.

Lozi symbolic dynamics and kneading methods have established owners
\cite{misiurewicz,zheng}. Our contribution is a directly proved, normalized
chamber theorem with independent exact evidence; no priority for the
horseshoe mechanism or cone method is claimed. Earlier project C116
certified a pruned finite prefix at $(a,b)=(2,1/2)$ of
$(1-a|x|+by,x)$. It did not prove the present statement.
The constant-diagonal affine horseshoe in C127 also does not provide the
word-dependent derivative matrices below.

\section{All bounded two-sided orbits}
Put $\Sigma=\{-1,+1\}^{\Z}$, with the left shift $\sigma$, and define
\[
 R=\frac1{a-2},\qquad q=\frac2a,\qquad
 \delta=\frac{a-4}{a(a-2)}>0.
\]
For $s\in\Sigma$ let
\[
 (\mathcal T_s x)_j=\frac{s_j}{a}(1-x_{j-1}-x_{j+1})
 \quad\text{on }\ell^\infty(\Z).
\]
\begin{theorem}\label{coding}
There is a homeomorphism $\pi_a:\Sigma\to K_a$ satisfying
$F_a\pi_a=\pi_a\sigma$. Every coordinate in the coded orbit obeys
$\delta\le s_jx_j\le R$. These are all bounded two-sided orbits in the plane.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The operator $\mathcal T_s$ contracts by $q<1$ and preserves the closed
radius-$R$ ball because $(1+2R)/a=R$. Its unique fixed sequence satisfies
\[
 s_jx_j=\frac{1-x_{j-1}-x_{j+1}}a\ge\frac{1-2R}a=\delta.
\]
Hence its signs are exactly $s_j$ and it solves
$x_{j+1}=1-a|x_j|-x_{j-1}$. Conversely, a bounded orbit with
$M=\sup_j|x_j|$ satisfies $aM\le1+2M$, so $M\le R$.
The same sign bound applies, and contraction uniqueness recovers that
orbit from its sign sequence.

Set $\pi_a(s)=(x_0,x_{-1})$. Starting the contraction at zero, the $N$th
approximation to either coordinate depends only on a finite symbol window
and has error at most $Rq^N$. This proves continuity in the product topology.
A state determines the full orbit under the continuous inverse of $F_a$,
so distinct sign sequences cannot map to the same state. Exhaustion gives
surjectivity. A continuous bijection from compact $\Sigma$ to its Hausdorff
image is a homeomorphism; the recurrence proves shift equivariance.
\end{proof}
The coding is also uniform in parameters: comparing the fixed-point
equations for $a,b>4$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{parameter}
 \|x_a(s)-x_b(s)\|_\infty
 \le \frac{|a-b|}{(a-2)(b-2)}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, after moving the $2/a$ difference term to the left, the remaining
bound is $|a^{-1}-b^{-1}|(1+2/(b-2))$.
The chamber boundary is deliberately not called sharp: at $a=4$ this
argument has zero sign margin and supplies no conclusion.

\section{Variable-matrix hyperbolicity and complete primitive data}
Define $r=(a-\sqrt{a^2-4})/2$, so $a-r=r^{-1}$ and $0<r<1$.
On the smooth sign branch $s$, the derivative is
\[
 A_s=\begin{pmatrix}-as&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det A_s=1.
\]
\begin{theorem}\label{hyperbolic}
The set $K_a$ is uniformly hyperbolic. The derivative has continuous
transverse invariant lines, with stable forward contraction and unstable
backward contraction bounded by $r^n$ in the max norm.
For every $n\ge1$, the whole plane has exactly $2^n$ points fixed by $F_a^n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $|v|\le r|u|$, the derivative gives
$|u'|\ge(a-r)|u|=r^{-1}|u|$ and $|v'|\le r|u'|$.
The slope map $t=v/u\mapsto(-as-t)^{-1}$ preserves $[-r,r]$ and
contracts by at most $r^2$. Composing from the infinite past gives a
uniformly convergent continuous unstable slope. The inverse matrices
preserve the cone $|u|\le r|v|$; composing their slope maps from the infinite
future gives the stable line with inverse-time expansion at least $r^{-1}$.
The two cones meet only at zero. In each cone its dominant coordinate
equals the max norm, giving the stated estimates with constant one.
Theorem~\ref{coding} keeps $K_a$ away from $x=0$, so these are actual
derivatives, not derivatives assigned to singular points.
The conjugacy gives $2^n$ periodic states. Every periodic orbit is bounded,
so none has been missed outside $K_a$.
\end{proof}

For a based word $w=s_0\ldots s_{n-1}$, compose in forward time:
\[
 M_w=A_{s_{n-1}}\cdots A_{s_0},\qquad
 c_w=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}A_{s_{n-1}}\cdots A_{s_{j+1}}\binom10.
\]
Empty products are identities. Hyperbolicity makes $I-M_w$ invertible,
and the unique based state is $(I-M_w)^{-1}c_w$.
Its least period is exactly the least repetition period of $w$.
Therefore the number of oriented primitive cycles is
\[
 P_n=\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)2^{n/d}.
\]
Let $\tau_w=\tr M_w$. The two real multipliers are reciprocal, with
\begin{equation}\label{multiplier}
 |\lambda_u(w)|\ge r^{-n},\qquad
 \operatorname{sign}\lambda_u(w)=(-1)^n\prod_j s_j,\qquad
 \lambda_s(w)=\lambda_u(w)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The sign follows from the sign of each dominant-coordinate factor
$-as_j-t_j$. Repeating $w$ raises $M_w$ and its multipliers to powers.
In particular the repeated traces satisfy
$\tau_{k+1}=\tau_w\tau_k-\tau_{k-1}$, $\tau_0=2$, $\tau_1=\tau_w$.

The involution $J(x,y)=(y,x)$ obeys $JF_aJ=F_a^{-1}$.
It reverses primitive words up to rotation and conjugates a return matrix
to its inverse. We retain both orientations rather than dividing the
primitive count by reversal. Branchwise determinant one and the global
piecewise-affine bijection also give area preservation.

\section{Separate source generating objects}
The unweighted Artin--Mazur zeta is
\begin{equation}\label{zeta}
 Z_a(z)=\exp\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{2^n z^n}{n}\right)
       =\frac1{1-2z}.
\end{equation}
Its ordinary primitive product converges for $|z|<1/2$; the rational
expression supplies its meromorphic continuation. It discards stability.

To retain the actual stability denominators, define instead
\[
 b_n=\sum_{x\in\Fix(F_a^n)}
      \frac1{|\det(I-DF_a^n(x))|},\qquad
 d_a(z)=\exp\left(-\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{b_nz^n}{n}\right).
\]
These definitions do not assert a trace-class operator realization.
If $L=|\lambda_u|\ge r^{-n}$, both possible signs of the multiplier satisfy
\[
 |\det(I-M_w)|\ge L(1-L^{-1})^2,\qquad
 0<b_n\le\frac{(2r)^n}{(1-r^n)^2}.
\]
Thus $d_a$ is holomorphic and nonzero for $2r|z|<1$. For its logarithm,
the omitted terms after order $N$ have absolute value at most
\begin{equation}\label{tail}
 \frac{(2r|z|)^{N+1}}{(N+1)(1-r)^2(1-2r|z|)}.
\end{equation}
Absolute convergence permits primitive-orbit regrouping, but repetition
$k$ uses $|\det(I-M_\gamma^k)|^{-1}$, not an arbitrary scalar weight
raised to the $k$th power. No continuation outside this disk is claimed.

\section{An instability roof with no exact rationally scaled integer clock}
Let $t_0^u(s)$ be the unstable slope. The positive continuous roof
\[
 \ell(s)=\log|-as_0-t_0^u(s)|\ge\log r^{-1}
\]
is derived from the source derivative. Its periodic sum is
$\log|\lambda_u(w)|$, by multiplying the dominant-coordinate factors.
It defines a new suspension, not the one-step time used in \eqref{zeta}.

\begin{theorem}\label{unit}
For integral $a\ge5$, every positive rational $c$ and every periodic word
$w$ satisfy
\[
 c\log|\lambda_u(w)|\ne\log m\qquad(m\in\Z,\ m>1).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The matrix $M_w$ belongs to $SL_2(\Z)$ and has $|\tau_w|>2$.
For $T=|\tau_w|\ge3$, the integer $T^2-4$ lies strictly between
$(T-1)^2$ and $T^2$, hence is nonsquare. The multiplier is a quadratic
algebraic integer unit with nontrivial conjugate its reciprocal.
If $|\lambda_u|^h$ were rational for $h\ge1$, conjugation would equate it
to $|\lambda_u|^{-h}$, contrary to expansion. Writing $c=h/j$,
the displayed equality would give $|\lambda_u|^h=m^j$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}
The result excludes primes and composites equally. It does not rule out
approximate matching, irrational clock rescaling or a different roof.
The nonintegral rational parameters in the numerical audit are not included
in this unit theorem.
