# Narrative report

P55 ended with a precise but open instruction: find a growing sequence of
higher-block incidence relations and test whether its Galois-excess
discrepancy can decay at a Hölder rate.  P56 finds that sequence.

The two cycle families differ only by a fixed marker after one long zero
run.  Lengthening either family inserts one zero.  At matching block width,
the insertion changes the cyclic incidence row by the same three atoms.
This is why a relation persists at every width; it is not an accidental
null vector in a finite matrix.

The first new equation needs one period-six orbit.  Reflection symmetry
reduces that orbit to three radical coordinates, and its multiplier field is
only degree four.  Its nonphysical excess is modest, while each period-five
excess contains a common large embedding.  The forced four-block equality
therefore fails by a large exact margin.

The finite witness nevertheless interpolates at width five.  That negative
control is mathematically important: it prevents the finite obstruction
from being mislabeled as an unrestricted regularity theorem.  The genuine
next target is now the asymptotic sequence `Delta_m`.  A recurrence or
nondecay theorem for that sequence would be a major Route-A bridge result.
