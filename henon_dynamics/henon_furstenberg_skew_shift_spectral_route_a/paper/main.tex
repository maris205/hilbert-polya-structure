\begin{abstract}
For every irrational $\alpha$, we solve the affine iterates of
$T_\alpha(x,y)=(x+\alpha,y+x)$ on the two-torus.  No positive iterate has a
fixed point, so the Artin--Mazur zeta is one.  On Haar $L^2$, the Koopman
operator acts explicitly on Fourier modes and admits an involutive reversal.
This establishes a sharp contrast between natural operator structure and an
empty periodic-orbit ledger.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Furstenberg skew shift; irrational rotation;
Koopman operator; Fourier decomposition; bilateral shift; time reversal.

\section{Iterates and periodic points}
Induction gives
\[
T_\alpha^n(x,y)=\left(x+n\alpha,
y+nx+\binom n2\alpha\right)\pmod1.
\]
A fixed point would imply $n\alpha=0\pmod1$, impossible for irrational
$\alpha$.  Thus $\#\operatorname{Fix}(T_\alpha^n)=0$ for every $n\ge1$ and
\[
\zeta_{AM}(z)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge1}
\#\operatorname{Fix}(T_\alpha^n)z^n/n\right)=1.
\]
The irrational hypothesis is essential: rational parameters can close in the
first coordinate and lie outside the frozen family.  In particular, the
constant zeta here is a proved empty-data statement, not a regularized
determinant or an approximation.
\section{Fourier action and reversal}
For $e_{m,k}=e^{2\pi i(mx+ky)}$ and $Uf=f\circ T_\alpha$,
\[
Ue_{m,k}=e^{2\pi i m\alpha}e_{m+k,k}.
\]
The $k=0$ sector is pure point.  For $k\ne0$, residue classes of $m$ modulo
$|k|$ give $|k|$ invariant weighted bilateral shifts.  Their unimodular
weights are removed recursively by diagonal unitaries, so every chain is
unitarily equivalent to the bilateral shift.  Summing over all $k\ne0$
gives Lebesgue spectrum of countably infinite multiplicity.  Moreover
$R(x,y)=(\alpha-x,y)$ obeys $R^2=I$ and
$RT_\alpha R=T_\alpha^{-1}$, so complex conjugation after $R$ is an
antiunitary reversal.
Indeed $RT_\alpha R(x,y)=(x-\alpha,y-x+\alpha)=T_\alpha^{-1}(x,y)$.
Haar invariance follows because $T_\alpha$ is an invertible affine toral map
whose linear part has determinant one.  Consequently $U$ is unitary with the
same clock and normalization as the source dynamics.
\section{Operator-ideal boundary}
The Fourier basis is carried bijectively to another orthonormal basis.  Thus
$U$ is not compact.  Every singular value of a unitary equals one, so $U$
belongs to no finite Schatten class.  For $z\ne0$, $zU$ is not trace class;
therefore the ordinary Fredholm determinant $\det(I-zU)$ is unavailable.
