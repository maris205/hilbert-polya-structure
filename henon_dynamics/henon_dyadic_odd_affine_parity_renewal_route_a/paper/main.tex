\begin{abstract}
For odd integers $a,b$, consider the parity map on the 2-adic integers that
sends an even $x$ to $x/2$ and an odd $x$ to $(ax+b)/2$.  Classical parity
coding conjugates this family to the one-sided binary shift; we use that
conjugacy as prior foundation, not as a novelty claim.  We derive an exact
first-return renewal description on the odd coset.  The return time is
$\tau(x)=v_2(ax+b)$ with conditional Haar law $2^{-k}$, and the return map is
a countable full shift with roof $r(k)=k$.  Its ordinary Artin--Mazur series
is unavailable because it has infinitely many fixed points, but the roof
recovers the original clock: $(1-\sum_{k\ge1}z^k)^{-1}=(1-z)/(1-2z)$, and
restoring the zero orbit gives $(1-2z)^{-1}$.  Moreover both the unweighted
fixed counts and reciprocal 2-adic derivative stability sums are independent
of every odd parameter pair.  Exact solvability therefore supplies a
parameter-blindness obstruction, not an arithmetic target model.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} 2-adic dynamics; parity coding; first return;
renewal shift; dynamical zeta; Koopman isometry.

\section{Frozen family and ownership boundary}
Fix odd $a,b\in\mathbb Z$ and normalized Haar probability $\mu$ on $\Ztwo$.
The one-tick map is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T}
T_{a,b}(x)=
\begin{cases}
x/2,&x\equiv0\pmod2,\\
(ax+b)/2,&x\equiv1\pmod2.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Oddness is structural: each numerator in the active branch is even.  The
parity vector $Q_{a,b}(x)=\sum_{j\ge0}\epsilon_j(x)2^j$, with
$\epsilon_j(x)=T^j(x)\bmod2$, conjugates $T_{a,b}$ to the dyadic shift.
Bernstein and Lagarias treated this conjugacy and its odd $ax+b$
generality~\cite{bernstein-lagarias}.  We claim no priority for it.  The
question here is what first-return acceleration does to clock and parameter
information.

If $s_j=\sum_{i<j}\epsilon_i$, the inverse parity series is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse}
Q_{a,b}^{-1}\!\left(\sum_{j\ge0}\epsilon_j2^j\right)
=-b\sum_{j\ge0}\epsilon_j2^j a^{-s_{j+1}}.
\end{equation}
It also shows that $Q_{a,b}$ preserves congruence cylinders and hence Haar
measure.  Multiplication by $b$ conjugates $T_{a,1}$ to $T_{a,b}$.

\section{Fixed words and stability blindness}
For a word $\epsilon=(\epsilon_0,\ldots,\epsilon_{n-1})$, put
\[
A_\epsilon=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\epsilon_j2^j a^{s_n-s_{j+1}}.
\]
Branch induction gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iterate}
2^nT^n(x)=a^{s_n}x+bA_\epsilon.
\end{equation}
Since $2^n-a^{s_n}$ is odd, every word has the unique fixed point
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed}
x_\epsilon=\frac{bA_\epsilon}{2^n-a^{s_n}}\in\Ztwo.
\end{equation}
The parity conjugacy makes all $2^n$ words admissible and distinct.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counts}
\#\Fix(T^n)=2^n,
\quad P(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)2^d,
\quad \zeta_{\rm AM}(z)=\frac1{1-2z}.
\end{equation}
Here $P(n)$ counts exact-period points, so $P(n)/n$ counts primitive cycles.

The branch derivatives are $1/2$ and $a/2$.  On the point with word
$\epsilon$,
\[
(T^n)'=\frac{a^{s_n}}{2^n},\qquad
\left|1-\frac{a^{s_n}}{2^n}\right|_2=2^n,
\]
because $2^n-a^{s_n}$ is odd.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stab}
W_n:=\sum_{x\in\Fix(T^n)}|1-(T^n)'(x)|_2^{-1}=1,
\qquad \zeta_{\rm stab}(z)=\frac1{1-z}.
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:counts} and~\eqref{eq:stab} are all-parameter theorems:
both invariants erase $a$ and $b$.

\section{First-return renewal and clock recovery}
Let $O=1+2\Ztwo$.  For $x\in O$ with $ax+b\ne0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:return}
\tau(x)=\val(ax+b),\qquad R(x)=\frac{ax+b}{2^{\tau(x)}}.
\end{equation}
The point $x_*=-b/a$ maps to zero and has infinite return time.  More
generally, let $E$ be the set of odd points whose parity strings contain
only finitely many ones.  Under the parity homeomorphism, $E$ is the union
over all finite binary prefixes of one eventually-zero tail; it is therefore
countable and Haar-null, and it contains $x_*$.  Its complement
$O_\infty=O\setminus E$ is invariant under successive returns and has full
conditional Haar measure.

The affine map $x\mapsto ax+b$ sends $O$ bijectively and
measure-preservingly onto $2\Ztwo$.  Hence the normalized Haar layers give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
\mu_O(\tau=k)=2^{-k},\qquad k\ge1.
\end{equation}
The parity block associated with $k$ is $10^{k-1}$.  Concatenation gives a
bijection from $O_\infty$ to $\{1,2,\ldots\}^{\mathbb N}$, conjugating $R$
to the left shift.  Bernoulli independence of disjoint parity blocks then
makes successive returns iid with the geometric product measure.  The
constant block $k$ has fixed point
\begin{equation}\label{eq:returnfixed}
x_k=\frac b{2^k-a}.
\end{equation}
Thus $R$ already has infinitely many time-one fixed points and its ordinary
Artin--Mazur series is undefined.

Return symbol $k$ represents $k$ original ticks.  With roof $r(k)=k$, the
first-return series and renewal zeta are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:roof}
F(z)=\sum_{k\ge1}z^k=\frac z{1-z},\qquad
\zeta_{\rm roof}(z)=\frac1{1-F(z)}=\frac{1-z}{1-2z}.
\end{equation}
Indeed
$\log\zeta_{\rm roof}=\sum_{n\ge1}(2^n-1)z^n/n$: these are exactly the
original-clock periodic points whose binary words contain a one.  The
omitted all-zero fixed orbit contributes $(1-z)^{-1}$, and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recovery}
\zeta_{\rm roof}(z)(1-z)^{-1}=\frac1{1-2z}=\zeta_{\rm AM}(z).
\end{equation}

\section{Operator boundary and Route-A decision}
On $H=L^2(\Ztwo,\mu)$, $Uf=f\circ T$ is an isometry.  It is not surjective:
under parity coding, its range consists of functions independent of the
first digit.  The tail intersection $\bigcap_{n\ge0}U^nH$ consists only of
constants, while $H\ominus UH$ is an infinite-dimensional copy of the tail
$L^2$ space.  The Wold decomposition is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wold}
U\simeq I_{\mathbb C}\oplus S^{(\aleph_0)}.
\end{equation}
In particular $\sigma(U)=\overline{\mathbb D}$ and
$\sigma_p(U)=\{1\}$.  The isometry is noncompact and lies in no finite
Schatten class, so no ordinary Fredholm determinant $\det(I-zU)$ is
available.  The two-sided inverse-limit shift is a same-clock unitary
extension, but it changes the phase space and is not a repair of the frozen
candidate.  Koopman methods have also been studied for the discrete
positive-integer $3x+1$ system~\cite{leventides-poulios}; we make no general
priority claim for a Collatz Koopman lift.

\section{Limitations and reproducibility}
For $(a,b)=(3,1)$ the 2-adic word $100$ gives
$1/5\mapsto4/5\mapsto2/5\mapsto1/5$.  This legal $\Ztwo$ cycle is not a
positive-integer orbit, so the paper gives no progress on the positive-integer
$3x+1$ conjecture.  If exactly one of $a,b$ is even, the odd-branch
numerator is odd and division by two leaves $\Ztwo$; the theorem does not
cross that boundary.
