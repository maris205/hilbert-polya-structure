# Source and ownership audit

## Primary source anchor

Trung Van Pham, “Orbits of rotor-router operation and stationary distribution of random walks on directed graphs,” arXiv:1403.5875, *Advances in Applied Mathematics* 70 (2015), 45–53, DOI:10.1016/j.aam.2015.06.006, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.5875>.

The author, title, arXiv identifier, journal metadata, and DOI are locked to the source record. Pham's Theorem 1 gives the exact common recurrent-orbit length and orbit count, and its proof establishes primitive return through a deleted-basepoint chip-addition quotient and the order of a Laplacian row class. These facts and their relationship with directed spanning trees are classical prior work. The Markov-chain tree theorem and finite-permutation spectral facts are also not claimed as new.

## Package contribution boundary

C181 packages the source-locked classical orbit theorem into an all-multigraph synthesis with explicit vertex and distinguished-arc frequencies, fixed-count/zeta/determinant/spectrum consequences, the Eulerian degeneration, and a Route-A stopping theorem. Its exhaustive small-graph ledger, independent checker, CAS layer, replay, mutations, and release manifest are reproducibility artifacts, not substitutes for proof and not literature-priority claims.

## Sandpile separation

The frozen object has no sink and no stabilization operation. Its recurrent states are unicycles, and one deterministic rotor advance plus chip move is one tick. C181 imports Pham's exact-return theorem rather than claiming that flow balance alone proves primitivity. The source proof temporarily deletes the chosen basepoint's outgoing arcs, uses chip addition on the remaining acyclic rotors, and computes a Laplacian row-class order; this proof device is not an identification of the frozen sinkless dynamics with translation on a sandpile critical group or recurrent sandpile torsor. Once exact length is source-locked, balance, the positive Laplacian kernel, and the direct unicycle count derive the local frequencies and downstream finite-cycle consequences. This separates C181 from the repository’s earlier sandpile-translation subtype.
