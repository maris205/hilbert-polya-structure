\begin{abstract}
For $N$ indistinguishable equal-mass rods of length $a$ on a circle of
circumference $\ell>Na$, we identify the exact dynamical object on which the
usual excluded-volume compression is globally valid.
\ifnum\CRevisionRound=0
After quotienting constant spatial rotations and gluing elastic collision
faces, compression to the free length $L=\ell-Na$ conjugates the hard-rod flow
to free phase points modulo common translations and pair permutations.
\fi
\ifnum\CRevisionRound>0
The conjugacy resolves binary, disjoint simultaneous, and genuine multiple
collisions, preserves momentum and energy, and proves an explicit
compact-interval no-Zeno bound.  It also exposes a necessary boundary: the

already demonstrates.
\fi
\ifnum\CRevisionRound>1

target claim.
\fi
\end{abstract}

\section{The corrected phase space}
The frozen release obstruction identifier is \texttt{HEN-O280}.
Write $\T_r=\R/r\Z$ and orient it clockwise.  A rod is represented by its
left endpoint.  Choose a cyclic lift $x_1,\ldots,x_N\in\R$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chamber}
 x_{i+1}-x_i\geq a\quad(1\leq i<N),
 \qquad x_1+\ell-x_N\geq a .
\end{equation}
The free gaps are the left sides of~\eqref{eq:chamber} minus $a$; they are
nonnegative and sum to
\[
 L=\ell-Na>0.
\]
We quotient global constant rotations $x_i\mapsto x_i+r$ and cyclic changes
of the first rod.  On a contact face, incoming and outgoing velocity labels
are glued by the equal-mass elastic reflection.  At an intersection of faces,
the gluing is generated by the adjacent transpositions in the maximal contact
block.  The resulting rotation-reduced, unlabeled, collision-glued phase
space is denoted by $\cR_{N,\ell,a}$.

The free target is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:freequotient}
 \cF_{N,L}=\bigl((\T_L\times\R)^N\bigr)
 \big/\bigl(S_N\times\T_L\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $S_N$ permutes position--velocity pairs and $c\in\T_L$ sends every
$y_i$ to $y_i+c$ but leaves every velocity unchanged.  Thus common drift is
retained as phase data even though its spatial motion disappears in shape
space.  No time-dependent Galilean quotient is taken.

\section{Compression and complete-flow conjugacy}
For a representative satisfying~\eqref{eq:chamber}, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compression}
 y_i=x_i-(i-1)a,\qquad 1\leq i\leq N,
\end{equation}
and reduce the $y_i$ modulo $L$ in~\eqref{eq:freequotient}.

\begin{lemma}[Cyclic-start identity]\label{lem:cyclic}
If the next rod is chosen as the first rod, then the compressed lifted list is
\[
 (y_2+a,\ldots,y_N+a,y_1+L+a).
\]
Consequently its class in~\eqref{eq:freequotient} is unchanged.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The new lifted endpoint list is
$(x_2,\ldots,x_N,x_1+\ell)$.  Subtracting $(i-1)a$ gives the displayed list,
because $\ell-(N-1)a=L+a$.  Modulo $L$ this is a cyclic permutation followed
by one common translation by $a$.  A global rod rotation similarly becomes a
common translation.  Hence~\eqref{eq:compression} is well defined precisely
on the reduced source.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact shape conjugacy]\label{thm:conjugacy}
Compression induces a bijection
\[
 C:\cR_{N,\ell,a}\longrightarrow\cF_{N,L}.
\]
For every $t\in\R$, it conjugates the complete hard-rod flow $R_t$ to the free
flow
\begin{equation}\label{eq:freeflow}
 F_t[(y_i,v_i)_{i=1}^N]=[(y_i+t v_i,v_i)_{i=1}^N],
 \qquad C R_t=F_t C.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In cyclic order,~\eqref{eq:compression} converts the free rod gaps exactly
into the cyclic gaps between the $y_i$; both are nonnegative and sum to $L$.
Conversely, cyclically order any representative of the free points, choose
lifts $y_1\leq\cdots\leq y_N\leq y_1+L$, and put
$x_i=y_i+(i-1)a$.  Then~\eqref{eq:chamber} holds.  Common translation changes
only the removed rod rotation, a different first point gives the cyclic
relation in Lemma~\ref{lem:cyclic}, and a permutation changes no unlabeled
state.  Ties are exactly contact faces, where the collision gluing removes
the remaining velocity-order ambiguity.  This proves bijectivity.

Between contacts, subtracting constants in~\eqref{eq:compression} does not
change velocities, so both sides follow~\eqref{eq:freeflow}.  At an ordinary
equal-mass collision, the rods exchange velocities; letting the two free
phase points cross produces the same unlabeled pair.  Adjacent
transpositions generate every permutation within a simultaneous contact
block.  Therefore the identity persists through every collision, and the
free group defines both forward and backward hard-rod evolution without an
event-order convention.
\end{proof}

Jepsen's equal-mass hard-rod dynamics and free-crossing mechanism are
classical~\cite{Jepsen1965}, as is the excluded-volume setting of the Tonks
gas~\cite{Tonks1936}.  Our result is a normalized source-model closure and not
a claim of literature priority.

\section{All events, conservation, and no Zeno}
Fix a free representative.  For $i\ne j$ with $v_i\ne v_j$, its pair
coincidences are exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eventset}
 E_{ij}=\{t\in\R:(y_i-y_j)+t(v_i-v_j)\in L\Z\}.
\end{equation}
At one event time, group all indices with the same compressed position.  A
group with at least two distinct velocities is a maximal collision block;
different positions give simultaneous disjoint blocks.

\begin{theorem}[All-event law]\label{thm:events}
Immediately before a collision block, its spatial velocity list is decreasing
up to ties; immediately after, it is increasing.  Thus the event replaces the
incoming list by the same multiset in sorted order.  Binary collision is
velocity exchange, and a genuine multiple collision is independent of any
binary resolution order in the collision-glued quotient.

Every event conserves
\[
 P=\sum_{i=1}^Nv_i,
 \qquad
 E=\frac12\sum_{i=1}^Nv_i^2.
\]
For a horizon $H\geq0$, each unequal-velocity pair has at most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bound}
 \left\lfloor\frac{H|v_i-v_j|}{L}\right\rfloor+2
\end{equation}
coincidences in $[0,H]$.  Hence only finitely many aggregated events occur on
every compact interval: the flow has no Zeno accumulation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Near a common free position at time $t_0$, the local positions are
$y_0+(t-t_0)v_i$.  For $t<t_0$, increasing spatial order therefore reads the
velocities from large to small; for $t>t_0$, it reads them from small to large.
Sorting is a permutation, so its first and second power sums, hence $P$ and
$E$, are unchanged.

Equation~\eqref{eq:eventset} is one affine congruence.  As $t$ ranges over
$[0,H]$, its left side crosses an interval of length $H|v_i-v_j|$; the number
of multiples of $L$ in that interval is bounded by~\eqref{eq:bound}.  Summing
over the finitely many pairs proves local finiteness.  If $v_i=v_j$, the two
free points either never coincide or remain coincident; the latter is a
persistent contact and not an isolated event.
\end{proof}

Initial contacts are allowed because the phase space glues both one-sided
velocity assignments.  The construction is reversible for all real time.
The strict inequality $L>0$ is indispensable; at close packing the target
circle collapses.

\section{Why the unreduced statement is false}
Lemma~\ref{lem:cyclic} shows the obstruction directly: changing the cyclic
starting rod translates all compressed points by $a$.  A permutation quotient
alone does not remove that translation.  There is an even shorter dynamical
counterexample.  For $N=1$ and $v\ne0$, the full physical rod returns after
\begin{equation}\label{eq:falseperiod}
 T_{\rm physical}=\frac{\ell}{|v|},
\end{equation}
whereas a naive point on the free circle $L=\ell-a$ would return after
$L/|v|$.  After rotation reduction, the configurational shape is one point
and every fixed-velocity phase state is an equilibrium, exactly as
Theorem~\ref{thm:conjugacy} says.

Thus the complete physical rotation angle is an additional cocycle.  This
paper neither reconstructs it nor claims a conjugacy of the unreduced phase
space.  The obstruction is part of the theorem boundary, not a removable
choice of notation.

\section{Complete return criterion}
A return in~\eqref{eq:freequotient} may use both defining symmetries.
Consequently a state returns at $T>0$ if and only if there exist
$\sigma\in S_N$ and $c\in\T_L$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:return}
 y_i+Tv_i\equiv y_{\sigma(i)}+c\pmod L,
 \qquad v_i=v_{\sigma(i)},\quad 1\leq i\leq N.
\end{equation}
The common $c$ is mandatory: deleting it would restore the angle that was
removed from the source.

For a velocity value $u$, let
\[
 Y_u=\{y_i:v_i=u\}
\]
be a multiset in $\T_L$ and define
\[
 H_u=\Stab(Y_u)=\{h\in\T_L:Y_u+h=Y_u\}.
\]
It is a finite cyclic subgroup.  Write its order as $d_u$; then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hd}
 H_u=\left\{\frac{kL}{d_u}:0\leq k<d_u\right\},
 \qquad d_u\mid |Y_u|.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Stabilizer and minimal-period theorem]\label{thm:return}
For $T>0$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the reduced hard-rod state returns at $T$;
\item the equation~\eqref{eq:return} holds for some $\sigma,c$;
\item the cosets $Tu+H_u$, over all distinct velocities $u$, have nonempty
      common intersection;
\item for every pair of distinct velocity values $u,w$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lcmcriterion}
 \lcm(d_u,d_w)T(u-w)\in L\Z.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Let
\[
 \mathcal W=\{\lcm(d_u,d_w)(u-w):u<w\}.
\]
If there is only one velocity value, the shape state is fixed and has no least
positive period.  Otherwise a positive return exists exactly when the nonzero
elements of $\mathcal W$ are commensurable.  If
$\mathcal W\subset\omega\Z$ with primitive integer coefficients, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minperiod}
 T_{\min}=\frac L\omega.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The equivalence of the first two statements is the conjugacy and the
definition of the quotient.  Since $\sigma$ must preserve velocity values,
equation~\eqref{eq:return} restricted to one class is the multiset identity
$Y_u+Tu=Y_u+c$, or $c\in Tu+H_u$.  This proves the third statement.

Two such cosets intersect precisely when
$T(u-w)\in H_u+H_w$.  By~\eqref{eq:Hd}, their sum is
$H_{\lcm(d_u,d_w)}$, giving~\eqref{eq:lcmcriterion}.  Pairwise compatibility
is sufficient: choose a common multiple of all $d_u$ and identify the finite
subgroups with subgroups of one finite cyclic group; the assertion becomes
the generalized Chinese remainder theorem for congruence classes.  This
proves the fourth equivalence.

Condition~\eqref{eq:lcmcriterion} is $Tz/L\in\Z$ for every
$z\in\mathcal W$.  Such a $T$ exists exactly when the finitely many nonzero
$z$ lie in one discrete cyclic subgroup of $\R$.  Taking its primitive
positive generator $\omega$ gives~\eqref{eq:minperiod}; Bezout's identity for
the primitive integer coefficients proves that no smaller positive $T$ works.
\end{proof}

If all velocities are pairwise distinct, every $Y_u$ is a singleton and
$d_u=1$.  Hence return reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distinct}
 T(v_i-v_j)\in L\Z\quad\hbox{for all }i,j.
\end{equation}
In particular, $(0,1,\sqrt2)$ has no positive return:~\eqref{eq:distinct}
would make both $T/L$ and $\sqrt2T/L$ integers.  With repeated velocities,
the factors $d_u$ can shorten a period.  The exact certificate includes two
velocity classes on $L=12$, each with order-two stabilizer; velocities
$\pm1$ then have reduced minimal period $3$, rather than the generic value
$6$.
