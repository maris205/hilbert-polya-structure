# Narrative report

The central distinction is between direction and speed.  The sign of
\(\mathbf E\log\rho\) determines which end of the line wins, whereas a
ballistic speed requires a stronger first-moment inequality.  The gap between
these tests creates the genuinely different transient zero-speed phase.

The paper begins locally: a quenched gambler's-ruin recurrence yields an exact
scale function in every finite environment.  The spatial strong law then turns
the scale weights into the three directional regimes.  For speed, first-hit
crossing times form a stationary ergodic sequence under the annealed law.  Their
mean is an explicit random-product series.  Finite mean gives the reciprocal
velocity; infinite mean is handled by truncating each crossing time before
applying Birkhoff, then letting the truncation grow.  This proves
\(T_n/n\to\infty\) almost surely and, by inversion of the running maximum,
zero velocity.  Thus the hard zero-speed conclusion is analytic rather than a
finite-data extrapolation.

The Beta family makes every wall visible: digamma monotonicity controls
direction, while the two rational Beta moments locate the narrower ballistic
cones.  Constant environments recover ordinary biased random walk.

The retained mathematical advance is a complete, convention-locked
reconstruction of this classical theorem family.  It is not a priority claim.
The Route-A evaluation is negative because random spatial biases and crossing
times possess no intrinsic arithmetic-prime ownership or target spectral
meaning.
