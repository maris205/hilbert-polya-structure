# Hostile audit

The mutation suite repaired every affected JSON payload hash before invoking
the independent checker.  It attacked identity, source, epoch, scope,
evaluator authority, exact-flow and theorem contracts, the matroid and tie
guards, actual support rates, simple and repeated case entries, Morse--Bott
dimensions, Route-A tuple/verdict/lock, forbidden flags, the C185 boundary,
and source owners.  A dedicated repaired-hash scope-escalation attack changes
the first canonical nonclaim into a positive target Euler/root-number claim.
Another repaired-hash attack forges the subset-sum collision-boundary text;
the complete canonical boundary map is locked recursively.
Three named regressions replace the S1 rank, a Pluecker
subset index, and an S2 eigenvalue by Boolean `true`.  A systematic lane also
replaces a representative numeric 0/1 leaf in each of all 36 structural path
classes by its Boolean lookalike.  Stale hashes, missing/unknown/duplicate keys, nested
unknown keys, and non-finite JSON constants were rejected.

The YAML lane attacked every class of frozen semantics: metadata and artifact
paths; candidate, obstruction, source, scope, and evaluator identity; each
axis verdict, evidence status, strongest evidence, strongest failure, and
artifact list; the tuple and overall verdict; the Route-B Boolean and lock
reason; forbidden-claim Booleans; theorem status, finite-evidence role, and
source-owner tokens.  Missing and unknown nested fields, exact-type changes,
duplicate keys, non-string keys, merge keys, anchors, and aliases were also
rejected.  In particular, integer zero cannot masquerade as Boolean false.
All 116 attacks failed closed.

Release checks additionally reject sidecars, producer imports in the
checker, stale evidence/evaluation/PDF hashes, altered manifest membership,
nonembedded fonts, missing round-specific PDF text, and settled LaTeX
warnings.

The central mathematical hostile question is whether tied subset sums can
destroy the claimed Schubert-cell limit.  The proof never assumes all subset
sums are distinct: nonzero Pluecker indices form the bases of a representable
matroid, distinct element weights give a unique greedy extremal basis, and
the convergence rate is computed from the next weight actually present in
that support.  For repeated eigenvalues the theorem instead retains the
entire tied leading component and identifies its associated-graded subspace.
