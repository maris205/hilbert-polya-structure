# Narrative report

C35 converted the infinite dilation orbit of the H6 Poisson boundary into a
candidate Mellin problem. C36 takes the largest possible step: it constructs
the forced two-sign matrix symbol and asks whether exact functional-equation
symmetry and critical-line unitarity protect the Riemann divisor.

They do not. The parity basis exposes an even symbol \(A\) and odd symbol
\(B\). The formal scattering matrix is diagonal with entries
\(A(1-z)/A(z)\) and \(B(1-z)/B(z)\), so reciprocity and critical-line
unitarity are algebraic. A complex-ball/Rouch\'e certificate nevertheless
places one simple zero of \(A\) in a radius-\(10^{-12}\) disc centered at
\(0.7286922241147175+1.6054479123346985i\). The mirror and odd factors are
nonzero on the required discs. The determinant therefore gains an
off-critical pole and its mirror zero, together with conjugates.

This rejects the unrenormalized candidate and establishes a reusable
obstruction: scattering symmetry does not control the divisor. The natural
linear parent has no matching factor, and post hoc zero removal is
inadmissible. Mellin diagonalization also gives no ordinary Fredholm
determinant because a nontrivial multiplier on non-atomic \(L^2\) is not
compact.

The failure identifies a sharper pivot. For the homogeneous H\'enon phase
\(2q^3\), the Mellin symbols reduce to gamma--trigonometric functions with no
open-strip divisor. Exact scaling homogeneity also makes the ambient cocycle
a coboundary. The next paper must therefore prove a Poisson-boundary index or
anomaly, or close this polynomial-chirp branch honestly.
