## NG2-V: original all-dimensional Vieta question, continued

Retain NL424-2 without changing its quantifiers: every integer `n>=3`,
every `a in Z`, the full lattice `Z^n`, and one tick of

`F_(n,a)(x_1,...,x_n)=(x_2,...,x_n,product_(j=2)^n x_j+a-x_1)`.

Classify every ordinary periodic point and its native least period,
on all levels of `K=sum x_j²-product x_j-a sum x_j`, with zeros,
units, all signs and singular levels included. The arithmetic carrier is
the invariant integral dynamics, not a target prime or Euler product.

This is a continuation, not a newly generated independent candidate.
C421 owns `n=3`; the first pass already proves the unbounded two-zero
signed-rotation locus at `a=0`. Hu–Tan–Zhang own the unforced
Markoff–Hurwitz automorphism and dihedral mechanisms. Counting positive
integer solutions modulo a group is not this fixed-clock problem.

The specific new route to test is adjacent-recurrence subtraction for
the periodic scalar sequence, followed by a maximum principle or a
uniform extremal-support reduction for its higher-order difference
equation. The forcing should cancel before any parameter bound is
claimed. Check whether the three-variable tridiagonal identity actually
survives the changed dimension. A valid exhaustion must still classify
every unbounded channel; more sampled heights cannot replace this.

The cheap decisive test is symbolic derivation plus boundary control,
not another census. If the new difference equation has no proved global
exhaustion, retain the full original question as unclosed. No `n=4`
slice, positive chamber, or a newly excluded zero locus may replace it.

## NG2-F: full integral fixed-word Fricke return problem

For every ordered `(A,B,C) in Z^3`, on the full lattice `Z^3`, put

`K=x²+y²+z²-xyz-Ax-By-Cz`,

`s_x(x,y,z)=(A+yz-x,y,z)`,

`s_y(x,y,z)=(x,B+xz-y,z)`,

`s_z(x,y,z)=(x,y,C+xy-z)`,

and use the native map `T=s_z after s_y after s_x`.
All three factors, in that order, constitute one tick. There are no
denominators or excluded affine points. Every integer level `K=D`,
including every singular level, is in scope. The object to classify is
the complete ordinary periodic locus, with least periods and effective
integer parametrization or proved exhaustive finite residual rules.
No universal period bound is assumed in advance.

This coefficient family is called the Fricke-form cubic family. Its
integral invariant polynomial is the arithmetic carrier. No assertion
is made that every coefficient quadruple has integral boundary traces
of an actual four-holed-sphere representation.

Closest local predecessors: C421 treats the equal-forcing cyclic
single-step map; the earlier C409–C413 geometric scout treats isolated
complex periodic counts for cubic automorphisms. Neither wording is
this full integer fixed-word classification, but their mechanisms must
be subtracted. When `A=B=C=a`, the present map equals `F_(3,a)^3`;
that equal-parameter slice is wholly old and its clock must not be
misreported as the single cyclic tick.

Closest new source: Eunju Shin, *Character varieties on a four-holed
sphere*, arXiv 2308.16614v3 (1 June 2026), whose Theorems 1.2–1.3
decide group-orbit equivalence, not fixed-word periodicity. The sign
convention is related by `z -> -z`. Source subtraction also includes
the classical positive-entropy cubic-surface escape/compactness theory
if its precise hypotheses can be verified.

Test route: a labelled height graph must control repeated words
`xyz xyz ...`, including fixed-factor loops and low-coordinate
horizontal edges. A generic finite group-orbit representative is
insufficient. First test whether an explicit periodic-point height
bound on each level is already a short source consequence; a resulting
finite graph search alone is not automatically a paper-level increment.
The stronger intended atlas requires a new full-parameter exhaustion.

Replacement boundary: do not count a new coefficient, a clock change,
general decidability, or a source-owned trace formula as the missing
substantial theorem. If those are all that survives, reject the current
paper proposal while distinguishing an unclosed atlas from a proved
impossibility result.
