## Primary source ledger

### D1. Reduction of dynatomic curves

John R. Doyle, Holly Krieger, Andrew Obus, Rachel Pries,
Simon Rubinstein-Salzedo and Lloyd W. West.
[arXiv:1703.04172v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04172v2), submitted
2017-03-12, revised 2017-03-29; journal reference ETDS 39 (2019),
2717–2768, DOI 10.1017/etds.2017.140.

Actual access: author PDF introduction/definitions; then
[arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1703.04172v2), selected Section 6
propositions and Proposition 7.1 including its proof. Not all 47 pages.
Later author-PDF opens timed out; NSF journal-PDF retrieval also failed.

Verified scope: Proposition 7.1 proves bad reduction for $p\nmid m$,
$p\mid n>3$. For odd quadratic degree this covers the frozen tower
except $(p,e)=(3,1)$; it does not assert reducibility.
V2 Proposition 6.14 requires $p\nmid(m-1)mn$ to transfer irreducibility
between $Y_0$ and $Y_1$. Its smooth totally ramified-point argument is
outside the frozen wild locus. Proposition 6.2 gives reduced special
fibres here, not a component count. The journal search extract numbers
the irreducibility proposition 6.15; the proof anchor used is **v2 6.14**,
so the numbering is not mixed. Theorem B is a discriminant-conditional
$Y_0$ statement, not an unconditional all-tower $Y_1$ conclusion.

### D2. Gonality of dynatomic curves and strong uniform boundedness

John R. Doyle and Bjorn Poonen.
[arXiv:1711.04233v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04233v2), submitted
2017-11-12, revised 2019-01-01; Compositio Math. 156 (2020), 733–743,
DOI 10.1112/S0010437X20007022.
[Author PDF](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/dyn_gonality.pdf)
has internal date 2018-12-30.

Actual access: metadata and author PDF introduction through Section 2,
especially definition in Section 1.1, Theorem 1.1, Remark 1.3,
Theorem 2.1 and its following paragraph; not a complete ten-page proof
read. The Cambridge landing-page retrieval failed.

Quantifier correction: their “dynatomic curve” in Theorem 1.1 is defined
by **an irreducible factor** of an iterate-difference polynomial.
Geometric irreducibility of each such factor is not irreducibility of
the entire standard $\Phi_n$. Remark 1.3 and the paragraph after
Theorem 2.1 explicitly leave the latter identification unproved in
positive characteristic in that source. This is a historical
source-scope statement, not a claim that no later solution exists.

### L1. Livšic analogy — not an applicable polynomial theorem

Boris Kalinin, *Livšic Theorem for matrix cocycles*, Annals of Mathematics
173 (2011), 1025–1042, DOI 10.4007/annals.2011.173.2.11.
[Official journal metadata and abstract](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2011/173-2/p11)
were read; full proof not read.

Its hypotheses are real matrix cocycles over a hyperbolic dynamical system,
and its transfer is Hölder continuous. This does not furnish a polynomial
transfer over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$. The source is included to prevent
an invalid analogy, not as positive support for PC424-L.
