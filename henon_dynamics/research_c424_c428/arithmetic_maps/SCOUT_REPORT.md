## AM1: exact contract and residual proof obligation

**Map, domain, family, clock.** Fix $a\in\mathbb Z$ and
$$F_a(x,y)=\left(y,\frac{y(y+1)}2+a-x\right),\qquad (x,y)\in\mathbb Q^2.$$
Its inverse is $(x,y)\mapsto(x(x+1)/2+a-y,x)$. Both preserve $\mathbb Z^2$,
but the full frozen domain is $\mathbb Q^2$, not a selected invariant level or
coordinate box. Swapping coordinates is a reversor. One application is one
time step; cyclic rotation presents an oriented cycle once, without quotienting
reversal or changing the clock. The observable is the actual periodic-point
locus with exact least periods and all parameter overlaps.

**Full question.** Give a uniform exhaustive arithmetic classification for
every integer $a$, with explicit orbit families and all residual exceptions.
No parameter-dependent enumeration recipe, integrality lemma, short-period
factorization or finite sample alone answers this question.

**Arithmetic carrier and intended increment.** The polynomial is integer-valued
but does not have integral coefficients. The dyadic denominator is intrinsic
to its rational normal form; the scalar polynomial is not even compatible
with reduction modulo two. The source-normalized constant is
$c=a/2+7/16$, and periodic coordinates lie on the quarter-shifted half-lattice.
The intended increment is a complete classification of this missing quadratic
integer-valued normal form, not a new height theory or interpolation theorem.
The companion proof also shows that this one family, together with C412,
would classify rational periodic points of **every integer-valued quadratic
polynomial Hénon map** by rational affine conjugacy. That is a conditional
application of a future full classification, not an additional admitted claim.

**Cheap decisive checks actually completed by hand.** The
[companion proof](PROOF_PACKAGE.md) establishes:

1. Every rational periodic point of $F_a$ is integral, including the prime two.
2. $S(x,y)=(x/2+1/4,y/2+1/4)$ gives $S F_a S^{-1}=H_{a/2+7/16}$.
3. At $a=0$, $(0,1)\to(1,1)\to(1,0)\to(0,-1)\to(-1,0)\to(0,1)$
   has exact period five. Thus $F_0$ is not rationally conjugate to any C412
   member. This does not prove that all $F_a$ are nonconjugate to all such maps.
4. For $a\ge2$ there are no rational periodic points; for $a=1$ the full set
   is the two fixed points $(1,1),(2,2)$.
5. A real maximum bound and an extremal adjacent-difference restriction are
   available, but do not yet exhaust the unbounded negative-parameter family.

These are elementary hand checks; **no mathematical program was run**. They
are too short and too close in method to the deducted predecessors to fill a
paper slot by themselves. The five-cycle is a separation witness, not an
experimental prediction of a universal period bound.

**Closest external sources.** Kim--Krieger--Postolache--Szeto's Theorems A--B
construct long cycles in growing odd degree, not this fixed degree-two family.
Pezda's cycle theorem assumes coordinate polynomials with coefficients in the
ground ring, not merely integer-valued polynomials. Its integer-coefficient
period list does not include five. General Hénon uniform-boundedness questions
remain broader, and the familiar specific Ingram conjecture has the opposite
Jacobian sign. [Kim et al. v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01668v2),
[Pezda original record/PDF](https://dml.cz/handle/10338.dmlcz/120574),
[journal-hosted arithmetic questions, Section 11](https://amj.math.stonybrook.edu/html-articles/Files-2015-2024/23-70/index.html)

**Next bounded gate, not yet executed.** First derive a parameter-independent
symbolic or extremal-difference reduction for every $a\le0$, including all

symbolic family with no period cutoff. Only after such a theorem fixes the

## AM2: the conservative odd-prime horseshoe is already owned

The full parameter family is every odd prime $p$, every integer $e\ge1$, and
every $c\in\mathbb Q_p$ with $v_p(c)=-2e$. Domain is $\mathbb Q_p^2$; the
bounded two-sided locus is
$$J_c=\{P:\{H_c^n(P):n\in\mathbb Z\}\text{ is bounded}\}.$$
The proposed observable is its exact genuine periodic locus and symbolic
periods under one $H_c$ step, including emptiness. Finite residue depth is
an accuracy parameter, never a replacement for native time.

The simple involution $L(x,y)=(-y,-x)$ gives
$$L H_c L^{-1}(u,v)=(-c-v-u^2,u)=\phi_{-c,-1}(u,v).$$
For Allen--DeMark--Petsche's notation this lies in region $H_{\rm III}$,
because $|-c|_p>\max(1,|-1|_p^2)=1$. Their Theorem 1(a) gives emptiness
exactly when $-c$ is nonsquare; Theorem 28 gives the two-sided full binary
shift otherwise. Hence the candidate's periodic count $2^n$, primitive-word
counts and $(1-2z)^{-1}$ zeta in the split case are direct corollaries.
Their proof already constructs shrinking horizontal/vertical tubes, so
rebranding that construction as native finite-word lifting is not an increment.
[Versioned source, Theorems 1 and 28](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.04271v3)

This source-stage substitution is the decisive cheap test; no residue graph
or periodic-point computation was run. **Reject and stop.** Residue
characteristic two is explicitly outside the cited theorem; it is neither
silently included nor declared impossible. A dyadic threshold change without
a substantial new complete mechanism would require a separately authorized
question, not an automatic fourth screen or a second paper cut from AM1.

## AM3: integral McMillan is an unchanged old subproblem

For each $A\in\mathbb Z$, use $M_A$ on all $\mathbb Q^2$; $1+y^2$ never
vanishes there. The classified observable would be those ordinary periodic
orbits all of whose states are integral, equivalently periodic points in the
maximal two-sided integral subset
$$X_A=\{P\in\mathbb Z^2:M_A^nP\in\mathbb Z^2\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb Z\}.$$
One step is one native iterate. The complete all-$A$ structural atlas must
retain zeros, singular invariant fibres and the degenerate parameter $A=0$.

The inherited invariant is $x^2y^2+x^2+y^2-Axy$. Generic elliptic translation
and action-angle descriptions are classical.
[Zolkin--Nagaitsev--Morozov v2, Section II](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05652v2)
The local CR2 contract already asks the larger all-rational-point question,
and the seventh scout explicitly rejects this integral restriction absent
a new mechanism. Those exact local collisions decide the screen.

For clarity, the tempting short helper is only this: each integral transition
requires $1+t^2\mid At$, hence $1+t^2\mid A$ since $\gcd(t,1+t^2)=1$.
When $A\ne0$, every coordinate on an integral orbit lies in the finite set
$D_A=\{t\in\mathbb Z:1+t^2\mid A\}$. The cyclic part of the partial map on
$D_A^2$ therefore gives a terminating fixed-parameter procedure. At $A=0$
the map is $(y,-x)$, of order four. This short division/finite-graph observation
does not classify cycles uniformly in $A$ and is not promoted to a theorem
package. No graph, height sweep, elliptic multiplication or old CR2 diagnostic
was rerun. **Reject and stop; do not reopen AY/Lyness/Somos as substitutes.**
