## S1: postcritical lifting, not a periodic-root multiplicity theorem

Richard Pink, *Finiteness and liftability of postcritically finite
quadratic morphisms in arbitrary characteristic*, arXiv:1305.2841v3,
26 August 2013. The version/date were checked on the
[primary HTML text](https://arxiv.org/html/1305.2841v3), independently
listed as version 3 on the
[author's publications page](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~pink/publications.html).

Actual body access: Definition 3.1, Proposition 3.2 and its proof,
Theorem 3.3, Corollaries 3.4--3.6 with their displayed proofs, and the
opening scope statement of Remark 3.7. The graph example in that remark
and the full Sections 7--9 proof of Theorem 3.3 were not audited.

Corollary 3.6 lifts a marked quadratic map over a finite field after a
finite residue-field extension to a characteristic-zero DVR model.
The marking preserves its postcritical mapping scheme; Remark 3.7
allows ramification. These statements do not state the new-prime-cycle
multiplicity bound required here. Their use as a route to such a bound
would need an additional specialization argument. This is a source-scope
limitation, not a claim that the lifting theorem is false or irrelevant.

## S2: a different multiplier-rigidity conclusion

Alon Levy, *The McMullen Map In Positive Characteristic*,
arXiv:1304.2834v2, 9 October 2014. Version/date were checked on the
[primary arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.2834v2); the related
statements were actually accessed in the
[primary HTML body](https://arxiv.org/html/1304.2834v2).

Actual body access includes the introduction's Theorem 1.1,
Definitions 1.5 and 1.8, Theorems 1.10 and 1.12, Remarks 1.9, 1.11 and
1.13, Corollary 1.14 and the following effectiveness limitations.
The full Section 2 proof was not read.

Theorem 1.12 excludes nontrivial tame isospectral families when the
multipliers are not all in the algebraic closure of the prime field.
For the frozen quadratic map, every cycle and its multiplier is already
over that algebraic closure, so this particular hypothesis cannot supply
the proposed shortcut. Generic finite-to-one multiplier parametrization
also does not state a maximum root-multiplicity bound. This rejects that
proposed application, not the theorem itself.
