## Original complete contract, unchanged

For every odd prime $p$, put $k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p$. For every
$c\in k$ and every polynomial $h\in k[x]$, the map is

$$T_{c,h}(x,y)=(x^2+c,y+h(x))$$

on all of $\mathbb A^2(k)$, with one application of $T_{c,h}$ as the
native tick. Its base map is $f_c=x^2+c$. On every ordinary geometric
primitive base orbit $O$, the observable is
$S_h(O)=\sum_{a\in O}h(a)$, each distinct point counted once. It is
not a repeated-orbit sum, a scheme length, or a Frobenius clock.

The full question is still whether

$$
K_c:=\{h:S_h(O)=0\ \hbox{for every primitive }O\}
=B_c:=\{Q\circ f_c-Q:Q\in k[x]\}
$$

for every $(p,c)$, or, if not, whether the complete defect admits a
uniform classification. No parameter, degree, period or ordinary-point
quantifier is discarded. The elementary fibre return of order $1$ or
$p$ is background, not a new complete theorem.

## Why this continuation is different

R2--R4 tested reduced periodic algebras, growing root multiplicities,
ordinary traces and residue pairings. R5 studied PC424-D's wild
dynatomic components. None supplies the finite algebraic transfer
considered here. The R2 contract mentioned an algebraic transfer as a
possible route, but its checked proof did not prove descent from one.

Let $K=k(x)$ and let $\sigma:K\to K$ fix $k$ and send $x$ to $f_c(x)$.
The new attempt asks whether the original ordinary-cycle hypothesis
can supply an element $u$ algebraic over $K$, in a field carrying an
injective extension of $\sigma$, such that

$$\sigma(u)-u=h.$$

This is a single finite-dimensional algebraic transfer, not unrelated
interpolants on larger finite fields. The proposed descent step is:
such a $u$, if it exists, must already be a polynomial in $x$.

The descent proof will be tested in the larger proof-device family of
all polynomials $f\in k[x]$ with degree $d\ge2$ prime to $p$. That
larger lemma is not a second question or a substitute paper.
