## Main sources and exact applicability

### S1. Doyle et al., Reduction of dynatomic curves

[arXiv:1703.04172v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1703.04172v2),
March 2017; ETDS 39 (2019), 2717–2768. Actual HTML read scope: introduction,
Theorem B; Propositions 6.2–6.5 and 6.14; Proposition 7.1 complete proof;
Proposition 8.1, Definition 8.2, Corollaries 8.3–8.4 complete proofs;
Theorem 9.1 statement and concluding proof, but not every intervening
combinatorial lemma. No full-paper or figure review is claimed.

The new-to-this-pass detail is the complete read of the specialization
criterion: surviving, noncolliding branch inertia must act transitively.
The two-edge connectivity theorem supports the valuation-one criterion
for $Y_0$. The passage to $Y_1$ explicitly requires $p\nmid n$ in
Corollary 8.4 and Proposition 6.14. Reducedness and generic separability
are distinct from that missing transitivity. These are existing inputs,
not newly proved all-wild results. Neither a collision classification
for all $p^e$ nor a wild point-level lifting theorem was obtained here.

### S2. Doyle–Poonen, Gonality of dynatomic curves

[arXiv:1711.04233v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1711.04233v2),
January 2019 version; Compositio Math. 156 (2020), 733–743.
Actual HTML read: §1.1 definition, Theorem 1.1, Remark 1.3, nearby
definition of the full $Y_1(n)$ and the §2 opening; not the full proof.
Their first use of “dynatomic curve” means a curve defined by an already
irreducible factor. Its geometricity theorem does not count those
factors. The remark's uncertainty describes that source's scope, not
the global state of research in 2026.
