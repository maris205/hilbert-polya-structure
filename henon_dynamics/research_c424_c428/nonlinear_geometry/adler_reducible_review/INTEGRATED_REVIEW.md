## 1. Exhaustiveness and preservation of the original object

For a given rational point the two invariant levels are rational, and the
pair-sum change is rationally invertible. The squarefree leading binary
cubic and its three smooth infinity points exclude repeated components
and infinity singularities. The line-direction substitution gives the
stated geometric reducibility iff, not just rational factorization.

Every component is rationally defined: each meets the infinity line,
each infinity point is rational and smooth on the total cubic, and hence
there is exactly one geometric component through that point for Galois
to preserve. The remaining irreducible cubic is smooth or has one
geometric singular point. Two distinct singularities would force the
line through them to be a component by intersection multiplicity. The
unique singular point must be affine and rational.

The all-equal global-swap shortcut is valid on every level, including

## 2. Smooth-fibre proof and converse

The expanded cubic and tangent at O check directly. An arbitrary rational
origin is not necessarily a flex. With the line-section constant K, the
two original factor restrictions are exactly

`rho12(P)=K-R-P`, `rho13(P)=K-P`,

so their frozen composition is translation by R. The root exchange on
lines x+y=constant identifies the first factor and the exchange on
y=constant identifies the second. Their rational identities extend on
the smooth projective curve; this does not extend the native affine
domain. Since R and O are different infinity points, the translation is
nonzero. The order-two counterexample and its four native denominators
were checked by hand; the rejected flex shortcut is not used anywhere
in the integrated argument.

The rational bad set B is finite. A noncancelled first denominator cannot
vanish identically on an irreducible cubic. Neither can the second after
the first automorphism: that would send the cubic into a line, or force
it to satisfy an additional nonzero lower-degree equation. The union of
all integer translates of B is therefore the correct two-sided exclusion.
Reversing regular involutions verifies inverse-factor regularity, rather
than assuming it from the simplified projective translation.

The standard rational torsion input supplies the finite-order restriction.
For sharpness, the coordinate normalization is valid for every elliptic
curve with a rational point A of order at least three. The points O,A,-A
are distinct and collinear; a rational projective transformation can put
them at the three prescribed infinity points. For the resulting leading
form xy(x+y), the proposed affine shifts give quadratic coefficients
-s,-2s,-s exactly: the middle coefficient is
`b-4s-2(a+c)=-2s`. The remaining coefficients determine the Adler
parameters with no lost equation or irrational coordinate change.

The Weierstrass origin remains a flex in this construction, so it lands
in beta1=beta3. This is a legitimate realization subfamily, not an
assumption imposed on all fibres. Published positive-rank examples with
the required torsion provide infinitely many rational points; removing
a finite torsion-saturated bad set leaves a native orbit. The review
imports their existence theorem, not a new rank calculation or a claim
to enumerate all rational points on all elliptic curves.

## 3. Singular irreducible branch independently checked

The equations from F=F_u=F_v=0 are correct. A zero among r,s,k forces
one of the explicit reducibility conditions. Conversely, for nonzero
r,s,k the singular relations exclude all three possibilities: b=0
forces c=-r^2*s, a=0 forces c=-r*s^2, and a=b forces
`c+b*t=-r*k^2`. Each is nonzero. Thus the new converse really describes
all singular irreducible levels, not merely necessary conditions.

The ordinary singular point is fixed by each original factor, with
denominators r and k nonzero. This remains true at a nonsplit node.
The rational projection normalization was checked from the translated
cubic. Its homogeneous coordinates have no common zero; its three
infinity parameters are exactly 0,-1,infinity. Away from the singular
point the slope is a rational inverse, including the proof that an
affine point with u=r must be the singular point itself.

The singular preimages are exactly q=0 and avoid those infinity
parameters. The distinct-root case gives the two nodal branches; a
double root has local vanishing orders 2 and 3, giving the cusp. The
ordinary singular point is added once, not once per rational branch.

The two factor maps on the normalization follow by direct root exchange:
M12 exchanges slopes at fixed u, while fixed w gives the root product
s/r and hence M13=s/(rz). Their composition has determinant rk, trace
r+k-s and fixed equation q=0. This verifies the stated nonidentity
Möbius map and rules out an unnoticed identity special case.

The intermediate denominator calculation was multiplied out independently:
after putting over a common denominator, the z^2 terms cancel and the
remaining numerator is

`k*z*((r+k-s)*z+(r-s))`.

The extra poles are therefore exactly M^-1(0) and M^-1(-1), with the
cancelled cases already at infinity parameters. Saturating both integer
directions gives the stated native domain. The fixed singular preimages
cannot enter these pole orbits.

The projective order argument and the parabolic eta=4 case are correct.
No nonfixed periodic parameter survives an infinite-order Möbius action.
For finite order, removal of finitely many rational parameters leaves
infinitely many ordinary smooth native orbits of the exact stated order.
The six successive parameters in the r=1,s=7,k=3 example were checked
by hand; its starting original point is (5,-21/2,11), as claimed.

## 5. What the finite decision test does and does not decide

For any rational input, at most twelve ticks means at most twenty-four
original factor evaluations. Encountering a forbidden denominator before
closing the word proves that input is not a native periodic point. A
closed regular word is automatically two-sided by factor reversibility,
and its first return is the least period. Conversely, any native periodic
point lies in an exhausted fibre case and hence must return within the
proved bound. Exact rational equality is decidable without rank or height
estimates. The finite procedure is proved, not implemented or executed.

Failure to return does not test general native-domain membership. A
concrete hand check distinguishes the two properties. In the third
reducible case take beta=(1,0,0), t=1, h=0 and line parameter r=3.
The original point is (1/2,5/2,-5/2), and its line action is M(r)=1+1/r.
Its forward values stay greater than 1. The first inverse values are
1/2,-2,-1/3, after which inverse iteration stays in (-1,0). Thus every
integer iterate avoids 0,1,infinity, while M has infinite order and r=3
is not fixed. This is a two-sided native aperiodic point; the proposed
test correctly returns only “not a native periodic point”.

The integration states this distinction explicitly. Its smooth-fibre
classification is a torsion-and-hole criterion on E(Q), not a uniform
algorithm for producing Mordell--Weil generators or deciding membership
in arbitrary infinite excluded orbits. The frozen contract did not demand
those stronger algorithmic claims, and they are not used for its periodic
membership conclusion.
