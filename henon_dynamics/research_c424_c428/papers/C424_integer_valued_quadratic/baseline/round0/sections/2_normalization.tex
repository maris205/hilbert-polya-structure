\section{Affine normalization of the full class}
\label{sec:normalization}

The normalization is elementary but must act on all rational points,
not just an integral sublattice. Negative leading coefficients are
included throughout.

\begin{lemma}[Newton coordinates]
\label{lem:newton}
Every $P\in\Int(\Z)_2$ has the unique representation
\eqref{eq:newton}. Conversely, every expression there is a degree-two
integer-valued polynomial.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set
\[
 r=P(0),\qquad n=P(1)-P(0),\qquad
 m=P(2)-2P(1)+P(0).
\]
These are integers. The polynomial in \eqref{eq:newton} agrees with
$P$ at $0,1,2$, so the two degree-at-most-two polynomials agree
identically. The condition $\deg P=2$ gives $m\ne0$.
Uniqueness follows by evaluating at the same three arguments.
Conversely, $t(t-1)$ is even for every $t\in\Z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two coefficient branches]
\label{prop:normalization}
For even $m=2\kappa$, the rational linear bijection
$T(x,y)=(\kappa x,\kappa y)$ conjugates $G_P$ to
\begin{equation}
 (u,v)\longmapsto
 (v,v^2+(n-\kappa)v+\kappa r-u).
 \label{eq:even_conjugacy}
\end{equation}
For odd $m$, define $q,A$ by \eqref{eq:odd_parameter}.
Then $q,A\in\Z$, and the rational affine bijection
\begin{equation}
 T(x,y)=(mx+q,my+q)
 \label{eq:odd_conjugacy}
\end{equation}
satisfies $TG_PT^{-1}=F_A$.
Both conjugacies preserve rational periodic points and exact native periods.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
When $m=2\kappa$, one has
$P(t)=\kappa t^2+(n-\kappa)t+r$ and $\kappa\ne0$.
Substitution gives \eqref{eq:even_conjugacy}.

If $m$ is odd, $q\in\Z$ and $q(3-q)$ is even, so $A\in\Z$.
First scale both coordinates by $m$. The scalar polynomial becomes
\[
 \frac{v^2}{2}+\left(n-\frac m2\right)v+mr
 =\frac{v^2}{2}+\left(q+\frac12\right)v+mr.
\]
Translating both coordinates by $q$ makes the linear coefficient $1/2$
and the constant term
\[
 mr+\frac{q^2}{2}-\left(q+\frac12\right)q+2q
 =mr+\frac{3q-q^2}{2}=A.
\]
This is \eqref{eq:F} with parameter $A$. Each change has nonzero
rational slope and a rational inverse. Applying the conjugacy and its
inverse to $G_P^d(p)=p$ proves preservation of the least positive $d$.
\end{proof}

The even branch is therefore exactly an application of C412, not a
new classification proof. Appendix~\ref{app:integral} gives every
word and parameter restriction of the imported theorem, including
its small-index degeneracies. Its bound of eight points will exclude
even $m$ from the equality case of \eqref{eq:bound17}.
It remains to prove Theorem~\ref{thm:half_classification}.

\begin{remark}[The normal form is not an integrality claim for $G_P$]
The periodic coordinates for $F_A$ will be integers, but their inverse
images are $(u-q)/m$; in the even branch they are $u/\kappa$.
For example, $P(t)=2t^2$ gives the fixed point $(1/2,1/2)$.
The theorem concerns rational periodic points of every map in
\eqref{eq:class}, not just those periodic orbits contained in $\Z^2$.
\end{remark}
