\begin{abstract}
An exact restricted mark cokernel $C$ was previously decomposed as
$D\oplus K$, where $D\cong\mathbb Z/8\oplus(\mathbb Z/2)^2$.  We classify
the global families generated by this decomposition.  The automorphism group
of $C$ acts transitively on the $D$-type direct factors and on the ordered
decompositions of type $(D,K)$ and on split embeddings of $D$.  Exact
stabilizer and mass formulas give
$5846893330432$ direct factors and
$12857454406351852314558464$ ordered decompositions.  A subgroup formula
counts all $D$-type subgroups and shows that $2947589144576$ are not direct
factors, giving the sharp boundary of the orbit statement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Input and scope}
The exact primary types, written as exponent partitions, are
\[
 C_{(2)}:(4,3,2,2,2,1^{10}),\qquad C_{(3)}:(2,1),
\]
\[
 D:(3,1,1),\qquad K_{(2)}:(4,2,2,2,1^8),\qquad K_{(3)}:(2,1).
\]
Thus $C=D\oplus K$.  C69 constructed one such decomposition and showed that
the fixed $D$ has $2^{41}$ complements.  Here we classify all direct factors
and all decompositions of these types.

The scope firewall is
\[
\texttt{NO\_BAD\_EULER\_OR\_ROOT\_NUMBER}.
\]
We do not claim a canonical decomposition, a full table of marks or Burnside
ring, arithmetic or local data, Euler factors, root numbers, automorphy, or a
Hilbert--Polya operator.

\section{Automorphism and Hom counts}
For a finite abelian $p$-group of exponent type $\lambda$, write $\lambda'$ for
the conjugate partition and $m_s(\lambda)$ for the multiplicity of $s$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aut}
 |\Aut(G_\lambda)|=
 p^{\sum_r(\lambda'_r)^2}
 \prod_s\prod_{j=1}^{m_s(\lambda)}(1-p^{-j}).
\end{equation}
Indeed, the first power counts endomorphisms.  Reduction on the summands of
each exact exponent $s$ must be invertible; its relative density is the
corresponding product.  We also use
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hom}
 |\Hom(G_\lambda,G_\mu)|=
 p^{\sum_{i,j}\min(\lambda_i,\mu_j)}.
\end{equation}

The conjugate partitions for $C_{(2)},D,K_{(2)}$ are respectively
$(15,5,2,1)$, $(3,1,1)$, and $(12,4,1,1)$.  Exact evaluation gives
\[
 |\Aut(D)|=2^7\cdot3=384,\qquad |\Aut(C_{(3)})|=|\Aut(K_{(3)})|=108,
\]
and
\[
 |\Aut(C)|=2^{194}3^{10}5^2 7^4\,11\,17\,31^2\,73\,127,
\]
\[
 |\Aut(K)|=2^{121}3^9 5^2 7^3\,17\,31\,127.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:hom} gives
\[
 \log_2|\Hom(K,D)|=3\cdot12+1\cdot4+1\cdot1=41.
\]
Although $|\Hom(D,K)|$ happens to have the same value, the setwise stabilizer
below specifically uses maps from $K$ to $D$.

\section{Two homogeneous spaces}
Any $D$-type direct factor has some complement.  Uniqueness of the cyclic
elementary divisors forces every such complement to have type $K$.  Given two
ordered decompositions, choose isomorphisms between their $D$ components and
between their $K$ components; their direct sum is an automorphism of $C$.
This proves transitivity on ordered decompositions and, after forgetting the
complement, on $D$-type direct factors.

For the fixed ordered pair, the stabilizer is block diagonal:
\[
 \Stab(D,K)=\Aut(D)\times\Aut(K).
\]
The setwise stabilizer of $D$ consists of invertible upper block-triangular
maps, hence
\[
 \Stab(D)=\Hom(K,D)\rtimes(\Aut(D)\times\Aut(K)).
\]
If an abstract copy of $D$ is identified with its image, its pointwise
stabilizer is $\Hom(K,D)\rtimes\Aut(K)$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact orbit masses]
The number of $D$-type direct factors in $C$ is
\[
 N_D=2^{25}\cdot7\cdot11\cdot31\cdot73=5846893330432.
\]
The number of ordered decompositions $C=D'\oplus K'$ of type $(D,K)$ is
\[
 N_{\rm dec}=2^{66}\cdot7\cdot11\cdot31\cdot73
 =12857454406351852314558464.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Orbit--stabilizer gives
\[
 N_D=\frac{|\Aut(C)|}{|\Aut(D)||\Aut(K)||\Hom(K,D)|},\qquad
 N_{\rm dec}=\frac{|\Aut(C)|}{|\Aut(D)||\Aut(K)|}.
\]
Exact integer division yields the displayed values.  The independent mass
identities
\[
 N_{\rm dec}=N_D2^{41},\quad
 |\Aut(C)|=N_D|\Stab(D)|=N_{\rm dec}|\Stab(D,K)|
\]
hold exactly.  The first also recovers the C69 complement count for every
member of the direct-factor orbit.
\end{proof}

Every direct factor has $|\Aut(D)|=384$ parameterizations by an abstract $D$.
Thus split embeddings form a third transitive $\Aut(C)$-space of size
\[
 N_{\rm emb}=N_D|\Aut(D)|=2245207038885888.
\]

\section{Why ``direct factor'' is essential}
Write the $2$-primary diagonal model with a generator $g_4$ of its $\Z/16$
factor and elementary generators $f_1,f_2$ from two $\Z/2$ factors.  Set
\[
 H=\langle2g_4,f_1,f_2\rangle.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Isomorphic non-factor]
The subgroup $H$ is isomorphic to $D$, but it is not a direct factor of $C$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The three generators have orders $8,2,2$ and are independent, so $H$ has type
$(3,1,1)$.  Quotienting changes the $\Z/16$ factor to $\Z/2$ and removes two
elementary factors.  Thus $(C_{(2)}/H)$ has type
$(3,2,2,2,1^9)$, whereas a complement to a $D$-type direct factor must have
type $K_{(2)}=(4,2,2,2,1^8)$.  Hence $H$ cannot be a direct factor.
\end{proof}

The boundary can be counted globally.  For $p$-group types $\mu\subseteq
\lambda$, the number of subgroups of type $\mu$ is
\[
 p^{\sum_i\mu'_{i+1}(\lambda'_i-\mu'_i)}
 \prod_i
 \genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{\lambda'_i-\mu'_{i+1}}
 {\mu'_i-\mu'_{i+1}}_p.
\]
For $\lambda=C_{(2)}$ and $\mu=D$, this becomes
\[
 2^{16}\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{14}{2}_2
 \genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{2}{1}_2
 =8794482475008.
\]
Subtracting the direct-factor orbit leaves exactly $2947589144576$
non-direct $D$-type subgroups.  Multiplication by $|\Aut(D)|$ gives
$3377081270403072$ monomorphisms in total, so the split qualification is also
essential for the embedding orbit.

\section{Reproducibility and conclusion}
The evidence binds the C66 and corrected C69 bytes.  The producer evaluates
the conjugate-partition formula, while the checker counts endomorphism blocks
and their invertible reductions.  SymPy checks the large prime factorizations,
GAP independently gives $|\Aut(D)|=384$, clean replay passes, and thirty
hostile semantic mutations are rejected.  The result is therefore a complete
orbit-and-stabilizer classification for direct factors, with no extension to
all abstractly isomorphic subgroups.
