\section{The rational fivefold and its nonconstant symmetry}
\label{sec:source}

\subsection{Split and rational equation models}

Put
\[
 K=\Q(\rho),\qquad \rho^2+\rho+1=0,\qquad
 \tau(\rho)=\rho^2.
\]
In split coordinates \(x_0,\ldots,x_7\), consider
\[
 C=\sum_{i=0}^7x_i^3,\qquad
 Q_\rho=\sum_{i=0}^6x_ix_{i+1}+\rho x_7x_0,\qquad
 X=V(C,Q_\rho)\subset\PP^7_K.                       \tag{2.1}
\]
The fivefold is smooth.  A rational model
\(X_0=V(C_0,Q_0)\subset\PP^7_\Q\), in coordinates
\(w_0,\ldots,w_7\), is given by
\[
\begin{aligned}
C_0={}&w_0^3+2w_1^3-18w_1w_2^2+2w_3^3-18w_3w_4^2\\
     &+2w_5^3-18w_5w_6^2-w_7^3,
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{2.2}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_0={}&w_0w_1+3w_0w_2+w_1w_3+3w_1w_4
       +3w_2w_3-3w_2w_4\\
     &+w_3w_5+3w_3w_6+3w_4w_5-3w_4w_6+2w_5w_7.
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{2.3}
\]
To describe the descent, set \(\theta=1+2\rho\), so \(\theta^2=-3\).
The change of coordinates \(x=Bw\) is
\[
\begin{gathered}
x_0=w_0,\quad x_1=w_1+\theta w_2,\quad
x_7=w_1-\theta w_2,\\
x_2=w_3+\theta w_4,\quad
x_6=\rho(w_3-\theta w_4),\\
x_3=w_5+\theta w_6,\quad
x_5=w_5-\theta w_6,\quad
x_4=(1+\rho)w_7.
\end{gathered}                                      \tag{2.4}
\]
Then \(\det B=24\theta\) and
\[
 C(Bw)=C_0(w),\qquad Q_\rho(Bw)=(1+\rho)Q_0(w).
\tag{2.5}
\label{eq:split-rational}
\]

Let \(M\) be the monomial matrix
\[
 (Mx)_i=\rho^{\epsilon_i}x_{-i\!\!\pmod 8},\qquad
 \epsilon_i=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&i\ne0\text{ even},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                       \tag{2.6}
\]
It satisfies \(M\tau(M)=I\), \(C(Mx)=C(x)\), and
\(Q_\rho(Mx)=\rho Q_{\rho^2}(x)\).  Moreover
\(M\tau(B)=B\), which realizes \cref{eq:split-rational} as effective
quadratic descent.

\subsection{The split dihedral action}

A projective monomial transformation has the form
\[
 x_i\longmapsto \rho^{a_i}x_{\sigma(i)}.
\]
The relevant split group is
\[
 G=\Dih(C_{12})
 =\langle r,s\mid r^{12}=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle,
 \qquad |G|=24.                                    \tag{2.7}
\]
One choice of generators is
\[
\begin{aligned}
r:&\quad \sigma=(6,7,0,1,2,3,4,5),&
(a_i)&=(0,1,1,0,1,0,1,0),\\
s:&\quad \sigma=(7,6,5,4,3,2,1,0),&
(a_i)&=(0,1,0,1,0,1,0,1).
\end{aligned}                                      \tag{2.8}
\]
Both preserve \(C=0\) and the line spanned by \(Q_\rho\).  The statement
classifies the projective \emph{monomial} source symmetry used here; it is
not a classification of the full projective automorphism group.

Conjugation by the descent datum transports the split group through
\[
 \delta(r)=r^{-1},\qquad \delta(s)=rs.              \tag{2.9}
\]
Consequently \(G\), with this Galois transport, descends to a finite
\'etale \(\Q\)-group scheme \(\Gscheme\) of rank \(24\).  It is
nonconstant: only two of its geometric elements are rational points.

\begin{lemma}[Ambient descent]
\label{lem:ambient-descent}
The split projective representation descends to a morphism
\[
 \Gscheme\longrightarrow\operatorname{PGL}_{8,\Q}
\tag{2.10}
\label{eq:ambient-action}
\]
whose action preserves \(X_0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For every \(g\in G\), the exact cocycle is
\[
 \delta(g)=M\tau(g)M^{-1}.
\]
Using \(M\tau(B)=B\) gives
\[
 \tau(B^{-1}gB)=B^{-1}\delta(g)B.                  \tag{2.11}
\]
Thus the conjugated split representation is equivariant for the quadratic
descent datum.  Effective descent gives \cref{eq:ambient-action}.  Direct
covariance of the two equation lines under all \(24\) split elements shows
that the descended action preserves the homogeneous ideal
\((C_0,Q_0)\).
\end{proof}

Here and below, summing over the group means summing over all \(24\)
geometric elements after a finite \'etale splitting base change.  It never
means averaging over only the two \(\Q\)-rational points.
