\section{The relative Reynolds core}
\label{sec:relative}

Set \(B=\Bcore\) and write \(\Gscheme_B=\Gscheme\times_\Q B\).  The
relative action defines
\[
\alpha:\Gscheme_B\times_B\mathcal X
\longrightarrow\mathcal X\times_B\mathcal X,\qquad
(g,x)\longmapsto(x,gx).
\]
Define the norm graph
\[
 e_{\mathrm{rel}}
 =\frac1{24}\alpha_*[\Gscheme_B\times_B\mathcal X]
 \in\CH^5(\mathcal X\times_B\mathcal X)_\Q.        \tag{4.1}
\label{eq:4.1}
\]
This expression is intrinsic over \(\Q\); it does not require \(24\)
individual rational graph cycles.

\begin{proposition}[Relative Reynolds projector]
\label{prop:relative-projector}
The cycle \(e_{\mathrm{rel}}\) is self-transpose and idempotent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After the finite \'etale splitting base change,
\[
 e_{\mathrm{rel}}=\frac1{24}\sum_{g\in G}\Gamma_g.
\]
In the square of the sum, every graph \(\Gamma_k\) occurs exactly \(24\)
times because \(gh=k\) has \(24\) solutions.  This proves idempotence.
Moreover \({}^t\Gamma_g=\Gamma_{g^{-1}}\), and inversion permutes the group,
so the sum is self-transpose.  The two cycle identities descend faithfully
to \(\Q\).
\end{proof}

An algebraic relative correspondence acts horizontally on
\(R^5f_*\Q\) and preserves its Hodge filtration.  Thus
\[
 \mathbb W:=\im\!\left(
 e_{\mathrm{rel}}:R^5f_*\Q\longrightarrow R^5f_*\Q
 \right)                                           \tag{4.2}
\]
is a rational sub-variation.  Since \(e_{\mathrm{rel}}\) is
self-transpose, the restriction of the middle polarization to its image is
nondegenerate.

At the central fiber, the action of \cref{eq:4.1} on \(H^5\) is the
Reynolds average already used to isolate the middle core.  Inserting the
central middle Chow--K\"unneth projector \(\pi_5\) does not change its action
on \(H^5\).  The central rank and Hodge ledger are therefore
\[
 \rank\mathbb W=10,\qquad
 (h^{4,1},h^{3,2},h^{2,3},h^{1,4})=(1,4,4,1).
                                                               \tag{4.3}
\]
After shrinking the connected germ, all four ranks are locally constant.

\begin{theorem}[CY3-type variation]
\label{thm:vhs}
The Tate twist
\[
 \Vcore=\mathbb W(1)                               \tag{4.4}
\]
is a polarizable rational variation of Hodge structure of weight \(3\),
rank \(10\), and Hodge type
\[
 (h^{3,0},h^{2,1},h^{1,2},h^{0,3})=(1,4,4,1).
 \tag{4.5}
\label{eq:4.5}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The untwisted variation has weight \(5\).  The convention
\(\Q(1):(p,q)\mapsto(p-1,q-1)\) sends
\[
 (4,1),(3,2),(2,3),(1,4)
 \longmapsto(3,0),(2,1),(1,2),(0,3).
\]
It lowers the weight by two and gives \cref{eq:4.5}.  Polarizability follows
from the nondegenerate restriction described above.
\end{proof}

Exactly one Tate twist occurs.  A twist by \(\Q(2)\) would give the wrong
weight and Hodge types.  We also emphasize that \cref{eq:4.1} acts directly
on \(R^5f_*\Q\); no relative Chow--K\"unneth projector is constructed or
used.
