## 4. Proof of Theorem A

### Lemma 4.1: unobstructed abstract deformations

Adjunction gives \(K_{X_0}=\mathcal O_{X_0}(-3)\), and contraction with a
local volume form identifies

\[
T_{X_0}\simeq\Omega^4_{X_0}(3).
\]

Akizuki--Nakano vanishing applies because
\(4+2>5\), so \(H^2(T_{X_0})=0\). The obstruction class of every
small extension lies in this group. Therefore the abstract Kuranishi germ is
smooth.

### Lemma 4.2: smoothness of the Hilbert point

Since \(X_0\) is a regular complete intersection,

\[
N_{X_0/\mathbf P^7}
=\mathcal O_{X_0}(2)\oplus\mathcal O_{X_0}(3).
\]

For \(k=2,3\),

\[
\mathcal O_{X_0}(k)
=K_{X_0}\otimes\mathcal O_{X_0}(k+3),
\]

and the second factor is ample. Kodaira vanishing gives
\(H^1(\mathcal O_{X_0}(k))=0\), hence \(H^1(N)=0\).
The lci Hilbert obstruction group therefore vanishes. The Hilbert scheme is
nonsingular at \([X_0]\) and its tangent space is \(H^0(N)\).

### Lemma 4.3: surjectivity of embedded Kodaira--Spencer

The restricted Euler sequence and Kodaira vanishing give

\[
H^1(T_{\mathbf P^7}|_{X_0})=0.
\]

The cohomology sequence of

\[
0\to T_{X_0}\to T_{\mathbf P^7}|_{X_0}\to N\to0
\]

then makes

\[
\kappa:H^0(N)\twoheadrightarrow H^1(T_{X_0})
\]

surjective. This proof does not subtract \(63\) from \(146\), and it does
not assume \(H^0(T_{X_0})=0\).

The release certificate supplies an independent closure by solving
\(\delta_AQ=\nu Q,\ \delta_AC=\mu C+LQ\). It obtains rank \(73\)
in \(74\) unknowns and identifies the one-dimensional kernel with scalar
\(\mathfrak{gl}_8\), explicitly generated by
\((A,\nu,\mu,L)=(I_8,2,3,0)\). Then the projective infinitesimal stabilizer, and hence
\(H^0(T_{X_0})\), vanish. The numerical conclusion
\(h^1(T_{X_0})=83\) is therefore used only after the certified stabilizer
gate.

### Lemma 4.4: the nonconstant group form acts on the ambient space

For each split element \(g\in G\), HCS-C53/HCS-C54 give

\[
\delta(g)=M\tau(g)M^{-1}.
\]

Conjugating by the rationalizing matrix \(B\) yields

\[
\tau(B^{-1}gB)=B^{-1}\delta(g)B.
\]

Thus the family \(B^{-1}gB\) is equivariant for the quadratic descent datum
on the constant split group. Effective descent gives
\(\mathscr G\to\operatorname{PGL}_{8,\mathbf Q}\). The separate
equation-covariance replay shows that the descended action preserves the
homogeneous ideal of \(X_0\). This proves that \([X_0]\) is a rational fixed
point of the induced Hilbert action.

### Lemma 4.5: the fixed Hilbert locus is smooth

The group scheme \(\mathscr G\) is finite locally free of order invertible in
\(\mathbf Q\), hence linearly reductive. Apply Romagny's smooth fixed-point
theorem to the smooth Hilbert neighborhood. It follows that the fixed locus
is smooth at \([X_0]\). First-order fixed points are precisely invariant
normal sections, so

\[
T_{H^{\mathscr G},[X_0]}=H^0(N)^{\mathscr G}.
\]

Exactness of invariants applied to Lemma 4.3 gives

\[
H^0(N)^{\mathscr G}
\twoheadrightarrow H^1(T_{X_0})^{\mathscr G}.
\]

### Lemma 4.6: construction of the four-slice

The exact Cayley and semilinear descent computation proves

\[
\dim_{\mathbf Q}H^1(T_{X_0})^{\mathscr G}=4.
\]

Choose a rational linear complement \(L\) to the kernel of the invariant
Kodaira--Spencer map. Because the fixed Hilbert locus is smooth over
\(\mathbf Q\) at a rational point, an etale coordinate chart can be chosen
whose differential identifies the first four coordinate axes with \(L\).
The inverse image of those axes is a smooth locally closed rational
four-germ \(B_{\rm core}\). Its Kodaira--Spencer map is an isomorphism.

The Hilbert universal family restricted to this slice is algebraic,
projective, and \(\mathscr G\)-stable. Smoothness of its central fiber is
open, so shrink the germ to make the family smooth. The induced completed
map to the Kuranishi base is \(G\)-equivariant after base change to \(K\).
The Kuranishi base is smooth by Lemma 4.1; since the constant finite group
\(G\) is linearly reductive in characteristic zero, its fixed germ is smooth
with tangent \(H^1(T_{X_0})^G\), hence has dimension four. Rim's equivariant
versal comparison identifies this fixed completion with the base germ for
equivariant deformations. The completed map has invertible differential and
is therefore formally etale. This proves Theorem A.

The construction neither identifies the whole fixed Hilbert locus with
\(B_{\rm core}\) nor identifies tangent representatives with equations of a
linear family.

## 5. Proof of Theorem B

### Lemma 5.1: the norm graph is a projector

Define

\[
e_{\rm rel}=\frac1{24}\alpha_*[
\mathscr G_B\times_B\mathcal X].
\]

This is a relative codimension-five rational cycle. After the finite etale
splitting base change it is \(\frac1{24}\sum_{g\in G}\Gamma_g\).
Composition of graphs follows multiplication in \(G\), so every graph occurs
exactly \(24\) times in the square of the sum. Therefore
\(e_{\rm rel}^2=e_{\rm rel}\). Since
\({}^t\Gamma_g=\Gamma_{g^{-1}}\) and inversion permutes \(G\),
\({}^te_{\rm rel}=e_{\rm rel}\). Faithfully flat descent brings both
identities back to \(\mathbf Q\).

### Lemma 5.3: local maximality

### Lemma 6.4: smoothness and geometric irreducibility

The independent exact calculation gives

\[
\operatorname{Hilb}(S/J_{\nabla Y_H};t)=(1+t)^4.
\]

The quotient is finite of length \(16\), so the four homogeneous partial

## 7. Proof of Theorem D

Let a pointed base-germ isomorphism and a polarized rational VHS isomorphism
be given. The VHS isomorphism is horizontal and filtration-preserving, so it
intertwines the Gauss--Manin connections and maps the one-dimensional
\(F^3\) lines to one another. Choose local generators of those lines. Their
images differ by a nonzero scalar at the base point. Applying the third
Griffiths derivative and pairing with the generator shows

\[
Y_Y(v)=\lambda Y_H(Av)
\]

for the derivative \(A\) of the base isomorphism and one
\(\lambda\in\mathbf C^\times\). Thus failure of projective
\(\operatorname{GL}_4(\mathbf C)\)-equivalence rules out the proposed
pointed local polarized-VHS identification.

The converse is false: a cubic records only one tensor at one point. A match
does not identify higher jets, connection, monodromy, integral structure, or
an algebraic correspondence. In particular, no Hodge or Yukawa match is
promoted to a motivic statement.
