## Proposed question and decisive ownership check

The prospective object was a fixed self-adjoint fractal Laplacian obtained
from a resistance form, with polynomial or multivariate rational spectral
decimation. For the compact, discrete-spectrum models, the observable is a
spectral Dirichlet series on its convergence half-plane, not an Artin–Mazur
periodic-point zeta. The unbounded alternatives considered below instead
involve hyperfunction regularization; they are not ordinary convergent trace
Dirichlet series. Refinement depth is a renormalization index; heat time
belongs to the operator. These clocks cannot be silently identified. The
family examined comprised the ordinary interval limit, bounded gasket limits
and self-similar fractafolds, with unbounded blow-ups checked as an alternative.

The intended increment was a source-faithful infinite-operator realization
and analytic continuation beyond the repository's finite-gasket C184,
possibly retaining genuine Riemann-zeta arithmetic in a decimation identity.
The cheap admission check was whether those very constructions and analytic
statements already had a primary owner. They do. No new comparison theorem,
new operator domain, or target zero-divisor correspondence was obtained.

## Elementary target sanity check

For `Delta=-d^2/dx^2` with Dirichlet boundary conditions on the unit interval
`(0,1)`, the eigenvalues are `lambda_n = pi^2 n^2`. Thus, directly on its
convergence half-plane,

`sum_n lambda_n^(-s/2) = pi^(-s) zeta(s)`.

The index `n` enumerates positive eigenvalues; `s` is the exponent in a trace.
Zeros of the resulting function of `s` are not the eigenvalues of that
Laplacian. In particular, its frequency counting function is
`N_sqrt(Delta)(T)=floor(T/pi)`, by the displayed eigenvalues. The decimation representation
alone supplies no new target zero-spectrum correspondence. This is a
definition-level diagnostic, not a new paper-level no-go theorem.
