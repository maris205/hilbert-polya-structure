# Proof Package

## Claim

Let $K=\mathbb Q(\lambda)$, where $\lambda\in\mathcal O_K^\times$ is not a
root of unity, $N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\lambda)=1$, and the assignment
$\iota(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1}$ defines a nontrivial $\mathbb Q$-automorphism
of $K$.  Write $K^+=K^{\langle\iota\rangle}$ and suppose that a two-dimensional
return matrix $M$ has eigenvalues $\lambda,\lambda^{-1}$.

For $r\geq1$ and $n\geq1$, define the principal ideals

$$
\mathfrak A_r=(1-\lambda^r),
\qquad
\mathfrak C_n=(\Phi_n(\lambda))
$$

in $\mathcal O_K$.  Then:

1. $\mathfrak A_r$ and $\mathfrak C_n$ are independent of choosing
   $\lambda$ or $\lambda^{-1}$, and
   $\mathfrak A_r=\prod_{n\mid r}\mathfrak C_n$.
2. The actual cyclic monodromy determinant satisfies

$$
(\det(I-M^r))\mathcal O_K=\mathfrak A_r^2,
   \qquad
   \left|N_{K/\mathbb Q}\det(I-M^r)\right|
   =\left|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(1-\lambda^r)\right|^2.
   $$

3. For every $n>2$, the algebraic integer

   $$
   \beta_n=\lambda^{-\varphi(n)/2}\Phi_n(\lambda)
   $$

   belongs to $\mathcal O_{K^+}$ and

   $$
   \left|N_{K/\mathbb Q}\Phi_n(\lambda)\right|
   =\left|N_{K^+/\mathbb Q}\beta_n\right|^2.
   $$

Consequently neither the **full multiplier-field norm** of the cyclic
determinant nor the full primitive cyclotomic norm for $n>2$ can be a rational
prime.  This statement must not be confused with the minimal trace-field
norm

$$
a_r=\left|N_{K^+/\mathbb Q}\det(I-M^r)\right|
=\left|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(1-\lambda^r)\right|,
$$

or with the primitive half norm
$b_n=|N_{K^+/\mathbb Q}(\beta_n)|$.  Those integer sequences survive the
square obstruction.  They depend on the repetition index and need not obey a
one-label Euler power law.  The principal ideal packets $\mathfrak C_n$ also
survive, but identifying their prime-ideal divisors with an all-rational-prime
Euler trace is an additional open theorem.

## Status

PROVABLE AS STATED.

The full-field scalar-prime conclusion is an exact obstruction.  The
trace-field norm and ideal-packet conclusions are exact positive structures.
No all-orbit prime-distribution or Riemann-determinant theorem is claimed.

## Assumptions

- $\lambda$ is an algebraic unit and is not a root of unity.
- $K=\mathbb Q(\lambda)$ admits the nontrivial inversion involution
  $\iota(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1}$.
- $N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\lambda)=1$.
- $M$ is a two-dimensional determinant-one return matrix with eigenvalues
  $\lambda$ and $\lambda^{-1}$.
- $\Phi_n$ denotes the $n$-th cyclotomic polynomial.

The three exact H6 multipliers used by the certificate satisfy these
assumptions.  For the period-three orbit the actual unstable eigenvalue is
negative; the code therefore uses $\lambda=-L_3$, not merely its positive
modulus $L_3$.

## Notation

- $\mathcal O_F$ is the ring of integers of a number field $F$.
- $(a)$ is the principal fractional ideal generated by $a$; here every
  displayed generator is integral, so the ideals are integral.
- $N_{F/E}$ is the field norm.
- $K^+=K^{\langle\iota\rangle}$ is the fixed trace field.
- $f_\lambda$ is the monic minimal polynomial of $\lambda$.

## Proof Strategy

Use the reciprocal involution before taking any absolute value.  It turns the
two eigenvalue factors of $\det(I-M^r)$ into associates and turns every
$\Phi_n(\lambda)$ with $n>2$ into a trace-field element after multiplication
by the unit $\lambda^{-\varphi(n)/2}$.  Taking norms from the quadratic
extension $K/K^+$ then forces a square.

## Dependency Map

1. Canonicality uses only that $\lambda$ is a unit and that cyclotomic
   polynomials are reciprocal for $n>1$.
2. The cyclic determinant identity uses the two eigenvalues of $M$.
3. The primitive square-norm identity uses that $\varphi(n)$ is even for
   $n>2$, that $K/K^+$ has degree two, and that $N(\lambda)=1$.
4. The minimal trace-field norm uses transitivity of field norms and is not
   forced to be a square.
5. The resultant certificate uses the standard identity
   $\operatorname{Res}(f_\lambda,g)=N_{K/\mathbb Q}(g(\lambda))$.
6. The Route-A conclusion additionally distinguishes an integer norm from an
   ideal packet; it uses no theorem asserting primitive divisors for these
   particular quartic fields.

## Proof

### Step 1: canonical principal packets

Since $\lambda$ is a unit,

$$
1-\lambda^{-r}=-\lambda^{-r}(1-\lambda^r).
$$

The two generators differ by the unit $-\lambda^{-r}$, so they generate the
same ideal.  For $n>1$, cyclotomic reciprocity gives

$$
\Phi_n(X)=X^{\varphi(n)}\Phi_n(X^{-1}).
$$

Thus $\Phi_n(\lambda^{-1})$ differs from $\Phi_n(\lambda)$ by the unit
$\lambda^{-\varphi(n)}$.  For $n=1$,
$\Phi_1(\lambda^{-1})=-\lambda^{-1}\Phi_1(\lambda)$, so canonicality also
holds.  Finally,

$$
1-\lambda^r=-\prod_{n\mid r}\Phi_n(\lambda).
$$

The harmless unit $-1$ disappears after passing to ideals, proving
$\mathfrak A_r=\prod_{n\mid r}\mathfrak C_n$.

### Step 2: the cyclic determinant is an ideal square

The eigenvalues of $M^r$ are $\lambda^r$ and $\lambda^{-r}$.  Therefore

$$
\det(I-M^r)
=(1-\lambda^r)(1-\lambda^{-r})
=-\lambda^{-r}(1-\lambda^r)^2.
$$

Because $-\lambda^{-r}$ is a unit, extending the principal ideal generated
by the trace-field element $\det(I-M^r)$ to $\mathcal O_K$ gives

$$
(\det(I-M^r))\mathcal O_K=(1-\lambda^r)^2=\mathfrak A_r^2.
$$

Taking norms and using $|N(\lambda)|=1$ yields

$$
\left|N_{K/\mathbb Q}\det(I-M^r)\right|
=\left|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(1-\lambda^r)\right|^2.
$$

The right-hand side is a nonnegative integer square.  It is nonzero because
$\lambda$ is not a root of unity.  Hence this full-field norm cannot be a
rational prime.

The determinant is fixed by $\iota$, so it already lies in $K^+$.  Norm
transitivity gives the sharper identity

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{K^+/\mathbb Q}(\det(I-M^r))
&=N_{K^+/\mathbb Q}
  \bigl(N_{K/K^+}(1-\lambda^r)\bigr)\\
&=N_{K/\mathbb Q}(1-\lambda^r).
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the cyclic resultant (up to the fixed sign convention).  It is the
minimal rational norm of the determinant and is not forced to be a square.

### Step 3: primitive cyclotomic norms are squares for $n>2$

For $n>2$, $\varphi(n)$ is even.  Define

$$
\beta_n=\lambda^{-\varphi(n)/2}\Phi_n(\lambda).
$$

Both factors are algebraic integers because $\lambda$ is a unit, so
$\beta_n\in\mathcal O_K$.  Cyclotomic reciprocity gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\iota(\beta_n)
&=\lambda^{\varphi(n)/2}\Phi_n(\lambda^{-1})\\
&=\lambda^{\varphi(n)/2}\lambda^{-\varphi(n)}
  \Phi_n(\lambda)\\
&=\beta_n.
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $\beta_n\in K^+$.  An element of $K^+$ integral over $\mathbb Z$ lies
in $\mathcal O_{K^+}$, so $\beta_n\in\mathcal O_{K^+}$.  Rewriting
$\Phi_n(\lambda)=\lambda^{\varphi(n)/2}\beta_n$ and taking norms gives

$$
N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\Phi_n(\lambda))
=N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\lambda)^{\varphi(n)/2}
 N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta_n).
$$

The first factor is one.  Since $[K:K^+]=2$ and $\beta_n\in K^+$,

$$
N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta_n)
=N_{K^+/\mathbb Q}(\beta_n)^2.
$$

Taking absolute values proves the primitive full-field square-norm identity.
The integer on the right can equal one or a composite square, but never a
rational prime.  Its canonical square root
$b_n=|N_{K^+/\mathbb Q}(\beta_n)|$ is an integer and is not forced to be
composite.

### Step 4: resultants and the finite H6 certificate

For the monic minimal polynomial $f_\lambda$,

$$
\operatorname{Res}(f_\lambda,X^r-1)
=N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\lambda^r-1)
$$

and

$$
\operatorname{Res}(f_\lambda,\Phi_n)
=N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\Phi_n(\lambda)).
$$

The exact certificate evaluates these integers for the primitive H6 orbits
of periods $1$, $3$, and $4$ through repetition index $12$.  It verifies the
divisor product, every square claim for $n>2$, the actual negative sign of
the period-three eigenvalue, and the failure of the false $n=2$ extension.

### Step 5: what remains open

The norm map forgets how prime ideals split, so the square obstruction does
not make $\mathfrak C_n$ trivial.  Factoring $\mathfrak C_n$ may reveal new
prime ideals as $n$ grows.  The trace-field sequence $a_r$ and primitive half
norms $b_n$ retain part of that information; exact H6 examples contain both
prime and composite $b_n$.  However, $a_2\ne a_1^2$ for each of the three
certified orbits, so this surviving sequence is not obtained by repeating one
fixed scalar label.

Existing primitive-divisor theorems motivate the packet question.  In the
period-four real-quadratic case, Flatters' theorem even supplies eventual
primitive rational divisors of the Lehmer--Pierce sequence.  It still does
not identify packets across all H6 orbits with all rational primes, supply
von Mangoldt weights for the H6 orbit sum, or construct a completed Riemann
determinant.  Therefore the ideal and trace-field packet routes remain open
while the full-field norm-prime promotion is rejected.  ∎

## Corrections or Missing Assumptions

- The positive modulus $L_3$ is not substituted for the signed period-three
  eigenvalue in the actual cyclic determinant.
- The primitive square theorem starts at $n>2$; $n=1,2$ are excluded and the
  certificate supplies nonsquare controls at $n=2$.
- The real-quadratic period-four primitive-divisor theorem is recorded only
  in its source scope.  It is not imported into the quartic H6 fields.

## Open Risks

- A source-native global trace would need a theorem assembling the varying
  ideals $\mathfrak C_{\gamma,n}$ across primitive H6 orbits.
- A rational-prime statement would need a controlled pushforward from prime
  ideals, including residue degrees, multiplicities, and repetition weights.
- No critical-line continuation, functional equation, or self-adjoint
  operator follows from the local ideal identities.
