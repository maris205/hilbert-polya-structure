\begin{abstract}
We join a period-three past to a Thue--Morse future in one two-sided symbolic
configuration and close its full shift orbit.  The closure has exactly the
Thue--Morse component, the interface orbit, and the three periodic phases.
Consequently its only primitive orbit has length three and its Artin--Mazur
zeta is $(1-z^3)^{-1}$.  This periodic layer is generated by one
heteroclinic closure rather than freely attached.  The full two-sided orbit
is dense, but isolated wandering interface points make standard forward
topological transitivity fail.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} symbolic dynamics; Thue--Morse subshift;
heteroclinic orbit closure; wandering point; nonwandering set; Artin--Mazur
zeta.

\section{The frozen interface and its closure}
Let $t_n$ be binary digit-sum parity for $n\geq0$, and let
$y_j=(2,3,4)_{j\bmod3}$.  Define
\[
x_j=\begin{cases}y_j,&j<0,\\t_j,&j\geq0,\end{cases}
\qquad X=\overline{\{\sigma^n x:n\in\mathbb Z\}},
\]
where $(\sigma x)_j=x_{j+1}$.  If $n_k\to+\infty$, every fixed window of
$\sigma^{n_k}x$ eventually lies in the binary tail, so each limit lies in
$X_{\rm TM}$.  Conversely, uniform recurrence puts every finite word of
every $X_{\rm TM}$ point arbitrarily far out in $t$; nested occurrences and
a diagonal subsequence realize every such point as a positive-shift limit.
If $n_k\to-\infty$, every fixed window lies eventually in the periodic tail;
one residue class modulo three gives one phase of $y$, and all phases occur.
A bounded convergent integer-shift sequence is eventually constant, while an
unbounded sequence has a subsequence escaping in one direction.  Thus
\begin{equation}
X=X_{\rm TM}\sqcup\operatorname{Orb}(x)\sqcup\operatorname{Orb}(y).
\tag{1}
\end{equation}

The pair $40$ occurs exactly once in $\sigma^n x$, at coordinates
$-n-1,-n$, and occurs in neither limit component.  Its cylinder is the
singleton $\{\sigma^n x\}$, so every interface point is isolated and
wandering.  The full two-sided $\mathbb Z$-orbit is dense by construction.
This is not standard forward topological transitivity: the open sets
$U=\{\sigma x\}$ and $V=\{x\}$ satisfy
$\sigma^n(U)\cap V=\varnothing$ for every $n\geq0$.  The period-three orbit
is a proper closed invariant set, so $X$ is not minimal.

\section{Nonwandering and periodic sets}
The Thue--Morse language has no periodic point.  For a proposed period $p$,
choose odd $k$ above its binary length and put $d=p(2^k-1)$.  Then $p\mid d$
and $\operatorname{popcount}(d)=k$, so $t_d\ne t_0$.  If $b$ is the binary
length of $d$, every length-$2^{b+1}$ interval contains a complete
$2^b$-aligned block; its symbols at offsets $0,d$ are opposite.  Hence no
language window of that length is $p$-periodic.

The isolated wandering interface orbit has no periodic point.  Points of the
minimal Thue--Morse component are nonwandering, as are the three phases of
$y$; (1) exhausts the complement.  Therefore
\[
\Omega(\sigma)=X_{\rm TM}\sqcup\operatorname{Orb}(y),\qquad
\#\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n)=
\begin{cases}3,&3\mid n,\\0,&3\nmid n.
\end{cases}\tag{2}
\]
M\"obius inversion leaves three exact-period points at $n=3$, hence one
primitive cycle.  Moreover
\[
\log\zeta_X(z)=\sum_{q\geq1}\frac{3z^{3q}}{3q}
=-\log(1-z^3),\qquad \zeta_X(z)=\frac1{1-z^3}.\tag{3}
\]
