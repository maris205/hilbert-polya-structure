\section{Context and claim boundary}

The initial H\'enon program begins from an area-preserving model proposed as
a dynamical framework for Riemann-zero exploration \cite{Wang2026HenonModel}.
Earlier work in this repository quantized the inhomogeneous H\'enon
generating kernel by a finite Fourier transform times a cubic phase and used
ordered kernel powers to recover H\'enon critical equations.  The general
cohomological rank and weight bound for a polynomial with smooth leading
form is classical, following Deligne's exponential-sum theorem
\cite{Deligne1974Weil}.  We reuse that theorem only as a uniform moment
majorant; the new content is the H\'enon-specific reversing symmetry,
augmentation factor, and exact first-prime obstruction.

Finite symmetry groups can factor transfer-operator determinants into
representation sectors.  Such reductions are well developed for symmetric
holomorphic iterated-function schemes and Selberg zeta functions
\cite{BorthwickWeich2016}.  Our setting differs in two ways.  First, a single
H\'enon time step reverses rather than commutes with the cubic grading.
Second, the arithmetic fibers have dimensions growing with $p$.  An integral
virtual representation gives a rational local determinant, but small virtual
dimension does not imply small reduced numerator and denominator.

Twisted Artin--Schreier--Kummer sums admit broad cohomological estimates
\cite{Fu2009Twisted}.  They also provide an essential control here.  A
Kummer lift of the homogeneous scalar phase is constant after the change of
variables $u/x$, so any residual cubic arithmetic character is externally
supplied and not generated by H\'enon chronology.  The present construction
instead uses the symmetry of the full generating kernel.

Finally, all claims are evaluated against a strict dynamical-determinant
workflow.  We prove an analytic Euler germ and local reciprocal duality, but
we do not infer a completed functional equation, a Gamma factor, or a
Hilbert--P\'olya operator.  The exact failure of same-point conjugation
symmetry is treated as a stopping theorem for the raw candidate.
