\section{A first-prime conjugation obstruction}

Local unitary divisors and reciprocal identities are not enough for a
Riemann determinant.  The raw product must also be of real type.  This fails
at the first split prime.

\begin{theorem}[Exact $p=7$ obstruction]\label{thm:p7}
For the raw single-additive-character augmentation product, $A_{7,1}$ is
nonreal.  Consequently
\[
\Emu(\bar s)\not\equiv\overline{\Emu(s)}.
\]
Thus global real-type symmetry is refuted on the defining half-plane.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take $\rho=2\in\mathbb F_7$ and $\xi=e^{2\pi i/7}$.  Direct exact
enumeration of the twisted phase
\[
2x^3+2y^3+3xy
\]
gives the residue histogram
\[
(4,9,18,3,6,6,3).
\]
Thus
\[
A_{7,1}=\frac27(4+9\xi+18\xi^2+3\xi^3+6\xi^4+6\xi^5+3\xi^6).
\]
The coefficient vector of $A_{7,1}-\overline{A_{7,1}}$ is
\[
(0,6,12,-3,3,-12,-6).
\]
If its degree-at-most-six polynomial vanished at $\xi$, it would be a scalar
multiple of the minimal polynomial
$\Phi_7(X)=1+X+\cdots+X^6$.  The coefficients are not constant, so the
difference is nonzero.

Finally, $\Daug_7(z)=1-A_{7,1}z+O(z^2)$.  In the absolutely convergent global
Dirichlet expansion, the coefficient carrying the unique prime label seven
cannot be canceled by another prime.  Hence same-point conjugation symmetry
fails globally.
\end{proof}

Replacing $\rho$ by $\rho^{-1}$ does not help: the two twisted traces are
equal by \cref{thm:symmetry}.  Multiplying by the conjugate local factor would
repair real type, but it doubles the already growing local degrees and gives
neither a global functional equation nor a canonical completion.  It is a
new candidate, not a correction to the object tested here.
