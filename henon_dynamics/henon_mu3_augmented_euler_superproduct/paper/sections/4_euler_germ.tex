\section{Chronological traces and an Euler half-plane}

Let
\[
P_k=\frac13\sum_{a=0}^2\omega^{-ka}R_p^a.
\]
The representation-ring identity
\[
2P_0-P_1-P_2=R_p+R_p^2
\]
gives the logarithmic moments
\begin{align}
A_{p,n}
&=2\Tr(T_{p,0}^n)-\Tr(T_{p,1}^n)-\Tr(T_{p,2}^n)\notag\\
&=\Tr((R_p+R_p^2)U_p^{2n})
=2\Tr(R_pU_p^{2n}).\label{eq:moment}
\end{align}
The last equality follows from the unitary equivalence of the two twisted
sectors.

Expanding the ordered matrix product, for $a=1,2$ and $N\ge1$,
\[
\Tr(R_p^aU_p^N)=p^{-N/2}
\sum_{x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1}\in\Fp}
\exp\!\left(\frac{2\pi i}{p}\Phi_{N,\rho^a}(x)\right),
\]
where
\[
\Phi_{N,\rho^a}
=\sum_{j=0}^{N-2}(x_jx_{j+1}+2x_j^3)
+\rho^a x_{N-1}x_0+2x_{N-1}^3.
\]
This is a twisted closure $x_N=\rho^a x_0$.  Its critical equations are the
ordered H\'enon recurrence, equivalently
$H_0^N(z_0)=g^az_0$.  Chronology has not been replaced by an averaged
transition matrix.

The highest homogeneous part of $\Phi_{2n,\rho}$ is
$2\sum_jx_j^3$.  Its gradient $(6x_j^2)_j$ has no common projective zero for
$p>3$.  Deligne's smooth-leading-form estimate therefore yields
\[
|\Tr(R_pU_p^{2n})|\le4^n,
\qquad |A_{p,n}|\le2\cdot4^n.
\]

\begin{theorem}[H\'enon augmentation Euler germ]\label{thm:euler}
The product
\[
\Emu(s)=
\prod_{\substack{p>3\\p\equiv1\ (3)}}
\Daug_p(p^{-s})
\]
has a canonical, locally uniformly convergent trace logarithm and is
holomorphic and nonzero on $\RePart s>1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $\sigma=\RePart s>1$,
\[
\sum_p\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{|A_{p,n}|}{n}
p^{-n\sigma}
\le2\sum_p\sum_{n\ge1}
\frac{(4p^{-\sigma})^n}{n}<\infty.
\]
The bound is locally uniform on closed sub-half-planes.
\end{proof}

Since every $T_{p,k}$ is unitary, the reduced local zeros and poles lie on
$|z|=1$.  For the canonical substitution $z=p^{-s}$ this is
$\RePart s=0$.  In fact, with $G_p=D_{p,0}/D_{p,1}$,
\[
G_p(z)=-z\,\overline{G_p(1/\bar z)},
\qquad
\Daug_p(z)=z^2\overline{\Daug_p(1/\bar z)}.
\]
The optional display coordinate $z=p^{1/2-s}$ places the local divisors on
$\RePart s=1/2$ but shifts the proved convergence domain to
$\RePart s>3/2$.  This local self-inversivity is not a global functional
equation: the formal
product of the resulting factors $p^{1-2s}$ diverges, and no conductor or
Gamma completion has been constructed.
