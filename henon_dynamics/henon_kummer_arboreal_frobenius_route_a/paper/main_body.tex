\begin{abstract}
For the $n$th preimages of $2$ under $z\mapsto z^2$, we determine the
splitting field and Galois action uniformly for every $n\ge3$.  The pure
radical and cyclotomic fields meet in exactly $\Q(\sqrt2)$, and this shared
quadratic field cuts the full affine group by the character equation
$(-1)^b=(2/a)$.  The resulting image has index two in
$\AGL_1(\Z/2^n\Z)$, and adjacent-level restriction is surjective with
kernel four.
\ifnum\CRevisionRound>0
We further classify the complete fixed-root spectrum and prove that the odd
primes for which $x^{2^n}=2$ has a root have density
$7/24+1/(3\cdot4^{n-1})$, tending to $7/24$; exactly one and four roots
never occur.
\fi
\ifnum\CRevisionRound>1
Finally, we identify the inverse-limit index-two $2$-adic affine image,
lock the all-iterate Frobenius-cycle dictionary, and independently exhaust
5,592,400 finite-image elements and 95,910 prime--level cells.  The
enumeration is a regression receipt, not a substitute for the proof.
The fixed-root ledger does not supply an all-level primitive-cycle,
orientation, phase, or monodromy atlas, so strict evaluator v0.2 records
\texttt{A1\_WEAK} and an exploratory overall status.  The finite unitary
structure supplies only \texttt{A4\_FORMAL\_HINT}.
\fi
\end{abstract}

\section{Preimage fields and the shared quadratic field}
Let $m=2^n$, choose compatible elements
\[
 \alpha_n=2^{1/m},\qquad \zeta_m=e^{2\pi i/m},
\]
and put
\[
 R_n=\{\zeta_m^j\alpha_n:j\in\Z/m\Z\},\qquad
 K_n=\Q(R_n)=\Q(\alpha_n,\zeta_m).
\]
These are the level-$n$ vertices and splitting fields of the preimage tree
of $2$ for $z\mapsto z^2$; see Jones~\cite{Jones} for the general arboreal
framework.  The present monomial tower is solvable, but it is not the
generic full-affine Kummer tower: $\alpha_n^{m/2}=\sqrt2$ already lies in
the cyclotomic field.

\begin{lemma}[Cyclotomic nonsquare]\label{lem:nonsquare}
For every $n\ge3$, $\sqrt2$ lies in $C_n:=\Q(\zeta_m)$ but is not a square
in $C_n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Using the standard cyclotomic description~\cite{Washington}, because
$\zeta_8=\zeta_m^{2^{n-3}}$,
$\sqrt2=\zeta_8+\zeta_8^{-1}\in C_n$.  If $y^2=\sqrt2$ in $C_n$, then
$y=\pm2^{1/4}$, so $\Q(2^{1/4})$ would be an intermediate field of the
abelian Galois extension $C_n/\Q$.  Every such intermediate extension is
Galois.  This is impossible: $X^4-2$ is Eisenstein at $2$, while the real
field $\Q(2^{1/4})$ does not contain its conjugate $i2^{1/4}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Radical--cyclotomic intersection]\label{thm:intersection}
For all $n\ge3$,
\[
 \Q(\alpha_n)\cap\Q(\zeta_m)=\Q(\sqrt2),\qquad
 [K_n:\Q]=2^{2n-2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Over $C_n$, $\alpha_n$ satisfies $X^{m/2}-\sqrt2$.  Capelli's binomial
criterion~\cite{Lang} reduces irreducibility to excluding a square and,
when $4\mid m/2$, the exceptional shape $\sqrt2=-4c^4$.  The first is
Lemma~\ref{lem:nonsquare}.  The second would also make $\sqrt2$ a square,
namely $(2ic^2)^2$, because $i\in C_n$.  Hence
$[K_n:C_n]=m/2$, and
\[
 [K_n:\Q]=(m/2)\varphi(m)=2^{2n-2}.
\]
Eisenstein gives $[\Q(\alpha_n):\Q]=m$.  The compositum formula now gives
intersection degree two; the contained field $\Q(\sqrt2)$ is therefore the
whole intersection.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact affine image and compatible restrictions}
For odd $a$, let
\[
 \chi_2(a)=\left(\frac2a\right)=(-1)^{(a^2-1)/8},
\]
viewed modulo $8$.  Define
\[
 H_n=\{(a,b)\in(\Z/m\Z)^\times\ltimes\Z/m\Z:
              (-1)^b=\chi_2(a)\},
\]
where $(a,b)$ sends $j$ to $aj+b$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact image and restriction]\label{thm:image}
The action on $R_n$ identifies $\Gal(K_n/\Q)$ with $H_n$.  This group has
order $2^{2n-2}$ and index two in $\AGL_1(\Z/m\Z)$.  Under compatible
choices $\alpha_n=\alpha_{n+1}^2$ and
$\zeta_{2^n}=\zeta_{2^{n+1}}^2$, restriction is
\[
 H_{n+1}\longrightarrow H_n,\qquad (a,b)\longmapsto(a,b)\pmod {2^n}.
\]
It is surjective and its kernel has order four.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every automorphism has unique parameters
$\sigma(\zeta_m)=\zeta_m^a$ and
$\sigma(\alpha_n)=\zeta_m^b\alpha_n$, hence acts by $j\mapsto aj+b$.
The two expressions for the shared square root give
\[
 \sigma(\sqrt2)=(-1)^b\sqrt2
 =\bigl(\zeta_8^a+\zeta_8^{-a}\bigr)=\chi_2(a)\sqrt2.
\]
Thus the image lies in $H_n$.  There are $\varphi(m)$ choices of $a$ and
$m/2$ choices of the forced parity of $b$, so
$|H_n|=2^{2n-2}=[K_n:\Q]$.  The faithful inclusion is an equality.

Squaring the radical and root-of-unity formulas proves coordinate
reduction.  Each coordinate has two lifts.  Reduction preserves $a$ modulo
$8$ and the parity of $b$, so all four lifts satisfy the same character
equation.  This proves surjectivity and the kernel count.
\end{proof}

are proved for all levels $n\ge3$; no finite-level extrapolation occurs.}}

\section{The complete fixed-root spectrum}
For $(a,b)\in H_n$, fixed roots correspond to solutions of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear}
 (a-1)j\equiv-b\pmod {2^n}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-root law]\label{thm:fixed}
The exact numbers of elements of $H_n$ having a prescribed number of fixed
level-$n$ roots are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}
\toprule
fixed roots & number of elements\\
\midrule
$2$ & $2^{2n-4}$\\
$2^k$, $3\le k<n$ & $2^{2n-2k-1}$\\
$2^n$ & $1$\\
$0$ & $2^{2n-2}-2^{2n-4}-\displaystyle\sum_{k=3}^{n-1}2^{2n-2k-1}-1$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
No element has exactly one or four fixed roots.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:linear} has
$g=\gcd(a-1,2^n)$ solutions exactly when $g\mid b$.  Since $a$ is odd,
$g\ge2$, excluding one root.  If $a\equiv7\pmod8$, then
$\chi_2(a)=1$, $b$ is even, and $v_2(a-1)=1$.  The
$2^{n-3}\cdot2^{n-1}=2^{2n-4}$ such pairs have two fixed roots.

For $3\le k<n$, there are $2^{n-k-1}$ residues with
$v_2(a-1)=k$ and $2^{n-k}$ admissible $b$ divisible by $2^k$, giving the
second row.  When $a=1$, only $b=0$ fixes all roots.  Finally,
$v_2(a-1)=2$ means $a\equiv5\pmod8$, so $\chi_2(a)=-1$ forces $b$ odd;
then $4\nmid b$, excluding four fixed roots.  The complement has none.
\end{proof}

\section{Chebotarev root densities}
All odd primes are unramified in $K_n$, since the polynomial discriminant
has no odd prime divisor.  Its reduction is separable, and
$x^{2^n}=2$ has a root over $\F_p$ exactly when arithmetic Frobenius fixes
a member of $R_n$.

\begin{theorem}[Root-prime density]\label{thm:density}
For every $n\ge3$, the odd primes at which $x^{2^n}=2$ has a root have
natural density
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
 \delta_n=\frac7{24}+\frac1{3\cdot4^{n-1}}.
\end{equation}
The sequence decreases to $7/24$.  Each nonzero root multiplicity in
Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed} separately has the density obtained by dividing
its group count by $2^{2n-2}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The fixed-point property and fixed-point count are conjugacy invariant.
Chebotarev~\cite{Neukirch} therefore identifies the requested densities
with the corresponding proportions in $H_n$.  The positive fraction is
\[
 \frac14+\sum_{k=3}^{n-1}2^{1-2k}+2^{-2n+2}
 =\frac7{24}+\frac1{3\cdot4^{n-1}}.
\]
The limit and monotonicity are immediate.
\end{proof}

$1$ and $4$, every multiplicity density, and the limit $7/24$ are added as

\section{Inverse limit and all-iterate Frobenius cycles}
The character condition is compatible because it depends only on
$a\bmod8$ and $b\bmod2$.

\begin{proposition}[Infinite arboreal image]\label{prop:limit}
The inverse-limit Galois image is
\[
 H_\infty=\{(a,b)\in\Z_2^\times\ltimes\Z_2:
          (-1)^{b\bmod2}=\chi_2(a)\}.
\]
It is the kernel of a continuous surjective character of the $2$-adic
affine group and hence is closed of index two.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:image} supplies a surjective inverse system.  Under
$(a,b)(c,d)=(ac,ad+b)$, oddness of $a$ gives
\[
 \chi_2(ac)(-1)^{ad+b}
 =\chi_2(a)(-1)^b\chi_2(c)(-1)^d.
\]
Thus the defining expression is a continuous character.  It takes both
signs and its finite quotients are exactly the $H_n$.
\end{proof}

For an odd prime $p$, the degrees of the irreducible factors of
$x^{2^n}-2\bmod p$ are the cycle lengths of arithmetic Frobenius.  If
$N_{p,n}(r)$ is the number of fixed roots of its $r$th power and
$E_{p,n}(d)$ counts points of exact period $d$, then the inherited finite-
permutation identities are
\[
 N_{p,n}(r)=\sum_{e\mid r}E_{p,n}(e),\qquad
 E_{p,n}(d)=\sum_{e\mid d}\mu(e)N_{p,n}(d/e).
\]
HCS-C12A already owns this universal mechanism.  C374 owns its specific
compatible Kummer image and the closed fixed-root/density atlas above.

\section{Finite Koopman realization and Route-A boundary}
Each $H_n$ acts canonically on $\ell^2(R_n)$ by permuting the orthonormal
point basis, hence by real permutation unitaries with the same level and
iterate clock.  This is a canonical finite Koopman realization.  The package
does not specify or prove a single family-wide antiunitary that sends every
action to its inverse, a nontrivial orbit phase/weight preservation law, or a
global self-adjoint Hamiltonian owner.  Strict evaluator v0.2 therefore
records only \texttt{A4\_FORMAL\_HINT}.  The intrinsic number-field tower
and Frobenius classes earn the structural-arithmetic verdict at \texttt{A0}.

The A0 audit uses three explicit controls.  For neighboring basepoint $3$,
valuation at primes above $3$ and Capelli irreducibility give trivial
radical--cyclotomic intersection and the full affine image, so the
basepoint-two shared-character cut disappears.  The simpler ambient
$\AGL_1(\Z/2^n\Z)$ control restores exactly $2^{2n-5}$ elements with four
fixed roots.  The exact odd-composite ledger below $100$ has $25$ labels.
Its five prime powers $9,25,27,49,81$ are retained as repetition controls
attached to the conjugacy classes of $\mathrm{Frob}_p^r$; they are not new
rational-prime Frobenius classes.  Only the other $20$ mixed composites,
each with at least two distinct prime factors, are rejected as having no
single-prime Frobenius owner.
Empirical density alone receives no A0 credit.

These A0 controls do not clear A1.  The proved affine action and fixed-root
law do not include an all-level primitive-cycle/repetition enumeration with
completeness control, orbit orientation and phase, multiplicity weights, or
monodromy and stability data.  Nor do they derive an intrinsic
$p\leftrightarrow\gamma_p$, prime-power, or $\log p$ period law.  The
mandatory shuffled-period, random-weight, random-phase, same-density-length,
neighboring-parameter, and simpler-parent tests therefore remain uncompleted
at the A1 orbit layer.  Strict evaluator v0.2 records \texttt{A1\_WEAK}.

There is no cross-prime Fredholm direct sum, no determinant-class
regularization, and no target Euler product.  We prove no target
continuation, functional equation, divisor, counting law, or zero match.
Thus both \texttt{A2} and \texttt{A3} fail.  The verdict is
\texttt{ROUTE\_A\_EXPLORATORY} under the literal scope \Scope.
The finite permutation unitaries are not a Hilbert--P\'olya operator, and
Route B is not invoked.

\section{Conclusion}
The basepoint $2$ produces one persistent quadratic entanglement and no
further loss: it cuts the affine image by exactly one character at every
level.  The resulting compatible group law turns a radical-field question
into a complete fixed-root and prime-density theorem.

The density converges to $7/24$, while the character obstruction explains
the otherwise surprising absence of exactly four roots.
\fi

not supply the missing target analytic bridge; the Route-A boundary is
therefore part of the theorem package rather than an afterthought.
