\section{Discussion and Route-A boundary}

The action coordinate identifies two period-five points, but chronological
linear stability does not disappear at the identification.  Its symmetric
product descends and remains nonsquare.  This is the paper's structural
conclusion: a singular action embedding can carry an arithmetic stability
cover that is invisible to the ordinary orbit permutation cover.

The result also narrows the next mathematical question.  Proposition
\ref{prop:S9} provides nine conjugate collision parameters, while
Theorem~\ref{thm:kummer} proves only one nontrivial square class over their
generic field.  Determining the rank of the conjugate classes would decide
whether the quadratic extension grows into a larger Kummer module or
collapses through hidden relations.  A different continuation would seek
compatible classes at varying periods and one trace law that preserves the
chronological action and Hill data.

Neither continuation has been proved here.  In particular, a finite
algebraic cover does not define a primitive-orbit Euler product, and its
Galois group does not imply analytic continuation, a functional equation,
or a critical-line spectrum.  Under the project's strict evaluation, the
current ceiling is
\[
(A1\_\mathrm{WEAK},A2\_\mathrm{FAIL},A3\_\mathrm{FAIL},
A4\_\mathrm{FORMAL\_HINT}),
\]
with overall decision \texttt{ROUTE\_A\_REJECTED}.  Route B is not
authorized because no self-adjoint operator has been constructed.

The fixed-period limitation is therefore both a boundary and a guide.  The
next large advance must prove an all-conjugate or all-period theorem; another
isolated prime or longer low-period ledger would not close the missing
logical bridge.
