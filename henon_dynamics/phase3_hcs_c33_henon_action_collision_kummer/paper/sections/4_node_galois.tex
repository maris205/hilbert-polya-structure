\section{The degree-nine equal-action node}

Exact elimination gives
\begin{equation}
\Disc_c\WA
=2^{12}3^{30}A^{60}P_2^5P_5^3\PN^2,                       \label{eq:discW}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
\PN(A)={}&110592A^9-294912A^8+159744A^7+225792A^6\notag\\
&-162816A^5-51520A^4+50672A^3+736A^2-6032A+1037.           \label{eq:P9}
\end{align}
The polynomial \(\PN\) is squarefree and coprime to \(AP_2P_5\).  Hence it
does not arise from the normalization ramification in \eqref{eq:discG}.

Let \(\K=\Q[A]/(\PN)\).  In \(\K[c]\), the gcd of \(\WA\) and
\(\partial_c\WA\) is \(c-c_0\).  Substituting \(c_0\) into the normalization
equations produces
\begin{equation}
g_2(q)=\gcd\bigl(\GA(q),3A^2c_0-R(A,q)\bigr)=q^2+bq+d.      \label{eq:g2}
\end{equation}
The discriminant \(b^2-4d\) is nonzero, and \(g_2\) is coprime to
\(\GA/g_2\).  Thus the repeated action value has exactly two distinct
normalization points.

\begin{theorem}[Ordinary node]\label{thm:node}
At the generic point of \(\PN=0\), the action image \(\WA=0\) has a
transverse ordinary node whose two branches are the two points cut out by
\eqref{eq:g2}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Exact evaluation in \(\K\) gives
\[
\WA=(\WA)_A=(\WA)_c=0,
\quad
(\WA)_{cc}\ne0,
\]
and
\begin{equation}
(\WA)_{Ac}^2-(\WA)_{AA}(\WA)_{cc}\ne0.                    \label{eq:tangentdisc}
\end{equation}
Therefore the first nonzero homogeneous term is a nondegenerate quadratic.
To identify its two tangents with the two normalization branches, differentiate
\(G(A,q)=0\) and \(c=R/(3A^2)\).  The branch slope is
\begin{equation}
s(q)=\frac{(A\partial_AR-2R)\partial_qG
-A(\partial_qR)\partial_AG}
{3A^3\partial_qG}.                                          \label{eq:slope}
\end{equation}
The denominator is invertible modulo \(g_2\).  Reduction gives
\(s(q)\equiv uq+v\), with
\[
(s(q_1)-s(q_2))^2=u^2(b^2-4d)\ne0.
\]
The two normalization branches therefore have distinct image tangents.
\end{proof}

The collision parameters themselves have a large arithmetic permutation
group.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:S9}
\(\Gal(\PN/\Q)\cong S_9\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The exact unramified factor degrees modulo \(7,17,23\) are respectively
\((9)\), \((5,2,1,1)\), and \((8,1)\).  The first makes \(\PN\)
irreducible.  The 8-cycle makes a point stabilizer transitive on the other
eight letters, so the group is 2-transitive and primitive.  Squaring an
element of type \((5,2,1,1)\) gives a pure 5-cycle.  Jordan's theorem gives
\(A_9\), while the 8-cycle is odd.
\end{proof}
