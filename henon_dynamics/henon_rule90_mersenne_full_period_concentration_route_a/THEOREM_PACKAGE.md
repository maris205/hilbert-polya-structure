# C155 proof package

## Claim and status

Let `L=2^r-1`, `r>=2`, and let Rule 90 act by multiplication with
`a=x+x^{-1}` on `R_L=F_2[x,x^{-1}]/(x^L-1)`.  Its periodic set is
`V=im(a)`, with `|V|=2^(L-1)`.  If `v` is uniform on `V`, then

```text
Pr(period(v)<L) <= 2L*2^(-L/3),
Pr(period(v)=L) -> 1.
```

If `C_L` is the total number of primitive cycles in `V`, then

```text
|L*C_L/2^(L-1)-1| <= 2L*2^(-L/3),
(2^(L-1)/C_L)/L -> 1.
```

The last numerator/denominator is the mean primitive-cycle length, averaging
each cycle once.  Exact-period and primitive-cycle counts remain given by the
polynomial-gcd/Möbius formulas.  **PROVABLE AS STATED.**

## Lemma 1: periodic image and exact ledger

Frobenius in characteristic two gives

```text
a^(2^r)=x^(2^r)+x^(-2^r)=a,       a^(L+1)=a.
```

After multiplication by the invertible monomial `x`, the multiplier is
`x^2+1=(x+1)^2`.  The polynomial `x^L+1` is squarefree for odd `L` and has
`x+1` as a simple factor.  Hence `dim ker(a)=1` and
`dim V=L-1`.  For `v=au`, `a^L v=a^(L+1)u=v`; conversely every periodic
state belongs to the image.  Thus `g=a|_V` is a permutation with `g^L=I`,
and every realized period divides `L`.

For every positive `n`, clearing the Laurent monomial gives

```text
Fix_L(n)=2^deg gcd(x^L+1,(x^2+1)^n+x^n),
P_L(n)=sum_(d|n) mu(n/d) Fix_L(d),
C_L(n)=P_L(n)/n.
```

The converse “every divisor occurs” is not asserted.

## Lemma 2: proper-time fixed spaces

Fix `1<=j<L` and put `d=gcd(j,L)`.  In the polynomial ring over `F_2`,

```text
gcd(U^j-1,U^L-1)=U^d-1.
```

Bézout identities therefore express `U^d-1` as a combination of the first
two polynomials and express each of them as a multiple of `U^d-1`.  After
substitution `U=g` and use of `g^L-I=0`, these inclusions give

```text
ker(g^j-I)=ker(g^d-I).
```

A state fixed by a positive power of `a` is already in `V`, so its dimension
is the degree of
`gcd(x^L+1,(x^2+1)^d+x^d)`, at most `2d`.  Since `d` is a proper divisor of
the odd number `L`, its complementary factor is at least three; hence

```text
dim ker(g^j-I) <= 2d <= 2L/3.                 (1)
```

## Theorem 3: state concentration

Every state whose period is strictly less than `L` is fixed by `g^j` for at
least one `1<=j<L`.  The union bound and (1) give

```text
# {v in V: period(v)<L}
 <= sum_(j=1)^(L-1) #Fix(g^j)
 <= (L-1) 2^(2L/3).
```

Division by `|V|=2^(L-1)` yields
`Pr(period<L)<=2(L-1)2^(-L/3)<=2L2^(-L/3)`, which tends to zero.  Since all
periods divide `L`, its complement consists exactly of the states of period
`L`, proving concentration.

## Theorem 4: Burnside and the mean cycle scale

Burnside's lemma for the cyclic action generated by `g` gives

```text
C_L=(1/L) sum_(j=0)^(L-1) #Fix_V(g^j).
```

The identity term is `2^(L-1)`, and the remaining sum satisfies the same
bound as in Theorem 3.  Therefore

```text
0 <= L*C_L/2^(L-1)-1 <= 2L*2^(-L/3) -> 0.
```

The average of the primitive-cycle lengths, with each cycle weighted once,
is `(sum cycle lengths)/C_L=2^(L-1)/C_L`.  Dividing by `L` gives the reciprocal
of `L*C_L/2^(L-1)`, hence tends to one.
