\section{Reversors and the mixed-axis closure}

Put $f(q)=1-6q^2$ and
\[
H(q,p)=(f(q)-p,q),\qquad R(q,p)=(p,q),\qquad J=RH.
\]
Both $R$ and $J$ are involutions, $RHR=H^{-1}$, and $H=RJ$.  The fixed line
of $J$ is parameterized by
\[
\gamma(X)=\left(X,\frac{f(X)}2\right).
\]
If $H^j\gamma(X)=(q_j,q_{j-1})$, then
\[
q_{j+1}=1-6q_j^2-q_{j-1},\qquad q_{-1}=q_1=f(X)/2.
\]

Let $n=2m+1$ and $\ell(q,p)=q-p$.  Reaching $\Fix(R)$ after $m+1$
iterates is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closure}
F_n(X):=\ell(H^{m+1}\gamma(X))=q_{m+1}(X)-q_m(X)=0.
\end{equation}
The reversor identities then close a period dividing $n$.  Differentiating
\eqref{eq:closure} gives the exact tangent formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent}
F_n'(X)=(1,-1)DH^{m+1}(\gamma(X))\gamma'(X).
\end{equation}
Thus a multiple root is precisely a tangency of
$H^{m+1}(\Fix J)$ with $\Fix R$.
