\section{One physical root per reversible necklace}

Label the four state rectangles by the sign pairs
$--,-+,+-,++$.  Swapping $(q,p)$ fixes the first and fourth labels and
interchanges the middle two, exactly the symbol involution
$\rho=(0)(1\ 2)(3)$.  The signed-square-root construction gives a unique
orbit for every admissible itinerary.  Applying a geometric reversor thus
produces the unique orbit with the reversed $\rho$-itinerary, proving that
the coding is symmetry equivariant.  In particular, a fixed symbolic
reflection gives a geometric point on its calibrated fixed axis.

At odd period every reflection axis is conjugate under rotation.  A primitive
necklace cannot be fixed by two distinct reflections, because their product
would be a nontrivial stabilizing rotation.  Therefore its $n$ representatives
occur one-to-one on the $n$ axes, and exactly one representative lies on the
axis calibrated to $\Fix(J)$.

\begin{proposition}[Physical incidence]\label{prop:incidence}
For odd $n$, the number of distinct primitive roots of $F_n$ in the survivor
is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Pn}
P_n=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)F_{(d+3)/2}.
\end{equation}
Every such root has local coefficient $+1$ in the formal primitive divisor.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The preceding axis argument identifies the roots bijectively with the exact
primitive reversible-necklace census, giving \eqref{eq:Pn}.  Distinct
necklaces give distinct periodic points, while $X$ parameterizes $\Fix(J)$
injectively.  A primitive period-$n$ point is absent from every proper-divisor
closure.  Corollary~\ref{cor:hyperbolic} gives multiplicity one in $F_n$, so
M\"obius subtraction leaves coefficient $+1$.
\end{proof}
