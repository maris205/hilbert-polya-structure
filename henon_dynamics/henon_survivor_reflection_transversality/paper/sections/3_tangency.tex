\section{A product-of-involutions tangency lemma}

For the odd period $n=2m+1$, define the pulled-back reversor
\[
K_m=H^{-(m+1)}RH^{m+1}.
\]
It is an involution.  At a root of $F_n$, $z=\gamma(X)$ lies on both
$\Fix(J)$ and $\Fix(K_m)$.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor}
JK_m=RH H^{-(m+1)}RH^{m+1}=H^mH^{m+1}=H^n.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Tangency implies neutrality]\label{lem:tangent}
If $F_n(X)=F_n'(X)=0$, then $1$ is an eigenvalue of $DH^n(\gamma(X))$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The nonzero vector $v=\gamma'(X)$ spans the tangent of $\Fix(J)$, so
$DJ(z)v=v$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:tangent} says that
$DH^{m+1}(z)v$ is tangent to $\Fix(R)$.  Pulling back gives
$DK_m(z)v=v$.  Differentiating \eqref{eq:factor} now yields
$DH^n(z)v=DJ(z)DK_m(z)v=v$.
\end{proof}

Only this implication is needed.  For arbitrary linear involutions, a
parabolic product can have eigenvalue one without coincidence of their
positive eigendirections; we therefore do not assert the converse.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:hyperbolic}
Every odd mixed-axis closure root in the certified uniformly hyperbolic H6
survivor is simple.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every periodic monodromy on a uniformly hyperbolic set has one multiplier of
modulus greater than one and one of modulus less than one.  Lemma
\ref{lem:tangent} would instead supply multiplier one.
\end{proof}
