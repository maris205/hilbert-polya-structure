# Source and claim audit

## Primary ownership

P. Erdős and A. Rényi, “On the evolution of random graphs,” *Publications of
the Mathematical Institute of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences* 5 (1960),
17–61.  Stable primary scan:
`https://static.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1960-10.pdf`.

Ownership role: the random graph process and connectivity-threshold theorem.
No priority is claimed here.  The package supplies a self-contained
specialization, an exact finite hitting atlas, and a strict evidence boundary.

## Claim-to-evidence boundary

The recurrence and probability identities hold for all finite `n,m`; the
proof, not the table, owns those quantifiers.  Machine rows stop at `n=12`,
and exhaustive graph masks stop at `n=6`.  Isolated-vertex decimal diagnostics
do not prove convergence.  The asymptotic theorem is owned by the displayed
factorial-moment calculation and the two-range non-isolated-component bound.

## Repository collision boundary

C301 is a parallel fair-bit partition-refinement birthday process, not graph
edge addition.  C291 is random greedy dimer adsorption on finite paths and
cycles, not connectivity of the complete graph.  C276 samples an entire
uniform random mapping and studies functional-graph components, rather than
evolving simple graphs one edge at a time.  These registry-checked neighbors
do not own C307's without-replacement edge clock, exact connected-graph
recurrence, absorbing connectivity law, or Gumbel window.

## Negative-claim audit

The connectivity/no-isolated equivalence is only asymptotic at deterministic
window times.  The paper does not assert equality of stopping times, moment
convergence, or an independent-edge finite model.  Connected graph counts are
not a target arithmetic counting law.  No arithmetic local data, Euler
factor, root number, automorphy, target divisor law, target functional
equation, target zero match, or Hilbert--Pólya operator is claimed.  Route B is
not invoked.
