\section{Circle ownership, operator limits and conclusion}\label{sec:scope}

For $a<1$, infinity is a repelling fixed point of the projective-real map
with local multiplier $a^{-1}$. Its primitive stability factor is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infinity-factor}
 D_{\infty,a}(u)=\prod_{k\ge1}(1-a^ku).
\end{equation}
Thus the full-circle product is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle}
 D_{\mathrm{circle},a}(u)=D_a(u)D_{\infty,a}(u)
 =(1-u)\prod_{j\ge1}(1-q^ju)^2.
\end{equation}
This is the classical Blaschke determinant mechanism
\citep{bandtlow2017spectral}. Dividing out the specified orbit factor explains
the poles in the finite-real product. However, deletion of a measure-zero
orbit leaves ordinary $L^p$ equivalence classes unchanged.
Equation~\eqref{eq:circle} is a periodic-product identity, not a theorem that
restricting the same operator changes its trace by that factor. A natural
physical transfer domain and a trace formula for it remain unspecified.

The Cayley scale $c=\sqrt{b/(1-a)}$ diverges at $a=1$. Consequently the
critical real map is not obtained by substituting $q=1$ in a nonsingular
fixed coordinate chart. The residue index and the all-$n$ bounds provide
the direct passage needed at the transition. They also show why matching
finitely many coefficients would not establish either locally uniform limit.

The source contract is now closed: every iterate weight is explicit, every
subcritical divisor coincidence is classified, and the critical germ has
the stated two-sided compensated limit. Its classical unweighted phase
structure was used to verify the physical periodic set, not counted as a
separate contribution. The exceptional diagonal realizations are abstract

Euler factors, root numbers, automorphy, functional equation, or
Hilbert--P\'olya construction follows from these products.
