\section{The physical domain and its periodic points}\label{sec:dynamics}

We include the phase geometry to justify the periodic set and multipliers used
in~\eqref{eq:definition}, not as an independent contribution.

\subsection{Inverse branches and invariant measures}

The deleted set in~\eqref{eq:domain} is countable, since a degree-two rational
map has at most $2^j$ inverse images of a specified point at depth $j$. Every
finite periodic point avoids zero: zero maps to infinity, which is fixed.
Thus every finite real periodic point belongs to $X_{a,b}$. Direct calculation
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse}
 T'(x)=a+\frac b{x^2}>0,\qquad
 h_\pm(y)=\frac{y\pm\sqrt{y^2+4ab}}{2a},\qquad
 h_\pm'(y)=\frac1{2a}\left(1\pm\frac y{\sqrt{y^2+4ab}}\right)>0.
\end{equation}
Each half-line maps increasingly onto the real line. Since
$h_+'+h_-'=1/a$, change of variables proves, for every measurable $A$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobian}
 m(T^{-1}A)=a^{-1}m(A),
\end{equation}
where infinite values are allowed and the countable deletions do not change
Lebesgue measure. At $a=1$ this proves invariance of infinite Lebesgue measure;
it is not an ergodicity argument.

Suppose $0<a<1$, and set $c=\sqrt{b/(1-a)}$. The Cayley map
$C(z)=(z-ic)/(z+ic)$ sends the upper half-plane to the disk and infinity to $1$.
Substitution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blaschke}
 C T C^{-1}(w)=B_q(w)=w\frac{w+q}{1+qw},\qquad -1<q<1.
\end{equation}
For $|w|<1$,
$|1+qw|^2-|w+q|^2=(1-q^2)(1-|w|^2)>0$.
Therefore $|B_q(w)|<|w|$ for nonzero disk points, so none is periodic.
Reflection in the circle gives the analogous assertion outside the closed
disk. The only off-circle periodic points are $0$ and infinity, both fixed
with local multiplier $q$; in the original coordinate these are $ic$ and
$-ic$.

For $B_q(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\psi(\theta)}$, logarithmic differentiation yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:angular}
 \psi'(\theta)=1+\frac{1-q^2}{|e^{i\theta}+q|^2}
 \ge1+\frac{1-|q|}{1+|q|}>1.
\end{equation}
All circle cycles are simple and repelling. At a finite real periodic point,
the coordinate derivatives in the full-period conjugacy cancel, so the real
multiplier equals the positive angular multiplier.

For completeness, $B_q$ preserves normalized angular measure: the average of
$B_q(w)^k$ on the circle is zero for every positive integer $k$, by the
holomorphic mean-value formula and $B_q(0)=0$. The negative Fourier modes
follow by conjugation; trigonometric approximation gives invariance for all
continuous functions. Pullback under $C$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cauchy}
 d\mu_{a,b}(x)=\frac{c}{\pi(x^2+c^2)}\,dx.
\end{equation}
The deleted set is null for this probability. No uniqueness or mixing claim
is needed.

\subsection{Supercritical survivor and exact escape}

Let $a>1$, $r=\sqrt{b/(a-1)}$ and $I=[-r,r]$. Its endpoints are fixed with
multiplier $q=2a-1>1$, and the inverse images are disjoint:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intervals}
 h_-(I)=[-r,-(a-1)r/a],\qquad h_+(I)=[(a-1)r/a,r].
\end{equation}
Each inverse has derivative less than $1/a$ there. The cylinders
$h_{\epsilon_1}\cdots h_{\epsilon_n}(I)$ have diameter at most $2ra^{-n}$;
distinct words give disjoint cylinders by injectivity and the first-level gap.
Nested cylinders therefore code a unique point for every infinite sign
sequence. This is a homeomorphism from the one-sided binary shift space onto
\begin{equation}\label{eq:survivor}
 K=\bigcap_{n\ge0}T^{-n}I,
\end{equation}
and $T$ deletes the first sign. No point of $K$ meets zero, so $K\subset X_{a,b}$.

For $x>r$, $T(x)-x=(a-1)x-b/x>0$. A forward orbit starting there is increasing
and cannot have a finite limit: such a limit would be a fixed point larger
than $r$. Hence it tends to positive infinity. Oddness gives negative escape
for $x<-r$. Every point of $X_{a,b}\setminus K$ eventually leaves $I$ and
escapes, so all finite real cycles lie in $K$. Iterating~\eqref{eq:jacobian}
gives the exact initial-Lebesgue survival law
\begin{equation}\label{eq:escape}
 m(T^{-n}I)=2ra^{-n},\qquad m(K)=0,
 \qquad-\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\frac{m(T^{-n}I)}{m(I)}=\log a.
\end{equation}
This is not an assertion of a Lebesgue invariant probability on the repeller.

\subsection{All iterates and the parabolic multiplicity}

For nonreal $z=x+iy$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:imaginary}
 \Im T(z)=y\left(a+\frac b{|z|^2}\right).
\end{equation}
If $a\ge1$, the absolute imaginary part strictly increases at every finite
step. A nonreal point cannot reach a real pole and cannot be periodic.
Every finite real periodic multiplier is greater than one, since each
derivative is greater than one. Together with~\eqref{eq:angular} this proves
positive simple repulsion in all three regimes.

The rational degree of $T^n$ is $2^n$. Its homogeneous fixed-point equation
is a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree $2^n+1$, so it has $2^n+1$
projective roots counted with local multiplicity. To account for infinity,
use $w=1/z$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local}
 g_a(w)=\frac1{T(1/w)}=\frac{w}{a-bw^2}.
\end{equation}
If $a\ne1$, the multiplier of $g_a^n$ at zero is $a^{-n}\ne1$, giving
multiplicity one. If $a=1$, write
$g_1^n(w)=w+A_nw^3+B_nw^5+O(w^7)$. Composition with
$g_1(w)=w+bw^3+b^2w^5+O(w^7)$ yields
$A_{n+1}=A_n+b$ and $B_{n+1}=B_n+3bA_n+b^2$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jet}
 g_1^n(w)=w+nbw^3+\frac{n(3n-1)}2 b^2w^5+O(w^7).
\end{equation}
The nonzero cubic coefficient makes infinity a fixed point of multiplicity
three for every $n$, not a simple fixed point of multiplier one.

In the subcritical case exactly two off-real fixed points, each simple for
every iterate, were found in~\eqref{eq:blaschke}. In the other regimes there
are none by~\eqref{eq:imaginary}. Subtracting these and the correct infinity
multiplicity proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counts}
 N_n:=\#\Fix_{X_{a,b}}T^n=
 \begin{cases}2^n-2,&0<a\le1,\\2^n,&a>1.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The primitive orbit counts follow by M\"obius inversion:
$P_n=n^{-1}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)N_d$. Consequently the unweighted
Artin--Mazur zeta is $(1-u)^2/(1-2u)$ for $a\le1$ and $1/(1-2u)$ for $a>1$,
initially on $|u|<1/2$. These elementary counts do not determine the weighted
product.
