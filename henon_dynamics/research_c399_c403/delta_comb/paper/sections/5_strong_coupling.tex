\section{The singular strong-coupling limit}
\label{sec:limit}

Let $H_\infty$ denote the orthogonal direct sum of the
Dirichlet Laplacians on the intervals $I_n$.
The strong-resolvent endpoint and its divisor spectrum are
classical for this chain \citep{egger2011infinite}.
We include their form description to prove norm convergence
and make the threshold convention explicit.

\begin{theorem}[Dirichlet endpoint and unequal iterated limits]
\label{thm:limit}
As $\kappa\uparrow\infty$, $H_\kappa$ converges to $H_\infty$
in norm resolvent sense. If eigenvalues are ordered with
multiplicity, then
\begin{equation}
E_j(\kappa)\uparrow E_j(\infty)
\quad\text{for every fixed }j.
\label{eq:eigen-limit}
\end{equation}
For every fixed real $E$,
\begin{equation}
N_\kappa(E)=N_\infty(E)
\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }\kappa.
\label{eq:inclusive-limit}
\end{equation}
This includes energies that are eigenvalues of $H_\infty$.
At the endpoint,
\begin{align}
N_\infty(k^2)
 &=\sum_{1\le n\le k}\lfloor k/n\rfloor
   =k\log k+(2\gamma-1)k+O(\sqrt k),\notag\\
Z_\infty(s)
 &=\zeta(2s)^2\qquad(\Repart s>1/2).
\label{eq:endpoint-spectrum}
\end{align}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}
\frac{N_\kappa(k^2)}{k\log k}&=2,\\
\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}
\frac{N_\kappa(k^2)}{k\log k}&=1.
\end{split}
\label{eq:iterated-limits}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Increasing forms and norm convergence}

Fix $\kappa_0>0$.
The forms $q_\kappa$ for $\kappa\ge\kappa_0$ increase,
and their supremum is finite exactly on
\begin{equation}
\DD_\infty
=\{f\in H^1_0(0,\infty):f(x_n)=0\text{ for every }n\ge1\},
\qquad q_\infty[f]=\norm{f'}_2^2.
\label{eq:limit-form}
\end{equation}
Every function in $\DD_\infty$ belongs to $\DD_\delta$:
its sampled sum is zero. Lemma~\ref{lem:sampling} then
also places it in the weighted domain $\DD$.
Continuity of each finite-position trace shows that
$\DD_\infty$ is closed in $H^1_0$, so the limit form is
closed.
It is densely defined in $\HH$ because finite sums of
smooth functions supported in separate open cells are
dense in $\bigoplus_nL^2(I_n)$.

The monotone convergence theorem for nonnegative closed
forms therefore gives strong-resolvent convergence to
the operator represented by \eqref{eq:limit-form}.
One may apply the theorem along an increasing integer
sequence of couplings; form order squeezes intermediate
couplings and gives the same continuous-parameter limit.
Restriction to each cell identifies the limit form with
the direct sum of the Dirichlet interval forms.
Conversely, gluing cell functions whose endpoint traces
are zero and whose $L^2$ and derivative norms are square
summable gives a global $H^1_0$ function.
The limiting operator is thus $H_\infty$.

Set
\[
R_\kappa=(H_\kappa+1)^{-1},
\qquad R_\infty=(H_\infty+1)^{-1}.
\]
Resolvent order for nonnegative closed forms gives
\begin{equation}
0\le R_\kappa-R_\infty\le R_{\kappa_0},
\qquad
R_\kappa-R_\infty\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{strongly}.
\label{eq:compact-domination}
\end{equation}
The upper operator $R_{\kappa_0}$ is compact by
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite}.

Here compact domination upgrades strong to norm convergence.
To see this directly, suppose $0\le T_\kappa\le K$
for a compact positive operator $K$ and that
$T_\kappa\to0$ strongly.
Choose a finite-rank spectral projection $P$ of $K$ such
that $\norm{(I-P)K(I-P)}<\delta$.
Then
$\norm{(I-P)T_\kappa(I-P)}<\delta$.
Cauchy--Schwarz for the positive form
$\langle T_\kappa\,\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ bounds each
off-diagonal block by $\sqrt{\norm K\delta}$.
On the finite-dimensional range of $P$, strong convergence
gives $\norm{PT_\kappa P}\to0$.
The four-block decomposition now shows
\[
\limsup_{\kappa\to\infty}\norm{T_\kappa}
\le\delta+2\sqrt{\norm K\delta}.
\]
Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ proves norm convergence.
Applying this argument to \eqref{eq:compact-domination}
gives $\norm{R_\kappa-R_\infty}\to0$, which is
norm-resolvent convergence.
This is an application of a general compact-operator
principle, not a new abstract convergence theorem.

\subsection{Eigenvalues and inclusive thresholds}

The Dirichlet eigenvalues on $I_n$ are
\[
\left(\frac{\pi m}{\ell_n}\right)^2=m^2n^2,
\qquad m\ge1.
\]
Thus $j^2$ has multiplicity $d(j)$, where $d(j)$ is the
number of positive divisors of $j$.
Only finitely many pairs $(m,n)$ lie below a fixed energy,
so this description also proves compactness of
$R_\infty$.
Norm convergence of compact positive resolvents and their
min--max characterization imply convergence of each
ordered resolvent eigenvalue, including repetitions.
Transforming by $r\mapsto r^{-1}-1$ proves convergence
of each $E_j(\kappa)$.
Form monotonicity and restriction of the form domain give
\[
E_j(\kappa)\le E_j(\kappa')\le E_j(\infty)
\qquad(\kappa\le\kappa'),
\]
which completes \eqref{eq:eigen-limit}.

Fix a real energy $E$ and put $J=N_\infty(E)$.
All indices $j\le J$ satisfy
$E_j(\kappa)\le E_j(\infty)\le E$ and are counted at
every finite coupling.
The next endpoint eigenvalue satisfies
$E_{J+1}(\infty)>E$.
By \eqref{eq:eigen-limit},
$E_{J+1}(\kappa)>E$ for sufficiently large $\kappa$,
and then all later eigenvalues are excluded as well.
This proves \eqref{eq:inclusive-limit}, including $E=j^2$.
When $J=0$, only the second part of the argument is needed.

The convention $\le$ is essential to this threshold
statement: it uses convergence from below.
For strict counts defined with $<$, convergence away from
endpoint eigenvalues still follows, but equality at an
endpoint eigenvalue is not asserted.
The coupling threshold in \eqref{eq:inclusive-limit}
need not be uniform as $E\to\infty$.

\subsection{Divisor count and limiting orders}

Counting the pairs with $m^2n^2\le k^2$ proves the first
identity in \eqref{eq:endpoint-spectrum}.
For real $k\to\infty$, let $L=\lfloor\sqrt k\rfloor$.
Every pair $mn\le k$ has $m\le L$ or $n\le L$.
The $L^2$ pairs with both inequalities have been counted
twice, so
\begin{equation}
\sum_{n\le k}\lfloor k/n\rfloor
=2\sum_{n\le L}\lfloor k/n\rfloor-L^2
=2kh_L-L^2+O(L).
\label{eq:hyperbola}
\end{equation}
The bounds \eqref{eq:harmonic-bounds} give
$h_L=\log L+\gamma+O(1/L)$, since their difference
is at most $1/L$.
Using $L=\sqrt k+O(1)$ in \eqref{eq:hyperbola}
yields
$k\log k+(2\gamma-1)k+O(\sqrt k)$.
For $\Repart s>1/2$, absolute convergence gives
\[
Z_\infty(s)=
\sum_{m,n\ge1}(m^2n^2)^{-s}
=\left(\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-2s}\right)^2
=\zeta(2s)^2.
\]
Finally \eqref{eq:count-law} gives the first iterated
limit in \eqref{eq:iterated-limits}.
At every fixed $k>1$, \eqref{eq:inclusive-limit}
gives the inner limit in the second expression, and the
endpoint asymptotic then gives one.
This completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:limit}.
