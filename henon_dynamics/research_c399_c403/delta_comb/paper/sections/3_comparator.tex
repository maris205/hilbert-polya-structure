\section{A uniform exponential comparison}
\label{sec:comparator}

The coefficient of the kinetic term in
\eqref{eq:form-bracket} will depend on energy.
For that reason a fixed-parameter exponential-potential
asymptotic is not enough. The following estimate controls the
whole compact parameter interval needed later.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform comparator count]
\label{lem:comparator}
For each fixed $C>0$ there exist $r_0(C)>0$ and $M_C<\infty$
such that, for every $a\in[1/2,3/2]$ and real $r\ge r_0(C)$,
\begin{equation}
\left|N_{A_{a,C}}(r^2)
 -\frac{2r}{\sqrt a}
       \left[\log\frac{2r}{\sqrt C}-1\right]\right|
\le M_C.
\label{eq:uniform-count}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Under the unitary rescaling $x=2\pi y$, the operator becomes
\begin{equation}
A_{a,C}\simeq\frac{a}{4\pi^2}\mathcal B_b,
\qquad
\mathcal B_b=-\frac{d^2}{dy^2}+b^2e^{2y},
\qquad
b=2\pi\sqrt{C/a}.
\label{eq:bessel-scale}
\end{equation}
It has a Dirichlet condition at $y=0$.
For energy $r^2$ define $K=2\pi r/\sqrt a$.
The parameter $b$ ranges over one fixed compact interval
$[b_-,b_+]\subset(0,\infty)$, depending only on $C$.
The weighted-domain and compactness argument of
Section~\ref{sec:forms} gives a positive compact-resolvent
operator $\mathcal B_b$.

The change $z=be^y$ turns its eigenvalue equation at energy
$K^2$ into the modified Bessel equation of order $iK$.
The decaying solution is $K_{iK}(be^y)$.
For each fixed $K$ its large-$y$ asymptotic is
$\sqrt{\pi/(2be^y)}e^{-be^y}$, whereas the independent
growing solution has size
$e^{be^y}/\sqrt{2\pi be^y}$.
These are the classical modified-Bessel solution asymptotics
\citep[10.25.3 and 10.40.1]{nist2026dlmf}.
Thus the square-integrable solution at infinity is unique up
to a scalar, and the positive eigenvalues are characterized
exactly by
\[
K_{iK}(b)=0.
\]
Here the subscript denotes imaginary \emph{order}, not an
imaginary spatial argument.

Put $d=b^2/4$.
The Bessel series and connection identity
\citep[10.25.2 and 10.27.4]{nist2026dlmf} give, for $K>0$,
\begin{align}
I_{-iK}(b)
 &=\frac{(b/2)^{-iK}}{\Gamma(1-iK)}S_b(K),\notag\\
S_b(K)
 &=\sum_{j=0}^\infty\frac{d^j}{j!(1-iK)_j},\notag\\
K_{iK}(b)
 &=\frac{\pi\,\Impart I_{-iK}(b)}{\sinh(\pi K)}.
\label{eq:bessel-series}
\end{align}
The last sign follows directly from
$\sin(\pi iK)=i\sinh(\pi K)$ and
$I_{iK}(b)=\overline{I_{-iK}(b)}$.
The rising product $(1-iK)_j$ has modulus at least $K^j$.
Differentiation of its reciprocal introduces at most $j$
factors, each of modulus at most $1/K$.
Normal convergence of the series and its derivative on
$K\ge K_0>0$ justifies termwise differentiation.
Writing $d_+=b_+^2/4$ therefore yields, for $K\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
|S_b(K)-1|\le e^{d_+/K}-1,\qquad
|S_b'(K)|\le \frac{d_+}{K^2}e^{d_+/K},
\label{eq:uniform-series}
\end{equation}
uniformly in $b\in[b_-,b_+]$.

Choose one $K_0$ with $e^{d_+/K_0}-1<1/2$.
For $K\ge K_0$, all $S_b(K)$ then lie in the disk
$|z-1|<1/2$, which carries a single logarithm.
Its imaginary part is $O_C(K^{-1})$, and its derivative
is $O_C(K^{-2})$.
For the Gamma function use the logarithm analytic in
the right half-plane and real at 1.
The sectorial Stirling and digamma expansions
\citep[5.11.1--2]{nist2026dlmf} imply
\begin{align}
\Impart\log\Gamma(1+iK)
 &=K\log K-K+\frac{\pi}{4}+O(K^{-1}),\notag\\
\Repart\psi(1+iK)
 &=\log K+O(K^{-2}).
\label{eq:gamma-phase}
\end{align}
The sector is fixed and no $b$ occurs in these estimates.

The unwrapped phase
\[
\Psi_b(K)=K\log(2/b)+\Impart\log\Gamma(1+iK)
                         +\Impart\log S_b(K)
\]
is therefore well defined for $K\ge K_0$ and satisfies
\begin{align}
\Psi_b(K)
 &=K\log(2K/b)-K+\frac{\pi}{4}+O_C(K^{-1}),\notag\\
\Psi_b'(K)
 &=\log(2K/b)+O_C(K^{-2})>0
\label{eq:phase-monotone}
\end{align}
after increasing the same $K_0$ if necessary.
All bounds and this choice are uniform in the compact
$b$ interval.
The amplitude in \eqref{eq:bessel-series} is nonzero.
Consequently, above $K_0$, the roots occur exactly once
at each crossing of an integer multiple of $\pi$
by $\Psi_b$.
At a root, the inclusive convention changes the count
relative to either neighboring open interval by at most one.

It remains to control the low-energy counting offset
uniformly. Form order gives
$\mathcal B_b\ge\mathcal B_{b_-}$.
Hence the number of roots at or below $K_0$ is bounded
by $N_{\mathcal B_{b_-}}(K_0^2)<\infty$.
The phase $\Psi_b(K_0)$ is bounded on the same compact
$b$ interval. Thus both the low-energy count and
the integer offset in the phase count are uniformly bounded.
We obtain
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathcal B_b}(K^2)
=\frac K\pi\left[\log(2K/b)-1\right]+O_C(1).
\label{eq:scaled-count}
\end{equation}
Finally $K=2\pi r/\sqrt a$ and $2K/b=2r/\sqrt C$.
Substitution into \eqref{eq:scaled-count} proves
\eqref{eq:uniform-count}.
\end{proof}

The use of special functions here evaluates a comparator,
not the spectrum or determinant of the actual delta-comb.
The estimates \eqref{eq:uniform-series} and the uniform
low-energy offset are what allow the kinetic coefficient
$a$ to move with the counting energy.
