\section{The Galois-excess pressure trichotomy}

Define the abscissa of the positive excess series by
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\rm Gal}
=\inf\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb R:
\sum_\gamma \Egal_\gamma
e^{-\sigma\widehat\ell_\gamma}<\infty\right\}.
\label{eq:sigma-gal}
\end{equation}
The P53 all-orbit majorant proves
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\rm Gal}
\le \frac{\log(2\phi)}{h_*\log J_*}
<3.125207.
\label{eq:sigma-upper}
\end{equation}
The physical series has abscissa one by Theorem~4.2 and the prime orbit
theorem.  Since both coefficient systems are nonnegative, the defining total
series has abscissa
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\Aamp}=\max\{1,\sigma_{\rm Gal}\}.
\label{eq:total-abscissa}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Pressure-access trichotomy]
Exactly one of the following occurs.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(\sigma_{\rm Gal}<1\), then \(\Aamp_{\rm Gal}\) is holomorphic
near \(1\), and the full amplitude has the physical pole and residue
\eqref{eq:physical-pole}.
\item If \(\sigma_{\rm Gal}=1\), then the excess occupies the same critical
abscissa, but the abscissa alone determines neither convergence at \(1\) nor
the existence or residue of a singularity there.  Those boundary data require
a weighted thermodynamic theorem or equivalent analytic input.
\item If \(\sigma_{\rm Gal}>1\), then the positive defining excess series
loses convergence before the physical pressure line.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Positive generalized Dirichlet series converge normally and define
holomorphic functions to the right of their abscissa.  Combine this fact with
\eqref{eq:amp-split}, Theorem~4.2 and \eqref{eq:total-abscissa}.
\end{proof}

The third case is a statement about the defining series.  It does not exclude
an analytic continuation established by a different theorem.  The
trichotomy replaces the crude safe half-plane by one exact unknown scalar,
\(\sigma_{\rm Gal}\).
