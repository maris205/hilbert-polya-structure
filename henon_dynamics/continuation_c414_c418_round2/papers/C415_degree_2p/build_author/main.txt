                                                 Abstract
           Let p be an odd prime, q = p with e ≥ 3, and let g ∈ Fq [y] have degree 2p. For every
                                         e

       a ∈ F∗q we determine the ordinary geometric fixed points of every iterate of S = H −1 Φq ,
       where H(x, y) = (y, y q + g(y) − ax) and Φq is coordinatewise q-Frobenius. The answer
       has two coefficient-support branches. If g has a nonzero term of degree strictly between
       p + 1 and 2p, a two-term degree block gives the count. If all such terms vanish, a different
       recurrence arises from a semilinear operator on a perfected polynomial ring. A two-term
       invariant controls every fractional and mixed remainder and descends to an ordinary
       polynomial, including arbitrary coefficients outside the prime field. Writing w = pvp (n) ,
       both branches yield an explicit integer Dw and the formula # Fix(S n ) = q 2n−w Dw . The
       relevant fixed schemes are finite and reduced. The resulting dynamical zeta function,
       and every positive integral power of it, has the circle |t| = q −2 as a meromorphic natural
       boundary.

Keywords: Hénon automorphism; finite field; Frobenius; ordinary fixed points; perfection;
dynamical zeta function.

1.1    Relation to existing mechanisms
Artin–Mazur zeta functions in positive characteristic already exhibit arithmetic dependence on
the iterate. Bridy determines rationality or transcendence for dynamically affine rational maps
on the projective line [2]. Byszewski, Cornelissen and Houben study smooth algebraic-group
endomorphisms and a class of finite-adelically distorted fixed-point sequences [3]. Those are
source frameworks and comparison classes; neither is used here as a theorem asserting the
present nonlinear coefficient-family formula. We make no claim that all positive-characteristic
zeta functions satisfy a single such dichotomy.
    The internal predecessor C404 [1] supplies the commuting-pullback route from a degree
calculation to reduced ordinary counts, and the positive-tail argument for a zeta natural
boundary. We reproduce both arguments so that no external proof file is needed to read this

                                                 2
paper. The integral strict-gap calculation is likewise an input mechanism, presented in full in
Section 4. The additional result here is the complete degree-2p support classification for all
odd p and all q = pe with e ≥ 3. Its essential step is the low-support semilinear induction,
including fractional tails and descent, in Sections 5–6. The natural boundary is a consequence
of that count, not a separate new analytic mechanism.
    Section 2 fixes the algebraic conventions, and Section 3 proves the conditional count
conversion. The two degree calculations then prove Theorem 1.1. Section 7 gives the explicit
zeta product and its boundary; Section 8 records a support-sensitive example and the limits of
the result.

    It remains to justify passage from length to ordinary points. The Jacobian of the defining
pair in (2.2) is DH n , since derivatives of q n -powers vanish in characteristic p. As det DH = a,
its determinant is an = ̸ 0 at every point. At each solution, the equations have independent
linear parts in the ambient two-dimensional cotangent space. The cotangent space of the
quotient local Artinian algebra is therefore zero: its maximal ideal modulo its square vanishes.
Nakayama’s lemma makes that maximal ideal zero. The local algebra is K, and every point
has length one. Thus En is reduced and its length is its ordinary geometric count.

support distinction enters only in establishing (2.6), to which we now turn.

    The theorem includes the pure pth-power support face, arbitrary constant term, q = p3 , and
all coefficients in Fq , not just Fp . The high-support result is independent of every coefficient
below y ℓ ; the low-support result is independent of every coefficient below the leading y 2p for
purposes of the final count. That independence follows from the proved degree estimates, not
from replacing these coefficients by zero.
    Several boundaries of the statement are essential to its present proof. Odd characteristic
keeps the leading factor 2 nonzero and makes the fractional gap in (6.2) strict. The assumption
e ≥ 3 gives the uniform high-support margins and the simple low-support estimates. We do
not assert either formula for p = 2 or e = 1, 2. Nor do we treat a = 0, a vanishing leading
coefficient, correction degrees other than 2p, or coefficients not fixed by q-Frobenius. The last
change would remove the commutation used in (3.1).
    The observable is the ordinary geometric fixed-point count for S on A2 (K). It is not a
formula for forward periodic points of an arbitrary separable polynomial, a count in one fixed
finite extension, or a scheme-length convention with multiplicities left in place. Reducedness
was proved before assigning the ordinary count. The zeta function is native to this same map
and clock; no identification with a prescribed arithmetic Euler product, target zero set, or
spectral realization is claimed.
    The result therefore consists of one full coefficient-family classification and its native analytic
consequence. The calculation in the perfected ring uses only finite algebraic expressions, with
each iterate lying at a finite denominator level. Its descent returns the calculation to ordinary
polynomials and ordinary points.
