\section{Support sensitivity and the limits of the theorem}\label{sec:scope}

\begin{example}[The smallest field boundary]\label{ex:q27}
Take $p=3$, $q=27$, and $a=1$. Both
$g_0(y)=y^6$ and $g_1(y)=y^6+y^5$ have degree six, but their
third fixed counts differ. The first is low support and the second
has $\ell=5$, $h=1$. Substitution into the proved formulas yields
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|r}
 g &D_3&N_3=27^3D_3\\\hline
 y^6&2484&48\,892\,572\\
 y^6+y^5&3030&59\,639\,490
\end{array}
\]
These numbers are evaluations of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, not a
finite-field census used to establish its quantifiers. They show
why total degree alone does not determine the resonant count.
\end{example}

The theorem includes the pure $p$th-power support face, arbitrary
constant term, $q=p^3$, and all coefficients in $\F_q$, not just
$\F_p$. The high-support result is independent of every coefficient
below $y^\ell$; the low-support result is independent of every
coefficient below the leading $y^{2p}$ for purposes of the final
count. That independence follows from the proved degree estimates,
not from replacing these coefficients by zero.

Several boundaries of the statement are essential to its present
proof. Odd characteristic keeps the leading factor $2$ nonzero
and makes the fractional gap in \eqref{eq:fractional-image} strict.
The assumption $e\ge3$ gives the uniform high-support margins and
the simple low-support estimates. We do not assert either formula
for $p=2$ or $e=1,2$. Nor do we treat $a=0$, a vanishing leading
coefficient, correction degrees other than $2p$, or coefficients
not fixed by $q$-Frobenius. The last change would remove the
commutation used in \eqref{eq:operator-factorization}.

The observable is the ordinary geometric fixed-point count for
$S$ on $\A^2(K)$. It is not a formula for forward periodic points
of an arbitrary separable polynomial, a count in one fixed finite
extension, or a scheme-length convention with multiplicities left
in place. Reducedness was proved before assigning the ordinary
count. The zeta function is native to this same map and clock;
no identification with a prescribed arithmetic Euler product,
target zero set, or spectral realization is claimed.

The result therefore consists of one full coefficient-family
classification and its native analytic consequence. The calculation
in the perfected ring uses only finite algebraic expressions, with
each iterate lying at a finite denominator level. Its descent
returns the calculation to ordinary polynomials and ordinary points.
