\section{The full coefficient-family count}\label{sec:intro}

In characteristic $p$, the first binomial term surviving a subtraction
can depend on the $p$-valuation of an exponent. For H\'enon--Frobenius
fixed-point equations this makes a lower coefficient decisive even
when the total degree is fixed. We determine this dependence for every
degree-$2p$ correction, rather than for a prescribed sparse support.

Throughout the paper let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameters}
 p\text{ be odd},\qquad q=p^e,\quad e\ge3,\qquad
 g(y)=\sum_{i=0}^{2p}b_i y^i\in\F_q[y],\quad b:=b_{2p}\ne0,
 \qquad a\in\F_q^*.
\end{equation}
Set $K=\overline{\F}_q$ and define maps on the whole affine plane by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maps}
 H(x,y)=(y,y^q+g(y)-ax),\qquad
 \Phi_q(x,y)=(x^q,y^q),\qquad S=H^{-1}\Phi_q.
\end{equation}
Our observable is
\[
 N_n=\#\{P\in\A^2(K):S^n(P)=P\},\qquad n\ge1,
\]
where points are counted without multiplicity. The time variable is
ordinary positive iteration of the one specified map $S$.

The following two alternatives partition all coefficients allowed
by \eqref{eq:parameters}.
\begin{itemize}
\item In the \emph{high-support class}, some $b_i\ne0$ for
  $p+2\le i<2p$. Define
  \[
  \ell=\max\{i:p+2\le i<2p,\ b_i\ne0\},\qquad
  c=b_\ell,\qquad h=2p-\ell.
  \]
  Thus $1\le h\le p-2$, $c\ne0$, and $p\nmid\ell$.
\item In the \emph{low-support class}, all those coefficients vanish,
  and we write
  \[
  g(y)=by^{2p}+cy^{p+1}+R_0(y),\qquad \deg R_0\le p.
  \]
  Here $c=b_{p+1}$ is allowed to vanish.
\end{itemize}
The notation $c$ is local to the applicable alternative. All lower
coefficients and the nonzero constants $a,b$ remain unrestricted in
$\F_q$.

\begin{theorem}[All-period ordinary counts]\label{thm:main}
Under \eqref{eq:parameters}, define, for $j\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degree-formulas}
 D_j=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{(\ell-1)q^j+q(h+1)-2p}{q-1},
       &\text{in the high-support class},\\[8pt]
 \displaystyle\frac{p q^j+(q-2p)p^j}{q-p},
       &\text{in the low-support class}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
These are positive integers with $D_j<q^j$. For every $n\ge1$, put
$w=p^{v_p(n)}$. The fixed scheme of $S^n$ is finite and reduced, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-count}
 \boxed{N_n=q^{2n-w}D_w.}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $p\nmid n$, then $N_n=2p\,q^{2n-1}$ in both classes.
\end{theorem}

The two formulas agree at $j=1$, but their resonant continuations
are different. The mechanism behind this distinction is summarized
in Table~\ref{tab:support}. The low-support argument is not obtained
by setting a coefficient equal to zero in the high-support formula:
the retained degree block itself changes.

\subsection{Relation to existing mechanisms}

Artin--Mazur zeta functions in positive characteristic already exhibit
arithmetic dependence on the iterate. Bridy determines rationality
or transcendence for dynamically affine rational maps on the
projective line~\cite{Bridy2016}. Byszewski, Cornelissen and Houben
study smooth algebraic-group endomorphisms and a class of
finite-adelically distorted fixed-point sequences~\cite{BCH2024}.
Those are source frameworks and comparison classes; neither is used
here as a theorem asserting the present nonlinear coefficient-family
formula. We make no claim that all positive-characteristic zeta
functions satisfy a single such dichotomy.

The internal predecessor C404~\cite{C404} supplies the commuting-pullback
route from a degree calculation to reduced ordinary counts, and the
positive-tail argument for a zeta natural boundary. We reproduce both
arguments so that no external proof file is needed to read this paper.
The integral strict-gap calculation is likewise an input mechanism,
presented in full in Section~\ref{sec:high}. The additional result here
is the complete degree-$2p$ support classification for all odd $p$ and
all $q=p^e$ with $e\ge3$. Its essential step is the low-support
semilinear induction, including fractional tails and descent, in
Sections~\ref{sec:perfection}--\ref{sec:low}. The natural boundary is
a consequence of that count, not a separate new analytic mechanism.

Section~\ref{sec:setup} fixes the algebraic conventions, and
Section~\ref{sec:conversion} proves the conditional count conversion.
The two degree calculations then prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
Section~\ref{sec:zeta} gives the explicit zeta product and its boundary;
Section~\ref{sec:scope} records a support-sensitive example and the
limits of the result.
