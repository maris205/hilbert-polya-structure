\section{From one degree sequence to every ordinary count}\label{sec:conversion}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional count conversion]\label{prop:conversion}
Suppose that for every $j\ge1$ the polynomial $\delta^j y$ has a unique
top term $c_jy^{D_j}$ with $c_j\in\F_q^*$ and $0<D_j<q^j$.
Then for every $n\ge1$, with $w=p^{v_p(n)}$, the scheme
$\mathcal E_n$ is finite and reduced and has $q^{2n-w}D_w$ points.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $n=ws$, where $p\nmid s$. The characteristic-$p$ binomial
identity in the algebra of commuting linear operators gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operator-factorization}
 T^w-U^w=\delta^w,\qquad
 T^n-U^n=
 \sum_{i=0}^{s-1}T^{wi}U^{w(s-1-i)}\delta^w.
\end{equation}
Either $T$ or $U$ sends a polynomial with unique top term $d_0y^D$
to one with unique top term $d_0y^{qD}$. Indeed, the substitution
of any lower-degree monomial has degree at most $q(D-1)$, whereas
the leading $y$-monomial acquires degree $qD$ from the monic $y^q$
term. Both operators fix the coefficient $d_0\in\F_q$.
Every summand of \eqref{eq:operator-factorization}, applied to $y$,
therefore has the same top term. Since $s$ is nonzero in $\F_q$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-leading}
 H_2^n(x,y)-y^{q^n}
 =s c_w y^{q^{n-w}D_w}
   +\text{terms of lower total degree}.
\end{equation}

For the first equation, $\deg H_1^n=q^{n-1}$. This follows by induction
from $H_1^{n+1}=H_2^n$ and the unique top term $y^{q^n}$ in $H_2^n$;
the latter persists because $\deg g<q$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-leading}
 H_1^n(x,y)-x^{q^n}
 =-x^{q^n}+\text{terms of lower total degree}.
\end{equation}
Choose a monomial order refining total degree. After rescaling the
two equations, their leading monomials are
\[
 x^M,\quad y^L,\qquad M=q^n,\quad L=q^{n-w}D_w.
\]
They are relatively prime, so these two polynomials form a Gr\"obner
basis. To recall the elementary criterion in this case, write them
as $F=x^M+F_0$ and $G=y^L+G_0$, with $\deg F_0<M$ and $\deg G_0<L$.
Their sole $S$-polynomial has the standard representation
\[
 y^L F-x^M G=F_0G-G_0F,
\]
whose summands have leading monomials strictly below $x^My^L$.
Buchberger's criterion applies. This is the standard argument for
coprime leading monomials; see~\cite{CLO2015} for the general framework.
Consequently the quotient over $K$ has basis
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rectangle-basis}
 \{x^u y^v:0\le u<M,\ 0\le v<L\}
\end{equation}
and dimension $ML=q^{2n-w}D_w$. This proves finiteness and the
scheme length. It also shows directly that no projective point at
infinity contributes: the leading forms $x^M,y^L$ have no common
zero on the line at infinity.

It remains to justify passage from length to ordinary points. The
Jacobian of the defining pair in \eqref{eq:equalizer} is $DH^n$,
since derivatives of $q^n$-powers vanish in characteristic $p$.
As $\det DH=a$, its determinant is $a^n\ne0$ at every point.
At each solution, the equations have independent linear parts in
the ambient two-dimensional cotangent space. The cotangent space
of the quotient local Artinian algebra is therefore zero: its
maximal ideal modulo its square vanishes. Nakayama's lemma makes
that maximal ideal zero. The local algebra is $K$, and every
point has length one. Thus $\mathcal E_n$ is reduced and its
length is its ordinary geometric count.
\end{proof}

The argument is independent of how the degree sequence is obtained.
The coefficient-support distinction enters only in establishing
\eqref{eq:leading-claim}, to which we now turn.
