\section{Introduction}
For a fixed nonperiodic orbit of a H\'enon map, forward and backward
canonical heights have opposite geometric scaling. Counting all
polynomial points requires an additional step: the different orbits
must be parametrized without overlap, with the correct arithmetic
multiplicity. For constant coefficients over a finite field, this
step has an exact answer. Each nonconstant orbit has a unique degree
valley of one of two types. Their multiplicities do not depend on the
coefficients of the map.

We use this classification to determine the full two-sided
canonical-height distribution on $\mathbb F_q[t]^2$. Its analytic
structure is not captured by a single leading pole. The sectors
coming from neighboring time shifts meet at the same candidate poles,
and their residues must be combined before any natural-boundary
conclusion is justified. We compute the combined residue explicitly
and prove that it never vanishes at the proposed simple poles.

The resulting contribution has three parts.
\begin{enumerate}
\item An exhaustive, disjoint edge/turn parametrization gives exact
height multiplicities for every prime power, every degree at least
two and every allowed constant coefficient.
\item The associated series has absolute-convergence abscissa one,
an explicit complete simple/double pole divisor in $\Re s>0$,
and a meromorphic natural boundary at $\Re s=0$.
\item A direct count gives the real-height asymptotic with its
nonconstant fractional-part oscillation. Replacing the floor in
the exponential main term by a real exponent would change the result.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Classical height inputs.}
Kawaguchi constructs local Green functions and global canonical
heights for regular polynomial automorphisms
\citep{kawaguchi2013local}. The height limits, functional equations
and two-sided orbit scaling are classical inputs. The global results
there are formulated over number fields, and the bounded-height
orbit count in Corollary D concerns a given orbit and naive height.
It does not give the distribution over all polynomial points studied
here. Our required specialization is proved directly by degrees.

Ingram develops canonical heights and non-Archimedean escape for
H\'enon maps \citep{ingram2014canonical}. The introduction and
Section 2 of arXiv:1111.3609v1, in particular Lemma 2.1, supply the
nearby framework used for comparison. The direct degree argument
below handles arbitrary nonzero leading coefficient without a
separability assumption. We do not claim a new canonical-height
construction or a general Northcott theorem. The bibliography uses
the verified journal issue metadata, while the text comparison remains
to the accessed arXiv version.

\paragraph{Height-zeta counting.}
Hsia's published development of dynamical height zeta functions
\citep{hsia1997dynamical} is a predecessor of the one-variable
counting program. We use its bibliographic attribution, not an
unaccessed theorem: the original full article was not available for
the present comparison. Takehira's account attributes the definition,
via Hsia, to Silverman's unpublished 1994 seminar talk; that talk
was also not accessed. Neither is a proof dependency here.

Takehira gives explicit one-variable function-field height-zeta
formulas and treats the effect of several vertical-line poles on
height counting \citep{takehira2025number}. Our comparison is to
the introduction, Section 2.2, Condition 3.1, Theorem 3.4 and the
opening of Section 4 in arXiv:2404.00955v1. Its finite local
height-discrepancy partition does not apply to the present whole
two-dimensional domain: for an edge with both coordinate degrees
$m$, the two-sided height is $2m$ and the naive height is $m$.
The discrepancy is therefore unbounded even with constant coefficients.
The theorem below retains the oscillation explicitly, without a
single-pole Tauberian assumption. These source comparisons are bounded
by the stated text access and do not assert worldwide priority.
