\section{Counting at arbitrary real height bounds}\label{sec:counting}
We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:counting} directly from the nonnegative
census, so the multiple poles on $\Re s=1$ do not create an
unjustified single-pole asymptotic. Set
$N=\lfloor B\rfloor$ and $\alpha=(d-1)/(d+1)$.
The central edge contribution, corresponding to $k=0$, is
\begin{align}\label{eq:central-count}
 (q-1)^2\sum_{j\le N}b_jq^j
 &=\alpha(q-1)q^{N+1}N+O_{q,d}(q^N).
\end{align}
Indeed \eqref{eq:b-j} has a bounded error, and differentiation
of the finite geometric sum gives
\[
 \sum_{j\le N}j q^j=
 \frac{q^{N+1}N}{q-1}+O_q(q^N).
\]

The adjacent edge shifts $k=1,-1$ each contribute
$O_{q,d}(q^B)$. For $k=1$ set $e=dn-m\ge1$.
Its height and multiplicity exponent become
\[
 d^{-1}m+dn=(d+1)n-e/d,\qquad m+n=(d+1)n-e.
\]
For fixed $e$, enlarge the degree range to all positive $n$
satisfying the height bound. The geometric sum of weights is
at most a constant depending on $q,d$ times
$q^{B-(1-1/d)e}$. Summing over $e\ge1$ converges.
Interchanging $m,n$ treats the negative shift. The two central
turn shifts $k=0,1$ also contribute $O_{q,d}(q^B)$, since
\[
 D_M\le c_{q,d}q^{(1+1/d)M}
\]
and their height is $(1+1/d)M$; the remaining sum in $M$
is geometric.

All other shifts have a strict exponential gap. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha-two}
 \alpha_2=\frac{d+1}{d^2+d^{-1}}<1.
\end{equation}
For edge shifts $|k|\ge2$, the cone inequalities give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edge-gap}
 m+n\le\alpha_2(d^{-k}m+d^kn).
\end{equation}
For $k>0$, the ratio of the left side to the height increases
with $m/n$ on $1/d<m/n<d$. Its supremum is
$(d+1)/L_k\le\alpha_2$, proving the claim; negative
indices follow by symmetry. For turns with $k\notin\{0,1\}$,
the analogous inequality is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:turn-gap}
 (1+1/d)M\le\alpha_2 M(d^{-k}+d^{k-1}).
\end{equation}
The first exterior turn shifts give equality in the ratio
bound, and the denominator grows farther away from the center.

At height at most $B$, the representative degrees are at most
$B$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:heights}. There are only
$O_d(1+\log(1+B))$ admissible integer shifts: every edge
height is at least $d^{|k|}$, and every turn height is at least
$d^{\max(k-1,-k)}$. Thus all the exterior contributions are
\[
 O_{q,d}\!\left(B^2(1+\log(1+B))q^{\alpha_2B}\right)
 =O_{q,d}(q^B).
\]
The strict gap $1-\alpha_2$ absorbs the polynomial and
logarithmic factors. The $q^2$ constant points are harmless.
Combining these estimates with \eqref{eq:central-count},
then replacing $N$ by $B$ only in its linear factor, proves
\eqref{eq:asymptotic}. The latter replacement costs
$O_{q,d}(q^B)$, but replacing $\lfloor B\rfloor$ in the
exponent would change the leading oscillation. This completes
the proof for arbitrary real $B$.
