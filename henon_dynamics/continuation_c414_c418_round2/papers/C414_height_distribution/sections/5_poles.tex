\section{Combined residues and the natural boundary}\label{sec:poles}
Candidate poles of separate sectors need not survive their sum.
We combine all sectors at each lattice point before deciding its
order. Fix $k\ge2$ and $s_0=s_{k,\ell}$. Since the $L_k$
are strictly increasing for $k\ge1$, only $C_{k-1}$, $C_k$
and $E_k$ can have a pole there, each with weight two in
\eqref{eq:sector-sum}. Formula \eqref{eq:cone-subtract} gives
the coefficients of $(1-q^{d+1-sL_k})^{-1}$ from the two
cone sectors, evaluated at $s_0$, as
\[
 \frac{(q-1)^2}{B_{k-1}(s_0)-1},\qquad
 \frac{(q-1)^2}{A_k(s_0)-1}.
\]
Set $A=A_k(s_0)$ and $v=(qz_k(s_0))^{-1}$. The pole
condition implies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pole-relations}
 v^d=q,\qquad A B_{k-1}(s_0)=q^2z_k(s_0)=q/v.
\end{equation}
Adding the turn numerator in \eqref{eq:turn-generating},
the full coefficient before the common weight two is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R-uncombined}
 R=(q-1)^2\left(\frac1{A-1}+\frac{A}{q/v-A}+1\right)
       +(q-1)q\sum_{r=1}^{d-1}v^{-r}.
\end{equation}
Using $v^d=q$, the finite geometric sum is
$(q-v)/(q(v-1))$. Also
\[
 \frac1{A-1}+\frac{A}{q/v-A}+1
 =\frac{A(q-v)}{(A-1)(q-vA)}.
\]
The remaining numerator identity is
\[
 (q-1)A(v-1)+(A-1)(q-vA)=(q-A)(vA-1).
\]
Consequently the exact aggregate factorizes as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R-factor}
 R=\frac{(q-1)(q-v)(q-A)(vA-1)}
         {(v-1)(A-1)(q-vA)}.
\end{equation}

Let $u=q^{1/d}$. At the selected lattice point $|v|=u$, and
for $k\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modulus-gap}
 q/u<|A|=q^{1-(d+1)/(d^kL_k)}<q.
\end{equation}
All numerator and denominator factors in \eqref{eq:R-factor}
are nonzero: $1<|v|<q$, $1<|A|<q$, and $|vA|>q>1$.
Thus every candidate at level $k\ge2$ is a genuine simple pole.
Since the derivative of $1-q^{d+1-sL_k}$ at $s_0$ is
$L_k\log q$, its residue is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residue}
 \Res_{s=s_0}Z_H(s)=\frac{2R}{L_k\log q},
 \quad R\text{ given by \eqref{eq:R-factor}}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The level-one collision.}
For $k=1$, both cone denominators of $C_0$ coincide.
Put $w=B_0(s_0)=q^{1-s_0}$. When $w\ne1$, formula
\eqref{eq:cone-subtract} gives singular coefficient
$2(q-1)^2/(w-1)$ from $C_0$. Although $C_0$ has weight
one in the sum, the coincident cone factors have thus supplied
the same weight two needed in \eqref{eq:R-uncombined}.
The other sectors $C_1,E_1$ already have weight two.
Equations \eqref{eq:R-factor} and \eqref{eq:residue} hold
unchanged.

At this level $|A|=q/u>1$ and $|vA|=q$. The factor
$q-vA$ vanishes exactly when $w=1$, by
\eqref{eq:pole-relations}. Excluding that case, it is nonzero,
and every numerator factor is still nonzero by its modulus.
Thus these level-one points are genuine simple poles.

Finally $w=1$ exactly when $(d+1)\mid\ell$. From
\eqref{eq:b-j}, write
$b_j=\alpha j+\rho_j$, where $\alpha=(d-1)/(d+1)$ and
$\rho_j$ is bounded and periodic of period $d+1$.
The series of $\rho_j w^j$ is rational with denominator
$1-w^{d+1}$ and hence has at most a simple pole at $w=1$.
The linear part gives $\alpha w/(1-w)^2$. Since
$w=q^{1-s}$ and $w(s_0)=1$, the coefficient of
$(s-s_0)^{-2}$ in $C_0$ is precisely
\eqref{eq:double-coeff}. It is positive. The other sectors
have at most simple poles, so cannot cancel it. We have now
proved the entire pole divisor, including every root-of-unity
phase and the cases $q=2$ or $d=2$.

\paragraph{Accumulation at every boundary point.}
As $k\to\infty$, $(d+1)/L_k\to0$ and
$2\pi/(L_k\log q)\to0$. Given $\tau\in\mathbb R$, choose
$\ell_k$ to be an integer nearest to
$\tau L_k\log q/(2\pi)$. The genuine poles $s_{k,\ell_k}$
converge to $i\tau$. A meromorphic continuation across that
point would have poles accumulating at an interior point,
which is impossible for a meromorphic function agreeing with
the initial finite-valued series. Hence no point of the axis
admits such a continuation. This completes the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:poles}.
