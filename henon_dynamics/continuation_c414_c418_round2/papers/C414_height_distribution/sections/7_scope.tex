\section{Scope and reproducibility}\label{sec:scope}
The exact distribution follows from an all-orbit degree-valley
census, not from the canonical-height definition alone. Its
coefficient uniformity includes arbitrary nonzero constant leading
coefficients, all lower coefficients, every nonzero determinant
and inseparable polynomials. The natural boundary follows only
after the neighboring sectors' residues have been combined and
shown nonzero. The point-count asymptotic retains the lattice
dependence on a real height bound.

The accompanying source package includes the exact diagnostic
\texttt{check\_height\_valleys.py} and its frozen execution
receipt. It compared literal polynomial iteration with the
independently specified valley weights in eleven prime-field
cases, for 77,974 polynomial pairs. Heights were exact rational
numbers, with escape certified by strict degree domination;
all tested distributions agreed. The test varies coefficients,
degree and determinant but does not establish the prime-power
or infinite-parameter theorem. Those quantifiers, the combined
residue and the analytic continuation are proved in the article.
The script was not rerun merely to produce this manuscript.

Our domain restriction is substantive. The result does not
count all rational-function points, treat nonconstant coefficients,
or assert a height distribution over an arbitrary constant field.
In particular, it does not classify the nonconstant-parameter
periodic loci of a different H\'enon family. Source version and
access limits are retained in the accompanying citation audit;
an independent internal review is not human journal peer review
or a worldwide novelty certificate.

Finally, $Z_H$ is a height Dirichlet series. Its pole divisor
and mean counting exponent are not a target zero-counting
correspondence. No target Euler factors, root numbers,
automorphy, arithmetic divisor matching or Hilbert--P\'olya
realization follow from these source results.
