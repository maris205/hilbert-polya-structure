\section{Sector formulas and meromorphic convergence}\label{sec:series}
The positive census \eqref{eq:positive-series} becomes a normally
convergent sum of rational sectors after two elementary summations.
First define the open-cone series
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cone}
 T_d(A,B)=\sum_{\substack{m,n\ge1\\m<dn,\ n<dm}}A^mB^n.
\end{equation}
Subtracting the two disjoint closed tails from the positive quadrant
gives, first on its domain of absolute convergence,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cone-subtract}
 T_d(A,B)=\frac{AB}{(1-A)(1-B)}
 -\frac{A^dB}{(1-A)(1-A^dB)}
 -\frac{AB^d}{(1-B)(1-AB^d)}.
\end{equation}
The tails are $m\ge dn$ and $n\ge dm$; they cannot intersect
for positive $m,n$ and $d\ge2$.

The factors $1-A$ and $1-B$ in this expression do not by
themselves give cone poles. To make the cancellation explicit,
let $\mathcal P_d$ be the finite set of lattice points in
\[
 \{\alpha(d,1)+\beta(1,d):0<\alpha\le1,\ 0<\beta\le1\}.
\]
A point in the strict cone has unique positive real coordinates
along the independent vectors $(d,1),(1,d)$. Subtract the ceilings
of these coordinates minus one. The remainder is still a lattice
point and lies in $\mathcal P_d$, while the subtracted coefficients
are nonnegative integers. This representation is unique. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cone-fundamental}
 T_d(A,B)=
 \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{(r,t)\in\mathcal P_d}A^rB^t}
 {(1-A^dB)(1-AB^d)}.
\end{equation}
Both coordinates of every point of $\mathcal P_d$ are positive.
Formula \eqref{eq:cone-fundamental} proves that the apparent
axial singularities in \eqref{eq:cone-subtract} are removable
away from the two actual cone denominators.

Second, summing the residue classes of $M$ in
\eqref{eq:turn-weights} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:turn-generating}
 D_{q,d}(z)=\sum_{M\ge1}D_Mz^M
 =\frac{(q-1)^2q^{d+1}z^d+
 (q-1)q\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{d-1}(qz)^r}
 {1-q^{d+1}z^d}.
\end{equation}
For $k\in\Z$ put
\begin{align}\label{eq:sectors}
 A_k&=q^{1-sd^{-k}},& B_k&=q^{1-sd^k},&
 C_k(s)&=(q-1)^2T_d(A_k,B_k),\notag\\
 z_k&=q^{-s(d^{k-1}+d^{-k})},&&&
 E_k(s)&=D_{q,d}(z_k).
\end{align}
The symmetries $C_{-k}=C_k$ and $E_{1-k}=E_k$ turn
\eqref{eq:positive-series} into
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sector-sum}
 Z_H(s)=q^2+C_0(s)+2\sum_{k\ge1}C_k(s)
                    +2\sum_{k\ge1}E_k(s).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Convergence and candidate poles]\label{prop:convergence}
The positive series converges absolutely exactly for $\Re s>1$.
Formula \eqref{eq:sector-sum} continues it meromorphically to
$\Re s>0$, with no possible poles except \eqref{eq:pole-lattice}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With $L_0=L_1=d+1$, the denominators in $C_k$ are
\[
 1-q^{d+1-sL_k},\qquad 1-q^{d+1-sL_{k+1}},
\]
and the denominator of $E_k$ is the first of these. The sequence
$L_k$ strictly increases for integer $k\ge1$. Thus all sector
power series are absolutely convergent when $\Re s>1$.

On a compact subset of $\Re s\ge\varepsilon>0$, as $k\to\infty$
the quantities $A_k$ are bounded,
$B_k=O(q^{-\varepsilon d^k})$ and
$z_k=O(q^{-\varepsilon d^{k-1}})$. The numerator of
\eqref{eq:cone-fundamental} has positive $B$ exponents, while
both denominators tend uniformly to one. Its tail and the tail
of \eqref{eq:turn-generating} are consequently uniformly summable.
This proves absolute convergence of the sector sum in
$\Re s>1$ and locally normal convergence of its holomorphic
tail in $\Re s>0$. Only finitely many meromorphic sectors
remain on any compact subset, giving the stated continuation
and the complete list of candidate poles.

For the converse on absolute convergence, put $j=m+n$.
The number of positive pairs of sum $j$ in the strict cone is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:b-j}
 b_j=j-2\lfloor j/(d+1)\rfloor-1
     =\frac{d-1}{d+1}j+O_d(1),\qquad j\ge1.
\end{equation}
Indeed the strict bounds on $m$ are
$j/(d+1)<m<dj/(d+1)$. The central edge terms alone are
$(q-1)^2\sum_{j\ge1}b_j q^{(1-s)j}$, which diverge for
real $s\le1$. At complex $s$, absolute values replace $s$
by $\Re s$. This proves the exact abscissa.
\end{proof}
