\section{Uniform graphs for large diameter}
\label{sec:large}

\begin{lemma}[The five endpoint alphabets]
\label{lem:alphabet}
Suppose $D\ge10$ and $E'\not\subseteq\{0,D,r\}$.
After the reflection $t\mapsto D-t$ if necessary,
$r\in\{-1,0,1,2,3\}$ and
$E'\subseteq S_r=T_r\cup\{D\}$, where the sets $T_r$ are
listed in Table~\ref{tab:large}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For an extra symbol $t\notin\{0,D,r\}$, integrality gives
$|t-r|\ge1$. If $3\le t\le D-3$, then
\[
 |t(t-D)(t-r)|\ge t(D-t)\ge3(D-3)>2D,
\]
contrary to \eqref{eq:secant-bound}. Thus the only possible extra
symbols are $1,2,D-2,D-1$. A lower extra at $1$ or $2$ satisfies
respectively
\[
 |1-r|\le\left\lfloor\frac{2D}{D-1}\right\rfloor=2,
 \qquad
 |2-r|\le\left\lfloor\frac{D}{D-2}\right\rfloor=1.
\]
Their union forces $r\in\{-1,0,1,2,3\}$. Upper extras force
$D-r$ to lie in the same set. These two ranges are disjoint for
$D\ge10$.

Reflection in both coordinates conjugates $H_g$ to $H_{\widetilde g}$,
where a direct expansion yields
\begin{align*}
 \widetilde g(z)&=2D-g(D-z)
   =\widetilde A+qz+z(z-D)(z-\widetilde r),\\
 \widetilde r&=D-r,\qquad \widetilde A=(2-q)D-A.
\end{align*}
It preserves the monic integral family, the ordinary clock and all
point counts. We may therefore use the lower range. Retaining its
roots inside $[0,D]$ and its allowed extras gives
$T_{-1}=T_0=\{0,1\}$,
$T_1=T_2=\{0,1,2\}$ and $T_3=\{0,1,2,3\}$.
If neither endpoint range occurs, there were no extra symbols and
Lemma~\ref{lem:affine} already applies.
\end{proof}

Write $T_r=\{0,\ldots,m_r\}$, so $m_r$ is $1$, $2$ or $3$.
For $D\ge16$, every sum of two symbols in $S_r$ has a unique
representation $kD+s$. Its coefficient pair belongs to
\begin{equation}
 \Sigma_r=\{(0,s):0\le s\le2m_r\}
 \cup\{(1,s):0\le s\le m_r\}\cup\{(2,0)\}.
 \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
Since $0,D$ both occur in the actual coordinate set, the endpoint
images are sums of two symbols. Thus
\begin{equation}
 A=kD+s,\qquad (k,s)\in\Sigma_r,\qquad
 (k+q,s)\in\Sigma_r.
 \label{eq:symbolic-cases}
\end{equation}
To justify the last expression, representations with constant terms
between $0$ and $2m_r\le6$ cannot differ by a nonzero multiple of
$D$. The same no-cancellation argument will justify every graph edge.

\begin{lemma}[No cancellation between the two coefficients]
\label{lem:no-carry}
For each case in \eqref{eq:symbolic-cases} and every $D\ge16$,
the partial graph of $H_g$ on $S_r^2$ is independent of $D$ when
symbols are represented by their coefficient pairs in $\Z[D]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $t\in T_r$, expansion of \eqref{eq:secant} gives
\begin{equation}
 g(t)=[k-t(t-r)]D+[s+qt+t^2(t-r)],
 \label{eq:symbolic-value}
\end{equation}
whereas $g(D)=(k+q)D+s$. An edge $(x,t)\mapsto(t,z)$ is
present precisely when $g(t)=x+z$. The constant coefficient of
$x+z$ belongs to $[0,2m_r]$. Allowing
$s,u\in[0,2m_r]$, $q\in[-2,2]$ and $t\in T_r$, the absolute
constant discrepancy
\[
 |s+qt+t^2(t-r)-u|
\]
is at most $6,5,12,8,14$ for $r=-1,0,1,2,3$, respectively.
These bounds follow by substituting the two, three or four listed
values of $t$ and the endpoints of the ranges for $s,u,q$.
For $t=D$ the bound is at most six. Every bound is less than $D$.
If the coefficient of $D$ in an edge discrepancy were a nonzero
integer, its magnitude at this $D$ would be at least $D$, which
the constant discrepancy cannot cancel. Hence numeric equality is
equivalent to equality of both coefficients, as claimed.
\end{proof}

This lemma concerns literal affine polynomials, not empirical stability
at one chosen diameter. It leaves only ninety finite graphs. Indeed,
for $s=0$ there are nine pairs of allowed values of $k$ and $k+q$;
for each $1\le s\le m_r$ there are four; and for each
$m_r<s\le2m_r$ there is one. The count is $9+5m_r$, giving the
Cases column of Table~\ref{tab:large}.

\begin{proposition}[The complete large graph computation]
\label{prop:large-computation}
The graphs in \eqref{eq:symbolic-cases} have the data in
Table~\ref{tab:large}. Every cycle uses at most three symbols and
has least period in $\{1,2,3,4\}$. Each graph has at most eight
periodic vertices. Including the affine-root cases, every map whose
global diameter is at least sixteen has at most nine periodic points.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the coefficient pairs $(0,t)$ for $t\in T_r$ and $(1,0)$ for
$D$. Enumerate \eqref{eq:symbolic-cases}, evaluate
\eqref{eq:symbolic-value}, and extract all cycles of
$(x,y)\mapsto(y,g(y)-x)$ by coefficientwise arithmetic.
Appendix~\ref{app:certificate} specifies every loop and the complete
cycle-extraction algorithm, and proves its correctness.
Its exact evaluation gives Table~\ref{tab:large}.

For a compact explicit enumeration of every endpoint-using graph,
Table~\ref{tab:large-patterns} lists all its cycles. There are five
baseline cases for each of the five $r$ values, together with four
additional cases, giving $25+4=29$ endpoint-using cases. Direct
substitution into \eqref{eq:secant} verifies their displayed words;
the full graph procedure also excludes any omitted cycles. Cases
without both ends are included in the first table and its algorithm.
Lemma~\ref{lem:alphabet} covers every non-affine actual map, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:no-carry} identifies its graph uniformly in $D$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:affine} supplies the remaining nine-point bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Four global symbols coexist]
\label{rem:four-symbols}
For any integer $D\ge16$, the polynomial
\[
 g_D(t)=2+t(t-D)(t-2)
\]
has the disjoint cycles $(1,1,D)$ and $(0,0,2,2)$, of least
periods three and four. Indeed $g_D(0)=g_D(2)=2$ and
$g_D(1)=D+1$, $g_D(D)=2$. Their seven points already use the
four distinct values $0,1,2,D$. Thus the full coordinate set need
not have at most three elements. No assertion about the absence of
additional cycles is needed for this counterexample.
\end{remark}

The endpoint condition in the graph tables is only a necessary
filter for a global-extrema tuple. It does not certify that a
polynomial has no cycles outside the displayed interval. For example,
the baseline $r=-1,A=0,q=2$ also has the fixed coordinate $-1$,
which lies outside its chosen graph. When the tables are applied to
our original map, this issue cannot hide a cycle: $0,D$ were chosen
from the entire finite periodic set in \eqref{eq:normalization}.
