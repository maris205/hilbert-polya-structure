\section{Reproducibility and limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} closes the full monic integral conservative
cubic question in ordinary rational dynamics. The integer third
secant root controls every coexisting cycle in one common normalized
graph. The finite complement then singles out one normalized
eleven-point graph, and short-word interpolation translates the
graph restrictions into exact coefficient identities.

The finite certificate is an explicit integer computation after a
proved reduction, not a proof-assistant formalization. The retained
producer evaluates the ninety symbolic graphs and the 9,373 bounded
small-diameter tuples using path traversal. Before manuscript drafting,
a separate internal implementation reconstructed the small computation
on the full square $\{0,\ldots,D\}^2$, without the secant-symbol filter,
and isolated the periodic vertices by iterating a partial injection
with repeated squaring. Its large-graph arithmetic represented affine
polynomials by their values at zero and one; equality of these two
values is exact because the expressions were already proved affine.
That independent reconstruction agreed with all table entries and the
unique maximizing tuple. The present typeset specification is complete
without either implementation; Appendix~\ref{app:certificate}
states the calculation and its correctness argument. This internal
verification is not described as human peer review.

Neither the threshold sixteen nor the number of finite cases is
claimed to be minimal. Those choices give a short no-cancellation
proof and an explicitly bounded complement. A purely hand-classified
replacement for the small graph table could change the form of the
proof, but is not assumed here. The source comparisons remain bounded
by the accessed primary records and the named repository predecessor.

The restrictions on coefficients and determinant are essential to
the stated theorem. No extension to all rational-coefficient cubics,
other degrees, number fields or positive characteristic is proved.
Finally, the finite ordinary zeta is a source-dynamical invariant.
It does not establish rational-prime primitive ownership, target
Euler factors, root numbers, automorphy, a target zero or divisor
correspondence, or a Hilbert--P\'olya operator.
