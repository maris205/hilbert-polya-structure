\section{The full cubic classification}
\label{sec:introduction}

The rational periodic points of an individual polynomial automorphism
can be finite even when a direct search depends strongly on its
coefficients. For the full monic integral conservative cubic family,
that dependence can be removed: every cycle has one of seven explicit
forms, and the largest possible union of cycles has eleven points.
The sharp bound concerns all cycles of the same map, not merely a
bound on their individual lengths.

Throughout the paper, fix
\begin{equation}
 f(t)=t^3+bt^2+ct+a,\qquad a,b,c\in\Z,\qquad
 H_f(x,y)=(y,f(y)-x).
 \label{eq:family}
\end{equation}
Its Jacobian determinant is $+1$ and its inverse is
$(x,y)\mapsto(f(x)-y,x)$. The domain is all of $\Q^2$.
Let $\cP_f=\Per(H_f,\Q^2)$ be the union of its periodic orbits.
All periods below are least periods for ordinary iteration by $H_f$.
An orbit with coordinate word $(v_0,\ldots,v_{n-1})$ consists of
the points $(v_i,v_{i+1})$, with indices taken modulo $n$.
The word is identified under cyclic rotation, never under reversal.
Its recurrence is
\begin{equation}
 v_{i-1}+v_{i+1}=f(v_i).
 \label{eq:recurrence}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[All coefficients, all rational periodic points]
\label{thm:main}
For every polynomial in \eqref{eq:family}, the following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Every rational periodic point is integral. Each cycle uses at
most three distinct coordinate values, and all its possibilities are
the seven rows of Table~\ref{tab:templates}.
\item Every least period belongs to $\{1,2,3,4,6\}$; each of these
five periods occurs for a member of the family.
\item The entire periodic set satisfies $\#\cP_f\le11$. Equality
holds if and only if there is $h\in\Z$ such that
\begin{equation}
 f(t)=(t-h)^3-5(t-h)+2h.
 \label{eq:equality-family}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

In the template table, the parameters are integers and every displayed
condition is an identity of polynomials, except the fixed-point
condition $f(u)=2u$. For compactness set
\begin{equation}
 R_{u,v}(t)=(t-u)(t-v)(t+u+v+b).
 \label{eq:Ruv}
\end{equation}
The $b$ in this expression is the original quadratic coefficient of
$f$. To obtain the cycles of one given polynomial, take the union of
all rows whose conditions hold. For the three-distinct-symbol
three-cycle, the identity forces $b=-(u+v+w)$; it leaves no extra
quadratic coefficient free.

The proof has three parts. First, integrality and a real escape bound
make the whole periodic set finite. Its two global coordinate extrema
then give an integral secant slope and an integral third root of the
cubic remainder. Second, the secant inequality forces either an affine
recurrence on three roots or one of finitely many endpoint graphs.
For large diameters these are exact graphs over $\Z[D]$, not graphs
sampled at one large integer. The remaining diameters have proved
finite parameter ranges. Third, short-word interpolation gives all
templates, and the unique maximizing graph gives
\eqref{eq:equality-family}.

The finite arithmetic tables are part of the proof. Their complete
enumeration is specified in Appendix~\ref{app:certificate}; every
input range is justified before it is used. They involve no floating-point
decision, guessed period cutoff, or bounded scan through the
original coefficients. The manuscript separates the analytic
reduction from the exact finite computation.
