\section{Arithmetic context and the scope of the increment}
\label{sec:sources}

General restrictions on integral cycles precede this classification.
Pezda studies polynomial mappings of $\Z^2$ and gives, in
Theorem~2.1, the cycle-length list
\[
 \{1,2,3,4,6,8,9,12,16,18,24\};
\]
see \citet{pezda2002cycles}. That is a restriction on cycle length,
not the sharp number of periodic points in the particular cubic
family \eqref{eq:family}. We do not claim the existence of a
uniform integral period bound as new. Nor do we use the displayed
list as a substitute for the cubic graph argument: the latter proves
the smaller period set and the coexistence bound directly.

Canonical heights provide a wider arithmetic setting for polynomial
automorphisms. Ingram develops height and specialization results for
H\'enon maps and explicitly discusses earlier boundedness work of
Pezda \citep{ingram2014canonical}. Our use of local maxima is a direct
elementary argument in a restricted monic integral family, not a new
general height theorem. The published bibliographic record is cited;
the text used for the comparison is the introduction and opening
local-height material of arXiv:1111.3609v1. In particular, the quadratic
conjecture for $(y,x+y^2+b)$ discussed there has determinant $-1$.
It is not the family studied here. No assertion about that conjecture
follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

The arithmetic questions collected in Section~11 of
\citet{dehenon2024problems} distinguish general rational-coefficient
uniform boundedness, interpolation constructions and special
determinants. Those questions are broader than the present all-integral
cubic classification. Elementary interpolation can produce
selected short orbits; it does not on its own exclude additional
orbits elsewhere. The main issue here is precisely that exclusion
and the resulting coexistence theorem. We therefore deduct word
interpolation as a method, while proving its exact seven-template
consequence under the global restrictions established below.

Rational coefficients cannot be silently substituted for integral
coefficients. The rational-coefficient, integer-valued polynomials
of \citet{kim2025many} have many periodic points as their odd degrees
grow. Their discrete-sine cubic is
\[
 s_3(t)=\frac{t^3-7t}{6},
\]
which is neither monic nor in $\Z[t]$. We use the explicitly dated
second preprint version for this comparison, not an asserted later
journal text. Sharing determinant $+1$ does not make that coefficient
family equal to \eqref{eq:family}.

There is also a nearby repository predecessor: the anonymous C412
manuscript classifies monic integral conservative quadratic H\'enon
maps and obtains a sharp eight-point bound
\citep{c412repository2026}. It is a repository manuscript, not a
journal publication. Its monic integrality argument, coordinate-word
encoding and finite-complement strategy are not new contributions of
this paper. In the cubic problem, the third secant root creates
additional branches and allows different three-symbol cycles to
coexist. The residual result is the complete closure of that cubic
coexistence problem, including every original coefficient triple and
the entire eleven-point equality locus.
