           Every rational cycle of the discrete-sine Hénon family


                                                    Abstract
          We classify every rational periodic point of the conservative Hénon map hd (x, y) = (y, −x +
      sd (y)), where sd is the integer-valued discrete-sine polynomial of any odd degree d ≥ 3. The result
      gives the exact multiplicity of every ordinary primitive cycle, including both degree-dependent
      central phases and the unique growing boundary cycle when d ≡ 1 (mod 6). The total number of
      rational periodic points is (3d2 −4d+152)/3, d2 +6d+24, or d2 +8d/3+14, according as d ≡ 5, 1, 3
      (mod 6). A nonarchimedean argument first proves integrality; a second-difference estimate then
      gives a uniform escape box. Inside its core, affine residue cells yield exact clipped multiplicities.
      Outside the core, a signed first-return graph with a complete endpoint complement provides the
      exhaustion. All formulas include the least admissible degrees. The resulting finite-cycle zeta is
      an immediate corollary of the ordinary cycle classification.

2    Prior work and the scope of the increment
Kim, Krieger, Postolache and Szeto introduced the Hénon family used here, building on integer-valued
polynomial constructions. Their Corollary 4.3 gives the rational/integer escape box, Theorem 5.1
gives the growing cycle, and Proposition 5.2 classifies the entire positive-phase lattice map, including
all 17 central exceptional points [2]. These results, the polynomial family itself, and the existence

                                                          2
of many rational periodic points are prior work. We rederive the needed identities to make the
argument complete.
    Our increment is the exact all-radius clipping of that lattice classification together with the
exhaustive signed boundary graph. This determines every multiplicity and excludes every additional
rational cycle at every odd degree, including the first radius in each congruence class. The long-cycle
argument below supplies its place and uniqueness in this graph, not a new claim of existence.
    The comparison is specifically with arXiv:2412.01668v2. In that text, Theorem 4.4 gives at
least 225 points for d = 13, while a subsequent exact-count remark gives 153. Formula (1.3) gives
271. We do not extend this observation to an unexamined final journal version or infer worldwide
priority from a bounded source comparison. The arXiv version header is dated 8 July 2025; the
served HTML separately displays an internal date of 24 August 2026. These are distinct metadata
fields, not a reason to conflate versions [2].
    Ingram’s arithmetic study provides related nonarchimedean denominator mechanisms and
rational-periodicity questions [1]. In particular, the computations in its Section 7 concern (y, x + y 2 +
b), whose sign differs from the conservative map in (1.1). We do not transfer a cycle classification
across that sign change. The present theorem concerns only the exact factorial-product family (1.1),
not shifted polynomials, all integer-valued polynomials, or arbitrary degree-d Hénon maps.

6    A signed first-return section with all endpoints
Every periodic orbit not wholly in B0 has a coordinate of absolute value R + 1 or R + 2, by
Proposition 3.3. Hence it meets the section

                                A = {(x, y) ∈ B : |y| ∈ {R + 1, R + 2}}.                              (6.1)

The map hd commutes with S. For a pair of opposite section points, use the unique representative
with positive second coordinate:

                                   [x, λ] = (x, R + λ),      λ ∈ {1, 2}.

This notation is only a proof device. Signs on the return edges retain the ordinary, nonquotiented
dynamics.

Lemma 6.1 (Ordinary lift of a signed return cycle). Let a primitive normalized section cycle have
total time T and edge-sign product ε. If ε = +1, it lifts to two distinct primitive hd -cycles of length
T . If ε = −1, it lifts to one primitive hd -cycle of length 2T .

Proof. Following the return edges once sends a representative z to εz. Since A has no point fixed
by S, a negative sign requires two traversals to return to z, whereas a positive sign requires one. An
earlier ordinary return would imply an earlier repeat of the normalized section point, contradicting
its primitive return cycle and the first-return property. In the positive case the lifts through z and
−z cannot coincide: such a coincidence would give a negative return to the same normalized starting
point before the positive traversal closed. Thus the stated periods and cycle counts are ordinary
ones.

8     Exhaustiveness, least radii and the return law
Completion of the proof of Theorem 1.1. Proposition 3.3 places every rational periodic orbit in B.
If it avoids A, it lies wholly in B0 . Indeed, a point outside B0 has a coordinate of magnitude R + 1
or R + 2, and that coordinate becomes a second coordinate on the same periodic orbit. The core
orbit is therefore exactly one of those in Propositions 4.1 and 5.1. Conversely, every clipped core
orbit agrees with hd at every step and is an actual periodic orbit of that map.
     If a periodic orbit meets A, its normalized section points follow the first-return rules of Section 6.
The inner section is the disjoint union of the generic residue strips and the endpoint complement
(6.4). Every other outer point escapes in one step. The three cases in Section 7 assign every possible
start to a primitive normalized cycle or an escape path. Lifting by Lemma 6.1 therefore gives every
remaining ordinary cycle exactly once. The explicit recurrence reconstructs all points between its
section nodes. This proves the classification and all three additive tables.
