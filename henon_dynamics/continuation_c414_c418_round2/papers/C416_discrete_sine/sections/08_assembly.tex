\section{Exhaustiveness, least radii and the return law}\label{sec:assembly}

\begin{proof}[Completion of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Proposition~\ref{prop:integer-box} places every rational periodic
orbit in $B$. If it avoids $A$, it lies wholly in $B_0$.
Indeed, a point outside $B_0$ has a coordinate of magnitude $R+1$
or $R+2$, and that coordinate becomes a second coordinate on the
same periodic orbit. The core orbit is therefore exactly one of
those in Propositions~\ref{prop:core} and~\ref{prop:phase}.
Conversely, every clipped core orbit agrees with $h_d$ at every
step and is an actual periodic orbit of that map.

If a periodic orbit meets $A$, its normalized section points
follow the first-return rules of Section~\ref{sec:boundary}.
The inner section is the disjoint union of the generic residue
strips and the endpoint complement \eqref{eq:complement}.
Every other outer point escapes in one step. The three cases in
Section~\ref{sec:closure} assign every possible start to a
primitive normalized cycle or an escape path. Lifting by
Lemma~\ref{lem:lift} therefore gives every remaining ordinary
cycle exactly once. The explicit recurrence reconstructs all
points between its section nodes. This proves the classification
and all three additive tables.
\end{proof}

All multiplicities in the tables are nonnegative on the allowed
ranges. The growing period has $q\ge1$, hence is at least $22$;
it never coincides with $36$ because $16q+6=36$ has no integer
solution. It also differs from every other listed period. The
repeated appearances of periods $4$ and $6$ in distinct tables
represent disjoint core and boundary cycles and must be added.
In particular, the three least radii give the following direct
substitutions into the uniform formulas.

These are not separate numerical degree-graph computations. The
empty $8$-cycle family at $R=2$ and empty $36$-cycle family at
$R=4$ were already treated as empty arithmetic progressions.
All affine inequalities and endpoint noncoincidences were proved
at the least admissible radius and throughout its progression.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:counts}]
There are always $17$ central points. Multiplying the generic
cycle counts by their lengths gives the respective bulk totals
\[
 36q^2-28q,\qquad 36q^2-4q,\qquad 36q^2+36q.
\]
Similarly, the boundary totals for $s=0,1,2$ are
\[
 8q+36,\qquad 52q+14,\qquad 16q+14.
\]
For example, in the middle case the second total is
$2\cdot6+2\cdot16+36(q-1)+(16q+6)=52q+14$.
Adding the central $17$ gives \eqref{eq:total}; substituting
$d=2R-1$ gives its degree forms.

An ordinary primitive $\ell$-cycle contributes all its $\ell$
points to $\Fix(h_d^n,\Q)$ exactly when $\ell\mid n$.
This proves the formula for $N_n(d)$. Consequently
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{N_n(d)}n u^n
 &=\sum_{\ell\ge1} C_\ell(d)\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{u^{\ell j}}j\\
 &=-\sum_{\ell\ge1}C_\ell(d)\log(1-u^\ell).
\end{align*}
There are only finitely many nonzero $C_\ell(d)$, so this is
valid formally and for $|u|<1$. Exponentiation proves
\eqref{eq:zeta}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Boundaries of the result.}
The proof classifies all of $\Q^2$ for the exact family
\eqref{eq:family} and its ordinary clock. It does not classify
nonperiodic orbits, complex periodic points, shifted families or
arbitrary rational H\'enon maps. The rational function $Z_d$
records a finite source cycle permutation. It does not establish
target Euler factors, a root number, automorphy, a target
zero-divisor correspondence or a Hilbert--P\'olya realization.
The three radius classes are cases of one family theorem, and
the zeta identity is its corollary, not a separate classification.
