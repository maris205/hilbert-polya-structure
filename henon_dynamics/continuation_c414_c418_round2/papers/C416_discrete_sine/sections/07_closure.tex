\section{Closing and exhausting the boundary graph}\label{sec:closure}

We now use the return rules to assign every normalized section state
to a cycle or an escape path. In the strings below, $[x,1]$ is
occasionally abbreviated by its coordinate $x$ when the layer is
unambiguous. Ordinary cycle multiplicities always come from
Lemma~\ref{lem:lift}, not from counting normalized cycles.

\subsection{Radius \texorpdfstring{\(R=3q\)}{R=3q}}

The $e=0,3$ strip consists precisely of the multiples of $3$
in $[-R,R]$. There are $2q+1$ such coordinates. Each is a
normalized fixed point of time $2$ and negative sign, hence gives
one ordinary $4$-cycle.

The endpoint cycle
\[
 L_{-1}\ \xto{1,+}\ U_1\ \xto{1,-}\ L_{-1}
\]
has total time $2$ and negative sign, giving one more $4$-cycle.
The other periodic endpoint string is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourteen}
 L_2\ \xto{9,+}\ U_{-2}\ \xto{2,-}\ V_{-2}
 \ \xto{1,+}\ V_2\ \xto{2,-}\ L_2.
\end{equation}
The $U_{-2}$ edge is the $e=4$ strip rule. Its total time is
$14$ and its sign is positive, giving two $14$-cycles.
The normalized nodes in these cycles are distinct: the inner
endpoints are separated by Lemma~\ref{lem:noalias}, the outer
layer is different, and its two offsets differ by $4$.

To prove exhaustion, consider each other strip residue. A generic
$e=1$ state first goes to $e=5$ by $x\mapsto x-2$, then to
the outer layer. Its outer coordinate is at most $R-7<R-2$.
A generic $e=2$ state goes to $e=4$ by $x\mapsto x-4$,
then to an outer coordinate at most $R-8<R-2$.
Both paths escape by \eqref{eq:outer-threshold}.
Of the generic $e=4,5$ states that go directly to the outer
layer, only $U_{-2}$ and $U_{-1}$ reach an outer coordinate
at least $R-2$. The first is in \eqref{eq:fourteen}, while
the second follows
\[
 U_{-1}\longrightarrow V_{-1}\longrightarrow U_2\longrightarrow E.
\]
The unused left endpoint path is
$L_{-2}\to V_1\to L_1\to U_{-1}$, and $V_0$ escapes.
This accounts for every start in Tables~\ref{tab:strips}
and~\ref{tab:endpoints}. Thus the boundary multiplicities are
\[
 4:2q+2,\qquad 14:2.
\]

\subsection{Radius \texorpdfstring{\(R=3q+1\)}{R=3q+1}}

The stationary $e=1$ strip contains exactly $q-1$ coordinates.
They are congruent to $R$ modulo $6$ and run from $-R+8$ to
$R-6$ in increments of $6$. At $q=1$ there is no such
coordinate. Each stationary state has return time $18$ and
negative sign, giving one ordinary $36$-cycle.

The two normalized cycles
\[
 L_0\ \xto{1,+}\ V_1\ \xto{2,-}\ L_0,
 \qquad
 V_0\ \xto{1,+}\ U_2\ \xto{2,-}\ V_0
\]
each have total time $3$ and negative sign, and give two ordinary
$6$-cycles in total. The $U_2$ edge here is a generic strip edge,
not an omitted exceptional start. The endpoint cycle
\[
 L_2\ \xto{3,-}\ U_0\ \xto{13,-}\ L_2
\]
has time $16$ and positive sign, hence gives two $16$-cycles.
All these nodes are separated by their layer, offsets and
Lemma~\ref{lem:noalias}.

\begin{proposition}[The full growing-cycle string]\label{prop:growing}
For $R=3q+1$, there is exactly one ordinary boundary cycle of
length $16q+6$. Its normalized section cycle consists of the
initial edge $L_1\xto{1,+}U_1$ and the interleaved strings
defined below. No generic $e=0$ or $e=2$ state lies outside
this cycle.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $0\le j\le q$, put
\[
 a_j=R+1-6j.
\]
These are exactly the generic $e=2$ coordinates, with
$a_0=R+1$ and $a_q=-R+3$. Their time-$2$ negative edges
go to $a_j-2$. For $j<q$, this is a generic $e=0$
coordinate; its time-$6$ negative edge goes to $a_{j+1}$.
For $j=q$, the target is $a_q-2=-R+1$, namely $L_1$.
Thus the normalized cycle is
\begin{align*}
 L_1&\xto{1,+}[a_0,1]\xto{2,-}[a_0-2,1]
       \xto{6,-}[a_1,1]\xto{2,-}\cdots\\
    &\hspace{25mm}\cdots\xto{6,-}[a_q,1]
       \xto{2,-}L_1.
\end{align*}
It has $q+1$ time-$2$ edges, $q$ time-$6$ edges, and
one time-$1$ edge. Its total time and sign are
\[
 T=1+2(q+1)+6q=8q+3,\qquad
 \eps=(-1)^{2q+1}=-1.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:lift} gives one ordinary cycle of length $2T=16q+6$.
The strict decrease by $6$ prevents repetition within either
string; their different residues separate the two strings.
The endpoint $L_1$ is outside the generic strip by
\eqref{eq:complement}. This proves primitivity.

Finally, the entire generic $e=0$ progression runs from
$-R+7$ to $R-1$, and the entire generic $e=2$ progression
runs from $-R+3$ to $R+1$, each in steps of $6$.
They are precisely the nodes just listed. This proves the
asserted coverage; exhaustion of the other residues below
then proves uniqueness in the full graph.
\end{proof}

Every generic $e=5$ state goes to $e=3$, then to an outer
coordinate at most $R-6<R-1$, and escapes. Among generic
$e=3,4$ states that go directly to the outer layer, only
$U_2$ reaches an outer coordinate at least $R-1$; it was
already assigned to the second $6$-cycle. The remaining endpoint
path is $V_{-1}\to V_2\to E$, and $L_{-2},L_{-1}$ escape
immediately. The stationary strip, the growing string, these
escape paths, and the displayed endpoint cycles exhaust every
section state. The boundary multiplicities are therefore
\[
 6:2,\qquad 16:2,\qquad 36:q-1,\qquad (16q+6):1.
\]

\subsection{Radius \texorpdfstring{\(R=3q+2\)}{R=3q+2}}

For a generic $e=3$ coordinate $x$, the target $x+1$ is
generic of residue $e=4$ and returns to $x$. The two edges
have times $6,2$ and signs $-,-$. Hence each pair gives
two ordinary $8$-cycles. The allowed $e=3$ coordinates are
\[
 -R+5,\ -R+11,\ \ldots,\ R-5,
\]
giving $q$ pairs; at $q=0$ the list is empty. Their partners
account for all generic $e=4$ coordinates. The residues are
distinct, so these normalized cycles are primitive.

The endpoint cycles are
\[
 L_{-1}\ \xto{1,+}\ V_1\ \xto{1,+}\ U_2\
 \xto{1,-}\ L_{-1},
 \qquad
 L_0\ \xto{1,+}\ U_1\ \xto{3,+}\ L_0.
\]
Their total times and signs are $(3,-)$ and $(4,+)$,
giving one ordinary $6$-cycle and two ordinary $4$-cycles.
Their nodes are distinct even at $R=2$, by
Lemma~\ref{lem:noalias} and their different layers.

For exhaustion, a generic $e=1$ state goes to $e=0$ and
then to an outer coordinate at most $R-3<R$. A generic
$e=2$ state goes to $e=5$ and then to an outer coordinate
at most $R-4<R$. Direct outer returns from generic $e=0,5$
also have outer coordinate below $R$. All these paths escape
by \eqref{eq:outer-threshold}. The unused endpoint paths are
\[
 U_0\longrightarrow L_1\longrightarrow E,\qquad
 V_0\longrightarrow V_2\longrightarrow E,\qquad
 L_{-2}\longrightarrow E.
\]
Here $L_1$ is a generic $e=5$ state; the arrow from it to $E$
allows its transient outer return before escape. Every section
state is assigned, yielding precisely
\[
 4:2,\qquad 6:1,\qquad 8:2q.
\]
