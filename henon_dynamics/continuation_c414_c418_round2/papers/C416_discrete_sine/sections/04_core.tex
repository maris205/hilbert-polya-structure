\section{Clipped affine cells in the core}\label{sec:core}

Let
\[
 B_0=[-R,R]^2\cap\Z^2,\qquad
 g_p(x,y)=(y,-x+p\sine(y)),\qquad g=g_+.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:values}, $h_d$ and $g_p$ agree as long as their
second coordinate remains in $[-R,R]$. We first study $g$ on the
whole lattice and then identify exactly which of its orbits stay in
$B_0$. This separates periodicity from the radius-dependent clipping.

\begin{proposition}[Exact positive-phase cells]\label{prop:core}
Fix $(e,f)\in\{0,\ldots,5\}^2$. The corresponding row of
Table~\ref{tab:affine} gives a generic period
$\ell\in\{4,12,20\}$, four offsets, and all points of smaller period
in that residue cell. The entire $g$-orbit of $(x,y)\equiv(e,f)\pmod6$
lies in $B_0$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rectangle}
 -R+L_x\le x\le R+U_x,\qquad
 -R+L_y\le y\le R+U_y.
\end{equation}
Except at the indicated exceptional points, its least period is
exactly $\ell$. The central exceptions form the origin, two
$5$-cycles and one $6$-cycle.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Within a fixed residue cell, write each coordinate as an affine
expression in the free variables $x,y$. The residue of $ax+by+c$ is
$ae+bf+c\bmod6$, so the exact affine iteration is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine}
 (X,Y)\longmapsto\bigl(Y,-X+\sine(Y\bmod6)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Table~\ref{tab:affine} gives all $36$ resulting identities
$g^\ell(x,y)=(x,y)$. Every intermediate coordinate is of the form
$\pm x+c$ or $\pm y+c$. Intersecting the inequalities
$-R\le\pm x+c\le R$ and their $y$ analogues gives precisely
\eqref{eq:rectangle}. This is an intersection of affine inequalities,
not a test at a chosen radius.

For clarity, the verification of any row consists of the following
finite exact operations:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item Start at the affine pair $(X_0,Y_0)=(x,y)$ and apply
       \eqref{eq:affine} through the listed value of $\ell$.
 \item Check $(X_\ell,Y_\ell)=(x,y)$ as an affine identity and
       intersect all coordinate bounds through time $\ell-1$.
 \item For each proper divisor $t$ of $\ell$, solve
       $X_t=x$, $Y_t=y$. Retain precisely the integral solutions
       with residues $(e,f)$.
\end{enumerate}
Every consistent proper-divisor system in these rows has rank two;
there is no consistent rank-one family to discard. The resulting
solutions are exactly the points in the last column of the table.
These operations, with \eqref{eq:sigma}, also give a direct
reproducible verification of the table without external code.
For example, in cell $(0,0)$ the affine orbit is
$(x,y),(y,-x),(-x,-y),(-y,x)$, giving period $4$, zero clipping
offsets, and only $(0,0)$ as a proper-period point.

In actual point notation the exceptional cycles are the fixed point
$(0,0)$, the cycle
\begin{equation}\label{eq:five-cycle}
 (0,2)\to(2,1)\to(1,-1)\to(-1,-2)\to(-2,0)\to(0,2)
\end{equation}
and its negative, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:six-cycle}
 (0,1)\to(1,1)\to(1,0)\to(0,-1)\to(-1,-1)\to(-1,0)\to(0,1).
\end{equation}
All $17$ points lie in $[-2,2]^2$, and thus occur for every $R\ge2$.
The proper-divisor equations exclude every other exceptional point.
\end{proof}

We next count the clipped rectangles exactly. Put $R=6m+r$ with
$0\le r\le5$. For a single coordinate of residue $e$ and offsets
$(L,U)$, write the coordinate as $e+6j$. The number of allowed
integers is
\begin{align}
 A_{r,e}^{L,U}(m)
 &=\left\lfloor\frac{R+U-e}{6}\right\rfloor
   -\left\lceil\frac{-R+L-e}{6}\right\rceil+1 \notag\\
 &=2m+\left\lfloor\frac{r+U-e}{6}\right\rfloor
   -\left\lceil\frac{-r+L-e}{6}\right\rceil+1.
 \label{eq:ap-count}
\end{align}
For the rows in Table~\ref{tab:affine}, these values are nonnegative
already at the least permitted $m$: it is $1$ for $r=0,1$ and $0$
for the other residues. No positive-part correction is omitted.

Let $\eta_{e,f}$ be $1$ if the last column of that row contains a
point, and $0$ otherwise. All such points satisfy the clipping
inequalities at every $R\ge2$. Thus the number of generic cycles
of a given period $\ell$ is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bulk-sum}
 \frac1\ell
 \sum_{\substack{(e,f)\\\text{row period }\,\ell}}
 \left(A_{r,e}^{L_x,U_x}(m)A_{r,f}^{L_y,U_y}(m)-\eta_{e,f}\right).
\end{equation}
Each generic orbit is counted at each of its $\ell$ points,
possibly in different residue cells; division by $\ell$ is therefore
necessary. Expanding the products gives Table~\ref{tab:bulk-six}.
