\section{Prior work and the scope of the increment}\label{sec:sources}

Kim, Krieger, Postolache and Szeto introduced the H\'enon family used
here, building on integer-valued polynomial constructions. Their
Corollary~4.3 gives the rational/integer escape box, Theorem~5.1 gives
the growing cycle, and Proposition~5.2 classifies the entire
positive-phase lattice map, including all $17$ central exceptional
points \cite{kim_2025_many_v2}. These results, the polynomial family
itself, and the existence of many rational periodic points are prior
work. We rederive the needed identities to make the argument complete.

Our increment is the exact all-radius clipping of that lattice
classification together with the exhaustive signed boundary graph.
This determines every multiplicity and excludes every additional
rational cycle at every odd degree, including the first radius in
each congruence class. The long-cycle argument below supplies its
place and uniqueness in this graph, not a new claim of existence.

The comparison is specifically with arXiv:2412.01668v2. In that text,
Theorem~4.4 gives at least $225$ points for $d=13$, while a subsequent
exact-count remark gives $153$. Formula~\eqref{eq:total} gives $271$.
We do not extend this observation to an unexamined final journal
version or infer worldwide priority from a bounded source comparison.
The arXiv version header is dated 8 July 2025; the served HTML separately
displays an internal date of 24 August 2026. These are distinct metadata
fields, not a reason to conflate versions \cite{kim_2025_many_v2}.

Ingram's arithmetic study provides related nonarchimedean denominator
mechanisms and rational-periodicity questions \cite{ingram_2011_heights}.
In particular, the computations in its Section~7 concern
$(y,x+y^2+b)$, whose sign differs from the conservative map in
\eqref{eq:family}. We do not transfer a cycle classification across that
sign change. The present theorem concerns only the exact factorial-product
family \eqref{eq:family}, not shifted polynomials, all integer-valued
polynomials, or arbitrary degree-$d$ H\'enon maps.
