\section{The exact cycle atlas}\label{sec:atlas}
Fix the data in \eqref{eq:map}. A cycle means an ordinary orbit of
least positive period. We do not identify points by a sign involution
and do not count fixed-scheme multiplicities. Write
\[
 K_a=\frac{(a+1)^2}{4}.
\]
Binary words below are words over the set $\{0,1\}$. Bits are mapped
to $k$ only when they occur in a field equation.

\begin{theorem}[Rationality and reconstruction]\label{thm:atlas}
If $H_{a,c}$ has a periodic point in $k(t)^2$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered}
 c=-P^2+C,\qquad P\in k[t]\setminus k,\quad C\in k.
\end{equation}
When such a representation exists, $C$ is unique and $P$ is unique
up to sign. Fix either choice of $P$. Every rational cycle is given
by Table~\ref{tab:atlas} and the reconstruction rule
\begin{equation}\label{eq:word-reconstruction}
 \sigma_{i+1}=\sigma_i(-1)^{\delta_i},\qquad
 y_i=\sigma_iP+
 \frac{(-1)^{\delta_i}+a(-1)^{\delta_{i-1}}}{2},
 \qquad (y_i,y_{i+1})\in k[t]^2.
\end{equation}
Here $(\delta_i)$ repeats the row's transition word in both directions,
and $\sigma_0$ is either sign. A row contributes if and only if its
conditions and its value of $C$ hold in $k$. Simultaneously applicable
rows contribute disjoint point sets and are added. If
\eqref{eq:centered} does not exist, or if it exists but no row applies,
there are no rational periodic points.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp totals and determinant restriction]\label{thm:bound}
There are at most fourteen rational periodic points. Equality holds
exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equality}
 \ch k=3,\qquad a=-1,\qquad c=-P^2-1
 \quad\text{for some }P\in k[t]\setminus k.
\end{equation}
These maps have one four-cycle and two five-cycles.
If $\ch k\notin\{2,3\}$, the sharp bound is eight when $-1$ is a
square in $k$, and six otherwise. Eight points occur exactly at
$a^2=-1$ and $C=K_a$, as two fixed points and a six-cycle. In the
nonsquare case, six points are attained at $a=1$ and $C=-1$.
In every allowed characteristic, a point of period greater than two
requires $a^4=1$.
\end{theorem}

In particular, odd degree of $c$ precludes every rational periodic
point. The converse of this last observation alone is false:
even a centered square parameter can miss every row of the atlas.
All sufficiency assertions in the theorem refer to the explicit
points in \eqref{eq:word-reconstruction}, without extending $k$.
