\section{The exact signed rationality graph}\label{sec:graph}
Assume $c=-P^2+C$ and fix $P$. Substituting
$y_i=\sigma_iP+b_i$ into \eqref{eq:recurrence} and comparing the
coefficients of the linearly independent polynomials $P$ and $1$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:offsets}
 2\sigma_i b_i=\sigma_{i+1}+a\sigma_{i-1},\qquad
 b_i^2+C=b_{i+1}+a b_{i-1}.
\end{equation}
Define transition bits by $\sigma_{i+1}=\sigma_i(-1)^{\delta_i}$.
The first equation is exactly the offset formula in
\eqref{eq:word-reconstruction}; equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:offset-bits}
 b_i=(a+1)/2-\delta_i-a\delta_{i-1}.
\end{equation}
Put $\Lambda=K_a-C$. Expanding the second equation of
\eqref{eq:offsets}, using $\delta_j^2=\delta_j$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bit-constraint}
 \Lambda=a^2\delta_{i-1}+2a\delta_i\delta_{i+1}+\delta_{i+2}.
\end{equation}
For clarity, the unshifted expansion is
\[
 K_a-C=a^2\delta_{i-2}+2a\delta_{i-1}\delta_i+\delta_{i+1}.
\]
Thus \eqref{eq:bit-constraint} is an identity over
$\mathbb Z[1/2]$ before specialization, not a characteristic-zero
argument applied without checking its denominators.

Take the eight states $V=\{0,1\}^3$. A state $uvw$ has an edge
to $vwz$ if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edge}
 \Lambda=a^2u+2avw+z.
\end{equation}
Table~\ref{tab:edges} lists all sixteen potential edges. The two
outgoing labels at a state differ by one, and the two incoming labels
at a prescribed successor differ by $a^2$. Both differences are
nonzero. Thus indegree and outdegree are at most one, and the recurrent
part is a disjoint union of simple directed cycles.

The bit graph has forgotten a global sign. Restore it by defining
\begin{equation}\label{eq:label}
 E(\varepsilon;u,v,w)=
 \left(\varepsilon P+\frac{(-1)^v+a(-1)^u}{2},\quad
 \varepsilon(-1)^vP+\frac{(-1)^w+a(-1)^v}{2}\right),
 \qquad \varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact restriction and injectivity]\label{prop:exact}
The sixteen labels in \eqref{eq:label} are distinct points of
$k[t]^2$. Every rational periodic point is among them. Their exact
transition rule is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lift}
 H_{a,c}E(\varepsilon;u,v,w)
 =E(\varepsilon(-1)^v;v,w,z)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \Lambda=a^2u+2avw+z.
\end{equation}
More generally, an image of a signed label can be another signed
label only in the shifted form on the right of \eqref{eq:lift}.
Consequently the directed signed cycles are exactly the ordinary
rational cycles of $H_{a,c}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The leading coefficient of the first coordinate of $E$ determines
$\varepsilon$ relative to the fixed nonconstant $P$. The leading
coefficient ratio of the two coordinates determines $v$. The first
constant offset then determines $u$, since $a\ne0$ and $1\ne-1$;
the second offset determines $w$. This proves injectivity, including
the cases $a=\pm1$ and characteristic three.

For an edge satisfying \eqref{eq:edge}, direct substitution of
\eqref{eq:label}, or equations \eqref{eq:offsets}--\eqref{eq:edge},
gives the equality in \eqref{eq:lift}. Conversely, suppose the image
is $E(\varepsilon';u',v',w')$. The two leading coefficients first
give $\varepsilon'=\varepsilon(-1)^v$ and $v'=w$. Comparing the
first constant offset then gives $u'=v$, using $a\ne0$. Set $z=w'$.
The remaining constant equation is precisely \eqref{eq:edge}.
The order of this recovery matters: one constant offset alone need
not determine two bits.

Proposition~\ref{prop:branches} and equations \eqref{eq:offsets}
show that every rational periodic point is one of these signed labels.
An actual cycle therefore follows the exact signed edges. In the
other direction, following a closed signed path proves the recurrence
at every point and constructs an actual cycle of the original map.
\end{proof}
