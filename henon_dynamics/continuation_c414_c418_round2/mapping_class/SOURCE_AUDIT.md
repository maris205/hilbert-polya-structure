## Primary sources and exact subtraction

### P1. Roberts--Baake (1994): foundational trace maps and periodic families

John A. G. Roberts and Michael Baake, *Trace maps as 3D reversible
dynamical systems with an invariant*, Journal of Statistical Physics
74 (1994), 829--888, DOI
[10.1007/BF02188581](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02188581).
[Author-hosted published PDF](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~jagr/RB94.pdf)
was accessed; the trace/substitution correspondence, generator structure
and periodic-family discussions are the relevant material, especially
Eq. (29) and Section 6, Propositions 11--12. The source uses half traces;
our coordinates equal twice theirs, and its Fricke invariant equals
$K/4-1$. Polynomial maps with integer coefficients do not mean that
their half-trace integer lattice is our full-trace integer lattice.
The map, invariant, symmetry and known Fibonacci periodic families are
fully classical. No claim here assigns them new ownership.

### P2. Roberts (1996): the closest methodological collision

John A. G. Roberts, *Escaping orbits in trace maps*, Physica A 228
(1996), 295--325, DOI
[10.1016/0378-4371(95)00428-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-4371(95)00428-9).
[Author-hosted published PDF](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~jagr/R96.pdf)
was accessed. Relevant locators: Section 2's word classes; Theorem 3.1;
Section 4, Proposition 4.1, Theorems 4.1--4.3 and Remark 4.1; Section 5.
This already provides escape criteria for general infinite-order trace
maps, not just Fibonacci. Thus extending the *escape method* to general
words is not an independent contribution. The candidate must stand on
its exact integer 39-line exhaustion and uniform word classifier after
that subtraction. No theorem with that precise integer all-word output
was identified in the inspected statements. That is source-bounded
non-location, not a theorem of global novelty.

### P3. Cantat--Loray (2007 preprint / 2009 publication)

Serge Cantat and Frank Loray, preprint *Holomorphic dynamics, Painlevé
VI equation and Character Varieties*,
[arXiv:0711.1579v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.1579), dated 5 December
2007. The published title is *Dynamics on Character Varieties and
Malgrange irreducibility of Painlevé VI equation*, Annales de l'Institut
Fourier 59(7) (2009), 2927--2978,
[publisher record, DOI 10.5802/aif.2512](https://aif.centre-mersenne.org/item/AIF_2009__59_7_2927_0/).
The preprint PDF and publisher metadata were accessed. Relevant preprint
locators are Section 2.4, Section 3.3/Proposition 3.2, and Theorem 5.3.
The trace-surface/mapping-class correspondence and matrix hyperbolicity
are classical. Theorem 5.3 concerns a bounded **group** orbit, not
periodicity for an arbitrarily fixed group element. The latter cannot
be inferred from it. The author-hosted long-PDF URL failed in this
session; the arXiv PDF supplied the verified text instead.

### P4. Humphries (2016): different quantifier

Stephen Humphries, *Points with finite orbits for trace maps*,
[arXiv:1611.02743v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02743), submitted
8 November 2016. The arXiv record does not list a journal publication;
no publication status beyond that record is asserted here.
[Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1611.02743v1) was accessed, notably
the introduction's definitions, Theorem 1 and the discussion of fixed
curves in Section 1. Its $\mathcal F$ is finite whole-group orbit and
its $\mathcal P$ means periodic under **each** group element; the
theorem identifies those sets. Our quantifier is periodic under one
specified positive word, varying over a full word family. The explicit
$A^6\circ B^6$ fixed line with escaping $A\circ B$ orbit proves
the difference; it is not merely a wording objection. Half traces must
again be doubled. The source's references 16--17 also identify prior
fixed/period-two curve papers.

### P5. Ghosh--Sarnak (2017 preprint / 2022 final version)

Amit Ghosh and Peter Sarnak, *Integral points on Markoff type cubic
surfaces*, [arXiv:1706.06712v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.06712),
30 May 2022; Inventiones Mathematicae (2022),
[DOI 10.1007/s00222-022-01114-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-022-01114-z).
The versioned arXiv record and
[full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1706.06712v3) were accessed, with
emphasis on Theorem 1.1 and Sections 2 and 4. This is the same integer
cubic surface normalization. Its descent and fundamental domains
classify or count orbits of the Markoff group; finitely many group
orbits does not say that those orbits are finite or classify a single
word's periodic points. All use of integer descent as a general
mechanism is deducted. No cyclic-periodicity theorem with the present
39-line output was found in the inspected material.

### P6. Humphries--Manning (2007), with the 2015 follow-up as an access limit

Stephen Humphries and Anthony Manning, *Curves of fixed points of trace
maps*, Ergodic Theory and Dynamical Systems 27(4) (2007), 1167--1198,
DOI [10.1017/S0143385707000016](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0143385707000016).
The [institutional record](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/673/)
and [published PDF](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/673/2/WRAP_manning_Curves_fixed_points.pdf)
were accessed. The introduction and Section 1 specify a particular
symmetric matrix family and real fixed curves, with half-trace
normalization. These curves and their mechanism are not new here.
Their follow-up, *Curves of period two points for trace maps*, Trans.
AMS 367 (2015), 5721--5751, is verified in Humphries' primary reference
list and [author CV](https://math.byu.edu/0000018b-abca-d20e-abab-abfe95f80001/humphriescv-pdf).
Its DOI is [10.1090/S0002-9947-2014-06367-8](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-2014-06367-8),
but the DOI fetch failed. Its full text was not verified in this
session; no assertion about all of its theorems is made.

### P7. Sasaki--Yoshida (2008): the exact two generators are classical

Takeshi Sasaki and Masaaki Yoshida, *Interpolation of Markoff
transformations on the Fricke surface*, Tohoku Mathematical Journal
60 (2008), 23--36.
[Published journal PDF](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tmj/60/1/60_1_23/_pdf)
was accessed. Section 2.1 explicitly gives their $T=B$ and $R=A$ in
our full-trace normalization, and gives the corresponding upper/lower
unipotent matrices. Lemma 2.1 gives the Chebyshev iterate formulas;
Section 2.2 describes conic rotation/translation. Thus even the exact
chosen generator pair and the finite-order small-coordinate mechanism
are pre-existing. The candidate is their all-positive-word integer
periodic exhaustion, not a new map or new twist formula.

### P8. Vishkautsan (2015 preprint / 2016 publication): residual versus ordinary

Solomon Vishkautsan, *Residual periodicity on the Markoff surface*,
[arXiv:1504.07099v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.07099), 8 July 2015;
Rendiconti Lincei Matematica e Applicazioni 27 (2016), 25--35,
[DOI 10.4171/RLM/720](https://doi.org/10.4171/RLM/720).
The primary abstract and metadata were verified this session. Its
question is strong residual periodicity for compositions of two
reflections and the removal of periodic conics without rational points.
This is not the ordinary integer periodic classification of every
positive word on every level. The abstract is sufficient for that
scope distinction; no uninspected proof is used.

## Local C413 collision

Read-only comparison used the existing
[C413 proof package](../../continuation_c409_c413_round2/nonlinear_geometry/PROOF_PACKAGE.md)
and [source ledger](../../continuation_c409_c413_round2/nonlinear_geometry/SOURCE_LEDGER.md).
No old proof, test, PDF or sealed checkpoint was rerun or edited.

C413 treats the one Fibonacci map $(x,y,z)\mapsto(y,z,yz-x)$,
with periodic levels $m^2$ and $m^2-m+2$ and its finite low-level
core. The old integral maximum/escape mechanism and the old periodic
families must be deducted in full. The current package is not another
iterate of that map: it classifies every positive word in two separate
twists, including words with trace four and the full additional level
family $m^2+1$. Its necessity argument tracks changing first/second
maxima and every letter phase, and its output is a word-dependent
partial-permutation classifier instead of the single map's fixed
list. Whether that quantified strengthening meets the batch's
substantiveness threshold is reserved for the independent reviewer
and coordinator, not decided by this source-audit role.
