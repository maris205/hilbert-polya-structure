# Bounded source audit: nonconstant function-field quadratic Hénon maps

Scope: all $H_{a,c}(x,y)=(y,y^2+c-a x)$ over $k(t)$, with
$\operatorname{char}k\ne2$, $a\in k^*$ and nonconstant $c\in k[t]$.
The proposed increment is the complete global rational-cycle locus,
all constant determinants, all allowed characteristics, ordinary signed
cycle lifts and sharp field-dependent counts. It is not general

## Primary comparisons actually accessed

### Ingram: height and bad-place bounds

Patrick Ingram, *Canonical heights for Hénon maps*, Proc. London Math.
Soc. 108 (2014), no. 3, 780–808,
[publisher record](https://academic.oup.com/plms/article-abstract/108/3/780/1571741),
DOI 10.1112/plms/pdt026. Actual proof-text access was the
[32-page arXiv:1111.3609v1 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.3609v1).
The coordinator read the introduction's Theorems 1.1–1.4 and surrounding
scope, selected local-height statements, the §3 root-disc/finiteness
argument and its final theorem proof. The independent reviewer additionally
checked the §3 quadratic routing and selected §4 bounds/closure; see
[their exact review scope](REVIEW_SOURCE_AND_REDUCTION.md). Neither
reader claims all 32 pages or the final journal text were read anew.

The source writes $\varphi_\alpha=(\alpha y,x+f(y))$. The direct
conjugacy $L(x,y)=(-a x,y)$ gives
$LH_{a,c}L^{-1}=\varphi_{-a}$ with $f(y)=y^2+c$. Its
$\alpha=1$ hypothesis is therefore our $a=-1$, not our conservative
$a=1$. Theorems 1.2/1.4 own the stated finiteness/height and bad-place
period bounds. Their existence and general method are fully deducted.
The accessed statements do not provide the present all-determinant
square-completion locus, every ordinary cycle or the characteristic-three
five/eight-cycle branches. The degree-at-least-three §3 estimate must
not be quoted directly as its quadratic proof.

The v1 header says 15 November 2011 while an internal date in the served
PDF says 21 August 2017. The journal issue is 2014 and online-first
metadata can say 2013. These are distinct version/publication records;
none is silently substituted for another or used as a current novelty
guarantee. The narrow comparison is to the actual accessed theorem text.

### Allen–DeMark–Petsche: the completion horseshoe is not a rational locus

Kenneth Allen, David DeMark and Clayton Petsche,
*Non-Archimedean Hénon maps, attractors, and horseshoes*,
[arXiv:1610.04271](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.04271), v3 dated
6 February 2018. Actual [primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.04271)
access: introduction, field assumptions, parameter regions, Theorem
1(a),(e), adjacent discussion and fixed/two-cycle statement; selected
§5 horseshoe statements were checked by the independent reviewer.
No journal version or full 31-page proof access is asserted here.

Its map is $\varphi_{A,B}(X,Y)=(A+B Y-X^2,X)$. The explicit
$J(x,y)=(-y,-x)$ converts our map to $(A,B)=(-c,-a)$.
The theorem assumes a complete, locally compact non-Archimedean field
of odd residue characteristic. In $|A|>\max(1,|B|^2)$ with $A$
a square it supplies a full two-symbol bilateral shift on the filled
Julia set. This is the nearest symbolic-dynamics mechanism and is
fully credited. Fixed and two-cycle equations are likewise classical.

For finite odd $k$, the completion $k((1/t))$ fits that field setting,
but its unrestricted shift does not tell which itineraries are rational
functions. For arbitrary $k$ even the local-compactness hypothesis may
fail. The global rational restriction and sixteen injective signed
states therefore cannot be read off as the same theorem. No extension
of their hypotheses is required by our elementary proof.

## Repository ownership deducted

The [C412 conservative integral quadratic proof](../../research_c409_c413/nonlinear_geometry/PROOF_INTEGER_HENON.md)
already uses sign/constant-offset equations at determinant $a=1$.
Its real large-parameter separation, six symbols and small-integer
completion are prior results. The algebraic two-branch argument here
is related, not an invention of symbolic encoding. Only the all-field
global locus, unrestricted nonzero constant determinant and exhaustive
field-dependent atlas can carry a new contract.

The [accepted constant-coefficient height proof](../../research_c414_c418/spectral/HEIGHT_PROOF_PACKAGE.md)
has finite constant-field coefficients and asks for polynomial-point
height distributions. Here $c$ is nonconstant and the object is every
periodic point in $k(t)^2$. Its elementary degree-escape reasoning is
deducted, but neither parameter family nor observable coincides.
The simultaneous cubic scout concerns $\mathbb Q^2$ and arbitrary
monic integral cubic coefficients, with a global integer secant root;
it does not supply this quadratic function-field locus.
