\section{The normal line field and the full Weyl group}
\label{sec:weyl-galois}

\subsection{The two fields}

Let \(K\) be the splitting field of the irreducible eliminant \(g\).  The
\(27\) embeddings \(E\hookrightarrow\Qbar\) send the class of \(d\) to the
\(27\) roots of \(g\), hence to the \(27\) geometric points of
\(\FanoY\).  The rational back-substitutions in
\cref{eq:back-substitution} recover \(a,b,c\) from each root.  Thus \(K\)
contains the coordinates of every line.

Conversely, a normal extension over which all \(27\) lines are defined
contains all of their \(d\)-coordinates and therefore every root of \(g\).
It contains \(K\).  Hence \(K\) is the least normal common field of
definition of the lines and is the normal closure of \(E\).

This distinction will matter numerically:
\[
 [E:\Q]=27,\qquad [K:\Q]=51840.
\]
In particular \(E\ne K\).

\subsection{Transitivity and the order-five gate}

Set
\[
 G=\Gal(K/\Q).
\]
The action of \(G\) on the roots is faithful by definition of a splitting
field and transitive by \cref{prop:g-irreducible}.  The intersections among
the reconstructed lines are defined geometrically and are preserved by
Galois.  Elsenhans--Jahnel Fact~3 therefore embeds \(G\) into the Weyl group
\(\WE\) acting on the Schläfli configuration
\cite{ElsenhansJahnel2009}.

At \(p=37\), the leading coefficient survives and the complete factorization
is squarefree.  The prime is therefore unramified in the root permutation,
and the factor degrees give a Frobenius element
\begin{equation}
 \varphi\in G\subseteq\WE
 \quad\text{with cycle type}\quad(2,5,5,5,10).
\label{eq:frob-type}
\end{equation}
Its order is \(10\), and \(\varphi^2\) has order \(5\).
Elsenhans--Jahnel Lemma~8 applies to the transitive group \(G\): it leaves
exactly the alternatives
\begin{equation}
  G=\Ucox\qquad\text{or}\qquad G=\WE,
\label{eq:two-alternatives}
\end{equation}
where \(\Ucox\) is the simple index-two subgroup of order \(25920\).
The order-five argument alone does not choose between them.

\subsection{The parity firewall}

The subgroup \(\Ucox\) is the kernel of determinant on the six-dimensional
\(E_6\) reflection representation.  Equivalently, it is the subgroup of
even Coxeter word parity.  This is not ordinary permutation parity on the
\(27\) lines.  Elsenhans--Jahnel Remark~5 states that the entire image of
\(\WE\) on those lines lies in \(A_{27}\).  In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{sgn}_{S_{27}}(w)=+1
 \quad\text{for every }w\in\WE.
\label{eq:s27-even}
\end{equation}
The target type in \cref{eq:frob-type} also has ordinary sign \(+1\);
ordinary sign cannot exclude \(\Ucox\).

The exact Picard-lattice enumeration constructs \(\WE\) from its six simple
reflections and obtains:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
quantity & exact value\\
\midrule
elements of \(\WE\) & \(51840\)\\
elements of \(\Ucox\) & \(25920\)\\
orbit size on line classes & \(27\)\\
elements of type \((2,5,5,5,10)\) & \(5184\)\\
target-type elements in \(\Ucox\) & \(0\)\\
target-type elements outside \(\Ucox\) & \(5184\)\\
elements acting oddly in \(S_{27}\) & \(0\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thus every element of the Frobenius type lies outside \(\Ucox\).  This
instance computation agrees with Elsenhans--Jahnel Algorithm~10 and
Remarks~11--13: the pattern \((2,5,5,5,10)\) is one of their selected
outside-\(\Ucox\) classes and simultaneously supplies an order-five
element.

\begin{theorem}[Maximal line-field Galois group]
\label{thm:weyl}
The common normal line field \(K\) satisfies
\[
 \Gal(K/\Q)\cong\WE,\qquad [K:\Q]=51840.
\]
The degree-\(27\) field \(E\) is not Galois over \(\Q\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Frobenius element \(\varphi\) lies outside \(\Ucox\), so the first
alternative in \cref{eq:two-alternatives} is impossible.  Hence
\(G=\WE\), whose exact order is \(51840\).  If \(E/\Q\) were Galois, its
normal closure would equal \(E\) and have degree \(27\), contradicting the
degree of \(K\).
\end{proof}
