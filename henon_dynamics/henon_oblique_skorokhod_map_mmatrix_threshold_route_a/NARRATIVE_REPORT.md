# Narrative report

The decisive step is an exact threshold theorem, not a collection of reflected
sample paths.  The two scalar one-sided regulators convert the coupled
Skorokhod problem into a nonlinear running-supremum fixed point.  When both
couplings are positive, the reflection geometry selects a weighted sup norm in
which the map contracts by exactly `sqrt(rho*sigma)`.  Banach's theorem then
gives the solution, quantitative stability and a constructive monotone Picard
scheme.  The triangular faces follow by direct substitution.

The same formulation makes causality and continuous time-change covariance
transparent.  At the wall, a null reflection direction produces infinitely
many regulators for zero input; at and beyond the wall, one simultaneous
negative jump is not even feasible.  Hence the strict M-matrix chamber is both
sufficient and necessary.

This source-local path theorem does not produce arithmetic primitive orbits,
an Euler product, target analytic data or a natural spectral quantization.
