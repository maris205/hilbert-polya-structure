# C243 experiment and certification plan

## Frozen question

Can the two-mode Bose--Josephson dimer be reduced to a complete analytic phase
portrait that distinguishes elliptic fixed points, the \(\Lambda=1\)
pitchfork, crossing/self-trapped energy components, and the \(H=1\)
homoclinic boundary without using the singular \((z,\phi)\) chart at the
Bloch poles?

## Claim-driven tests

1. Derive Hamilton's equations and the regular Bloch-vector field; verify the
   sphere constraint and pole velocities for \(\Lambda=0,1,2,3\).
2. Enumerate all fixed points and linear stability for those parameters,
   including the symmetry-broken pair for \(\Lambda>1\) and its coalescence at
   \(\Lambda=1\).
3. Eliminate phase to obtain the quartic in \(z\), certify its roots and
   component intervals, and compare complete-elliptic-\(K\) periods with
   independently transformed quadratures on crossing and self-trapped levels.
4. Verify the \(H=1\) sech profile, the \(\Lambda=2\) pole limit, and the
   strict component-level criterion \(H>1\) versus \(H<1\), including the
   reverse-sign statement.

## Falsifiers and boundaries

Any wrong sign in the canonical equations, a pole treated as a regular
\(\phi\)-coordinate point, a period formula outside its energy component, or a
   finite period assigned to a separatrix is a release failure. Regular levels
   form a continuum, so A1 remains WEAK; no discrete primitive atlas or target
   determinant is claimed.
