\begin{abstract}
For every fixed prime $p$ and $\alpha>0$, we define a nonnegative operator on
$L^2(\Zp)$ directly as the Fourier multiplier $0$ on constants and
$p^{\alpha n}$ on characters of exact conductor $n$.  Its spectrum and a
conditional-expectation reconstruction prove a positive conservative Markov
semigroup with an exact heat trace.
\ifnum\CRevisionRound>0 We prove the exact eigenvalue staircase, a
discrete-scale oscillation and log-periodic heat profile, and the sharp
Schatten threshold for fractional resolvents.\fi\space
\ifnum\CRevisionRound>1 The mean-zero spectral zeta has a complete vertical
pole lattice and an explicit primed determinant; all $\alpha=0$, $\mu=0$, and
$t=0$ faces and a finite-quotient DFT certificate are closed.\fi\space
The fixed local prime is not promoted to all-prime target arithmetic.
\end{abstract}

\section{The frozen conductor multiplier}
Let $p$ be any fixed rational prime, let $G=\Zp$ have Haar mass one, and use
$\widehat G=\Qp/\Zp$.  A nonzero class $\xi$ has exact conductor $n(\xi)=n$
when $\chi_\xi$ is trivial on $p^n\Zp$ but not on $p^{n-1}\Zp$.  Exactly
$p^n-p^{n-1}=(p-1)p^{n-1}$ characters have conductor $n$.

For $\alpha>0$ we freeze
\begin{equation}
 D_{p,\alpha}1=0,\qquad
 D_{p,\alpha}\chi_\xi=p^{\alpha n(\xi)}\chi_\xi,             \tag{1}
\end{equation}
with its maximal diagonal domain.  For $\mu\geq0$, set
$A_{p,\alpha,\mu}=D_{p,\alpha}+\mu I$ and
$T_t=e^{-tA_{p,\alpha,\mu}}$.  Equation (1) is the normalization: the paper
does not identify it with every convention called a Vladimirov operator.
Pseudodifferential spectra over p-adic fields, p-adic wavelet eigenbases, and
ultrametric Markov semigroups form the surrounding literature
\cite{Vlad,Kozyrev,BGPW,VVZ}.  More directly, Example 5.1 of
Chac\'on-Cort\'es--Z\'u\~niga-Galindo \cite{CZ}, at dimension one and exponent
$\alpha$, already gives exactly our positive shell spectrum, multiplicities,
geometric zeta, and vertical pole lattice.  Those receive zero priority
credit.  We retain a source-local Markov, scale-boundary, determinant, and
finite-reconstruction closure, not a literature-novelty claim.

Let $E_n$ denote conditional expectation onto functions constant on cosets of
$p^n\Zp$, so $E_0=P_0$ projects onto constants and $E_n\uparrow I$ strongly.
Put
\[
 W_0=\ran E_0,\qquad W_n=\ran E_n\ominus\ran E_{n-1}\quad(n\geq1).
\]
Then $\dim W_n=(p-1)p^{n-1}$ and the character and filtration descriptions
give the same orthogonal decomposition.

\begin{theorem}[spectrum and Markov reconstruction]
The spectrum of $D_{p,\alpha}$ consists of the simple eigenvalue zero and
$p^{\alpha n}$ with multiplicity $(p-1)p^{n-1}$, $n\geq1$.  Its resolvent is
compact.  The spectrum of $A_{p,\alpha,\mu}$ is the simple eigenvalue $\mu$
and $\mu+p^{\alpha n}$ with the same multiplicities.  Moreover
$e^{-tD_{p,\alpha}}$ is a positive, conservative,
self-adjoint contraction semigroup, while $T_t$ is positive sub-Markov and
$\lVert T_t\rVert=e^{-\mu t}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Character counting and (1) prove the spectral assertion; the eigenvalues tend
to infinity.  A second reconstruction is
\begin{equation}
 D_{p,\alpha}=\sum_{n\geq1}p^{\alpha n}P_{W_n}
 =\sum_{n\geq0}a_n(I-E_n),                                \tag{2}
\end{equation}
where $a_0=p^\alpha$ and
$a_n=p^{\alpha(n+1)}-p^{\alpha n}$ for $n\geq1$.  On $W_m$, the right side
is $\sum_{n<m}a_n=p^{\alpha m}$.  For every conditional expectation $E$,
\[
 e^{-s a(I-E)}=E+e^{-sa}(I-E)
\]
is positive, constant-preserving, and contractive.  The finite factors in
(2) commute; their strong limit proves the Markov claims.  The scalar killing
factor $e^{-\mu t}$ gives the final norm and sub-Markov statement.
\end{proof}

This proof derives positivity from the residue filtration.  Nonnegative
eigenvalues alone would not have established positivity preservation.

\ifnum\CRevisionRound>0
\section{Heat trace, exact staircase, and Schatten boundary}
For $q>0$, write $\mathcal S_q$ for the operators whose singular values have
finite $q$-sum.  When $0<q<1$, this is the quasi-Schatten ideal with its usual
quasi-norm, not a Banach Schatten class.

\begin{theorem}[sharp scale theorem]
For $t>0$, $T_t\in\mathcal S_q$ for every $q>0$ and
\begin{equation}
 \Tr T_t=e^{-\mu t}\left[1+\sum_{n\geq1}(p-1)p^{n-1}
 e^{-tp^{\alpha n}}\right].                              \tag{3}
\end{equation}
If $N(\Lambda)$ counts the positive spectrum, then
\begin{equation}
 p^{\alpha m}\leq\Lambda<p^{\alpha(m+1)}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad N(\Lambda)=p^m-1.             \tag{4}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\[
 \limsup_{\Lambda\to\infty}\frac{N(\Lambda)}{\Lambda^{1/\alpha}}=1,
 \qquad
 \liminf_{\Lambda\to\infty}\frac{N(\Lambda)}{\Lambda^{1/\alpha}}=\frac1p.
\]
For $\sigma,q>0$,
\begin{equation}
 (I+D_{p,\alpha})^{-\sigma}\in\mathcal S_q
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \alpha\sigma q>1;          \tag{5}
\end{equation}
the equality case diverges.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The spectral theorem and shell multiplicities give (3), and exponential decay
in $p^{\alpha n}$ dominates every geometric multiplicity; the same argument
applies after taking any positive power of the singular values.  Summing the
first $m$ multiplicities gives $p^m-1$, proving (4).  At a shell and just
before its successor, the scaled ratios approach $1$ and $1/p$, respectively.
Finally, the $q$-sum in (5) is comparable, up to finitely many terms, to
$\sum_n p^{n(1-\alpha\sigma q)}$, which converges exactly under the displayed
strict inequality.
\end{proof}

The staircase also has an exact small-time scaling profile.  If
$t=p^{-\alpha m}\tau$, then locally uniformly for $\tau>0$,
\begin{equation}
 t^{1/\alpha}\bigl(\Tr e^{-tD_{p,\alpha}}-1\bigr)
 \xrightarrow[m\to\infty]{} \Phi_{p,\alpha}(\tau)
 =\frac{p-1}{p}\tau^{1/\alpha}
 \sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}p^j e^{-\tau p^{\alpha j}}.           \tag{6}
\end{equation}
The negative tail is geometric and the positive tail superexponential, so the
limit follows by reindexing $j=n-m$.  Reindexing once more gives
$\Phi_{p,\alpha}(p^\alpha\tau)=\Phi_{p,\alpha}(\tau)$.  Equations (4) and (6)
record a genuine discrete-scale oscillation, not a missing smooth Weyl
constant.  Notice the category distinction: positive-time heat is in every
$\mathcal S_q$, whereas the fractional resolvent has the sharp boundary (5).

\ifnum\CRevisionRound>1
\section{Complete zeta lattice and primed determinant}
All zeta statements are on $L^2_0(\Zp)$; the prime symbol means that the
constant zero mode is omitted.

\begin{theorem}[mean-zero spectral zeta]
Initially for $\Re s>1/\alpha$,
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_{p,\alpha}(s)=\Tr' D_{p,\alpha}^{-s}
 =(1-p^{-1})\frac{p^{1-\alpha s}}{1-p^{1-\alpha s}}.       \tag{7}
\end{equation}
This is the meromorphic continuation.  Its complete pole set is
\begin{equation}
 s_k=\frac1\alpha+\frac{2\pi i k}{\alpha\log p},
 \qquad k\in\mathbb Z,                                   \tag{8}
\end{equation}
all poles are simple, and each residue is
$(1-p^{-1})/(\alpha\log p)$.  Furthermore
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_{p,\alpha}(0)=-1,
 \quad \zeta'_{p,\alpha}(0)=-\frac{\alpha\log p}{p-1},
 \quad \det{}'_{\zeta}D_{p,\alpha}=p^{\alpha/(p-1)}.      \tag{9}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Shell summation gives a geometric series of ratio $p^{1-\alpha s}$, proving
(7).  The denominator vanishes exactly at (8); its derivative there is
$\alpha\log p$, while the numerator is $1-p^{-1}$, proving completeness,
simplicity, and the residue.  Substitution and differentiation at zero give
(9), including the convention $\det{}'_\zeta D=\exp[-\zeta'(0)]$.
\end{proof}

The vertical pole lattice is the Mellin counterpart of discrete spatial
scaling.  It is not a target zero set, and the source-local primed determinant
is not a target Euler product.

\section{Boundary atlas and finite-quotient reconstruction}
At $\alpha=0$ the natural face is $D_{p,0}=I-P_0$.  It is bounded and
noncompact, with eigenvalue one of infinite multiplicity.  For fixed $t>0$,
$e^{-t(D_{p,\alpha}+\mu I)}$ converges strongly as $\alpha\downarrow0$ to
$e^{-t(I-P_0+\mu I)}$, but not in operator norm: the mean-zero norm defect is
$e^{-(\mu+1)t}$.  Thus trace class, compact resolvent, and (7) do not extend to
that face.

At $\mu=0$ constants remain the zero mode and the heat semigroup is Markov;
this is why (7)--(9) are mean-zero and primed.  At $t=0$, $T_0=I$ is not
compact and belongs to no finite $\mathcal S_q$.  Conversely, for fixed
$t>0$, $\alpha\to\infty$ gives $T_t\to e^{-\mu t}P_0$ in operator norm, since
the mean-zero norm is $e^{-\mu t-tp^\alpha}$.  No odd-prime step was used, so
$p=2$ is included.

On $G_N=\mathbb Z/p^N\mathbb Z$, character $k\ne0$ has conductor
$N-v_p(k)$.  Hence the finite DFT multiplier agrees exactly with
\[
 \sum_{n=1}^N p^{\alpha n}(E_n-E_{n-1}).                  \tag{10}
\]
