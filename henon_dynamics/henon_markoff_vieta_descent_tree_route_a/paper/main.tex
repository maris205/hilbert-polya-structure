\begin{abstract}
We orient the entire positive Markoff graph, modulo coordinate permutations,
by replacing the unique largest coordinate with the other Vieta root.  Every
non-root normalized solution has a positive parent of strictly smaller height;
integer descent terminates at $(1,1,1)$, reverse edges generate every positive
solution, and the quotient graph is a rooted tree.  A producer-independent
bounded scan and separate symbolic reconstruction test the exact convention
without replacing the all-solution proof.  The integer cubic gives intrinsic
Diophantine structure but no rational-prime primitive carrier, prime-power
repetition or logarithmic clock.  Strict descent also removes every non-root
periodic orbit, so the candidate remains below Route A despite its arithmetic
origin.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Markoff equation; Vieta involution; infinite
descent; Diophantine dynamics; rooted tree; Route-A obstruction.

\section{Frozen Diophantine dynamics}
Let
\[
 P(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2+z^2-3xyz.
\]
A normalized Markoff triple is a positive integral zero with $x\le y\le z$.
For example $R_z(x,y,z)=(x,y,3xy-z)$.  Direct expansion gives
$P\circ R_z=P$ and $R_z^2=\mathrm{id}$; the other maps follow by symmetry.
As a quadratic in $z$, the two roots $z,z'$ obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vieta}
 z+z'=3xy,\qquad zz'=x^2+y^2,\qquad z'=3xy-z.
\end{equation}
The classical equation and reduction genealogy go back to
Markoff~\cite{M79}; Bombieri gives a modern tree account~\cite{B07}.

\section{Unique parent, termination, and completeness}
First, every non-root normalized solution has a unique maximum.  If $y=z$,
the equation becomes
\[
 (3x-2)y^2=x^2.
\]
For $x\ge2$ the quotient $x^2/(3x-2)$ is smaller than $x$, contradicting
$y^2\ge x^2$; for $x=1$ it forces $y=1$, the root.

Now let $q(t)=t^2-3xyt+x^2+y^2$.  At $t=y$,
\[
 q(y)=x^2-(3x-2)y^2\le0.
\]
Together with~\eqref{eq:vieta}, this places $y$ between the two positive
roots.  Hence every non-root solution satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:descent}
 0<z'=3xy-z\le y<z.
\end{equation}
Define $D(x,y,z)=\operatorname{sort}(x,y,z')$ and fix $(1,1,1)$.  The integer
height $h=z$ strictly decreases, so every orbit terminates.  The only terminal
point is the root, since~\eqref{eq:descent} supplies a parent otherwise.

No Vieta edge is lost by this orientation.  Replacing either nonmaximal
coordinate gives
\[
 (3yz-x)-z=z(3y-1)-x>0,\qquad
 (3xz-y)-z=z(3x-1)-y>0.
\]
Thus both nonparent mutations ascend strictly, while the largest-coordinate
mutation is the unique descending edge.

The source-locked Vieta-orbit statement records that all positive triples are
obtained from the root by coordinate permutations and Vieta
involutions~\cite{BGS16}.  Reversing $D$ therefore generates every normalized
positive solution.  The descending coordinate is unique, and the preceding
inequalities exhaust all other Vieta edges, so each non-root vertex has
exactly one parent.  Connectivity and strict height exclude cycles: the
normalized solution graph is a rooted tree.  Repeated coordinates merely
collapse duplicate outgoing mutations at the first two levels
$(1,1,1)\to(1,1,2)\to(1,2,5)$.

\paragraph{Open-problem and scope firewall.}
Unique parenthood of a triple does not say that its largest Markoff number
determines the other coordinates.  The Frobenius uniqueness conjecture remains
open and is not used.  The modular strong-approximation program of
Ref.~\cite{BGS16} supplies context only: this package uses no reduction modulo
a prime, local arithmetic datum, Euler factor, root number or automorphy claim.
