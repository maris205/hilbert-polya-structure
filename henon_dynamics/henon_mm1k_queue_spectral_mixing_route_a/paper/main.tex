\begin{abstract}
We give a convention-complete spectral atlas for the finite-capacity
continuous-time M/M/1/K birth--death queue.  The capacity $K$ includes the
customer in service, so the two reflecting endpoint diagonals differ from the
interior diagonal.  Detailed balance conjugates the generator to a symmetric
Jacobi matrix.  Its Robin endpoint equations yield every nonzero mode and the
closed gap
$\gamma_K=\lambda+\mu-2\sqrt{\lambda\mu}\cos(\pi/(K+1))$.  Reassembling the
modes gives the exact transient kernel and a reversible total-variation
mixing bound.  We also treat $K=0$, zero-rate absorbing faces, equal rates,
and the three controlled $K\to\infty$ regimes.  The infinite section records
only stationary, gap and mass-escape limits; no continuous-spectrum claim is
made.  Independent exact, symbolic, replay and hostile-mutation audits ship
with the theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and endpoint convention}
States are $\{0,1,\ldots,K\}$, with $K$ including the customer in service.
For $K\geq1$ and positive rates,
\begin{equation}
 Q_{0,0}=-\lambda,\quad Q_{0,1}=\lambda,\qquad
 Q_{n,n-1}=\mu,\ Q_{n,n}=-(\lambda+\mu),\ Q_{n,n+1}=\lambda,
 \label{eq:interior}
\end{equation}
for $0<n<K$, and $Q_{K,K-1}=\mu$, $Q_{K,K}=-\mu$.  Thus rows sum to zero.
At $K=0$ the generator is the one-by-one zero matrix.  Time is the physical
continuous-time Markov clock and $P_t=\exp(tQ)$ is row stochastic.

\begin{theorem}[Finite M/M/1/K spectral atlas]\label{thm:atlas}
Put $\rho=\lambda/\mu$ and $Z_K=\sum_{r=0}^K\rho^r$.  For $\lambda,\mu>0$,
\[
 \pi_n=\frac{\rho^n}{Z_K},\qquad 0\leq n\leq K,
\]
and $S=D_\pi^{1/2}QD_\pi^{-1/2}$ is symmetric tridiagonal with off-diagonal
$r=\sqrt{\lambda\mu}$.  Its eigenvalues are $0$ and
\[
 \nu_j=-(\lambda+\mu)+2r\cos\theta_j,
 \qquad \theta_j=\frac{j\pi}{K+1},\quad j=1,\ldots,K. \tag{2}
\]
For $j\geq1$, an eigenvector is proportional to
\[
 v_j(n)=\sin((n+1)\theta_j)-\sqrt{\mu/\lambda}\,\sin(n\theta_j),
 \quad 0\leq n\leq K,                                      \tag{3}
\]
and $v_0(n)=\sqrt{\pi_n}$.  With normalized vectors,
\[
 P_t(i,j)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi_j}{\pi_i}}
 \sum_{m=0}^{K}v_m(i)v_m(j)e^{\nu_m t},\qquad \nu_0=0.  \tag{4}
\]
The spectral gap is
\[
 \gamma_K=\lambda+\mu-2\sqrt{\lambda\mu}\cos\frac{\pi}{K+1},       \tag{5}
\]
and
\[
 \|P_t(i,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\rm TV}
 \leq\tfrac12\sqrt{\pi_i^{-1}-1}\,e^{-\gamma_Kt}.                    \tag{6}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The ratios $\pi_{n+1}/\pi_n=\lambda/\mu$ give detailed balance, hence the
displayed similarity and the common off-diagonal $r$.  The interior equation
for an eigenvector is the second-order recurrence
$r v_{n-1}-(\lambda+\mu)v_n+r v_{n+1}=\nu v_n$.
Writing $\nu=-(\lambda+\mu)+2r\cos\theta$ gives (3) after imposing the
left endpoint equation.  The right endpoint residual is
$(\lambda+\mu-2r\cos\theta)\sin((K+1)\theta)$, so the nonzero modes have
$\theta_j=j\pi/(K+1)$.  The constant stationary mode supplies $v_0$; symmetry
then gives an orthonormal basis and (4) by conjugating $e^{tS}$.  The smallest
positive decay rate is (5).  The reversible $L^2(\pi)$ spectral estimate
$\chi_i^2(t)\leq(\pi_i^{-1}-1)e^{-2\gamma_Kt}$ and Cauchy--Schwarz yield
(6).
\end{proof}

\section{What the finite formula buys us}
The endpoint convention is mathematically active: replacing $Q_{K,K}$ by the
interior diagonal changes the Robin equation and the angle grid.  Formula (4)
is an entrywise finite sum, so it gives nonnegative, row-stochastic transition
probabilities without an unevaluated matrix exponential.  It also makes the
gap bound auditable for every starting state, including the least likely wall
state.  At $\lambda=\mu$, $\pi$ is uniform and (5) becomes
$2\lambda(1-\cos(\pi/(K+1)))$.

\section{Singular faces and the capacity boundary}
If $K=0$, state $0$ is the sole absorbing state.  If $\lambda=0$ and $\mu>0$,
state $0$ absorbs; if $\mu=0$ and $\lambda>0$, state $K$ absorbs.  When both
rates vanish every state is absorbing, so there is no unique stationary law.
These faces are not limits of the positive-rate eigenvector formula.

For fixed positive rates, let $K\to\infty$.  If $\rho<1$, then
$\pi_n\to(1-\rho)\rho^n$ for each fixed $n$, while
$\gamma_K\to(\sqrt\mu-\sqrt\lambda)^2$.  If $\rho=1$, the finite law is
uniform but no probability stationary law exists on the half-line, and
\[
 \gamma_K=2\lambda\{1-\cos(\pi/(K+1))\}
 \sim \frac{\lambda\pi^2}{(K+1)^2}.                     \tag{7}
\]
If $\rho>1$, $\pi_n^{(K)}\to0$ for every fixed $n$: mass escapes toward the
reflecting wall, and the half-line chain has no stationary probability.  We do
not infer a continuous-spectrum decomposition from these elementary limits.
