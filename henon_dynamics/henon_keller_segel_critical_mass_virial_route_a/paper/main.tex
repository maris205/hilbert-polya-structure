\begin{abstract}
For the parabolic--elliptic Keller--Segel equation on the plane, we derive the
critical mass \(8\pi\) from two independent exact mechanisms.  A
mass-preserving dilation changes free energy by
\(2M(1-M/(8\pi))\log\lambda\), while Newtonian-kernel symmetrization gives
the finite-moment virial law \(I'=4M(1-M/(8\pi))\).  The latter forces every
supercritical classical finite-moment solution to leave that regime no later
than an explicit time.  At critical mass we verify the full translated and
dilated stationary family, then compute its logarithmically infinite second
moment; this hypothesis check removes an apparent conflict with the zero
virial slope.  We also derive the radial cumulative-mass equation and its
critical equilibrium.  Exact symbolic, rational, replay, and hostile receipts
audit coefficients but do not replace the PDE proof.  No literature-priority,
weak-continuation, or arithmetic-target claim is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{Equation, hypotheses, and main result}

We use the Newtonian convention
\begin{equation}
 \rho_t=\Delta\rho-\nabla\!\cdot(\rho\nabla c),\quad
 -\Delta c=\rho,\quad
 c(x)=-\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\Rtwo}\rho(y)\log|x-y|\,dy. \label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
Every assertion is made on an open interval where $\rho$ is nonnegative,
$C^1$ in time and $C^2$ in space, has finite mass, the logarithmic convolution
defining $c$ exists, and all displayed fluxes, derivatives, and cutoff limits
are integrable.  Barycenter conservation requires a finite first moment; the
virial law requires a finite second moment.  The free-energy identity further
requires finite entropy and interaction energy, strict positivity when
$M>0$, and finite dissipation.  The zero solution is treated separately
without invoking $\log\rho$.  These assertion-specific hypotheses are part of
the theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Critical-mass atlas]\label{thm:atlas}
Let $M=\int\rho$ and, when finite,
$B=\int x\rho$ and $I=\int |x|^2\rho$.  Along~\eqref{eq:pde},
\begin{align}
 M'&=0,\quad B'=0,                                   \label{eq:conserve}\\
 \frac{d}{dt}\F[\rho]
 &=-\int\rho\left|\nabla(\log\rho-c)\right|^2,\quad
 \F[\rho]=\int\rho\log\rho-\frac12\int\rho c,        \label{eq:diss}\\
 I'&=4M\left(1-\frac{M}{8\pi}\right).                \label{eq:virial}
\end{align}
For $\rho_\lambda(x)=\lambda^2\rho(\lambda x)$,
\begin{equation}
 \F[\rho_\lambda]-\F[\rho]
 =2M\left(1-\frac{M}{8\pi}\right)\log\lambda.        \label{eq:scale}
\end{equation}
If $M>8\pi$, a classical finite-second-moment solution cannot persist beyond
\begin{equation}
 T_*=\frac{2\pi I(0)}{M(M-8\pi)}.                    \label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
At $M=8\pi$, every $\lambda>0$ and $a\in\Rtwo$ give a stationary pair
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{\lambda,a}(x)=
 \frac{8\lambda^2}{(\lambda^2+|x-a|^2)^2},\quad
 c_{\lambda,a}(x)=-2\log(\lambda^2+|x-a|^2)+C.       \label{eq:profile}
\end{equation}
It has mass $8\pi$ and infinite second moment.
\end{theorem}

\section{Conservation, energy, and scaling}

Integrating~\eqref{eq:pde} proves mass conservation.  For the barycenter, diffusion
integrates to zero and
\[
 \int\rho(x)\nabla c(x)\,dx
 =-\frac1{2\pi}\iint \rho(x)\rho(y)
       \frac{x-y}{|x-y|^2}\,dx\,dy=0,
\]
because exchanging $x,y$ negates the integrand.  This proves~\eqref{eq:conserve}.

The first equation in~\eqref{eq:pde} is
\begin{equation}
 \rho_t=\nabla\!\cdot\{\rho\nabla(\log\rho-c)\}.      \label{eq:gradient}
\end{equation}
The first variation of $\F$ is $\log\rho+1-c$; the constant contributes
nothing because mass is conserved.  Integration by parts gives~\eqref{eq:diss}.

Dilation contributes $2M\log\lambda$ to $\int\rho\log\rho$.  Since
\[
 -\frac12\int\rho c
 =\frac1{4\pi}\iint\rho(x)\rho(y)\log|x-y|\,dx\,dy,
\]
changing variables in the double integral contributes
$-M^2\log\lambda/(4\pi)$.  Their sum is~\eqref{eq:scale}.  Hence free energy increases
along concentration dilations below $8\pi$, is scale invariant at $8\pi$,
and decreases along concentration dilations above $8\pi$.  This
scaling statement alone is not an existence or blow-up theorem.

\section{Virial obstruction and critical equilibria}

Diffusion contributes $4M$ to $I'$.  Put
$J=\int x\cdot\rho(x)\nabla c(x)\,dx$.  Kernel substitution and exchange of
the variables give the following identity.  To justify the exchange, first
exclude $|x-y|\le\varepsilon$; local integrability of the Newtonian gradient
and the stated decay and moment assumptions permit the cutoff limit:
\begin{align}
 2J&=-\frac1{2\pi}\iint\rho(x)\rho(y)
 \frac{x\cdot(x-y)+y\cdot(y-x)}{|x-y|^2}\,dx\,dy\\
 &=-\frac{M^2}{2\pi}.
\end{align}
The drift contribution is $2J$, proving~\eqref{eq:virial}.  For $M>8\pi$,
\[
 I(t)=I(0)-\frac{M(M-8\pi)}{2\pi}t.
\]
A nonnegative density cannot have negative second moment.  Thus the stated
classical finite-moment regime fails by~\eqref{eq:bound}, possibly earlier; the argument
does not select a weak continuation.

We verify~\eqref{eq:profile} directly.  By translation take $a=0$ and write $r=|x|$.
Radial differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
 -\left(c_{rr}+\frac1r c_r\right)
 =\frac{8\lambda^2}{(\lambda^2+r^2)^2}=\rho_{\lambda,0},
 \quad \partial_r\log\rho_{\lambda,0}=\partial_r c_{\lambda,0}. \label{eq:stationary}
\end{equation}
The flux in~\eqref{eq:gradient} vanishes.  Polar integration yields
\begin{equation}
 2\pi\int_0^R r\rho_{\lambda,0}(r)\,dr
 =\frac{8\pi R^2}{\lambda^2+R^2}\longrightarrow8\pi. \label{eq:mass}
\end{equation}
The truncated second moment is
\begin{equation}
 8\pi\lambda^2\left(
 \log\frac{\lambda^2+R^2}{\lambda^2}
 +\frac{\lambda^2}{\lambda^2+R^2}-1\right),          \label{eq:moment}
\end{equation}
and diverges as $R\to\infty$.  Therefore~\eqref{eq:virial}, whose proof requires finite
$I$, cannot be applied to this stationary family.  The endpoint is consistent.

\section{Radial cumulative equation and boundaries}

For a radial solution regular at the origin define
\begin{equation}
 m(r,t)=2\pi\int_0^r s\rho(s,t)\,ds,\quad n=m/(2\pi).
\end{equation}
Then $m(0,t)=m_r(0,t)=0$.  For $r>0$, the elliptic equation gives
$c_r=-m/(2\pi r)$.  Integrating the parabolic equation over a disc and using
$\rho=m_r/(2\pi r)$ yields
\begin{equation}
 m_t=m_{rr}-\frac1r m_r+\frac{m\,m_r}{2\pi r},
 \quad n_t=n_{rr}-\frac1r n_r+\frac{n\,n_r}{r}.      \label{eq:radial}
\end{equation}
For~\eqref{eq:profile}, $n=4r^2/(\lambda^2+r^2)$, and substitution makes the
right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:radial} zero.  At $r=0$,~\eqref{eq:radial} is
understood through the regular radial limit.

Zero density is stationary, but its logarithmic dissipation formula is not
invoked.  Below $8\pi$,~\eqref{eq:virial} has positive slope only while
the classical finite-moment solution exists; no convergence claim follows.
At $8\pi$, the profiles~\eqref{eq:profile} lie outside that moment class.  As
$\lambda\downarrow0$ they converge weakly to $8\pi\delta_a$, outside the
smooth phase space.  As $\lambda\to\infty$ they tend pointwise to zero while
mass spreads to infinity.  Above $8\pi$,~\eqref{eq:bound} is a classical-persistence
obstruction, not a complete measure-solution theory.
