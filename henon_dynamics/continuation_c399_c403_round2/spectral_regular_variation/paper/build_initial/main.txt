               Regular-variation limits for nonmultiplicative
                        divisibility Gram matrices


                                         5 September 2026


                                              Abstract
         Let σ < 1/2, write ρ = 1 − 2σ, and let L be a positive measurable slowly varying function,
     bounded above and away from zero on compact subintervals of [1, ∞). We consider the
     divisibility matrices TN (r, m) = (r/m)−σ L(r/m) when m | r ≤ N , with all other entries
     zero. Without assuming multiplicativity, we prove that the normalized Gram operators
     ρTN∗ TN /(N ρ L(N )2 ) converge to the classical LCM operator with entries (mn)σ / lcm(m, n)
     in every Schatten ideal Sq such that qρ > 1, including 0 < q < 1. The range is exact: for
     qρ ≤ 1, the difference does not belong to Sq for any finite N . A positive-congruence argument
     gives eigenvalue bounds Cη j −η , uniform in N , for every 0 < η < ρ. These bounds turn
     an explicit regular-variation limit of the Gram entries into operator-norm and then ideal
     convergence. The sharpness uses the known eigenvalue asymptotics of the LCM operator.
     We also obtain cumulative-normalization, fixed-index singular-value, and spectral-moment
     consequences, allowing nonconstant oscillatory slowly varying factors.

Keywords: divisibility matrix; regular variation; LCM kernel; Schatten ideal; singular-value
asymptotics.


1    Introduction
Divisibility matrices replace the difference of two indices in an ordinary Toeplitz matrix by their
integer ratio. Their Gram matrices therefore couple analytic growth of coefficients with the
arithmetic relation of having a common multiple. For power coefficients, the limiting kernel
is expressed by the least common multiple. The question studied here is whether that same
kernel persists when the coefficients are modified by an arbitrary slowly varying factor, without
a multiplicative assumption, and in which operator ideals the limit holds.
    Fix σ < 1/2 and set ρ = 1−2σ > 0. Throughout the paper, L : [1, ∞) → (0, ∞) is measurable
and slowly varying, and both L and 1/L are bounded on every compact subinterval of [1, ∞).

small loss suffices to obtain every ideal in the exact range qρ > 1.

1.1      Antecedents and ownership of the inputs
Hilberdink developed regular-variation limits for multiplicative Toeplitz matrices [2]. For
completely multiplicative coefficients, his Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3 give Hilbert–Schmidt
and trace-norm convergence under the corresponding growth conditions. Theorems 3.1 and 3.2
treat multiplicative coefficients with additional local-series and summability hypotheses. Regular
variation of the cumulative coefficient energy is a central hypothesis there. Our coefficients need
not be multiplicative, but we require the specific pointwise form k −σ L(k); neither statement
should be read as containing the other in full generality.
    Hilberdink and Pushnitski studied the LCM family that contains (1.2), proving its spectral
asymptotics and relating it to power-coefficient multiplicative Toeplitz matrices [3]. Theorem 2.1
in the accessed 2021 preprint version, arXiv:2110.14323v1, states the power-coefficient Gram
convergence for even integers q satisfying qρ > 1. When L = 1, Theorem 1.1 gives every real q
in that range. This is a comparison with that explicitly identified version, not a claim that the
parity restriction remains an open problem in subsequent literature. The known LCM eigenvalue
asymptotic and its arithmetic factorization remain attributed to Hilberdink and Pushnitski.

0 < q < 1 as well as for Banach ideals.
    Sections 2 and 3 set up the classical inputs and prove the entrywise limit, including its
small-index tail. Section 4 obtains uniform spectral control and operator-norm convergence.
Section 5 proves the full ideal range and sharpness. Section 6 gives the normalization and
singular-value consequences.

Lemma 2.2 (Singular-value sum bound). For compact U, V and integers j, k ≥ 1,
                                 sj+k−1 (U + V ) ≤ sj (U ) + sk (V ).                       (2.8)

Proof. Choose finite-rank approximants F, G with ranks less than j, k, respectively. Then
rank(F + G) ≤ j + k − 2, and
                             sj+k−1 (U + V ) ≤ ∥U − F ∥ + ∥V − G∥.
Taking the two infima proves (2.8). For closure under sums, take k = j, use monotonicity for
the intervening even index, and apply
                   (x + y)q ≤ cq (xq + y q ),        cq = max{1, 2q−1 },     x, y ≥ 0.
The resulting summable majorant proves the assertion for all q > 0. Negation does not change

   For positive compact operators, singular values and eigenvalues agree. We also use the
standard min–max implications 0 ≤ U ≤ V ⇒ λj (U ) ≤ λj (V ) and
                                    |λj (U ) − λj (V )| ≤ ∥U − V ∥                          (2.9)
for positive compact U, V . All operator inequalities here are in quadratic-form order. Entrywise
domination of nonnegative matrices is not, by itself, such an inequality.

being substituted for slow variation.


7     Scope of the result
The conclusion concerns one normalized Gram family with truncation parameter N . Its arith-
metic input is the divisibility relation and the already known LCM operator. The extension
established here is the allowance of arbitrary admissible pointwise slowly varying factors without
multiplicativity, together with convergence throughout the exact positive-exponent ideal range.
The uniform spectral tails and the consequences of Section 6 are components of that same result.
    Several boundaries are worth stating explicitly. First, the hypotheses do not merely require
regular variation of k≤N |a(k)|2 for an arbitrary arithmetic sequence. The pointwise repre-
                      P

sentation a(k) = k −σ L(k) is what controls the two rescaled factors in each off-diagonal Gram
entry. The more general multiplicative settings of [2] therefore remain distinct from the present
theorem. Second, no convergence rate uniform over all admissible slowly varying functions is
asserted. Third, the N -independent eigenvalue bound is proved for every η < ρ, not at its
endpoint. Fourth, no simultaneous large-index relative singular-value asymptotic is claimed.
    Finally, the LCM eigenvalue asymptotics, their spectral constant, and the prime-based
factorization used to obtain them belong to [3]. The comparison with its even-integer Gram
statement is bound to the accessed 2021 preprint version. The source comparison here does
not certify global priority or an open problem in the later literature. No target Euler factors,
functional equation, prescribed zero set, or Hilbert–Pólya realization is identified by this Gram-
limit theorem.
