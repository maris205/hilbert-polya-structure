\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Divisibility matrices replace the difference of two indices in an ordinary
Toeplitz matrix by their integer ratio. Their Gram matrices therefore
couple analytic growth of coefficients with the arithmetic relation of
having a common multiple. For power coefficients, the limiting kernel
is expressed by the least common multiple. The question studied here is
whether that same kernel persists when the coefficients are modified by
an arbitrary slowly varying factor, without a multiplicative assumption,
and in which operator ideals the limit holds.

Fix $\sigma<1/2$ and set $\rho=1-2\sigma>0$. Throughout the paper,
$L:[1,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ is measurable and slowly varying, and both
$L$ and $1/L$ are bounded on every compact subinterval of $[1,\infty)$.
With $a(k)=k^{-\sigma}L(k)$, define on $\Hsp$
\begin{equation}
 T_N(r,m)=
 \begin{cases}
 a(r/m),&m\mid r,\quad 1\le m\le r\le N,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 A_N=\frac{\rho}{N^\rho L(N)^2}T_N^*T_N.
 \label{eq:operators}
\end{equation}
Thus $T_N$ and $A_N$ are finite matrices extended by zero to the same
infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. Let $E_\sigma$ denote the classical
LCM operator
\begin{equation}
 E_\sigma(m,n)=\frac{(mn)^\sigma}{\lcm(m,n)},\qquad m,n\ge1.
 \label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
Its compactness, positivity, and eigenvalue asymptotics are known; the
precise input is recalled in Theorem~\ref{thm:classical}.

For $q>0$, membership in $\Sq$ means summability of the $q$th powers
of the singular values. We use the standard quasi-norm
$\qnorm{K}=(\sum_j s_j(K)^q)^{1/q}$; it is a norm when $q\ge1$.
All eigenvalue sequences below are ordered nonincreasingly, with
multiplicity, and finite sequences are continued by zeros.

\begin{theorem}[Regular-variation Gram limit]
\label{thm:main}
Under the hypotheses above, the following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $0<\eta<\rho$, there is a constant
$C_\eta=C(\sigma,L,\eta)$, independent of $N$ and $j$, such that
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_j(A_N)\le C_\eta j^{-\eta},\qquad
 \lambda_j(E_\sigma)\le C_\eta j^{-\eta}
 \quad(N,j\ge1).
 \label{eq:main-tail}
\end{equation}
\item $\opnorm{A_N-E_\sigma}\to0$ as $N\to\infty$.
\item For every real $q>0$ with $q\rho>1$,
\begin{equation}
 \qnorm{A_N-E_\sigma}\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:main-ideal}
\end{equation}
If $q\rho\le1$, then $A_N-E_\sigma\notin\Sq$ for every $N$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The coefficient condition is pointwise regular variation, not
multiplicativity. In particular, it admits
$L(x)=(1+\log x)^\beta$ for every real $\beta$, and also positive
oscillatory slowly varying functions without a limit. The endpoint
$\eta=\rho$ is not asserted in the uniform bound
\eqref{eq:main-tail}; an arbitrarily small loss suffices to obtain every
ideal in the exact range $q\rho>1$.

\subsection{Antecedents and ownership of the inputs}

Hilberdink developed regular-variation limits for multiplicative
Toeplitz matrices~\cite{Hilberdink2017}. For completely multiplicative
coefficients, his Theorem~2.2 and Corollary~2.3 give Hilbert--Schmidt and
trace-norm convergence under the corresponding growth conditions.
Theorems~3.1 and~3.2 treat multiplicative coefficients with additional
local-series and summability hypotheses. Regular variation of the
cumulative coefficient energy is a central hypothesis there. Our
coefficients need not be multiplicative, but we require the specific
pointwise form $k^{-\sigma}L(k)$; neither statement should be read as
containing the other in full generality.

Hilberdink and Pushnitski studied the LCM family that contains
\eqref{eq:kernel}, proving its spectral asymptotics and relating it to
power-coefficient multiplicative Toeplitz matrices
\cite{HilberdinkPushnitski2023}. Theorem~2.1 in the accessed
2021 preprint version, arXiv:2110.14323v1, states the power-coefficient
Gram convergence for even integers $q$ satisfying $q\rho>1$.
When $L=1$, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} gives every real $q$ in that range.
This is a comparison with that explicitly identified version, not a
claim that the parity restriction remains an open problem in subsequent
literature. The known LCM eigenvalue asymptotic and its arithmetic
factorization remain attributed to Hilberdink and Pushnitski.

The regular-variation tools are the classical uniform convergence
theorem and Potter bounds~\cite{BinghamGoldieTeugels1987};
the compact-operator tools are also standard~\cite{Simon2005}.
The argument specific to the present family combines uniform domination
by a shifted LCM kernel with a positive diagonal congruence. This gives
an $N$-independent summable spectral tail after choosing an exponent
strictly inside the allowed range. Counting-measure dominated
convergence then works for $0<q<1$ as well as for Banach ideals.

Sections~\ref{sec:framework} and~\ref{sec:gram} set up the classical
inputs and prove the entrywise limit, including its small-index tail.
Section~\ref{sec:tails} obtains uniform spectral control and
operator-norm convergence. Section~\ref{sec:ideal} proves the full
ideal range and sharpness. Section~\ref{sec:consequences} gives the
normalization and singular-value consequences.
