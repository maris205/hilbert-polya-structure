Let $\sigma<1/2$, write $\rho=1-2\sigma$, and let $L$ be a positive
measurable slowly varying function, bounded above and away from zero on
compact subintervals of $[1,\infty)$. We consider the divisibility matrices
$T_N(r,m)=(r/m)^{-\sigma}L(r/m)$ when $m\mid r\le N$, with all other
entries zero. Without assuming multiplicativity, we prove that the
normalized Gram operators $\rho T_N^*T_N/(N^\rho L(N)^2)$ converge to
the classical LCM operator with entries $(mn)^\sigma/\lcm(m,n)$ in
every Schatten ideal $\mathcal S_q$ such that $q\rho>1$, including
$0<q<1$. The range is exact: for $q\rho\le1$, the difference does not
belong to $\mathcal S_q$ for any finite $N$. A positive-congruence
argument gives eigenvalue bounds $C_\eta j^{-\eta}$, uniform in $N$,
for every $0<\eta<\rho$. These bounds turn an explicit regular-variation
limit of the Gram entries into operator-norm and then ideal convergence.
The sharpness uses the known eigenvalue asymptotics of the LCM operator.
We also obtain cumulative-normalization, fixed-index singular-value,
and spectral-moment consequences, allowing nonconstant oscillatory
slowly varying factors.
