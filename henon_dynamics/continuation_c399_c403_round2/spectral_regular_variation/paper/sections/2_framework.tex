\section{Framework and classical inputs}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Regular variation}

A positive measurable function $L$ is slowly varying at infinity if
\begin{equation}
 \frac{L(tx)}{L(x)}\longrightarrow1\qquad(x\to\infty)
 \label{eq:slow}
\end{equation}
for each fixed $t>0$. The measurability and local upper and lower bounds
in our standing assumptions are retained throughout. The classical
uniform convergence theorem gives, for every $0<c<C<\infty$,
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{t\in[c,C]}
 \left|\frac{L(tN)}{L(N)}-1\right|\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:uct}
\end{equation}
Only values with $tN\ge1$ will be used. Potter's estimate, together
with the local bounds, gives for every $\varepsilon>0$ a constant
$K_\varepsilon$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \frac{L(x)}{L(y)}\le K_\varepsilon
 \max\left\{\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^\varepsilon,
             \left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^{-\varepsilon}\right\}
 \qquad(x,y\ge1).
 \label{eq:potter}
\end{equation}
These are standard regular-variation facts
\cite{BinghamGoldieTeugels1987}; the global form
\eqref{eq:potter} under local upper and lower bounds is also stated in
Section~1.1 of~\cite{Hilberdink2017}.

For clarity, the extension of eventual Potter bounds to all $x,y\ge1$
does not discard any behavior on a compact interval. Choose $b\ge1$
so that the eventual estimate holds when both arguments are at least
$b$. If $x\ge b>y$, factor the ratio as
$[L(x)/L(b)][L(b)/L(y)]$ and use the positive minimum bound on
$[1,b]$; then $(x/b)^\varepsilon\le(x/y)^\varepsilon$.
If $x<b\le y$, use the analogous factorization and the upper bound on
$[1,b]$. If both arguments lie in $[1,b]$, their ratio is bounded by
the local upper-to-lower bound, while the maximum on the right of
\eqref{eq:potter} is at least one. Enlarging the constant proves the
displayed global estimate.

\subsection{The classical LCM operator}

We use the following specialization of an existing spectral theorem.
It is an input, not a result proved anew here.

\begin{theorem}[Hilberdink--Pushnitski]
\label{thm:classical}
For every real $s<1/2$, the matrix
\begin{equation}
 E_s(m,n)=\frac{(mn)^s}{\lcm(m,n)}
 \label{eq:shifted-kernel}
\end{equation}
defines a positive, compact, injective operator on $\Hsp$. There is
a constant $\kappa(s,1)>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_j(E_s)\sim\kappa(s,1)j^{-(1-2s)}
 \qquad(j\to\infty).
 \label{eq:classical-asymptotic}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every real $q>0$,
\begin{equation}
 E_s\in\Sq\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q(1-2s)>1.
 \label{eq:classical-threshold}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

This is Theorem~1.1 of~\cite{HilberdinkPushnitski2023}, specialized
to the second kernel parameter equal to one. The theorem numbering and
the specialization were checked in arXiv:2110.14323v1 (2021); the
bibliography also identifies the subsequent journal publication.
The equivalence~\eqref{eq:classical-threshold} follows immediately
from~\eqref{eq:classical-asymptotic} and the positive constant there.
No new value or formula for $\kappa(s,1)$ is claimed.

\subsection{Singular values for all positive ideal exponents}

For a compact operator $K$, write $s_1(K)\ge s_2(K)\ge\cdots\ge0$
for its singular values. We recall the standard inequality below and
include its short proof because it is the step used in the
quasi-Banach range. Approximation numbers on a Hilbert space satisfy
\begin{equation}
 s_j(K)=\inf_{\rank F<j}\opnorm{K-F}.
 \label{eq:approximation}
\end{equation}
This follows from truncating the singular-value decomposition and the
min--max principle; see, for example,~\cite{Simon2005} for the
compact-operator framework.

\begin{lemma}[Singular-value sum bound]
\label{lem:sv-sum}
For compact $U,V$ and integers $j,k\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 s_{j+k-1}(U+V)\le s_j(U)+s_k(V).
 \label{eq:sv-sum}
\end{equation}
For every $q>0$, the class $\Sq$ is closed under sums and differences.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose finite-rank approximants $F,G$ with ranks less than $j,k$,
respectively. Then $\rank(F+G)\le j+k-2$, and
\[
 s_{j+k-1}(U+V)\le\opnorm{U-F}+\opnorm{V-G}.
\]
Taking the two infima proves~\eqref{eq:sv-sum}. For closure under
sums, take $k=j$, use monotonicity for the intervening even index,
and apply
\[
 (x+y)^q\le c_q(x^q+y^q),\qquad
 c_q=\max\{1,2^{q-1}\},\quad x,y\ge0.
\]
The resulting summable majorant proves the assertion for all $q>0$.
Negation does not change singular values, so differences are included.
\end{proof}

For positive compact operators, singular values and eigenvalues agree.
We also use the standard min--max implications
$0\le U\le V\Rightarrow\lambda_j(U)\le\lambda_j(V)$ and
\begin{equation}
 |\lambda_j(U)-\lambda_j(V)|\le\opnorm{U-V}
 \label{eq:eigen-lipschitz}
\end{equation}
for positive compact $U,V$. All operator inequalities here are in
quadratic-form order. Entrywise domination of nonnegative matrices is
not, by itself, such an inequality.
