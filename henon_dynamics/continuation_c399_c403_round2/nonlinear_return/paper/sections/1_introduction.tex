\section{Introduction}

Global sums over periodic points can be easier to compute than the
individual points, but a finite computation at each period does not by
itself give a finite representation valid at all periods. This distinction
is explicit for a polynomial H\'enon map. Its period-$n$ orbit equations
form a zero-dimensional complete intersection of length $d^n$, where $d$
is the degree of the nonlinear polynomial. A separate quotient algebra
therefore gives a finite answer for every $n$, while its dimension grows
with $n$. The question considered here is whether one fixed matrix can
represent all of these answers for a prescribed polynomial coordinate
weight.

The observable is the all-complex signed stability sum. At a nondegenerate
fixed point of $H^n$, its denominator is $\det(I-DH^n)$, with no absolute
value. The numerator is the product of a single one-variable polynomial
$q$ along the first coordinates of the orbit. At a multiple fixed point,
the pointwise quotient is undefined. We use the sum of local
Grothendieck residues as the definition at such a parameter. This choice
retains the complete fixed scheme and makes the parameter dependence
algebraic.

\paragraph{Classical inputs.}
The global-residue algorithms of
Cattani, Dickenstein, and Sturmfels
\citep[Theorem~2.3 and Lemma~4.2 of the author preprint]{cattani1996residues}
provide Laurent coefficient and Gr\"obner normal-form descriptions for a
fixed polynomial system. Their pure-power initial-form hypothesis holds
for the cyclic H\'enon equations. The conversion from the cyclic Jacobian
to the return derivative is the scalar cyclic Hill identity, contained
in Molinari's block-tridiagonal determinant formula
\citep[Eq.~(1) of the author preprint v3]{molinari2008determinants}.
Both ingredients are used here with their original ownership.

A preceding time-ordered H\'enon calculation in the accompanying research
notes \citep[Lemma~4 and Theorem~5]{timeordered2026notes} already records
the Hill relation for its family, the all-period unit-weight residue
cancellation, a numerator given by the return-derivative trace, and a
signed scheme-residue convention at degenerate points. The present result
does not count any of these facts as a new contribution. Likewise, the
Fredholm determinant argument used later is classical operator theory
\citep[Sec.~3 of the author manuscript]{bornemann2010fredholm}.

\paragraph{Uniform finite-state result.}
The additional step is a local bound in the Laurent expansion. Each term
has a left-going and a right-going nonnegative exponent at every cyclic
position. Nonvanishing imposes a system of nearest-neighbour inequalities.
A column-stochastic matrix redistributes these exponents, and the number
of sites that can reach a fixed site in $k$ steps is at most $2k+1$.
The resulting geometric series bounds every local exponent by a constant
depending only on $d$ and $\deg q$. After this bound, the residue sum is
the trace of a finite edge-state matrix.

More precisely, for every degree bound $m$ we construct one matrix $W$ of
size $(B+1)^2$, with
\[
 B=\left\lfloor
 \frac{\max(0,m-d+1)(d+1)}{(d-1)^2}
 \right\rfloor,
\]
such that the signed residue traces satisfy $\tau_n=-\Tr(W^n)$ for all
$n\ge1$. The same matrix works at every period, including periods one and
two with their coincident neighbours. Its entries are polynomial in all
source parameters, so specialization does not require a genericity
assumption. The associated generating function is the reciprocal of
$\det(I-zW)$.

The comparison made here is between this uniform construction and the
specific fixed-system residue and Hill inputs just cited. No assertion
of global priority follows from that comparison. The theorem concerns
the stated coordinate-weight observation on all complex fixed points.
It does not construct a natural transfer operator for real dynamics.
Sections~\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:flow} give the complete proof.
Section~\ref{sec:consequences} treats quadratic weights and the ordinary
trace-class boundary, and Section~\ref{sec:scope} records the scope and
finite verification evidence.
