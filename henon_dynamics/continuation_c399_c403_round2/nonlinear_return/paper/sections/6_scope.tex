\section{Scope and reproducibility}\label{sec:scope}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} replaces the period-dependent cyclic quotient
algebras by a single edge-state matrix for a fixed map and coordinate
weight. Its local bound is uniform in the period, but the matrix size
still depends on the degree bound $m$. No minimal-dimension assertion
is made. A different degree bound for the same polynomial can give a
larger matrix with the same power traces and generating function.

The scope is tied to both the domain and the numerator. The residue
includes every finite complex fixed point, with its scheme structure.
Restricting to a real invariant set would change the sum. An absolute
stability denominator is not the signed holomorphic denominator in
\eqref{eq:pointwise-trace}. The argument also does not cover arbitrary
two-variable weights, nonpolynomial weights, or compositions with a
time-dependent polynomial. These changes are outside the theorem,
irrespective of whether a finite representation might exist for some
of them.

The quadratic calculation displays a loss of information in this
observable: the coefficients $c,w$ in
\eqref{eq:quadratic-parameters} disappear from
\eqref{eq:quadratic-determinant}. The residue determinant therefore
does not encode all parameters of the underlying map and weight.
Its rationality, and its coefficient ring in
\eqref{eq:coefficient-ring}, do not supply target Euler factors,
root numbers, automorphy, a correspondence of zero divisors, or a
Hilbert--P\'olya realization.

\paragraph{Exact finite checks.}
The accompanying files \nolinkurl{exact_check.py} and
\nolinkurl{EXACT_CHECK_OUTPUT.json} record exact symbolic checks
performed during proof development. Across seven choices of $p,q,a$
with $d=2,3,4$, there are 27 comparisons through period five between
the edge-matrix power traces and the normal-form coefficient in
\eqref{eq:normal-form}. Two nondegenerate fixed schemes are also
checked by constructing quotient-algebra multiplication matrices
and evaluating $\Tr(m_{g_n}m_{\det J_n}^{-1})$. The checks assert
that this inverse exists in those examples. The output includes six
symbolic Hill identities, for $n=1,\ldots,6$, and a direct symbolic
calculation of the quadratic determinant with free coefficients.

The stored run used Python~3.12.3 and SymPy~1.14.0 and exited
successfully. These checks test signs, local coefficients, short
periods, and an explicit degeneracy. They were not rerun to produce
the manuscript, and they do not establish its all-period
quantifier. That quantifier follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-flow} and the bijection
\eqref{eq:edge-bijection}. No empirical data or stochastic numerical
experiment is used in the proof.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The finite-state result follows from a local fact about every
nonzero Laurent configuration: nearest-neighbour propagation and
the factor $d>1$ impose a period-independent exponent bound.
This converts the complete global residue sum into the power trace
of one polynomial matrix. The residue definition keeps the result
valid at degenerate parameters, while the Hill sign fixes the
reciprocal determinant and the ordinary trace-class boundary.
