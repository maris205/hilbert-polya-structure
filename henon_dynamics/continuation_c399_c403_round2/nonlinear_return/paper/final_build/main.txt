                 Finite-state residue traces for Hénon maps
                    with polynomial coordinate weights


                                         5 September 2026
                                  Internal research manuscript C402



                                              Abstract
         We construct a finite matrix that represents every global residue trace of a complex
     Hénon map with a fixed polynomial coordinate weight. For H(x, y) = (p(x) − ay, x), where
     p is monic of degree d ≥ 2 and a ̸= 0, the weight at a point is q(x) with deg q ≤ m. The
     cyclic fixed-point scheme at period n has length dn , but the matrix constructed here has size
     (B + 1)2 , where B = ⌊max(0, m − d + 1)(d + 1)/(d − 1)2 ⌋ is independent of n. Its entries
     are integer-coefficient polynomials in the parameters. The proof expands a global residue
     into nearest-neighbour exponent flows and bounds each local flow by a column-stochastic
     propagation argument. The signed stability trace is − Tr(W n ), and the associated generating
     function is det(I − zW )−1 . All identities remain valid at degenerate parameters when the
     trace is defined by the full local-residue sum. For quadratic maps and quadratic coordinate
     weights, we obtain an explicit factorization of degree at most four. We also characterize when
     this trace sequence has an ordinary trace-class representative: exactly when W is nilpotent.

Keywords: Hénon maps; global residues; polynomial weights; transfer matrices; periodic points;
dynamical determinants.


1    Introduction
Global sums over periodic points can be easier to compute than the individual points, but a
finite computation at each period does not by itself give a finite representation valid at all
periods. This distinction is explicit for a polynomial Hénon map. Its period-n orbit equations
form a zero-dimensional complete intersection of length dn , where d is the degree of the nonlinear
polynomial. A separate quotient algebra therefore gives a finite answer for every n, while its
dimension grows with n. The question considered here is whether one fixed matrix can represent
all of these answers for a prescribed polynomial coordinate weight.
     The observable is the all-complex signed stability sum. At a nondegenerate fixed point of
H , its denominator is det(I − DH n ), with no absolute value. The numerator is the product of
   n

a single one-variable polynomial q along the first coordinates of the orbit. At a multiple fixed
point, the pointwise quotient is undefined. We use the sum of local Grothendieck residues as the
definition at such a parameter. This choice retains the complete fixed scheme and makes the
parameter dependence algebraic.

Classical inputs. The global-residue algorithms of Cattani, Dickenstein, and Sturmfels [2,
Theorem 2.3 and Lemma 4.2 of the author preprint] provide Laurent coefficient and Gröbner
normal-form descriptions for a fixed polynomial system. Their pure-power initial-form hypothesis
holds for the cyclic Hénon equations. The conversion from the cyclic Jacobian to the return
derivative is the scalar cyclic Hill identity, contained in Molinari’s block-tridiagonal determinant

ownership.
   A preceding time-ordered Hénon calculation in the accompanying research notes [4, Lemma 4
and Theorem 5] already records the Hill relation for its family, the all-period unit-weight residue
cancellation, a numerator given by the return-derivative trace, and a signed scheme-residue
convention at degenerate points. The present result does not count any of these facts as a new
contribution. Likewise, the Fredholm determinant argument used later is classical operator
theory [1, Sec. 3 of the author manuscript].

Uniform finite-state result. The additional step is a local bound in the Laurent expansion.
Each term has a left-going and a right-going nonnegative exponent at every cyclic position.
Nonvanishing imposes a system of nearest-neighbour inequalities. A column-stochastic matrix
redistributes these exponents, and the number of sites that can reach a fixed site in k steps is at
most 2k + 1. The resulting geometric series bounds every local exponent by a constant depending
only on d and deg q. After this bound, the residue sum is the trace of a finite edge-state matrix.
    More precisely, for every degree bound m we construct one matrix W of size (B + 1)2 , with

The finite-state residue formula does not itself supply an ordinary operator with traces τn . In
particular, replacing W by −W would give (−1)n Tr(W n ), not − Tr(W n ) for every n. There is
a complete criterion for the existence of any ordinary trace-class representative of the frozen
sequence.

while a nonconstant reciprocal polynomial has a pole. It is a consequence of the specific residue
sequence, not a separate construction of a dynamical operator. It does not exclude supertraces,
distributional traces, different observables, or operators with absolute stability weights.


6     Scope and reproducibility
Theorem 2.2 replaces the period-dependent cyclic quotient algebras by a single edge-state matrix
for a fixed map and coordinate weight. Its local bound is uniform in the period, but the matrix
size still depends on the degree bound m. No minimal-dimension assertion is made. A different
degree bound for the same polynomial can give a larger matrix with the same power traces and
generating function.
    The scope is tied to both the domain and the numerator. The residue includes every finite
complex fixed point, with its scheme structure. Restricting to a real invariant set would change
the sum. An absolute stability denominator is not the signed holomorphic denominator in
(2.8). The argument also does not cover arbitrary two-variable weights, nonpolynomial weights,
or compositions with a time-dependent polynomial. These changes are outside the theorem,
irrespective of whether a finite representation might exist for some of them.
    The quadratic calculation displays a loss of information in this observable: the coefficients c, w
in (5.1) disappear from (5.2). The residue determinant therefore does not encode all parameters
of the underlying map and weight. Its rationality, and its coefficient ring in (2.15), do not
supply target Euler factors, root numbers, automorphy, a correspondence of zero divisors, or a
Hilbert–Pólya realization.

Conclusion. The finite-state result follows from a local fact about every nonzero Laurent
configuration: nearest-neighbour propagation and the factor d > 1 impose a period-independent
exponent bound. This converts the complete global residue sum into the power trace of one
polynomial matrix. The residue definition keeps the result valid at degenerate parameters, while
the Hill sign fixes the reciprocal determinant and the ordinary trace-class boundary.
