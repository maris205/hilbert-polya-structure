\section{Diagonal iteration and the resonant boundary}\label{sec:diagonal}

\subsection{One fixed dynamical system}

Continue to assume that $d$ is not a power of $p$. For positive integers
$b,s$, define the single polynomial self-map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diagonalmap}
 S=H^{-b}\circ\Phi_s:\A^2_k\longrightarrow\A^2_k.
\end{equation}
The exponent $m$ in $S^m$ now advances both original clocks, in the
fixed ratio $(n,r)=(bm,sm)$. This is different from keeping $n$ fixed.

\begin{proposition}[Diagonal point counts and dynamical zeta]
\label{prop:diagonal}
For every $m\geq1$, the fixed-point scheme of $S^m$ is finite and
reduced, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diagonalcount}
 \#\Fix(S^m)(k)=\Lambda^m,
 \qquad \Lambda=\max\{q^{2s},q^s d^b\}.
\end{equation}
Hence its Artin--Mazur zeta function, defined by the fixed-point counts,
is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diagonalzeta}
 \zeta_S(t)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{m\geq1}\#\Fix(S^m)(k)\frac{t^m}{m}\right)
 =\frac{1}{1-\Lambda t}.
\end{equation}
Its compactly supported cohomological determinant is instead
$(1-q^{2s}t)^{-1}$; these two rational functions agree if $d^b<q^s$
and differ if $d^b>q^s$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
All coefficients of $H$ lie in $\F_q$, so for each $s\geq1$,
\[
 f(y^{q^s})=f(y)^{q^s},\qquad a^{q^s}=a.
\]
Substitution into \eqref{eq:map} shows $H\circ\Phi_s=\Phi_s\circ H$.
The inverse $H^{-1}$ is also defined over $\F_q$, or one can compose
this equality with $H^{-1}$ to obtain commutation for the inverse.
It follows by induction on $m$ that
\[
 S^m=H^{-bm}\circ\Phi_{sm}.
\]
The equation $S^m(P)=P$ is equivalent, as an equalizer scheme, to
$H^{bm}(P)=\Phi_{sm}(P)$ after applying the automorphism $H^{bm}$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:allclocks} therefore give
\begin{align*}
 \#\Fix(S^m)(k)
 &=M_H(bm,sm)\\
 &=\max\{d^{bm}q^{sm},q^{2sm}\}\\
 &=\left(\max\{d^bq^s,q^{2s}\}\right)^m.
\end{align*}
They also give finiteness and reducedness. Substituting this count
in the formal logarithm of the zeta gives
$\sum_{m\geq1}\Lambda^m t^m/m=-\log(1-\Lambda t)$,
which proves \eqref{eq:diagonalzeta}.

By \eqref{eq:affinecohom}, only the top compactly supported group
contributes to the cohomological determinant. The pullback by $S$ acts
there as $q^{2s}$, since $H^{-b}$ acts as one. Thus the alternating
determinant is $(1-q^{2s}t)^{-1}$. There is no equality $d^b=q^s$
under the degree hypothesis. In the smaller-degree regime $\Lambda=q^{2s}$;
in the larger-degree regime $\Lambda=d^bq^s>q^{2s}$.
\end{proof}

There is no conflict with an eventual trace theorem for a fixed
correspondence. The powers $S^m$ change the H\'enon correspondence as
well as its Frobenius twist. If $d^b>q^s$, this diagonal sequence stays
on the short-twist side of the threshold at every $m$.
The rational function \eqref{eq:diagonalzeta} is a determinant identity
for this source dynamical system, with no proposed identification with
an external arithmetic Euler factor.

\subsection{A nonlinear resonant counterexample}

We now allow $d$ to be a power of $p$, solely to test the excluded case.
The nonresonance condition was used at a precise point in
Lemma~\ref{lem:minuslength}. If $D=Q$, the second local equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resonantlocaleq}
 v^D-v^QC=v^Q(1-C).
\end{equation}
The factor $1-C$ can fail to be a unit. The formula for its local
length is then no longer determined by the two degrees alone through
the preceding argument.

\begin{proposition}[Failure at equal degrees]\label{prop:resonance}
Over $\F_3$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:resonantmap}
 H(x,y)=(y,y^3+y^2-x).
\end{equation}
For $n=r=1$ one has $d^n=q^r=3$, but
\[
 M_H(1,1)=6\ne9=\max\{d q,q^2\}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The equations $H(x,y)=(x^3,y^3)$ are
\[
 y=x^3,\qquad y^3+y^2-x=y^3.
\]
After substituting the first equation, the second becomes $x^6-x=0$.
Thus the coordinate ring of the equalizer is
$k[x]/(x^6-x)$. In characteristic three the derivative of $x^6-x$
is $-1$. This degree-six polynomial has six distinct roots over $k$,
each of which determines exactly one $y=x^3$. The count is six.
\end{proof}

The term $y^2$ in \eqref{eq:resonantmap} makes this a nonadditive
polynomial example. Affine reducedness is still guaranteed by
Lemma~\ref{lem:affine}; it is the degree-only formula at the boundary,
not reducedness, that fails. A classification of resonant coefficients
would require additional local information and is not part of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
