\section{The exact trace threshold and fixed-time slices}\label{sec:slices}

For this section assume that $d$ is not a power of $p$. This hypothesis
ensures that the count is available at every positive Frobenius power
for any fixed $n$, with no missing resonant coefficient in a generating
series.

\subsection{Compactly supported trace and its exact defect}

Fix a prime $\ell\ne p$. The isomorphism $H^{-n}$ and the finite
morphism $\Phi_r$ are proper, so their composite acts by pullback on
compactly supported \texorpdfstring{\'etale}{etale} cohomology. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cohomtrace}
 T_H(n,r)=\sum_{i=0}^4(-1)^i
 \Tr\!\left((H^{-n}\circ\Phi_r)^*;
 H_c^i(\A^2_k,\Ql)\right).
\end{equation}
The classical cohomology computation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affinecohom}
 H_c^i(\A^2_k,\Ql)=
 \begin{cases}\Ql(-2),&i=4,\\0,&i\ne4.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Indeed, acyclicity of affine space and Poincar\'e duality give
\eqref{eq:affinecohom}; see the inputs cited in
Section~\ref{sec:context}. A polynomial automorphism has degree one
and acts as one on this top group. The power morphism has degree $Q^2$
and acts as $Q^2$. This can also be computed on $\Pj^2$: Frobenius
pulls a hyperplane class back to $Q$ times itself, and hence its square
to $Q^2$ times itself; the top compactly supported group of $\A^2$
identifies with that top projective group. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tracevalue}
 T_H(n,r)=q^{2r}.
\end{equation}
This cohomological calculation interprets, but does not prove, the
geometric count.

\begin{proposition}[Sharp trace-agreement threshold]\label{prop:threshold}
For each fixed $n\geq1$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rn}
 R_n=\left\lfloor\log_q(d^n)\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect}
 M_H(n,r)-T_H(n,r)=
 \begin{cases}
 d^nq^r-q^{2r}>0,&1\leq r\leq R_n,\\
 0,&r\geq R_n+1.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The first positive integer at which equality occurs, and the first
integer from which equality always holds, are both $R_n+1$.
In particular, this threshold equals
$n\log_qd+O(1)$ as $n\to\infty$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The number $\log_q(d^n)$ is not an integer, since an integer value
would give a resonance excluded by Corollary~\ref{cor:allclocks}.
Thus $q^r<d^n$ holds exactly for positive integers $r\leq R_n$,
and $q^r>d^n$ holds exactly for $r\geq R_n+1$. Combining
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} with \eqref{eq:tracevalue} gives
\eqref{eq:defect}. The inequality is strict at every integer before
$R_n+1$, and equality holds at every later integer, proving both
threshold statements. Finally,
$R_n+1=n\log_qd+\theta_n$ with $0<\theta_n<1$, which proves the
stated asymptotic without an unspecified coefficient-dependent error.
\end{proof}

For example, the all-clock degree family contains every quadratic map
\eqref{eq:map} in odd characteristic and every cubic map of that form
in characteristic two. Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold} computes an
exact source-system threshold in these families. It does not assert
that iteration-dependent thresholds or eventual trace agreement are
new phenomena.

\subsection{Fixed-H\'enon-time Frobenius slices}

\begin{definition}\label{def:slice}
For fixed $n\geq1$, the Frobenius slice generating function is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slicedef}
 \mathcal Z_{H,n}(t)=
 \exp\!\left(\sum_{r\geq1}M_H(n,r)\frac{t^r}{r}\right)
 \in\mathbb Q[[t]].
\end{equation}
The word ``slice'' records that $n$ stays fixed while $r$ varies.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Slice formula and transcendence]\label{prop:slice}
For every $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sliceformula}
 \mathcal Z_{H,n}(t)=\frac{\exp(P_n(t))}{1-q^2t},
 \qquad
 P_n(t)=\sum_{r=1}^{R_n}(d^nq^r-q^{2r})\frac{t^r}{r}.
\end{equation}
If $d^n<q$, then $P_n=0$ and the slice is rational. If $d^n>q$,
then the slice is transcendental over $\C(t)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:defect}, the logarithm in \eqref{eq:slicedef} is the sum of
\[
 \sum_{r\geq1}q^{2r}\frac{t^r}{r}=-\log(1-q^2t)
\]
and the finite polynomial $P_n(t)$. Exponentiating the formal identity
gives \eqref{eq:sliceformula}. If $d^n<q$, then $R_n=0$ and the finite
sum is empty.

Suppose $d^n>q$. Then $R_n\geq1$, and every coefficient appearing
in $P_n$ is positive by \eqref{eq:defect}. In particular $P_n$ is a
nonconstant real polynomial with positive leading coefficient. We give
an elementary proof that $\exp(P_n)$ is transcendental. If it were
algebraic over $\C(t)$, clearing the denominators of an algebraic
relation would give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algebraicrelation}
 \sum_{j=0}^{J}b_j(t)\exp(jP_n(t))=0,
 \qquad b_j\in\C[t],\quad b_J\not\equiv0,
\end{equation}
with $J\geq1$. A relation holding as a formal series at zero holds
analytically near zero, because each term is entire, and hence holds
everywhere by the identity theorem. For all sufficiently large positive
real $t$, divide \eqref{eq:algebraicrelation} by
$b_J(t)\exp(JP_n(t))$ to obtain
\[
 1+\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}
 \frac{b_j(t)}{b_J(t)}\exp(-(J-j)P_n(t))=0.
\]
Each rational quotient has at most polynomial growth on this ray.
Since $P_n(t)\to+\infty$ with positive polynomial leading term,
each summand after the $1$ tends to zero. The limit would be $1=0$,
a contradiction. Thus $\exp(P_n)$ is transcendental. Multiplying it
by the nonzero rational function $(1-q^2t)^{-1}$ preserves
transcendence, proving the assertion about the slice.
\end{proof}

The defining equations in \eqref{eq:equalizer} change with $r$.
Definition~\ref{def:slice} therefore does not start from the rational
points of one fixed variety. The classical fixed-variety rationality
theorem of Dwork~\cite{dwork1960rationality} imposes no rationality
conclusion on this construction. When $d^n<q$, the resulting function
does happen to coincide with the Hasse--Weil zeta function of $\A^2$;
when $d^n>q$, Proposition~\ref{prop:slice} proves its transcendence.
The construction also differs from the cohomologically defined zeta
function discussed in Section~\ref{sec:context}. Its defining clock
must be specified even when two resulting rational functions coincide.

\subsection{A finite-dimensional trace obstruction}

\begin{proposition}[No invertible-Frobenius weighted trace for a defective slice]
\label{prop:traceobstruction}
Fix $n\geq1$ with $d^n>q$. There do not exist finitely many
finite-dimensional vector spaces $V_i$ over a characteristic-zero field,
endomorphisms $A_i$ and invertible endomorphisms $F_i$ of $V_i$, and
signs $\epsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weightedtrace}
 M_H(n,r)=\sum_i\epsilon_i\Tr(F_i^r A_i)
 \quad\text{for every }r\geq1.
\end{equation}
No commutation condition on $F_i$ and $A_i$ is imposed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Suppose \eqref{eq:weightedtrace} holds. For each $i$, write the
characteristic polynomial of $F_i$ as
\[
 \chi_i(z)=z^{m_i}+c_{i,m_i-1}z^{m_i-1}+\cdots+c_{i,0}.
\]
Invertibility of $F_i$ implies $c_{i,0}\ne0$; zero-dimensional spaces
may be omitted. By Cayley--Hamilton, $\chi_i(F_i)=0$. Multiplying
on the left by $F_i^r$, on the right by $A_i$, and taking the trace
shows that $u_{i,r}=\Tr(F_i^rA_i)$ satisfies the recurrence with
characteristic polynomial $\chi_i$ for all $r\geq1$.
This operation does not move $A_i$ past $F_i$, which is why no
commutation is required.

Let $\mathcal E$ denote the forward shift of a sequence indexed by
$r\geq1$. The polynomial
\[
 \chi(z)=(z-q^2)\prod_i\chi_i(z)
\]
has nonzero constant coefficient and annihilates
\[
 v_r=\sum_i\epsilon_i u_{i,r}-q^{2r}=M_H(n,r)-q^{2r}.
\]
Indeed, each summand is annihilated by one of the factors of
$\chi(\mathcal E)$, and the polynomial shift factors commute.
Writing $\chi(z)=b_0+b_1z+\cdots+b_Lz^L$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:backward}
 b_0v_r+b_1v_{r+1}+\cdots+b_Lv_{r+L}=0,
 \qquad r\geq1,\qquad b_0\ne0.
\end{equation}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold}, $v_r=0$ for every
$r\geq R_n+1$. Substituting $r=R_n$ into \eqref{eq:backward}
therefore gives $v_{R_n}=0$. Repeating with $r=R_n-1$ and then
decreasing $r$ one at a time proves $v_r=0$ for every $r\geq1$.
But $v_1=d^nq-q^2>0$, a contradiction in a characteristic-zero field.
\end{proof}

The obstruction is both finite-dimensional and all-clock. It permits
eventual trace agreement, as \eqref{eq:defect} already demonstrates.
It also leaves infinite-dimensional constructions and explicit boundary
corrections outside its conclusion.
