# Bounded primary-source audit: phase-safe wild FAD natural boundaries

2026-09-06. Author-side search and theorem comparison, not peer review,
a human reading attestation, exhaustive novelty certification, or
permission to create a manuscript. Mathematical proof is in
`PROOF_PACKAGE.md`; the decisive classical deduction is in
`POSTCLASSICAL_DELTA.md`.

## Outcome

The initial 2024-source comparison had to be narrowed after finding
BHN's 2023 preprint / 2025 publication. The no-wild nonhyperbolic
conclusion is already owned by that work and its elementary deduction
for FAD data. The surviving candidate is the **wild nonhyperbolic**
theorem and the exact complex-coefficient active-fibre criterion.
No direct solution of that surviving package was located in the
bounded sources inspected below. This is a scoped negative search
result, not a worldwide priority assertion.

## 1. Central public source

Jakub Byszewski, Gunther Cornelissen, Marc Houben (BCH), *Dynamics of
endomorphisms of algebraic groups*,
[arXiv:2209.00085v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00085v2), 19 April 2024;
[original 176-page PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.00085v2).

Actually inspected for this candidate: Definitions 7.1.1–7.1.2,
Proposition 10.2.1, Definitions 10.3.4/10.3.9, Lemmas 10.3.2/10.3.10,
Propositions 11.2.1/11.3.3, Theorems 11.3.4/11.3.8,
Remark 11.3.10, and Problem 14.1.2, including the relevant proofs.
Their roles are data, realizability restrictions, dominant expansion,
classical rationality and uniqueness, and the exact stated open
natural-boundary regime. The natural-boundary theorem assumes
hyperbolicity and integral wild data. The problem asks about the
general non-root-rational case. The current arXiv page still listed
v2 as latest on the audit date.

The framework, fixed-count formulas, dominant-factor positivity,
root-rational branch, and uniqueness theorem are all deducted. Our
proof is compared with their natural-boundary hypothesis, not with an
unrelated meaning of hyperbolicity. The full no-wild deduction against
BHN is recorded separately and must accompany any novelty assessment.

## 2. Crucial newer owner: BHN

Félix Baril Boudreau, Erik Holmes, Khoa D. Nguyen,
*Adelic perturbation of rational functions and applications*,
[original arXiv:2307.07910v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.07910v1),
16 July 2023, 20 pages;
[*Math. Ann.* 392 (2025), 2253–2275](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00208-025-03155-0),
published 12 April 2025, DOI 10.1007/s00208-025-03155-0.

Actually read: introduction; Definitions 2.4/2.8/2.11;
Examples 2.6/2.13; Theorems 1.2/2.14 and the full three-step proof of
2.14; Corollary 4.4; Theorem 4.6/Remark 4.7 and its proof.
The stable-rational-series theorem uses an almost quasi-polynomial
perturbation, including a number-field condition and sublinear height.
It already solves the abelian-variety and finite-place solenoid
dominant-root questions. `POSTCLASSICAL_DELTA.md` shows explicitly that
the no-wild FAD branch is a corollary. For the genuine wild example,
height at $n=p^a$ is $n\log p$, violating Definition 2.11(P2).

Version honesty: publisher metadata and abstract were obtained;
the full publisher PDF was not. The requested PDF URL returned HTML,
not a PDF. The current author research/home pages still link the
arXiv v1; no later arXiv version was listed. We do not certify that
every detail of the final 23-page version equals the 20-page preprint.

## 3. Earlier natural-boundary owners

### Bell–Miles–Ward

Jason Bell, Richard Miles, Thomas Ward, *Towards a Pólya–Carlson
dichotomy for algebraic dynamics*, *Indagationes Mathematicae* 25(4)
(2014), 652–668;
[institutional original manuscript](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/17223/1/Miles-TowardsaPoly-CarlsonDichotomy%28AM%29.pdf),
[repository record](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/17223/).

Read: introduction; one-solenoid count and realization formula;
Theorem 13; Proposition 14, Theorem 15, Corollary 16, Lemma 17 and
the proofs of the latter results. Theorem 15 requires no archimedean
conjugate on the unit circle and explicitly discusses removing that
condition. The finite-place unweighted kernel theorem and the
logarithmic-derivative transfer are classical. Its sparse infinite-place
theorem concerns a very thin *complement* and does not establish the
new infinite-$S$ order-summability proposal. BHN later owns the finite-
place removed-hypothesis result; BMW alone is not the final comparator.

### Byszewski–Cornelissen and Royals–Ward

Jakub Byszewski, Gunther Cornelissen, *Dynamics on abelian varieties in
positive characteristic*, with an appendix by Robert Royals and
Thomas Ward, *Algebra & Number Theory* 12(9) (2018), 2185–2235,
[publisher PDF](https://msp.org/ant/2018/12-9/ant-v12-n9-p06-p.pdf),
[DOI 10.2140/ant.2018.12.2185](https://doi.org/10.2140/ant.2018.12.2185).

Read for the present comparison: Theorem 5.5 and proof, Question 5.6,
and Appendix Theorem A.1 with its stated adelic-perturbation question.
The main theorem retains unique dominance; the appendix already
supplies a single unweighted periodic valuation-kernel dichotomy.
Question 5.6 is solved by BHN and must not be advertised as still open
or as newly resolved here.

### Fel'shtyn–Klopsch

Alexander Fel'shtyn, Benjamin Klopsch, *Pólya–Carlson dichotomy for
coincidence Reidemeister zeta functions via profinite completions*,
*Indagationes Mathematicae* 33(4) (2022), 753–767,
[original MPIM preprint 2021-8](https://archive.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/id/eprint/4526/1/mpim-preprint_2021-8.pdf),
[publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S001935772200012X),
DOI 10.1016/j.indag.2022.02.004.

Read: Theorem 1.4, especially part (3), Theorem 4.3 and full proof,
and the neighboring kernel/transfer lemmas. Its finite-place theorem
requires unequal archimedean moduli for the paired eigenvalues;
the proof exhibits a unique dominant term. It is a close owner of
the method in a different native counting setting, not a proof of the
wild multiple-phase assertion proposed here.

### Bell–Gunn–Nguyen–Saunders

Jason P. Bell, Keira Gunn, Khoa D. Nguyen, J. C. Saunders,
*A general criterion for the Pólya–Carlson dichotomy and application*,
*Transactions of the AMS* 376 (2023), 4361–4382,
[original arXiv:2206.00862v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00862v1),
[AMS record](https://www.ams.org/tran/2023-376-06/S0002-9947-2023-08876-6/),
DOI 10.1090/tran/8876.

Read: Theorems 1.2/1.6, surrounding motivation and denominator
discussion; Theorem 4.2, Proposition 4.8, and their application setup.
The criterion asks for unit-disc convergence at every number-field
embedding. The application is to positive-characteristic tori.
`REALIZED_EXAMPLE.md` verifies why the normalized Salem example fails
the all-embeddings hypothesis. BCH's use of the single-phase criterion
is classical; it does not itself rule out inter-phase cancellation.

## 4. Analytic mechanism and recent Fourier near-owner

Oliver Knill, John Lesieutre, *Analytic continuation of Dirichlet
series with almost periodic coefficients*,
[author-hosted revised original](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~knill/kam/papers/denjoy_revised.pdf),
[arXiv:0811.1362](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.1362).

Read: Proposition 2.2, its Fourier/Cauchy expansion and radial
singularity argument, and Remarks 2.3–2.6. The dense-Fourier-frequency
mechanism is classical. The candidate does **not** claim to invent
atomic Cauchy transforms or the general inference from dense genuine
boundary singularities. Its work is proving that the actual grouped
FAD measure has the required dense nonzero atoms without imposing a
Diophantine condition on the archimedean phases.

Gunther Cornelissen, Sun Woo Park, *Orbit decomposition statistics
for discrete dynamical systems: the Cesàro mean and a large deviation
principle*, [arXiv:2605.24504v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24504v2),
19 June 2026; the PDF is dated 23 June 2026.

Read: introductory theorem statements, Definition 4.1, proof of
Theorem C in Section 4, Remarks 4.4–4.5, bibliography entries 4–8.
This is a genuine Fourier-analytic near-owner: it proves existence
and positivity of a Cesàro mean for FAD coefficients and treats the
archimedean exponential phases. The inspected argument does not
establish dense nonzero atoms after all phase collisions. Thus even
the general description “use Fourier analysis on FAD coefficients”
is not claimed as an innovation.
