## 1. Outcome and exact candidate boundary

The native circle/solenoid action, rectangular fixed sets, gcd count,
common-base identity, divisor expansions, ordinary diagonal
nonrationality/natural boundaries, and general Hartogs machinery are
not new. Rectangular *zeta functions* themselves already occur in
Ward's 1989 thesis. Any paper claiming to introduce those notions
would fail this audit.

The proposed remaining question is the complete joint analytic
classification, for every integer pair $a,b\geq2$, of
$$
 R_{a,b}(x,y)=\sum_{n,m\geq1}\gcd(a^n-1,b^m-1)x^ny^m.
$$
Its theorem specifies the exact open absolute-convergence domain,
the full interior polar divisor in the dependent branch, and
nonexistence of a joint meromorphic cap at every boundary point of
the bidisc in both branches. It distinguishes joint continuation
from continuation of an exceptional individual slice.

No exact theorem giving this whole classification was located in
the bounded original sources inspected below. That is a qualified
search result, not evidence that no such theorem exists elsewhere.
The proof is complete author-side and independently reviewed in
the workspace; substantive independence remains the coordinator's
decision. The dependent and independent cases are one classification,
not two contracts. Retaining a second variable alone is not the
substantive claim.

A useful diagnostic is $a=b=2$. The diagonal generating function is
the elementary rational function
$$
 \sum_{n\geq1}(2^n-1)z^n
 =\frac{2z}{1-2z}-\frac{z}{1-z}.
$$
In contrast, the two-clock theorem has convergence domain
$\{|x|,|y|<1,\ 2|xy|<1\}$, continuation to the whole bidisc with
the genuine divisors $2x^iy^j=1$ for primitive $(i,j)$, and no
joint cap at any bidisc boundary point. This example makes clear
why a diagonal nonrationality result does not by itself yield the
proposed full classification. It is a diagnostic specialization of
one theorem, not an additional theorem or contract.

## 2. Native rectangular owner: Ward 1992

T. B. Ward, *Periodic points for expansive actions of $\mathbb Z^d$
on compact abelian groups*, *Bulletin of the London Mathematical
Society* 24(4) (1992), 317–324.

- [Publisher record and DOI](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1112/blms/24.4.317).
- [Institutional original manuscript](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/18593/1/pp2.pdf).
- Actually read: introduction and formulas (1.1)–(1.3); complete
  Example 3.1 and its preceding rectangular-period definition.

Example 3.1 uses precisely the $\times2,\times3$ circle action,
independent clocks $(n,m)$, and its $6$-adic solenoid extension.
It studies periodic-point measures, including sequences converging
to Haar measure and sequences remaining atomic. The common-kernel
count is already classical in this setting. The generalization from
$2,3$ to arbitrary integer $a,b$ is elementary and is not proposed as
theorem novelty.

The manuscript PDF was successfully retrieved from the institutional
URL after browser retrieval failed. Its PDF creation date is 2001;
that is not the journal publication date. Publisher metadata fixes
the latter as July 1992. The final typeset 8-page article was not
retrieved; the inspected manuscript has 9 pages. No claim of
line-for-line equality of versions is made.

## 4. Closest modern dynamical-zeta owner: Miles 2015

Richard Miles, *A natural boundary for the dynamical zeta function
for commuting group automorphisms*, *Proceedings of the American
Mathematical Society* 143(7) (2015), 2927–2933,
DOI 10.1090/S0002-9939-2015-12515-4.

- [Institutional publication record](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/17220/).
- [Accepted manuscript](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/17220/1/Miles-NaturalBoundaryForTheDynamicalZetaFunction%28AM%29.pdf).
- Actually read: the full accepted manuscript, including Theorem
  1.1, Section 2's $\times2,\times3$ example, the Section 3 proof
  using the general $S$-unit estimate, and the conclusion.

The dynamical zeta here sums over **all finite-index subgroups** of
$\mathbb Z^d$, weighted by index in a single variable. Its $2,3$
example writes a subgroup in Hermite form and obtains the count
$\gcd(2^A-1,2^B3^C-1)$. Rectangular subgroups are the case $B=0$.
The natural-boundary theorem uses a mixing action on a compact
connected finite-dimensional abelian group. These dynamics and
the arithmetic/natural-boundary architecture are close prior art.

They do not identify the bidisc continuation geometry of an ordinary
series in two independently marked rectangular clocks. The dependent
branch here also contains relations between the generators and
cannot simply be identified with the source's mixing setup.

## 6. Sole deep arithmetic input: Corvaja–Zannier

Pietro Corvaja and Umberto Zannier, *A lower bound for the height of
a rational function at $S$-unit points*, *Monatshefte für Mathematik*
144(3) (2005), 203–224.

- [Original arXiv:math/0311030v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0311030v2),
  22 April 2004.
- [Author-institution publication record](https://air.uniud.it/handle/11390/852374).
- Actually read: introductory Theorem 1, Corollary 1, equivalent
  inequality (1.3), and the complete proof of Corollary 1.

Corollary 1 applies to multiplicatively independent $S$-unit pairs,
with finitely many exceptional pairs. It supplies the two-index
estimate needed for $u=a^n,v=b^m$ when $a,b$ are multiplicatively
independent. This is stronger in scope than a statement restricted
to $\gcd(a^n-1,b^n-1)$ with one shared index. The bidisc absolute-
convergence argument is an elementary deduction from this source,
not a new Diophantine bound. There is no effective exceptional
threshold in the candidate and none is needed for its theorem.

The proof's precise locator is the inspected 2004 arXiv version;
the published article's metadata does not itself certify that its
numbering is unchanged.

The elementary positive atomic Cauchy-transform radial-limit
