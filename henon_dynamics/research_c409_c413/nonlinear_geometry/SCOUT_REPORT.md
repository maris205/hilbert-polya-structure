## 2. Rational-surface pole recurrence: explicit local collision

The putative family is

\[
f(x,y)=\left(y,-x+cy+\sum_{\ell=2,\ \ell\text{ even}}^{k-2}
a_\ell y^{-\ell}+y^{-k}\right),
\qquad k\ge2\text{ even},\quad c\in C_N.
\]

The native clock is the actual iterate, and a native cycle must have every
recurrence denominator nonzero. Local algebra length and reduced point count
are different observables. Bedford–Kim's admissibility set must retain the
product condition in their Section 1; merely prescribing the order of the
Möbius action at infinity is insufficient.

The current primary read checked their introduction, Theorem 1, Section 1
parameter discussion, Theorem 3.5 and the Section 8 formulas through Theorem
8.1. They already construct the automorphism and identify periodic boundary
components; many are fixed by the `2N` iterate with identity differential.
This means that a Lefschetz number cannot simply be called a finite fixed-point
count. [Bedford–Kim, arXiv 0804.2078v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0804.2078).

Iwasaki–Uehara's Theorem 7.2 gives the exact isolated-index count after
periodic-curve contributions under its type-II/absence-of-type-I hypotheses.
Those hypotheses must be verified, and the indices still need computation.
[Iwasaki–Uehara, arXiv 0710.0706](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0710.0706).

The local file `research_c404_c408/nonlinear_geometry/SCOUT_REPORT.md`,
Sections 1.1–1.3, already contains this precise unclosed proposal, its source
owners and its period-three distinction between scheme length and ordinary
points. It was read, not rerun. Re-listing the old obstacle provides no new
contract. No new uniform boundary lemma has been proved in this scout.

## 3. Cubic-surface candidate

Take the affine Fricke family

\[
x^2+y^2+z^2+xyz=Ax+By+Cz+D
\]

over C, and a positive-entropy word in the three coordinate involutions.
One could ask for ordinary or weighted isolated fixed points of every
iterate, including singular fibers. A valid contract must specify which
observable it uses, whether the singular surface or its resolution is the
state space, and which exceptional points are excluded from native iteration.

The primary read of Iwasaki–Uehara Section 8 found an explicit singular-cubic
resolution computation and a closed all-iterate isolated-index formula.
Thus “apply Saito to the singular Markov surface” is already represented by
a worked owner example. This does not claim their example proves the whole
four-parameter classification; it rejects that unqualified novelty pitch.
[Primary example](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0710.0706).

The current landscape also includes Abboud's periodic-point unlikely
intersection theorem for certain Markov parameters. Only its abstract and
version metadata were read here, not its proof or every parameter hypothesis.
[Abboud, arXiv 2401.05762v4, revised April 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05762).
The torus-quotient specialization is a separate classical collision risk.
No new full-parameter or inverse theorem has yet emerged from this branch.

## 4. Elliptic-fibration return divisors

For a relatively minimal Jacobian elliptic surface `E -> B` over a
characteristic-zero field, zero section `O`, and non-torsion section `P`,
translation by `P` has a periodic fiber precisely where `nP` specializes to
zero. The natural base divisor is `D_n=O^*(nP)`, not a finite count of all
points in that fiber. A proposed theorem would classify the primitive
divisors and their local multiplicities for every positive integer `n`.

Ulmer–Urzúa already construct the primitive effective divisors, prove their
disjoint supports and stable multiplicities under divisible clocks, and
identify a finite set of primitive clocks accounting for nonreduced return
divisors. The relevant material was read in Theorems 1.1, 1.4 and 1.7,
Sections 2.3–2.7, including the exact divisor identities. The paper also
treats very general transversality and explicit singular-fiber examples.
[Ulmer–Urzúa, arXiv 1908.02208v4](https://arxiv.org/html/1908.02208v4).

Consequently a QRT rewriting of those divisor facts is not an independent
contribution. A claim about all singular parameters would require a new
explicit theorem, not silently dropping the source's hypotheses. No such
theorem is currently offered. Further primary leads include Silverman's
function-field common-divisor work and the primitive-divisor literature;
their abstracts were used as ownership leads, not as proof dependencies.

## 5. A current inverse-problem owner

The candidate inverse question was: for each degree `d>=2`, classify all
regular polynomial skew products

\[
F(z,w)=(p(z),q(z,w))
\]

extending holomorphically to `P^2_C` for which the multipliers of **all**
periodic points lie in one fixed number field. This is a geometric rigidity
question rather than a trace calculation.

It is exactly covered by Zhang's April 2026 preprint. Theorem 1.5 equates
this condition with the stated special maps and a finite rational
semiconjugacy from an affine map on a connected commutative algebraic group.
The paper includes Dickson-polynomial skew products, so restricting attention
to products of one-variable power/Chebyshev maps would miss part of the
owner's answer. The abstract, introduction, Definitions 1.2–1.4 and Theorem
1.5 were read; no proof-reconstruction or new classification is claimed.
[Zhang, arXiv 2604.12173v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12173v1).

## 6. Non-confining birational recurrence: follow-up owner found

For `epsilon` in C consider the actual rational map

\[
K_\epsilon(y,z)=
\left(z+1-\epsilon,\;y\frac{z-\epsilon}{z+1}\right).
\]

The proposed observable is the number of distinct points whose first `n`
steps are all defined in the specified affine chart and return to the
starting point. It is not a projective Lefschetz count. The proposed scope
would include a precise classification of excluded/integrable parameters,
critical or indeterminate cycles, and all native clocks.

Abarenkova and collaborators explicitly proposed rational dynamical-zeta
formulas for this map and a larger two-parameter family. The current read
checked the introduction and defining equations (especially equation (3)),
the discussion of generic versus exceptional parameters, and the statement
that the displayed zeta formulas are conjectural. Their older conjecture is
**not** being called an open problem in 2026 before follow-up verification.
[Original preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/chao-dyn/9807014),
[published paper on an author's institutional site](https://www.lptmc.jussieu.fr/files/maillard/RatioZetaPhysicaA.pdf).

The follow-up owner check is now complete for the most immediately tractable
parameter region. With `S(y,z)=(-z,-y)`, direct substitution gives

\[
S K_\epsilon S^{-1}(x,y)
=\left(y\frac{x+\epsilon}{x-1},x+\epsilon-1\right)=f_\epsilon(x,y),
\]

the Bedford–Diller family. Their Theorem 6.3 gives the periodic-word
bijection, with the alternating words accounting for the point at infinity;
Theorem 6.4 gives saddle type for finite periodic points. The scope is
negative real parameters other than `-1`, with Section 11 treating the
second interval. Thus the native periodic-data problem in that region is
already owned, including simplicity and the boundary distinction. Actual
reading covered the introduction, Section 1's exceptional-parameter
description, and Section 6's statements and counting proof. No all-complex
claim is inferred from the real theorem.
[Bedford–Diller, arXiv math/0306276](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0306276).

The larger all-complex-parameter question would require a new uniform
classification of resonances and exceptional cycles. None is proved here.
Replacing the known real region by the word “generic” would not supply that
missing theorem. This fifth branch is therefore rejected without a symbolic
census, rather than promoting an old conjecture to an allegedly open result.
