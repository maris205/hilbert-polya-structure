## Decision

**REJECT_SUBSTANCE for an additional paper-level batch contribution.**
Retain the mathematical package as an explicit note, subject to the
coordinator's proof review. This is a substantive subtraction judgment,
**not** a finding that a named publication contains the entire exact tower.

No exact published owner of the complete fixed-map ramification statement
was located in the sources actually read. That negative observation does
not establish novelty. Two relevant classical originals remain unread
because of access failures; their author's later account was accessible.

The generic all-height group upgrade is available through the
finite-group argument underlying König–Neftin–Rosenberg (KNR).
The remaining map-specific work is a short local AS stability/propagation
calculation and a height-two ramification-support test. The other displayed
formulas are consequences, not independent increments. The local mechanism
also substantially overlaps the already developed wild cubic package.
The rationale is expanded below; it is not based on counting lemmas or
on the assertion that a short proof cannot be important.

## Scope and core-claim ledger

For a prime \(p\geq5\), put \(m=(p-1)/2\), \(b=m+1\), and
\(f(X)=X^p+X^{-1}\). The generic inverse tree has degree \(p+1\).
The proposed contract concerns every height and every constant field of
characteristic \(p\), with geometric local statements after algebraic
closure of constants.

| Claim | Ownership/substance assessment | Surviving question |
|---|---|---|
| First cover has group \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb F_p)\) in its projective action | Exact classical collision, resolved separately through Serre's appendix | None counted |
| All-height geometric group is the full iterated wreath product; arithmetic equality/regularity | Generic upgrade is standard composition machinery after a direct separable-characteristic adaptation; regularity is the geometric/arithmetic upper-bound sandwich | Map-specific height two and strict local growth |
| All pole-child splitting fields coincide locally, and \(e_n=mp^n\) | Exact fixed-map publication not located; mechanism is elementary AS continuity plus propagated closeness | A genuine explicit local calculation, but small after subtraction |
| Inertia is \((C_p)^n\rtimes C_m\), with one positive lower jump \(b\), different \(p^{n+1}-(m+2)\), and exact genus | Derived from the preceding local index/closeness, the derivative/different formula, and standard ramification structure | No separate rank-classification or obstruction problem remains |

Method novelty is **low**; exact-finding priority is **unresolved**.
The qualitative recommendation is not to count this as an additional
paper-level batch increment.

### 1. The closest general composition owner

Joachim König, Danny Neftin, Shai Rosenberg,
*Polynomial compositions with large monodromy groups and applications to
arithmetic dynamics*, arXiv:2401.17872, v2 (1 February 2024).
Primary sources:
[arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17872),
[author PDF](https://neftin.net.technion.ac.il/files/2024/02/version-2.pdf).
Read: introduction; relevant §3 group arguments; Theorem 4.1 and proof;
Corollary 4.6 and proof, pp. 18–19; Appendix A, pp. 34–35.

Corollary 4.6.B treats a chain of minimal extensions with nonabelian
almost-simple closure groups. Distinct prefix closures and non-diagonal
adjacent two-step kernels force the composition factors of the wreath
product of their socles. Appendix A recasts the preceding map argument
in field language.

**Scope caution:** §2 imposes a standing characteristic-zero convention,
repeated at the beginning of §4. Appendix A does not explicitly revoke
it. Thus the published corollary must not be cited as literally
characteristic-free. Its subgroup-chain, socle and block-partition
argument uses finite-group theory and separable Galois correspondence;
the adaptation below is an inference, not a quotation of a stated
positive-characteristic theorem. The polynomial corollaries, Ritt
classification, and characteristic-zero inertia shortcuts are not used.

### 2. A genuinely recent composition classification

Angelot Behajaina, Joachim König, Danny Neftin,
*Monodromy groups of polynomials of composition length 2*,
arXiv:2603.27609, v2 (31 July 2026).
Read: abstract, introduction/Theorems 1.2–1.3, §2.2/Theorem 2.5, and
the relevant §3 kernel discussion.
[Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.27609v2).

Its main classification is for indecomposable **polynomials in

map does not satisfy those main-theorem hypotheses. Its abstract group

## Our deduction: the generic upgrade after height two

This is an application/adaptation analysis, not a source quotation.
Fix a top preimage \(x_n\), put \(x_{i-1}=f(x_i)\), and
\(F_i=k(x_i)\), \(F_0=k(t)\). All these extensions are separable.

- The natural projective PSL action is primitive, so every
  \(F_i/F_{i-1}\) is minimal.
- Each adjacent two-step extension is the same rational composition
  \(f^{\circ2}\) after renaming the transcendental base variable.
- The height-two argument uses only the solvable first point stabilizer
  to retain the simple group after base change, and a place where one
  selected first-level root is small while another is a pole to
  distinguish two factors. Nonabelian simple-factor independence gives
  the full height-two kernel.
- The independent local induction gives \(e_i=mp^i\). Hence the prefix
  Galois closures are pairwise distinct.
- The separable-field version of the KNR finite-group argument now
  supplies the wreath-product composition factors. Inside the already
  proved arithmetic wreath upper bound, their order forces equality.

The characteristic-\(p\) adaptation does not use tame branch cycles,
polynomial indecomposable classification, Ritt moves, or a
characteristic-zero lifting theorem. It should be credited as an
adaptation of known machinery, not promoted into a new contribution.

## Residual dependency analysis and comparison with the cubic

After removing the first-level model and the generic upgrade, the
remaining work has **two map-specific inputs**, followed by consequences:

1. **One local input with propagation.** For pole parameters \(A,B\)
   with \(v(A)=v(B)=-m\) and \(v(A-B)\geq1\), compare their small roots.
   The identity
   \(f(s_A)-f(s_B)=(s_A-s_B)^p-(s_A-s_B)/(s_As_B)\)
   gives \(v(s_A-s_B)\geq2m+1\). Aligning their \(m\)-th roots makes
   the two AS right sides differ integrally. Integral AS equations
   split over an algebraically closed residue field. Thus all pole
   children contribute the same cyclic extension. The analogous
   difference identity for pole children preserves their closeness;
   the local degree is multiplied by precisely \(p\).
2. **A height-two global input.** The first point stabilizer is
   solvable and the target factor is simple. First-level pole supports
   distinguish the base-changed factors. No new higher-level
   relation module has to be classified after using the composition
   argument above.

The different follows by differentiating
\(u^p/(1+u^{p+1})\), summing the root-tower derivative orders, and
using the remaining tame degree \(m\). The closeness lower bound on
ramification numbers saturates the resulting exact different; standard
ramification graded quotients give elementary-abelian wild inertia,
and the tame character gives the scalar action. Riemann–Hurwitz gives
the genus. Arithmetic regularity is the upper-bound sandwich.
These deductions are useful and complete, but add no independent
obstruction beyond the two inputs.

The comparator is the actual
[wild cubic proof](../positive_characteristic/WILD_CUBIC_PROOF.md),
especially §§5–8, which were read for this audit. Its local infinity
argument already separates **global independence from local AS rank
one**: after a Kummer step splits locally, aligned AS parameters have
integral differences, so Hensel lifting identifies their local classes
and multiplies the infinity index by 3. It then uses the same
root-different/tame-closure/Riemann–Hurwitz sequence and the same
arithmetic/geometric degree sandwich.

There is a real distinction: the PSL case has pole order \(b>1\),
so leading-coefficient cancellation alone is insufficient; a quantitative
small-root estimate is needed. It also has a nonabelian simple global
group rather than the cubic's signature-constrained solvable group.
Neither distinction should be erased. Nevertheless, the new estimate
is supplied directly by the displayed difference identity and tame
root extraction. The simple-group case **removes** the cubic's need
to classify an all-height sibling Kummer relation space and then
separate global AS character lines. It does not replace those tasks
with a new comparable obstruction.

Thus the reason for rejection is not “only one lemma” or “few pages.”
The remaining result is a closely related local-AS-coalescence
realization using the exact classical first cover; its all-height
global difficulty is discharged by standard machinery after a
two-level test. It answers a complete explicit example question, but
does not additionally solve an independent parameter/ramification
classification, a new global-relation obstruction, or a distinct
arithmetic decomposition problem. Such additional questions are not
required merely to lengthen a paper; they indicate what kind of
independent mathematical content is currently absent from this proposed
*additional* batch contract.

Do not split its index, inertia, jump, genus, regularity or each prime
into separate contributions. Even assuming the exact local formula is
unpublished, this reviewer recommends **a mathematical note, not one
more counted paper-level contribution**. Final adjudication belongs
to the coordinator.
