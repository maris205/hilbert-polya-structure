\begin{abstract}
Pressure normalization turns the instability roof of a certified
area-preserving H\'enon survivor into an entropy-one suspension with the
prime-orbit law $\Pi(T)\sim e^T/T$.  Its repetition-compatible scalar labels
are $P_\gamma=|\Lambda_\gamma|^{h_*}$, but their arithmetic type was open.
We exhibit primitive survivor orbits of periods one, three and four whose
positive unstable moduli $L_1,L_3,L_4$ generate a degree-$32$ compositum.
The independent inversion automorphisms prove that
$\log L_1,\log L_3,\log L_4$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$.
For rational $h>0$, algebraic-unit integrality prevents $L_j^h$ from being a
rational prime.  For irrational $h$, the Six Exponentials Theorem forces at
least one of $L_1^h,L_3^h,L_4^h$ to be transcendental.  Thus, for every real
$h>0$, the three labels cannot all be rational primes.  This unconditionally
rejects an all-orbit termwise prime interpretation of the pressure labels,
including the actual pressure root, while leaving the dynamical prime-orbit
counting theorem intact.  An exact symbolic certificate reconstructs the
orbits, monodromy polynomials, ramification ladder and degree-$32$ primitive
element.
\end{abstract}

\section{The remaining scalar gate}
For
\[
H_6(q,p)=(1-6q^2-p,q),
\]
the certified four-rectangle survivor is conjugate to a mixing finite-type
shift.  Its positive non-lattice instability roof has a unique pressure root
$h_*$ with
\[
0.277980<h_*<0.277987.
\]
The pressure-normalized suspension has entropy one and obeys the dynamical
prime-orbit theorem
\[
\Pi(T)\sim \frac{e^T}{T}.
\]
Its intrinsic scalar label is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:label}
P_\gamma=|\Lambda_\gamma|^{h_*},
\end{equation}
which preserves every orbit repetition.  Earlier all-period algebra proves
that every raw multiplier is an algebraic unit, and the rational functorial
classification leaves real powers as the precise scalar survivor.  The
question addressed here is stronger than counting:
can \eqref{eq:label} be a rational prime for every primitive orbit?

Our result uses no conjecture about the arithmetic nature of $h_*$.  It also
does not assign primes to orbits or inspect a prime table.

\section{Three exact primitive survivor orbits}
Periodic coordinates obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
q_{i+1}=1-6q_i^2-q_{i-1},
\end{equation}
and the chronological derivative step is
\[
J(q_i)=\begin{pmatrix}-12q_i&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Write $X_-=[-5/8,-1/3]$ and $X_+=[1/3,5/8]$.  In state order
$(--,-+,+-,++)$, with state $(\operatorname{sgn}q_i,
\operatorname{sgn}q_{i-1})$, the frozen graph has edges
\[
\begin{aligned}
(--)&\longrightarrow\{(--),(+-)\},&
(+-)&\longrightarrow\{(-+),(++)\},\\
(-+)&\longrightarrow\{(--)\},&
(++)&\longrightarrow\{(-+)\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
All coordinates below belong to $X_-\cup X_+$, and their sign-state cycles
use these allowed edges.  Hence the exact periodic points lie in the
source-locked local survivor, not merely in the full planar H\'enon map.

\begin{lemma}[Exact orbit and monodromy ledger]\label{lem:orbits}
The survivor contains the following primitive periodic orbits.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The negative fixed point
\[
q_*=-(1+\sqrt7)/6
\]
has positive unstable modulus
\[
L_1=1+\sqrt7+\sqrt{7+2\sqrt7}
\]
and minimal polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f1}
f_1(X)=X^4-4X^3-22X^2-4X+1.
\end{equation}
\item The period-three sequence
\[
(a,b,a),\qquad a=-\frac{\sqrt5}{6},\quad
b=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{6},
\]
has sign-state cycle $(--,+-,-+)$, return trace
$-38-42\sqrt5$, and positive unstable modulus
\[
L_3=19+21\sqrt5+\sqrt{2565+798\sqrt5}.
\]
Its minimal polynomial is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f3}
f_3(X)=X^4-76X^3-7374X^2-76X+1.
\end{equation}
\item The period-four sequence
\[
(-1/\sqrt6,-1/\sqrt6,1/\sqrt6,1/\sqrt6)
\]
has return trace $578$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:l4}
L_4=289+24\sqrt{145},\qquad
f_4(X)=X^2-578X+1.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Substitution into \eqref{eq:recurrence} is direct.  The period-three sequence
is nonconstant, hence primitive because three is prime.  The period-four
sequence has no period one or two.  Chronological multiplication gives
\[
J(a)J(b)J(a)=
\begin{pmatrix}
-40-44\sqrt5&21+4\sqrt5\\
-21-4\sqrt5&2+2\sqrt5
\end{pmatrix},
\]
whose determinant is one and trace is $-38-42\sqrt5$.  If
$T_3=38+42\sqrt5$, then $L_3+L_3^{-1}=T_3$.  Multiplying the two conjugate
quadratics over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ gives \eqref{eq:f3}.  The fixed and
period-four calculations give \eqref{eq:f1} and \eqref{eq:l4} in the same
way.
\end{proof}

\section{The degree-32 compositum}
Put $K_j=\mathbb Q(L_j)$.  The key point is not merely that the three
multipliers are different; their fields permit independent inversion.

\begin{proposition}[Field independence]\label{prop:degree}
The compositum
\[
E=K_1K_3K_4
\]
has degree
\[
[E:\mathbb Q]=4\cdot4\cdot2=32.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $K_1$, the quadratic discriminant over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt7)$ is
$28+8\sqrt7$, whose norm is $336$, not a square.  Moreover
$\sqrt7=(L_1+L_1^{-1}-2)/2$, so $[K_1:\mathbb Q]=4$.

For $K_3$, set
\[
d_3=T_3^2-4=10260+3192\sqrt5.
\]
Its norm is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:d3norm}
N_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)/\mathbb Q}(d_3)
=54323280=2^4 3^2 5\cdot11\cdot19^3.
\end{equation}
Thus $d_3$ is not a square in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$, and
$K_3=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5,\sqrt{d_3})$ has degree four.  Finally
$K_4=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{145})$ has degree two.

It remains to show that these degrees multiply.  The polynomial
discriminants are
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{disc}(f_1)&=2^{12}3\,7^3,\\
\operatorname{disc}(f_3)&=2^{12}3^6 5^3 7^4 11\,19^3.
\end{align*}
The field $K_1$ is therefore unramified at $5$, so it cannot contain the
ramified quadratic field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$.  Hence
$[K_1\mathbb Q(\sqrt5):\mathbb Q]=8$.

Because $11$ splits in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ and occurs to odd exponent in
\eqref{eq:d3norm}, one prime above $11$ has odd valuation in $d_3$.
The extension obtained by base-changing $K_1$ is unramified there, since
$11\nmid\operatorname{disc}(f_1)$.  The odd valuation therefore remains
odd, so $d_3$ is still nonsquare after adjoining $K_1$.  Adjoining
$\sqrt{d_3}$ doubles the degree to $16$.

Both $K_1$ and $K_3$ are unramified at $29$, and a compositum of unramified
local extensions remains unramified.  But $29$ ramifies in
$\mathbb Q(\sqrt{145})$.  Consequently $K_4$ is not contained in $K_1K_3$,
and the final degree is $32$.
\end{proof}

The certificate independently computes a primitive element
$L_1+L_3+L_4$ and an irreducible degree-$32$ polynomial.  This exact machine
check supports, but does not replace, Proposition~\ref{prop:degree}.

\section{Multiplicative and logarithmic independence}
Each $K_j$ has a $\mathbb Q$-automorphism $\iota_j$ taking
$L_j$ to $L_j^{-1}$.  For $j=1,3$ it is the sign change of the quadratic
square root over the trace field; for $j=4$ it is conjugation of
$\sqrt{145}$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:degree} says the three fields are
linearly disjoint, so each $\iota_j$ extends to $E$ while fixing the other
two fields.

\begin{theorem}[Logarithmic independence]\label{thm:logs}
The positive real numbers
\[
\log L_1,\qquad\log L_3,\qquad\log L_4
\]
are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
After clearing denominators, a rational relation would give
\[
L_1^{m_1}L_3^{m_3}L_4^{m_4}=1
\]
with integers $m_j$.  Apply the extension of $\iota_1$ and divide the two
equalities.  This gives $L_1^{2m_1}=1$, hence $m_1=0$ because $L_1>1$.
The other two inversion automorphisms similarly give $m_3=m_4=0$.
\end{proof}

\section{The all-prime obstruction}
We use the real Six Exponentials Theorem: if $x_1,x_2,x_3$ are linearly
independent over $\mathbb Q$ and $\xi_1,\xi_2$ are linearly independent over
$\mathbb Q$, at least one of the six numbers $\exp(x_i\xi_j)$ is
transcendental \cite[Theorem~3.1]{Pila}.  This is a theorem, not the open Four
Exponentials Conjecture.

\begin{theorem}[No common-power all-prime triple]\label{thm:main}
For every real $h>0$, the three numbers
\[
L_1^h,\qquad L_3^h,\qquad L_4^h
\]
cannot all be rational primes.  In fact, if $h$ is rational, none is a
rational integer greater than one; if $h$ is irrational, at least one is
transcendental.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First let $h=a/b>0$ in lowest terms.  If $L_j^h=N$ were a rational integer
greater than one, then
\[
L_j^a=N^b.
\]
The left side is an algebraic unit, while a positive rational integer is a
unit only when it is one, a contradiction.

Now let $h$ be irrational.  Apply the Six Exponentials Theorem with
\[
(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(\log L_1,\log L_3,\log L_4),
\qquad (\xi_1,\xi_2)=(1,h).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:logs} supplies the first independence hypothesis, and
irrationality supplies the second.  The three exponentials with $\xi_1=1$
are the algebraic numbers $L_1,L_3,L_4$.  Therefore at least one of the
remaining three exponentials $L_j^h$ is transcendental.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Pressure labels]\label{cor:pressure}
For the actual H\'enon pressure root $h_*$, the labels of the three orbits in
Lemma~\ref{lem:orbits} cannot all be rational primes.  Hence no
multiplicity-preserving assignment that declares every intrinsic pressure
label $|\Lambda_\gamma|^{h_*}$ to be a rational prime can exist.
\end{corollary}

If $h_*$ were algebraic irrational, the stronger Gelfond--Schneider theorem
would make each $L_j^{h_*}$ transcendental \cite[Theorem~3.2]{Pila}; the
original solution of Hilbert's seventh problem is due to Gelfond
\cite{Gelfond}.  Corollary~\ref{cor:pressure} needs no such hypothesis and
also covers transcendental $h_*$.

It verifies all recurrence equations, determinant-one monodromy, the three
minimal polynomials, the discriminant factorizations, and the degree ladder
$4,8,16,32$.  The full degree-$32$ primitive-element polynomial is stored in
the JSON certificate.  Its coefficient-vector SHA256 is

A duplicate-orbit mutation drops the compositum degree from $32$ to $16$,

\section{Evaluator verdict and claim boundary}
For the termwise pressure-prime lane, the strict Route-A tuple is
\[
(A1_{\rm WEAK},A2_{\rm FAIL},A3_{\rm PARTIAL},A4_{\rm FORMAL}),
\]
with overall \texttt{ROUTE\_A\_REJECTED}.  Coordinatewise positive evidence
from the distinct HCS-P45 dynamical counting object is not merged into this
certificate.  Route B is not authorized: no Hilbert space, operator domain,
self-adjointness theorem, von Mangoldt trace identity or completed-$\xi$
determinant is supplied.

The obstruction is precisely scoped.  It proves that at least one of three
labels is not a rational prime; for transcendental $h$ it need not identify
which one.  It does not refute the pressure prime-orbit law, prime-ideal or
Galois-packet labels, cyclic resultants, or a collective signed trace.

\section{Conclusion}
The pressure-power scalar road is now closed at its strongest termwise
interpretation.  Three source-native periodic orbits suffice: exact field
independence plus a classical transcendence theorem handles every possible
real common exponent.  The surviving large road is necessarily nonlocal.
Arithmetic information must be carried collectively by packets, ideals,
resultants or a distributional determinant rather than by one rational prime
attached to each primitive orbit.
