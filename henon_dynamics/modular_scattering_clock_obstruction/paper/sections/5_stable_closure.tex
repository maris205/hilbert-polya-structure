\section{Stable closure returns to Selberg length}

The literal denominator height fails exact repetition, but its growth under
powers has a canonical linear part.  Cayley--Hamilton identifies it without a
fit.

\begin{proposition}[Chebyshev power identity]\label{prop:chebyshev}
For $g\in\GammaSL$, $t=\tr g$, and $n\geq1$,
\[
 g^n=U_{n-1}(t/2)g-U_{n-2}(t/2)I,
\]
where $U_{-1}=0$, $U_0=1$, and
$U_{k+1}(x)=2xU_k(x)-U_{k-1}(x)$.  In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c-cheb}
 c(g^n)=c(g)U_{n-1}(t/2).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The characteristic polynomial of $g$ is $x^2-tx+1$, so
$g^2=tg-I$.  The claimed formula holds at $n=1,2$.  Multiplication by $g$
and substitution of $g^2=tg-I$ turns the step from $n$ to $n+1$ into the
defining recurrence for $U_n$.  Taking lower-left entries proves
\eqref{eq:c-cheb}.
\end{proof}

Choose the lift of a hyperbolic class with $t>2$, and define
\[
 \lambda=\frac{t+\sqrt{t^2-4}}2,\qquad
 \Length(g)=2\log\lambda,\qquad
 H_\alpha(g)=2\log(\alpha|c(g)|).
\]
Hyperbolicity forces $c(g)\ne0$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact defect and stable homogenization]\label{thm:stable}
For every $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect}
 H_\alpha(g^n)
 =n\Length(g)
 +2\log\frac{\alpha|c(g)|}{\sqrt{t^2-4}}
 +2\log(1-\lambda^{-2n}).
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{H_\alpha(g^n)}n=\Length(g).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The eigenvalues are $\lambda$ and $\lambda^{-1}$, and
\[
 U_{n-1}(t/2)
 =\frac{\lambda^n-\lambda^{-n}}{\lambda-\lambda^{-1}}
 =\frac{\lambda^n(1-\lambda^{-2n})}{\sqrt{t^2-4}}.
\]
Combine this identity with \eqref{eq:c-cheb}, multiply by $\alpha$, and take
$2\log$.  This proves \eqref{eq:defect}.  After division by $n$, both defect
terms tend to zero.
\end{proof}

The limiting clock is exactly the translation length used in the
Selberg--Mayer closed-geodesic determinant~\cite{Selberg1956,Mayer1991}.  This
positive closure explains why enforcing power stability removes the endpoint
denominator information and returns to eigenvalue growth.

The conclusion is not a universal uniqueness theorem.  It concerns the
canonical limit of the specific sequence $H_\alpha(g^n)/n$.  A useful scoped
rigidity statement does follow: if a homogeneous closed clock $L$ satisfies
\[
 L([g^n])-H_\alpha(g^n)=o(n)
\]
along the powers of a fixed $g$, then division by $n$ and
\cref{thm:stable} give $L([g])=\Length(g)$.  Homogeneous class functions
outside this asymptotic class remain unrestricted.
