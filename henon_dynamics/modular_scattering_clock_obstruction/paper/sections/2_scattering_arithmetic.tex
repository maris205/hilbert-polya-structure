\section{Open modular scattering arithmetic}

Let $\Gamma=\GammaPSL$ and let $P=\Gamma_\infty=\langle T\rangle$ be the
cusp stabilizer, where
\[
 T=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Calculations use lifts
$g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ in
$\GammaSL$.  In the big Bruhat cell we choose the PSL sign by requiring
$c>0$.

\begin{proposition}[Oriented cusp double cosets]\label{prop:double-coset}
The cell with $c=0$ is the identity double coset $P$.  The nonidentity double
cosets in $P\backslash\Gamma/P$ are in bijection with
\[
 \{(c,\bar d):c\geq1,\ \bar d\in(\Z/c\Z)^\times\}.
\]
Consequently, the number of oriented algebraic channels at denominator $c$
is $\varphi(c)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Left multiplication by $T^m$ leaves the bottom row fixed, while right
multiplication by $T^n$ sends $(c,d)$ to $(c,d+nc)$.  Hence $c$ and
$d\bmod c$ are double-coset invariants.  The determinant equation
$ad-bc=1$ implies $(c,d)=1$.  Conversely, for a unit $\bar d\bmod c$, choose
$a$ with $ad\equiv1\pmod c$ and put $b=(ad-1)/c$.

For uniqueness, use a right translation to make two representatives with the
same label have the same bottom row.  The difference of their top rows is an
integer multiple of that primitive bottom row, so a left translation makes
the matrices equal.  If $c=0$, determinant one forces the diagonal entries to
be both $1$ or both $-1$, and the PSL class lies in $P$.
\end{proof}

Assign the open height
\[
 \tau_P(PgP)=2\log c(g).
\]
Then \cref{prop:double-coset} gives, initially for $\Re s>1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:totient-dirichlet}
 \sum_{PgP\ne P}e^{-s\tau_P(PgP)}
 =\sum_{c\geq1}\frac{\varphi(c)}{c^{2s}}
 =\frac{\zeta(2s-1)}{\zeta(2s)}.
\end{equation}
The last equality follows from
$\varphi(n)=n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)/d$ and absolute rearrangement of the
Dirichlet series.  This arithmetic series is not the complete scattering
coefficient.  The constant term of the normalized Eisenstein series is
\[
 E(z,s)=y^s+\PhiSc(s)y^{1-s}+\text{nonconstant modes},
\]
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scattering}
 \PhiSc(s)=\sqrt\pi\frac{\Gamma(s-\tfrac12)}{\Gamma(s)}
 \frac{\zeta(2s-1)}{\zeta(2s)}
 =\frac{\Lambda(2s-1)}{\Lambda(2s)}.
\end{equation}
The Eisenstein/zeta relation is classical~\cite{Zagier1981}.

Guillemin introduced sojourn times as the geometric clock of scattering
geodesics~\cite{Guillemin1976}, and Ji--Zworski related scattering matrices to
scattering geodesics on locally symmetric spaces~\cite{JiZworski2001}.  In
the modular case, recent explicit counting uses a clock
$2\log(cT_0)$ after a cusp cutoff $T_0$~\cite{PujahariSatpathy2025}.  This
supports the denominator as an \emph{open} clock.  The count
$\varphi(c)$ uses oriented algebraic double cosets; identifying a geometric
trajectory with its reversal can change the multiplicity convention.

Nothing in this section defines a primitive closed orbit.  In particular,
$Pg^nP$ is not declared to be an $n$-fold repeat of $PgP$.  Powers enter only
after one proposes to descend final-monodromy data to the closed category.
