\section{Why closure changes the indexing object}

A closed geodesic on the modular surface is indexed here by a hyperbolic
conjugacy class.  We retain the orientation encoded by the conjugacy class and
do not quotient $[g]$ by inversion $[g^{-1}]$; making the unoriented quotient
would not affect the matrix obstruction below.  A total period $L$ must be
representative independent and obey
\[
 L([g^n])=nL([g]).
\]
The two indexing categories impose different invariance conditions.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
 & Open scattering channel & Closed hyperbolic orbit \\
\midrule
Index & $PgP$ & conjugacy class $[g]$ \\
Invariant data & $(c,d\bmod c)$ & trace/eigenvalue and cyclic word \\
Clock & $2\log(cT_0)$ & $2\operatorname{arcosh}(|\tr g|/2)$ \\
Repetition & not imposed on $P\backslash\Gamma/P$ & $[g^n]$ traverses $[g]$ $n$ times \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{proposition}[Failure of conjugacy invariance]\label{prop:conjugacy}
The function $g\mapsto|c(g)|$ does not descend to hyperbolic conjugacy
classes in $\GammaPSL$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take
\[
 g=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\2&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then
\[
 S^{-1}gS=\begin{pmatrix}3&-2\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The two matrices have trace $4$ and are conjugate in $\GammaPSL$, but their
absolute lower-left entries are $2$ and $1$.
\end{proof}

The same failure appears inside the continued-fraction coding of closed
geodesics.  Let
\[
 A_a=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The even words $(1,1,1,2)$ and $(1,2,1,1)$ give
\[
 A_1A_1A_1A_2=\begin{pmatrix}2&5\\3&8\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A_1A_2A_1A_1=\begin{pmatrix}3&5\\4&7\end{pmatrix}.
\]
With $Q=A_1A_1=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
the second product is $Q^{-1}$ times the first times $Q$.  The conjugator has
determinant one, the traces agree, and the denominators are $3$ and $4$.
The two-digit shift keeps the witness inside PSL rather than relying on a
single-digit PGL conjugacy.  Series' coding makes cyclic recoding the natural
closed-geodesic equivalence~\cite{Series1985}.

\begin{remark}[Scope of the type test]
A local roof need not be a class function.  Only its full periodic Birkhoff
sum must descend to a closed orbit.  Thus \cref{prop:conjugacy} rules out a
\emph{total} period that depends only on the final denominator.  It does not
rule out local denominator increments, cyclic sums, coboundary corrections,
or endpoint-extended state spaces.
\end{remark}
