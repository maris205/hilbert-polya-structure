\section{Exact double-six and orientation fields}
\label{sec:double-six-fields}

The degree theorem identifies the subgroup that must occur at equality.  We
now identify its fixed field inside the particular extension \(K/\Q\).  The
construction begins with characteristic-zero incidence, passes to two
separating orbit invariants, and ends with a degree-\(12\) carrier for the
selected lines.

\subsection{The exact configuration set}

On the complete Grassmann chart used to construct \(E\), let
\(\widetilde g(d)\in\Z[d]\) be the raw primitive degree-\(27\) eliminant,
let \(c_g=[d^{27}]\widetilde g\ne0\), and put
\begin{equation}
 g(d)=c_g^{-1}\widetilde g(d).
\label{eq:monic-eliminant}
\end{equation}
A geometric line is recovered from a root \(x=d\) of the monic eliminant
\(g\).
The remaining line coordinates are rational polynomial functions of \(x\).
For roots \(x,y\), exact divided differences give a polynomial \(J(x,y)\)
whose vanishing is equivalent to incidence of the corresponding lines.  In
the algebra \((\Q[x]/(g(x)))[y]\), put
\begin{equation}
 H_x(y)=\gcd_y(g(y),J(x,y)).
\label{eq:incidence-gcd}
\end{equation}
The characteristic-zero identities are
\begin{equation}
 \deg_yH_x=10,\qquad \gcd(H_x,y-x)=1,\qquad
 g(y)=H_x(y)Q_x(y),\quad \deg_yQ_x=17.
\label{eq:incidence-identities}
\end{equation}
Thus every line meets exactly ten other lines, with no diagonal counted.  The
number of unordered meeting pairs is \(27\cdot10/2=135\).  Exact independent
set enumeration then gives
\begin{equation}
 135\text{ meeting pairs},\qquad
 72\text{ sixers},\qquad
 36\text{ double-sixes}.
\label{eq:configuration-counts}
\end{equation}
Good-prime graphs provide label-compatible cross-checks, while
\cref{eq:incidence-identities} is the characteristic-zero authority; no
ordering of approximate roots enters \cref{eq:configuration-counts}.

The exact \(\WE\)-action on these configurations has stabilizer data
\begin{equation}
 |U_1|=1440,\quad [\WE:U_1]=36,\quad
 \core_{\WE}(U_1)=1,\quad N_{\WE}(U_1)=U_1.
\label{eq:u1-exact-data}
\end{equation}
Its action on the twenty-seven lines has orbits of sizes \(12\) and \(15\),
and \(U_1\) fixes exactly the selected double-six.

\subsection{Separating invariants and resolvers}

Let \(d_i\) be the corresponding root of \(g\), and define the fixed integral
scaled coordinate
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_i=c_gd_i.
\label{eq:alpha-scaling}
\end{equation}
For an unordered double-six \(D\), define
\begin{equation}
 \theta_D=\sum_{\ell_i\in D}\alpha_i.
\label{eq:theta-def}
\end{equation}
For an orientation \(D=(\cE,\cG)\), define
\begin{equation}
 \beta_D=\sum_{\ell_i\in\cE}\alpha_i-
          \sum_{\ell_i\in\cG}\alpha_i,\qquad
 \delta_D=\beta_D^2.
\label{eq:beta-delta-def}
\end{equation}
The orbit products
\begin{equation}
 R_\theta(T)=\prod_D(T-\theta_D),\qquad
 R_\delta(T)=\prod_D(T-\delta_D)
\label{eq:resolvers}
\end{equation}
belong to \(\Q[T]\), are separable and irreducible, and have degree \(36\).
Candidate-blind Chinese remaindering, with proved coefficient bounds, is
recorded in \cref{app:incidence-resolvers}.  At two good reductions the
factor-degree patterns are
\begin{equation}
 [1,5,5,5,10,10],\qquad [9,9,9,9].
\label{eq:resolver-factor-patterns}
\end{equation}
Their proper subset-sum degree sets are disjoint, whereas a rational factor
would give a degree in both.  Hence the resolvers are irreducible.

The action on the exact orbit values gives
\begin{equation}
 \Stab(\theta_D)=\Stab(\delta_D)=U_1,\qquad
 \Stab(\beta_D)=U_1^+.
\label{eq:invariant-stabilizers}
\end{equation}
The \(36\) values \(\delta_D\) are distinct, the \(36\) values \(\beta_D\)
are nonzero, and all \(72\) oriented values are separated exactly.  The
central involution \(\iota\in U_1\setminus U_1^+\) sends \(\beta_D\) to
\(-\beta_D\).

\begin{theorem}[Configuration and orientation fields]
\label{thm:configuration-fields}
For every double-six \(D\),
\begin{equation}
 \Q(\theta_D)=\FD=\Q(\delta_D),
 \qquad
 \FDp=\FD(\beta_D)=\FD(\sqrt{\delta_D}).
\label{eq:field-identities}
\end{equation}
Both resolvers in \cref{eq:resolvers} have normal closure \(K\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The stabilizers in \cref{eq:invariant-stabilizers} and the Galois
correspondence give
\[
 \Q(\theta_D)=K^{U_1}=\Q(\delta_D).
\]
Their orbit sizes are \(36\), so the degree-\(36\) orbit polynomials are the
minimal polynomials.  The kernel of the coset action on the roots is
\(\core_{\WE}(U_1)\), which is trivial by
\cref{eq:u1-exact-data}.  Hence the splitting field of either polynomial is
the full \(K\).

The same argument for \(\beta_D\) gives
\(\Q(\beta_D)=K^{U_1^+}\).  Since \(U_1^+\) has index two in \(U_1\) and
\(\beta_D^2=\delta_D\), this field is exactly the quadratic extension in
\cref{eq:field-identities}.
\end{proof}

\Cref{tab:field-invariants} summarizes the three invariants.  The equality
\(\Q(\theta_D)=\Q(\delta_D)\) is a labeled stabilizer theorem.  We neither
need nor certify an expanded polynomial identity
\(\delta_D=P(\theta_D)\).

\subsection{The twelve-line carrier}

Let \(d_1,\ldots,d_{12}\) be the line parameters in \(D\), and let the
remaining fifteen roots of \(g\) be \(d_{13},\ldots,d_{27}\).  The orbit
decomposition \([12,15]\) gives monic polynomials
\begin{equation}
 \Acarrier(d)=\prod_{i=1}^{12}(d-d_i)\in\FD[d],\qquad
 \Bcarrier(d)=\prod_{i=13}^{27}(d-d_i)\in\FD[d].
\label{eq:carrier-factors}
\end{equation}
By \cref{eq:theta-def,eq:alpha-scaling}, its subtop coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 [d^{11}]\Acarrier=-\sum_{i=1}^{12}d_i=-\theta_D/c_g.
\label{eq:carrier-subtop}
\end{equation}
With the fixed monic normalization of \(g\), exact division and an independent
forward product prove
\begin{equation}
 g(d)=\Acarrier(d)\Bcarrier(d).
\label{eq:carrier-identity}
\end{equation}
The \(13\times36\) coefficient table is reconstructed with certified CRT
bounds.  Exact division and an independent forward product agree in all
\(28\) coefficients.  Together with the orbit binding, this identity---not
the stored table alone---certifies that \(\Acarrier\) cuts out exactly the
twelve lines of \(D\); see \cref{app:incidence-resolvers}.
