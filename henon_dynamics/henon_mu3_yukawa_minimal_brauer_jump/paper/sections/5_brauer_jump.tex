\section{Picard cohomology and the Brauer jump}
\label{sec:brauer-jump}

The preceding sections locate the smallest possible image subgroup.  To prove
that its fixed field actually attains the bound, we now pass from exact
integral group cohomology to the arithmetic Brauer quotient.  This passage is
a written number-field argument, not a semantic label attached to a Smith
normal form.

\subsection{Integral cohomology}

The exact Picard action is generated from the six standard \(E_6\)
reflections on the marked lattice \(\Lambda\).  Rebuilding cocycle relations
and principal cocycles over \(\Z\), and then computing Smith normal forms,
gives
\begin{equation}
 H^1(\WE,\Lambda)=0,\qquad
 H^1(U_1,\Lambda)\cong\Z/2.
\label{eq:exact-H1}
\end{equation}
The calculation is integral: it does not tensor with \(\Q\), reduce modulo
two, or infer torsion from a rational rank.  The full Smith data and the
independent cochain convention appear in \cref{app:cohomology}.

\begin{lemma}[Inflation for the line-field image]
\label{lem:inflation}
Let \(k/\Q\) be a number field, and suppose the \(G_k\)-action on \(\Lambda\)
has finite image \(H\subseteq\WE\).  Then
\[
 H^1(k,\Lambda)\cong H^1(H,\Lambda).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(N\) be the kernel of \(G_k\to H\).  It acts trivially on \(\Lambda\).
Hence
\[
 H^1(N,\Lambda)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(N,\Lambda).
\]
A continuous image of the profinite group \(N\) in the discrete lattice
\(\Lambda\) is finite, while the torsion-free group \(\Lambda\) has no
nonzero finite subgroup.  Thus \(H^1(N,\Lambda)=0\).  The
inflation--restriction sequence now makes inflation
\(H^1(H,\Lambda)\to H^1(k,\Lambda)\) an isomorphism.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The number-field Hochschild--Serre bridge}

Over \(\Qbar\), a smooth cubic surface is rational, so
\begin{equation}
 \Br(Y_{\Qbar})=0.
\label{eq:geometric-brauer-zero}
\end{equation}
For a number field \(k\), the low-degree Hochschild--Serre sequence has the
segment
\begin{equation}
 \Br(Y_k)/\operatorname{im}\Br(k)
 \longrightarrow H^1(k,\Picbar)
 \longrightarrow H^3(k,\Gm).
\label{eq:HS-segment}
\end{equation}
The last group vanishes in this number-field setting.  Because of
\cref{eq:geometric-brauer-zero}, every Brauer class on \(Y_k\) is algebraic,
and \cref{eq:HS-segment} yields
\begin{equation}
 \Br(Y_k)/\operatorname{im}\Br(k)
 \cong H^1(k,\Lambda).
\label{eq:brauer-picard-bridge}
\end{equation}
This is the number-field form of the Picard/Brauer bridge used by
Swinnerton-Dyer \cite{SwinnertonDyer1993}; a modern account of the relevant
low-degree sequence is given by Viray \cite{Viray2023}.

\begin{theorem}[The degree-\(36\) Brauer jump]
\label{thm:brauer-jump}
For the fixed cubic surface,
\begin{equation}
 \Br(Y)/\operatorname{im}\Br(\Q)=0,
\label{eq:base-brauer-zero}
\end{equation}
whereas for every double-six field \(\FD\),
\begin{equation}
 \Br(Y_{\FD})/\operatorname{im}\Br(\FD)\cong\Z/2.
\label{eq:FD-brauer}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Over \(\Q\), the image on \(\Lambda\) is the full group \(\WE\) by
\cref{eq:frozen-line-field}.  Apply
\cref{lem:inflation,eq:brauer-picard-bridge} and the first equality in
\cref{eq:exact-H1}; this proves \cref{eq:base-brauer-zero}.

For \(k=\FD=K^{U_1}\), the image of \(G_{\FD}\) on the lines and Picard
lattice is \(U_1\).  The same two bridges and the second equality in
\cref{eq:exact-H1} give \cref{eq:FD-brauer}.  Thus the field constructed in
\cref{thm:configuration-fields} attains the lower bound of
\cref{thm:degree}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Cohomological scope]
\Cref{eq:FD-brauer} identifies an algebraic/cohomological quotient.
It neither evaluates its nonzero element at a completion of \(\FD\) nor
compares adelic points.  The explicit global representative will be
constructed in \cref{sec:quaternion}; no local conclusion is implicit in the
group isomorphism above.
\end{remark}
