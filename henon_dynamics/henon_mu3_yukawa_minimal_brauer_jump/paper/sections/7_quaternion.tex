\section{Divisor exhaustion and the explicit Brauer generator}
\label{sec:quaternion}

The linear system in \cref{sec:canonical-quartic} only proves vanishing on
twelve lines.  We first show that those lines exhaust the quartic divisor.
We then use the oriented quadratic field to express the divisor of a rational
function as a norm.  Finally, and separately, we compare the associated
Picard cocycle with the unique nonzero cohomology class.

\subsection{The exact divisor}

\begin{proposition}[Degree exhaustion]
\label{prop:divisor-exhaustion}
The normalized determinant quartic satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \divv_{Y_{\FD}}(Q_D)=\cE+\cG.
\label{eq:QD-divisor}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The exact carrier and restriction replay supplies twelve distinct lines and
shows that \(Q_D\) vanishes identically on each.  Thus every line in
\(\cE+\cG\) is a prime component of \(\divv(Q_D)\) with multiplicity at
least one.  The quartic is not \(F\) times a linear form: its gauge class is
the normalized nonzero vector in \(\ker M_D\).  It therefore defines a
nonzero effective divisor on \(Y_{\FD}\) of class \(4H_Y\).

Intersecting with a hyperplane gives
\begin{equation}
 (4H_Y)\cdot H_Y=4H_Y^2=4\deg(Y)=12.
\label{eq:quartic-degree}
\end{equation}
Each of the twelve distinct carrier lines has hyperplane degree one, so the
known components already contribute the full degree \(12\).  Every nonzero
effective residual divisor has positive hyperplane degree, and any
multiplicity greater than one would also increase the degree.  Neither is
possible.  Hence \cref{eq:QD-divisor} holds exactly.
\end{proof}

This argument is the geometric boundary between the last two machine gates:
the \(60\times31\) rank calculation constructs \(Q_D\), while distinctness
and \cref{eq:quartic-degree} identify its divisor.  Restriction vanishing
alone would not rule out a residual curve.

\subsection{The norm divisor and unramifiedness}

Let
\begin{equation}
 \ell=u_0,\qquad \cL_0=\divv_Y(\ell),\qquad
 f_D=\frac{Q_D}{\ell^4}.
\label{eq:fD-def}
\end{equation}
The exact line replay verifies that no carrier line is contained in the
hyperplane \(\ell=0\).  Over the oriented field \(\FDp\), define
\begin{equation}
 \cD=\cE-2\cL_0.
\label{eq:oriented-divisor}
\end{equation}
The nontrivial automorphism \(\iota\) of \(\FDp/\FD\) exchanges \(\cE\) and
\(\cG\), and fixes \(\cL_0\).  Therefore
\begin{align}
 \Norm_{\FDp/\FD}(\cD)
 &=\cD+\iota(\cD)\nonumber\\
 &=\cE+\cG-4\cL_0\nonumber\\
 &=\divv(f_D).
\label{eq:norm-divisor}
\end{align}
The cyclic-algebra divisor criterion now shows that
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal A_D=(\FDp/\FD,f_D)
             =(\delta_D,Q_D/u_0^4)
\label{eq:quaternion}
\end{equation}
has zero residue at every codimension-one point of \(Y_{\FD}\), and hence
extends to an unramified class in \(\Br(Y_{\FD})\).  This is the same general
double-six mechanism that appears in the prior class construction
\cite{ElsenhansJahnel2010Brauer}; the input specific to this surface is the
exact divisor \cref{eq:QD-divisor}.

\subsection{Unramified does not yet mean nonzero}

We identify the class of \cref{eq:quaternion} in
\(H^1(U_1,\Lambda)\).  In the basis of \cref{eq:picard-basis}, put
\begin{equation}
 d_0=e_\Sigma-2h.
\label{eq:d0-def}
\end{equation}
The first sixer has class \(e_\Sigma\), while its opposite has class
\(12h-5e_\Sigma\).  Since \([\cL_0]=H_Y\),
\begin{equation}
 [\cD]=e_\Sigma-2H_Y
       =3e_\Sigma-6h
       =3d_0.
\label{eq:D-class}
\end{equation}

Write \(U_1=U_1^+\times\langle\iota\rangle\), with \(\iota\) the central
involution exchanging the sixers.  The exact Picard action gives
\begin{equation}
 \iota(h)=5h-2e_\Sigma,\qquad
 \iota(e_\Sigma)=12h-5e_\Sigma,\qquad
 \iota(d_0)=-d_0.
\label{eq:iota-action}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda^{U_1^+}=\Z h\oplus\Z e_\Sigma.
\label{eq:S6-invariants}
\end{equation}
For \(x=ah+be_\Sigma\), direct substitution into
\cref{eq:iota-action} yields
\[
 (1+\iota)x=(6a+12b)h-(2a+4b)e_\Sigma,
\]
and
\[
 (\iota-1)x=4(a+3b)h-2(a+3b)e_\Sigma.
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \ker(1+\iota\mid\Lambda^{S_6})=\Z d_0,\qquad
 (\iota-1)\Lambda^{S_6}=2\Z d_0.
\label{eq:coboundary-lattice}
\end{equation}

With the norm-divisor convention in \cref{eq:norm-divisor}, the
Hochschild--Serre cocycle of \(\mathcal A_D\) is zero on \(U_1^+=S_6\) and
takes \(\iota\) to
\[
 [\cD]=3d_0.
\]
If this cocycle were a \(U_1\)-coboundary, its vanishing on \(S_6\) would
force a cobounding class into \(\Lambda^{S_6}\).  Its value at \(\iota\)
would then lie in \((\iota-1)\Lambda^{S_6}=2\Z d_0\), contrary to
\[
 3d_0\notin2\Z d_0.
\]
Thus the class is nonzero.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit generator]
\label{thm:explicit-generator}
The quaternion
\[
 \mathcal A_D=(\delta_D,Q_D/u_0^4)
\]
is unramified on \(Y_{\FD}\) and represents the unique nonzero element of
\[
 \Br(Y_{\FD})/\operatorname{im}\Br(\FD)\cong\Z/2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Unramifiedness follows from \cref{eq:norm-divisor}.  The calculation
\cref{eq:D-class,eq:coboundary-lattice} proves that its Picard cocycle is
nonzero.  By \cref{eq:exact-H1,eq:brauer-picard-bridge}, this cocycle is the
unique nonzero cohomology class and therefore the generator of the displayed
Brauer quotient.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Global boundary]
The proof separates three logically different assertions: the norm divisor
proves unramifiedness, the integral lattice proves nontriviality, and neither
computes a local evaluation.  In particular,
\cref{thm:explicit-generator} does not assert that \(Y(\FD)\) is empty or
nonempty, and it does not establish a Brauer--Manin obstruction, a
Hasse-principle failure, or a failure of weak approximation.
\end{remark}
