\begin{abstract}
For every ring length $N\ge1$ and every conserved particle number
$0\le k\le N$, we classify the periodic states of cyclic Rule 184 and prove
finite attraction to them. Below half density, the periodic core consists
exactly of words without cyclic $11$ and the rule is right rotation there;
above half density, holes are isolated and the rule is left rotation. A
gap-defect Lyapunov argument gives an explicit $m^2$ entry bound, where
$m=\min(k,N-k)$. Cyclic independent-set counts then yield a closed formula
for every iterate, all primitive temporal cycles, the Artin--Mazur zeta, and
the periodic-core Koopman determinant. The whole sector is a permutation
exactly when $m\le1$; otherwise its transients block full-system unitarity.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Rule 184; traffic cellular automaton; periodic
core; gap Lyapunov function; cyclic independent set; dynamical zeta.

\section{Model and complete periodic core}
For labelled cyclic sites, set
\[
X_{N,k}=\left\{x\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z}:
\sum_i x_i=k\right\},\qquad
F(x)_i=x_{i-1}(1-x_i)+x_ix_{i+1}.
\]
Thus every cyclic $10$ is updated simultaneously to $01$. One update is the
clock, $(\rho x)_i=x_{i-1}$ is right rotation, and
$m=\min(k,N-k)$.

\begin{theorem}[All-size, all-density classification]
If $k\le N/2$, a state is periodic exactly when it has no cyclic $11$, and
$F=\rho$ on that core. If $k\ge N/2$, a state is periodic exactly when it
has no cyclic $00$, and $F=\rho^{-1}$ on that core. Every state reaches the
applicable core within $m^2$ updates. Every temporal least period divides
$N$ and equals the least cyclic-rotation period of the word.
\end{theorem}

At half filling the two conditions leave exactly the two alternating states,
both of period two. At $k=0,N$, the unique uniform state is fixed. These
statements include $N=1$ and $N=2$ without changing the update convention.

\section{Gap defects and finite attraction}
Assume first $0<k\le N/2$ and label the $m=k$ particles in cyclic order.
Let $g_i\ge0$ be the number of holes between particle $i$ and its successor,
and put $u_i=\mathbf1_{g_i>0}$. Particle $i$ moves precisely when $u_i=1$;
the simultaneous gap update is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
g_i'=g_i-u_i+u_{i+1}.
\end{equation}
A new zero at index $i$ occurs exactly when the old $g_{i+1}=0$ and
$g_i\le1$. Hence every old zero-gap marker shifts one index backward unless
its predecessor is at least two, in which case that marker is absorbed. The
defect count $Z(g)=\#\{i:g_i=0\}$ never increases.

Suppose $Z(g)>0$. Since $\sum_i g_i=N-k\ge k=m$, some gap is at least two.
An excess gap remains at its index until a zero arrives immediately after it:
if its successor is positive, both terms in \eqref{eq:gap} cancel; if the
successor is zero, the excess absorbs that zero and decreases by one. Every
unabsorbed zero moves backward once per update, so within at most $m$ steps a
zero meets an excess and $Z$ strictly decreases. Initially $Z\le m$;
therefore after at most $m^2$ updates every gap is positive. This is exactly
the no-$11$ core, where every particle advances and $F=\rho$.

Above half density, holes move left. Exchanging particles with holes and
reversing cyclic orientation reproduces \eqref{eq:gap}, proving attraction
to the no-$00$ core and $F=\rho^{-1}$ there. Conversely each core word is
periodic because rotation has order dividing $N$. If a periodic non-core
word entered the forward-invariant core, a sufficiently large multiple of
its period would put the original word in the core, a contradiction. This
proves completeness rather than merely eventual regularity.

\section{All iterates, primitive cycles, and zeta}
Let $I(g,r)$ count cyclic length-$g$ words with $r$ isolated marked sites:
\[
I(g,r)=
\begin{cases}
1,&r=0,\\[1mm]
\dfrac{g}{g-r}\binom{g-r}{r},&1\le r\le\lfloor g/2\rfloor,\\[1mm]
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
For $r>0$, count linearly nonadjacent words and remove those with both
endpoints marked, with out-of-range binomial coefficients interpreted as zero:
\[
\binom{g-r+1}{r}-\binom{g-r-1}{r-2}
=\frac{g}{g-r}\binom{g-r}{r}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Every-iterate fixed count]
For $n\ge1$, put $g=\gcd(N,n)$ and $q=N/g$. Then
\[
A_{N,k}(n):=\#\operatorname{Fix}(F^n|X_{N,k})=
\begin{cases}
I(g,m/q),&q\mid m,\\
0,&q\nmid m.
\end{cases}
\]
\end{theorem}
An $F^n$-fixed word is periodic and hence lies in the isolated-minority core.
There $F^n$ is rotation by $n$ in one of the two directions. Its invariant
words are $q$ repetitions of a cyclic block of length $g$. Divisibility
$q\mid m$ is necessary, and the block contains $m/q$ isolated minority
symbols, proving the formula. At half filling it gives two fixed points
exactly for even $n$; at $k=0,N$ it gives one for every $n$.

For $d\mid N$, Möbius inversion separates repeated from primitive data:
\[
E_{N,k}(d)=\sum_{e\mid d}\mu(d/e)A_{N,k}(e),\qquad
P_{N,k}(d)=\frac{E_{N,k}(d)}d.
\]
Here $E$ counts labelled points of exact period $d$, while $P$ counts
geometric primitive cycles. Consequently
\[
\zeta_{N,k}(z)=\prod_{d\mid N}(1-z^d)^{-P_{N,k}(d)},\qquad
\det(I-zU_{\rm core})=\prod_{d\mid N}(1-z^d)^{P_{N,k}(d)}.
\]
The determinant belongs to the finite permutation on the periodic core; it
is exactly the reciprocal source zeta and is not a determinant invented for
the transient full system.

\section{Exact Koopman boundary}
Every word in $X_{N,k}$ has isolated minority symbols exactly when $m\le1$.
Thus the whole-sector map is a rotation permutation exactly in those sectors,
and its uniform Koopman composition operator is unitary. If $m\ge2$, a word
with adjacent minority symbols exists and is transient by the classification
theorem. A finite self-map with a transient state is not bijective, so its
uniform whole-sector Koopman operator is not unitary.

The periodic core remains canonical for every sector. Restriction gives a
finite rotation permutation, and spatial reflection reverses that rotation.
This is a natural source-level operator on the core, but the restriction
deletes transient traffic states. The all-sector family therefore receives
only \texttt{A4\_FORMAL\_HINT}, not natural quantization of the original full
phase spaces.

\paragraph{Limitations.}

finite zeta factors do not supply prime semantics. There is no target
