\begin{abstract}
The locally normalized ratio of the odd H\'enon reflection-packet Euler
product to the reverse-action Lind zeta has one rational singular channel
for every positive integer.  Its first positive singularity can be removed,
but all later channels leave essential singularities.  We construct an exact
counterterm for the complete positive and complex ladder.  A level $m$ has
$2m$ poles
$\alpha_{m,k}=2^{-1/(2m)}e^{\pi\ii k/m}$ with principal coefficients
$b_{m,k}=c_m(-1)^k/(\sqrt2m)$.  Directly multiplying these pole factors is
invalid: their absolute logarithmic mass diverges even at the origin.  We
subtract Taylor degrees $0,\ldots,m-1$ from each level-$m$ pole.  Root
orthogonality makes the subtractions cancel within each level, while the
regularized double series becomes absolutely normally convergent and hence
independent of every pole ordering.  Its sum is exactly the full residual
channel tail.  After adding the remaining negative first-channel source
factor and a base-point normalization, the resulting counterterm
$\Kall$ satisfies $\Kall\Crel=1$ on compatible branches.  This theorem gives
exact punctured analytic renormalization.  It does not construct a transfer
operator or attach arithmetic meaning to the channel index.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The full area-preserving H\'enon horseshoe is conjugate to the full two-shift
in the frozen hyperbolic regime \citep{DevaneyNitecki1979}.  Reflection-fixed
periodic words therefore define an exact symbolic packet inside the H\'enon
system.  Uniform cyclic sampling of that packet leads to an orbit-resolved
Euler product, while the reverse action on the full shift has the distinct
Lind zeta calculated by \citet{KimLeePark2003}.  Their locally normalized
ratio is analytic across the first positive entropy boundary, but its exact
continuation contains infinitely many later singular channels.

The remaining problem is not to locate another singularity.  Every channel
and every principal coefficient is already explicit.  The problem is to
turn the complete singularity ledger into one counterterm without imposing
an arbitrary summation order.  Splitting a rational channel into its complex
simple poles initially makes matters worse: signed cancellation occurs only
after all poles of a level are grouped, and the ungrouped absolute mass
diverges.  Thus a formal product over the complex divisor does not yet define
an analytic object.

We resolve this ordering defect with a level-dependent Weierstrass
regularization.  At level $m$, each simple pole is stripped of its Taylor
terms through degree $m-1$.  The $2m$ roots annihilate all of those terms
exactly, so the signed level remains unchanged.  Each individual term now
starts at degree $m$, which yields a geometric compact majorant.  The same
construction simultaneously proves arbitrary pole-order independence and
recovers the original scalar-channel tail.

The contributions are concrete.
\begin{enumerate}
\item We determine the full complex pole divisor and an exact simple-pole
partial fraction for every scalar channel.
\item We prove that the raw individual pole factors are not absolutely
summable, so channel-grouped convergence cannot be promoted to arbitrary
pole ordering without a new argument.
\item We prove that genus $m-1$ regularization preserves every channel and
makes the complete double series absolutely normally convergent.
\item We include the negative first-channel source factor and prove the
normalized identity $\Kall(t)\Crel(t)=1$ on compatible branches.
\end{enumerate}
The last identity is an analytic cancellation theorem, not an operator
realization: $\Kall$ deliberately copies the channel ledger that it removes.

\section{The frozen relative germ}

For the full two-shift with reverse flip, the source formula is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lind}
 \zflip(t)=(1-2t^2)^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left(\frac{2t+3t^2}{1-2t^2}\right).
\end{equation}
The odd primitive reflection packet instead gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packet}
 \Zorb(t,1)=\prod_{\substack{n\ge1\\n\ \mathrm{odd}}}(1-t^n)^{-D_n},
\end{equation}
where $D_n$ counts primitive marked reflection words.  Equation
\eqref{eq:packet} is not the full infinite-dihedral Lind zeta.

Put $u=1-\sqrt2t$.  The first-boundary normalization is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative}
 \Crel(t)=u^{1/2}e^{-3/(4u)}\frac{\zflip(t)}{\Zorb(t,1)}.
\end{equation}
On a compatible local branch, this function extends holomorphically and
nonvanishingly through $u=0$.  The power $1/2$ and coefficient $3/4$ are
forced within the power-exponential class.

The exact primitive/repetition regrouping uses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi-c}
 \Phi(x)=\frac{2x}{1-2x^2},\qquad
 c_m=\frac1m\sum_{\substack{d\mid m\\d\ \mathrm{odd}}}d\mu(d)
     =\frac1m\prod_{\substack{p\mid m\\p\ \mathrm{odd}}}(1-p).
\end{equation}
Every $c_m$ is nonzero and $|c_m|\le1$.  Define the later-channel tail
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
 \Lm(t)=\sum_{m\ge2}c_m\Phi(t^m).
\end{equation}
Previous exact regrouping gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:previous-cont}
 \log\Crel(t)=H_{\rm rel}(1-\sqrt2t)-\Lm(t),
 \qquad
 H_{\rm rel}(u)=-\frac12\log(2-u)
 -\frac{3(2u-3)}{4(u-2)}.
\end{equation}
The channel series is normally convergent on compact subsets of $\D$ after
its poles are removed.  That grouped statement does not yet justify a
product over individual complex poles.

\section{The full complex divisor}

For $m\ge2$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:roots}
 \rho_m=2^{-1/(2m)},\qquad
 \alpha_{m,k}=\rho_m e^{\pi\ii k/m},\quad 0\le k<2m,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bmk}
 b_{m,k}=\frac{c_m(-1)^k}{\sqrt2m}.
\end{equation}
The points in \eqref{eq:roots} are the roots of $1-2t^{2m}$.  Their moduli
$\rho_m$ increase strictly to one, so distinct levels have disjoint pole
sets.  Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:singular-set}
 \mathcal S=\{\alpha_{m,k}:m\ge2,\ 0\le k<2m\},\qquad
 \Omega=\D\setminus\mathcal S.
\end{equation}
The set $\mathcal S$ is locally finite in $\D$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact complex partial fractions]\label{thm:partial}
For every $m\ge2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial}
 c_m\Phi(t^m)=\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}
 \frac{b_{m,k}}{1-t/\alpha_{m,k}}.
\end{equation}
At the pole $\alpha_{m,k}$, the coefficient of
$(1-t/\alpha_{m,k})^{-1}$ is $b_{m,k}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $v=1-t/\alpha_{m,k}$.  Since
$\alpha_{m,k}^m=2^{-1/2}(-1)^k$, expansion of the denominator gives
\[
 \Phi(t^m)=\frac{(-1)^k}{\sqrt2m}\frac1v+O(1).
\]
Thus the two sides of \eqref{eq:partial} have identical principal parts.
Their difference is a rational function without finite poles and vanishes at
infinity, so it is zero.

For a coefficient-level check, put $\zeta=e^{\pi\ii/m}$.  The coefficient of
$t^j$ on the right contains
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}(-1)^k\zeta^{-jk}
 =\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}\zeta^{(m-j)k}.
\]
This root sum vanishes unless $j\equiv m\pmod{2m}$.  At
$j=m(2\ell+1)$, the surviving coefficient is $c_m2^{\ell+1}$, exactly the
expansion of $c_m\Phi(t^m)$.
\end{proof}

The theorem shows that each positive singularity belongs to a complete
$2m$-point circle.  Any counterterm confined to the positive ray omits
$2m-1$ poles at level $m$.

\section{Why the raw pole product fails}

A tempting construction is to exponentiate each summand in
\eqref{eq:partial} and multiply over $(m,k)$.  Channel by channel, this
recovers $e^{\Lm(t)}$.  The ungrouped product, however, has no
order-independent meaning.

\begin{proposition}[Raw absolute-mass obstruction]\label{prop:raw}
The double series
\begin{equation}\label{eq:raw-series}
 \sum_{m\ge2}\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}
 \frac{b_{m,k}}{1-t/\alpha_{m,k}}
\end{equation}
is not absolutely summable at $t=0$.  Consequently the associated raw
exponential pole factors cannot be multiplied in arbitrary pole order.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At level $m$, the absolute logarithmic mass at the origin equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:level-mass}
 \sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}|b_{m,k}|=\sqrt2|c_m|.
\end{equation}
For every odd prime $p$, formula \eqref{eq:phi-c} gives
$|c_p|=(p-1)/p\ge2/3$.  The subsum of \eqref{eq:level-mass} over the
infinitely many odd primes diverges.
\end{proof}

The obstruction does not contradict the channel-grouped continuation in
\eqref{eq:tail}.  Within each fixed $m$, the alternating roots cancel exactly.
It proves that this grouping is mathematically active and cannot be silently
discarded.

\section{Genus-dependent pole regularization}

We now replace conditional level cancellation by an absolutely convergent
double series.  Define
\begin{align}
 R_{m,k}(t)
 &=b_{m,k}\left[
   \frac1{1-t/\alpha_{m,k}}
   -\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\frac{t}{\alpha_{m,k}}\right)^j
   \right] \label{eq:R-def}\\
 &=b_{m,k}\frac{(t/\alpha_{m,k})^m}
 {1-t/\alpha_{m,k}}. \label{eq:R-tail}
\end{align}
This is a genus-$m-1$ Weierstrass subtraction for the logarithm of an
exponential pole factor.  The increasing genus is essential: each
individual term must begin at a degree that tends to infinity with $m$.

\begin{lemma}[Levelwise Taylor cancellation]\label{lem:cancellation}
For $0\le j<m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-cancel}
 \sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}b_{m,k}\alpha_{m,k}^{-j}=0.
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:level-identity}
 \sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}R_{m,k}(t)=c_m\Phi(t^m).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
After removing a nonzero factor independent of $k$, the sum in
\eqref{eq:root-cancel} is
$\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}e^{\pi\ii(m-j)k/m}$.  For $0\le j<m$, its exponent is not
divisible by $2m$, so the geometric root sum is zero.  Summing
\eqref{eq:R-def} over $k$ therefore removes all subtraction polynomials.
The remaining pole terms equal $c_m\Phi(t^m)$ by \cref{thm:partial}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Absolute normal convergence and order independence]
\label{thm:normal}
The double series
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regularized-double}
 \sum_{m\ge2}\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}R_{m,k}(t)
\end{equation}
converges absolutely and normally on compact subsets of $\Omega$.  Its sum
is $\Lm(t)$, and every enumeration of the individual pairs $(m,k)$ has the
same sum.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pole-product}
 \prod_{m\ge2}\prod_{k=0}^{2m-1}e^{R_{m,k}(t)}
 =e^{\Lm(t)}
\end{equation}
is a locally uniform, nonzero, order-independent product on $\Omega$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a compact $K\subset\Omega$ and choose $r<q<1$ with $|t|\le r$ on $K$.
Because $\rho_m\nearrow1$, all sufficiently large $m$ satisfy
$|t/\alpha_{m,k}|\le q$ uniformly for $t\in K$ and all $k$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:R-tail}, $|c_m|\le1$, and the $2m$ poles give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:majorant}
 \sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}|R_{m,k}(t)|
 \le \frac{\sqrt2|c_m|q^m}{1-q}
 \le \frac{\sqrt2q^m}{1-q}.
\end{equation}
The right side is summable in $m$.  The finitely many earlier levels are
bounded on $K$ because $K$ avoids their poles.  The Weierstrass test proves
absolute normal convergence.  Arbitrary rearrangements preserve an
absolutely convergent series, while \cref{lem:cancellation} identifies its
sum with \eqref{eq:tail}.  Exponentiation yields \eqref{eq:pole-product}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The proof distinguishes two statements that look similar in channel
notation.  P72 supplies normal convergence after each rational level is
assembled.  \Cref{thm:normal} supplies absolute normal convergence before
level assembly, which is precisely the property needed for an unordered
product over the complex divisor.
\end{remark}

\section{The normalized full-ladder counterterm}

The tail product \eqref{eq:pole-product} removes all channels $m\ge2$, but
the source remainder in \eqref{eq:previous-cont} still contains the negative
first-channel boundary.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:w}
 w=1+\sqrt2t=2-u.
\end{equation}
Direct substitution into $H_{\rm rel}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-w}
 H_{\rm rel}(u)=\frac{3}{4w}-\frac12\log w-\frac32.
\end{equation}
Thus $w=0$ carries both an exponential singularity and a square-root branch.

Choose a simply connected slit subdomain
$U\subset\Omega\setminus\{-1/\sqrt2\}$ that contains the origin and on which
$\log w$ is defined, and take the compatible branch of $w^{1/2}$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Kall}
 \Kall(t)=e^{3/2}w^{1/2}e^{-3/(4w)}
 \prod_{m\ge2}\prod_{k=0}^{2m-1}e^{R_{m,k}(t)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact full-ladder cancellation]\label{thm:main}
The product in \eqref{eq:Kall} is locally uniform and independent of the
enumeration of its individual complex pole factors.  On every compatible
branch,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
 \boxed{\Kall(t)\Crel(t)=1.}
\end{equation}
The normalization is fixed at the origin:
$\Kall(0)=e^{3/4}$ and $\Crel(0)=e^{-3/4}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:normal}, the logarithm of the double product in
\eqref{eq:Kall} is $\Lm(t)$.  Equations \eqref{eq:previous-cont} and
\eqref{eq:H-w} therefore give
\begin{align*}
 \log\Kall(t)+\log\Crel(t)
 &=\left(\frac32+\frac12\log w-\frac{3}{4w}+\Lm(t)\right)\\
 &\quad+\left(\frac{3}{4w}-\frac12\log w-\frac32-\Lm(t)\right)=0.
\end{align*}
Exponentiating proves \eqref{eq:identity}.  At $t=0$, one has $w=1$ and
$\Lm(0)=0$, giving the two stated values.
\end{proof}

The factor $w^{1/2}e^{-3/(4w)}$ is not another later channel.  It removes the
source-native negative boundary left after the positive local normalization
in \eqref{eq:relative}.  Omitting it would leave \eqref{eq:H-w} singular.

\section{Certification and claim boundary}

The accompanying exact certificate checks the divisor and coefficients at
three distinct layers.  First, integer M\"obius sums agree with the
odd-radical product for $c_m$.  Second, 96 formal coefficients of
\eqref{eq:tail} agree with the primitive/repetition ledger after the first
channel is removed.  Third, the root cancellations and rational geometric
majorants are recorded level by level.  An independent program reconstructs
63 regularized levels at three complex sample points.  Those floating checks
audit signs and indexing; the proof of \cref{thm:normal} is the exact root
sum and majorant, not a numerical extrapolation.

Six hashes bind the result to the P71 and P72 proof packages, certificates,
and PDFs.  Eight tests run under ordinary and optimized Python, and a hostile
mutation audit rejects promotions of the analytic identity to an operator,
an arithmetic trace, or Route B.

The scope restriction is structural.  Formula \eqref{eq:Kall} is built by
copying every scalar channel of $\Lm$ and reversing its sign in
$\log\Crel$.  It supplies no independently defined Banach space, kernel,
iteration law, or trace formula.  A rank-one or diagonal operator could be
reverse-engineered from almost any nonzero holomorphic function; such an
encoding would not show that H\'enon dynamics owns the determinant.  The next
operator test must define its state space and action before matching
\eqref{eq:identity}.

Likewise, the integer $m$ labels a M\"obius/repetition channel.  Nothing in
the construction identifies $m$ with a rational prime or prime power, and no
von-Mangoldt amplitude or explicit formula follows.  Route B is not
authorized.

\section{Conclusion}

The complete complex singularity ladder admits an exact counterterm, but
only after its conditionally grouped pole decomposition is regularized.
Genus $m-1$ subtraction preserves each rational channel by root
orthogonality and makes the individual pole family absolutely normally
convergent.  The resulting order-independent product, together with the
negative first-channel source factor, cancels the relative germ exactly.

This closes the analytic renormalization question raised by the infinite
ladder.  It does not close the ownership question.  The counterterm knows the
answer because it contains the full channel ledger.  A substantive next step
is therefore a rigidity theorem modulo holomorphic gauge, followed by a
transfer model whose kernel and trace iterates are fixed independently of the
target product.
