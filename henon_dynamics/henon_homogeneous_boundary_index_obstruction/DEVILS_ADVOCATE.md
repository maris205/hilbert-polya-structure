# Devil's advocate

## “A coboundary can still have an anomaly”

Correct, but only after choosing a polarization for which the gauge is not
admissible.  This project tests the standard Hardy polarization and finds
that the cubic gauge is too singular for the restricted-unitary determinant
line: the commutator is noncompact.  That is an unavailable index, not a
nonzero index.

## “The Poisson map might create a nonzero index”

Also possible in principle.  C35 does not prove injectivity on the two
boundary directions.  C37 therefore states the zero pair index only before
the Poisson map.  Any proposed post-Poisson index must specify the quotient,
compute its kernel, prove boundedness/Fredholmness, and show gauge
invariance.  Calling the distributional boundary term an index is not
enough.

An explicit finite-codimension model shows the danger: a noninjective map
can kill the unique direction on one side and retain it on the other,
changing essential codimension from zero to \(-1\).  Thus no post-Poisson
index follows merely from the two quotient dimensions being at most one.

## “The homogeneous Mellin symbol is strip-safe, so Route A passes”

No.  Strip safety is an A3 compatibility fact.  The normalized scalar
increment is one, prime holonomies are trivial, and no Hénon-generated
Fredholm determinant exists.  Importing \(\xi\) from the scaling mother
system cannot count as Hénon evidence.

## “The theorem closes all Hénon quantizations”

It does not.  Matrix-valued, projective, graded, and Kummer extensions need
not be scalar coboundaries.  The next proposed door is precisely a
\(\mathbb Z/3\)-graded cubic lift.

## “The groupoid theorem is tautological”

The algebra is elementary, but the research consequence is not: it closes
the exact missing bridge declared by C35 and C36.  It also prevents a
formally attractive gamma symbol from being promoted to a false Route-A
success.
