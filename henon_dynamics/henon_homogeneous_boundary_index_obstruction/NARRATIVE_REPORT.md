# Narrative report

C36 removed the linear term from the H6 generating phase because the
inhomogeneous Mellin scattering factor had a certified off-line strip
divisor.  The resulting homogeneous map had an exact gamma symbol with no
open-strip zeros or poles.  That apparent success isolated a sharper
question: can the scalar homogeneous phase survive passage to the Poisson
boundary as an index anomaly?

C37 answers the canonical versions negatively.  The chronological scaling
cocycle is literally \(\phi(ax)/\phi(x)\).  The gauge \(\phi^{-1}\)
simultaneously untwists rational quotient descent and idele scaling, so all
prime loop weights telescope to one.  Before applying Poisson summation, the
two natural boundary hyperplanes have essential codimension zero.  This
index cannot be transported through a noninjective Poisson map without a
kernel theorem; an explicit model shows one direction can collapse while
the other survives.

The most natural mechanism for a coboundary anomaly is a Hardy
polarization.  Here it fails decisively.  On explicit shrinking intervals,
the cubic chirp approaches a nonconstant half-wave, leaving normalized
mean-square oscillation greater than \(51/100\).  The physical and
log-scaling chirps are not VMO, their Hardy commutators are noncompact, and
the restricted-Grassmannian determinant line is unavailable.

The conclusion is scoped but substantial.  Scalar homogeneous chirps do
not create a functorial Hénon increment for the inherited Riemann scaling
system.  The next large experiment must change representation category:
retain cubic residue/Kummer channels as a nonscalar \(\mathbb Z/3\)-graded
object and ask whether their monodromy survives the global product formula.
