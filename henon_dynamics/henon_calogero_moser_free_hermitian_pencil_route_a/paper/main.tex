\begin{abstract}
For every particle number $N\ge2$, repulsive coupling $g>0$, ordered real
initial positions, and real momenta, we recover the rational
Calogero--Moser trajectory as the ordered spectrum of one Hermitian pencil
$Q_0+tL_0$.  A rank-one commutator proves that this pencil is simple for
every real physical time.  Eigenvalue perturbation then gives the Hamilton
equations with the exact force coefficient $2g^2$, hence collision-free
global completeness, while unitary transport of $L_0$ gives every trace
integral and $\operatorname{Tr}L^2=2H$.  Diagonalizing $L_0$ also gives a
global scattering atlas: ordered asymptotic velocities and arbitrary real
intercepts reconstruct one and only one ordered phase point.  The two time
ends reverse particle rank but preserve the intercept attached to each
spectral line; distinct velocities also rule out bounded nonconstant periodic
motion.  These are classical Calogero--Moser/Moser structures assembled with
explicit convention and scope locks, not a new priority claim.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Calogero--Moser system; Hermitian pencil; Lax
pair; complete flow; isospectral integrals.

\section{Frozen Hamiltonian and matrix convention}
On the chamber $q_1<\cdots<q_N$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 H(q,p)=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^N p_j^2+
 \sum_{1\le j<k\le N}\frac{g^2}{(q_j-q_k)^2},\qquad g>0.
\end{equation}
With $Q_0=\operatorname{diag}(q_1,\ldots,q_N)$, define the Hermitian matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L}
 (L_0)_{jk}=p_j\delta_{jk}+
 \frac{ig(1-\delta_{jk})}{q_j-q_k},\qquad X(t)=Q_0+tL_0.
\end{equation}
The sign, unordered-pair normalization in~\eqref{eq:H}, and physical clock
$t$ are part of the claim.  Direct multiplication gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:comm}
 [Q_0,L_0]=ig(J-I),\qquad J=ee^*,\quad e=(1,\ldots,1)^T,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \operatorname{Tr}L_0^2=\sum_jp_j^2+2\sum_{j<k}
 \frac{g^2}{(q_j-q_k)^2}=2H.
\end{equation}
Calogero's inverse-square model and Moser's isospectral solution retain
classical ownership~\cite{C69,M75}.

\section{A rank-one proof of simplicity}
For all real $t$,
$[X(t),L_0]=[Q_0,L_0]=ig(J-I)$.  Suppose an eigenspace $E$ of $X(t)$ had
dimension at least two, and let $P_E$ be its orthogonal projector.  Because
$X(t)|_E$ is scalar,
\[
 P_E[X(t),L_0]P_E=0.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:comm} would force $P_EJP_E=P_E$.  The left side has rank
at most one, whereas the right side has rank at least two, a contradiction.
Thus the increasingly ordered eigenvalues
\[
 x_1(t)<\cdots<x_N(t)
\]
of $X(t)$ exist without a crossing for every $t\in\mathbb R$.  The same
argument, compressing~\eqref{eq:comm} to an eigenspace of $L_0$, also proves
that $L_0$ has simple spectrum.

\section{Newton equations and global completeness}
Choose a smooth unitary eigenbasis $U(t)$ of $X(t)$ and gauge its columns so
that $e^*U(t)=(1,\ldots,1)$.  The gauge is possible: the diagonal compression
of~\eqref{eq:comm} says that every overlap has modulus one.  Put
$\widetilde L=U^*L_0U$.  The off-diagonal part of the commutator gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:off}
 (x_j-x_k)\widetilde L_{jk}=ig\quad(j\ne k).
\end{equation}
Hermitian eigenvalue perturbation gives
\[
 \dot x_j=\widetilde L_{jj},\qquad
 \ddot x_j=2\sum_{k\ne j}
 \frac{|\widetilde L_{kj}|^2}{x_j-x_k}
 =2g^2\sum_{k\ne j}(x_j-x_k)^{-3}.
\]
At $t=0$, $(x_j,\dot x_j)=(q_j,p_j)$, so these are precisely Hamilton's
equations for~\eqref{eq:H}.  Local uniqueness on the chamber identifies the
pencil curve with the physical solution.  Since $X(t)$ exists and remains
simple for every real $t$, the solution is collision-free and complete in
both time directions.

Finally $L(t)=U(t)^*L_0U(t)$, hence for every integer $m\ge1$,
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}L(t)^m=\operatorname{Tr}L_0^m.
\]
In particular~\eqref{eq:energy} is the Hamiltonian integral.

\section{A global spectral atlas}
Let $\lambda_1<\cdots<\lambda_N$ be the simple eigenvalues of $L_0$, and let
$v_a$ be normalized eigenvectors.  Compressing~\eqref{eq:comm} to
$\mathbb Cv_a$ shows $|e^*v_a|=1$; fix the remaining phase by $e^*v_a=1$.
Define the real intercept coordinates
\[
 a_a=v_a^*Q_0v_a.
\]
In this gauged $L_0$-eigenbasis, the commutator determines every entry of
$\widetilde Q$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:atlas}
 \widetilde L=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N),\qquad
 \widetilde Q_{aa}=a_a,\quad
 \widetilde Q_{ab}=\frac{ig}{\lambda_b-\lambda_a}\quad(a\ne b).
\end{equation}
Thus a phase point determines a unique pair
$(\lambda_1<\cdots<\lambda_N,a\in\mathbb R^N)$.

Conversely, start with any such pair and define the Hermitian matrix
$\widetilde Q$ by~\eqref{eq:atlas}.  It obeys
$[\widetilde Q,\widetilde L]=ig(J-I)$.  The same compression/rank argument
proves that $\widetilde Q$ has simple spectrum.  Order its eigenvalues and
diagonalize it.  The diagonal compression of the commutator makes every
component of the transformed $e$ have modulus one; a diagonal phase gauge
makes all components equal to one.  In that basis
\[
 Q=\operatorname{diag}(q_1<\cdots<q_N),\qquad
 L_{jk}=p_j\delta_{jk}+\frac{ig(1-\delta_{jk})}{q_j-q_k},
\]
with real $p_j$.  This construction is inverse to the forward one, so
$(\lambda,a)$ is a global bijective spectral atlas, not merely asymptotic
notation.  No symplectic-form claim is needed here.

\section{Both scattering ends and ordering reversal}
Apply nondegenerate Hermitian perturbation theory to
$t(L_0+t^{-1}Q_0)$ as $t\to+\infty$.  The gauge above identifies the first
correction with $a_j$, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:plus}
 x_j(t)=t\lambda_j+a_j+O(t^{-1}),\qquad
 \dot x_j(t)=\lambda_j+O(t^{-2}).
\end{equation}
For $t\to-\infty$, multiplication by the negative scalar reverses the order
of the eigenvalues.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minus}
 x_j(t)=t\lambda_{N+1-j}+a_{N+1-j}+O(|t|^{-1}),\qquad
 \dot x_j(t)=\lambda_{N+1-j}+O(t^{-2}).
\end{equation}
The spectral line $(\lambda_m,a_m)$ consequently has incoming ordered rank
$N+1-m$ and outgoing ordered rank $m$.  Its intercept is the same at both
ends; ``zero shift'' here refers only to these source-native spectral-line
coordinates, not to a target normalization.

Since $L_0$ is simple and $N\ge2$, $\lambda_N-\lambda_1>0$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:plus}--\eqref{eq:minus} make the relative diameter grow linearly in
both time directions.  Hence every orbit is unbounded in relative
configuration and there is no bounded nonconstant periodic orbit.
