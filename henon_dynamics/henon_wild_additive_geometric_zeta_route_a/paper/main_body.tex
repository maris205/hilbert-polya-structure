\begin{abstract}
For every prime characteristic, including two, we distinguish the geometric
fixed points of $x\mapsto x+x^p$ from the lengths of its fixed schemes.
At iterate $n$, writing $e=p^{v_p(n)}$, the scheme has length $p^n$ but only
$p^{n-e}$ geometric points, each of multiplicity $p^e$.
This identifies the exact wild correction and all primitive-cycle counts.

A linearized-polynomial Euclidean argument gives every finite-extension
count without assuming that either clock is prime to the characteristic.
We determine the least splitting degree and separate dynamical least period
from residue degree by a double M\"obius inversion.
\fi
\ifnum\CRevisionRound>1
A valuation expansion yields a Lambert series with an explicit interior
tail bound. At every nontrivial root of unity of $p$-power order its radial
logarithmic-derivative residue is strictly between zero and one, ruling
out local meromorphic continuation of the geometric zeta itself. Thus its
circle $|z|=1/p$ is a meromorphic natural boundary, although length counting
gives the rational function $(1-pz)^{-1}$. Independent exact computations
validate 1,344 iterate cells and 5,760 extension cells. The result is a
source-specific theorem synthesis and audit, not a new Riemann determinant
or a claim of priority over the classical work of Bridy.
\fi
\end{abstract}

\section{The source and the precise contribution}
Fix a prime $p$ and $f(x)=x+x^p$ on $\mathbb A^1(\overline\F_p)$.
The parameter $p$ indexes source fibres; it is not a period label assigned
to a rational prime. The clock is composition number $n\ge1$.
The point at infinity is not adjoined. For a polynomial $Q(T)=\sum q_jT^j$
over $\F_p$, set $L_Q(X)=\sum q_jX^{p^j}$. Direct expansion gives
$L_{AB}=L_A\circ L_B$ and $L_{A+B}=L_A+L_B$.

Transcendence for additive-polynomial dynamical zeta functions is classical:
Bridy's earlier theorem covers $x^{p^m}+ax$ in odd characteristic
\cite[Theorem 2]{br12}, and the later additive-polynomial theorem includes
all positive characteristics \cite[Theorem 1.3]{br16}. We claim no priority
for transcendence or natural boundaries. Our contribution within this
research sequence is a complete, source-locked account of geometric versus
scheme counting, with independent evidence and explicit restrictions on
determinant promotion. The all-parameter proof does not depend on the
finite computational grid.

\section{All iterates and their wild multiplicities}
\noindent\textbf{Round 0 advance.} The geometric census and the scheme-length
census differ at every iterate, and the difference has an exact formula.

\begin{theorem}[Geometric and scheme ledgers]
Write $n=em$, $e=p^{v_p(n)}$, with $p\nmid m$. The fixed scheme of $f^n$
has length $p^n$. Its geometric points number $N_n=p^{n-e}$ and each has
local multiplicity $p^e$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In the commutative additive-endomorphism ring, $f=1+F$ with $F(X)=X^p$.
Thus $f^n-\id=L_{Q_n}$, where
\[
 Q_n(T)=(1+T)^n-1=T^eH_m(T)^e,
 \qquad H_m(T)=\frac{(1+T)^m-1}{T}.
\]
Since $H_m(0)=m\ne0$, the linearized polynomial $L_{H_m^e}$ is separable
of degree $p^{e(m-1)}=p^{n-e}$. Moreover
\[
 f^n(X)-X=L_{H_m^e}(X)^{p^e}.
\]
Every root is therefore repeated exactly $p^e$ times. The product of
the number of roots and their common multiplicity is $p^n$, the degree
of the original monic polynomial.
\end{proof}

For $n=p^v$ one has $H_1=1$, so the only geometric fixed point is $0$,
and $f^{p^v}(X)-X=X^{p^{p^v}}$. In particular no nonzero point has pure
$p$-power period. The differential of $f$ is $1$ everywhere, and every
periodic multiplier is $1$. Wild scheme multiplicity is not a real
Lyapunov exponent or a nondegenerate flat-trace denominator.

On each finite fixed set, $f$ is a permutation because $f^n=\id$ there.
Consequently forward-oriented primitive-cycle counts are
\begin{equation}
 P_n=\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)p^{d-p^{v_p(d)}}\in\Z_{\ge0},
 \qquad N_n=\sum_{d\mid n}dP_d.\label{eq:primitive}
\end{equation}
No reversal quotient is imposed; every geometric orbit has weight one.
The scheme-length exponential instead satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_p^{\rm len}(z)=\exp\left(\sum_{n\ge1}p^n\frac{z^n}{n}\right)
 =\frac1{1-pz}.\label{eq:length}
\end{equation}
This is a scalar length-counting determinant, not a geometric orbit trace.
The discrepancy is already $p$ versus $1$ at the first iterate.

\section{Every extension and the two-clock dictionary}
\noindent\textbf{Round 1 advance.} The extension census is closed without
discarding repeated factors in characteristic-divisible extension degrees.

\begin{theorem}[All extension degrees]
For all $n,r\ge1$,
\[
 N_{n,r}:=\#\{x\in\F_{p^r}:f^n(x)=x\}
 =p^{\deg\gcd((1+T)^n-1,T^r-1)}.
\]
For $m>1$, let $o_m$ be the order of the unit $T$ in
$\F_p[T]/(H_m)$. The least extension containing all geometric fixed
points of $f^n$ has degree $eo_m$. For $m=1$ that degree is $1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Euclidean division $A=BQ+R$ implies
$L_A=L_Q\circ L_B+L_R$. Hence $L_A,L_B$ have the same common roots
as $L_B,L_R$. Iterating the Euclidean algorithm shows that their common
roots are the roots of the monic linearized gcd. Apply this with
$A=(1+T)^n-1$, $B=T^r-1$; then $L_B=X^{p^r}-X$.
The gcd $g$ has nonzero constant coefficient because $T\nmid B$,
so $L_g$ is separable and has $p^{\deg g}$ roots. This argument permits
$p\mid n$ and $p\mid r$; replacing $B$ by its radical would be an error.

The polynomial $H_m$ is squarefree, since $(1+T)^m-1$ has no common
root with its derivative $m(1+T)^{m-1}$. Its constant coefficient is
nonzero. Thus the finite algebra $\F_p[T]/(H_m)$ is a product of finite
fields, and $o_m$ is prime to $p$. The gcd attains degree $n-e$ exactly
when $H_m^e\mid T^r-1$. Factoring the latter polynomial into its
prime-to-$p$ part with multiplicity $p^{v_p(r)}$ shows this is equivalent
to $eo_m\mid r$. For $m=1$ the root set is $\{0\}$.
\end{proof}

Let $E_{n,r}$ count points of exact dynamical period $n$ and exact residue
degree $r$. A polynomial defined over $\F_p$ cannot increase residue
degree. On a periodic cycle the field inclusions return to the starting
point, so all inclusions are equalities. Double M\"obius inversion gives
\begin{equation}
 E_{n,r}=\sum_{a\mid n}\sum_{b\mid r}\mu(n/a)\mu(r/b)N_{a,b},
 \qquad E_{n,r}/n\in\Z_{\ge0}.\label{eq:double}
\end{equation}
The quotient counts $f$-cycles whose points have exact residue degree $r$.
It does not count joint orbits under $f$ and Frobenius: the latter could
identify several $f$-cycles. Thus no hidden identification of the two
clocks is used.

Since $N_n\le p^n$ and $N_n=p^{n-1}$ when $p\nmid n$, the logarithmic
series has radius exactly $1/p$. Within $|z|<1/p$,
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_p(z)=\exp\left(\sum_{n\ge1}N_n\frac{z^n}{n}\right)
 =\prod_{n\ge1}(1-z^n)^{-P_n}.\label{eq:euler}
\end{equation}
Indeed $nP_n\le N_n$ ensures absolute convergence; expanding each
logarithm and using \eqref{eq:primitive} justifies the exchange of sums.
We call $D_p=1/\zeta_p$ the native primitive determinant germ, without
assuming an operator realization.

\section{An explicit meromorphic natural boundary}
\noindent\textbf{Round 2 advance.} The natural-boundary claim is proved
for the zeta itself, not inferred informally from a singular derivative.
The argument also supplies a rigorous interior tail bound.

Set $w=pz$, $\mathcal Z_p(w)=\zeta_p(w/p)$, and
\[
 A_p(w)=w\frac{\mathcal Z_p'(w)}{\mathcal Z_p(w)}
       =\sum_{n\ge1}p^{-p^{v_p(n)}}w^n.
\]
Define $c_0=p^{-1}$ and
$c_k=p^{-p^k}-p^{-p^{k-1}}<0$ for $k\ge1$.
The telescoping coefficient identity yields
\begin{equation}
 A_p(w)=\sum_{k\ge0}c_k\frac{w^{p^k}}{1-w^{p^k}},\qquad |w|<1.
 \label{eq:lambert}
\end{equation}
For $|w|\le\rho<1$ and $K\ge0$, its tail is bounded by
\begin{equation}
 \left|\sum_{k>K}c_k\frac{w^{p^k}}{1-w^{p^k}}\right|
 \le p^{-p^K}\frac{\rho^{p^{K+1}}}{1-\rho^{p^{K+1}}}.
 \label{eq:tail}
\end{equation}
This follows from $\sum_{k>K}|c_k|=p^{-p^K}$ and monotonicity of
$\rho^h/(1-\rho^h)$. It proves normal convergence on compact subdisks.
With each logarithm normalized at zero, integration gives the local formula
\[
 \log\mathcal Z_p(w)=-\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{c_k}{p^k}\log(1-w^{p^k}).
\]
No branch continuation beyond the unit disk is built into this equality.

\begin{theorem}[Natural boundary and HEN-O368]
The circle $|z|=1/p$ is a meromorphic natural boundary for both
$\zeta_p$ and $D_p$. Neither has a same-variable meromorphic global
completion, even after multiplication by an entire zero-free function.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a root of unity $\omega$ of exact order $p^K$, $K\ge1$. In
\eqref{eq:lambert}, the finitely many terms with $k<K$ vanish after
multiplication by $1-r$ and substitution $w=r\omega$, $r\uparrow1$.
For $k\ge K$, $\omega^{p^k}=1$, and
\[
 0\le(1-r)\frac{r^{p^k}}{1-r^{p^k}}\le1,
 \qquad (1-r)\frac{r^{p^k}}{1-r^{p^k}}\longrightarrow p^{-k}.
\]
Summability of $|c_k|$ permits dominated convergence. Hence
\[
 (1-r)A_p(r\omega)\longrightarrow
 S_K:=\sum_{k\ge K}c_kp^{-k}<0,
 \qquad 0<-S_K\le p^{-K-p^{K-1}}<1.
\]
If $\mathcal Z_p$ had a meromorphic continuation near $\omega$, it
would be nonzero as a germ and its local order $j$ would be an integer.
The logarithmic derivative would then have residue $j$. But its radial
limit is
\[
 \lim_{r\uparrow1}(r\omega-\omega)
 \frac{\mathcal Z_p'(r\omega)}{\mathcal Z_p(r\omega)}=-S_K\in(0,1),
\]
which is impossible. This includes the boundary case $p=2,K=1$.
Nontrivial $p$-power roots are dense on the unit circle. Any continuation
through another point would define a meromorphic germ at one of these
roots and agree on the connected overlap inside the disk, a contradiction.
The reciprocal has the same obstruction because reciprocation preserves
nonzero meromorphic germs. Multiplication by an entire zero-free factor
cannot help: division by that factor would restore a forbidden continuation.
\end{proof}

The theorem does not exclude an operator determinant defined only inside
the original disk, distributional boundary values, a changed variable with
a different domain, or a genuinely different dynamical object.
It does exclude replacing the geometric ledger by \eqref{eq:length}
and claiming that the original primitive determinant thereby continues.
