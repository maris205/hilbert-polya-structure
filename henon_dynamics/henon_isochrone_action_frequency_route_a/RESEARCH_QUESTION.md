# Research question

For the planar Hénon isochrone Hamiltonian

\[
H(r,p_r,L)=\frac{p_r^2}{2}+\frac{L^2}{2r^2}
-\frac{\mu}{b+\sqrt{b^2+r^2}},\qquad \mu,b>0,
\]

can one produce a single rigorous certificate that simultaneously answers the following questions?

1. What is the exact allowed energy interval at fixed \(\ell=|L|\), including the circular minimum?
2. What are the radial action and period, and why is the period independent of angular momentum?
3. What is the apsidal frequency ratio under the \(L\geq0\) convention, and exactly when does a noncircular bound phase-space orbit close?
4. How do circular motion, zero-angular-momentum center crossings, marginal escape, and the \(b\downarrow0\) limit change the generic statement?
5. What Route-A information genuinely follows, without importing forbidden arithmetic or target spectral data?

The answer proved here is

\[
J_r=\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{-2E}}
-\frac{\ell+\sqrt{\ell^2+4\mu b}}2,qquad
T_r=\frac{2\pi\mu}{(-2E)^{3/2}},
\]

on \(E_c(\ell)\leq E<0\), where

\[
E_c(\ell)=-\frac{2\mu^2}
{\bigl(\ell+\sqrt{\ell^2+4\mu b}\bigr)^2}.
\]

For \(L\geq0\) and \(\ell>0\),

\[
\frac{\Omega_\phi}{\Omega_r}
=\frac12\left(1+\frac{\ell}{\sqrt{\ell^2+4\mu b}}\right).
\]

A noncircular bound orbit closes exactly when this number is rational.  Circular orbits are always closed and must not be put through that test; nonstationary \(L=0\) trajectories cross the smooth center and recover their full Cartesian phase point after \(2T_r\).  These distinctions are part of the theorem, not afterthoughts.
