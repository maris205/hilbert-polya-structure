# Devil's advocate

## Strongest objection

The Hénon kick may be only a clever coordinate change around a classical
Tate/Connes construction. The adapted-domain identity proves exactly that
the known Riemann range survives; it does not prove the nonlinear map
contributes anything.

## Existing controls

- Removing the cubic chirp leaves the inherited scaling zeta unchanged.
- Replacing the chirp by any integral polynomial retains the theta and
  spherical-vacuum identities.
- Random unitary phases retain the raw critical-line zero accumulation.
- The local chirp is not a compact perturbation of the identity, so a formal
  Fredholm determinant cannot hide the problem.

## Why the route remains open

The fixed-domain theorem gives only a static rank bound. Scaling generates
infinitely many independent boundary functionals before the Poisson map, so
the hoped-for two-channel inference is false. The route remains open only if
a genuine scaling cocycle plus Poisson renormalization or a crossed-product
trace compresses this orbit in a canonical way. The exact Poisson formula
does put every fixed-scale defect into the same outgoing mode, but it does
not prove that this asymptotic mode belongs to the scaling Hilbert space,
control the orbit of its coefficient functionals, or prove determinant
class.

## Falsifiers

Any one of these closes the positive route:

1. no scaling-site Hénon bundle/cocycle exists with the required holonomy;
2. the anomaly is a removable coboundary;
3. the anomaly determinant has an extra zero or pole;
4. no canonical Poisson or crossed-product quotient is determinant class;
5. the reciprocal functional equation requires an external, fitted
   normalization.

## Anti-overclaim firewall

The current Riemann divisor is inherited. The current Route-A status is
exploratory. Static rank two is not dynamic two-channel scattering. Route B
is not authorized.
