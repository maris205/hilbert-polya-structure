\begin{abstract}
The weighted reflection Euler family of the full H\'enon horseshoe has an
explicit punctured scalar continuation.  We ask whether a Fredholm
determinant representation gives that continuation meaningful operator
ownership.  Its logarithmic channels can indeed be placed on the diagonal
of a locally trace-class holomorphic family $A(z,q)$, and
$\Fdet(\exp A)=\exp(\operatorname{Tr}A)$ recovers the continuation exactly.
This realization is nevertheless tautological: every nonvanishing
holomorphic scalar $F$ satisfies
$F=\Fdet(I+(F-1)P)$ for a fixed rank-one projection $P$.  In the opposite
direction, each primitive source word owns a finite weighted cyclic block
$B_\omega$ with
$\det(I-zB_\omega)=1-z^nq^{S_n\chi(\omega)}$.  This is the P70 Euler
denominator, whose reciprocal is the corresponding Euler factor.  Its singular values are the
physical edge weights in $\{1,q\}$.  Primitive singleton reflection words at
every odd length therefore put a uniform positive singular-value floor in
the undamped full orbit-block direct sum.  That sum is bounded but
noncompact, belongs to no finite Schatten ideal, and has no ordinary
trace-class Fredholm determinant.  The comparison furnishes a claim
firewall: analytic determinant realization is proved but tautological,
finite source blocks are exact, and a genuine source-derived transfer owner
remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{The operator question after scalar continuation}

The H\'enon horseshoe admits a full-shift model in the hyperbolic regime
relevant here \cite{DevaneyNitecki1979,Arai2007}.  Previous steps of the
present programme associated to its marked reflection packets the weighted
channel expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:channel-expansion}
 \mathcal L(z,q)=\sum_{m\geq1}h_m(z,q),\qquad
 h_m(z,q)=c_m\frac{2(qz)^m}
 {1-(1+q^{2m})z^{2m}},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient}
 c_m=\frac1m\prod_{\substack{p\mid m\\p\ \mathrm{odd}}}(1-p),
 \qquad 0<|c_m|\leq1.
\end{equation}
For fixed $q>0$, put
\begin{align}
 L(q)&=\min(1,q^{-1}),\\
 \rho_m(q)&=(1+q^{2m})^{-1/(2m)},\\
 \Sigma_q&=\{\rho_m(q)e^{\pi i k/m}:m\geq1,\ 0\leq k<2m\},\\
 \Omega_q&=\{z:|z|<L(q)\}\setminus\Sigma_q.
\end{align}
The preceding scalar theory proves that \eqref{eq:channel-expansion}
converges normally on compact subsets of $\Omega_q$ and defines the
nonvanishing continuation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zch}
 \Zch(z,q)=\exp \mathcal L(z,q).
\end{equation}
It also classifies the limiting circle; we do not revisit or cross that
boundary.  Instead we distinguish two questions:

\begin{enumerate}
 \item Can the known scalar \eqref{eq:zch} be represented by a legitimate
 trace-class Fredholm determinant?
 \item Does an operator built independently from the source dynamics own
 the same determinant?
\end{enumerate}

The first answer is yes.  The second is not implied by the first.

\section{A locally trace-class channel realization}

Let $(e_m)_{m\geq1}$ be the standard basis of $\ell^2(\mathbb N)$ and let
$P_m$ project onto $\mathbb C e_m$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
 A(z,q)=\sum_{m\geq1}h_m(z,q)P_m
       =\operatorname{diag}(h_1(z,q),h_2(z,q),\ldots).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Channel-diagonal determinant]\label{thm:channel}
For every fixed $q>0$, $A:\Omega_q\to\Sch$ is holomorphic in trace norm.
The family
\[
 K_{\mathrm{ch}}(z,q)=\exp A(z,q)-I
\]
is likewise trace-class holomorphic, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:channel-det}
 \Fdet(I+K_{\mathrm{ch}}(z,q))
 =\exp(\operatorname{Tr}A(z,q))
 =\Zch(z,q).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $C\Subset\Omega_q$.  Choose $r<L(q)$ such that $|z|\leq r$ on $C$.
Then $r<1$ and $qr<1$.  All finitely many early denominators in
\eqref{eq:channel-expansion} are uniformly separated from zero on $C$.
For all sufficiently large $m$ and all $z\in C$,
\[
 \left|1-(1+q^{2m})z^{2m}\right|
 \geq1-r^{2m}-(qr)^{2m}\geq\frac12.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:coefficient},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail}
 \sup_{z\in C}|h_m(z,q)|\leq4(qr)^m
\end{equation}
for the same tail.  Thus $\sum_m h_mP_m$ converges locally uniformly in
$\Sch$, since the trace norm of a diagonal operator is the sum of the
absolute diagonal entries.  The Banach-valued Weierstrass theorem gives
trace-norm holomorphy, and
$\operatorname{Tr}A=\sum_mh_m=\mathcal L$.

The exponential remains in $I+\Sch$.  More explicitly, for the diagonal
tail,
\[
 |e^{h_m}-1|\leq e^{|h_m|}|h_m|,
\]
so \eqref{eq:tail} again gives locally uniform trace-norm convergence.
The standard trace-ideal identity
$\Fdet(e^A)=e^{\operatorname{Tr}A}$ \cite{Simon2005} now yields
\eqref{eq:channel-det}.
\end{proof}

This theorem is an exact analytic statement.  It is not yet a test of
dynamical provenance, because the entries of $A$ were read from the already
completed scalar logarithm.

\section{The universal rank-one firewall}

\begin{lemma}[Universal scalar realization]\label{lem:rankone}
Let $U\subset\mathbb C$ be a domain, let $F$ be a nonvanishing holomorphic
function on $U$, and let $P$ be a fixed rank-one orthogonal projection on an
arbitrary Hilbert space.  Then
\[
 K_F(z)=(F(z)-1)P
\]
is trace-class holomorphic and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rankone}
 \Fdet(I+K_F(z))=F(z),\qquad z\in U.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Scalar multiplication makes $K_F$ holomorphic in trace norm.  It has one
possibly nonzero eigenvalue, $F(z)-1$.  The finite-rank definition of the
Fredholm determinant therefore gives
$\Fdet(I+K_F)=1+(F-1)=F$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{lem:rankone} is deliberately elementary.  It shows that the bare
existence of a parameter-dependent trace-class family with determinant $F$
is universal and therefore cannot, by itself, identify a transfer
mechanism, periodic-point trace, or dynamically selected state space.  We
call such a construction a \emph{scalar-built realization}.  A genuine
source owner would have to be specified from the dynamics independently of
the already known $F$ and then derive $F$ as a consequence.  In this sense
\Cref{thm:channel} is \texttt{PROVED\_TAUTOLOGICAL}: correct and useful for
analysis, but insufficient as an ownership theorem.

\section{Finite source-native cyclic blocks}

The literal source construction has more provenance.  Let $\omega$ be a
primitive marked reflection word of odd length $n$.  Write
\[
 \chi_j=\chi(\sigma^j\omega)\in\{0,1\},\qquad
 S(\omega)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\chi_j.
\]
On $\mathbb C^n$, with indices read modulo $n$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block}
 B_\omega e_j=q^{\chi_j}e_{j+1}.
\end{equation}
These are the physical edge weights; no period damping has been inserted.

\begin{proposition}[Exact orbit block]\label{prop:block}
For every $q>0$ and every such $\omega$,
\begin{align}
 B_\omega^n&=q^{S(\omega)}I,\label{eq:turn}\\
 \det(I-zB_\omega)&=1-z^nq^{S(\omega)},\label{eq:block-det}
\end{align}
and the singular values of $B_\omega$ are precisely the multiset
$\{q^{\chi_0},\ldots,q^{\chi_{n-1}}\}\subset\{1,q\}$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-bounds}
 \min(1,q)\|x\|\leq\|B_\omega x\|
 \leq\max(1,q)\|x\|.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After one full cycle, every $e_j$ has acquired every edge weight once, which
proves \eqref{eq:turn}.  Moreover
$e_0,B_\omega e_0,\ldots,B_\omega^{n-1}e_0$ are nonzero multiples of the
standard basis, so $e_0$ is cyclic.  Hence the minimal polynomial has degree
$n$; by \eqref{eq:turn} it is $\lambda^n-q^{S(\omega)}$, which is also the
characteristic polynomial.  Substitution gives \eqref{eq:block-det}.
Finally,
\[
 B_\omega^*B_\omega e_j=q^{2\chi_j}e_j.
\]
Taking positive square roots proves the singular-value statement and
\eqref{eq:block-bounds}.
\end{proof}

Thus every finite Euler denominator has an exact source-native owner, and
the corresponding P70 Euler factor is the reciprocal determinant
\(\det(I-zB_\omega)^{-1}\).  The issue is whether all of these blocks are
summable in a determinant class.

\section{The full source sum is not compact}

For every odd $n\geq3$, let $\omega_n=(1,0,\ldots,0)$ with the singleton at
the chosen reflection center.  Reflection fixes $\omega_n$.  It is
primitive: repetition of a shorter word would create more than one symbol
equal to $1$.  Exactly the two sites adjacent to the singleton have unequal
distance-two neighbours, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:singleton-energy}
 S(\omega_n)=n-2.
\end{equation}

Let $\mathfrak R$ be the countable collection of physical primitive marked
reflection words and form the Hilbert direct sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direct-sum}
 \mathcal H=\bigoplus_{\omega\in\mathfrak R}\mathbb C^{|\omega|},
 \qquad B_q=\bigoplus_{\omega\in\mathfrak R}B_\omega.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Source-native obstruction]\label{thm:obstruction}
For every $q>0$, $B_q$ is bounded and noncompact.  It belongs to no Schatten
class $\mathcal S_p$ with $0<p<\infty$.  In particular, for $z\ne0$ the
expression $\Fdet(I-zB_q)$ is not an ordinary trace-class Fredholm
determinant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The uniform bounds \eqref{eq:block-bounds} pass to the Hilbert direct sum:
\[
 \min(1,q)\|x\|\leq\|B_qx\|\leq\max(1,q)\|x\|.
\]
Choose one unit basis vector $u_n$ from each singleton block.  The vectors
$u_n$ are orthonormal, their images lie in mutually orthogonal blocks, and
$\|B_qu_n\|\geq\min(1,q)>0$.  Hence $(B_qu_n)$ has no convergent subsequence,
so $B_q$ is not compact.  Every finite Schatten class is contained in the
compact operators.  Thus $B_q\notin\mathcal S_p$ for every finite $p$; in
particular $zB_q\notin\Sch$ when $z\ne0$, which excludes the ordinary
trace-class determinant.
\end{proof}

The theorem concerns the literal undamped source blocks.  It does not refute
every conceivable transfer operator.  It says that compactness or
nuclearity must come from additional source-derived structure, rather than
from taking the naive full direct sum.

\section{A graded ledger is not an operator trace}

There is a useful but dangerous remnant of trace combinatorics.  From the
cyclic shift action,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ledger}
 \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\langle e_j,B_\omega^re_j\rangle=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&n\nmid r,\\
 nq^{(r/n)S(\omega)},&n\mid r.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For fixed $r$, only the finitely many lengths $n\mid r$ contribute, and
there are finitely many binary source words at each such length.  Therefore
\eqref{eq:ledger} reproduces a locally finite coefficient ledger after
summing over blocks.

This does not contradict \Cref{thm:obstruction}.  The left side of
\eqref{eq:ledger} is a finite-block diagonal sum; its aggregate is tied to
the canonical block basis.  Since $B_q^r$ is still bounded below and
noncompact, it is not trace class.  The aggregate cannot be promoted to a
basis-independent Hilbert-space trace or used in the Fredholm trace
expansion.  We retain it only as a formal graded identity.

\section{Executable certificate and claim boundary}

The accompanying standard-library certificate performs exact rational
Gaussian elimination on finite cyclic blocks, verifies representative
weighted full turns, compares channel trace exponentials with products of
diagonal exponentials, and tests unrelated rank-one realizations.  A second
program reconstructs the formulas without importing the primary module.
Normal and optimized unit-test runs enforce the same schema, and mutation
tests reject promotions of the operator and arithmetic claims.  These
computations audit finite identities; the local trace-norm and noncompactness
arguments remain the proofs.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Claim & Status\\
\midrule
Punctured analytic determinant & \texttt{PROVED\_TAUTOLOGICAL}\\
Universal rank-one firewall & \texttt{PROVED}\\
Finite source cyclic blocks & \texttt{PROVED}\\
Full source direct sum trace class & \texttt{REFUTED}\\
Genuine source-derived transfer owner & \texttt{OPEN}\\
Arithmetic trace or Route B & \texttt{NO / NOT AUTHORIZED}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

No rational-prime semantics, von Mangoldt trace, self-adjoint
Hilbert--P\'olya operator, or arithmetic advance follows.  The constructive
next question is precise: find a dynamically forced state space and a
source-derived compactness mechanism whose genuine operator traces produce
the weighted channels without inserting the completed scalar function.
