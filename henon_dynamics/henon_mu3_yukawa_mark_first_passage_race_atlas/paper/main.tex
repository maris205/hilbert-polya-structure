\begin{abstract}
The frozen C88 receipt gives exact first-passage laws for twenty actual
subgroup targets generated by sixteen named labels.  We determine the joint
race law for every unordered pair that is incomparable in the subgroup
poset: 108 pairs, each split into left-first, tie, and right-first outcomes.
The certificate resolves every outcome by first-passage rank and by the
boundary edges that create it.  All counts are exact integers under the scope
firewall \texttt{NO\_BAD\_EULER\_OR\_ROOT\_NUMBER}; no simulation or arithmetic
local-data assertion is used.
\end{abstract}

\section{Frozen finite model}
Let $L$ be the sixteen named C75 labels and let $A_k$ denote the support of
the first $k$ labels in a uniformly random permutation.  For each frozen C88
subgroup target $H_i$ define
\[
 T_i=\min\{k:H_i\leq\Phi(A_k)\}.
\]
The C88 receipt supplies the hit indicator of every one of the $2^{16}$
supports.  We restrict to pairs $(i,j)$ for which neither subgroup is
contained in the other; the frozen inclusion matrix has exactly 108 such
unordered pairs.

\section{Race theorem}
\begin{theorem}
For every unordered incomparable pair $(i,j)$, the C91 receipt contains exact
counts $R_{i,j}^{<}$, $R_{i,j}^{=}$, and $R_{i,j}^{>}$ for
$T_i<T_j$, $T_i=T_j$, and $T_j<T_i$, respectively.  They satisfy
\[
 R_{i,j}^{<}+R_{i,j}^{=}+R_{i,j}^{>}=16!
 =20{,}922{,}789{,}888{,}000.
\]
The receipt also contains the same three counts for each rank $k=0,\ldots,16$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a support $S$ of size $k$ and a label $x\in S$, consider the boundary
edge $S\setminus\{x\}\to S$.  If a target is not hit below the edge and is
hit on $S$, the edge is pivotal.  Every rank-$k$ edge represents exactly
$(k-1)!(16-k)!$ permutations: the first $k-1$ positions are an ordering of
$S\setminus\{x\}$ and the remaining positions are an ordering of its
complement.  For pair $(i,j)$, a left-only pivotal edge contributes to
$R_{i,j}^{<}$ precisely when $i$ is first hit on the edge and $j$ is not hit
on $S$; the symmetric condition gives $R_{i,j}^{>}$, while simultaneous
pivotality gives $R_{i,j}^{=}$.  These three disjoint classes partition every
permutation by its first rank at which either target is hit, proving the
identity and the rank decomposition.
\end{proof}

rank cells, and the corresponding boundary-edge counts.  Ninety-nine rows
have a nonzero tie class.  The independent checker rebuilds all bitset

SymPy checks all 324 outcome probability-generating functions and the exact

\[
