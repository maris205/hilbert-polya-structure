## Exact proposed increment

Let $P(z)=\sum_{j=0}^J w_jz^j$, $w_j\ge0$, $\sum_jw_j=1$. Average the
unnormalized sharp cutoffs $N+j$, and normalize by the corresponding
averaged positive partition sum. The observable is still the expectation
of $(-1)^{n+1}n^{-it}$ in a genuine positive state on the same $N+J$ levels.

The candidate classification is governed by the **exact order of the zero
of $P$ at $-1$**, not by a changed zero of the zeta function. If that order
is $k$, the natural-log thermodynamic rate is $1-\beta$ away from eta
zeros and $1+k$ at a zero in $0<\beta<1$. Every nonnegative integer $k$
is realized by the binomial filter. All these states are $O(1/N)$ in
trace norm from the unfiltered Gibbs state on the same Hilbert space.
The local complex-zero displacement depends on $\beta+k$ and on the
multiplicity of the encoded zero. See the full proposed proof package.

This would close a stability/classification question for an actual published
arithmetic encoding, not an arithmetic origin of its input spectrum. It
does **not** refute the sharp-cutoff theorem of Wei et al.; it distinguishes
that theorem from regulator-independent critical exponents. No claim is
made about arbitrary noisy quantum dynamics, gate complexity, the second
Riemann–Siegel protocol, or optimal zeta algorithms.

## Admission risks

The active proof has now been written in
[FINITE_FILTER_PROOF.md](FINITE_FILTER_PROOF.md), with full quantifiers,
the positive-state normalization, trace-norm estimate and local zero
proof. It has not yet received nonauthor mathematical review. Subsequent
primary method checking read [NIST DLMF §3.9(ii)](https://dlmf.nist.gov/3.9#ii),
including the Euler transform and finite-difference identities (3.9.2)–(3.9.4).
This confirms that binomial cancellation is a classical mechanism, not
a new summation algorithm. The same-system $O(1/N)$ state-proximity
interpretation is the proposed residual increment, but its standalone
substance is weak enough that this is now a reserve, not the preferred
fifth-contract route. This is not a claim that DLMF states the entire
thermal theorem or its exact local-zero formula.

1. Finite-filter cancellation is elementary enough that its full theorem
   may be owned by summability/finite-difference literature. Check this,
   separately from its current physical interpretation.
2. Even if correct and not literally published, the increment might still
   be a short methodological note rather than the required independent
   substantial paper. No slot is filled before nonauthor adjudication.
3. Trace-norm closeness here is a proved statement for these positive
   finite-rank states; smooth cutoffs on an entire macroscopic energy
   window do not automatically have that closeness.
4. Compact-$s$ asymptotics do not imply uniformity for high zeta zeros or
   the joint time/cutoff limit in the second experimental protocol.
