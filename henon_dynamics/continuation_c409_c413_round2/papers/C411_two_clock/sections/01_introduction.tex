\section{Introduction}

Two commuting circle endomorphisms have common periodic points at two
independent times. For multiplication by \(a\) and by \(b\), the
common-return count at \((n,m)\) is the elementary arithmetic quantity
\(\gcd(a^n-1,b^m-1)\). We retain both times in the ordinary series
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-series}
R_{a,b}(x,y)=
\sum_{n,m\geq1}\gcd(a^n-1,b^m-1)x^ny^m,\qquad a,b\geq2.
\end{equation}
Our question is the full complex-analytic geometry of this series:
where it converges absolutely, which poles appear after continuation,
and which points obstruct joint meromorphic continuation.

Theorem~\ref{thm:classification} answers these questions for every pair
of integer bases. Multiplicative dependence changes the initial
convergence domain and creates an explicit locally finite polar
divisor inside the bidisc. It does not change the terminal joint
boundary: the entire boundary of the bidisc obstructs meromorphic
continuation in both branches. In particular, the curved part of
the dependent convergence boundary must not be confused with the
final joint natural boundary.

Neither the dynamics nor the use of rectangular return times is new.
Ward's thesis already defines a rectangular zeta function, aggregating
rectangles by volume into one variable
\citep[Chapter IV, Definition IV.1.2]{ward1989}. Ward's later paper
explicitly treats the two independent times of the multiplication
by \(2\) and \(3\) circle action and its solenoid extension
\citep[Example 3.1]{ward1992}. These are direct precedents for the
native count and the two clocks in \eqref{eq:intro-series}.
Merely keeping a second variable would not constitute the analytic
classification proved here.

There are also close one-variable invariants for commuting systems.
Miles studies weak and strong synchronization through equalities
between corresponding iterates at a common time, together with
generating functions and arithmetic growth bounds \citep{miles2013}.
His natural-boundary theorem for commuting group automorphisms concerns
the dynamical zeta function summing over all finite-index subgroups
by their index \citep{miles2015}. Rectangular subgroups form a special
subfamily of those subgroups, but the ordinary series
\eqref{eq:intro-series} is not an index-weighted specialization of
that one-variable analytic problem.

The diagonal sequence has its own current classification.
Nguyen-Dang proves that
\(\gcd(a^n-1,b^n-1)\) satisfies a constant-coefficient linear recurrence
exactly when the bases are multiplicatively dependent
\citep[Theorem 1.1]{nguyendang2026}. We do not claim the corresponding
diagonal rationality or natural-boundary consequences as new.
The simplest dependent pair already illustrates the distinction:
for \(a=b=2\), the diagonal generating function is
\[
\sum_{n\geq1}(2^n-1)z^n
=\frac{2z}{1-2z}-\frac{z}{1-z},
\]
whereas \eqref{eq:intro-series} has genuine interior hypersurfaces
\(2x^iy^j=1\), for every primitive positive pair \((i,j)\), and
no joint continuation across any bidisc boundary point.
A rational diagonal therefore does not recover the two-variable
polar or continuation geometry.

Our only deep arithmetic input is the two-exponent \(S\)-unit gcd
estimate of Corvaja--Zannier \citep{cz2005}. The independent
convergence-domain argument is a direct deduction from that theorem,
not a new gcd bound. The analytic ingredients---totient expansions,
positive atomic Cauchy transforms, primitive-ray summation and
subharmonic coefficient envelopes---are classical as well.
We prove the precise propagation statement needed here using the
standard principles described by Korevaar--Wiegerinck \citep{kw2017}.
The work is their complete application to this return family,
including actual noncancelling poles and the parameter regimes that
cannot be handled by a holomorphic-product argument.
