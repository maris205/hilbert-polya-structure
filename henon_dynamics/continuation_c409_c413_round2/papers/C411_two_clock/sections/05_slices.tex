\section{Dense atomic boundaries on positive real slices}
\label{sec:slices}

The following argument works for every pair of bases, regardless of
multiplicative dependence.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:slice}
For every real \(t\) with \(0<t<1/b\), the function
\(x\mapsto R_{a,b}(x,t)\) is holomorphic in \(\D\) and has the
whole unit circle as a natural boundary, even for meromorphic
continuation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The elementary estimate \(C_{a,b}(n,m)\leq b^m-1\) shows that
\[
R_{a,b}(x,t)=\sum_{n\geq1}A_n(t)x^n,\qquad
A_n(t)=\sum_{m\geq1}C_{a,b}(n,m)t^m
\]
has coefficients bounded uniformly in \(n\).
For \(d\) coprime to \(a\), put \(k_a(d)=\ord_d(a)\), with
\(k_a(1)=1\). Let \(\nu_k\) be uniform probability on the \(k\)-th
roots of unity. The totient identity
\(N=\sum_{d\mid N}\varphi(d)\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:totient}
C_{a,b}(n,m)=
\sum_{\substack{d\mid b^m-1\\(d,a)=1}}
\varphi(d)\one_{k_a(d)\mid n}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the divisors common to \(a^n-1\) and \(b^m-1\) are precisely
those in this sum for which \(k_a(d)\mid n\).

Define a positive atomic measure on \(\T\) by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positive-measure}
\mu_t=\sum_{m\geq1}t^m
\sum_{\substack{d\mid b^m-1\\(d,a)=1}}
\varphi(d)\nu_{k_a(d)}.
\end{equation}
Its mass is finite:
\[
\mu_t(\T)\leq\sum_{m\geq1}(b^m-1)t^m<\infty.
\]
Character orthogonality on a finite cyclic group gives
\(\int\xi^n\,d\nu_k(\xi)=\one_{k\mid n}\).
Consequently \eqref{eq:totient} implies
\[
A_n(t)=\int_{\T}\xi^n\,d\mu_t(\xi).
\]

Choose a prime \(\ell\nmid ab\). For every \(j\geq1\),
\(\ell^j\mid b^{m_j}-1\) when \(m_j=\ord_{\ell^j}(b)\).
Thus \(\mu_t\) contains a positive multiple of
\(\nu_{k_j}\), where \(k_j=\ord_{\ell^j}(a)\).
The integers \(k_j\) are unbounded. Otherwise, if they were bounded
by \(K\), their orders would all divide
\(N=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,K)\); hence
\(\ell^j\mid a^N-1\) for every \(j\), impossible for \(a\geq2\).
The union of the complete \(k_j\)-th root grids is therefore dense
in \(\T\). Positivity in \eqref{eq:positive-measure} makes every
displayed atom a nonzero mass of the actual measure.

For \(|x|<1\), finite total mass permits the geometric series to be
integrated:
\[
R_{a,b}(x,t)
=\int_{\T}\frac{\xi x}{1-\xi x}\,d\mu_t(\xi).
\]
For \(\xi,\xi_0\in\T\) and \(0<\varrho<1\),
\[
\left|(1-\varrho)
\frac{\varrho\xi\xi_0^{-1}}{1-\varrho\xi\xi_0^{-1}}\right|\leq1.
\]
The integrand tends to the indicator of \(\xi=\xi_0\).
Dominated convergence gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slice-radial}
\lim_{\varrho\uparrow1}(1-\varrho)
R_{a,b}(\varrho\xi_0^{-1},t)=\mu_t(\{\xi_0\}).
\end{equation}
At every nonzero atom this limit precludes a holomorphic extension
at \(\xi_0^{-1}\). Those points are dense in the unit circle.
A meromorphic continuation across an arc would require a pole at
each of them, contradicting discreteness of poles. This proves the
proposition.
\end{proof}

No quantitative gcd estimate is used in this slice argument.
The positive real parameter is what ensures noncancellation in the
measure; no analogous positivity assertion is made for arbitrary
complex parameter values.
