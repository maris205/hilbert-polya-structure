\section{Common returns and the joint classification}\label{sec:statement}

Let \(T_a,T_b:\R/\Z\to\R/\Z\) be multiplication by \(a,b\geq2\).
They commute and define an \(\N^2\)-action. For \(n,m\geq1\), put
\[
C_{a,b}(n,m)=\#\bigl(\Fix(T_a^n)\cap\Fix(T_b^m)\bigr).
\]
The common kernel of multiplication by positive integers \(A,B\)
on \(\R/\Z\) is the cyclic subgroup of order \(\gcd(A,B)\).
Indeed, if \(d=\gcd(A,B)\), B\'ezout's identity shows that being killed
by \(A\) and \(B\) is equivalent to being killed by \(d\).
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
C_{a,b}(n,m)=\gcd(a^n-1,b^m-1).
\end{equation}
The same counts can be realized by the automorphisms dual to
multiplication by \(a,b\) on \(\Z[(ab)^{-1}]\): the gcd in
\eqref{eq:count} is coprime to \(ab\), so localization does not change
the common finite quotient. We shall only use the circle realization.

Write \(\D=\{|z|<1\}\), \(\T=\partial\D\), and
\[
\prim=\{(i,j)\in\Z_{\geq1}^2:\gcd(i,j)=1\}.
\]
The \emph{open absolute-convergence domain} of a double series is
the interior of the set on which the sum of absolute values is finite.
This convention matters on the coordinate axes: every summand in
\eqref{eq:intro-series} is zero if \(x=0\) or \(y=0\), even at points
outside the open domain.

A \emph{joint meromorphic cap} at a boundary point of a domain is a
meromorphic function on a neighborhood of that point which agrees
with the given function on the interior overlap. All continuation
statements below have this local meaning.

\begin{theorem}[Two-clock analytic classification]\label{thm:classification}
Let \(a,b\geq2\) be integers.

\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(a,b\) are multiplicatively independent, the open
absolute-convergence domain of \(R_{a,b}\) is exactly \(\D^2\).

\item If they are multiplicatively dependent, write uniquely
\[
a=c^r,\qquad b=c^s,\qquad
c\in\Z_{\geq2},\quad r,s\geq1,\quad \gcd(r,s)=1.
\]
Then the open absolute-convergence domain is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omega}
\Omega_{r,s,c}
=\{(x,y)\in\D^2:c^{rs}|x|^s|y|^r<1\}.
\end{equation}
On this domain the series agrees with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:meromorphic-expansion}
R_{a,b}(x,y)
=\sum_{(i,j)\in\prim}
\frac{c^{h_{i,j}}x^iy^j}{1-c^{h_{i,j}}x^iy^j}
-\frac{xy}{(1-x)(1-y)},\qquad
h_{i,j}=\gcd(ri,sj).
\end{equation}
This expansion extends it meromorphically to \(\D^2\): locally, it
is a finite sum of meromorphic terms plus a normally convergent
holomorphic tail. Its polar hypersurfaces there are exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar-set}
P_{i,j}=\{(x,y)\in\D^2:c^{h_{i,j}}x^iy^j=1\},
\qquad (i,j)\in\prim.
\end{equation}
Every component is genuine and has pole order one.
For fixed \(y_0\in\D\), the residue at any slice pole \(x_0\in\D\) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residue}
\Res_{x=x_0}R_{a,b}(x,y_0)
=-x_0
\sum_{\substack{(i,j)\in\prim\\c^{h_{i,j}}x_0^iy_0^j=1}}\frac1i
\ne0.
\end{equation}
The sum is finite.

\item In both branches the resulting meromorphic function on \(\D^2\)
has no joint meromorphic cap at any point of \(\partial(\D^2)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The third assertion is joint, not slice-wise. For instance,
\(R_{a,b}(x,0)=0\) is an entire slice. Nevertheless a neighborhood of
\((x_0,0)\), \(|x_0|=1\), includes other parameter values, and the
theorem excludes a joint cap there.
In the dependent case, \(\Omega_{r,s,c}\) is the initial power-series
domain, whereas \(\D^2\) is the larger meromorphic domain with the
specified interior poles. The theorem does not label every point of
the curved part of \(\partial\Omega_{r,s,c}\) a singularity.
