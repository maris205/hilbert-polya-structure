\begin{abstract}
For integers \(a,b\geq2\), we study the ordinary two-variable series whose
coefficient at \(x^ny^m\) is the number of common fixed points of the
\(n\)-th iterate of circle multiplication by \(a\) and the \(m\)-th iterate
of circle multiplication by \(b\). These coefficients are
\(\gcd(a^n-1,b^m-1)\). We classify the open domain of absolute convergence
and the joint meromorphic continuation for every pair of bases.
For multiplicatively independent bases the convergence domain is the
unit bidisc. For \(a=c^r,b=c^s\) with coprime positive \(r,s\), it is the
portion of that bidisc satisfying \(c^{rs}|x|^s|y|^r<1\).
In the dependent case a primitive-ray expansion continues the series
meromorphically to the whole bidisc and identifies every polar
hypersurface, with noncancelling simple slice residues. In both cases no
point of the bidisc boundary admits joint meromorphic continuation.
The boundary proof combines a dense positive atomic measure on real
parameter slices with a classical subharmonic propagation argument and,
where necessary, interior pole grids. The conclusion is genuinely joint:
it does not require every individual complex slice to have a natural
boundary.
\end{abstract}
