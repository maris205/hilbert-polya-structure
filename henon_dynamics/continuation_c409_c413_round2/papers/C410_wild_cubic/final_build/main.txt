                                                   Abstract
           Let k be any field of characteristic three and let a ∈ k ∗ . We determine the splitting fields
      of (X 3 + aX 2 )◦n − t over k(t) for every inverse-image height n ≥ 1, with t transcendental.
      Their arithmetic and geometric Galois groups coincide with the classical signature-constrained
                                                  n−1   n
      ternary groups En , and have order 23 3(3 −1)/2 . The proof separates two different kinds of
      independence. At each step the only relations among the relevant square classes are the sibling
      relations, and all 3n resulting Artin–Schreier classes are globally independent. In contrast, after
      extending the constants to an algebraic closure, these classes span only one dimension at every
      completion above infinity. A simultaneous induction on the tree group and this local rank gives
      the complete geometric branch data: the ramification indices at zero and infinity are 2n and
      2 · 3n , and the corresponding different exponents are 2n − 1 and 3n+1 − 2. The geometric genus is
      1 + |E2n | ( 12 − 2−n − 3−n ), giving genus 46 at height two. The result is a wild characteristic-three
      realization with ramification data, not a new construction of the abstract groups En . It concerns
      generic inverse images, not forward periodic points or finite-field extension degrees.


1    Introduction
An iterated splitting field can have many independent global generators while acquiring very little
ramification at a particular place. For the cubic

                                f (X) = X 3 + aX 2          (char k = 3, a ̸= 0),

this distinction determines the entire generic inverse-image tower. The finite critical point is fixed,
and infinity is wildly ramified. At height n, a quadratic radical step produces 3n Artin–Schreier
equations. Their classes are independent over the global function field, but become proportional
over each geometric completion at infinity. Tracking these two ranks simultaneously yields both the
Galois group and the ramification at every height.
    The groups that occur here are already known. Benedetto, Faber, Hutz, Juul and Yasufuku
introduced the signature-constrained ternary groups En , computed their orders, and realized them for
the cubic −2X 3 + 3X 2 in a number-field setting; their geometric realization is given in Corollary 1.3
of [2]. Theorem 2.3.1 of Bouw–Ejder–Karemaker [3] places these groups in the wider theory of
normalized dynamical Belyi maps. Ejder [4] uses the same local product-sign description in studying
arithmetic monodromy. Thus neither En nor the formula for its order is a new group-theoretic
object in this paper.
    Those characteristic-zero Belyi realizations do not supply the wild local calculation required
here. Our map has the two geometric branch values 0 and ∞, with ramification divisible by the
characteristic at infinity. The elementary square-root and Artin–Schreier normal form retains all
constants in a, so the result does not require a square root of a or an initial extension of the base field.

    Recent general monodromy results have different characteristic hypotheses. The version of
Adams–Hyde [1] cited here treats unicritical power polynomials of degree prime to the characteristic.
The cited version of Hlushchanka–Lukina–Wardell [5] treats a number-field cubic setting and
discusses extension to characteristics prime to two and three. Neither stated scope includes the
present characteristic-three family. These comparisons concern the specified sources and versions;
they are not an assertion of exhaustive literature priority.
    The main results answer one question: what are the generic splitting fields of f ◦n (X)−t, together
with their geometric branch data, for all n? Three parts of the answer are useful to distinguish. First,
a single compatible labelling of the inverse-image tree gives arithmetic and geometric group En over
an arbitrary base field. Second, over algebraically closed constants, the square-class relations and
the two Artin–Schreier ranks are determined exactly. Third, the resulting ramification indices give
explicit different exponents and genera. The substantive calculation is the simultaneous global/local
induction, not a list of orders or low-height examples.
    Section 2 states the results and their precise base-field conventions. Section 3 records the cubic
normal form and the classical local facts needed in the proof. Section 4 establishes the tree-group
upper bound before any maximality assertion is used. Sections 5 and 6 prove the mixed-radical
induction, including descent to the original constants. Section 7 computes the different and genus.

8    Conclusion and scope
The cubic X 3 + aX 2 realizes the classical group E over every characteristic-three field, without
enlarging the constant field. Its geometric ramification is determined at all heights by the same
induction that proves maximality of the tree group. The zero-place parity vectors detect exactly the
sibling square relations. Distinct Kummer characters then separate all global Artin–Schreier classes,
while cancellation of their principal parts makes the local class space at infinity one-dimensional.
The different and genus follow from this local information by standard formulas.
     The theorem is specific about its domain. The parameter a is nonzero, the characteristic is
three, and the target t is transcendental. Specializing t, changing the characteristic, or replacing the
inverse-image height by forward iteration time requires separate arguments. In particular, the result
supplies no formula for ordinary periodic points and no assertion that a finite specialization has the
full generic Galois group. The geometric branch data likewise make no residue-degree assertion over
a non-algebraically-closed constant field.
     Finally, an inverse-image Galois tower and its genera are source arithmetic data. No target Euler
factors, functional equation, root number, correspondence with zeros, or Hilbert–Pólya operator is
constructed here. The conclusion is the explicit wild tower and the simultaneous global/local rank
mechanism proved above.
