\section{Zero-place valuations and the square-class rank}\label{sec:kummer}

In this section and until the descent in Section~\ref{sec:descent},
we work over $\kk$. We retain the bars in $\Lbar_n$ and $\Mbar_n$
to make that convention visible. The zero-place analysis does not
assume maximality of the next Galois group.

\begin{lemma}[Branch support and the tame place]\label{lem:zero}
The extension $\Lbar_n/\kk(t)$ is unramified away from zero and
infinity. Its ramification index at every place above zero is $2^n$.
If $P$ is such a place, with normalized valuation $v_P$, then
every $\alpha\in\mathcal A_n$ is integral, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero-valuations}
 v_P(\alpha)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&\alpha\text{ specializes to }0,\\
 0,&\alpha\text{ specializes to a nonzero element of }\kk.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Exactly $2^n$ roots specialize to zero. In every bottom sibling
triple their valuation-parity vector is either $000$ or one of
$110,101,011$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:iterate-derivative} shows that every finite
critical value of the root cover $t=f^{\circ n}(X)$ is zero:
a vanishing intermediate iterate remains zero thereafter. Thus
that cover is unramified outside zero and infinity. The same is
true of its Galois closure, since conjugates and composita of
unramified local extensions remain unramified.

Above zero, $f$ has local degree two at $0$ and local degree one
at its other preimage $-a$. The ramification index of a point in
the $n$th root cover is the product of these local degrees along
its orbit, hence a power of two dividing $2^n$. At $X=0$ it is
exactly $2^n$. These local extensions are tame. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:local}, their local Galois closure has index the
least common multiple of the root-cover indices, which is $2^n$.
Consequently $v_P(t)=2^n$.

Every root is integral at $P$, by monicity. The reduction of
$f^{\circ n}(X)-t$ is $f^{\circ n}(X)$; its zero root has
multiplicity $2^n$. Since the polynomial splits into linear
factors upstairs, precisely that many roots specialize to zero.
Near zero, $f^{\circ n}(X)$ has a nonzero leading term of degree
$2^n$. Thus if $\alpha$ specializes to zero,
\[
 2^n v_P(\alpha)=v_P(f^{\circ n}(\alpha))=v_P(t)=2^n,
\]
which gives $v_P(\alpha)=1$. All other roots are units.

At a vertex specializing to zero, the reduced child equation is
$X^2(X+a)=0$, so two children specialize to zero and one to
$-a$. A vertex specializing to a nonzero value has no zero child.
The zero-specializing vertices therefore form a binary subtree,
and the asserted parity alternatives follow.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact square-class relations]\label{prop:kummer}
Assume $\Gal(\Lbar_n/\kk(t))=E_n$ in the compatible labelling.
Then the square-class assertion of Theorem~\ref{thm:ranks} holds
at height $n$, and
\[
 [\Mbar_n:\Lbar_n]=2^{2\cdot3^{n-1}}.
\]
Every class $[\alpha/a^3]$ is nonzero, and the classes belonging
to distinct roots are distinct.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $r_n=3^{n-1}$ be the number of bottom sibling triples.
Inside $\F_2^{\mathcal A_n}$ define
\[
 V_n=\bigoplus_{\text{sibling triples}}
       \{(u_1,u_2,u_3)\in\F_2^3:u_1+u_2+u_3=0\}.
\]
It has dimension $2r_n$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:zero}, the parity
vectors of root valuations at places over zero all lie in $V_n$.
We claim that their span is all of $V_n$.

Choose a place and a bottom triple on which its parity vector is
nonzero. Such a triple exists because $2^n$ roots specialize to
zero. The induction hypothesis and Lemma~\ref{lem:order} give a
Galois element acting as a three-cycle on that triple and fixing
all other triples. Conjugating the place permutes its valuation
vector accordingly. Adding the original and conjugated vectors
over $\F_2$ cancels every other triple and leaves a nonzero even
vector on the chosen triple. Its three cyclic translates span
the two-dimensional even subspace on that triple. The Galois
group acts transitively on bottom parents, since
$f^{\circ(n-1)}(X)-t$ is irreducible; for $n=1$ there is just
the parent $t$. Conjugation therefore gives the even subspace on
every triple. This proves the claim.

If
\[
 \prod_{\alpha\in\mathcal A_n}(\alpha/a^3)^{e_\alpha}
 \in\Lbar_n^{*2},\qquad e_\alpha\in\F_2,
\]
its valuation is even at every place over zero. The exponent
vector $e$ is consequently orthogonal to $V_n$ under the
coordinate pairing. The orthogonal complement $V_n^\perp$ is
the span of the all-one vectors on the disjoint sibling triples.
It remains to verify that these necessary relations actually hold.

For a sibling triple with parent $\eta$, formula
\eqref{eq:discproduct} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sibling-square}
 \prod_{f(\alpha)=\eta}\frac{\alpha}{a^3}
    =\frac{\eta}{a^9}
    =\left(\frac{\beta_\eta}{a^3}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
Here one may choose $\beta_\eta=\Delta_\eta/a^3\in\Lbar_n$ by
\eqref{eq:betavandermonde}, because $f(X)-\eta$ has split at
height $n$; it satisfies $\beta_\eta^2=\eta/a^3$. This includes
$n=1$, when $\eta=t$. Thus every
sibling vector is a genuine relation. There are no others.

The square classes have rank $3^n-r_n=2r_n$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:radicaldegree} gives the degree assertion. A
nonzero vector in the span of disjoint sibling indicators has
weight at least three, and its weight is a multiple of three.
Neither a weight-one vector nor a weight-two vector can be a
relation. The individual classes are therefore nonzero and
pairwise distinct.
\end{proof}

This proposition uses only the group at height $n$. The parity
vectors come from the tame zero-place geometry, and the relations
are verified by a product identity in the already known splitting
field. No degree or independence statement at height $n+1$ has
entered its proof.
