\section{The local and global Artin--Schreier induction}\label{sec:as}

We now carry two induction hypotheses at height $n$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-induction}
 \Gal(\Lbar_n/\kk(t))=E_n,
 \qquad e_\infty(\Lbar_n/\kk(t))=2\cdot3^n.
\end{equation}
Both hold at height one by Lemma~\ref{lem:first}. The second
hypothesis controls valuations before the next global degree is
known.

\subsection{Kummer splitting at infinity}

\begin{lemma}[Pole order after the quadratic step]\label{lem:pole}
Assume \eqref{eq:joint-induction}. The extension
$\Mbar_n/\Lbar_n$ splits completely at every place above infinity.
At each such normalized place of $\Mbar_n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta-pole}
 v(\alpha)=-2,\qquad v(\beta_\alpha)=-1
 \quad\text{for every }\alpha\in\mathcal A_n.
\end{equation}
In particular, every $[\beta_\alpha]$ is nonzero in
$\Mbar_n/\wpmap(\Mbar_n)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $Q$ be a normalized place of $\Lbar_n$ over infinity.
The second hypothesis gives $v_Q(t)=-2\cdot3^n$.
A root $\alpha$ cannot be integral, since $f^{\circ n}(\alpha)=t$.
The polynomial $f^{\circ n}$ is monic of degree $3^n$, with
constant-field coefficients. Its leading term therefore has
strictly smallest valuation at $\alpha$, giving
\[
 3^n v_Q(\alpha)=v_Q(t)=-2\cdot3^n.
\]
Thus $v_Q(\alpha)=-2$.

Each $\alpha/a^3$ is a unit times an even power of a uniformizer.
Every such unit has a square root in the geometric completion by
Lemma~\ref{lem:local}. All the quadratic polynomials used to
define $\Mbar_n$ already split in that completion. Since
$\Mbar_n/\Lbar_n$ is Galois, it splits completely there. The
normalized valuation is consequently unchanged after the quadratic
step, and $v(\beta_\alpha)=-1$.

An element with pole order one cannot be an Artin--Schreier image
in the completion, by Lemma~\ref{lem:local}. A global equation
$\beta_\alpha=u^3-u$ would remain such an equation in every
completion, which is impossible. Hence its global class is nonzero.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Independent global classes}

The nonvanishing in Lemma~\ref{lem:pole} is only an individual
statement. To pass from nonvanishing to independence, we use the
distinct characters produced by the exact Kummer relations.

\begin{proposition}[Global Artin--Schreier rank]\label{prop:AS-global}
Under \eqref{eq:joint-induction}, the $3^n$ elements
$[\beta_\alpha]\in\Mbar_n/\wpmap(\Mbar_n)$ are linearly
independent over $\F_3$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:AS-field}
 \Lbar_{n+1}
   =\Mbar_n(z_\alpha:\alpha\in\mathcal A_n),
 \qquad z_\alpha^3-z_\alpha=\beta_\alpha,
\end{equation}
and $[\Lbar_{n+1}:\Mbar_n]=3^{3^n}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $H=\Gal(\Mbar_n/\Lbar_n)$. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:kummer}, this is an elementary abelian
two-group. The square-root line for each $\alpha$ is a character
line:
\[
 h(\beta_\alpha)=\chi_\alpha(h)\beta_\alpha,
 \qquad \chi_\alpha:H\longrightarrow\{1,-1\}.
\]
The characters are nontrivial and pairwise distinct. Indeed,
triviality would put $\beta_\alpha$ in $\Lbar_n$, making
$\alpha/a^3$ a square. Equality of two characters would put
$\beta_\alpha/\beta_\gamma$ in $\Lbar_n$, making their square
classes equal. Both conclusions are excluded by
Proposition~\ref{prop:kummer}.

The Artin--Schreier image is stable under $H$, so the quotient
$V=\Mbar_n/\wpmap(\Mbar_n)$ is an $\F_3[H]$-module.
Its dimension need not be finite. For any character $\chi$ of
$H$, the finite group-algebra operator
\[
 P_\chi=|H|^{-1}\sum_{h\in H}\chi(h)^{-1}h
\]
is defined over $\F_3$, since $|H|$ is a power of two.
Character orthogonality gives
$P_{\chi_\alpha}[\beta_\gamma]
 =\delta_{\alpha,\gamma}[\beta_\gamma]$.
Apply this operator to any proposed finite linear relation among
the $[\beta_\gamma]$. Lemma~\ref{lem:pole} says that the
selected class is nonzero, so its coefficient must vanish.
Doing this for every $\alpha$ proves independence.

For each parent $\alpha$, Lemma~\ref{lem:normal} gives its
three children from $\beta_\alpha,z_\alpha$. Thus the right
side of \eqref{eq:AS-field} contains all roots at height $n+1$.
Conversely, their Vandermondes recover the quadratic radicals,
and the differences in \eqref{eq:roots} recover each
$z_\alpha$. The right side is exactly the splitting field.
The degree formula now follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:radicaldegree} and the proved rank $3^n$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Only one local class}

The preceding character projections are global. There is no reason
to expect that they preserve one chosen place, and no such claim
is used. At a chosen completion the direct comparison of principal
parts gives a different answer.

\begin{proposition}[Local rank and the next infinity index]\label{prop:AS-local}
Under \eqref{eq:joint-induction}, the images of all
$[\beta_\alpha]$ in each completion of $\Mbar_n$ above infinity
span exactly one dimension over $\F_3$. The corresponding local
extension to $\Lbar_{n+1}$ is totally ramified of degree three.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:next-infinity}
 e_\infty(\Lbar_{n+1}/\kk(t))=2\cdot3^{n+1}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix such a completion $K$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:pole}, every
root has valuation $-2$. For two roots $\alpha,\gamma$, the
equations $f^{\circ n}(\alpha)=f^{\circ n}(\gamma)=t$ and
monicity show that the residue of the unit $\alpha/\gamma$
has $3^n$th power one. In characteristic three,
$X^{3^n}-1=(X-1)^{3^n}$, so this residue is one.

It follows that the residue of
$\beta_\alpha/\beta_\gamma$ is either $1$ or $-1$.
Both square roots have pole order one. For a suitable
$\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}\subset\F_3$, their principal
parts therefore cancel:
\[
 \beta_\alpha-\varepsilon\beta_\gamma\in\mathcal O_K.
\]
Every integral element of $K$ is an Artin--Schreier image by
Lemma~\ref{lem:local}. Hence
$[\beta_\alpha]=\varepsilon[\beta_\gamma]$ in
$K/\wpmap(K)$. At least one, and in fact every one, is nonzero
because its pole order is one. The span has dimension exactly one.

Formula~\eqref{eq:AS-field} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:radicaldegree} now give local degree three.
The pole-order calculation in Lemma~\ref{lem:local} makes this
extension totally ramified. The preceding Kummer step is split,
so multiplication of ramification indices gives
\eqref{eq:next-infinity}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Completing the induction}

The last two propositions establish different conclusions: one
gives the degree of the global compositum, and the other gives the
degree of a single local extension. Neither rank is inferred from
the other. Combining Proposition~\ref{prop:kummer} with
Proposition~\ref{prop:AS-global} yields
\[
 [\Lbar_{n+1}:\Lbar_n]
   =2^{2\cdot3^{n-1}}3^{3^n}
   =\frac{|E_{n+1}|}{|E_n|}.
\]
The first induction hypothesis and the upper bound
$\Gal(\Lbar_{n+1}/\kk(t))\subseteq E_{n+1}$ therefore force
equality at height $n+1$. Proposition~\ref{prop:AS-local}
establishes the second induction hypothesis at that height.
Both base cases were proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:first}, so the
simultaneous induction is complete for every $n\geq1$.

Proposition~\ref{prop:kummer} and the two Artin--Schreier
propositions also prove every assertion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:ranks}, including complete Kummer splitting at
infinity and the precise local rank. The infinity-index assertion
of Theorem~\ref{thm:genus} has been proved without using a
different or genus calculation.

\subsection{Descent to the original field}\label{sec:descent}

Return to an arbitrary field $k$ of characteristic three. The
signature upper bound, with its compatible labelling, was proved
over $k$. The geometric calculation just completed gives
\[
 |E_n|=[\Lbar_n:\kk(t)]
      \leq[L_n:k(t)]\leq|E_n|.
\]
All inequalities are equalities. The natural restriction of the
geometric group into the arithmetic tree action is therefore an
isomorphism, and both groups are exactly $E_n$ in the chosen
labelling.

Equality of the degrees also says that extension of constants to
$\kk$ does not split the function field: the natural map
\[
 L_n\otimes_{k(t)}\kk(t)\longrightarrow\kk L_n
\]
is an isomorphism. Indeed, it is a surjective homomorphism of
$\kk(t)$-algebras of the same finite dimension. The tensor
product is thus a field. Since $L_n/k(t)$ is separable, this is
geometric integrality over $k$, equivalently regularity of
$L_n/k$. In particular no nontrivial algebraic constant extension
of $k$ is contained in $L_n$. This argument does not require $k$
to be perfect.

The labelling was chosen once on the infinite tree, so these
finite-height equalities respect restriction. Passing to inverse
limits proves the last assertion of Theorem~\ref{thm:tower}.
