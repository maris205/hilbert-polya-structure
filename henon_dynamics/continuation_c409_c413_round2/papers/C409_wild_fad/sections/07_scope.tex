\section{Conclusions and scope}

For the coefficient class \eqref{eq:intro-coeff}, rationality is
equivalent to the disappearance of every active unbounded valuation
fibre after torsion-related phases have been aggregated.
An unmasked fibre forces dense actual Fourier masses and, by a
radial limit, a natural boundary even for meromorphic continuation.
This gives a finite exact criterion, not merely a sufficient
generic noncancellation condition.

The assumptions are part of the conclusion's scope: only finitely
many primes and phases are allowed; the weights and exponents are
periodic; and the exponents are nonnegative real numbers.
The proof does not extend by assertion to infinitely many distortion
primes, arbitrary continuous local functions, or general sums of
natural-boundary series.

For realized FAD systems, the own-prime coprimality of exponent
periods and positivity of the determinant's leading factor force
the active branch whenever genuine distortion is present.
The no-wild application is already obtained from the cited
adelic-perturbation theorem. The additional wild application includes
the explicit nonhyperbolic product in Section~\ref{sec:example},
without a height or all-embeddings assumption.
The statements concern ordinary integer iterates and their native
fixed-point counts; no change of clock or identification with a
different arithmetic zeta function is made.
