\section{Conductor grids and the exact dichotomy}\label{sec:boundary}

\begin{lemma}[An active fibre has unbounded conductor]\label{lem:conductor}
Suppose \(C,p,a\) satisfy \eqref{eq:af}. In the expansion of \(q_C\)
there are nonzero coefficients with arbitrarily high \(p\)-conductor.
More precisely, for every \(K\geq\nu_p\), some character with nonzero
coefficient is nontrivial on the subgroup having finite coordinate zero,
\(p\)-coordinate in \(p^K\Z_p\), and all other coordinates zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix the finite coordinate at \(a\). For every \(q\ne p\), choose a
nonzero element \(x_q\) in \(a+q^{\nu_q}\Z_q\).
Every corresponding kernel value is strictly positive. Along the
remaining coordinate ball \(p^{\nu_p}\Z_p\), formula \eqref{eq:q}
becomes
\[
q_C(a,x)=B_0H_{p;s_{p,a},t_{p,a}}(x_p),\qquad B_0\ne0.
\]
Its values at \(x_p=0\) and \(x_p=p^K\) differ for every
\(K\geq\nu_p\). If all characters with nonzero coefficient were trivial
on the subgroup in the statement, uniform convergence in
\eqref{eq:q-fourier} would make \(q_C\) invariant under its translations,
contradicting these two values.

Represent such a character using a product character whose
\(p\)-component has the form
\[
x_p\longmapsto
\exp(2\pi i b[x_p]_{p^k}/p^k),\qquad p\nmid b.
\]
Nontriviality on \(p^K\Z_p\) forces \(k>K\). When \(k>\nu_p\), this
high conductor cannot be absorbed in the finite coordinate: its
restriction to the \(p\)-coordinate subgroup \(p^{\nu_p}\Z_p\)
still has conductor \(p^{k-\nu_p}\). Thus these conductors are
unbounded in one fixed class \(C\) and at one fixed prime \(p\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Full grids of actual nonzero coefficients]\label{lem:grids}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:conductor}, the nonzero
frequencies \(\chi(g_0)\) of \(q_C\) contain rotated full root grids of
unbounded orders, with a common nonzero Fourier coefficient on each grid.
In particular, those frequencies are dense in \(\T\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set \(\kappa=\max(1,\nu_p)\). For a \(p\)-adic unit
\(v\equiv1\pmod{p^\kappa}\), let \(\alpha_v\) multiply \(x_p\) by \(v\)
and leave every other coordinate, including \(a\), fixed.
Equation \eqref{eq:group} shows that \(\alpha_v\) is an automorphism of
\(D\). Radiality implies \(q_C\circ\alpha_v=q_C\). Invariance of Haar
probability under this automorphism gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unit-invariance}
\widehat q_C(\chi\circ\alpha_v)=\widehat q_C(\chi).
\end{equation}

Take a nonzero coefficient with \(p\)-conductor \(p^k\), \(k>\kappa\),
and primitive numerator \(b\), as in Lemma~\ref{lem:conductor}.
For \(v=1+p^\kappa t\), \(0\leq t<p^{k-\kappa}\), evaluation at \(g_0\)
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grid}
(\chi\circ\alpha_v)(g_0)
=\chi(g_0)\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i bt}{p^{k-\kappa}}\right).
\end{equation}
Since \(p\nmid b\), these are the distinct points of a rotated full
grid of order \(p^{k-\kappa}\). Equation \eqref{eq:unit-invariance}
assigns the same nonzero coefficient to each point. The conductors
\(k\) are unbounded, so every nonempty open arc contains a point of one
of these grids.
\end{proof}

Unbounded conductor alone would not suffice: an arbitrary infinite set
of rational frequencies need not be dense. The complete unit orbits
in \eqref{eq:grid} are the step that supplies density.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Evaluation on the diagonal integers and undoing the phase grouping give
\[
a_n=\sum_C\rho_C^n q_C(ng_0)
=\sum_C\sum_\chi\widehat q_C(\chi)
       \bigl(\rho_C\chi(g_0)\bigr)^n.
\]
Define the finite-variation atomic measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actual-measure}
\mu=\sum_C\sum_\chi\widehat q_C(\chi)\,
\delta_{\rho_C\chi(g_0)}.
\end{equation}
Its total variation is finite by Lemma~\ref{lem:fourier}.
Within one class, distinct characters have distinct generator values
by \eqref{eq:injective}. Between different classes, an equality
\(\rho_C\chi(g_0)=\rho_{C'}\chi'(g_0)\) would make
\(\rho_C/\rho_{C'}\) a root of unity, contradicting the definition of
the classes. Thus no further collisions remain in
\eqref{eq:actual-measure}. Each displayed nonzero coefficient is a
nonzero mass of the actual measure.
Equation \eqref{eq:moments} follows, and if \(\AF\) holds,
Lemma~\ref{lem:grids} proves density of its nonzero atoms.

For \(|z|<1\), absolute summability allows the geometric series to be
integrated term by term:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cauchy}
F(z)=\int_{\T}\frac{\xi z}{1-\xi z}\,d\mu(\xi).
\end{equation}
For \(\xi,\xi_0\in\T\) and \(0<\varrho<1\),
\[
\left|(1-\varrho)
\frac{\varrho\xi\xi_0^{-1}}{1-\varrho\xi\xi_0^{-1}}\right|
\leq\varrho\leq1.
\]
The expression tends to one if \(\xi=\xi_0\) and to zero otherwise.
Dominated convergence with respect to \(|\mu|\) proves
\eqref{eq:radial}, including at points that are not atoms.

A nonzero right-hand side in \eqref{eq:radial} excludes a holomorphic
extension at \(\xi_0^{-1}\), since local boundedness would force that
limit to vanish. The inverse atom points are dense, so no boundary
arc admits holomorphic continuation. If a meromorphic continuation
existed in a neighborhood of any boundary point, every inverse
nonzero atom in the enclosed arc would have to be a pole. Poles of a
meromorphic function are discrete, which is incompatible with this
dense set. This proves the active branch.

Suppose instead that \(\AF\) fails. On a fibre over \(a\), either
\(b_C(a)=0\), or each active coordinate has
\(a\not\equiv0\pmod{p^{\nu_p}}\). In the latter situation its valuation
is fixed throughout \(a+p^{\nu_p}\Z_p\), strictly below \(\nu_p\).
Every inactive factor equals one. Hence \(q_C\) is constant on the
fibre; denote this value by \(d_C(a)\). With the zero residue
represented by \(W\), summation on residue classes yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational}
F(z)=\sum_C
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{W}d_C(a)(\rho_Cz)^a}
{1-(\rho_Cz)^W}.
\end{equation}
This proves rationality, including the empty-data cases.
\end{proof}
