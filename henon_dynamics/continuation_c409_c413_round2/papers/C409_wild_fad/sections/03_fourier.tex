\section{An absolutely summable adelic Fourier representation}
\label{sec:fourier}

The compact group that keeps the periodic and local coordinates
compatible is
\[
D=\overline{\{(n\bmod W,(n)_{p\in S}):n\in\Z\}}
\ \subset\ (\Z/W\Z)\times\prod_{p\in S}\Z_p.
\]
Let \(g_0=(1\bmod W,(1)_{p\in S})\).
The Chinese remainder theorem gives the explicit description
\begin{equation}\label{eq:group}
D=\{(a,x):x_p\equiv a\pmod{p^{\nu_p}}\text{ for every }p\in S\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the right-hand side is closed and contains every diagonal
integer. Conversely, a neighborhood of a point on the right specifies
the finite coordinate modulo \(W\) and each local coordinate modulo
some \(p^{K_p}\), with \(K_p\geq\nu_p\). Compatibility in
\eqref{eq:group} is precisely the condition for this simultaneous
congruence system to have an integer solution.

For any continuous characters \(\chi,\chi'\) of \(D\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:injective}
\chi(g_0)=\chi'(g_0)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\chi=\chi',
\end{equation}
since equality then holds on all integer multiples of \(g_0\), a
dense subgroup. All characters used below are restrictions of
finite-order product characters, so their values at \(g_0\) are
roots of unity.

\begin{lemma}[Radial Fourier expansion]\label{lem:fourier}
Each function \(H_{p;s,t}\) has a uniformly absolutely convergent
character expansion on \(\Z_p\), with sum of absolute coefficients at
most \(2\). For each phase class \(C\), the function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
q_C(a,x)=b_C(a)\prod_{p\in S}H_{p;s_{p,a},t_{p,a}}(x_p)
\end{equation}
therefore has an expansion on \(D\)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q-fourier}
q_C(g)=\sum_{\chi}\widehat q_C(\chi)\chi(g),
\qquad
\sum_\chi|\widehat q_C(\chi)|
\leq 2^{|S|}\sum_{a\in\Z/W\Z}|b_C(a)|.
\end{equation}
The coefficients in this expression are the Haar Fourier coefficients
after equal restricted characters have been combined.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For an active pair, put \(h(v)=p^{-sv-tp^v}\) for \(v\geq0\) and
\(\delta_j=h(j-1)-h(j)\). The sequence \(h\) decreases strictly to zero,
so \(\delta_j>0\) and \(\sum_{j\geq1}\delta_j=h(0)\leq1\).
Telescoping yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:balls}
H_{p;s,t}(x)
=h(0)-\sum_{j\geq1}\delta_j\one_{p^j\Z_p}(x).
\end{equation}
At \(x\ne0\), the sum telescopes to \(h(v_p(x))\); at zero it
telescopes to zero. Its functional tail is bounded uniformly by its
numerical tail.

Writing \([x]_{p^j}\) for a residue representative, the finite
orthogonality identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ball-fourier}
\one_{p^j\Z_p}(x)
=\frac1{p^j}\sum_{b=0}^{p^j-1}
\exp\left(\frac{2\pi i b[x]_{p^j}}{p^j}\right).
\end{equation}
Thus each ball indicator has Fourier norm one.
Combining \eqref{eq:balls} and \eqref{eq:ball-fourier} gives total
absolute coefficient mass at most \(2h(0)\leq2\).
The inactive kernel is simply the trivial character.

On the ambient product group, express \eqref{eq:q} as a finite sum
over the singleton indicators of \(a\in\Z/W\Z\). Each such indicator
also has Fourier norm one. Multiplication of the absolutely summable
local expansions, followed by this finite sum, gives the bound in
\eqref{eq:q-fourier}. Restricting to \(D\) and combining characters that
coincide cannot increase the sum of absolute coefficients.

Finally, uniform absolute convergence permits termwise integration
against a conjugate character. A nontrivial character has Haar
integral zero: translation by a point at which it differs from one
multiplies the integral by that value while leaving it invariant.
This identifies the combined coefficients with the Fourier integrals.
\end{proof}

The lemma is a consequence of the particular monotone radial kernels.
No absolute-Fourier-convergence assertion for arbitrary continuous
functions on a compact group is being used.
