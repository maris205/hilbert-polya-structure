                         Active fibres and natural boundaries
                            in wild finite-adelic dynamics



                                                      Abstract
          We give an exact finite criterion for a power series with finite-adelic radial coefficients and
      a finite sum of unit-modulus exponential phases to be rational or to have the unit circle as a
      meromorphic natural boundary. The periodic phase weights may be complex, and the periodic
      valuation and wild exponents may be arbitrary nonnegative real numbers. The criterion first
      groups phases whose ratios are roots of unity, then tests whether a nonzero grouped weight
      survives on a residue fibre with unbounded valuation at an active prime. Its proof constructs the
      actual finite atomic coefficient measure after all coincident frequencies have been combined. An
      active fibre forces unbounded conductor; invariance under local units then supplies arbitrarily fine
      grids of nonzero atoms. Radial limits detect these masses and exclude meromorphic continuation.
      We apply the criterion to confined finite-adelically distorted systems, without a unique dominant
      root or integral wild exponents, and exhibit an actual nonhyperbolic wild product system.
      The no-wild application is explicitly separated from the conclusion already obtainable from
      adelic-perturbation theorems.


1    Introduction
Local distortion of periodic-point counts often changes a rational dynamical zeta function into
one with a natural boundary. When several archimedean phases have the same maximal modulus,
however, a natural-boundary argument must account for cancellation between them. This issue
persists even if every individual phase-weighted summand has a natural boundary: the sum need
not have one. We resolve this cancellation question for finite-adelic radial coefficients with periodic
data by an exact finite test.

    Natural-boundary questions for algebraic dynamics have a substantial history. Bell–Miles–Ward
developed a Pólya–Carlson programme for algebraic systems, including finite-place distortion and
the role of archimedean unit roots [2]. Byszewski–Cornelissen treated abelian varieties in positive
characteristic, with a valuation-kernel dichotomy in the appendix by Royals–Ward [4]. Byszewski–
Cornelissen–Houben organized a much wider class through finite-adelically distorted systems [5].
Their inspected Theorem 11.3.8 assumes a unique dominant root and integral wild data. Our
dynamical application removes these two assumptions for the finite-adelic presentation considered
here.
    It is important to distinguish this conclusion from known no-wild results. Baril Boudreau–
Holmes–Nguyen prove a natural-boundary alternative for stable rational series perturbed by almost
quasi-polynomials [1]. Their theorem already gives the no-wild nonhyperbolic finite-adelic conclusion;
we give the deduction in Section 5. The genuinely wild example in Section 6 fails their sublinear-
height hypothesis. Likewise, the normalized example does not satisfy the all-embeddings unit-disc
convergence condition in the criterion of Bell–Gunn–Nguyen–Saunders [3]. These failed sufficient
hypotheses delimit the applications of those theorems; they are not, by themselves, priority assertions.
    The Fourier-to-boundary passage is classical in character. For example, Knill–Lesieutre use
Fourier expansions and radial singularities for almost-periodic coefficients [7, Proposition 2.2 in the
revised manuscript]. Cornelissen–Park also use Fourier analysis of finite-adelic coefficients, including
archimedean phases, to study orbit-decomposition statistics [6]. We do not claim a new general
Cauchy-transform principle or the first Fourier treatment of these coefficients. The point is the
exact active-fibre criterion and the conductor-grid argument proving dense noncancelled masses in
its active branch.

7     Conclusions and scope
For the coefficient class (1), rationality is equivalent to the disappearance of every active unbounded
valuation fibre after torsion-related phases have been aggregated. An unmasked fibre forces dense
actual Fourier masses and, by a radial limit, a natural boundary even for meromorphic continuation.
This gives a finite exact criterion, not merely a sufficient generic noncancellation condition.
    The assumptions are part of the conclusion’s scope: only finitely many primes and phases are
allowed; the weights and exponents are periodic; and the exponents are nonnegative real numbers.
The proof does not extend by assertion to infinitely many distortion primes, arbitrary continuous
local functions, or general sums of natural-boundary series.
