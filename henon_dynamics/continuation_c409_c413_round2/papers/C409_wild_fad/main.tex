\begin{abstract}
We give an exact finite criterion for a power series with finite-adelic
radial coefficients and a finite sum of unit-modulus exponential phases
to be rational or to have the unit circle as a meromorphic natural
boundary. The periodic phase weights may be complex, and the periodic
valuation and wild exponents may be arbitrary nonnegative real numbers.
The criterion first groups phases whose ratios are roots of unity, then
tests whether a nonzero grouped weight survives on a residue fibre with
unbounded valuation at an active prime. Its proof constructs the actual
finite atomic coefficient measure after all coincident frequencies have
been combined. An active fibre forces unbounded conductor; invariance
under local units then supplies arbitrarily fine grids of nonzero atoms.
Radial limits detect these masses and exclude meromorphic continuation.
We apply the criterion to confined finite-adelically distorted systems,
without a unique dominant root or integral wild exponents, and exhibit an
actual nonhyperbolic wild product system. The no-wild application is
explicitly separated from the conclusion already obtainable from
adelic-perturbation theorems.
\end{abstract}
