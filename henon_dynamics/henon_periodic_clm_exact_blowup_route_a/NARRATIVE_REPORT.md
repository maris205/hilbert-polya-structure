# Narrative report

The main difficulty is not solving a scalar Riccati equation; it is entering that equation without losing the periodic mean or flipping the Hilbert sign. The fixed convention gives `H sin=-cos`. Subtracting the conserved mean and choosing `z=Hf+if` then yields exactly `z_t=iμz+z²/2`.

At nonzero mean, the complex denominator is periodic in time. Setting it to zero simultaneously forces `ω_0(x)=0` and `h_0(x)=|μ|cot(|μ|t/2)`. Cotangent is a bijection over one fundamental time cell, so every initial zero contributes one pole and the earliest is a compact minimum on the principal `arccot` branch. This proves the iff statement, not merely a sufficient criterion. At zero mean, the denominator is a sum of squares, yielding the signed-zero formula.

The one-mode family makes all regimes visible: zero-free global evolution, tangent threshold, crossing zeros, and the zero-mean limit. The tangent threshold also shows why one must not advertise one universal blow-up profile. A simple spatial zero makes the complex denominator transverse in space-time; only then does the scale `x-x*≈T-t` yield a bounded nontrivial inverse-time profile. A global rate needs every simultaneous first pole to share that transversality.

The exact closure is theorem-scale and still fails Route A at every gate: it has no arithmetic carrier, isolated orbit ledger, determinant bridge, target analytic structure, or natural quantization.
