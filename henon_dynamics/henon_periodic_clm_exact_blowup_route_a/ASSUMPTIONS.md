# Assumptions and conventions

1. `T=R/(2πZ)` and the Hilbert transform is fixed by `H(e^{ikx})=-i sign(k)e^{ikx}`; hence `H sin(kx)=-cos(kx)` for positive `k`.
2. Initial data are real and smooth. The formulas hold on the maximal classical smooth interval before a denominator pole.
3. `μ=<ω_0>`, `f=ω-μ`, `h=Hf`, and `z=h+if`. This choice—not its complex conjugate—is used everywhere.
4. `arccot:R→(0,π)` is the strictly decreasing principal branch.
5. A “breakdown” is loss of classical smoothness detected by an unbounded Riccati/Hilbert component. It need not mean that the point value `ω(t,x*)` itself diverges at a zero.
6. Local vorticity profiles require a simple initial zero. The global `L∞` two-sided rate additionally requires every simultaneous first pole to be simple.
7. Finite Gaussian-rational evidence checks algebra; it is not proof by sampling.
