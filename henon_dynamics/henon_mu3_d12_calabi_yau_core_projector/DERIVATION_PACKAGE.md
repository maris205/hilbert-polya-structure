## 1. Completeness of the monomial enumeration

Let a projective monomial transformation be represented by

\[
 x_i\longmapsto a_i x_{\sigma(i)}.
\]

Preservation of the Fermat cubic hypersurface implies
\(a_i^3=\alpha\) for one common \(\alpha\).  Dividing all \(a_i\) by
\(a_0\) is a projective rescaling, so every ratio is a cube root of unity.
Because \(\mu_3\subset K\), every projective class has a representative

\[
 a_i=\rho^{e_i},
 \qquad
 e_i\in\mathbf F_3,
 \qquad
 e_0=0.                                             \tag{D1}
\]

The monomial support of the quadric is the eight-cycle.  Hence \(\sigma\)
must lie in its dihedral automorphism group, written

\[
 r_k(i)=i+k,
 \qquad
 s_k(i)=k-i
 \pmod 8.                                           \tag{D2}
\]

Give the seven ordinary edges coefficient exponent zero and the closing
edge \(\{7,0\}\) coefficient exponent one.  If \(c(E)\in\mathbf F_3\)
is this edge exponent, the quadric condition is the finite affine system

\[
 c(\{i,i+1\})+e_i+e_{i+1}
 =q_g+c(\{\sigma(i),\sigma(i+1)\})
 \quad(0\le i\le7),                                 \tag{D3}
\]

where indices are cyclic and \(q_g\) is independent of \(i\).

Solving (D3) with \(e_0=0\) leaves exactly the following rows; an
eight-digit word denotes \((e_0,\ldots,e_7)\):

| permutation | normalized phase words |
|---|---|
| \(r_0\) | `00000000`, `01010101`, `02020202` |
| \(r_2\) | `01010110`, `02020211`, `00000012` |
| \(r_4\) | `01011010`, `02021111`, `00001212` |
| \(r_6\) | `01101010`, `02111111`, `00121212` |
| \(s_1\) | `01101010`, `02111111`, `00121212` |
| \(s_3\) | `01011010`, `02021111`, `00001212` |
| \(s_5\) | `01010110`, `02020211`, `00000012` |
| \(s_7\) | `00000000`, `01010101`, `02020202` |

Thus the complete projective monomial source stabilizer has \(24\)
elements.  No odd rotation or even reflection solves (D3).

Choose

\[
 r=(r_6,01101010),
 \qquad
 s=(s_7,01010101).                                  \tag{D4}
\]

Substitution into (D3) gives \(C(rx)=C(sx)=C(x)\) and

\[
 Q(rx)=Q(sx)=\rho Q(x).                             \tag{D5}
\]

Exact phase-permutation composition yields

\[
 r^{12}=s^2=1,
 \qquad
 srs=r^{-1}.                                        \tag{D6}
\]

The subgroup generated by \(r,s\) already has \(24\) elements, so it is
the whole enumerated group and is isomorphic to
\(\operatorname{Dih}(C_{12})\).

## 2. Correspondence composition

For cycles \(a,b,c,d\) of complementary degrees on a smooth fivefold, the
action of \(a\times b\) is

\[
 \alpha\longmapsto\left(\int_X\alpha a\right)b,
\]

and decomposable correspondences compose as

\[
 (a\times b)\circ(c\times d)
 =\left(\int_Xda\right)c\times b.                  \tag{D7}
\]

With

\[
 \pi_{2i}=\frac1{6}h^{5-i}\times h^i,
\]

equation (D7) and \(\int_Xh^5=6\) give

\[
 \pi_{2i}\circ\pi_{2j}=\delta_{ij}\pi_{2i}.        \tag{D8}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \pi_5=\Delta_X-\sum_{i=0}^{5}\pi_{2i}             \tag{D9}
\]

is an exact Chow idempotent.  Weak Lefschetz identifies all nonmiddle
cohomology with the ambient projective-space classes, so the realization of
\(\pi_5\) is precisely \(H^5(X)\).  Since
\(\pi_{2i}^t=\pi_{10-2i}\), both the sum in (D9) and \(\pi_5\) are
self-transpose.

For a projective linear automorphism \(g\), one has \(g^*h=h\).  Hence

\[
 [\Gamma_g]\pi_{2i}=\pi_{2i}[\Gamma_g],
 \qquad
 [\Gamma_g]\pi_5=\pi_5[\Gamma_g].                  \tag{D10}
\]

Graph composition and transpose are

\[
 [\Gamma_g][\Gamma_h]=[\Gamma_{gh}],
 \qquad
 [\Gamma_g]^t=[\Gamma_{g^{-1}}].                   \tag{D11}
\]

Summing (D11) over the group proves

\[
 e_G^2=e_G,
 \qquad
 e_G^t=e_G.                                         \tag{D12}
\]

Equations (D8)--(D12) prove every Chow identity for
\(\pi_{\mathrm{core}}\) and \(\pi_{\mathrm{lev}}\) without an MCK
assumption.

## 3. Cayley ring and residue action

Let \(u=(y,z)^t\), \(F=(C,Q)^t\), so

\[
 \mathscr F=u^tF.
\]

If \(F(M_gx)=A_gF(x)\), then

\[
 \widetilde g:(x,u)\longmapsto(M_gx,A_g^{-t}u)      \tag{D13}
\]

preserves \(\mathscr F\).  The ambient top form on the Cayley variables
changes by

\[
 \det(M_g)\det(A_g^{-t})
 =\frac{\det M_g}{\det A_g}.                        \tag{D14}
\]

Consequently the residue action on a Jacobian-ring representative is its
polynomial pullback under (D13), multiplied by (D14).

Changing the scalar lift \(M_g\) to \(\lambda M_g\) changes the equation
matrix to

\[
 A_g\longmapsto
 \begin{pmatrix}\lambda^3&0\\0&\lambda^2\end{pmatrix}A_g.  \tag{D15}
\]

Every monomial representing a class in \(R_{p,-3}\) has total scalar
\(\lambda^{-3}\) under the simultaneous change in the Cayley polynomial
pullback induced by (D15): the \(x\)-degree contributes a positive power
of \(\lambda\), while the \(y\)- and \(z\)-powers contribute
\(\lambda^{-3}\) and \(\lambda^{-2}\), and the bidegree identity makes
their product \(\lambda^{-3}\).  In contrast, (D14) changes by
\(\lambda^{8-3-2}=\lambda^3\).  The two scalars cancel.  For the explicit
control \(\lambda=2\), the polynomial part contributes \(2^{-3}\) and the
determinant ratio contributes \(2^3\).  Thus the residue action descends
from \(\operatorname{GL}_8\) lifts to the declared projective group.
Omitting (D14) destroys this invariance.

The ambient monomials of bidegree \((2,-3)\) are

\[
 y^2K[x]_3\oplus yzK[x]_2\oplus z^2K[x]_1,          \tag{D16}
\]

whose dimension is

\[
 \binom{10}{3}+\binom{9}{2}+8=120+36+8=164.         \tag{D17}
\]

The bidegree piece of the Jacobian ideal has rank \(81\); hence

\[
 \dim R_{2,-3}=164-81=83.                           \tag{D18}
\]

Likewise \(R_{1,-3}=Ky\) has dimension one.

Exact matrices for the action on the quotient give the traces

\[
 t_k:=\operatorname{tr}(r^k)
 =(83,-1,-3,-1,-7,-1,3,-1,-7,-1,-3,-1),            \tag{D19}
\]

and

\[
 \operatorname{tr}(sr^k)=3.                         \tag{D20}
\]

For a one-dimensional character \(\chi_{a,b}\) with
\(\chi_{a,b}(r)=a\), \(\chi_{a,b}(s)=b\), the multiplicity is

\[
 m_{a,b}=\frac1{24}\sum_{k=0}^{11}
 \left(a^kt_k+ba^k\cdot3\right).                   \tag{D21}
\]

Substitution gives

\[
 (m_{1,1},m_{1,-1},m_{-1,1},m_{-1,-1})
 =(4,1,3,3).                                        \tag{D22}
\]

For the two-dimensional character \(\vartheta_j\), reflection traces
vanish and

\[
 m_j=\frac1{24}\sum_{k=0}^{11}
 2\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi jk}{6}\right)t_k,
 \qquad 1\le j\le5.                                \tag{D23}
\]

The result is

\[
 (m_1,m_2,m_3,m_4,m_5)=(7,8,6,8,7).                \tag{D24}
\]

For the chosen generators, \(A_r=A_s=\operatorname{diag}(1,\rho)\)
and \(\det M_r=\det M_s=\rho\), so the residue-orientation multiplier is
one on the generators.  The explicit calculation nevertheless retains
(D14), since scalar-lift invariance and other group elements must not be
encoded by an accidental generator simplification.

## 4. Hodge ranks

The invariant subspace in \(H^{4,1}\) has dimension one.  Equation (D22)
gives four invariant copies in \(H^{3,2}\).  Complex conjugation supplies
the same dimensions in \(H^{2,3}\) and \(H^{1,4}\).  Therefore

\[
 \dim e_GH^5(X)=1+4+4+1=10.                        \tag{D25}
\]

Since \(\dim H^5(X)=168\),

\[
 \dim(1-e_G)H^5(X)=168-10=158.                     \tag{D26}
\]

Removing four invariant copies from each 83-dimensional middle Hodge piece
gives

\[
 83-4=79,                                           \tag{D27}
\]

which yields the ledgers

\[
 (1,4,4,1),
 \qquad
 (0,79,79,0).                                       \tag{D28}
\]

## 5. Augmentation obstruction

Let \(V^{\mathrm{triv}}\) be the full trivial isotypic subspace of
\(H^5(X)\).  For

\[
 a=\sum_{g\in G}a_gg\in\mathbf Q[G],
\]

and every \(v\in V^{\mathrm{triv}}\),

\[
 av=\left(\sum_ga_g\right)v=\varepsilon(a)v.        \tag{D29}
\]

If \(a^2=a\), then \(\varepsilon(a)^2=\varepsilon(a)\), so its scalar on
the trivial block is either zero or one.  Retaining the extreme
\(H^{4,1}\) line forces \(\varepsilon(a)=1\), and hence retains all four
trivial \(H^{3,2}\) copies.  Rationality retains their complex conjugates.
Thus

\[
 \operatorname{rank}(aH^5(X))\ge10.                 \tag{D30}
\]

The Reynolds idempotent realizes equality in (D30).  Nothing in (D29)
controls correspondences outside the image of \(\mathbf Q[G]\) in the Chow
ring.

## 6. Exact scope

The derivation proves an algebraic projector and a graph-algebra optimum.
It does not derive coniveau, niveau, finite-dimensionality of the motive,
an abelian variety over \(K\), automorphy, a functional equation, a new
Euler half-plane, or a spectral generator.
