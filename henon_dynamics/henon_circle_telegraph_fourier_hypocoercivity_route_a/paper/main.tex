\begin{abstract}
We give a complete source-local theorem for the velocity-jump process on the
circle.  A speed $c\geq0$ transports a sign velocity and a rate
$\lambda\geq0$ flips it.  Every Fourier mode is a two-by-two block whose
centered square is scalar.  This yields an exact matrix exponential, the
telegraph equation, a sharp spectral-abscissa gap, all stationary boundaries,
and the essential-norm obstruction.  Critical Jordan blocks and both
degenerate faces are included.  The finite receipt is a regression witness,
not a proof by enumeration.  Route A is rejected under the declared scope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Owner and block reduction}
Let $\mathbb T_{2\pi}=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$ and let
$v\in\{+1,-1\}$.  On normalized
$L^2(\mathbb T_{2\pi}\times\{\pm1\})$ consider
\[
 Lf(x,v)=cv\,\partial_xf(x,v)+\lambda\bigl(f(x,-v)-f(x,v)\bigr).
\]
Here $L$ is the backward (observable) generator with periodic domain
$D(L)=H^1_{\rm per}(\mathbb T_{2\pi})\otimes\mathbb C^2$.  Densities evolve
under the adjoint $L^*$; its Fourier blocks are the conjugate-index blocks
$G_{-k}$, so the spectra and block norms below are unchanged.
The clock is physical elapsed time.  For the Fourier coefficient of mode
$k\in\mathbb Z$ in the ordered velocity basis,
\[
 G_k=\begin{pmatrix}-\lambda+ick&\lambda\\[2pt]
 \lambda&-\lambda-ick\end{pmatrix},\qquad N_k=G_k+\lambda I.
\]
Direct multiplication gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:square}
 N_k^2=(\lambda^2-c^2k^2)I.
\end{equation}

\section{Exact semigroup and critical blocks}
Put $\delta_k^2=\lambda^2-c^2k^2$.  Equation \eqref{eq:square} and the
power series for the exponential imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exp}
 e^{tG_k}=e^{-\lambda t}\left(\cosh(\delta_kt)I+
 \frac{\sinh(\delta_kt)}{\delta_k}N_k\right),
\end{equation}
where the quotient is $t$ when $\delta_k=0$.  Thus hyperbolic,
oscillatory, and critical regimes are one formula.  The characteristic
polynomial is
\[
 \det(rI-G_k)=(r+\lambda)^2-(\lambda^2-c^2k^2),
 \qquad \rho_{k,\pm}=-\lambda\pm\sqrt{\lambda^2-c^2k^2}.
\]
If $\lambda=c|k|>0$, the nonzero nilpotent $N_k$ gives one Jordan block at
$-\lambda$.  At $k=0$ the two eigenvalues are $0$ and $-2\lambda$.

\section{Telegraph equation and the gap atlas}
For $c>0$, writing the total density $\rho=f_++f_-$ and flux
$j=f_+-f_-$, the forward (adjoint) equations give
$\rho_t=-c j_x$ and $j_t=-c\rho_x-2\lambda j$.
Elimination yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:telegraph}
 \rho_{tt}+2\lambda\rho_t=c^2\rho_{xx}.
\end{equation}
For $c>0$ and $\lambda>0$, the sharp spectral-abscissa gap from the invariant
constant mode is
\[
 g(c,\lambda)=\begin{cases}
 \lambda,&\lambda\leq c,\\
 \lambda-\sqrt{\lambda^2-c^2},&\lambda>c.
 \end{cases}
\]
It is zero when $c=0$ or $\lambda=0$.  The word ``gap'' here means spectral
abscissa: it is not a constant-free $L^2$ operator-norm decay assertion.
Non-normal and critical blocks can have polynomial transient factors.

\section{Stationary and essential boundaries}
For $c>0,\lambda>0$, only the constant density is stationary.  If $c=0$,
positions are frozen and every common spatial profile is stationary, an
infinite-dimensional space.  If $\lambda=0$ and $c>0$, the stationary space
is exactly the two velocity-wise spatial constants.  At $c=\lambda=0$ all
functions are stationary.

For $c>0,\lambda>0$, high-frequency blocks satisfy (this is an essential
quotient statement, not a finite-mode estimate)
\[
 \lim_{|k|\to\infty}\|e^{tG_k}\|=e^{-\lambda t}
\]
in the essential quotient on the complement of constants.  Consequently the
essential norm is $e^{-\lambda t}>0$ and the semigroup is noncompact; hence it
belongs to no finite Schatten class.  On $c=0$ or $\lambda=0$ the essential
norm is one.  The latter face is the same-clock unitary pair of translations,
whereas $c=0$ is velocity-only mixing without spatial decay.
