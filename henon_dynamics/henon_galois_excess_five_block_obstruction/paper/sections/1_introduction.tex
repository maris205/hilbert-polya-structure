\section{Introduction}

Periodic-orbit weights in hyperbolic dynamics are often generated by
H\"older observables, and locally constant observables provide the finite
memory models on a symbolic presentation
\citep{Bowen1975,ParryPollicott1990}.  This elementary fact creates a sharp
consistency test: whenever cyclic block-incidence rows satisfy an integer
relation, the corresponding periodic sums of every potential of that memory
must satisfy the same relation.

The HCS-P54 project split the multiplier Mahler height on a certified mixing
area-preserving H\'enon survivor into a physical instability length and a
nonnegative Galois excess \(\Egal(\gamma)\) \citep{WangP54}.  HCS-P55 found
the first three-block violation, and HCS-P56 replaced isolated searches by
the primitive families
\begin{equation}\label{eq:families}
 A_m=0^{m-2}21,\qquad B_m=0^{m-3}231,
\end{equation}
which obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ladder-intro}
 \Nblk_m(A_m)+\Nblk_m(B_{m+2})
 =\Nblk_m(A_{m+1})+\Nblk_m(B_{m+1})
 \qquad(m\ge3).
\end{equation}
The width-four member excludes every memory-at-most-four realization
\citep{WangP55,WangP56}.

This paper resolves the next member.  Its algebraic input is not a black-box
orbit solve: reversibility converts the required six- and seven-cycles into
one-dimensional elimination problems.  The seven-cycle problem is
especially useful because the two physical words \(A_7\) and \(B_7\) occur
as different real embeddings of one irreducible trace field.

Our main conclusion is the following.

\begin{theorem}[Five-block obstruction]\label{thm:main}
On the frozen H6 survivor, no locally constant potential depending on at
most five consecutive symbolic states has primitive-periodic sums equal to
the Galois excess \(\Egal(\gamma)\) for every primitive orbit \(\gamma\).
For the four witnessing cycles,
\[
 \Delta_5:=\Egal(A_5)+\Egal(B_7)-\Egal(A_6)-\Egal(B_6)
 =139.7325728699720846\ldots>0.
\]
The sign is certified by exact polynomial root isolation and an integer
comparison, not by the displayed decimal.
\end{theorem}

The theorem is deliberately finite-memory.  A width-six unimodular minor
fits all cumulative witness values through this stage.  Finite periodic data
can also be interpolated by a general H\"older function on disjoint periodic
orbits.  Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is not an unrestricted H\"older no-go
and not a Hilbert--P\'olya construction.  Its value is to move the exact
regularity frontier and to expose a tractable reflection-chain asymptotic
problem.
