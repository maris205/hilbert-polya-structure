\section{Frozen survivor, trace, and excess}

We use the area-preserving map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H6}
 H_6(q,p)=(1-6q^2-p,q),
\end{equation}
restricted to the four-state mixing survivor certified in the preceding
projects.  A periodic orbit with first coordinates
\((q_0,\ldots,q_{n-1})\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
 q_{j+1}=1-6q_j^2-q_{j-1},
 \qquad j\pmod n.
\end{equation}
The chronological derivative product is assembled from
\begin{equation}\label{eq:derivative}
 D(q)=\begin{pmatrix}-12q&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \det D(q)=1.
\end{equation}
If \(T_\gamma\) denotes its trace, the two multiplier roots satisfy
\(z+z^{-1}=T_\gamma\).

For an irreducible trace polynomial with real embeddings
\(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_d\), all of absolute value greater than two in the
cases below, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:length}
 L(\theta)=\acosh\!\left(\frac{|\theta|}{2}\right).
\end{equation}
The multiplier Mahler height is \(\sum_iL(\theta_i)\).  For a selected
physical embedding \(\theta_{\rm phys}\), the Galois excess is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:excess}
 \Egal(\gamma)=\sum_iL(\theta_i)-L(\theta_{\rm phys})\ge0.
\end{equation}
This is the P54 convention.  We do not rescale it or fit any values.

For a word \(w\), let \(\Nblk_r(w)\) be its cyclic incidence vector of
length-\(r\) blocks.  Pairing a width-\(r\) potential with
\(\Nblk_r(w)\) gives its periodic sum.  The P56 insertion theorem gives
\eqref{eq:ladder-intro} for every \(m\ge3\); the present proof uses its
\(m=5\) instance.
