# C393 full generic binary tower and vanishing periodic density

## 1. Frozen source and critical values

Let f(X)=X²+1, c_0=0 and c_i=f^i(0). Let t be transcendental over Q.
The height-n tree T_n has root t and levels f^j(X)=t, 1<=j<=n,
with edges x -> f(x). It is a full rooted binary tree away from the branch
values. Write K_n for the splitting field of f^n(X)-t over Q(t), and G_n
for Aut(T_n). Its order is d_n=2^(2^n-1). This is a generic basepoint
theorem, not a theorem about every specialization t=a in Q.

The critical values 0,1,2,5,26,... are distinct: c_1>c_0 and
c_(i+1)=c_i²+1>c_i for i>=1. The derivative is
(f^n)'(X)=2^n product_(j=0)^(n-1) f^j(X).
Thus the finite branch values at height n are exactly c_1,...,c_n.
Over the algebraic closure of Q, the polynomial f^n(X)-t is irreducible:
the rational-function extension Qbar(X)/Qbar(f^n(X)) has degree 2^n.
Consequently its geometric monodromy acts transitively on all leaves.

## 2. New branch inertia forces the full tower

At t=c_n, the only critical point in the fibre of f^n is X=0.
Indeed if f^j(X)=0 with j>=1 then f^n(X)=c_(n-j), a different value.
The local expansion f^n(X)=c_n+A_n X²+O(X⁴) has
A_n=2^(n-1) product_(i=1)^(n-1)c_i !=0, by the chain rule.
Therefore local inertia is a single transposition of the two leaves meeting
at 0. They are siblings because their common image under f is 1.
The previous height has no branch above c_n, so this transposition lies in
the kernel of restriction to height n-1.

Inductively suppose the previous group is G_(n-1). Restriction of a splitting
field Galois group is onto the previous splitting field group. The geometric
group is contained in G_n and its last-level kernel is contained in the
elementary abelian group generated by swaps of the 2^(n-1) sibling pairs.
Conjugating the single new swap by elements transitive on those parents
supplies every sibling swap. These independent swaps generate the entire
kernel. The induction begins with f(X)-t and yields the full G_n.

The arithmetic group over Q(t) also embeds faithfully in G_n and contains
the full geometric group, so equality holds for both. In particular K_n has
constant field Q. Restrictions have kernel (C_2)^(2^(n-1)) and are onto;
the inverse limit is the full automorphism group of the infinite binary tree.
This is the classical Odoni/iterated-monodromy mechanism, rederived for this
frozen source rather than claimed as new general Galois theory.

## 3. All finite branch types and genus of the Galois closure

Above t=c_i, the critical points of f^n are precisely the roots of
f^(n-i)(X)=0. They are 2^(n-i) distinct simple roots, because an earlier
critical value cannot be 0. Each has ramification index 2, and the remaining
points in the degree-2^n fibre are unramified. Hence inertia on leaves has
cycle type 2^(2^(n-i)) 1^(2^n-2^(n-i+1)), and order 2.
At infinity the polynomial map has a single point of index 2^n; its inertia
is a 2^n-cycle. The Galois closure is tame in characteristic zero and has
no other branch values. Riemann–Hurwitz for that closure gives

    2g_n-2 = d_n [ -2 + n/2 + (1-2^-n) ],
    g_n = 1 + (d_n/2)(n/2-1-2^-n).

This is the genus of the smooth projective Galois-closure curve, not of
the original polynomial covering (whose source is P¹).
In particular g_1=g_2=0 and g_3=25. Finite exact checks do not establish
this all-n formula; the inertia proof does.

## 4. Complete cycle index and the root law

For a uniform g in G_n let Z_n(x_1,x_2,...) be its averaged cycle monomial.
Start Z_0=x_1. Writing G_n=(G_(n-1) x G_(n-1)) semidirect C_2 gives

    Z_n = [ Z_(n-1)(x)^2 + Z_(n-1)(x_2,x_4,...) ]/2.

Without swapping the two top subtrees the cycle partitions add. With a swap,
two applications act on one subtree by the product of two independent uniform
elements, itself uniform; every product-cycle length doubles. This proves
the entire distribution, including all lengths, multiplicities and repeated
Frobenius cycles. For a permutation with m_l l-cycles,
Fix(g^r)=sum_(l|r) l*m_l. Repetition changes the leaf permutation, not the
height or original f clock. Its finite native determinant is
det(I-u U_g)=product_l (1-u^l)^m_l; this is a permutation determinant only.

Let delta_n be the probability of at least one fixed leaf. The no-swap
case contributes one minus the probability that both halves have none;
the swap case has no fixed leaf. Thus

    delta_0=1, delta_n=delta_(n-1)-delta_(n-1)^2/2.

The positive sequence decreases to zero (its limit satisfies L=L-L²/2).
Moreover 1/delta_n-1/delta_(n-1)=1/[2(1-delta_(n-1)/2)] -> 1/2.
Cesaro summation yields n*delta_n ->2. The expected number of fixed leaves
is exactly one for every n, either by the recursion or by transitivity/Burnside.
This differs sharply from saying a typical element fixes a positive fraction
of the leaves. All fixed-leaf probabilities are exact rational numbers.

## 5. Fixed-level good reduction and finite-field dynamics

Set B_n=2 product_(0<=i<j<=n)(c_j-c_i), a nonzero integer.
For any prime p not dividing B_n, the first n+1 critical values remain
distinct modulo p and p is odd. The same simple-branch inertia proof over
Fbar_p(t) gives the full G_n. The arithmetic group over F_p(t) again contains
that group and embeds in it, so the extension is regular and full.
The argument is tame: all inertia orders divide powers of 2, not p.

The standard finite-field Chebotarev theorem for a regular finite cover,
obtained from the Weil bound on its twists, now applies. At each fixed n,
as p tends to infinity through these good primes, the proportion of
unramified a in F_p for which f^n(X)-a has a given cycle partition equals
the coefficient of that partition in Z_n, plus O_n(p^-1/2).
Factor degrees equal Frobenius cycle lengths. There are at most n excluded
finite branch values; treating them separately changes proportions by O_n(1/p).
Consequently

    |f^n(F_p)|/p = delta_n + O_n(p^-1/2).

This estimate is fixed-level, not uniform in n. The constants can grow with
the enormous Galois-closure genus. No p has infinitely many distinct critical
values in its finite residue field, so a uniform-all-height good-prime claim
would be false. The evidence includes such collision controls explicitly.

Every periodic point of f on F_p lies in f^n(F_p) for every n, since its
cycle supplies n backward steps. Therefore for every fixed n,
limsup_(p->infinity) |Per(f,F_p)|/p <=delta_n. Only after taking this limsup
do we let n tend to infinity. Since delta_n ->0, we obtain the unconditional
limit |Per(f,F_p)|/p ->0 over all primes. This double-limit argument claims
no rate in p and no permitted exchange of a p-dependent height cutoff.
The general wreath-product/periodic-density theorem is owned by
Juul–Kurlberg–Madhu–Tucker; here the full source hypotheses and deduction
are proved with the classical Chebotarev/Weil theorem explicitly imported.

## 6. Controls, clocks and scope

The simpler source f_0=X² has a postcritical collision already at height one;
its geometric monodromy is cyclic, not the full wreath group. The neighboring
source X²-1 has orbit 0,-1,0 and fails the distinct-value proof. Reduction
modulo bad primes supplies a third intrinsic control; composite moduli are
not fields and are never passed off as single Frobenius owners.

Tree height, f iteration and residue-field Frobenius iteration are three
different operations. Frobenius repetition is intrinsic arithmetic, but a
prime is not an assigned periodic orbit with period log p. Leaf permutations
are finite native unitary operators, not an infinite Hilbert–Pólya operator.
No assertion of generic Galois equality for every numerical basepoint is made.
