# Source, ownership, and collision audit

## Verified source records

1. J. J. Thomson, *A Treatise on the Motion of Vortex Rings*, Macmillan,
   London, 1883.  The original book record and digitized title page verify the
   author, title, publisher, date, and the classical polygon discussion.
2. T. H. Havelock, “The stability of motion of rectilinear vortices in ring
   formation,” *The London, Edinburgh, and Dublin Philosophical Magazine and
   Journal of Science* **11** (70), 617–633 (1931),
   DOI `10.1080/14786443109461714`.  Crossref publisher metadata verifies the
   complete record.  This is the direct linear ring-stability owner used for
   historical attribution.
3. H. E. Cabral and D. S. Schmidt, “Stability of Relative Equilibria in the
   Problem of N+1 Vortices,” *SIAM Journal on Mathematical Analysis* **31**
   (2), 231–250 (2000), DOI `10.1137/S0036141098302124`.  SIAM and Crossref
   verify the metadata.  It is cited for later stability context and, in
   particular, to prevent the package's linear heptagon calculation from being
   misreported as a nonlinear theorem.
4. M. Celli, E. A. Lacomba, and E. Pérez-Chavela, “On polygonal relative
   equilibria in the N-vortex problem,” *Journal of Mathematical Physics*
   **52** (10), article 103101 (2011), DOI `10.1063/1.3646115`.  AIP/Crossref
   metadata verify the record.  It supports the polygonal-relative-equilibrium
   lineage and the role of equal polygon circulations.

There are no generated or memory-only bibliography records.  Every DOI was
checked against publisher or Crossref metadata on 2 September 2026.

## Exact use boundary

- Thomson and Havelock establish classical ownership of the polygon question
  and its linear stability history.
- Cabral–Schmidt is later nonlinear-stability context.  No nonlinear theorem
  from that paper is imported into the C284 claim.
- Celli–Lacomba–Pérez-Chavela supports the relative-equilibrium/equal-vorticity
  lineage.
- No displayed Hessian formula, DFT identity, proof implication, executable
  count, or Route-A verdict is outsourced.  All are reconstructed in the
  package.

The manuscript explicitly says both “classical owner” and “no literature
priority claim.”  “Independent” refers to producer/checker implementation
independence, not historical discovery.
