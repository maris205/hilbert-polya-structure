\begin{abstract}
For simple random walk on the rank-$d$ free group, we give a single
finite-to-infinite theorem joining operator spectrum, exact returns, and
radial escape.  With $D=2d$, the Markov operator has purely absolutely
continuous spectrum $[-2\sqrt{D-1}/D,2\sqrt{D-1}/D]$ and root density
$\sqrt{4(D-1)-D^2x^2}/[2\pi(1-x^2)]$.  Weighted Dyck decomposition gives
closed formulas for every even return and first return, including total
return probability $1/(D-1)$.  A pathwise boundary-flip coupling then proves
speed $(D-2)/D$ and radial central-limit variance $4(D-1)/D^2$.  The
rank-one boundary becomes the recurrent walk on $\mathbb Z$, with arcsine
spectrum and half-normal radial scaling.  A 1,997-row exact certificate is a
regression receipt, not a substitute for the all-time proofs.  We claim
neither priority nor an arithmetic-target interpretation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Frozen walk and complete theorem}

Let $F_d=\langle a_1,\ldots,a_d\rangle$, $d\ge2$, and put $D=2d$.
Starting from the identity $o$, multiply on the right at each step by one
element of $\{a_i,a_i^{-1}:1\le i\le d\}$, uniformly.  Its Cayley graph is
the $D$-regular tree $T_D$.  On $\ell^2(T_D)$ the transition operator is
the bounded self-adjoint operator
\[
 (Pf)(v)=D^{-1}\sum_{w\sim v}f(w).
\]
Write $R_n=|X_n|$ for reduced-word length, and let $u_n=\Pp_o(X_n=o)$.
Set $\rho=2\sqrt{D-1}/D$ and $C_m=(m+1)^{-1}\binom{2m}{m}$.

\begin{theorem}[Spectral, return, and escape atlas]\label{thm:main}
For the frozen walk:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item $P$ is purely absolutely continuous and
$\spec(P)=[-\rho,\rho]$.  The spectral measure at $o$ has density
\begin{equation}
 k_D(x)=\frac{\sqrt{4(D-1)-D^2x^2}}{2\pi(1-x^2)}
 \1_{\{|x|<\rho\}}.                                      \label{eq:density}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for $z\notin[-\rho,\rho]$ and the branch
$\sqrt{z^2-\rho^2}\sim z$ at infinity,
\begin{equation}
 \langle\delta_o,(z-P)^{-1}\delta_o\rangle
 =\frac{D\sqrt{z^2-\rho^2}-(D-2)z}{2(z^2-1)}.             \label{eq:resolvent}
\end{equation}
\item $R_0=0$, $R_{n+1}=1$ from $R_n=0$, while for $r\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 \Pp(R_{n+1}=r+1\mid R_n=r)=p=\frac{D-1}{D},\qquad
 \Pp(R_{n+1}=r-1\mid R_n=r)=q=\frac1D.                   \label{eq:radial}
\end{equation}
All odd returns vanish and, for $n\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 u_{2n}=D^{-2n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}
 \frac{k}{2n-k}\binom{2n-k}{n}D^k(D-1)^{n-k}.             \label{eq:returns}
\end{equation}
\item If $\tau_o^+=\inf\{n\ge1:X_n=o\}$, then
\begin{equation}
 \Pp_o(\tau_o^+=2k)=\frac{C_{k-1}(D-1)^{k-1}}{D^{2k-1}},
 \qquad \Pp_o(\tau_o^+<\infty)=\frac1{D-1}.               \label{eq:firstreturn}
\end{equation}
\item With $v=(D-2)/D$ and $\sigma^2=4(D-1)/D^2$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{R_n}{n}\longrightarrow v\quad\text{a.s.},\qquad
 \frac{R_n-vn}{\sqrt n}\Longrightarrow N(0,\sigma^2).     \label{eq:escape}
\end{equation}
\item At the excluded rank-one boundary $d=1$, $F_1\cong\mathbb Z$:
$P$ is purely absolutely continuous on $[-1,1]$ with arcsine density,
$u_{2n}=4^{-n}\binom{2n}{n}$, return is recurrent,
$R_n/n\to0$ a.s., and $R_n/\sqrt n\Rightarrow |N(0,1)|$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section{Cavity resolvent and the root measure}

Delete the edge from a vertex to its parent and let $h(z)$ be the diagonal
resolvent of the resulting rooted forward tree.  Schur complementation at
the root of that tree and then at $o$ gives
\begin{equation}
 h=\frac1{z-(D-1)h/D^2},\qquad
 G_o(z)=\frac1{z-h/D}.                                    \label{eq:cavity}
\end{equation}
The solution satisfying $h(z)\sim z^{-1}$ is
\[
 h(z)=\frac{D^2}{2(D-1)}\bigl(z-\sqrt{z^2-\rho^2}\bigr).
\]
Substitution and rationalization prove \eqref{eq:resolvent}.  For
$|x|<\rho$, the upper
boundary value has
\[
 -\pi^{-1}\operatorname{Im}G_o(x+i0)
 =\frac{D\sqrt{\rho^2-x^2}}{2\pi(1-x^2)}=k_D(x).
\]
The apparent singularities at $z=\pm1$ in \eqref{eq:resolvent} are removable:
the numerator
vanishes there with the branch fixed above.  There are therefore no exterior
atoms.  Stieltjes inversion gives \eqref{eq:density} on the open band.
Its integral is one (substitute $x=\rho\cos\theta$ and evaluate the resulting
rational sine integral), while the square-root endpoints have no poles.
Thus no singular-continuous or endpoint mass remains.  The independent
asymptotic check $zG_o(z)\to1$ confirms the total normalization.  Hence
\eqref{eq:density} is the full probability measure of the cyclic root vector
in the radial subspace.

\section{Weighted Dyck paths and exact returns}

Distance from $o$ changes by one.  From a positive radius, precisely one
letter cancels the last reduced letter and $D-1$ extend it; at radius zero
all $D$ letters lead to radius one.  This proves \eqref{eq:radial}.  A return of length
$2n$ is thus a Dyck path with $n$ rises and $n$ falls.  Suppose it has
exactly $k$ irreducible excursions from zero.  Its $k$ rises from zero have
$D$ choices each; the remaining $n-k$ rises have $D-1$ choices, and every
fall is forced.

It remains to count the paths.  If $C(t)=1+tC(t)^2$ is the Catalan series,
the required number is $[t^{n-k}]C(t)^k$.  Lagrange inversion gives
\begin{equation}
 [t^{n-k}]C(t)^k=\frac{k}{2n-k}\binom{2n-k}{n}.            \label{eq:lagrange}
\end{equation}
Multiplying \eqref{eq:lagrange} by $D^k(D-1)^{n-k}$, summing over $k$, and
dividing by $D^{2n}$ proves \eqref{eq:returns}.  This also proves the renewal identity
$u_{2n}=\sum_{k=1}^n\Pp(\tau_o^+=2k)u_{2n-2k}$ directly at word-count level.

\section{Why the whole operator is purely absolutely continuous}

The root measure alone controls only radial functions, so we record the
complete orthogonal decomposition.  Let $S_m$ be the sphere of radius $m$
and let $\phi_m=|S_m|^{-1/2}\1_{S_m}$.  The radial subspace generated by
$\phi_0$ reduces $P$ and has Jacobi matrix
\begin{equation}
 J_0=J(0;a_0,a,a,\ldots),\qquad
 a_0=D^{-1/2},\quad a=\frac{\sqrt{D-1}}D.                 \label{eq:radialjacobi}
\end{equation}
It is cyclic at its first coordinate, so
\eqref{eq:density}--\eqref{eq:resolvent}, including the absence of
singular mass, show that $J_0$ is purely absolutely continuous on
$[-2a,2a]$.

For every vertex $v$, let ${\rm Ch}(v)$ be its children relative to $o$,
and choose an orthonormal basis of the zero-sum space
\[
 E_v=\{\eta\in\ell^2({\rm Ch}(v)):\sum_{w\in{\rm Ch}(v)}\eta(w)=0\}.
\]
Its dimension is $D-1$ when $v=o$ and $D-2$ otherwise.  Given
$\eta\in E_v$, let $e_j^{v,\eta}$, $j\ge0$, assign to every descendant
at depth $j$ below ${\rm Ch}(v)$ the value of its first child divided by
$(D-1)^{j/2}$, and vanish elsewhere.  These vectors are orthonormal, the
zero-sum condition cancels the edge back to $v$, and direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}
 Pe_0^{v,\eta}=ae_1^{v,\eta},\qquad
 Pe_j^{v,\eta}=a(e_{j-1}^{v,\eta}+e_{j+1}^{v,\eta})\quad(j\ge1).
                                                               \label{eq:freejacobi}
\end{equation}
For clarity, $e_0^{v,\eta}=\eta$ and the displayed normalization at depth
$j$ uses $(D-1)^{-j/2}$.

These half-line spaces and the radial space are mutually orthogonal.
They are complete: on every finite rooted truncation, recursively split the
values on each sibling set into its mean and zero-sum parts; induction on the
outermost occupied level exhausts every finitely supported vector.  Density
then exhausts $\ell^2(T_D)$.  Thus $P$ is an orthogonal direct sum of $J_0$
and copies of the free half-line Jacobi matrix $J(0;a,a,\ldots)$.  The latter
is carried by the sine transform to multiplication by $2a\cos\theta$ on
$L^2(0,\pi)$, hence is purely absolutely continuous with spectrum
$[-2a,2a]$.  This proves the full-space assertion in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(i),
without extrapolating from one spectral measure.

\section{First return and transience}

A first return at time $2k$ is one irreducible Dyck excursion.  Removing its
first rise and last fall leaves one of $C_{k-1}$ Dyck paths.  The first rise
has $D$ choices, the other $k-1$ rises have $D-1$ choices, and falls are
forced.  Hence the word count is $DC_{k-1}(D-1)^{k-1}$, proving the first
formula in \eqref{eq:firstreturn}.  With $p=(D-1)/D$ and $q=1/D$, the Catalan
generating function
yields
\[
 \sum_{k\ge1}C_{k-1}p^{k-1}q^k
 =\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4pq}}{2p}=\frac qp=\frac1{D-1},
\]
because $p>q$.  The missing mass is escape, not an uncounted finite return.

\section{Escape strong law and central limit theorem}

The boundary at zero requires an explicit coupling.  Let
$\xi_1,\xi_2,\ldots$ be i.i.d. with
$\Pp(\xi_i=1)=p$ and $\Pp(\xi_i=-1)=q$, and recursively set
\begin{equation}
 \widehat R_{n+1}=\widehat R_n+\xi_{n+1}
 +2\1_{\{\widehat R_n=0,\,\xi_{n+1}=-1\}},\qquad \widehat R_0=0.
                                                               \label{eq:coupling}
\end{equation}
This chain has exactly \eqref{eq:radial}, so it has the law of $R$.  From radius one the
probability of hitting zero is $q/p$: solve the bounded gambler's-ruin
recurrence $h_r=ph_{r+1}+qh_{r-1}$ with $h_0=1$ and $h_r\to0$.  After every
departure from zero, the strong Markov property therefore gives fixed escape
probability $1-q/p>0$.  The number of returns, and hence the number
\[
 B_\infty=\sum_{n\ge0}\1_{\{\widehat R_n=0,\,\xi_{n+1}=-1\}},
\]
is finite almost surely.  Iterating \eqref{eq:coupling} gives
\begin{equation}
 \widehat R_n=\sum_{i=1}^n\xi_i+2B_n,\qquad B_n\uparrow B_\infty<\infty.
                                                               \label{eq:correction}
\end{equation}
The i.i.d. strong law and central limit theorem now apply to the first term;
the finite correction divided by $n$ or $\sqrt n$ vanishes.  Since
$\E\xi_i=p-q=(D-2)/D$ and
$\operatorname{Var}\xi_i=4pq=4(D-1)/D^2$, \eqref{eq:escape} follows.
Equation \eqref{eq:correction} is
also why no conditioning-on-the-last-return gap is hidden in the CLT.

\section{Rank-one boundary, exact receipt, and scope}

When $d=1$ and $D=2$, the Cayley graph is $\mathbb Z$.  Fourier transform
takes $P$ to multiplication by $\cos\theta$, giving the arcsine density
$[\pi\sqrt{1-x^2}]^{-1}$ on $[-1,1]$.  Direct word counting gives
$u_{2n}=4^{-n}\binom{2n}{n}$; its divergence criterion or the symmetric
gambler's-ruin limit gives recurrence.  Here $R_n$ has the law of the
absolute value of symmetric walk, so the strong law and invariance principle
give $R_n/n\to0$ a.s. and $R_n/\sqrt n\Rightarrow|N(0,1)|$.  Thus
\eqref{eq:escape} is not
silently continued through the critical boundary.
