\section{Half-word transfer formulas}

Define the column vectors
\[
u=(1,0,0,1)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
v=(1,0,1,0)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
\ell=(1,1,0,0)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Here $u$ selects symbols fixed by $\rho$, while
$v_i=A_{i,\rho(i)}$ and $\ell_i=A_{\rho(i),i}$ encode the two possible
axis-edge conditions.

\begin{theorem}[Reflection fixed words]\label{thm:fixed}
For $n=2m+1$, every $R_k$ is conjugate under cyclic rotation and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-fixed}
|\Fix(R_k)|=u^{\mathsf T}A^m v=F_{m+2}.
\end{equation}
For $n=2m$, the even and odd values of $k$ form two conjugacy classes.  With
the physical labels fixed above, their cardinalities are
\begin{align}
G_m&:=|\Fix(R_k)|_{\EE}=u^{\mathsf T}A^m u=L_m,
\label{eq:edge-fixed}\\
H_m&:=|\Fix(R_k)|_{\VV}=\ell^{\mathsf T}A^{m-1}v \notag\\
&=F_m+\frac25L_m-\frac45\cos(\pi m/2)
       -\frac25\sin(\pi m/2).
\label{eq:vertex-fixed}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For odd $n$, multiplication by two is invertible modulo $n$, hence all
reflections are rotationally conjugate.  A fixed word consists of a free
half-path, one $\rho$-fixed central symbol and one closing axis edge.  Reading
that path gives $u^{\mathsf T}A^m v$.

For even $n$, the parity of $k$ is invariant under rotational conjugacy.
One class has two $\rho$-fixed boundary symbols and gives
$u^{\mathsf T}A^m u$; the other has two axis-edge constraints and gives
$\ell^{\mathsf T}A^{m-1}v$.  Diagonalizing $A$ using
\eqref{eq:charpoly}, or checking the induced fourth-order recurrence and four
initial values, yields the displayed Fibonacci--Lucas expressions.
\end{proof}

The trigonometric terms in \eqref{eq:vertex-fixed} are integral-periodic
corrections arising from the eigenvalues $\pm i$.  In particular, although
the formula contains fifths, its numerator is divisible by five for every
integer $m$.
