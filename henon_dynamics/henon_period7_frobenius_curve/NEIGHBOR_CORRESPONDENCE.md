# Exact period-seven neighbor correspondence

## Statement

Let

\[
K=\mathbb Q(\sigma),\qquad a=\sigma^2-2\sigma,
\]

and let \(P(\sigma,x)\) be the frozen monic septic.  On the generic root
field

\[
A=K[x]/(P(\sigma,x)),
\]

form the two polynomials in \(y\)

\[
P(\sigma,y),\qquad P(\sigma,a-y^2-x).
\]

The exact subresultant chain, reduced coefficientwise in \(A\), has degrees

\[
14,7,6,5,4,3,2,1,0,
\]

with the degree-one and degree-zero members equal to zero and the degree-two
member nonzero.  Hence the generic gcd has degree two.  If its last nonzero
subresultant is

\[
S_2(y)=c_2y^2+c_1y+c_0,
\]

then exact reduction gives

\[
c_1=c_2(x^2-a).
\]

Its two roots therefore obey the Hénon neighbor identity

\[
\boxed{y_1+y_2=a-x^2}.
\]

The quadratic discriminant and the value \(S_2(x)\) are both nonzero in
\(A\), so the generic relation has two distinct neighbors and no loop.

## From the correspondence to a seven-cycle

Let \(\Omega\) be the seven roots of \(P\) over \(\overline K\).  Declare
\(x\sim y\) when

\[
P(y)=P(a-y^2-x)=0.
\]

The gcd calculation gives two neighbors at every generic vertex.  The
relation is symmetric.  Indeed, if \(y\) is one neighbor of \(x\), the other
is

\[
z=a-x^2-y,
\]

and \(P(z)=0\).  Thus \(P(a-x^2-y)=0\), which is precisely the condition that
\(x\) is a neighbor of \(y\).

The graph is defined over \(K\), so geometric monodromy preserves its
connected components.  The septic is geometrically integral, hence geometric
monodromy is transitive on \(\Omega\).  Components therefore form equal-sized
blocks.  Since \(|\Omega|=7\) is prime and the generic graph is simple of
degree two, singleton components are impossible.  The graph is consequently
one seven-cycle.

This proves more than the original one-fibre audit: the adopted polynomial is
generically a Hénon period-seven coordinate carrier.  Its seven roots have sum
\(\sigma\) by the \(-\sigma x^6\) coefficient, and either orientation of the
cycle satisfies

\[
x_{i+1}=a-x_i^2-x_{i-1}.
\]

## Oriented edge cover

Let \(\widetilde C\) be the normalization of the closure of the ordered-edge
correspondence.  Generically it has degree \(14\) over the \(\sigma\)-line and
degree two over the marked-coordinate curve \(C\).  On an ordered state
\((x,y)=(x_i,x_{i-1})\), define

\[
\tau(x,y)=(a-x^2-y,x),
\qquad R(x,y)=(y,x).
\]

The neighbor identity proves that these maps preserve the correspondence.
On every generic fibre, \(\tau\) advances an oriented edge around the unique
seven-cycle, so

\[
\tau^7=1,
\qquad R^2=1,
\qquad R\tau R=\tau^{-1}.
\]

The involution \(J=R\tau\) fixes the marked coordinate and exchanges its two
neighbor choices.  Thus \(C\) is the generic quotient obtained by forgetting
orientation at the marked coordinate, while \(\widetilde C\) retains phase
and handedness.
