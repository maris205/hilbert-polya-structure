# C191 narrative report

C191 takes one large step: it replaces isolated matrix-balancing examples by
a boundary-complete dynamical atlas for every square nonnegative matrix.  The
same paper distinguishes existence of a limiting doubly stochastic matrix,
finite positive scalability, factor uniqueness, positive-cone contraction and
the exact local asymptotic rate.  These are different questions, and the
support/total-support/full-indecomposability trichotomy prevents them from
being conflated.

The strongest analytic statement lives on the positive stratum.  There the
full scaling-vector update is a strict Hilbert-projective contraction, while
its logarithmic derivative at the doubly stochastic limit is exactly
`S^T S`.  The gauge direction contributes eigenvalue one and the true
projective rate is `sigma_2(S)^2`.  This simultaneously explains geometric
convergence and its sharp nonuniform boundary.

The negative Route-A result is equally structural.  Every supported orbit
converges, so it cannot own a nonconstant primitive-periodic ledger.  Perfect
matchings and scaling cross-ratios contain no endogenous rational-prime
carrier or logarithmic clock.  Exact finite-dimensional operator formulas do
not change that arithmetic failure.  The candidate is therefore a useful
nonarithmetic control with a complete theorem package, not a partial
Hilbert--Polya proposal.
