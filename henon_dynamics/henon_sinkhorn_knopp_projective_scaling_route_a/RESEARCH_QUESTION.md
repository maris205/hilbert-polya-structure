# C191 research question

For an arbitrary square nonnegative matrix, how much of alternating
row/column normalization can be classified exactly at the level of support,
projective dynamics and local spectrum, and does any part survive the strict
Route-A arithmetic gate?

The frozen object is a matrix with no zero row or column, acted on by one full
clock step consisting of row normalization followed by column normalization.
The theorem must cover all three combinatorial strata:

1. support: at least one positive diagonal;
2. total support: every positive entry lies on a positive diagonal;
3. full indecomposability: no nontrivial rectangular zero block after row and
   column permutations.

The required large step is a single boundary-complete classification:

- convergence to a doubly stochastic limit exactly on support;
- finite positive diagonal scalability exactly on total support;
- uniqueness of the scaled matrix and the exact gauge boundary for factors;
- data-dependent Hilbert-metric geometric convergence for positive matrices;
- the exact full-cycle local log-Jacobian and rate at the limit;
- a proof that convergence leaves no nonconstant primitive-cycle owner.

The kill gates are equally explicit.  A finite census cannot prove the
all-matrix theorem; a fixed point cannot be relabelled as a primitive orbit;
and support matchings do not create rational primes, prime powers, a
logarithmic clock, a target divisor or a Hilbert--Polya operator.
