normalization by support, total support and full indecomposability.  The

\section{One all-matrix classification}
Let $A\ge0$ have no zero row or column.  Write $R(B)$ and $C(B)$ for row
and column normalization and freeze one clock step
$\mathcal S(B)=C(R(B))$.  A positive diagonal is a permutation $\pi$ with
$\prod_i a_{i\pi(i)}>0$.  The matrix has \emph{support} if one exists,
\emph{total support} if every positive entry belongs to one, and is
\emph{fully indecomposable} if no independent row and column permutations
expose a nontrivial rectangular zero corner.

Sinkhorn and Knopp~\cite{SK67} prove that $\mathcal S^k(A)$ converges to a
doubly stochastic limit exactly when $A$ has support.  The limit is reachable
as $D_1AD_2$ with finite positive diagonal factors exactly on total support.
The doubly stochastic representative is unique.  The
Brualdi--Parter--Schneider theorem~\cite{BPS66} identifies the sharper gauge
boundary: the factors are unique up to
$(D_1,D_2)\mapsto(cD_1,c^{-1}D_2)$ exactly on a fully indecomposable block.
If support is not total, positive entries outside every positive diagonal tend
to zero; no finite positive factors can produce that limit.

\section{Projective dynamics and exact local rate}
Assume $A>0$.  On positive column rays the full scaling-vector update is
\[
 T_A(x)=\left[A^T\bigl((Ax)^{-1}\bigr)\right]^{-1},
\]
with componentwise inverses.  For Hilbert distance $d_H$, set
\[
 \Theta(A)=\max_{i,j,k,l}\frac{a_{ik}a_{jl}}{a_{il}a_{jk}},\qquad
 \kappa(A)=\frac{\sqrt{\Theta(A)}-1}{\sqrt{\Theta(A)}+1}.
\]
Birkhoff contraction applied to $A$ and $A^T$ yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contraction}
 d_H(T_Ax,T_Ay)\le\kappa(A)^2d_H(x,y),
\end{equation}
the Franklin--Lorenz geometric mechanism~\cite{FL89}.  This coefficient is
data-dependent and approaches one as projective diameter diverges.

Let $S>0$ be the doubly stochastic limit.  In logarithmic column-scaling
coordinates $u$, the convention-locked map is
\[
 F(u)=-\log\!\left(S^T\exp[-\log(S\exp u)]\right).
\]
Differentiating at zero gives $DF(0)=S^TS$.  The gauge vector $\one$ has
eigenvalue one; on the quotient $\one^\perp$ the spectral radius is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rate}
 \rho_{\rm loc}=\sigma_2(S)^2<1,
\end{equation}
in agreement with the local-rate analysis of Knight~\cite{K08}.  Equation
\eqref{eq:rate} is local and asymptotic, not a global equality at every step.
