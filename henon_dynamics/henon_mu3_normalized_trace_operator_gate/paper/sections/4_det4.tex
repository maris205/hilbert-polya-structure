\section{The fourth-order determinant}

For a graded $X=X^+\oplus X^-$ in $L^4$ with $\|X\|<1$, define the two
trace-associated regularized determinants
\[
 \Det_{4,\tau_\pm}(I-X^\pm)
 =\exp\!\left(-\sum_{n\ge4}
       \frac{\tau_\pm((X^\pm)^n)}n\right)
\]
and their graded ratio
\[
 \Det_{4,\tau,\mathrm{gr}}(I-X)
 =\frac{\Det_{4,\tau_+}(I-X^+)}
        {\Det_{4,\tau_-}(I-X^-)}
 =\exp\!\left(-\sum_{n\ge4}\frac{\Str(X^n)}n\right).
\]
Here the global supertrace is applied only to trace-class powers.  H\"older's
inequality puts $X^4$ in $L^1$, and for $n\ge4$,
\[
 |\Str(X^n)|\le\tau(|X|^n)
 \le\|X\|^{n-4}\tau(|X|^4).
\]
For $X=X_s$ on a compact subset of $\RePart s>1/2$, both $\|X_s\|<1$ and
$\tau(|X_s|^4)$ are uniformly controlled.  Thus the defining series is
locally normally convergent and the determinant is holomorphic and nonzero.
This is the fourth-order trace-ideal regularization specialized to the graded
semifinite trace setting \cite{Simon2005}.

For $n=1,2,3$, put
\[
 \ell_n(s)=\sum_pc_{p,n}p^{-ns}.
\]
The exact first moment gives convergence of $\ell_1$ on $\RePart s>0$.
The uniform H\'enon moment bounds give convergence of $\ell_2$ on
$\RePart s>1/2$ and $\ell_3$ on $\RePart s>1/3$.  Hence all three are
holomorphic in the fourth-order half-plane.

These are sums of finite-dimensional \emph{local} supertraces.  When
$X_s^n\notin L^1$, the notation $\ell_n(s)$ is not an application of the
global semifinite trace to $X_s^n$; the cancellation has already occurred
inside each prime block.  This is precisely why the three counterterms must
remain explicit.

Expanding the canonical local logarithms and separating repetitions below
four proves the equality in \cref{thm:main}.  The counterterms are not fitted:
they are the exact first three local chronological-supertrace series.  Since
$L^3$ only holds for $\RePart s>2/3$, no regularized determinant of fixed
integer order at most three covers the entire half-plane by positive Schatten
membership.

Finally, all $c_{p,n}$ are rational.  Normal convergence therefore gives
$\mathcal G(\overline s)=\overline{\mathcal G(s)}$ on the proved half-plane.
The displayed counterterm exponential is holomorphic and nowhere zero there,
so it introduces no hidden divisor.
