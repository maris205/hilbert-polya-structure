\section{Three determinant notions}

On $\RePart s>2$, $X_s$ is $\tau$-trace class.  The
$\tau$-trace-associated analytic determinants in the two grades exist, and
their ratio has logarithm
\[
 -\sum_{n\ge1}\Str(X_s^n)/n=\log\mathcal G(s).
\]
This is the unregularized region for the normalized semifinite trace, not the
classical Fredholm region.  The latter starts at $\RePart s>3$ for the same
Hilbert blocks and gives the unnormalized Galois norm.

On $\RePart s>1/2$, the fourth-order regularized graded determinant is the
appropriate analytic object.  It retains complex phase through the
supertrace and requires the three disclosed counterterms.

Where a positive Fuglede--Kadison determinant, or its semifinite relative
version on $I+L^1$, is defined, it uses $\tau(\log|A|)$ and is positive
\cite{FugledeKadison1952,DeLaHarpe2013,HochsKaadSchemaitat2018}.  On the
ordinary domain, the ratio in the two grades can encode
$\RePart\log\mathcal G=\log|\mathcal G|$, but not the analytic phase.  On the
$L^4$ domain one would first need a fourth-order positive regularization.
Neither positive object replaces the analytic determinant in
\cref{thm:main}.  This distinction also prevents signed cancellation from
being misreported as positive trace-class summability.
