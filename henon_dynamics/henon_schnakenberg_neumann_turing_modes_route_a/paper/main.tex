\section{Frozen model and kinetic chamber}
Let $a,b,d_u,d_v,L>0$ and consider, on $0<x<L$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pde}
 \begin{split}
 u_t&=d_u u_{xx}+a-u+u^2v,\\
 v_t&=d_v v_{xx}+b-u^2v,
 \end{split}
 \qquad u_x=v_x=0\quad\text{at }x=0,L.
\end{equation}
Put $s=a+b$.  A positive spatially homogeneous equilibrium must satisfy
$u^2v=b$ and $a-u+b=0$.  Thus the unique positive spatially homogeneous
equilibrium is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eq}
 (u_*,v_*)=(s,b/s^2).
\end{equation}
The reaction Jacobian there is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
 J=\begin{pmatrix}(b-a)/s&s^2\\-2b/s&-s^2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \tau:=\operatorname{tr}J=\frac{b-a}{s}-s^2,
 \qquad \det J=s^2.
\end{equation}
Thus the kinetic equilibrium is asymptotically stable exactly when
$\tau<0$.  At $\tau=0$ its eigenvalues are $\pm \mathrm{i}s$; for
$\tau>0$ it has positive spectral abscissa.

Expand perturbations in the normalized Neumann cosine basis.  With
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mu}
 \mu_n=(n\pi/L)^2,\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots,
\end{equation}
the $n$th coefficient evolves under
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M}
 M(\mu)=J-\begin{pmatrix}d_u\mu&0\\0&d_v\mu\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Direct expansion gives
\begin{align}
 T(\mu)&=\operatorname{tr}M(\mu)=\tau-(d_u+d_v)\mu,\label{eq:T}\\
 D(\mu)&=\det M(\mu)=d_ud_v\mu^2-B\mu+s^2,
 &B&:=d_v\frac{b-a}{s}-d_us^2.\label{eq:D}
\end{align}
Consequently the complete modal pair is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambdas}
 \lambda_{n,\pm}=\frac{T(\mu_n)\pm
 \sqrt{T(\mu_n)^2-4D(\mu_n)}}{2}.
\end{equation}
The phrase \emph{kinetic and modal dispersion closure} marks the baseline
manuscript: no continuum-wavenumber test has yet been promoted to a finite
interval claim.

\section{Continuous window and exact finite-domain selection}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q}
 Q=B^2-4d_ud_vs^2.
\end{equation}
\begin{theorem}[Complete linear Turing criterion]\label{thm:turing}
Assume $\tau<0$.  A nonempty open interval of unstable positive squared
wavenumbers exists if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:windowcond}
 B>0,\qquad Q>0.
\end{equation}
When these inequalities hold, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:roots}
 \mu_\pm=\frac{B\pm\sqrt Q}{2d_ud_v}.
\end{equation}
Then $0<\mu_-<\mu_+$ and the finite Neumann interval has a genuine linear
Turing instability if and only if some $n\geq1$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strictmode}
 \mu_-<\mu_n<\mu_+.
\end{equation}
Writing $r_\pm=(L/\pi)\sqrt{\mu_\pm}$, the exact number of unstable modes is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
 N_{\rm unst}=\max\{0,\lceil r_+\rceil-\lfloor r_-\rfloor-1\}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $n\geq1$, its precise domain-length chamber is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:length}
 \frac{n\pi}{\sqrt{\mu_+}}<L<\frac{n\pi}{\sqrt{\mu_-}}.
\end{equation}
Equality at either wall is neutral rather than unstable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $\tau<0$, equation~\eqref{eq:T} is negative for every $\mu\geq0$.
A real two-by-two matrix with negative trace has positive spectral abscissa
exactly when its determinant is negative.  Indeed, a negative determinant
gives two real eigenvalues of opposite signs, whereas a positive determinant
and negative trace put both eigenvalues in the open left half-plane.

The polynomial $D(\mu)$ has positive leading and constant coefficients.  It
is negative somewhere on the positive axis precisely when its vertex is
positive and its minimum is negative, which is exactly~\eqref{eq:windowcond}.
The two roots are~\eqref{eq:roots}; their sum and product are positive, so
both are positive and $D<0$ precisely between them.  This proves the
continuous criterion and~\eqref{eq:strictmode} after restricting to the
actual Neumann spectrum.

Taking positive square roots of~\eqref{eq:strictmode} gives
$r_-<n<r_+$.  The number of positive integers in this strict interval is
exactly~\eqref{eq:count}; the ceiling and floor conventions delete integer
endpoints.  Solving the same strict inequalities for $L$ yields
\eqref{eq:length}.
\end{proof}

At a window endpoint $D=0$ and $T<0$, so the two modal eigenvalues are
$0$ and $T$: the zero eigenvalue is simple.  The phrase
\emph{strict interval mode-count closure} marks the first substantive
revision: continuous dispersion, discrete mode selection, multiplicity, and
length walls now form one if-and-only-if theorem.

\section{Operator closure and all frozen boundaries}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
 \mathcal A=\operatorname{diag}(d_u\partial_{xx},d_v\partial_{xx})+J,
 \qquad \mathcal D(\mathcal A)=H_N^2(0,L)^2
\end{equation}
on $L^2((0,L);\R^2)$.  The diagonal Neumann diffusion operator has compact
resolvent; the constant matrix $J$ is bounded.  Hence $\mathcal A$ also has
compact resolvent.  After complexification, the cosine basis gives an
orthogonal direct sum of the blocks $M(\mu_n)$, so~\eqref{eq:lambdas}
exhausts its spectrum with algebraic multiplicity.  Since
$\mu_n\to\infty$, no further spectral component is hidden beyond the modal
list.

The boundary atlas is exact.
\begin{itemize}
\item The homogeneous mode $n=0$ is precisely $J$, so a Turing claim always
requires kinetic stability and a distinct unstable mode $n\geq1$.
\item If $d_u=d_v=d$, then $B=d\tau<0$ in the kinetic chamber; equal
diffusion cannot create a Turing window.
\item If $B>0$ and $Q=0$, then $D$ has a double zero as a polynomial in
$\mu$.  It never becomes negative.  A discrete mode exactly at the contact
has a simple zero temporal eigenvalue and a negative companion; there is no
open unstable window.
\item If $Q>0$, an equality $\mu_n=\mu_-$ or $\mu_n=\mu_+$ is a neutral
tie.  Strict inequalities are essential in both~\eqref{eq:count} and
\eqref{eq:length}.
\item The faces $d_u=0$ or $d_v=0$ are excluded.  They are not obtained by
dividing~\eqref{eq:roots} by zero, and no degenerate-parabolic extension is
claimed.
\end{itemize}

Exact rational receipts contain nine chambers, 63 modal rows, and 20
length-wall polynomials.  Separate modes land exactly on the lower and upper
window endpoints, and each open-window chamber records the strict
floor/ceiling count.  The receipts verify equilibrium, Jacobian, determinant,
state classification, both endpoints, and double walls without floating
