# Narrative report

The central distinction is between a dispersion curve and an actual bounded
domain.  The Schnakenberg equilibrium may admit a continuous band of unstable
wavenumbers while a short Neumann interval contains no cosine mode inside
that band.  The paper first reduces kinetic stability to one exact trace
inequality.  In that chamber every spatial modal trace stays negative, so the
sign of one upward determinant quadratic completely decides instability.

This produces two nested gates.  `B>0` and `Q>0` create the continuous open
window.  A positive integer inside the rescaled window creates a genuine
finite-domain Turing mode.  Strict floor/ceiling bookkeeping then gives the
unstable dimension and converts every endpoint into an explicit domain-length
wall.  Equal diffusion and the determinant double wall fail for different
algebraic reasons and are kept separate.

The finite receipt realizes the lower and upper endpoint ties separately and
records the exact floor/ceiling count for every open-window chamber.  The
operator is real, while its complete modal spectrum is stated after the
canonical complexification needed to include conjugate temporal eigenvalues.

The contribution is a proof-complete source-local atlas, not a numerical
pattern paper.  It makes no nonlinear branch or global nonlinear PDE claim.
Its arithmetic Route-A coordinates all fail, so the dynamical theorem remains
inside its reaction--diffusion owner.
