\section{What the physical stable tail controls}

The negative fixed point of \eqref{eq:henon} is
\begin{equation}
q_-=-(1+\sqrt7)/6.
\end{equation}
The derivative there has trace \(2+2\sqrt7\).  Its expanding and contracting
eigenvalues are reciprocal, and the contracting eigenvalue is
\begin{equation}
\lambda_s=
\left(1+\sqrt7+\sqrt{7+2\sqrt7}\right)^{-1}
=0.1398273783\ldots.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
0<\lambda_s<\frac{2}{\sqrt{17}}<1.
\label{eq:contraction}
\end{equation}
The signed inverse branches used by the reflection chains therefore localize
the selected physical coordinates exponentially near \(q_-\).

Equation~\eqref{eq:contraction} is useful but has a strict scope.  It follows
one real branch and controls the local correction to its physical trace.  By
contrast, \eqref{eq:excess} requires every nonphysical root of a trace
polynomial whose degree grows with the closure.  A local stable eigenvalue
does not bound either the number of those roots or their instability lengths.
The degree split $12+6$ at period eight and the degree-$28$ merger at period
nine make this missing input visible before taking any limit.
