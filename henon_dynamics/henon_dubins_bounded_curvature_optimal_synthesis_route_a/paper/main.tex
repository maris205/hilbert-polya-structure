\section{Six-word global synthesis}
At unit speed and turning radius $R>0$, the car satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:car}
 \dot x=\cos\vartheta,\qquad \dot y=\sin\vartheta,
 \qquad |\dot\vartheta|\le R^{-1},
\end{equation}
and moves only forward.  Rigid motion fixes the initial pose at $(0,0,0)$.
Divide the target position by $R$ and write
\[
 d=R^{-1}\sqrt{x^2+y^2},\quad \theta=\operatorname{atan2}(y,x),
 \quad \alpha=\M(-\theta),\quad \beta=\M(\phi-\theta).
\]
Here $\M z$ is the representative in $[0,2\pi)$, with $\M(2\pi)=0$.
Put $s_a=\sin\alpha$, $c_a=\cos\alpha$ and similarly for $b$, and
$c_{ab}=\cos(\alpha-\beta)$.  The following table is an explicit algorithm.
Every square root requires its radicand $P\ge0$.
\begin{center}\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.07\linewidth}p{0.83\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
word & normalized segments $(t,p,q)$ and feasibility \\
\midrule
$LSL$ & $P=2+d^2-2c_{ab}+2d(s_a-s_b)$,
$\psi=\operatorname{atan2}(c_b-c_a,d+s_a-s_b)$;
$(\M(-\alpha+\psi),\sqrt P,\M(\beta-\psi))$.\\
$RSR$ & $P=2+d^2-2c_{ab}+2d(s_b-s_a)$,
$\psi=\operatorname{atan2}(c_a-c_b,d-s_a+s_b)$;
$(\M(\alpha-\psi),\sqrt P,\M(-\beta+\psi))$.\\
$LSR$ & $P=-2+d^2+2c_{ab}+2d(s_a+s_b)$,
$\psi=\operatorname{atan2}(-c_a-c_b,d+s_a+s_b)-\operatorname{atan2}(-2,\sqrt P)$;
$(\M(-\alpha+\psi),\sqrt P,\M(-\beta+\psi))$.\\
$RSL$ & $P=-2+d^2+2c_{ab}-2d(s_a+s_b)$,
$\psi=\operatorname{atan2}(c_a+c_b,d-s_a-s_b)-\operatorname{atan2}(2,\sqrt P)$;
$(\M(\alpha-\psi),\sqrt P,\M(\beta-\psi))$.\\
$RLR$ & $h=(6-d^2+2c_{ab}+2d(s_a-s_b))/8$, $|h|\le1$;
$p=\M(2\pi-\arccos h)$,
$t=\M(\alpha-\operatorname{atan2}(c_a-c_b,d-s_a+s_b)+p/2)$,
$q=\M(\alpha-\beta-t+p)$.\\
$LRL$ & $h=(6-d^2+2c_{ab}+2d(-s_a+s_b))/8$, $|h|\le1$;
$p=\M(2\pi-\arccos h)$,
$t=\M(-\alpha-\operatorname{atan2}(c_a-c_b,d+s_a-s_b)+p/2)$,
$q=\M(\beta-\alpha-t+p)$.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{theorem}[Complete Dubins minimizer]\label{thm:dubins}
Compute every feasible row above, allowing zero segments.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:min}
 D_R((0,0,0),(x,y,\phi))
   =R\min_{w\in\{LSL,RSR,LSR,RSL,RLR,LRL\}}(t_w+p_w+q_w).
\end{equation}
Every minimizing row is a global shortest curve, and every global shortest
curve has one of these descriptions, up to zero-piece degeneracy.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Pontryagin switching function makes a nonsingular extremal a concatenation
of maximum-curvature left/right arcs and straight segments.  The classical
Dubins shortening argument removes repeated equal turns, excludes four or
more essential pieces, and leaves precisely $CSC$ or $CCC$, hence the six
rows.  External and internal circle tangencies give the four radicands;
the law of cosines for the three unit circles gives the two values $h$.
Solving the remaining oriented angles yields the table.  Thus evaluating all
feasible rows proves~\eqref{eq:min}; taking all equal minima retains every
global tie.
\end{proof}

\section{Boundaries, ties, and symmetries}
For a pose $(X,Y,H)$, a normalized primitive of length $a$ is
\begin{align*}
 L_a &: (X+\sin(H+a)-\sin H,\;Y-\cos(H+a)+\cos H,\;H+a),\\
 R_a &: (X+\sin H-\sin(H-a),\;Y+\cos(H-a)-\cos H,\;H-a),\\
 S_a &: (X+a\cos H,\;Y+a\sin H,\;H).
\end{align*}
Substitution of each table row gives $(x/R,y/R,\phi)$ modulo $2\pi$; this is
a direct endpoint proof independent of comparing lengths.  It also shows
that $R(t+p+q)\ge\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$.

At $P=0$ the straight tangent collapses.  At $h=1$ the middle $CCC$ arc may
collapse; at $h=-1$ it is a half turn.  In these cells multiple word labels
can describe the same geometric curve, and~\eqref{eq:min} deliberately keeps
all of them.  For deterministic boundary coordinates we set
$\operatorname{atan2}(0,0)=0$; changing that representative only redistributes
zero or full-turn pieces and cannot lower the geometric minimum.  Coincident
poses, a pure heading change, heading wrap, and a completely zero path are
therefore covered without an informal exception.

For every $s>0$, simultaneous replacement
$(x,y,R)\mapsto(sx,sy,sR)$ multiplies the answer by $s$.  Rigid motions of
both poses preserve it.  Reflection $(x,y,\phi)\mapsto(x,-y,-\phi)$ exchanges
$L\leftrightarrow R$, pairing $LSL/RSR$, $LSR/RSL$, and $LRL/RLR$.
