## 3. Arithmetic helper (E): adversarial checks

The bound is
$$B_H=\prod_{p\leq4H}(p-1),\qquad
L_H=H B_H(2H)^{2H},\qquad H\in\mathbb Z_{\geq1}.$$
The actual proof establishes it under the stronger mean-square hypothesis
$A(\alpha)\leq H$. The following risks were checked.

1. **Integral relative decompositions.** For $p^r\Vert N$, $r\geq2$,
   the degree-$p$ basis has integral coefficients because the integer
   rings are generated by the relevant roots. Relative averaging removes
   cross terms. The $p=2,r=2$ case still has degree two; subsequently
   replacing an index $2m$ by $m$ preserves the child field. In the
   $p\Vert N$ case the prior normalization eliminates the redundant
   factor two, and averaging over the $p-1$ nontrivial powers gives
   the stated pair-difference identity.
2. **Ambient index versus minimal conductor.** The induction is on a
   specified containing-field index, not on a falsely inferred minimal
   conductor of every coefficient. Every child has a strictly smaller
   tag, including unary root twists.
3. **Small-prime induction.** The inequality
   $\ell(\beta)\leq B(N)A(\beta)$ holds for arbitrary prime powers.
   A squarefree prime costs at most its factor $p-1$; a higher prime
   power costs no new factor. Rational integers, zero, negative
   coefficients and repeated roots are handled correctly.
4. **Large-prime branching.** If the largest equal-coefficient class
   has size $r$, the number of unequal pairs is at least
   $p(p-r)/2=pX/2$. Every nonzero integral difference has mean square
   at least one, giving $X\leq2H$. The individual-child estimate is
   valid because $p-X>2H\geq2A(\beta)$. Every genuine branch drops
   mean square by at least $1/2$; unary steps preserve it.
5. **Uniform bound despite unary paths.** Each individual tree is
   finite by induction on its initial ambient index. Suppressing unary
   nodes leaves at most $2(H-1)$ genuine branches along a path, so the
   looser leaf bound $(2H)^{2H}$ is valid. Terminal fields have only
   small primes. Substitution back up the tree multiplies summands
   only by roots of unity, not by uncontrolled algebraic coefficients.

No mathematical correction is required. The initial global choice
$H=(|c|+3)^2$ and the revised sharper $H=2|c|+4$ both dominate the
square of the inherited house bound. The revised choice is used consistently.
This is a coarse computable bound, not a practical algorithm or a separately
credited new length theorem.

## 4. Torsion helper (T): adversarial checks

The following details establish an exact algorithm rather than a qualitative
torsion-coset existence assertion.

- The elementary rational Mann proof handles indexed repeated roots,
  signed nonzero rational weights and $p=2$. Its degree argument forces
  the normalized relative conductor to be squarefree; a missing residue
  class for $p>b$ then contradicts minimality. Only ratios, not absolute
  torsion orders, are bounded.
- Cyclotomic coefficients are expanded into rational weights times fixed
  roots first. The term count is the expanded count. In particular
  $x-\zeta_\ell=0$ retains the prescribed possibly large conductor
  instead of being falsely reduced to $x=\pm1$.
- Finite partitions and bounded ratios include each actual minimal
  vanishing partition. Retaining nonminimal patterns is harmless because
  the exact weighted zero test separately proves soundness of every
  output binomial coset. Zero and one-term polynomials are handled first.
- Smith normal form uses the correct convention $h_Ah_B=h_{AB}$.
  Every root of every diagonal power equation is retained. After
  splitting, character lattices are saturated. Rank zero, inconsistent
  constants and the zero-dimensional torus fit the same formulas.
  Enlarged cyclotomic fields for translations are explicitly permitted.
- Intersections of torsion cosets split into torsion cosets or are empty.
  Hence intersections of the computed single-equation torsion closures
  give the common torsion closure. This is not an unrestricted assertion
  that arbitrary Zariski closures commute with intersection.
- Irreducibility reduces containment in a finite union to containment
  in one component. Substitution into character equations gives exact
  equality tests, including point components and empty lists.
- Rectangular Smith normal form proves closed coordinate images and
  gives a torsion lift for **every** torsion point in an image.
  Taking roots of torsion constants handles disconnected projection
  kernels; connectedness of those kernels is not falsely assumed.

No mathematical correction is required. The inaccessible original Mann
proof is not an unchecked dependency because the exact needed version
is completely proved here. Its source-access limitation must nevertheless
remain disclosed. The output is geometric, not a multiplicity computation.

## 6. Non-author finding: one torsion closure suffices

This reduction was derived during the independent review and sent to the
global author before revision. It is recorded for provenance and contribution
assessment, not as another candidate theorem.

Let
$$R=\{(u,v)\in T^2:F_c(\phi(u))=\phi(v)\},\qquad
\Gamma=\operatorname{TC}(R).$$
For any finite union $V$ of connected torsion cosets, put
$D=\Gamma\cap(V\times V)$. The proved intersection kernel makes $D$ a
finite union of torsion cosets. It is contained in $R\cap(V\times V)$,
and $\Gamma_{\rm tor}=R_{\rm tor}$ gives
$$D_{\rm tor}=(R\cap(V\times V))_{\rm tor}.$$
Since torsion is dense in every component of $D$,
$$\operatorname{TC}(R\cap(V\times V))=\Gamma\cap(V\times V).$$
Thus the original descent is exactly
$$V_0=T,\qquad
V_{j+1}=\pi_1\bigl(\Gamma\cap(V_j\times V_j)\bigr).$$

Only the initial $\Gamma$ needs general torsion-closure computation.
Later steps are finite torsion-coset intersections, projections and equality
tests. Repeated torsion-closure computation is therefore not an independent
new mechanism. The revised global equation (4.5) and algorithm correctly
incorporate this equivalent formulation.
