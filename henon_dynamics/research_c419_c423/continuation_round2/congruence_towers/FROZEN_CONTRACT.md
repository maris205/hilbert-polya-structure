# CT1: complete ordinary period distributions on congruence quotients

2026-09-07. AI-generated candidate; NOT PROVED, NOT ADMITTED.

## Object and whole question

Fix a prime p, dimension d >= 1 and a polynomial automorphism F over
Z_p, with polynomial inverse over Z_p. For every k >= 1, its reduction
F_k permutes (Z/p^k Z)^d. The native clock is one application of F_k;
cycles and least periods are ordinary, without an averaging or
Frobenius reinterpretation.

Write each ordinary cycle length uniquely as m p^r with p not dividing m.
Let c(k,m,r) count cycles of that least period. The complete question is
whether only finitely many m occur for a fixed F and whether, for each,
the exact series

$$
C_{F,m}(u,v)=\sum_{k\ge1}\sum_{r\ge0}c(k,m,r)u^k v^r
$$

is rational, with an effective finite construction from return maps,
their displacement ideals and finite residue data, including singular
fixed loci. The variables record height and the p-power exponent of
the true least period; v is not silently substituted for physical time.

The intrinsic arithmetic carrier is the entire congruence tower. This
is different from the actual periodic locus of F on Z_p^d, but that
distinction alone does not prove novelty or sufficient substance.

## First decisive subproblem

For a polynomial return map T that is coefficientwise congruent to
identity modulo p (modulo 4 when p=2), test the proposed valuation identity

$$
v_p(T^{p^r}(x)-x)=v_p(T(x)-x)+r
$$

for nonfixed x, using minimum coordinate valuation. Determine exactly
which coefficient, derivative and ball-normalization hypotheses are
needed. Then check whether a finite return-map decomposition really
reduces every F above to that situation without losing minimal periods.

Before computing any large quotient, search the p-adic dynamics and
Poincare-series literature for this exact full-tower statement.
Poonen-style interpolation, Denef rationality of displacement-ideal
congruences, cycle Mobius inversion and the local C414 interpolation
result are classical/owned inputs to deduct, not proposed discoveries.

## Admission and replacement boundaries

Success requires a complete all-level theorem and effective assembly
including p=2, singular displacement ideals, nontrivial residue cycles
and the prime-to-p part of least periods, plus a substantial residual
after primary-source comparison. A near-identity lemma alone is too thin.

Stop or replace if the whole claim is already a named theorem or a short
direct corollary of existing interpolation and rationality, if the return
normalization loses periods, or if the general statement needs an
unproved uniformity assertion. Finite quotient tables do not repair
such a gap. No theorem status, evaluation or manuscript is assigned.
