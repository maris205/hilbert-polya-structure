# Proof Package: a fixed-cusp-coordinate counterexample to AS2

## Claim

The frozen [original conjecture](../AS2_CONJECTURE.md) proposes that the
full width-one cusp scattering family for $\Gamma_0(N)$, with trivial
nebentypus, is pairwise commuting if and only if every primitive character
$\chi$ of conductor $q$ with $q^2\mid N$ has real square $\chi^2$.

The corrected, bounded claim proved here is:

**Counterexample.** The original character condition holds at $N=50$, but
$$
[\Phi_{50}(2),\Phi_{50}(3)]\ne0.
$$
This is not a replacement all-level classification. The auxiliary lemma
below also checks exactly what the proposed primitive-block argument
proves at its minimal level $q^2$.

## Status

**NOT CURRENTLY JUSTIFIED** for the original conjecture; more precisely,
its sufficiency is **FALSE** by the counterexample proved below.

The counterexample and the explicitly bounded auxiliary lemma are
**PROVABLE AS STATED**. No all-level scattering criterion is asserted.

## Assumptions

- Weight zero, trivial nebentypus, the full collection of cusps, and
  width-one scaling matrices, exactly as in the
  [frozen AS2 contract](../FROZEN_CONTRACTS.md).
- Young's character-series definitions and normalization are used
  literally. In that notation trivial nebentypus means the pair
  $(\chi_1,\chi_2)=(\chi,\chi)$, not $(\chi,\bar\chi)$.
- $q=5$, $N=50$, and $\chi$ is the primitive character with $\chi(2)=i$.
  Its conjugate is $\bar\chi$, and $\eta=\chi^2=\bar\chi^2$ is the
  nonprincipal even quadratic character modulo $5$.
- All meromorphic functional equations are interpreted after analytic
  continuation. Factors such as $\Gamma(1-s)L(2-2s,\eta)$ are not
  separately evaluated at their apparent zero/pole points.

## Notation

Write $E_{f,u}(z,s)=E_{1/(uf)}(z,s)$, with $f\mid N$ and $u$ a unit
modulo $g_f=(f,N/f)$, represented coprime to $N$.
Write $E_\chi(z,s)=E_{\chi,\chi}(z,s)$ in Young's notation, and
$$
D_{\chi,f}(z,s)=\sum_{u\in(\mathbb Z/g_f\mathbb Z)^\times}
 \chi(-u)E_{f,u}(z,s),\qquad q\mid g_f.
$$
Let $D_\chi=(D_{\chi,5},D_{\chi,10})^{\mathsf T}$ and
$\mathcal E_\chi=(E_\chi(z,s),E_\chi(2z,s))^{\mathsf T}$.
The analogous symbols with bars use $\bar\chi$.
Put
$$
A_\chi(s)=\frac{(5/\pi)^s\Gamma(s)L(2s,\eta)}{\tau(\chi)},
\qquad \kappa=\frac{\tau(\chi)}{\tau(\bar\chi)}\ne0.
$$
Here $\tau$ denotes the primitive Gauss sum; it is nonzero.

## Proof Strategy

Construct a four-dimensional invariant block using a fixed Fourier
transform on cusp residue classes, apply the actual character-series
functional equation, and then use one fixed Walsh transform. Remove
only a common nonzero scalar and one constant Gauss-sum similarity.
A two-dimensional restriction then has an exact nonzero commutator.

2. The coefficient matrix $C_\chi(s)$: Young Theorem 7.1,

3. The functional equation: completion immediately before Young

5. The last commutator calculation is rational arithmetic in

   for the invariant-subspace or normalization arguments.

## Proof

### Step 1. The proposed arithmetic condition holds

The only possible primitive conductors with $q^2\mid50$ are $q=1,5$.
The unit group modulo $5$ is generated by $2$ and has order $4$.
Every character modulo $5$ therefore has order dividing $4$, so its
square takes values in $\{0,1,-1\}$. The conductor-$1$ character
also has real square. Thus the right-hand side of the original
conjecture is true. Its proposed valuation inequalities also hold:
$v_2(50)=1$, $v_5(50)=2$, and all other valuations vanish.

### Step 2. The cusp-character coordinates are genuinely fixed

At each denominator $f$, the characters of
$(\mathbb Z/g_f\mathbb Z)^\times$ form the finite Fourier basis.
Each is induced by a unique primitive character. Thus assembling the
rows $\chi(-u)$ for all denominators and all their characters gives
one invertible matrix $T$, independent of $s$, on the entire cusp
space. It has no spectral powers in its entries.

For $N=50$, the possible denominators are $1,2,5,10,25,50$;
their $g_f$ values are $1,1,5,5,1,1$. Hence there are $12$ cusps,
and the primitive conductor-$5$ character occurs only at $f=5,10$.
The four rows for $(\chi,5),(\chi,10),(\bar\chi,5),(\bar\chi,10)$
are distinct Fourier rows. They are not a truncation of a larger
same-character sector.

### Step 3. Reconstruct the actual oldform coefficient matrix

Set $L=N/q^2=2$. Young (7.1) has oldform label $B\mid L$,
$A=L/B$, summation variables $d\mid A$, $e\mid B$, and $(d,e)=1$.
Here its cusp denominator and common prefactor are
$$
f=5Bd/e,\qquad
\left(\frac{50}{(5Bd/e,5Ae/d)}\right)^s=10^s.
$$
For $B=1$ the pairs are $(d,e)=(1,1),(2,1)$; for $B=2$
they are $(1,1),(1,2)$. Since $\chi(2)=i$, the resulting identity is
$$
\mathcal E_\chi(s)=C_\chi(s)D_\chi(s),\qquad
C_\chi(s)=10^s
\begin{pmatrix}1&i2^{-s}\\i2^{-s}&1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1}
$$
For the conjugate character replace $i$ by $-i$; do not conjugate
the complex parameter $s$. In particular, these matrices themselves
depend on $s$ and cannot be used as a fixed diagonalizing basis.

### Step 4. Transfer the functional equation into the fixed basis

Young (4.6) gives, for each oldform label $B$ separately,
$$
A_\chi(s)E_\chi(Bz,s)=A_{\bar\chi}(1-s)E_{\bar\chi}(Bz,1-s).
$$
Substituting (1) gives
$$
D_\chi(s)=\frac{A_{\bar\chi}(1-s)}{A_\chi(s)}
C_\chi(s)^{-1}C_{\bar\chi}(1-s)D_{\bar\chi}(1-s).
\tag{2}
$$
Both scalar factors have the same $L$-function because $\chi^2=\eta$
is real. Define
$$
k(s)=\left(\frac5\pi\right)^{1-2s}
\frac{\Gamma(1-s)L(2-2s,\eta)}{\Gamma(s)L(2s,\eta)},
\qquad g(s)=10^{1-2s}k(s),
$$
and
$$
M_\chi(s)=10^{2s-1}C_\chi(s)^{-1}C_{\bar\chi}(1-s),
\qquad
M_{\bar\chi}(s)=10^{2s-1}C_{\bar\chi}(s)^{-1}C_\chi(1-s).
$$
The full four-row fixed-basis block is therefore
$$
g(s)
\begin{pmatrix}
0&\kappa M_\chi(s)\\
\kappa^{-1}M_{\bar\chi}(s)&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{3}
$$
This is a block of $T\Phi_N(s)T^{-1}$, not a new definition of
scattering. To verify this directly, expand each row of (2) at every
width-one cusp and compare its $y^{1-s}$ coefficient. The left side
has the row of $T\Phi_N(s)$, while the right side has the displayed
coefficient times the corresponding row of $T$. The paired formula
does the same for $\bar\chi$. Thus these four Fourier rows are
invariant under the matrix family. If the alternate convention uses
the transpose scattering matrix, commutator vanishing is unchanged.

### Step 5. The removed scalar is regular and nonzero

Let $\epsilon_\eta\ne0$ be the root number of the primitive even
quadratic character $\eta$. Its completed Dirichlet functional equation
yields the meromorphic identity
$$
k(s)=\epsilon_\eta\sqrt{\frac\pi5}
\frac{\Gamma(s-\tfrac12)}{\Gamma(s)}
\frac{L(2s-1,\eta)}{L(2s,\eta)}.
\tag{4}
$$
At $s=2,3$, both Dirichlet arguments are real and exceed $1$.
The Euler product is absolutely convergent and nonzero; both gamma
factors are finite and nonzero. Hence $g(2)g(3)\ne0$.
The original cusp series is holomorphic for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$,
so both are regular parameters of the full scattering matrix.
Equation (4), rather than a zero-times-infinity substitution into
$k(s)$, justifies evaluation at these integers.

### Step 6. Use only constant similarities and compute the commutator

Conjugating (3) by the constant matrix
$S=\operatorname{diag}(\kappa I_2,I_2)$ removes $\kappa$.
The constant Walsh matrix
$$
W=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}
$$
diagonalizes the two denominator matrices in (1) and their products.
Apply $\operatorname{diag}(W,W)$ and reorder coordinates once to
pair the plus channels. After division by the common scalar $g(s)$,
the resulting invariant two-dimensional block is
$$
K_+(s)=\begin{pmatrix}0&R_i(s)\\R_{-i}(s)&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
R_i(s)=\frac{1-i2^{s-1}}{1+i2^{-s}},\quad
R_{-i}(s)=\frac{1+i2^{s-1}}{1-i2^{-s}}.
\tag{5}
$$
In particular,
$$
R_i(2)=\frac{8-36i}{17},\qquad
R_i(3)=\frac{32-264i}{65},
$$
and $R_{-i}(2),R_{-i}(3)$ are their complex conjugates. Consequently
$$
[K_+(2),K_+(3)]
=\begin{pmatrix}384i/221&0\\0&-384i/221\end{pmatrix}\ne0.
\tag{6}
$$
The corresponding actual block commutator is (6) times
$g(2)g(3)\ne0$. Fixed similarities preserve commutator vanishing;
an invariant restriction of a commuting family would commute.
Therefore $[\Phi_{50}(2),\Phi_{50}(3)]\ne0$. ∎

## Auxiliary lemma: what the minimal primitive block actually proves

Let $\chi\ne\bar\chi$ be primitive of conductor $q$ and work only at
the minimal level $N=q^2$. Young (7.1) now gives
$E_\chi(s)=q^sD_{\chi,q}(s)$. Thus its fixed paired block has
off-diagonal coefficients
$$
h_\chi(s)=q^{1-2s}\frac{\tau(\chi)}{\tau(\bar\chi)}
\left(\frac q\pi\right)^{1-2s}
\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{\Gamma(s)}
\frac{L(2-2s,\bar\chi^2)}{L(2s,\chi^2)}
\tag{7}
$$
and $h_{\bar\chi}(s)$. This paired block commutes for all regular
parameters **if and only if** $\chi^2=\bar\chi^2$.

Proof. A two-by-two off-diagonal family commutes precisely when the
ratio of its two nonzero meromorphic coefficients is constant.
If $\eta=\chi^2$ is real, (7) immediately gives a constant ratio.

For the converse, let $\xi$ of conductor $r$ be the primitive character
inducing $\eta$, and put $P=\{p:p\mid q,\ p\nmid r\}$.
The square $\eta$ need not be primitive. Use its missing Euler factors
before applying the primitive functional equation. That equation and
$$
1-\bar\xi(p)p^{2s-2}
=-\bar\xi(p)p^{2s-2}(1-\xi(p)p^{2-2s})
$$
show that $h_\chi(s)=c_\chi U(s)H_\xi(s)$, where $c_\chi\ne0$ is
constant, $U(s)$ is the same for $\chi$ and $\bar\chi$, and
$$
H_\xi(s)=
\prod_{p\nmid q}\frac{1-\xi(p)p^{-2s}}{1-\xi(p)p^{1-2s}}
\prod_{p\in P}\frac{1-\xi(p)p^{2-2s}}{1-\xi(p)p^{1-2s}}.
\tag{8}
$$
For completeness one can take, with $d=\prod_{p\in P}p$,
$$
U(s)=q^{1-2s}(q/\pi)^{1-2s}(r/\pi)^{2s-3/2}
d^{2s-2}\frac{\Gamma(s-\tfrac12)}{\Gamma(s)},\qquad
c_\chi=\frac{\tau(\chi)}{\tau(\bar\chi)}
\epsilon_{\bar\xi}\prod_{p\in P}(-\bar\xi(p)).
$$
Only the nonreal-$\eta$ case is needed for the converse, so $\xi$ is
nonprincipal and its primitive completed functional equation applies
without principal-character pole exceptions.

Both products in (8) have absolutely convergent Dirichlet expansions
in $n^{-2s}$ when $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, and tend to $1$ as real
$s\to+\infty$. A constant quotient $H_\xi/H_{\bar\xi}$ must therefore
be $1$. Uniqueness of absolutely convergent Dirichlet series forces
their coefficient at every prime $\ell\nmid q$ to agree. That
coefficient is $(\ell-1)\xi(\ell)$, so $\xi(\ell)$ is real for all
such primes. Every positive integer coprime to $q$ factors into these
primes; multiplicativity then makes $\eta$ real on every residue
class, contrary to the hypothesis. Uniqueness here can be proved by
taking the least nonzero coefficient of a difference series, multiplying
by its index to the power $2s$, and letting real $s\to+\infty$;
absolute convergence controls the remaining tail. This proves the
lemma. ∎

The lemma explains why the frozen conjecture looked plausible at
minimal conductor-square levels. It makes no assertion about its
embedding into larger levels. At $N=50$, the additional unramified
prime $2$ has $\chi(2)=i$; the factors in (5), absent at $N=25$,
invalidate the proposed extension.

## Corrections or Missing Assumptions

- Reality of $\chi^2$ controls the minimal primitive paired scalar,
  not every oldform multiplier at primes outside its conductor.
- A principal-character oldform commutativity proof does not apply to
  a quartic character with a nonreal value at an added prime.
- Neither the all-level necessity nor a repaired all-level sufficiency
  is proved here. No conjectural replacement is substituted for the
  frozen original statement.
