## 2. An all-length mod-eight identity

**Lemma.** Every active word of length \(n\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{tr}M_w\equiv(-1)^n\pmod8.
\]
Consequently
\[
v_2(D_w)=1\quad(n\text{ odd}),\qquad
v_2(D_w)\geq3\quad(n\text{ even}).
\]

**Proof.** Cayley--Hamilton gives
\[
B^2=7B-8I\equiv-B\pmod8.
\]
Also \(\operatorname{tr}(AB)=25\), \(\det(AB)=64\), so
\[
(AB)^2=25AB-64I\equiv AB\pmod8.
\]
If a cyclic no-\(aa\) word contains \(r\geq1\) letters \(a\) and
\(s\) letters \(b\), cyclically rotate its matrix product into the form
\[
AB^{\ell_1}AB^{\ell_2}\cdots AB^{\ell_r},
\qquad \ell_i\geq1,\quad \sum_i\ell_i=s.
\]
Trace is unchanged by cyclic rotation. This argument uses the matrix
order actually present in the chronological product; reversing the
written itinerary does not alter the no-adjacent-\(a\) property.
Reduction modulo eight gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}M_w
\equiv(-1)^{s-r}\operatorname{tr}(AB)^r
\equiv(-1)^{s-r}
=(-1)^{s+r}.
\]
For an all-\(b\) word,
\(\operatorname{tr}B^s\equiv(-1)^{s-1}\operatorname{tr}B
\equiv(-1)^s\). These exhaust the active words. Finally,
\(D_w\equiv1-(-1)^n\pmod8\), proving both valuation claims.
\(\square\)

This is a symbolic identity for every length, not an extrapolation from
the inherited finite valuation table.

## 3. Isolating a genuinely analytic remainder

Put
\[
a_n=\sum_{\substack{|w|=n\\w\ {\rm active}}}
(1-2^{-v_2(D_w)}).
\]
The preceding lemma gives
\[
a_n=\tfrac12L_n\quad(n\text{ odd}),\qquad
\tfrac78L_n\leq a_n\leq L_n\quad(n\text{ even}).
\tag{1}
\]
Write
\[
\Delta_n=8^na_n+e_n,\qquad
e_n=\sum_{w\ {\rm active}}
(1-\operatorname{tr}M_w)(1-2^{-v_2(D_w)}).
\]
Then
\[
|e_n|\leq(2\beta^n+1)L_n.
\]
Thus
\[
G(z)=\sum_{n\geq1}a_n(8z)^n/n+E(z),\qquad
E(z)=\sum_{n\geq1}e_nz^n/n
\tag{2}
\]
with \(E\) holomorphic for \(|z|<1/(\beta\varphi)>R\).
The remainder is bounded near both \(R\) and \(-R\).

## 4. Nonmeromorphic points survive exponentiation

For \(0<t<\varphi^{-1}\),
\[
H(t):=\sum_{n\geq1}L_nt^n/n=-\log(1-t-t^2).
\]
Its even and odd subseries are \((H(t)+H(-t))/2\) and
\((H(t)-H(-t))/2\). As \(t\uparrow\varphi^{-1}\), each equals
\[
\tfrac12\log\frac1{1-\varphi t}+O(1).
\]
Indeed \(H(-t)\) stays bounded and the second factor of
\(1-t-t^2=(1-\varphi t)(1+\varphi^{-1}t)\) stays nonzero.

Let \(Q(r)=\log(1/(1-r/R))\), for \(0<r<R\).
Equations (1)--(2) yield
\[
\frac{11}{16}\leq
\liminf_{r\uparrow R}\frac{G(r)}{Q(r)}
\leq\limsup_{r\uparrow R}\frac{G(r)}{Q(r)}
\leq\frac34,
\tag{3}
\]
and, because odd terms change sign,
\[
\frac3{16}\leq
\liminf_{r\uparrow R}\frac{G(-r)}{Q(r)}
\leq\limsup_{r\uparrow R}\frac{G(-r)}{Q(r)}
\leq\frac14.
\tag{4}
\]
No existence of either limit is asserted.

Suppose \(f(z)=e^{-G(z)}\), initially on \(|z|<R\), extended
meromorphically to a neighborhood of \(z_0\in\{R,-R\}\).
It cannot be identically zero there, since it is nonzero on the interior
overlap. Hence \(f(z)=(z-z_0)^m h(z)\), with \(m\in\mathbb Z\)
and \(h\) holomorphic and nonzero near \(z_0\). Along the real radial
approach to \(z_0\), reality of \(G\) and this local factorization imply
\[
G(\pm r)/Q(r)\longrightarrow m.
\]
Both intervals (3)--(4) avoid every integer, a contradiction.

The rational prefactor \(Z_\infty\) is finite and nonzero at both
points. Its denominator zeros are \(1/16,1/2\), and its numerator
zeros are the reciprocals of \((13\pm\sqrt{37})/2\), neither of
which has modulus \(R\). Dividing a hypothetical local meromorphic
extension of \(Z_2\) by this nonvanishing prefactor would extend \(f\).

**Conclusion.** The full \(Z_2\), not merely its logarithm or a single
return factor, has no meromorphic extension through \(R\) or \(-R\).
\(\square\)

The existing pole at \(1/16\) does not affect this local argument:
\(Z_\infty e^{-G}\) already defines the inherited meromorphic
continuation inside \(|z|<R\).

## 5. What the entire congruence tower does control

For \(k\geq1\), let
\[
b_{n,k}=\#\{w:|w|=n,\ w\text{ active},\ v_2(D_w)\geq k\}.
\]
The exact identity \(1-2^{-v}=\sum_{k=1}^v2^{-k}\) gives
\[
a_n=\sum_{k\geq1}2^{-k}b_{n,k},\qquad 0\leq b_{n,k}\leq L_n.
\]
Normalize \(u=8\varphi z\), and set
\[
P_k(u)=\sum_{n\geq1}b_{n,k}\varphi^{-n}u^n/n,\qquad
P(u)=\sum_{k\geq1}2^{-k}P_k(u).
\]
Then \(G(z)=P(8\varphi z)+E(z)\).
Absolute convergence and the coefficient bound prove, for \(|u|<1\),
\[
\left|\sum_{k>K}2^{-k}P_k(u)\right|
\leq2^{-K}H(|u|/\varphi).
\tag{5}
\]
Near \(|u|=1\), the right side is
\(2^{-K}[-\log(1-|u|)+O(1)]\).

There is also useful normalized derivative control. Write
\(Q_k(u)=uP_k'(u)\). For every \(|\zeta|=1\) and \(0<r<1\),
\[
(1-r)\left|\sum_{k>K}2^{-k}Q_k(r\zeta)\right|
\leq 2^{-K}(1-r)
\sum_{n\geq1}L_n\varphi^{-n}r^n
\leq2^{1-K}.
\tag{6}
\]
The last bound follows from
\(L_n\varphi^{-n}=1+(-\varphi^{-2})^n\), whose absolute value is
at most two. Thus, whenever the individual fixed-layer radial residues
are known, their weighted sum is the full radial residue. It is
incorrect to call this inside-disk interchange an unproved uniformity
assumption.

Each fixed \(k\) is a finite-state residue computation: keep the current
matrix modulo \(2^k\), the first/last letters for the cyclic constraint,
and the required finite length residue/transient. Consequently \(Q_k\)
is rational. Its coefficients are bounded by \(2\), so it has no
higher-order pole on the unit circle, and its normalized radial limit
exists at every fixed direction. This observation does not require
computing any new layer.

## 6. Exact remaining gap and sufficient closure routes

Define the actual aggregated radial residue
\[
c(\zeta)=\sum_{k\geq1}2^{-k}
\lim_{r\uparrow1}(1-r)Q_k(r\zeta).
\]
Equation (6) justifies this definition and its equality to
\(\lim_{r\uparrow1}(1-r)uP'(u)|_{u=r\zeta}\).

A sufficient whole-circle lemma would assert that
\(\{\zeta:|\zeta|=1,\ c(\zeta)\ne0\}\) is dense.
It would imply dense singularities of the full logarithmic derivative.
Any meromorphic continuation of \(Z_2\) through a boundary arc would
make its logarithmic derivative meromorphic there. The nonzero residues
would force a dense set of poles, impossible for a meromorphic function.
This handles exponentiation without assuming that individual residues
are nonintegral: zeros and poles of a nonzero meromorphic function are
also discrete.

**That density/noncancellation lemma has not been proved here.**
Fixed-layer peripheral phases must be combined before drawing any
conclusion. Finite-state positivity, growing state counts, and growing
orders in the matrix residue semigroup do not by themselves supply
dense nonzero aggregate residues. If exact peripheral phases remain in
a finite parity set, this particular sufficient lemma cannot close the
whole circle.

In that event, near-peripheral eigenvalues at increasing congruence
depth could still matter. A different proof would have to control the
full sum at those accumulating candidate singularities, with a valid
analytic continuation domain and cross-layer noncancellation.
Bounds (5)--(6) are only inside-disk bounds; they do not prove normal
convergence in any annulus crossing \(|u|=1\). Neither an isolated
finite-layer pole nor an observed sequence of near-peripheral
eigenvalues is a proved singularity of the complete infinite sum.

No uniform all-layer peripheral classification, no such annular
continuation argument, and no representation placing this switching
sequence in the required finite radial/FAD class has been established
in this review. These are substantive missing assertions, not tasks
that can be replaced by increasing an inherited numerical cutoff.
