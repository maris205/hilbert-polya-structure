## Verified mathematical dependencies

### S1. The exact third-order QRT reduction is classical

Junta Matsukidaira and Daisuke Takahashi, *Third-order integrable difference
equations generated by a pair of second-order equations*, arXiv:nlin/0512072v1
(26 December 2005), Journal of Physics A 39 (2006), 1151–1161.
[Primary HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/nlin/0512072),
[arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0512072).

Access: complete relevant §II.1, equations (6)–(14), including the two parity
constants, both reduced maps and both symmetric biquadratic invariants; PDF
endpoint metadata also accessible. The HTML's body date “August 24, 2026”
does not override its explicit 2005 version header or the journal year.

Ownership: the reduction and invariant in the auxiliary proof are exactly
classical inputs. The paper does not, in this accessed section, give the
all-signed-integer periodic atlas. No such claim is inferred from “QRT”.

### S2. Alternate reduction reference and positive-domain boundary

Anna Cima, Armengol Gasull and Víctor Mañosa, *Some properties of the
k-dimensional Lyness' map*, arXiv:0801.4360v1 (28 January 2008).
[Primary HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/0801.4360).

Access: Theorem 2, its proof, §3.1 (especially equation (16)), §4.1 and the
relevant reference entries. §3.1 gives the explicit plane reduction for fixed
$W$ and credits S1. Theorem 2 and the geometric rotation discussion use the
positive domain. The algebraic identity can be rechecked on a signed ordinary
orbit; the positive topological conclusions cannot simply be transferred.
No proof novelty is assigned to this dimension reduction.

### S3. Automorphism classification over the actual coefficient field

Danesh Jogia, John A. G. Roberts and Franco Vivaldi, *An algebraic geometric
approach to integrable maps of the plane*, Journal of Physics A 39 (2006),
1133–1149. [Institutional author PDF](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~jagr/IntegrabilityRS.pdf).

Access: definitions in §2, the full statement and proof of Theorem 3 on author
pages 5–6, Proposition 2, and Theorem 4 with its field remarks. The theorem
requires a genus-one curve with a point over the chosen field. It retains
finite-order affine automorphisms as well as translations; generic infinite
order is not necessary for the auxiliary bound. Over $\mathbb Q$ the proof's
root-of-unity condition leaves only $\pm1$ group automorphisms. The theorem
does not classify the singular/reducible fibers; those require the separate
argument in our proof. This is a classical automorphism input, not new ownership.

### S4. Rational torsion list, not the wrong Lyness map's classification

Armengol Gasull, Víctor Mañosa and Xavier Xarles, *Rational periodic sequences
for the Lyness recurrence*, arXiv:1004.5511; Discrete and Continuous Dynamical
Systems 32 (2012), 587–604.
[Primary HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1004.5511),
[journal metadata](https://www.aimsciences.org/article/doi/10.3934/dcds.2012.32.587).

Access: introduction including Theorem 1, the explicit Mazur order list,
Theorem 3 normal form statement, and the displayed singular-fiber discussion
and §3.2 proof. This is the **second-order** recurrence, not LY4. Its all-rational
period list and individual singular fibers are not reused as our classification.
It corroborates the imported rational torsion orders and demonstrates why
singular fibers need separate treatment. Neither its positive-real periods nor
its examples establish integrality in our third-order model.

### S5. Mazur input and visible retrieval limit

Barry Mazur, *Modular curves and the Eisenstein ideal*, Publications
Mathématiques de l'IHÉS 47 (1977), 33–186, DOI 10.1007/BF02684339.
[Official metadata](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1977__47__33_0/).

Access: author/title/year/pages/DOI metadata. Numdam legacy PDF paths failed;
the journal PDF endpoint exceeded the browser's size limit (16,309,727 bytes).
One timed curl-to-pdftotext fallback failed with an SSL error and an empty
document stream. No local PDF was saved or read, and the original proof was
not accessed. The theorem is an explicitly imported classical dependency,
whose needed order list was actually read in S4, not a theorem proved here.
The separate 1978 prime-isogeny search was a lead, not a substitute for torsion.
