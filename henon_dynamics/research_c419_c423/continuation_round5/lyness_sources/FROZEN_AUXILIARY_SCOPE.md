# LY4 source audit and auxiliary period-bound attempt

Frozen 2026-09-08 before any mathematical program. This is a coordinator-owned
dependency of the original LY4 question, **not an independent candidate**.
The separate proof author owns `../lyness_closure/` and the proposed integral
period exhaustion. No duplication of that author's period-five/six strata.

Object: ordinary, two-sided rational periodic sequences satisfying
$$x_i x_{i+3}=a+x_{i+1}+x_{i+2},\qquad a\in\mathbb Q.$$
For the auxiliary claim only, exclude all coordinates $0,-1$ and put
$$u=(x_0+1)(x_2+1)/x_1\ne0.$$
Use the classical even-subsequence map
$$R_{a,u}(X,Z)=\left(Z,\frac{(u+1)Z+au+1}{X(Z+1)}\right).$$

Proposed auxiliary theorem: every such orbit has native least period at most
48, uniformly in the rational parameter and rational initial conditions.
This bound is provisional, coarse, and not claimed sharp. It is stronger in
domain than the integral subproblem but much weaker than the original full
necessary-and-sufficient integral atlas. It does not establish its desired
period list or its integer parameter families.

Intended proof: the classical symmetric biquadratic invariant; local
biregularity along an ordinary orbit; reduced geometric components and
normalization over Q; Mazur's rational torsion theorem on genus-one components;
the elementary finite-order classification of PGL2(Q) on genus-zero components;
a separate bound on singular points of a reduced curve of bidegree at most
(2,2). Include nonreduced/reducible fibers, component permutations and descent
of the orbit point to Q. Do not substitute a generic-fiber assertion for these
cases. A failure in one of these steps leaves the auxiliary claim unclosed.

Source work is scoped to these dependencies and the exact ownership of the
QRT reduction. Existing positive-real rotation results, ordinary two-dimensional
Lyness rational period lists, and coefficient-free cluster periodicity are
not used outside their hypotheses. Source identities and standard consequences
are credited and deducted from novelty. A proved period bound remains a
