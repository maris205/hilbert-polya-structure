## AF5-G: all-period reversible Galois structure — deep attempt 1

**Object and family.** Let $t$ be transcendental over $\mathbb Q$ and
$F_t(x,y)=(y,y^2+t-x)$ over $k=\mathbb Q(t)$. Let
$R(x,y)=(y,x)$. The domain is all $\mathbb A^2(\overline{k})$; $F_t$ is a
polynomial automorphism, with no deleted zero-coordinate locus.

**Observable.** For every $n\geq1$, let $X_n$ be the set of points of exact
period $n$, and let $L_n$ be the field generated over $k$ by both coordinates
of every point of $X_n$. Determine the faithful permutation action
$G_n=\operatorname{Gal}(L_n/k)$ on $X_n$. The involutions and rotation generate
a dihedral action because $RF_tR=F_t^{-1}$.

**Full intended theorem.** Give, uniformly for every $n$, the dihedral orbit
types in $X_n$ and the exact group $G_n$, including all relations between
different dihedral-type blocks within that $n$. In particular decide, rather
than assume, whether the evident bound
$G_n\subseteq C_{\operatorname{Sym}(X_n)}(\langle F_t,R\rangle)$ is equality.
A different exact subgroup formula is an equally valid full outcome. No
independence of fields for different $n$ is included in this contract.

**Classical inputs versus intended increment.** Reversibility, dihedral
permutation theory, symbolic horseshoe coding and reflection closure degrees
are source material. The intended new work is a proved all-$n$ field/monodromy
lower bound and all required block-intersection relations, not a wreath
embedding, a handful of specialized factorizations or a period table.

**Decisive check and stop.** First test the maximal-centralizer proposal against
its reflection-fixed field constraints and small periods analytically. One
exact missing relation refutes equality, but does not close the replacement
classification. Stop if the only proof is a centralizer upper bound, if
all-$n$ transversality/irreducibility or independent branch generation remains
unproved, or if the apparent increment is already P60/C34/source-owned. Do not
run high-degree resultant/factorization censuses to mask that gap.

## AF5-C: effective cyclotomic atlas — deep attempt 2

**Object and family.** For every $c\in\mathbb Z$, take
$F_c(x,y)=(y,y^2+c-x)$ on $\mathbb A^2(\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{cyc}})$, where
$\mathbb Q^{\mathrm{cyc}}=\mathbb Q(\mu_\infty)$. Again zeros are allowed,
all ordinary iterates exist, and no degree/conductor cutoff is imposed.

**Observable and full success.** Give an explicit terminating exact procedure,
proved for every input integer $c$, that outputs all cyclotomic periodic points
and their least native periods, including zero coordinates and exceptional
parameters. Its termination must be justified by computable bounds from $c$
on both needed periods and cyclotomic fields, or an equally explicit finite
algebraic alternative with a proof of exhaustion. A uniform bound independent
of $c$ is welcome but not required. Merely proving finiteness separately for
each $c$ is not the full effective contract.

**Mechanism.** Bounded-house cyclotomic integers, finite sums of roots of unity,
torus rigidity and invariant Zariski closures, rather than bounded-degree
Northcott or an integer-lattice height census. This is an infinite-degree
arithmetic-field problem, not a DH1 Jacobian variant.

**Classical inputs versus intended increment.** Ji–Xie–Zhang's non-density,
Loxton-type representations, and absence of periodic algebraic curves for
plane Hénon maps are fully deducted. An invariant-closure deduction of
finiteness is retained as a source consequence, not promoted to a paper.
The intended increment is the effective all-parameter exhaustion just stated.

**Decisive check and stop.** Check whether source rigidity actually supplies
computable period/conductor bounds. Bounded house without degree is not
Northcott. A cyclotomic point outside a proposed finite list is a decisive
counterexample; no list is presently conjectured. Stop if a required effective
torsion-pattern or conductor bound cannot be proved. Never replace the full
atlas by non-density, finiteness, a fixed-$c$ sample or low-period families.

## AF5-D: effective orbit–curve intersections — shallow screen only

**Object.** For every number field $K$, polynomial Hénon automorphism $F/K$,
nonperiodic $P\in\mathbb A^2(K)$ and affine algebraic curve $C/K$, determine
the finite hit set $\{n\geq0:F^n(P)\in C\}$ by an explicit terminating
algorithm from the input equations and $P$.

**Mechanism and plausible bridge.** Dynamical Mordell–Lang and effective
height/Diophantine bounds can constrain arithmetic recurrence and chronology.
Qualitative finite intersection is already a classical consequence of étale
Mordell–Lang and the absence of periodic curves. It is not an inverse-orbit
gcd variation or a source-to-target A2 claim.

**Full success/counterexample/stop.** Success requires a computable last-hit
bound or a different proved terminating algorithm for every input, not
enumeration until no new hit appears. A later exact hit invalidates a proposed
last-hit bound. Shallow-stop immediately if only qualitative DML is available;
this lane will not open a third deep Diophantine project.
