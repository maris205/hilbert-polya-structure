## 2. Primary sources actually used

### S1. Ji–Xie–Zhang: recent cyclotomic rigidity input

Zhuchao Ji, Junyi Xie and Geng-Rui Zhang, *Cyclotomic integral points for affine
dynamics*, [official record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13443),
[inspected v2 text](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13443v2).
The record has v1 on 17 November 2025 and v2 on 20 January 2026, 22 pages.
No journal-publication or peer-review status is asserted.

Read: v1 introduction/application statement; then v2 introduction, Definition
1.7, Theorem 1.8, Corollary 1.9, Loxton Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2, and
the §4 proof of Theorem 1.8, including equations (4.5)–(4.10). V1's relevant
numbers were Theorem 1.7 and Corollary 1.8; the frozen contract has an explicit
version-correction addendum. The operative input is v2 non-density for
Hénon-type maps, not the regular-projective-endomorphism application.
The proof package separately derives finite cardinality for this plane
family. No effective period/conductor bound was extracted or claimed.

### S2. Dullin–Meiss–Sterling: classical reversible coding

Holger R. Dullin, James D. Meiss and David G. Sterling, *Symbolic Codes for
Rotational Orbits*, [official record](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0408015),
[author preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/nlin/0408015).
The official record gives *SIAM Journal on Applied Dynamical Systems* 4,
515–562 (2005), DOI 10.1137/040612877. The inspected preprint has 47 pages.
Read abstract and §3.4, printed page 14, equations (16)–(19), and nearby
reflection discussion. This verifies reversibility and symbolic symmetry
as prior art. It does not verify an exact arithmetic Galois-group theorem.
No claim to have read all 47 pages is made.

### S3. Sterling–Meiss: anti-integrable limit

D. G. Sterling and J. D. Meiss, *Computing periodic orbits using the
anti-integrable limit*, [official record](https://arxiv.org/abs/chao-dyn/9802014),
[11-page preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/chao-dyn/9802014).
The record gives *Physics Letters A* 241(1/2), 46–52 (1998),
DOI 10.1016/S0375-9601(98)00094-2. Read abstract, §2 recurrence and scaled
equations (1)–(10), and Theorem 1 with its proof. The scaled recurrence in
equation (5) is the classical mechanism behind Proposition 2.1's formal
power-series version. The present package claims no new symbolic coding
or horseshoe-existence theorem. The
[Meiss institutional publication list](https://www.colorado.edu/amath/directory/james-meiss/research-and-publications)
was also inspected for the publication metadata of S2–S3.

### S4. Endler–Gallas: period-four arithmetic ownership

A. Endler and J. A. C. Gallas, *Arithmetical signatures of the dynamics of the
Hénon map*, *Physical Review E* 65, 036231 (2002),
[publisher record and abstract](https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.65.036231).
Metadata and the abstract's all-period-four parameterization claim were
verified. An [author copy](https://inaesp.org/PublicJG/pre_EG02.pdf) initially
returned an 11-page parsed document, but targeted body finds/read attempts
did not establish the exact formula; two screenshot attempts failed with a
content-type error, and a later body open timed out. Therefore this audit
does **not** certify that the source contains our exact $G_4$ formula or
the same common-field relation. It does deduct low-period parameterization
as classical territory. Theorem 3.1 is independently proved, but neither
novelty nor independent-slot significance is asserted for it.

### S5. Bell–Ghioca–Tucker: qualitative étale DML

Jason Bell, Dragos Ghioca and Thomas J. Tucker, *The dynamical Mordell–Lang
problem for etale maps*, [official record](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.3266),
[inspected v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0808.3266v1), 19 pages, submitted
24 August 2008. Read Theorem 1.3, printed page 3, and surrounding discussion
of progression suborbits and singletons. The source's convention allows
$N=0$ for a singleton. The statement supplies qualitative orbit intersection
structure, not an effective last-hit algorithm. Later proof snippets were
viewed, but no complete independent verification of its full proof is claimed.
Its role here is the shallow stop for AF5-D, not a new theorem discovery.

### S6. Bedford–Smillie: classical attribution with a body-access limit

Eric Bedford and John Smillie, *Polynomial diffeomorphisms of C2: currents,
equilibrium measure and hyperbolicity*, *Inventiones Mathematicae* 103,
69–99 (1991). The
[Stony Brook author-hosted PDF](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~ebedford/PapersForM655/BS1.pdf)
and [Warwick author-hosted PDF](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/sci/maths/people/staff/john_smillie/polydiff/bs_i.pdf)
returned document metadata, but repeated targeted finds/body opens failed
or timed out. No exact theorem number or direct quotation of the original
no-periodic-curve proof is asserted.

A later [primary research article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00208-018-1643-6)
explicitly attributes the no-invariant-algebraic-curve fact to Bedford–Smillie
and provides the bibliographic entry. This confirms attribution, not a direct
reading of the original theorem. The needed special-family result is proved
from pole orders in Lemma 4.2; no hidden dependence on an unread proof remains.
