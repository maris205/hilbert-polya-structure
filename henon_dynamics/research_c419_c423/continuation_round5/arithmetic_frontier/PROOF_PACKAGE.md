## 1. Conventions and source deductions

Put $F_t(x,y)=(y,y^2+t-x)$, $R(x,y)=(y,x)$ and $k=\mathbb Q(t)$, with $t$
transcendental. Then $F_t^{-1}(x,y)=(x^2+t-y,x)$ and $RF_tR=F_t^{-1}$.
An orbit is represented by a bi-infinite scalar sequence

$$q_{j+1}+q_{j-1}=q_j^2+t,\qquad F_t^j(q_0,q_1)=(q_j,q_{j+1}).$$

Periods always mean least native iteration periods. $X_n$ consists of
distinct exact-period-$n$ geometric points, and $L_n$ is generated by both
coordinates of all of them. Galois acts faithfully on $X_n$ and commutes
with $F_t,R$, since both maps are defined over $k$. Consequently

$$G_n\subseteq C_{\operatorname{Sym}(X_n)}(\langle F_t,R\rangle).$$

Reversibility and its symbolic reflection structure are classical; see
Dullin–Meiss–Sterling, [§3.4, equations (16)–(19)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/nlin/0408015).
The anti-integrable construction below is also a classical mechanism; compare
Sterling–Meiss, [§2, equation (5) and Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/chao-dyn/9802014).
Endler–Gallas already give an all-period-four parameterization in their
[2002 paper](https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.65.036231).
We do not claim novelty for a low-period parameterization or these mechanisms.
The original source's exact period-four field formula was not verified from
its body; the field calculation below has its own proof.

## 2. Generic binary coding: a complete helper, not a Galois lower bound

**Proposition 2.1.** The fixed-point scheme of $F_t^n$ has $2^n$ distinct
geometric points over $k$. Its dihedral permutation structure is that on binary
words of length $n$, with rotation $(\sigma\varepsilon)_i=\varepsilon_{i+1}$
and reflection $(\rho\varepsilon)_i=\varepsilon_{1-i}$, indices modulo $n$.
Exact period corresponds to primitive words. In particular

$$|X_n|=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)2^d.$$

**Proof.** In $\mathbb Q[t][q_0,\ldots,q_{n-1}]$ impose

$$f_i=q_i^2+t-q_{i-1}-q_{i+1}=0,$$

with cyclic indices. The two neighbor terms are both retained when they
coincide, so this also covers $n=1,2$. For a degree-compatible monomial order
in the $q_i$, the leading monomials are the pairwise coprime $q_i^2$.
The monic coprime-leading-term criterion, or its direct S-polynomial reduction,
makes these a Gröbner basis over the coefficient ring $\mathbb Q[t]$.
The quotient is therefore free with basis $\prod_i q_i^{e_i}$, $e_i\in\{0,1\}$,
and rank $2^n$. Its points are precisely cyclic recurrence solutions; assigning
$(q_0,q_1)$ identifies them with $\operatorname{Fix}(F_t^n)$, including $n=1$.

Embed $k$ in $\mathbb Q((h))$ by $t=-h^{-2}$ and write $q_i=h^{-1}z_i$.
The equations become

$$z_i^2-1=h(z_{i-1}+z_{i+1}).$$

For each $\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}^n$ there is exactly one solution
$z_i\in\mathbb Q[[h]]$ with $z_i(0)=\varepsilon_i$: recursively, the coefficient
of order $h^r$ in the $i$th equation determines the unknown coefficient of
$z_i$ by multiplication by the nonzero rational number $2\varepsilon_i$;
all other terms are already determined. Distinct sign words give distinct
solutions. The algebra has dimension $2^n$ after base change and now has
$2^n$ distinct rational points, so it is the product of these fields and is
reduced. Reducedness descends to $k$.

Shifting coordinates realizes $F_t$. Replacing $q_i$ by $q_{1-i}$ realizes
$R$ and preserves the equations. Uniqueness of each formal lift gives the
stated word actions. If a word is invariant under a shift by $d$, its unique
lift is invariant under that shift; conversely equality of the lifted points
forces equality of their leading sign words. Thus least periods agree.
Möbius inversion of $2^n=\sum_{d\mid n}|X_d|$ proves the formula. $\square$

This determines the generic dihedral set, but not the arithmetic permutation
group inside its centralizer. It does not establish irreducibility of every
block, independent branch generation, or field intersections. Nor can generic
reducedness be specialized without proof to the fixed parameter in local P60.

## 3. A decisive exact-period-four field relation

**Theorem 3.1.** Write

$$s^2=-t,\qquad \alpha^2=s(s+2),\qquad\beta^2=s(s-2).$$

Then

$$L_4=k(s,\alpha,\beta),\qquad
G_4\simeq (C_2\times C_2)\rtimes C_2\simeq D_4,$$

where the last $C_2$ interchanges the two factors and $D_4$ denotes the
dihedral group of **order 8**. In contrast,

$$C_{\operatorname{Sym}(X_4)}(\langle F_t,R\rangle)
\simeq C_2\times(C_2\wr S_2)$$

has order 16. Thus the maximal-centralizer proposal in AF5-G is false.

**Proof: exhaustive exact-period classification.** A four-periodic scalar
tuple $(a,b,c,d)$ satisfies

$$a^2+t=b+d=c^2+t,\qquad b^2+t=a+c=d^2+t.$$

Hence $c=\pm a$, $d=\pm b$. The case $c=a,d=b$ has period dividing two
and is excluded. In the case $c=-a,d=-b$, the equations give
$a^2=b^2=-t$; the four resulting points are exactly the rotations of

$$E=(s,s,-s,-s).$$

If $c=a,d=-b$, then $a^2=-t$ and $b^2=-t+2a$. This gives rotations of

$$V_+=(s,\alpha,s,-\alpha),\qquad
V_-=(-s,\beta,-s,-\beta).$$

The remaining sign choice $c=-a,d=b$ gives their odd rotations.
Because $s$ is transcendental, $s,\alpha,\beta$ are nonzero and
$\alpha^2,\beta^2\ne s^2$. The three displayed cycles are pairwise disjoint
and have least period four: $E$ has opposite second-half coordinates; each
$V$ has a nonzero alternating sign in one coordinate. This is exactly 12
points. All their coordinates lie in $k(s,\alpha,\beta)$, and conversely
$s,\alpha,\beta$ occur among their coordinates. This proves the field identity.

**Proof: field degree and automorphisms.** Let $K_0=k(s)=\mathbb Q(s)$.
The polynomial $Z^2+t$ is irreducible over $k$, for example by its odd
$t$-adic valuation, so $[K_0:k]=2$. In $K_0^*/K_0^{*2}$ the classes of
$A=s(s+2)$ and $B=s(s-2)$ are independent. Indeed their valuations at
$s=-2$ are respectively $1,0$, and at $s=2$ respectively $0,1$.
If $A^uB^v$ were a square for $u,v\in\{0,1\}$, these valuations would force
$u=v=0$. Thus $[K_0(\alpha,\beta):K_0]=4$, with independent sign changes
$\epsilon_\alpha,\epsilon_\beta$.

The nontrivial $k$-automorphism $s\mapsto-s$ of $K_0$ exchanges $A$ and $B$.
It lifts to an automorphism

$$\tau(s)=-s,\qquad\tau(\alpha)=\beta,\qquad\tau(\beta)=\alpha.$$

The square-class independence makes these assignments well-defined, and
$\tau^2=1$. It exchanges the two independent sign-change generators by
conjugation. We have exhibited all eight automorphisms of a degree-eight
extension, proving the stated Galois group.

**Proof: the centralizer and its missing relation.** On the four points
of $E$, $R$ fixes two points. On each $V$ cycle it fixes none: using the
displayed initial point, $R$ acts on cycle indices as $j\mapsto1-j$, whereas
on $E$ it acts as $j\mapsto-j$. A permutation commuting with $F_t$ must send
an entire four-cycle to another four-cycle, and one also commuting with $R$
must preserve this reflection type. It therefore preserves $E$ and may
interchange $V_+,V_-$.

Within any cycle a commuting map is a rotation by $j$; commuting with its
reflection is equivalent to $2j=0\pmod4$, giving $j=0,2$. The two $V$ cycles
have the same reflected indexing, so exchanging them with matched indices
commutes with both maps. This gives precisely the asserted
$C_2\times(C_2\wr S_2)$, with $2\cdot2^2\cdot2=16$ elements.

In the actual Galois action, $\epsilon_\alpha$ rotates only $V_+$ by two
steps and $\epsilon_\beta$ rotates only $V_-$ by two. The involution $\tau$
interchanges $V_+,V_-$ and simultaneously rotates $E$ by two. Therefore

$$G_4=\{(e,w)\in C_2\times(C_2\wr S_2):
e=\operatorname{swap}(w)\},$$

where $\operatorname{swap}$ is the quotient onto the permutation group of
the two $V$ cycles, identified with $C_2$. The centralizer element that
rotates $E$ by two and fixes every $V$ point is not Galois: the $V$ coordinates
already contain $s$, so they cannot be fixed while $s$ changes sign.
This is an exact within-period field relation, not a specialization artifact.
$\square$

**AF5-G status.** Maximality is **REFUTED**, already for $n=4$. The frozen
contract allows a replacement exact all-$n$ subgroup formula, but none has
been established. That full question is **NOT CURRENTLY JUSTIFIED**. The
short field calculation and classical coding do not constitute admission.

## 4. Cyclotomic finiteness with the effective gap exposed

Fix $c\in\mathbb Z$ and $F_c(x,y)=(y,y^2+c-x)$.

**Lemma 4.1 (local integrality and bounded house).** Every algebraic periodic
coordinate is an algebraic integer, and all its complex conjugates have
absolute value at most $B(c)=1+\sqrt{1+|c|}$.

**Proof.** Work in a number field containing one finite scalar periodic
cycle $(q_i)$. At any finite place put $M=\max_i|q_i|_v$. If $M>1$, choose
$i$ attaining $M$. Since $c$ is integral and
$q_i^2=q_{i-1}+q_{i+1}-c$, the ultrametric inequality gives
$M^2\leq\max(M,1)=M$, a contradiction. This holds at every finite place,
so every coordinate is integral. For each complex embedding the conjugate
tuple is again periodic. With $M=\max_i|q_i|$ the usual triangle inequality
gives $M^2\leq2M+|c|$, whence $M\leq B(c)$. $\square$

**Lemma 4.2 (no periodic algebraic curve for this family).** For any
$c\in\overline{\mathbb Q}$, no irreducible affine algebraic curve
$C\subset\mathbb A^2$ satisfies $F_c^r(C)=C$ for any $r\geq1$.

**Proof.** Suppose otherwise. The restriction $H=F_c^r|_C$ is an
automorphism of $C$, hence lifts to its normalization and extends to an
automorphism of its smooth projective completion $\widetilde C$. The finite
boundary of this normalized affine curve is permuted by $H$. Some boundary
point $P$ is a pole of $x$ or $y$: otherwise both coordinates would extend
to regular functions on the projective curve and hence be constant.
Choose $\ell\geq1$ such that $H^\ell$ fixes $P$ and set $m=r\ell$.

For all integers $j$ let $q_j=(x\circ F_c^j)|_C$, a rational function on
$\widetilde C$. Negative iterates are polynomial too. We have $q_0=x$,
$q_1=y$, the same recurrence as above, and
$q_{j+m}=q_j\circ H^\ell$. Because an automorphism fixing $P$ preserves
orders there, the pole orders $a_j=\max(0,-\operatorname{ord}_P q_j)$ are
$m$-periodic. At least one is positive. Let $a_j=a>0$ be their maximum.
Then $q_j^2+c$ has pole order exactly $2a$, whereas $q_{j-1}+q_{j+1}$
has pole order at most $a$. This contradicts the recurrence. $\square$

The absence-of-periodic-curves input is classical, not claimed new. This
proof avoids relying on an unverified theorem number in the original
Bedford–Smillie paper; source-access details are in `SOURCE_AUDIT.md`.

**Theorem 4.3 (source-dependent finiteness).** For each $c\in\mathbb Z$,

$$S_c=\operatorname{Per}(F_c,\mathbb A^2(\mathbb Q^{\rm cyc}))$$

is finite.

**External input.** Ji–Xie–Zhang,
[arXiv:2511.13443v2, Theorem 1.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13443v2),
proves non-density of cyclotomic periodic points for Hénon-type polynomial
automorphisms over number fields. The inspected version is dated
20 January 2026; it is treated here as a preprint, not a peer-reviewed theorem
certification. The next deduction is ours, not a quotation of their statement.

**Proof from that input.** The homogenization of $F_c$ has unique
indeterminacy point $[1:0:0]$, while that of its polynomial inverse has
unique indeterminacy point $[0:1:0]$. Its degree is two, so it satisfies the
Hénon-type hypothesis. The external theorem makes the Zariski closure
$Z=\overline{S_c}$ a proper closed subset of $\mathbb A^2$ over
$\overline{\mathbb Q}$. Because both $F_c$ and its inverse are defined over
$\mathbb Q$ and preserve periodicity, $F_c(S_c)=S_c$. The automorphism is a
Zariski homeomorphism, so $F_c(Z)=Z$.

If $S_c$ were infinite, the proper closed set $Z$ would have at least one
one-dimensional irreducible component. It has only finitely many irreducible
components, and $F_c$ permutes them, so some positive iterate would preserve
an irreducible curve. Lemma 4.2 rules this out. Thus $Z$ is zero-dimensional,
hence finite, and so is $S_c$. $\square$

**Why this does not close AF5-C.** Neither this argument nor the inspected
source supplies here a computable conductor/period bound or an effective
finite alternative certifying that all points have been listed. Enumerating
cyclotomic fields and periods will eventually encounter every point of a
finite set, but gives no justified stopping test. Bounded house does not
repair the omission: roots of unity form an infinite collection of algebraic
integers of house one with unbounded degree. This example concerns the
invalid finiteness inference, not a claim that these roots are $F_c$-periodic.
Even a count bound alone would not certify completeness unless a proved
exhaustion or stopping criterion were also supplied.

The full every-$c$ effective atlas remains **NOT CURRENTLY JUSTIFIED**.
There is no fixed-parameter list, conductor cutoff or least-period bound
asserted in this package. Non-density and its finite-set deduction are fully
deducted as external/classical material, not an independent slot.

## 5. AF5-D: shallow classical screen, no third deep attempt

Bell–Ghioca–Tucker,
[Theorem 1.3, printed page 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0808.3266v1),
gives the qualitative dynamical Mordell–Lang description for étale maps.
Together with the classical absence of periodic curves, it implies finiteness
of the hit set for a nonperiodic Hénon orbit and an affine curve. This does
not provide the required effective last-hit bound in the inspected statement.

For the present quadratic family, the implication can be checked directly:
an infinite arithmetic-progression suborbit would be contained in the curve;
its Zariski closure has a curve component. Removing its first point does
not change the positive-dimensional closure, so the corresponding iterate
permutes these finitely many components and gives a periodic curve, contrary
to Lemma 4.2. The nonperiodicity ensures that such a suborbit is infinite.
For the broader frozen Hénon family, the standard no-periodic-curve theorem
is a classical input, not proved here or elevated to a new general theorem.

No effective strengthening was established. AF5-D is **SHALLOW-STOPPED**,
and its full terminating-algorithm contract remains unclosed. This is not a
third deep proof attempt.
