## 1. Exact local ownership collisions

### L1. C417: CR1 is literally contained in a completed theorem

The actual [C417 main theorem and seven-template table](../../../continuation_c414_c418_round2/papers/C417_integral_cubic/sections/1_introduction.tex)
and [template proof](../../../continuation_c414_c418_round2/papers/C417_integral_cubic/sections/6_templates.tex)
were read. They cover $H_f(x,y)=(y,f(y)-x)$ for every monic integral
cubic $f(t)=t^3+bt^2+ct+a$ on all $\mathbb Q^2$, with ordinary least
periods and oriented cycles modulo rotation, not reversal.

The theorem proves integrality, at most three coordinate values per
cycle, the complete seven-template list, possible periods
$\{1,2,3,4,6\}$, and the sharp eleven-point bound. The CR1 Duffing family
$f(t)=t^3-At$ is the substitution $a=b=0$, $c=-A$.
This is a literal subset, not an analogy or an unverified search hit.
CR1 is rejected without new code or a mathematical rerun.

### L2. C115 and the earlier elliptic-translation gate

The [C115 research question](../../../henon_mcmillan_rational_route_a/RESEARCH_QUESTION.md),
its [README](../../../henon_mcmillan_rational_route_a/README.md), and
[source audit](../../../henon_mcmillan_rational_route_a/SOURCE_AUDIT.md)
were read. Its convention is
$(-4x/(1+x^2)-y,x)$, corresponding after coordinate reversal to
$A=-4$ here. It certifies inverse/invariant, fixed and low-period
structure, excluded complex poles, and monodromy controls. It explicitly
does **not** claim a global arithmetic periodic-locus atlas.

Thus C115 deducts local McMillan reconstruction but does not by itself
contain the full CR2 contract. The earlier
[AR2 Somos scouting boundary](../../arithmetic/SCOUT_REPORT.md)
was also read: generic elliptic translation plus rational torsion is
already recognized as insufficient independent substance. C390 is an
inherited related-owner pointer there, not a newly reread full proof.
The [continuous Duffing question](../../../henon_duffing_hamiltonian_separatrix_route_a/RESEARCH_QUESTION.md)
has a different continuous-time clock and is not used as a CR1 theorem.

## 2. Primary sources: exact input and applicability

### P1. McMillan's invariant and modern real dynamics

T. Zolkin, Sergei Nagaitsev, and Ivan Morozov,
*Dynamics of McMillan mappings I. McMillan multipoles*,
Physica D 476 (2025), 134620,
[arXiv:2405.05652v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.05652v2).
The [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05652) dates v1 to
2024-05-09 and v2 to 2025-03-27 and supplies the journal metadata.

Actual access: abstract/introduction, Section II equations (1)–(5),
and the octupole subsection including equations (11)–(12) and Table I.
The map/invariant and real elliptic/action-angle structure are direct
classical ownership. These accessed sections do not give CR2's
all-integer-parameter rational-point atlas. Publisher full-text access
failed; journal metadata was not substituted for a publisher full read.

### P2. Biquadratic QRT maps and the clock distinction

A. Iatrou and J. A. G. Roberts,
*Integrable mappings of the plane preserving biquadratic invariant
curves II*, Nonlinearity 15 (2002), 459–489,
[author-hosted article](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~jagr/IR02.pdf),
DOI 10.1088/0951-7715/15/2/313.

Actual access: printed pages 459–462, including the McMillan map and
invariant, symmetric QRT construction, and Proposition 1's involution
description. This owns the geometric mechanism. A composition of two
involutions and its one-step symmetric version must not be confused:
changing to a two-step iterate changes ordinary periods. The DOI endpoint
was robots-blocked, but the original author PDF was readable. No claim
is made to have read all 31 pages or obtained an arithmetic atlas from it.

### P3. Genus-one automorphisms, with their hypotheses

D. Jogia, J. A. G. Roberts, and F. Vivaldi,
*An algebraic geometric approach to integrable maps of the plane*,
Journal of Physics A 39 (2006), 1133–1149,
[author-hosted article](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~jagr/JRV06.pdf),
DOI 10.1088/0305-4470/39/5/008.

Actual access: introduction, Theorem 3 and proof (printed 1137–1138),
Theorem 4 and its field/base-point remarks, and Proposition 4. Theorem 3
works with an elliptic curve over a field of characteristic different
from two and three, with a rational origin, and distinguishes translation
from nontrivial finite linear parts. The complex/base-field qualifications
matter. Proposition 4 bounds globally finite-order maps in its stated
surface setting; it is **not** a bound on periods on each specialized
rational fibre. No such substitution is made in CR2.

### P4. The precise torsion fact used in the partial proof

Barry Mazur, *Modular curves and the Eisenstein ideal*,
Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS 47 (1977), 33–186,
[original record and article](https://numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1977__47__33_0/),
DOI 10.1007/BF02684339.

Actual access: the original cover and printed pages 33–35, including
Theorem (7') and the group classification (8). The bound used is on
the order of a rational torsion **point**: at most ten or equal to
twelve. The whole torsion group can have sixteen elements. This applies
to the explicitly nonsingular $E/\mathbb Q$ in the partial proof.

The browser could not extract the oversized PDF. A successful read-only
`curl` stream into `pdftotext -f 1 -l 4 -layout - -` supplied the actual
theorem pages without saving a local PDF. No full 154-page reading is claimed.

### P5. Existing torsion/division-polynomial finiteness machinery

Ruichao Jiang, Javad Tavakoli, and Yiqiang Zhao,
*An upper bound and finiteness criteria for the Galois group of weighted
walks with rational coefficients in the quarter plane*,
Comptes Rendus Mathématique 359 (2021), 563–576,
[primary article](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.5802/crmath.196/).

Actual access: introduction/definitions and printed pages 571–576,
including Theorems 20, 25–27, Proposition 29 and Corollaries 30–32.
These exhibit the already classical reduction from an involution group
to a rational torsion element and exact division-polynomial tests.
Their walk assumptions and dihedral-group order are not CR2's native
map period.

One extracted line in the proof of Theorem 25 bounds the cardinality
of the full torsion subgroup by twelve, although Theorem 20 includes
$\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z/8$. The needed cyclic element-order bound
is twelve and repairs that deduction. We use this corrected distinction,
not the erroneous cardinality inference; this observation is not a
claim that the article's final bound of 24 is false.

The latter pages were actually read through a successful PDF-to-text
stream after browser follow-up failures. A PDF metadata warning did
not prevent text extraction; no visual page verification is claimed.

### P6. Recent continuation: not a rational arithmetic atlas

T. Zolkin, Sergei Nagaitsev, Ivan Morozov, and Sergei Kladov,
*Dynamics of McMillan mappings III. Symmetric map with mixed nonlinearity*,
Nonlinear Dynamics 114 (2026), article 635,
[publisher article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11071-026-12509-5)
and [arXiv:2410.10380v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10380v3).

Actual access: arXiv abstract/introduction and Section II equations
(1)–(2), plus the publisher's abstract/metadata, not subscription full
text. The [arXiv version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10380)
dates v3 to 2026-03-23; the publisher dates publication to 2026-05-01.
The four-author primary record was used over conflicting search metadata.
This checks recent geometric McMillan ownership without conflating
real stability/nonlinear trajectories with the full rational locus.

## 3. Leads, exclusions, and access limits

Roberts's 2011 author-hosted review
[R11.pdf](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~jagr/R11.pdf) was read in its
opening pages for the McMillan and translation formulas. It is an
author's review of the same mechanism, not an independent proof source
to be counted again. The rational Poncelet and Pezda search results
were leads/metadata, not full-theorem evidence for this candidate.

Lutz's 1937 paper was reached at metadata level only. A later Châtelet
seminar exposition and a teaching statement of the integral-torsion
criterion were inspected while considering an alternative infinitude
proof. Neither is a dependency of the delivered proof: the frozen
Mazur-plus-twelve-multiples route was retained. We do not label those
secondary pages as having verified Lutz's original full theorem.

A January 2026 anti-integrable McMillan perturbation result appeared as
an abstract-only lead; its real symbolic/parameter-limit question is
different and its publication precedes the six-month discovery window.
It is not cited as either a CR2 collision or an arithmetic result.

This bounded screen accessed the six directly useful primary works
above instead of manufacturing ten to fifteen purported full readings.
No citation-network completeness, retraction/erratum database sweep,
author-conflict audit, journal-rank judgment, or worldwide novelty
certificate was performed. Lack of an accessed complete atlas is
reported as a search limitation, not proof that none exists.

## 4. Claim/source boundary and final decision

| Claim or proposed increment | Evidence and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| All-integer Duffing classification is new | False as a local increment: exact containment in L1. |
| McMillan invariant and genus-one translation are new | Deducted by P1–P3 and L2. |
| Rational torsion gives a finite set of algebraic order tests | Classical P4–P5 mechanism; a procedure is not the required explicit atlas. |
| $A=-5$, $K=4$ has infinitely many rational least-three-period points | Direct identities plus explicit elliptic charts and the single exact certificate, in [PARTIAL_PROOF.md](PARTIAL_PROOF.md). No priority claim. |
| All $A\in\mathbb Z$, all $\mathbb Q^2$ periodic loci and singular exceptions are classified here | Not justified; the varying torsion-fibre/rational-point locus remains unclosed. |

CR1 is rejected as a literal already completed subfamily. CR2 is stopped
at its frozen full-family-closure boundary. The new exact obstruction
is retained, but its classical/short nature does not fill a paper slot.
Neither source arithmetic nor the torsion calculation establishes target
Euler factors, root numbers, automorphy, or a zero correspondence.
