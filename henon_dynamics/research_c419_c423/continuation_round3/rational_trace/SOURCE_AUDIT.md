## Actual primary reads and applicability

### Jang, Tropical Dynamics of Markov Surfaces

Read the [v2 primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.11357v2), its
introduction, definitions of the surface/action and complete Theorems
A--C, not the full 64-page proof. The revision states December 2025.
Theorem A concerns the action on the tropical skeleton, with different
models for negative and nonnegative parameter valuations. Theorem C
concerns whole-group orbits of certain $\mathbb Z[1/p]$ points at
$A=B=C=0$ and $0<D<1$, not all rational single-map cycles. Theorem B
has a negative-valuation parameter hypothesis. Our general levels may
have either valuation regime. No transfer from a skeleton statement
to all ordinary periodic points is assumed; the denominator lemma is
proved directly and independently of these source assertions.

### Vishkautsan, Residual periodicity on the Markoff surface

Read the [primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.07099), particularly
the rational conic-fiber analysis in section 3, equations (9)--(13)
and its conclusion. The source fixes the third coordinate under a
two-reflection composition, reducing the action on each conic to a
two-by-two matrix and testing roots of unity. It also distinguishes
rational points from points after finite-field reduction. Our cyclic
three-coordinate trace map does not preserve that coordinate fibration.
Its rational cycles therefore are not classified by the source's
two-reflection calculation. The complete 13-page proof was not reviewed.

### Humphries, Points with finite orbits for trace maps

Read the [primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.02743), introduction,
half-trace normalization and complete Theorem 1. Its set of points
periodic for **each** group element equals the finite whole-group orbit
set. Being periodic for our one specified automorphism is a weaker
condition. The source does not say that all such one-map points belong
to its classification. Its torus-coordinate rationality also must not
be confused with rationality of the three trace coordinates. No
whole-paper review was performed here.

### Abboud, Unlikely intersections problem for automorphisms of Markov surfaces

Read the [v4 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.05762v4), introduction
and Theorem A. The latter compares common periodic loci of two
loxodromic automorphisms at specified parameters
$D=0$ or $D=2-2\cos(\pi/q)$. It is a rigidity/intersection statement,
not an exhaustive rational periodic-point atlas for the forced
$T_a$ family. No single-map rational classification is inferred.

Cantat--Loray's primary PDF was also opened as a previously used
ambient source; no new full theorem verification from it is claimed in
this screen. Roberts' known curve/escape work remains the classical
ownership credited in C413, not a newly read all-rational theorem.

## Decision

The source checks reveal no verified theorem closing this full rational
contract. That does **not** establish novelty. The actual proof here
ends at a denominator restriction and an integral-level IR1 corollary.
The explicit period-two family permits every $q\ge2$, and the remaining
rational centered cases are not classified uniformly in $q$.

Recommendation: **do not admit; retain the companion lemma and exact
gap**. A new attempt needs a substantial all-denominator mechanism,
not a larger rational-height scan or a new name for the IR1 corollary.
No target Euler factors, root numbers, automorphy or zero correspondence
follow from any source-level statement here.
