## B1: the ordinary integral periodic locus of Hietarinta–Viallet

For every $c\in\mathbb Z$, define
$$H_c(x,y)=(y,-x+y+c/y^2).$$
The domain is the maximal two-sided invariant subset of
$(\mathbb Q^\times)^2$ on which this displayed map and its rational inverse
are defined and nonzero. The observable is the full periodic subset whose
every scalar coordinate is in $\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$, with least native
period, no sign or time-reversal quotient, and no period bound. One $H_c$
application is one step. Zero-crossing singularity confinement on a blown-up
surface is not silently included in this ordinary affine domain.

**Closest deductions.** Takenawa's original map, blow-up automorphism and
entropy mechanism are prior. Kanki–Mase–Tokihiro explicitly discuss the
coefficient scaling over an algebraic extension. Merely repeating entropy,
Laurent lifts, singularity confinement or a cubic-twist observation does
not qualify. The $c=0$ linear order-six boundary is deducted in full.

**Complete question.** Classify every such periodic orbit for every $c$,
or prove a complete all-parameter rigidity statement with its exceptions.
The fixed points satisfy $x=y=r$, $c=r^3$; these are a baseline, not a
claimed classification. Any integral periodic coordinate satisfies
$x_i^2\mid c$ when $c\ne0$, so a divisor graph is available for each
individual parameter. That observation alone is expressly insufficient.

**Decisive gate and cheap falsifier.** Test the tentative rigidity claim
that the nonzero-parameter integral cycles are all fixed, using one small,
predeclared exact divisor-graph diagnostic. In parallel seek a global
primitive-cycle argument: divide a cycle by its coordinate gcd (scaling
$c$ by the cube of that gcd), then try to exclude nonconstant primitive
cycles through the recurrence and divisor constraints. A counterexample
kills the fixed-only guess, not automatically the whole question; any
repair must supply a coherent exhaustive mechanism, not an extra period
word. A surviving finite test is not evidence of universal correctness.

**Stop boundary.** If the argument reaches only $x_i^2\mid c$, a
parameter-dependent finite graph, low-period exclusions, or an unproved
rational-height principle, record that exact gap and stop B1. No second
larger divisor census is authorized to replace a missing global lemma.

## B2: integral returns of the non-Lyness Bedford–Kim family

For $(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2$ with $b\ne0$, define
$$f_{a,b}(x,y)=\left(y,\frac{y+a}{x+b}\right).$$
Use the maximal two-sided invariant domain on which both this formula
and $f_{a,b}^{-1}(x,y)=((x+a)/y-b,x)$ are defined. Require every scalar
coordinate on the periodic orbit to be an integer. This domain excludes
the inverse-indeterminate zero-coordinate branches explicitly, rather
than counting their resolutions as ordinary points. One displayed map
application is one step, with no orientation quotient or period cutoff.

**Closest deductions.** Bedford–Kim already classify global finite order
and degree growth and construct rational-surface automorphisms in this
exact family. Their classification of parameters with $f^N=\mathrm{id}$
does not say that a generic infinite-order map has no specialized rational
or integral periodic point. Their entire globally periodic and integrable
mechanism is deducted; $b=0$ (classical Lyness) is not a new candidate.

**Complete question.** Classify the ordinary integral periodic set for
all the stated $(a,b)$, including native least periods and exceptional
parameter subfamilies, without a parameter box or unclassified recurrent
component.

**Decisive gate and cheap falsifier.** Start from the exact scalar relation
$$(x_i+b)x_{i+2}=x_{i+1}+a.$$
Try to close a maximum-coordinate section and its parameter-uniform
first-return transitions. In particular a maximum $M>|a|$ forces its
two-step predecessor to satisfy $x+b\in\{-1,1\}$; this is a possible
entrance mechanism, not a full recurrent-core proof. Before any finite
diagnostic, specify a finite parameter sample and the particular guessed
transition or rigidity statement it tests. Global finite-order identities
from the source may be used only as controls and may not be new output.

**Stop boundary.** An elementary parameter-dependent height bound or
another instruction to enumerate a bounded integer rectangle is not a
classification. If no complete transition lemma emerges, stop and report
the unresolved component. Do not replace B2 with its shorter-period
subproblem or with the known global-map periodicity classification.

## Pre-computation declaration: B2, 2026-09-07

Use the single finite sample $-8\le a,b\le8$, $b\ne0$ (272 parameter
pairs). For each pair let $A=|a|$, $B=|b|$, and use every nonzero integer
coordinate $|x|\le2A+B+1$ with $x+b\ne0$. Retain every exact integer
edge and extract every cycle, with no period cutoff. The diagnostic asks
whether all such cycles have native period at most three, and records the
first longer witness if not. This deliberately strong guess is not a
source theorem. Separately record whether a nonfixed cycle can avoid the
putative section $x+b=\pm1$; an explicit hand witness already shows this
is possible outside the sample: $(a,b)=(-11,-6)$, word $(2,3)$.

The box for each parameter is exhaustive for the stated integral domain:
if $M=\max |x_i|>A$, then from
$|x_{i-2}+b|M\le M+A<2M$ the nonzero integer denominator equals
$\varepsilon=\pm1$. Thus $x_{i-2}=-b+\varepsilon$ and
$x_{i-1}=\varepsilon x_i-a$; the preceding recurrence, whose denominator
is also a nonzero integer, gives
$|x_{i-1}|\le|a-b+\varepsilon|$. Therefore
$M\le A+|a-b+\varepsilon|\le2A+B+1$. If $M\le A$ the bound is
automatic. This short height bound is explicitly not the sought
all-parameter periodic classification. No second enlarged sample is
authorized if the uniform recurrent component remains unclassified.
