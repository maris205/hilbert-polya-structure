\section{An exact universal integer locus}\label{sec:intro}

Periodicity for one trace-map word is different from finiteness of
an entire group orbit. An integer point can be fixed by a positive
word whose substitution matrix is hyperbolic and still escape under
another positive word. We determine the exact union of these
single-word periodic loci and use it to classify each individual word.

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:maps}
 A(x,y,z)=(x,z,xz-y),\qquad B(x,y,z)=(z,y,yz-x),
 \qquad K(x,y,z)=x^2+y^2+z^2-xyz.
\end{equation}
A word $w=\ell_1\cdots\ell_s$ is \emph{admissible} if each
$\ell_i\in\{A,B\}$ and both letters occur. The notation is
chronological:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clock}
 W=\ell_s\circ\cdots\circ\ell_1,\qquad
 P,\ W(P),\ W^2(P),\ldots .
\end{equation}
One application of $W$, not one letter or one substitution length,
is the ordinary time step. In particular $AB$ means $B\circ A$.

Put $C=\{-1,0,1\}$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:locus}
 \begin{aligned}
 \D_0&=\{(t,a,b),(a,t,b),(a,b,t):t\in\Z,\ a,b\in C\},\\
 \D_x&=\{(c,t,ct+b):t\in\Z,\ c\in\{-1,1\},\ b\in C\},\\
 \D_y&=\{(t,c,ct+b):t\in\Z,\ c\in\{-1,1\},\ b\in C\},\\
 \D&=\D_0\cup\D_x\cup\D_y,\\
 \E&=\{(2\epsilon,2\eta,2\epsilon\eta):
                  \epsilon,\eta\in\{-1,1\}\}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here a line means its set of integer points. The line collections
have respectively $27,6,6$ distinct members. Lines may intersect
as sets of points. For $k\in\Z$ set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Xk}
 X_k=(\D\cup\E)\cap\{K=k\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For the maps \eqref{eq:maps}, the following assertions hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every admissible word, every integer periodic point of $W$
belongs to $\D\cup\E$. Every intermediate letter phase of a periodic
orbit belongs to the same set.
\item Every point of $\D\cup\E$ is fixed by some admissible word.
Consequently
\[
 \bigcup_w\Fix(W;\Z^3)=\bigcup_w\Per(W;\Z^3)=\D\cup\E.
\]
\item For every integer $k$, $|X_k|\le40$, and $X_k$ is empty unless
\[
 k\in\{m^2,\ m^2+1,\ m^2-m+2:m\in\Z\}.
\]
The quadratic-root and partial-permutation algorithm of
Section~\ref{sec:classifier} gives every ordinary cycle for every
admissible word on every level. In particular every least period
is at most $40$.
\item If an integer point $P$ is not periodic for an admissible $W$,
then $\norm{W^n(P)}\to\infty$ and $\norm{W^{-n}(P)}\to\infty$
as $n\to\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The union in the theorem is existential over words. It is not the
set periodic under every group element. Nor does the candidate bound
assert a word with 40 periodic points or a 40-cycle. The exact
word-dependent cycles, rather than such an unproved sharpness
claim, are the output of the algorithm.

\subsection{Classical inputs and the residual question}

The trace-map structure, invariant and reversible substitutions are
classical \citep{RobertsBaake1994}. The exact displayed generators
appear in full-trace coordinates in
\citet[Section~2.1]{SasakiYoshida2008}, together with their iterate
formulas and conic dynamics. General infinite-order trace-map escape
methods already occur in \citet{Roberts1996}. Our cone arguments
below are proved directly and are not offered as the introduction
of escape theory for general words.

Character-variety group dynamics \citep{CantatLoray2009},
finite whole-group orbits \citep{Humphries2016}, and integer Markoff
group descent \citep{GhoshSarnak2022} address neighboring but
different quantifiers. The difference is exhibited by an explicit
fixed-line/escaping-word calculation in
Section~\ref{sec:separators}; it is not inferred only from titles.
The entire integer classification for the single Fibonacci trace
map in the predecessor manuscript \citep{C413} is also deducted.
The present family includes a new level family and words that are
not powers of that map.
