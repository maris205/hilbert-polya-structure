\section{Coordinates, reversibility and separating examples}\label{sec:conventions}

Direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverses}
 A^{-1}(x,y,z)=(x,xy-z,y),\qquad
 B^{-1}(x,y,z)=(xy-z,y,x).
\end{equation}
Both are integer polynomial automorphisms. The invariant identity
for the first is
\[
 K(A(x,y,z))=x^2+z^2+(xz-y)^2-xz(xz-y)=K(x,y,z).
\]
The coordinate exchange $J(x,y,z)=(y,x,z)$ satisfies $JAJ=B$
and $JBJ=A$, and preserves $K$. It proves the other identity.
We will use this exact conjugacy whenever the first and second
coordinate cases are interchanged.

For determinant-one matrices $X,Y$, use the full traces
$x=\tr X$, $y=\tr Y$, $z=\tr(XY)$. Cayley--Hamilton gives
$\tr(X^2Y)=xz-y$ and $\tr(XY^2)=yz-x$. The corresponding pair
substitutions are $(X,Y)\mapsto(X,XY)$ and $(X,Y)\mapsto(XY,Y)$.
With rows for exponent vectors,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrices}
 R_A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 R_B=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 R_w=R_{\ell_s}\cdots R_{\ell_1}.
\end{equation}
These are the same lower/upper unipotent generators as in
\citet{SasakiYoshida2008}, with the stated order convention.
An adjacent pair of distinct letters has trace three.
Multiplication on either side by a nonnegative elementary
unipotent cannot decrease any entry. Thus every admissible
product has determinant one and trace at least three, so its
eigenvalues are positive reciprocal numbers, one greater than one.
This is a matrix statement, not uniform hyperbolicity of every
real surface orbit.

The four points $\E$ already form an invariant finite set.
Writing a point by its signs $(\epsilon,\eta)$ as in
\eqref{eq:locus}, the letter actions are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:E}
 A:(\epsilon,\eta)\mapsto(\epsilon,\epsilon\eta),\qquad
 B:(\epsilon,\eta)\mapsto(\epsilon\eta,\eta).
\end{equation}
Each has square the identity there. In particular a point outside
$\E$ cannot enter $\E$ under either bijection.

\subsection{Why the group and predecessor results do not suffice}\label{sec:separators}

The powers $A^6$ and $B^6$ are identities on the planes $x=1$
and $y=1$, respectively, as also follows from the explicit
rotations in Section~\ref{sec:locus}. Hence the conventional
composition $A^6\circ B^6$ fixes $(1,1,m)$ for every $m\in\Z$.
Its exponent matrix is
\[
 R_A^6R_B^6=\begin{pmatrix}1&6\\6&37\end{pmatrix}.
\]
In contrast,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:groupseparator}
 (A\circ B)(1,1,m)=(m,m-1,m^2-m-1)
\end{equation}
belongs to the forward escape cone defined below whenever $m\ge3$.
The whole-group orbit is therefore infinite. Finite-group-orbit
classifications cannot supply the periodic locus of this word.
Half-trace sources such as \citet{RobertsBaake1994} and
\citet{Humphries2016} also require doubling coordinates; an
integer lattice in half traces is not the full integer lattice
used here.

For the chronological word $ABB$, namely $B^2\circ A$, direct
application of its letters gives the four-cycle
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ABB}
 (1,m,m),\quad(-1,m,-m),\quad(1,-m,-m),\quad(-1,-m,m)
 \qquad(m\in\Z\setminus\{0\}).
\end{equation}
The four triples are distinct and have level $m^2+1$.
For $|m|\ge2$ this level family is absent from the predecessor's
Fibonacci-map periodic levels, which are of the forms $n^2$ and
$n^2-n+2$ \citep{C413}. The matrix for $ABB$ has trace four.
Even positive powers of the Fibonacci matrix have traces
$3,7,18,\ldots$, strictly increasing; odd powers have determinant
minus one. Thus this is not merely a power of that single map.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} treats the entire admissible family, not
only the separating example.
