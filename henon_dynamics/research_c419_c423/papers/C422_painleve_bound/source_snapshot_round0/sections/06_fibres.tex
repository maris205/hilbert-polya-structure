\section{Every finite fibre is integral and reduced}\label{sec:fibres}

We now prove Theorem~\ref{thm:fibres-intro}. The argument treats an
arbitrary finite fibre over the algebraic closure. There is no passage
from a generic irreducibility statement to a special fibre.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:fibres-intro}]
The morphism and its infinity fibre were constructed in
\eqref{eq:exact-fibration}. Fix any \(c\in\kkbar\), and write
the finite fibre divisor as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre-decomposition}
C_c=f_t^{-1}(c)=\sum_{j=1}^{h}n_jZ_j,\qquad n_j\ge1,
\end{equation}
with distinct integral geometric components \(Z_j\).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-poles}, this fibre is disjoint from \(D\)
and is linearly equivalent to \(rD\). Each \(Z_j\) is
therefore disjoint from \(D\). Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary-lattice}
gives
\[
Z_j\sim d_jD,\qquad d_j\in\ZZ_{>0}.
\]

This is linear equivalence in \(\Pic(S_{\kkbar})\), so there
is a rational function \(g_j\) with
\[
\divisor(g_j)=Z_j-d_jD.
\]
Since \(Z_j\cap D=\varnothing\), its polar divisor is exactly
\(d_jD\). In particular \(g_j\) is nonconstant and regular on
\(U_{\kkbar}\). Lemma~\ref{lem:pole-order} applies separately
to each component and forces \(r\mid d_j\).

Comparison of classes in \eqref{eq:fibre-decomposition} now gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:component-sum}
\sum_{j=1}^{h}n_jd_j=r.
\end{equation}
The class \(D\) is nonzero in the free Picard group, so its
integer coefficient can be compared. Every summand in
\eqref{eq:component-sum} is a positive multiple of \(r\).
Thus \(h=1\), \(n_1=1\), and \(d_1=r\).

This establishes a unique geometric component with multiplicity one.
To obtain reducedness of the whole fibre as a scheme, note that it is
an effective Cartier divisor on the smooth surface. Locally it is
cut out by a nonzerodivisor in a regular local ring, so the quotient
is Cohen--Macaulay of pure dimension one. There are no embedded
associated points. Since it is reduced at the generic point of its
unique component, a nilpotent cannot be supported at a remaining
closed point: a nonzero such submodule would have a closed associated
point. The entire fibre is therefore reduced. It is geometrically
integral.

Finally, adjunction and \(K_S=-D\), \(D^2=0\), give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre-genus}
\pa(C_c)=1+\frac{(rD)^2+(rD)K_S}{2}=1.
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-poles} already places this projective curve
entirely inside \(U\), completing all assertions.
\end{proof}

The componentwise use of linear equivalence in this proof is essential.
An invariant of degree \(rD\) alone would not exclude a sum of
smaller components; the minimal-pole obstruction excludes each smaller
positive class separately. In particular, no permutation of components
in a reducible finite fibre can create a longer exceptional orbit,
because such finite fibres do not occur. Multiple finite fibres are
excluded by the same argument.

This reasoning does not require a section of the fibration, a
connected-fibre descent theorem, generic smoothness, or the exclusion
of quasi-elliptic behaviour. None of those additional statements is
being asserted here.
