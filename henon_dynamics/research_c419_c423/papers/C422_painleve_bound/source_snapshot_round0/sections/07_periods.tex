\section{Rational points and the original least period}\label{sec:periods}

The fibre theorem turns the orbit question into a point-count problem
for a projective geometrically integral curve. Singular fibres require
a separate argument, but arithmetic genus one makes that argument short
and uniform in the characteristic.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:point-bound}
Let \(C\) be a projective geometrically integral curve of arithmetic
genus one over \(k=\F_q\), and suppose \(C(k)\ne\varnothing\).
Then
\[
\#C(k)\le q+1+2\sqrt q.
\]
If \(C\) is singular, the stronger estimate \(\#C(k)\le q+2\)
suffices.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(C\) is smooth, its rational point makes it an elliptic curve
over \(k\). Hasse's theorem gives the stated upper bound; a proof
for arbitrary finite fields is \citet[Theorem 7.3]{Sutherland2021}.
No odd-characteristic hypothesis is part of that theorem.

Suppose instead that \(C\) is singular, and let
\(\nu:\widetilde C\to C\) be its normalization. The field
\(k\) is perfect, so \(\widetilde C\) is a smooth projective
geometrically integral curve. Over \(\kkbar\), the normalization
exact sequence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normalization-sequence}
0\longrightarrow\OO_{C_{\kkbar}}
\longrightarrow\nu_*\OO_{\widetilde C_{\kkbar}}
\longrightarrow\mathcal Q\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal Q\) has finite support at the singular points.
Its local length at a geometric singular point \(Q\) is the
positive integer \(\delta_Q\). These normalization and
delta-invariant facts are recorded in
\citet[\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0C3Q}{Tag 0C3Q},
Lemmas 33.39.2--33.39.4]{stacks-project}; the genus comparison is
also discussed in
\citet[\href{https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CE0}{Tag 0CE0}]{stacks-project}.
Taking Euler characteristics in \eqref{eq:normalization-sequence}
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-genus}
1=\pa(C)=g(\widetilde C)+
\sum_{Q\in\Sing(C_{\kkbar})}\delta_Q.
\end{equation}
The curve is singular, so the sum is positive. Hence
\(g(\widetilde C)=0\), and there is exactly one geometric
singular point, with \(\delta=1\).

We always have \(\#\widetilde C(k)\le q+1\). If
\(\widetilde C(k)\) is empty, this is immediate. Otherwise,
genus-zero Riemann--Roch applied to a rational point supplies a
degree-one map to \(\PP^1_k\), so
\(\widetilde C\simeq\PP^1_k\) and the count is \(q+1\).
Normalization is an isomorphism over the smooth locus. Thus every
smooth rational point of \(C\) has a unique rational lift, and
at most one remaining rational point can be singular. It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:singular-bound}
\#C(k)\le\#\widetilde C(k)+1\le q+2
\le q+1+2\sqrt q,
\end{equation}
where the last inequality holds for every finite field, \(q\ge2\).
\end{proof}

The singular argument does not assume that the given rational point
is smooth. It also makes no assumption about split nodes or cusps.
These distinctions therefore cause no missing cases in small
characteristic.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:period}]
Proposition~\ref{prop:permutation} makes every state periodic on
the finite phase union. Let \(\ell\) be its least native period,
and let \(t\) be its starting phase. A return after \(n\) steps
requires \(s^nt=t\). Since \(t\ne0\) and \(s\) has exact
order \(r\), every return time is divisible by \(r\); in
particular \(r\mid\ell\).

The fixed-phase return map is
\[
G=F_{s^{r-1}t}\circ\cdots\circ F_{st}\circ F_t.
\]
For \(r=1\) this means \(G=F_t\). It is a bijection of
\(U_{t,s}(k)\), and \eqref{eq:invariance}, extended to every
ordinary point, shows that it preserves \(I_{r,t}\).
The starting state has a finite invariant value \(c\in k\) by
\eqref{eq:integral-regular}. Its \(G\)-orbit is contained in the
finite fibre \(C_c(k)\).

The least period under \(G\) is exactly \(\ell/r\): a return
under \(G^m\) is a native return after \(rm\) steps, and the
least positive native return is \(\ell\), itself a multiple of
\(r\). Consequently the \(\ell/r\) points of this return
orbit are distinct ordinary rational points of \(C_c\).
By Theorem~\ref{thm:fibres-intro}, this is a projective
geometrically integral curve of arithmetic genus one. Applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:point-bound} gives
\[
\frac{\ell}{r}\le\#C_c(k)\le q+1+2\sqrt q.
\]
This proves the theorem for every phase and every state in the
seven-branch domain.
\end{proof}
