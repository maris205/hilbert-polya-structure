\section{A fixed basis for every principal oldform chain}\label{sec:principal}

We now prove the local algebra needed in the real-square sectors.
Let \(p>1\) be a prime, \(e\ge0\), and \(X\in\C^\times\).
Put \(Y=p/X\), \(R=Y/X=p/X^2\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:principal-C}
 C_e(X)_{j,a}=X^{r_a-|j-a|},\qquad
 r_a=\max(a,e-a),\qquad 0\le j,a\le e.
\end{equation}
Define the rational matrix family
\[
 P_{p,e}(X)=C_e(X)^{-1}C_e(Y)
\]
generically and then meromorphically. The variables \(X,Y\) in this
section may be arbitrary nonzero complex numbers with product \(p\).
This freedom will allow \(X=p^s/\chi(p)\) in the phase calculation.

\begin{proposition}[Principal fixed basis]\label{prop:principal-basis}
For every \(p,e\), the family \(P_{p,e}(X)\) has a full basis
independent of \(X\) that diagonalizes it at every regular value.
The basis and its eigenvalues are given in the proof below.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Invertibility, reversal and the difference channels}

Let \(\ee_0,\ldots,\ee_e\) be the coordinate vectors and let
\(J\ee_a=\ee_{e-a}\). Factoring \eqref{eq:principal-C} as
\[
 C_e(X)=(X^{-|j-a|})_{j,a}\diag_a X^{r_a}
\]
and using the row elimination of \cref{lem:local-incoming} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:principal-det}
 \det C_e(X)=X^{\sum_a r_a}(1-X^{-2})^e .
\end{equation}
Also \(C_e(X)J=JC_e(X)\) because both \(r_a\) and the distance
\(|j-a|\) are preserved by simultaneous reversal.

Put \(m=\lfloor e/2\rfloor\) and \(d_a=\ee_a-\ee_{a+1}\).
For \(0\le b\le m-1\), define the fixed vectors
\begin{equation}\label{eq:difference-vectors}
 v_b^L=\sum_{a=0}^b p^a d_a,\qquad v_b^R=Jv_b^L.
\end{equation}
Direct subtraction of columns, for \(0\le a<m\), gives
\[
 (C_e(X)d_a)_j=
 \begin{cases}
 (X^2-1)X^{e-2a+j-2},&0\le j\le a,\\
 0,&j>a.
 \end{cases}
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:difference-images}
 (C_e(X)v_b^L)_j=
 \begin{cases}
 (X^2-1)X^{e+j-2}\displaystyle\sum_{a=j}^b R^a,&0\le j\le b,\\
 0,&j>b.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Replacing \(X\) by \(Y\) replaces \(R\) by \(R^{-1}\), while
\[
 \sum_{a=j}^b R^{-a}=R^{-(j+b)}\sum_{a=j}^b R^a .
\]
Substitution in \eqref{eq:difference-images}, without division by
any possibly vanishing finite sum, proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:difference-eigenvalue}
 C_e(Y)v_b^L=\lambda_b(X)C_e(X)v_b^L,\qquad
 \lambda_b(X)=\frac{Y^2-1}{X^2-1}R^{e-2-b}.
\end{equation}
The same identity holds for \(v_b^R\) by reversal. These are the
left and right difference eigenchannels of \(P_{p,e}\).

\subsection{The even central channel}

Suppose \(e=2m\), \(m\ge1\). Set
\[
 \mu_a=\varphi(p^{\min(a,e-a)}),\qquad
 v^{\mathrm{cen}}=\sum_{a=0}^{2m}\mu_a\ee_a,
\]
where \(\varphi(1)=1\).
For \(0\le j\le m\), the telescoping totient sum is
\(\sum_{a=0}^j\mu_a=p^j\).
Summing the three ranges \(a\le j\), \(j<a\le m\), and \(a>m\)
in \eqref{eq:principal-C} therefore gives, for \(0\le j<m\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:even-Q}
 (C_e(X)v^{\mathrm{cen}})_j=p^jX^jQ_{m-j}(X),\qquad
 Q_h(X)=X^{2h}+(p-1)\sum_{b=1}^{h-1}p^{b-1}X^{2h-2b}+p^h.
\end{equation}
At \(j=m\), the image is \(p^{m-1}(p+1)X^m\).
The coefficients in \eqref{eq:even-Q} give the reciprocal identity
\[
 Q_h(p/X)=p^hX^{-2h}Q_h(X).
\]
Indeed the endpoint coefficients pair \(1\) with \(p^h\), and the
interior coefficients pair by
\((p-1)p^{h-b-1}=p^{h-2b}(p-1)p^{b-1}\).
For every row \(j\le m\), it follows that
\[
 (C_e(Y)v^{\mathrm{cen}})_j
 =R^m(C_e(X)v^{\mathrm{cen}})_j .
\]
Reversal gives the other rows. Thus the central eigenvalue is \(R^m\).
When \(e=0\), the matrix is \((1)\) and the same conclusion holds
with \(m=0\) and \(v^{\mathrm{cen}}=\ee_0\).

\subsection{The two odd central channels}

Now let \(e=2m+1\), including \(m=0\), and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-vectors}
 u_L=\sum_{a=0}^m\varphi(p^a)\ee_a,\qquad
 u_R=Ju_L,\qquad v_\varepsilon=u_L+\varepsilon u_R
 \quad(\varepsilon=\pm1).
\end{equation}
For \(0\le j\le m\), set \(h=m-j\). Range summation as above gives
\begin{align}
 (C_e(X)v_\varepsilon)_j&=p^jX^jT_h^\varepsilon(X),
 \label{eq:odd-T}\\
 T_h^\varepsilon(X)&=
 X^{2h+1}+(p-1)\sum_{b=1}^h p^{b-1}X^{2h+1-2b}
       +\varepsilon p^h. \nonumber
\end{align}
The sum is empty if \(h=0\). Its value at \(X=-\varepsilon\) is
zero: the first two terms sum to \(-\varepsilon p^h\). Hence
\(U_h^\varepsilon(X)=T_h^\varepsilon(X)/(X+\varepsilon)\)
is a polynomial. Finite geometric summation also gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-U}
 U_h^\varepsilon(X)=
 \frac{X^{2h+2}-\varepsilon X^{2h+1}
       +\varepsilon p^hX-p^{h+1}}{X^2-p}.
\end{equation}
Let the numerator be \(Q(X)\). Directly,
\[
 Q(p/X)=-p^{h+1}X^{-2h-2}Q(X),\qquad
 (p/X)^2-p=-pX^{-2}(X^2-p).
\]
It follows that
\[
 U_h^\varepsilon(p/X)=p^hX^{-2h}U_h^\varepsilon(X).
\]
This is a polynomial identity after clearing powers of \(X\);
\eqref{eq:odd-U} does not introduce an exclusion at \(X^2=p\).
Restoring the factor \(X+\varepsilon\) in
\eqref{eq:odd-T} gives, first on the left half and then by reversal,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:central-eigenvalues}
 C_e(Y)v_\varepsilon
 =\lambda_\varepsilon(X)C_e(X)v_\varepsilon,\qquad
 \lambda_\varepsilon(X)=R^m\frac{Y+\varepsilon}{X+\varepsilon}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Completeness and fixed cusp normalization}

We finish the proof of \cref{prop:principal-basis}.
The vectors \(v_b^L\) span the same space as
\(d_0,\ldots,d_{m-1}\): their coefficient matrix is triangular with
nonzero diagonal entries \(p^b\). The right-hand statement follows
by reversal.

For \(e=2m\), the left difference space consists of vectors supported
on \([0,m]\) with coordinate sum zero. The right difference space
has the analogous support \([m,2m]\). A vector in their intersection
would be supported only at \(m\) and have sum zero, so their
intersection is trivial. Their sum has dimension \(2m\), and
\(v^{\mathrm{cen}}\), whose coordinate sum is positive, lies outside
it. These \(e+1\) vectors form a fixed basis.

For \(e=2m+1\), the support intervals \([0,m]\) and
\([m+1,2m+1]\) are disjoint. Each difference space has codimension
one on its interval. The respective vectors \(u_L,u_R\), both of
coordinate sum \(p^m\), give the missing complements; their sum
and difference are independent. Again there are \(e+1\) fixed basis
vectors. Equations \eqref{eq:difference-eigenvalue} and
\eqref{eq:central-eigenvalues}, and the even central calculation,
give all eigenvalues. Inverting \(C_e(X)\) generically proves the
diagonalization. Meromorphic continuation proves it at every regular
value. Coincident eigenvalues do not affect independence of the
explicit basis. This proves the proposition. \(\square\)

For clarity, this principal matrix has exactly the width-one
normalization already present in \cref{lem:local-incoming}.
A direct cusp calculation gives the same answer. At the cusp
\(u/p^a\), put \(w_a=p^{e-\min(2a,e)}\) and choose
\(\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)\) with first column \((u,p^a)^T\).
Then
\(\sigma=\gamma\diag(\sqrt{w_a},1/\sqrt{w_a})\) is width-one.
The dilation \(z\mapsto p^jz\) has determinant-one matrix
\(\alpha_j=\diag(p^{j/2},p^{-j/2})\). With
\(g=p^{\min(a,j)}\), the first column of \(\alpha_j\sigma\)
is \(g\sqrt{w_a/p^j}\) times the primitive column
\((p^ju/g,p^a/g)^T\). Reducing that column by a matrix in
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)\) leaves an upper triangular matrix whose
height multiplier is
\[
 p^{e-\min(2a,e)+2\min(a,j)-j}
 =p^{r_a-|j-a|}.
\]
Thus the incoming coefficient of the level-one oldform \(E(p^jz,s)\)
is \(C_e(p^s)_{j,a}\); its outgoing coefficient is
\(\phi_1(s)C_e(p^{1-s})_{j,a}\), where \(\phi_1\) is the
level-one scattering scalar. This is also the principal specialization
of \citet[Lemma 2.19]{BookerLeeStrombergsson2020}; the different
normalization of the primitive-pair series there is a common factor
two that cancels.

The number of cusps in class \(a\) is \(\mu_a\). If the class sum
of cusp series is divided by \(\sqrt{\mu_a}\), then the reduced
row scattering matrix becomes
\[
 \phi_1(s)D_\mu^{-1/2}P_{p,e}(p^s)D_\mu^{1/2},
 \qquad D_\mu=\diag(\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_e).
\]
Both normalizing matrices are fixed. At a composite principal level,
\[
 C_N(s)_{d,c}
 =\left(\frac{N\gcd(d,c)^2}{d\gcd(c^2,N)}\right)^s
 =\bigotimes_{p\mid N} C_{v_p(N)}(p^s),
\]
so the principal sector has the tensor product of the bases just
proved. This statement concerns only that sector; it does not
discard the nonprincipal blocks in \cref{prop:paired-blocks}.
