\section{Real squares, local phases and tensor assembly}\label{sec:parity}

After \cref{prop:nonreal-obstruction}, every remaining character is
real or has order four. We first isolate a tensor fact that prevents
several noncommuting local factors from cancelling each other.

\begin{lemma}[Tensor commutation]\label{lem:tensor}
Let \(A_1(s),\ldots,A_h(s)\) be meromorphic matrix families over
\(\C\), all generically invertible, and set
\(A(s)=\bigotimes_{j=1}^h A_j(s)\). Then \(A(s)\) is pairwise
commuting as a meromorphic family if and only if every \(A_j(s)\)
is pairwise commuting.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Sufficiency follows from multiplication of tensor products.
For necessity, choose \(s_0\) where all matrices are holomorphic
and invertible, and work in a small connected neighborhood of
\((s_0,s_0)\). Tensor commutativity gives
\[
 \bigotimes_j R_j(s,t)=I,\qquad
 R_j(s,t)=A_j(s)A_j(t)\bigl(A_j(t)A_j(s)\bigr)^{-1}.
\]
Taking the trace shows that each \(\tr R_j\) is nonzero:
their product is the positive dimension of the tensor space.
Taking the partial trace over all factors except \(j\) then makes
\(R_j\) scalar. Since \(\det R_j=1\), that scalar belongs to the
finite set of roots of unity of order dividing \(\dim A_j\).
It is continuous, so is constant on the chosen neighborhood.
Its value at \((s_0,s_0)\) is one. Thus the local commutators vanish
on an open set and therefore identically by meromorphic
continuation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Real characters}

If \(\chi=\bar\chi\), each unramified value is \(c=\pm1\).
With \(D_c=\diag(1,c,\ldots,c^e)\), the incoming factor satisfies
\[
 C_{\chi,p}(s)=D_c C_e(p^s)D_c,
 \qquad D_c^2=I.
\]
This follows from \(c^{|j-a|}=c^{j+a}\).
Hence the local scattering factor is a fixed conjugate of
\(P_{p,e}(p^s)\), and it commutes by \cref{prop:principal-basis}.
Every ramified factor is diagonal by \eqref{eq:ramified-C}.
The single character sector in \cref{prop:paired-blocks} is thus
a scalar multiple of a commuting tensor product for every \(L\).
No restriction on the oldform exponents has been introduced.

\subsection{A constant reduction of the quartic paired sector}

Suppose \(\chi\ne\bar\chi\) but \(\chi^2=\bar\chi^2\).
Then \(\chi\) has order four. The factors \(a_\chi\) and
\(a_{\bar\chi}\) have a common meromorphic factor \(k_\chi(s)\)
and the reciprocal constants
\[
 a_\chi(s)=k_\chi(s)\frac{\tau(\chi)}{\tau(\bar\chi)},\qquad
 a_{\bar\chi}(s)=k_\chi(s)\frac{\tau(\bar\chi)}{\tau(\chi)}.
\]
This is immediate from \eqref{eq:completion} because the two
square \(L\)-functions coincide. The common factor is not
identically zero. Conjugation by the fixed block diagonal matrix
\(\diag(\tau(\chi)/\tau(\bar\chi)\,I_d,I_d)\) removes the two
constant ratios from \eqref{eq:paired-block}.

For \(p\nmid q\), put
\[
 J_p=\diag_{0\le b\le e_p}(\chi(p)^2)^b,\qquad e_p=v_p(L);
\]
for \(p\mid q\), set \(J_p=I\).
Equations \eqref{eq:ramified-C} and \eqref{eq:unramified-C} give
\[
 C_{\bar\chi,p}(s)=J_pC_{\chi,p}(s)J_p.
\]
Here the parameter \(s\) is not conjugated.
With \(J=\bigotimes_{p\mid N}J_p\), we have
\(U_{\bar\chi}(s)=JU_\chi(s)J\).
The further fixed similarity \(\diag(I_d,J)\) changes the paired
block, after division by \(k_\chi\), into
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0&\mathcal A_\chi(s)\\
                 \mathcal A_\chi(s)&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-A}
 \mathcal A_\chi(s)=U_\chi(s)J
 =C_\chi(s)^{-1}JC_\chi(1-s)
 =\bigotimes_{p\mid N}
       \bigl(C_{\chi,p}(s)^{-1}J_pC_{\chi,p}(1-s)\bigr).
\end{equation}
A fixed two-block Walsh transform gives
\(\mathcal A_\chi(s)\oplus(-\mathcal A_\chi(s))\).
Thus the quartic paired sector commutes exactly when
\(\mathcal A_\chi(s)\) does. Every factor in \eqref{eq:global-A}
is generically invertible. All coordinate transformations used
in this reduction are constant.

\subsection{The phase identity and its parity boundary}

Fix an unramified prime. Write \(e=v_p(L)\), \(c=\chi(p)\),
\(x=p^s\), \(y=p^{1-s}\), \(r_a=\max(a,e-a)\), and
\[
 C_c(x)_{j,a}=c^{|j-a|}x^{r_a-|j-a|}.
\]
Since the square is real, \(c\in\{1,-1,i,-i\}\).
Define the constant diagonal matrices
\[
 \kappa=c^2,\qquad
 D=\diag_j\kappa^j,\qquad
 H=\diag_a c^{r_a},\qquad
 F=\diag_a\kappa^{r_a+a}.
\]
The local factor of \eqref{eq:global-A} is
\(A_c(s)=C_c(x)^{-1}DC_c(y)\).

\begin{lemma}[Fixed phase identity]\label{lem:phase-identity}
With \(X=x/c\), one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase-identity}
 A_c(s)=H^{-1}P_{p,e}(X)F H.
\end{equation}
If \(c=\pm1\), or if \(c=\pm i\) and \(e\) is even, then \(F=I\).
If \(c=\pm i\) and \(e=2m+1\), then \(F\) is minus the identity
on coordinates \(0,\ldots,m\) and the identity on
\(m+1,\ldots,2m+1\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put \(V=y/c\). The entries give
\[
 C_c(x)=C_e(X)H,\qquad C_c(y)=C_e(V)H.
\]
Because \(|j-a|\equiv j+a\pmod2\) and \(\kappa=\pm1\),
\[
 DC_e(V)=C_e(\kappa V)F.
\]
Also \(X\kappa V=p\). Substitution yields
\eqref{eq:phase-identity}.
If \(\kappa=1\), \(F=I\). If \(\kappa=-1\), the exponent
\(r_a+a\) equals \(e\) on the left half of the index interval
and \(2a\) on the right half. This gives the asserted even
and odd cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact local obstruction]\label{prop:local-parity}
For real-square data, the local factor \(A_c(s)\) is pairwise
commuting except precisely when \(c=\pm i\) and \(e\) is odd.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
When \(F=I\), \cref{lem:phase-identity,prop:principal-basis}
give a fixed diagonalizing basis, hence commutativity.

Let \(c=\pm i\), \(e=2m+1\). On the left and right difference
eigenlines of \eqref{eq:difference-vectors}, \(F\) acts by
\(-1\) and \(+1\), respectively. On the central space
\eqref{eq:odd-vectors}, it satisfies
\[
 Fv_+=-v_-,\qquad Fv_-=-v_+.
\]
Consequently the matrix of \(P_{p,e}(X)F\) in the fixed central
basis \((v_+,v_-)\) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-obstruction-block}
 \begin{pmatrix}0&-\lambda_+(X)\\
                 -\lambda_-(X)&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \lambda_\pm(X)=\left(\frac{p}{X^2}\right)^m
                    \frac{p/X\pm1}{X\pm1}.
\end{equation}
Neither off-diagonal entry is identically zero. Their ratio is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
 \rho_p(X)=\frac{(p+X)(X-1)}{(p-X)(X+1)}.
\end{equation}
If this rational function were a constant \(k\), comparison
of quadratic coefficients would give \(k=-1\), whereas comparison
of linear coefficients would give \(p-1=-(p-1)\).
This is impossible for \(p>1\). Since \(X=p^s/c\) has nonzero
derivative and takes values in an open set locally, the composed
meromorphic ratio is nonconstant.

For an off-diagonal family
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&a(s)\\b(s)&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\)
with neither entry identically zero, pairwise commutativity is
equivalent to
\(a(s)b(t)=a(t)b(s)\), or equivalently to constancy of \(a/b\).
Equation \eqref{eq:rho} therefore proves noncommutativity on a
fixed invariant two-dimensional subspace. The whole local
factor cannot commute.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The full character criterion}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:characters}]
If the full scattering family commutes, then
\cref{prop:nonreal-obstruction} gives real squares for every
primitive conductor. Real characters satisfy
\eqref{eq:phase-condition} automatically. In a quartic sector,
the constant reductions leading to \eqref{eq:global-A} preserve
commutator vanishing; division by the common nonzero meromorphic
scalar also preserves its being an identity. By \cref{lem:tensor},
each local factor must commute. Ramified factors are diagonal,
while \cref{prop:local-parity} excludes exactly the imaginary
unramified values at odd \(e_p\). For \(p\nmid q\),
\(e_p=v_p(N)\). This proves \eqref{eq:phase-condition}.

Conversely, assume \eqref{eq:real-square-condition} and
\eqref{eq:phase-condition} for all primitive conductors.
Each real sector commutes by the first part of this section.
In a quartic sector, every local factor of
\eqref{eq:global-A} commutes by \cref{prop:local-parity}, so
their tensor product commutes. Reversing the constant similarities
shows that the actual paired block commutes. The Fourier
decomposition in \cref{prop:paired-blocks} exhausts the cusp
space, proving commutativity of the whole family.

All arguments with inverses and quotients were made first on
generic open sets. The resulting commutator is a meromorphic
matrix identity in \((s,t)\). It therefore holds at every pair
of regular values of the actual \(\Phi_N\), including values at
which an intermediate formula has a removable singularity.
In the necessity direction, an assumed identity at all regular
pairs is a meromorphic identity, so the same generic arguments
apply. No exceptional regular parameter is excluded.
\end{proof}

The tensor argument also explains why two odd-exponent imaginary
phases cannot repair one another. It does not require independence
of logarithms of distinct primes: generic invertibility, partial
traces and the finite set of scalar roots of unity suffice.
