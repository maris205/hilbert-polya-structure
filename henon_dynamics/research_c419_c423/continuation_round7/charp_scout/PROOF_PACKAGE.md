## Assumptions and notation

Let $p$ be any prime, $e\ge1$, $Q=p^e$, and work on the ordinary point set
$\mathbb F_Q^3$. Put $\Delta=xz+y^2$. The native map is

$$
\mathcal N=\mathcal N_1,
\qquad
\mathcal N_a(x,y,z)=
(x-2ay\Delta-a^2z\Delta^2,\ y+az\Delta,\ z)
\quad(a\in\mathbb F_Q).
$$

Tame means generated by affine and elementary triangular polynomial
automorphisms. We will use only individual elementary shears, so no
classification theorem about tame groups is required.

## Strategy and dependency map

The full result depends on four hand steps:

1. Verify preservation of $\Delta$ and the additive composition law.
2. Count the fixed locus as an ordinary set, separating $p=2$.
3. Recover all cycle lengths and all iterate counts from order dividing $p$.
4. Exhibit shears with identical fixed counts and cycle lengths over every
   extension. Equality of data does not assert equality of the maps.

No external theorem is a mathematical input to these four steps. Prior
ownership of the action and of stronger finite-field mimicking phenomena
is deducted separately in [the source audit](SOURCE_AUDIT.md).

## Proof

### Step 1: invariant and composition

Write $(X,Y,Z)=\mathcal N_a(x,y,z)$. Expansion gives the polynomial identity

$$
XZ+Y^2
=xz-2ayz\Delta-a^2z^2\Delta^2
 +y^2+2ayz\Delta+a^2z^2\Delta^2
=\Delta.
$$

Consequently, applying $\mathcal N_b$ after $\mathcal N_a$ changes the
second coordinate to $y+(a+b)z\Delta$ and the first coordinate to

$$
x-2(a+b)y\Delta-(a^2+2ab+b^2)z\Delta^2.
$$

Thus $\mathcal N_b\circ\mathcal N_a=\mathcal N_{a+b}$ in every
characteristic; $\mathcal N_0$ is the identity and $\mathcal N_{-a}$ is the
inverse of $\mathcal N_a$. In particular
$\mathcal N^n=\mathcal N_{n\cdot1}$ and $\mathcal N^p=\mathrm{id}$.

### Step 2: all ordinary fixed points

The fixed equations for $\mathcal N$ are

$$z\Delta=0,\qquad \Delta(2y+z\Delta)=0.$$

If $p$ is odd and $\Delta\ne0$, the first equation forces $z=0$, and the
second then forces $y=0$. This contradicts $\Delta=y^2\ne0$.
Hence the fixed point set is exactly $\{\Delta=0\}$. When $z\ne0$, each
of the $Q$ choices of $y$ determines a unique $x=-y^2/z$, giving
$Q(Q-1)$ points. When $z=0$, one must have $y=0$, leaving $Q$ choices
of $x$. The total is $Q^2$.

If $p=2$, the first fixed equation $z\Delta=0$ already implies the second,
which becomes $z\Delta^2=0$. The subset $z=0$ contributes $Q^2$ points.
For $z\ne0$, the equation $\Delta=0$ contributes $Q(Q-1)$ points as
above. These subsets are disjoint. Define

$$
f_p(Q)=\#\operatorname{Fix}(\mathcal N)=
\begin{cases}
Q^2,&p\ne2,\\
2Q^2-Q,&p=2.
\end{cases}
$$

### Step 3: every native return and primitive cycle

Since $\mathcal N$ is a permutation and its $p$th power is the identity,
each ordinary orbit has length dividing the prime $p$. There are exactly
$f_p(Q)$ singleton orbits, and all other orbits have length $p$.
Therefore, for every $n\ge1$,

$$
N_{p,e}(n)=
\begin{cases}
Q^3,&p\mid n,\\
f_p(Q),&p\nmid n,
\end{cases}
$$

and the complete cycle multiplicities are

$$
c_1=f_p(Q),\qquad
c_p=\frac{Q^3-f_p(Q)}p,\qquad
c_m=0\quad(m\notin\{1,p\}).
$$

The ordinary permutation zeta, with the original native clock, is

$$
Z_{p,e}(u)=
(1-u)^{-f_p(Q)}(1-u^p)^{-(Q^3-f_p(Q))/p}.
$$

The exponent is an integer because the nonfixed points are partitioned
into $p$-cycles. This also covers $p=2,Q=2$ without an omitted exception.

### Step 4: one tame comparator per prime, uniform in every extension

For odd $p$, take $T_p(x,y,z)=(x+y,y,z)$. It is an elementary shear,
$T_p^p=\mathrm{id}$, and its fixed set $y=0$ has $Q^2$ points.

For $p=2$, take $T_2(x,y,z)=(x+yz,y,z)$. It is an elementary shear,
$T_2^2=\mathrm{id}$, and its fixed set is $yz=0$. There are
$2Q-1$ choices of $(y,z)$ in this union of two coordinate lines and
$Q$ choices of $x$, giving $2Q^2-Q$ fixed points.

These maps are defined over $\mathbb F_p$, independent of $e$ and $n$.
Every nonfixed orbit of either comparator has length $p$. Their full cycle
types and all return zetas consequently agree with those of $\mathcal N$
for every finite extension and every native time. This proves the frozen
negative answer. $\square$

## Corrections or missing assumptions

No extra assumption was needed for N7. The conclusion compares only ordinary
cycle data. It neither constructs one global polynomial conjugacy nor says
that a tame map equals $\mathcal N$ on the algebraic closure.

For P7, the source's monodromy invariant is not an all-parameter proof of
the geometry of its dynamical fibres. To use a curve point bound uniformly,
one still needs a justified identification of the relevant invariant
normalizations, their genus and all singular-fibre/orbit contributions.
The map from a spectral curve or its quotient to these dynamical fibres is
not supplied by merely writing their spectral equation. This is an
identified missing proof step, not a disproof of the source conjecture.

For R7, a fixed-point trace identity is insufficient without a complete
finite-size spectral/soliton parametrization and its multiplicities.
Existing unidirectional translation sectors cannot be substituted for all
Boolean configurations. Nor do stochastic-boundary decay modes yield the
closed-ring temporal spectrum. No general completeness or count theorem
is proved here.
