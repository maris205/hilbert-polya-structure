## Primary-source receipts and ownership

### P7: Joshi–Roffelsen

[Arithmetic dynamics of a discrete Painlevé equation, arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18578v2),
revised 2026-01-16: accessed introduction, Conjecture 1.2, Definitions
2.1–2.2, Algorithm 1, Theorem 3.1 and its monodromy construction/proof,
Conjecture 3.6, Remarks 3.7–3.8 and conclusion. The invariant is a theorem;
the uniform period bound and general fibre-genus claim are labelled
conjectures in this body. Low-order Magma checks are not all-order proofs.
The exact state update is transcribed in the frozen contract.

The [v2 metadata abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18578) uses stronger
language than those explicit body labels. This inconsistency is retained,
not silently resolved in favour of a theorem. The
[publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8121/ae67bf) was robots-denied;
indexed publisher text is not treated as an independently read final proof.
The published numbering need not match the arXiv HTML numbering.

The proposed increment would be a proof of the already-owned full bound,
not discovery of the map, invariant, state space or conjecture. No such proof
was found or supplied in this bounded pass. This does not certify global
open status as of today.

[On integrals of non-autonomous dynamical systems in finite characteristic,
2602.09298v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09298v1): accessed introduction,
the stated object definitions, the difference-first-Painlevé reduction in
§5, and conclusion. Its principal systems are differential PIV and
difference PII, with a difference-PI reduction. Those are not automatically
the frozen multiplicative q-PI state system; its conclusion describes an
analogous invariant construction rather than proving P7's full period bound.

The [author's writing page](https://proffelsen.github.io/writing) lists
*Geometric addition on high-degree elliptic curves* as submitted work with
Joshi and Latimer. No accessible manuscript was linked there or found by
the exact-title query. Its contents, relevance and conclusions are unknown
here; the title cannot close P7's gap.

### N7: Maubach–Willems

[Polynomial automorphisms over finite fields: Mimicking non-tame and tame
maps by the Derksen group, 0912.3387v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0912.3387):
accessed §2's tame-map definitions, Definition 3.1, Example 3.2 and
Corollary 3.6 with surrounding explanation. The example includes the exact
Nagata map. The corollary owns finite-extension-dependent tame mimicking
for three-variable automorphisms fixing a variable. Its comparator may
depend on the extension degree; it is not a claim of one tame polynomial
equal to Nagata on the algebraic closure.

N7 asks the weaker equality of cycle data, but with one comparator per
characteristic uniform in all extensions. The hand proof supplies this
distinction explicitly. It is a short consequence of the classical
additive-action mechanism, not a substantive new wildness detector.

[Metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.3387) gives submission 2009-12-17 and
v1. The accessed PDF has that arXiv stamp but an internal date 2018-10-17.
That visible date discrepancy is recorded; no provenance inference beyond
the actual version stamp is needed by the elementary proof. A separate
CCSENET Nagata PDF locator failed and is not a proof input.

### R7: reversible Rule 54 and finite-system completeness limits

[Buča–Klobas–Prosen, 2103.16543, accessed PDF v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.16543):
accessed introduction/model definition, §§3.3 and 4.2, equation (68) and
its surrounding conjecture language, §7.1, and relevant bibliography.
This owns the nonlinear staggered model and soliton/conserved-charge
framework. Its boundary-driven spectral result is not the periodic-ring
permutation problem. The displayed higher-orbital extension is expressly
conjectural there. Its infinite-chain correlation formulas are a different
observable from $A_L(n)$.

[Friedman–Gopalakrishnan–Vasseur, 1905.03265, accessed PDF v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.03265):
accessed the main model/FFA-limit discussion and equations (4)–(6);
supplement §I's two-particle and general-sector discussion, equations
(14a)–(14c), and §II's equations (38)–(41). At zero dispersion this is
the same classical gate. Reversing which parity updates first only
conjugates the two full-step permutations by a half-step, so it cannot
create new cycle counts.

The main text states finite-system spectral completeness. However, the
supplement before (14) explains that general recurrence quantization was
inferred from small sectors and checked numerically; after (39) it again
reports finite-size checks. This audit therefore records strong prior
ownership of the proposed parametrization, not a separately verified
all-size completeness proof with every degeneracy and multiplicity.
No claim that those authors' results are false is made. Merely assuming
their parametrization and rewriting a trace as a zeta would not be a new
mechanism; proving an actual residual gap needs its own complete argument.

[The 2026 decorated-Rule-54 preprint, 2605.18622v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18622v1):
accessed §II's model and hard-core sector, §III.2–3's finite translation
orbits, and their explicit scope. It treats a protected subspace, not all
classical configurations. Its one-way soliton/translation sector is not a
replacement answer to R7.

[Bobenko–Bordemann–Gunn–Pinkall, author-listed 1993 paper](https://page.math.tu-berlin.de/~bobenko/papers/1993_Bob_Bor_Gun_Pin.pdf):
the author page and eight-page scan were located. Extraction returned no
text, and page-image requests did not expose readable page content in this
session. Thus no theorem or proof from that scan is claimed as read.
The DOI access also failed. Later primary sources above identify the model's
1993 origin; the original abstract's complete-solution claim is not used
to fill a modern all-period counting gap.
