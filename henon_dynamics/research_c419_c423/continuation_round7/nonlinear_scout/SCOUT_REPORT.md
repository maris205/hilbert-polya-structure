## AY7: all-parameter ordinary integral Adler–Yamilov returns

### Exact object, full family and native domain

For every $k\in\mathbb Z$, freeze
$$
Y_k(p,q,r,s)=\left(r-\frac{kp}{1+ps},\ s,\ p,
                       q+\frac{ks}{1+ps}\right).
$$
This is the Adler–Yamilov map of Kassotakis, Example 2.4, with
$k=a-b$. The map itself has only this effective parameter; taking
$(a,b)=(k,0)$ loses no map in the displayed family. Its inverse is
$$
Y_k^{-1}(p,q,r,s)=\left(r,\ s-\frac{kq}{1+rq},
                              p+\frac{kr}{1+rq},\ q\right).
$$
The native ordinary domain is the maximal two-sided subset of $\mathbb Q^4$
on which these **displayed** forward and inverse formulas remain defined
at every iterate. Thus their denominators must not vanish. Even at $k=0$
we do not silently fill the displayed holes. Zero coordinates otherwise
remain in scope; resolving a pole on another variety is not an ordinary
point of this contract.

One application of $Y_k$ is one native step. The observable is the full set
of states whose periodic orbit has all four coordinates in $\mathbb Z$,
with exact least native period and actual state coordinates. Cyclic
rotation may present an orbit once, but signs, inverse time, coordinate
permutations and parameter exchanges do not change the claimed clock.

### Complete question and ownership deduction

**every** $k\in\mathbb Z$, including all exceptional parameter and
zero-coordinate branches, with exact least periods and no parameter,
height or period cutoff. A program which enumerates a fresh finite box

The exact map, its refactorization origin, Liouville integrability, and
the two invariants
$$
I_1=pq+rs,\qquad
I_2=pqrs+ps+qr+krs
$$
are inherited, not new. For the fixed displayed map and $k=a-b$, the
second formula above is the specialization of Konstantinou-Rizos–Mikhailov,

### Frozen cheap check and result

The decisive first check is whether the denominator argument really fails
on zero-coordinate channels. For an integral transition, $D=1+ps$ divides
$k$, since $D\mid kp$ and $\gcd(D,p)=1$. For $k\ne0$, when both $p$ and
$s$ are nonzero this bounds them by $|k|+1$.

The coordinator supplied a sharper zero-channel completion during this
screen; the author checked its signs and indices and supplied a telescoping
proof excluding the identically-zero channel. The resulting helper is
$$
\max(|p|,|q|,|r|,|s|)\le \max(|k|+1,4)
\quad(k\ne0)
$$
on every integral periodic orbit. Its complete short proof is in
[CHEAP_CHECKS.md](CHEAP_CHECKS.md). This is a coordinated hand derivation,
**not an independent nonauthor review**. The author's earlier weaker
height estimate is not counted as another result.

This resolves the initial zero-channel worry, but also removes the most
immediate claim to a substantial new algorithm: the entire fixed-$k$
finite algorithm is now a short consequence. No graph was executed.

**Replacement boundary.** Do not run a larger coefficient or height census,
do not rename this height lemma an atlas, and do not promote the finite
algorithm. Continue AY7 only if a new all-$k$ cycle-classification mechanism
is first formulated and independently justified. At this stopping point
the full question is `NOT CURRENTLY JUSTIFIED`.

## HK7: ordinary integral returns of the general discrete Euler top

### Exact object, full family and native domain

For every $\delta=(\delta_1,\delta_2,\delta_3)\in\mathbb Z^3$, put
$$
A_\delta(x)=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-\delta_1x_3&-\delta_1x_2\\
-\delta_2x_3&1&-\delta_2x_1\\
-\delta_3x_2&-\delta_3x_1&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad E_\delta(x)=A_\delta(x)^{-1}x.
$$
Equivalently, for cyclic $(i,j,k)$ the update $X=E_\delta(x)$ satisfies
$$
X_i-x_i=\delta_i(X_jx_k+x_jX_k).
$$
The denominator of the matrix update is
$$
D_\delta(x)=1-\delta_2\delta_3x_1^2
-\delta_3\delta_1x_2^2-\delta_1\delta_2x_3^2
-2\delta_1\delta_2\delta_3x_1x_2x_3.
$$
The inverse is $E_{-\delta}$. Freeze the maximal two-sided rational domain
where both displayed matrix updates are defined at all iterates. In
particular, a point with $D_\delta=0$ is not inserted through cancellation
or a resolution of indeterminacy. All coordinates and all zero parameter
components otherwise remain in scope. One application is one native step;
the observable is the entire integral periodic locus and its least periods.

This is the arbitrary-parameter discretization used explicitly in
[Petrera–Suris, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4382), not an assumption
that every integer triple comes from positive physical moments of inertia.

### Complete question and ownership deduction

Classify every ordinary integral periodic orbit for every integer triple
$\delta$, with exact least period, including nonzero triples, zero-component
faces, axes and singular invariant levels that remain in the native domain.
No finite parameter set, period prefix or generic-fibre exception is
permitted in a complete answer.

The discretization, birational inverse, invariant ratios
$$
F_i=\frac{1-\delta_k\delta_i x_j^2}
          {1-\delta_i\delta_j x_k^2}
$$
where defined, and the classical integrability mechanisms are inherited.
Generic elliptic parametrization plus a rational torsion theorem is a
reduction, not an arithmetic atlas with all degenerate fibres treated.
The ratio denominators are not added as new exclusions to the native map
domain. [Petrera–Suris, Proposition 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4382)
Their introduction also identifies a related 1951 Jonas map as an earlier
elliptic-integrable precursor. That older paper was not directly accessed;

mechanism itself is new.

Saito–Saitoh derive periodic invariant varieties for low periods and an
axially symmetric rotation formula. Those formulas are important prior

physical-parameter hypotheses and ordinary poles must be checked before

## PG7: integral periodic arrays of the labelled pentagram corner map

### Exact object, full family and native domain

For every integer $n\ge4$, let $i\in\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ and freeze
$$
T_n^*x_i=x_i\frac{1-x_{i-1}y_{i-1}}{1-x_{i+1}y_{i+1}},\qquad
T_n^*y_i=y_{i+1}\frac{1-x_{i+2}y_{i+2}}{1-x_i y_i}.
$$
These are the right-labelled, **one-step** corner-coordinate formulas in
Ovsienko–Schwartz–Tabachnikov, Lemma 2.4. To fix the reverse-domain convention
as well, for an input array $(X_i,Y_i)$ set $\psi_i=1-X_iY_{i-1}$; the
displayed inverse is
$$
x_i=X_i\frac{\psi_{i+1}}{\psi_{i-1}},\qquad
y_i=Y_{i-1}\frac{\psi_{i-1}}{\psi_{i+1}}.
$$
Indeed these formulas give $1-x_iy_i=\psi_i$, and substitution in the
forward formulas returns $(X_i,Y_i)$ wherever the displayed denominators
are nonzero. The native arithmetic chart starts
with rational $x_i,y_i\notin\{0,1\}$ and $1-x_i y_i\ne0$ for every $i$;
retain its maximal two-sided subset on which this map and its birational
inverse are defined and stay in the chart. This agrees with the
nondegenerate corner-chart convention used in the 2025 source. The
contract concerns the displayed coordinate map, not an unproved extension
to every degenerate geometric polygon.

The integer observable is the full periodic set whose every corner
coordinate at every iterate is an integer, with exact least native period.
It is **not** a claim that projective vertex coordinates are integers.
There is no quotient by cyclic relabelling, reflection, scaling or
projective equivalence beyond the fixed corner-coordinate definition.
Twisted polygons are in scope; closedness is not imposed. In particular,
the familiar closed-pentagon/hexagon periodicity is not substituted for
this problem. [OST, Sections 2.1–2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.5605)

### Complete question and ownership deduction

Give a structural classification, for every $n\ge4$, of all those periodic
integer arrays, their exceptional families and exact least periods,
without a size, product, height or period cutoff. An algorithm that constructs
a new finite graph for every invariant value is not the intended answer.

The map, scaling symmetry, product/Casimir invariants, Poisson structure
and integrability are prior. More strongly, Weinreich's Theorem 1.4
identifies generic invariant varieties with Jacobians: for odd $n$ the
native map is a translation; for even $n$ it exchanges a pair and its
**second** iterate is a translation. These dimensions are $n-1$ and
$n-2$, respectively, not a uniform elliptic-curve problem. Birational
generic statements do not classify the omitted ordinary exceptional
fibres or the integrality of every iterate.
[Weinreich, Theorem 1.4 and Remark 1.5](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/8D3E1A5A55AB36D89F17B29B8F98E04B/S0143385722000827a.pdf/algebraic_dynamics_of_the_pentagram_map.pdf)

The 2025 cluster/dimer identification is also inherited. Reconstructing
that integrable mechanism or a finite-type cluster identity is not an
increment here. [Affolter–George–Ramassamy, Sections 1.2 and 5](https://sigma-journal.com/2025/040/sigma25-040.pdf)

The potentially meaningful increment would be a size-uniform arithmetic
cycle-family/exhaustion theorem, with a correct native-clock treatment
of the even-size exchange and all surviving exceptional fibres. That is
not supplied by generic Jacobian translation or the following observation.

### Frozen cheap check and result

For every $n$, telescoping the displayed formulas preserves
$$
P=\prod_i x_i,\qquad Q=\prod_i y_i.
$$
On a nonzero integral orbit each $x_i$ is a signed divisor of $P$ and each
$y_i$ a signed divisor of $Q$. Thus there is a finite graph of ordered
signed factorizations for each fixed $(n,P,Q)$; its valid cycles determine
that invariant slice. The short proof and the restriction to native
ordinary edges are in [CHEAP_CHECKS.md](CHEAP_CHECKS.md).

This disposes of the simplest effective-finiteness proposal. It gives no
uniform structural list across all product values and all sizes, and
does not solve the high-dimensional torsion or exceptional-fibre problem.
No factorization graph was executed.

**Replacement boundary.** Stop PG7 here unless a substantive new
size-uniform arithmetic theorem is first proposed. Do not count the
product-bound graph, generic Jacobian translation, closed-polygon
periodicity, or an extra size sample as the full question. Present status:
`NOT CURRENTLY JUSTIFIED`.
