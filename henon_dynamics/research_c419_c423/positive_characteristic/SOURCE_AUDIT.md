## Selected external primary sources

### S1. Minimal ramification

Karl-Olof Lindahl and Juan Rivera-Letelier, *Generic parabolic points are
isolated in positive characteristic*. [arXiv metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.03965)
records v1 on 2015-01-16 and latest v2 on 2016-02-25; it supplies the related
DOI [10.1088/0951-7715/29/5/1596](https://doi.org/10.1088/0951-7715/29/5/1596).
The actual [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1501.03965v2) was accessed,
including the introduction, definitions and Theorem D.

Use: Theorem D's odd-characteristic minimality test requires nonzero
iterative residue with the prescribed first index. Our $i_0=1$ germ has
residue zero, so that theorem does not close PC-F. It does own the minimal
case used by earlier local scouts. No claim is made that its periodic-point
isolation theorem classifies our exceptional all-parameter tower.

### S2. Nonminimal/high-multiplicity ramification

Jonas Nordqvist, *Wildly ramified power series with large multiplicity*.
[Metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10782) records v1 on 2019-09-24 and
latest v3 on 2020-01-07. The official
[publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022314X21000548)
gives *Journal of Number Theory* 225 (2021), 174–197,
DOI 10.1016/j.jnt.2021.01.019. Publisher metadata/abstract was read.

The [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1909.10782v2) was initially opened,
then [v3 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1909.10782v3) was accessed after
checking current metadata. Relevant reads: definitions, Theorems A/B,
the Laubie–Saïne restatement and references. The versions are not conflated:
v3 Theorem B restricts its index $j$, unlike the broader displayed v2 text.

Use: the displayed starting-index assumption is larger than $p$, not our
initial $i_0=1$. Applying it after a $p$-iterate still requires exceptional
coefficient/residue classification. No such classification is presumed.

### S3. Laubie–Saïne: limited access, no unsafe formula import

François Laubie and Mustapha Saïne, *Ramification of some automorphisms of
local fields*, *Journal of Number Theory* 72(2) (1998), 174–182. This
bibliography is present in S2; the DOI
[10.1006/jnth.1998.2273](https://doi.org/10.1006/jnth.1998.2273) was also
identified in the institutional NUMDAM record. The direct
[publisher endpoint](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022314X98922739)
returned an internal access error in this audit. The original article's
theorem and proof were **not read**.

S2 v3 Proposition 5.5 attributes its continuation condition to this source.
Its displayed sum ends at $p^n$ while claiming a formula for $i_n$;
substitution of $n=1$ makes that display internally inconsistent. This
flags a restatement/rendering issue, not a disproof of Laubie–Saïne.
Institutional thesis/search snippets also disagreed in one divisibility
sign. They were not silently substituted for primary proof access.
Consequently no all-tower formula in PC-F depends on that display.

### S4. Classical rank-two point-module structure

Mohamed Ahmed Mohamed saadbouh, *The A-module Structure Induced by a
Drinfeld A-module over a Finite Field*.
[Metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0412368) records submission
2004-12-18 and latest v5 on 2005-04-01.
[v5 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0412368v5) was accessed, including
§4.1, Proposition 4.1 and the surrounding statements.

Use: direct ownership evidence that finite rank-two point-module structure
and ordinary-module existence questions are established research topics.
Its ordinary existence theorem and the abbreviated abstract are not
imported as an unrestricted classification of our entire family.
The actual rejection of PC-D does not rely on that theorem: it follows
from elementary finite-module kernels and S5's directly stated framework.

### S5. Current general Drinfeld submodule algorithms

Antoine Leudière and Renate Scheidler, *Computing submodules of points of
general Drinfeld modules over finite fields*.
[Metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03803) records v1,
2026-02-03; no later version was displayed at access.
[v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03803v1) was read in its introduction,
§2.5.1/Proposition 2.12, Algorithm 5, and §4.1.2 torsion discussion.

Use: the finite point-module decomposition and gcd description of
torsion directly collide with PC-D. The input finite field can be the
extension field in that contract. No computational benchmark or independent
verification of the paper's complexity/implementation is claimed. Some
surrounding displayed pseudocode/indices are visibly inconsistent; none is
needed for our elementary kernel-count deduction. This source is cited
for its actual framework and ownership, not treated as infallible code.

### S6. General twisted Frobenius estimates

K. V. Shuddhodan and Yakov Varshavsky, *The Hrushovski–Lang–Weil estimates*.
[Metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.10682) records only v1,
2021-06-20. A speculative v3 HTML endpoint failed; the verified
[v1 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.10682v1) was then accessed.
Read: Theorem 0.3 and Corollary 0.6. The official journal
[article record/PDF endpoint](https://content.algebraicgeometry.nl/2022-6/2022-6-020.pdf)
identifies *Algebraic Geometry* 9(6) (2022), 651–687,
DOI 10.14231/AG-2022-020; its PDF was not downloaded/read.

Use: the theorem supplies estimates with complexity-dependent constants
and sufficiently large Frobenius size. Corollary 0.6 keeps a correspondence
fixed. This does not by itself give an exact resonant count when the native
twisted Hénon product grows with the period. The PC-H collision was instead
proved by the local semilinear reduction; no asymptotic was promoted to an
all-period exact law.
