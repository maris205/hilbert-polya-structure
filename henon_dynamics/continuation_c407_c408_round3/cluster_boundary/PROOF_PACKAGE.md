## 1. Revised contract and explicit boundary

The original target was the entire boundary length of every cyclic scheme for
`F_k(x,y)=(y,(1+y^k)/x)`. This package instead proposes a complete local
classification on the **alternating-zero stratum**, simultaneously for every
odd integer `k >= 3` and every even clock `n=2m >= 2`. The coordinator explicitly
identified this full-period stratum as a potentially substantive revised
contract, provided the adjacency-kernel resonances and all local branch
overlaps are proved, not extrapolated.

Work over C and put

`A_{k,n}=C[x_0,...,x_{n-1}]/(x_i^k+1-x_{i-1}x_{i+1})`, indices modulo n.

The neighbor product is literal also for n=2. Its total length is k^n by the
pairwise-coprime leading monomials x_i^k. This known elementary input proves
that all local algebras considered below are finite; it is not the proposed
new result.

An alternating-zero point, with one phase chosen, is

`x_{2j}=0, x_{2j+1}=t_j`, where `t_j^k=-1` and `t_{j-1}t_j=1`.

Consequently `t_j=t_0^{(-1)^j}`. If m is even, t_0 is any of the k roots of
-1. If m is odd and k is odd, t_0=-1 is the only choice. The other phase is
obtained by a one-step cyclic shift and is disjoint. Thus there are 2k such
points when m is even and two when m is odd. These are points whose period
**divides** n, not necessarily points of exact period n.

Observable: the full local Artin length `ell_m(k)` at any one of these points.
All the permitted root choices and both phases have the same local algebra
up to the formal isomorphism constructed below.

The algebra A_{k,n} here is the **unsaturated finite cyclic relation algebra**.
It is not being identified at its zero-coordinate points with the fixed
scheme of an automorphism on a smooth rank-two cluster surface. In fact the
presentation in Section 3 has no linear relations and hence embedding
dimension m. For m>=3 this rules out an isomorphism to a fixed subscheme of
a smooth surface, whose local embedding dimension is at most two. The
support's usual four-periodic cluster interpretation does not identify
these different nonreduced structures. Smoothness of the relevant cluster
surface is also covered by the Jacobian description in
[Benito--Faber--Mourtada--Schober, Theorem 5.2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.06758v2#S5).
The original torus-domain map has none of these zero-coordinate points in
its admissible domain.

The alternating support is already owned by
[Beyer--Muller, *Deep Points of Cluster Algebras*, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.15589v1#S4),
especially Corollary 4.6 in the arXiv v1.
Only the full cyclic-scheme thickness calculation is proposed as an increment;
see SOURCE_AUDIT.md for the primary-source reading boundary.

## 2. Proposed all-period formula

Regard C_m as the indexed cyclic word of m sites. For m=1 it has one loop;
for m=2 it has two copies of the edge between its two sites. These conventions
only affect the full-cycle Hessian; every nonempty proper induced component
is an ordinary path, including an isolated vertex.

For a proper subset S of the m sites, let Runs(S) be its maximal cyclic runs
of consecutive selected sites. Empty S has an empty product equal to 1. Put

```
w_L(k) = 1       if L is even,
         k-1     if L = 1 mod 4,
         2k-1    if L = 3 mod 4;

c_m(k) = 1       if 4 does not divide m,
         4k+1    if 4 divides m.
```

The candidate theorem is

`ell_m(k) = c_m(k) + sum_{S proper subset of C_m} product_{R in Runs(S)} w_|R|(k)`.

This is a finite combinatorial formula for every m, not a small-period fit.
It gives ell_1=2 and ell_2=2k under the literal small-cycle conventions.
The full contribution of alternating-zero points to the boundary length is
`2 ell_m(k)` for odd m and `2k ell_m(k)` for even m.
Other zero supports are not classified by this theorem and cannot be
discarded when computing the torus localization length.

## 3. Formal reduction to one logarithmic potential

At a chosen point write `z_j=x_{2j}` and `b_j=x_{2j+1}`. The equations with
odd indices give

`b_j^k=z_j z_{j+1}-1`.

Since each t_j is nonzero, their derivatives in b_j are units. The formal
implicit-function theorem eliminates every b_j uniquely as

`b_j=t_j (1-z_j z_{j+1})^(1/k)`.

The remaining equations are `b_{j-1}b_j=1+z_j^k`. Because t_{j-1}t_j=1,
taking formal logarithms of units with constant term 1 shows that the
remaining ideal is generated by

`L_j=log(1-z_{j-1}z_j)+log(1-z_j z_{j+1})-k log(1+z_j^k)`.

Here replacing U-1 by log U and raising U to the kth power each multiply a
generator by a unit, so this is an equality of ideals, not just of zero sets.
Define the formal series

`V_m(z)=sum_j Li_2(z_j z_{j+1}) - sum_j Li_2(-z_j^k)`,

where `Li_2(s)=sum_{r>=1} s^r/r^2`. Then, with repeated edges as above,

`L_j=-z_j (partial V_m/partial z_j)`.

Thus the completed local algebra is

`C[[z_0,...,z_{m-1}]]/(z_j partial_j V_m : all j)`.

No global analytic continuation or dilogarithm identities are being assumed:
only the explicitly displayed power series in the maximal ideal are used.
This formal presentation is independent of the t_j.

## 4. Intersection additivity gives the subset formula

For every subset S choose partial_j V_m for j in S and z_j for j outside S.
After setting the unselected coordinates to zero this is the gradient ideal
of the restricted potential V_m|_S. For proper S it splits as a sum of path
potentials on the runs; for full S it is the cycle potential. Sections 5-7
prove that every such gradient ideal is zero-dimensional.

The local intersection multiplicity of the products `z_j partial_j V_m` is
the sum of the local intersection multiplicities from all 2^m choices. One
can justify this without assuming that the branches are reduced: after
fixing m-1 of the factors the remaining quotient is a one-dimensional
complete-intersection Cohen-Macaulay ring. Both factors are nonzerodivisors,
since each completed choice has finite colength. For two such factors a,b,
the exact sequence induced by multiplication by b proves

`length R/(ab) = length R/(a) + length R/(b)`.

Iterate this argument over the m generators. Tensor products of the disjoint
path local algebras multiply their lengths. Therefore the length at S is
the product of their gradient-colengths, or the full-cycle gradient-colength
if S is full. This proves the formula once the following computations hold.

## 5. Path gradient-colengths: nonsingular and first kernel case

For a path of L sites put

`P_L(z)=sum_{j=1}^{L-1} Li_2(z_j z_{j+1}) - sum_{j=1}^{L} Li_2(-z_j^k)`.

Its quadratic part is `sum z_j z_{j+1}`. The Hessian is the adjacency matrix
of the ordinary path. It is nonsingular for even L. Hence the gradient ideal
has colength 1 in that case, by the formal implicit-function theorem.

For odd L the Hessian kernel has dimension one, spanned by

`v=(1,0,-1,0,1,0,-1,...)`.

Use the standard bilinear dot product; v.v=(L+1)/2 is nonzero. The Hessian is
invertible on its orthogonal complement. Write z=a v+u with u perpendicular
to v, and uniquely solve the complementary gradient equations for u=u(a).
The effective one-variable potential is `p_L(a)=P_L(a v+u(a))`.
The full gradient ideal has the same colength as `(p_L'(a))`: in these linear
coordinates the complementary equations eliminate u, and the remaining
equation is exactly the derivative of the effective potential.

If L=4r+1, the sum of the kth powers of the nonzero entries of a v is a^k,
since k is odd. The pair potential restricts to zero. Complementary
corrections begin at degree k-1 in u and change the effective potential only
at degree at least 2k-2>k. Therefore

`p_L(a)=a^k+O(a^(k+1))`, and its gradient-colength is k-1.

This includes L=1.

## 6. Path gradient-colengths: the cancellation case L=4r-1

Here r>=1. Reflection of the path sends v to -v. The unique complementary
solution respects that reflection, so p_L is even. Its degree-k restriction
vanishes because the 2r odd sites have alternating signs.

Let q=k-1. At a v the leading nonlinear gradient is `k a^q` at every odd
site and zero at every even site. Its kernel projection vanishes. Solving
the complementary Hessian equation gives the first correction u_0(a):

```
(u_0)_{2j-1}=0;
(u_0)_{2j}= -k a^q if j is odd, and 0 if j is even.
```

There are r nonzero even entries. All odd entries of u_0 are zero, hence
u_0 is perpendicular to v. The first and last odd equations are included:
the missing endpoint variables are exactly zero.

At z=a v+u_0 the complementary gradient error has order at least k+1.
Indeed the pair corrections at an even site start with `u_even a^2`, of
order k+1; at an odd site they start with `a u_even^2`, of order 2k-1.
The unary derivative at a corrected even site has order q^2, which is at
least k+1 for odd k>=3, with equality at k=3. Higher unary terms at odd sites
start at order 2k-1. Consequently the exact complementary solution differs
from u_0 by O(a^(k+1)), and substituting the correction changes the effective
potential only at order at least 2k+2. This last assertion follows either by
Taylor expansion using the stated residual and the invertible Hessian, or
by completing the quadratic term in the complementary variables.

It therefore suffices to evaluate P_L at a v+u_0 through degree 2k:

- Every nonzero even entry sits between +a and -a. Its two pair terms are
  `Li_2(-k a^k)+Li_2(k a^k) = (1/2)Li_2(k^2 a^(2k))`.
  Their combined coefficient is k^2/2, and there are r such entries.
- Each pair of opposite odd sites contributes
  `-Li_2(-a^k)-Li_2(a^k)=-(1/2)Li_2(a^(2k))`.
  There are r pairs, giving coefficient -r/2.
- The r corrected even sites contribute `(-k a^q)^k` at leading order.
  Its degree k(k-1) exceeds 2k if k>=5. If k=3 it equals 2k and contributes
  -27r a^6.

Thus

```
p_(4r-1)(a) = [r(k^2-1)/2] a^(2k) + higher terms,  k>=5 odd;
p_(4r-1)(a) = -23r a^6 + higher terms,              k=3.
```

All coefficients are nonzero in characteristic zero. Hence the gradient
colength is 2k-1. In particular this computation accounts for the cancelled
leading term rather than assuming a nondegenerate Hessian at odd L.

## 7. Cycle gradient-colengths, including the two-dimensional kernel

The Hessian of V_m is the cycle adjacency matrix with the specified loop
and double-edge conventions. Its eigenvalues are `2 cos(2 pi j/m)`.
It is invertible unless 4 divides m, giving colength 1 in those cases.
This statement includes m=1 (Hessian [2]) and m=2 (off-diagonal entries 2).

Suppose m=4r. Its kernel consists of the period-four patterns

`(a,b,-a,-b,a,b,-a,-b,...)`.

Solve the complementary gradient equations and call the effective potential
p_m(a,b). Uniqueness and invariance under shift by four force its full
complementary solution to be period four as well. Thus

`p_m(a,b)=r p_4(a,b)`.

The two reflections of the four-cycle act on this kernel as `(a,b)->(-a,b)`
and `(a,b)->(a,-b)`. The effective potential is therefore even in each
variable separately, and shift by one exchanges a and b up to signs.
Consequently

`p_4(a,b)=W(a^2,b^2)`, with W symmetric in its arguments.

We need only two coefficients of this exact formal series:

`p_4(a,b)=alpha a^2 b^2 + beta(a^(2k)+b^(2k)) + ...`,

where there are no lower-degree pure-axis terms and alpha,beta are nonzero.

### 7.1 The mixed quartic coefficient

The pair potential on the kernel equals `Li_2(a^2 b^2)`.
For k>=5 the unary terms and complementary corrections do not affect its
quartic coefficient, so alpha=1.

For k=3 the leading complementary gradient is
`g=3(a^2,b^2,a^2,b^2)`. On the complementary, period-two subspace the Hessian
interchanges the two entries with coefficient 2. Hence
`-(1/2) g^T H^{-1} g = -9 a^2 b^2`.
Adding the original quartic term gives alpha=-8.

### 7.2 The first pure-axis coefficient

Set b=0. The first complementary correction is the period-two shift

`u_0=(0,c,0,c), c=-(k/2)a^(k-1)`.

As in Section 6, its complementary gradient residual has order at least
k+1, so further elimination changes the effective potential only in order
at least 2k+2. At `(a,c,-a,c)` the four pair terms sum to
`Li_2(a^2 c^2)`, whose degree-2k coefficient is k^2/4.
The opposite a-sites contribute `-(1/2)Li_2(a^(2k))`.
The two c-sites have leading total 2c^k; this is higher than degree 2k for
k>=5, and is `-(27/4)a^6` for k=3. Therefore

```
beta=(k^2-2)/4,  k>=5 odd;
beta=-5,         k=3.
```

This calculation also proves the absence of earlier pure-axis terms.
Separate evenness excludes any odd powers on either axis.

### 7.3 The exact gradient-colength without a Newton formula

Write A=a^2 and B=b^2. The obtained coefficients imply

`W(A,0)=beta A^k+O(A^(k+1))`,
`W(0,B)=beta B^k+O(B^(k+1))`,
`W(A,B)=alpha A B + terms of total degree >=3`.

The Jacobian of `(W_A,W_B)` at (0,0) is invertible, since alpha is nonzero
and there are no pure quadratic terms for k>=3. Hence
`(W_A,W_B)=(A,B)` in C[[A,B]]. After substituting a^2,b^2, the ideal has
colength 4 in C[[a,b]].

Since `partial_a p_4=2a W_A` and `partial_b p_4=2b W_B`, intersection
additivity gives

```
mu(p_4)
 = I(a,b) + I(a,W_B) + I(W_A,b) + I(W_A,W_B)
 = 1 + (2k-2) + (2k-2) + 4
 = 4k+1.
```

The axis orders follow from beta nonzero. All four intersections are finite,
so the additivity argument is valid. Multiplication of the potential by
r does not change its gradient ideal over C. This proves c_m=4k+1 for every
m divisible by four.

## 8. What this does and does not settle

The claimed increment is the full local-length classification at every
alternating-zero point, for all odd degrees and all clocks where they occur.
It handles infinitely many genuinely nontrivial local Artin rings, all
proper-subset branch intersections, the one-dimensional path cancellation,
and the two-dimensional cyclic kernel. It is not a Picard-trace calculation,
a generic-parameter torus count, or a single low-period counterexample.

It does **not** classify an arbitrary boundary point with longer nonzero
runs. In the full cyclic algebra, the identity

`x_i[-x_{i-2}-x_{i+2}+x_i x_{i-2}x_{i+2}-((1+x_i^k)^k-1)/x_i]=0`

shows that a zero coordinate is already zero in the local algebra whenever
`x_{i-2}+x_{i+2}` is nonzero at the point. Resonant longer runs require an
additional local return analysis; nothing in Sections 3-7 proves they are
absent for odd k. Thus no total torus length or ordinary Artin-Mazur zeta
function should be inferred from this package.

## 10. Same-contract rational generating function

This is a consequence of the local-length theorem, not another paper
contract. Let P_m denote the proper-subset sum in Section 2 and put w_0=1.
The path weights give

```
W(t)=sum_(L>=1) w_L t^L
    = ((k-1)t+t^2+(2k-1)t^3+t^4)/(1-t^4),

B(t)=t(1+W(t))
    = (t+(k-1)t^2+t^3+(2k-1)t^4)/(1-t^4).
```

A proper cyclic subset has q>=1 unselected sites. Starting at a marked
unselected site, it consists of q blocks, each formed by an unselected site
followed by L>=0 selected sites. Its weight is the product of the w_L.
For any fixed m,q, the weighted count with one marked unselected site is
both `q P_(m,q)` and `m [t^m]B(t)^q`: the first chooses one of q marks in a
labeled cyclic subset, and the second chooses the mark's labeled position
and then the ordered block lengths. This remains true for rotationally
symmetric words because the positions, not rotation classes, are labeled.
Therefore

`sum_(m>=1) P_m t^m/m = sum_(q>=1) B(t)^q/q = -log(1-B(t))`.

Define

`D_k(t)=1-t-(k-1)t^2-t^3-2k t^4`,

so that `1-B(t)=D_k(t)/(1-t^4)`. Let T_m be defined exactly by

`sum_(m>=1) T_m t^m/m=-log D_k(t)`.

Then `P_m=T_m-4*1_(4|m)`, and the cycle contribution yields

`ell_m(k)=T_m+1+4(k-1)*1_(4|m)`.

Equivalently, the multiplicity-generating series is rational:

`Z_k(t):=exp(sum_(m>=1) ell_m(k)t^m/m)`
`       =1/[(1-t)D_k(t)(1-t^4)^(k-1)]`.

For example the formula gives `ell_3=3k+2` and `ell_4=2k(k+6)`.
These identities are algebraic consequences of the all-period proof, not
new empirical inputs. If desired, T_m satisfies the fourth-order recurrence
associated to D_k, with initial power sums determined by -log D_k.

For the original clock n, let b_n be the full alternating-zero boundary
contribution: b_n=0 for odd n, while

`b_(2m)=[k+1+(k-1)(-1)^m] ell_m(k)`.

As k is odd, the following expression is again a rational function:

`exp(sum_(n>=1) b_n u^n/n)`
` = Z_k(u^2)^((k+1)/2) Z_k(-u^2)^((k-1)/2)`.

Both exponential series are defined from scheme multiplicities. Neither is
asserted to be the ordinary Artin--Mazur zeta function of F_k, of a cluster
surface automorphism, or of a newly constructed finite dynamical system.
