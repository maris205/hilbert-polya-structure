                                                 Abstract
          For an odd integer k ≥ 3, consider the finite cyclic relation algebra defined over C by
      xki + 1 = xi−1 xi+1 , with indices modulo n. We determine its complete local Artin length

      proof resolves the one-dimensional cancelled path kernel and the two-dimensional cycle

1    Introduction
Clearing the denominators in a rational recurrence produces polynomial relations that remain
meaningful at zero coordinates. Their solutions may carry nilpotent structure that is invisible
in the recurrence’s original domain. Even when the possible zero patterns are understood,
determining the lengths of the corresponding local algebras is a separate intersection problem.
This distinction is particularly concrete for the exchange recurrence

                                  xi−1 xi+1 = 1 + xki ,      k ≥ 3 odd.                                   (1.1)

Imposing a cyclic clock gives a finite algebra. At an alternating-zero point its local embedding
dimension grows with the clock, so a description of the support on a two-dimensional cluster
variety cannot by itself determine the retained infinitesimal structure.
    The support in question is classical in this setting. Beyer and Muller classify the deep
points of coefficient-free rank-two cluster algebras; their alternating pattern is (0, α, 0, α−1 ),
with the relevant root condition, and the shifted phase [1, Section 4, especially Corollary 4.6
in arXiv v1]. We use this attribution while deriving the elementary finite-clock restriction
directly. Benito, Faber, Mourtada, and Schober classify the ambient rank-two singularities;
their Jacobian description implies smoothness for the positive rank-two exponents over C [2,
Section 5, Theorem 5.2.3 in arXiv v2]. The smooth ambient surface and the unsaturated cyclic
relation algebra studied here are different schemes. A further related, but distinct, direction
is the birational dynamics of cluster mutation maps studied by Grigorev, Kalidindi, Quintero

                                                     1
Santander, and Roeder [3]. For equal exponents their mutation composition corresponds to the
square of Fk (x, y) = (y, (1 + y k )/x). We do not use a Picard-action or entropy calculation to
infer a boundary length.
    Our result determines the local thickness of the entire alternating-zero stratum, simultaneously
for every odd k ≥ 3 and every clock at which it occurs. The local equations reduce to products
of coordinates and gradient components of one formal potential. Expanding their intersection
multiplicity over all factor choices leaves path and cycle gradient colengths. Nonsingular Hessians
account for the simplest terms, but they do not settle the problem: paths of odd length have
a one-dimensional kernel, and cycles of length divisible by four have a two-dimensional kernel.
The cancelled path case has leading effective degree 2k rather than k. The cycle case requires
both a mixed quartic coefficient and a pure-axis coefficient. Both calculations have additional
terms at k = 3.
    The resulting weights depend only on path length modulo four. This makes the local lengths
amenable to a labeled cyclic-word enumeration and yields a rational multiplicity-generating
function. The rational series is a consequence of the same local intersection calculation, not a
new dynamical realization. The original rational recurrence excludes these zero-coordinate points
from its admissible torus domain, and other boundary supports remain outside the theorem.
    Section 2 states the formula and fixes the small-clock conventions. Section 3 proves the formal
reduction and the required nonreduced intersection additivity. The path and cycle calculations
occupy sections 4 and 5. We derive the rational series in section 6, and distinguish the finite
computational controls from the proof and the unresolved boundary problem in section 7.

Remark 3.2 (The ambient-scheme distinction). Any closed subscheme of a smooth surface has
local embedding dimension at most two: its cotangent space is a quotient of the surface’s
cotangent space. For m ≥ 3, proposition 3.1 consequently rules out an identification of the
local algebra here with the fixed-point local scheme of an endomorphism of a smooth cluster
surface. The smoothness statement for the relevant rank-two cluster surface is an existing
result [2, Theorem 5.2.3 in arXiv v2]; it is not a conclusion about (2.1). The support’s cluster
interpretation does not identify these nonreduced structures.

7.2   The boundary that remains unclassified
The theorem concerns exactly the alternating-zero stratum. It neither exhausts nor declares
absent the other possible zero supports in (2.1). One elementary relation indicates where a
broader boundary analysis would require more information. Multiplying the relations at i − 1
and i + 1, and then using the relation at i, gives the identity in Ak,n

The displayed quotient is a polynomial, since its numerator is divisible by xki . At a point where
xi = 0 and xi−2 + xi+2 ̸= 0, the bracket is a unit in the local algebra, so xi vanishes in that local
algebra, not just at the reduced point. When the neighboring sum is zero, this unit argument
fails. Longer nonzero runs can therefore present additional local return problems not resolved
by the path decomposition at an alternating point.
    No total torus-localization length is obtained by subtracting only (6.8) from k n . Nor has
a comparison been constructed between the unsaturated local algebra and a saturated fixed
scheme on a cluster surface. For m ≥ 3 the embedding-dimension argument in remark 3.2
explicitly precludes the smooth-surface fixed-scheme identification. Even for the smaller values
of m, where that particular obstruction does not apply, no such identification is asserted.
    The proof uses characteristic zero in its formal logarithms, fractional powers, nonzero leading
coefficients, and elimination steps. Oddness of k is used in both the support restriction and the
cancellations of the effective potentials. Results for even exponents or positive characteristic
require separate arguments. Finally, the series (2.6) and (2.7) have been defined using these
local scheme multiplicities; they are not claimed to be Artin–Mazur zeta functions of Fk , of a
smooth cluster-surface automorphism, or of another constructed dynamical system.
    Within these boundaries the conclusion is complete: every alternating-zero local algebra,
at every admissible clock and every odd exponent at least three, has the explicit length (2.4).
Its two degenerate Hessian types account for the nontrivial weights, and their exact cyclic
aggregation gives the rational local-multiplicity series.
