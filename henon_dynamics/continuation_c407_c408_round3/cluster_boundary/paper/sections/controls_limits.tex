\subsection{The boundary that remains unclassified}

The theorem concerns exactly the alternating-zero stratum. It neither
exhausts nor declares absent the other possible zero supports in
\eqref{eq:algebra}. One elementary relation indicates where a broader
boundary analysis would require more information. Multiplying the
relations at $i-1$ and $i+1$, and then using the relation at $i$, gives
the identity in $\Acyc_{k,n}$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundaryidentity}
 x_i\!\left[
 -x_{i-2}-x_{i+2}+x_ix_{i-2}x_{i+2}
 -\frac{(1+x_i^k)^k-1}{x_i}
 \right]=0.
\end{equation}
The displayed quotient is a polynomial, since its numerator is divisible
by $x_i^k$. At a point where $x_i=0$ and $x_{i-2}+x_{i+2}\ne0$, the
bracket is a unit in the local algebra, so $x_i$ vanishes in that local
algebra, not just at the reduced point. When the neighboring sum is zero,
this unit argument fails. Longer nonzero runs can therefore present
additional local return problems not resolved by the path decomposition
at an alternating point.

No total torus-localization length is obtained by subtracting only
\eqref{eq:bclock} from $k^n$. Nor has a comparison been constructed between
the unsaturated local algebra and a saturated fixed scheme on a cluster
surface. For $m\geq3$ the embedding-dimension argument in
\cref{rem:ambient} explicitly precludes the smooth-surface fixed-scheme
identification. Even for the smaller values of $m$, where that particular
obstruction does not apply, no such identification is asserted.

The proof uses characteristic zero in its formal logarithms, fractional
powers, nonzero leading coefficients, and elimination steps. Oddness of
$k$ is used in both the support restriction and the cancellations of the
effective potentials. Results for even exponents or positive
characteristic require separate arguments. Finally, the series
\eqref{eq:Z} and \eqref{eq:native} have been defined using these local
scheme multiplicities; they are not claimed to be Artin--Mazur zeta
functions of $F_k$, of a smooth cluster-surface automorphism, or of another
constructed dynamical system.

Within these boundaries the conclusion is complete: every alternating-zero
local algebra, at every admissible clock and every odd exponent at least
three, has the explicit length \eqref{eq:main_length}. Its two degenerate
Hessian types account for the nontrivial weights, and their exact cyclic
aggregation gives the rational local-multiplicity series.
