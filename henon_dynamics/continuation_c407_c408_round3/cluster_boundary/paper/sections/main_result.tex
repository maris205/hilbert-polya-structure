\section{Cyclic relation algebras and the main theorem}\label{sec:main}

Fix an odd integer $k\geq3$. For $n\geq1$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algebra}
 \Acyc_{k,n}
 =\C[x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1}]
   \big/(x_i^k+1-x_{i-1}x_{i+1}:0\leq i<n),
\end{equation}
where indices are modulo $n$. The neighbor product is literal: at $n=2$
the equation for $x_i$ contains the square of the other coordinate.
This is the \emph{unsaturated cyclic relation algebra}. No saturation by
the coordinates, cluster-surface replacement, or passage to reduced
points is part of its definition.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:finite_support}
The algebra $\Acyc_{k,n}$ has dimension $k^n$ over $\C$.
For $n=2m$, its alternating-zero points in one phase are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alt_point}
 x_{2j}=0,\qquad x_{2j+1}=t_j,\qquad
 t_j^k=-1,\quad t_{j-1}t_j=1 \quad(j\bmod m).
\end{equation}
There are $k$ choices in each phase when $m$ is even, and one choice
in each phase when $m$ is odd. The two phases are disjoint.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a monomial order refining total degree, the displayed relations have
leading monomials $x_i^k$, since $k>2$. These monomials are pairwise
relatively prime, so the relations form a Gr\"obner basis by the product
criterion. The standard monomials have all exponents below $k$; there are
$k^n$ of them. Thus every localization at a point is an Artin local algebra.

Substitution of alternating zeros gives exactly
\eqref{eq:alt_point}. The product conditions imply
$t_j=t_0^{(-1)^j}$. If $m$ is even this is cyclic for any of the $k$
roots of $-1$. If $m$ is odd, closure forces $t_0^2=1$; for odd $k$,
the condition $t_0^k=-1$ leaves only $t_0=-1$.
Shifting all indices by one gives the other phase. Its zero coordinates
and nonzero coordinates are interchanged, hence the phases cannot coincide.
\end{proof}

These are labeled solutions of the cyclic equations, not a count of
orbits of exact period $2m$. In particular the support pattern has period
dividing four. We shall prove that all its permitted root choices and both
phases have isomorphic local algebras. Denote their common length by
$\ell_m(k)$.

For combinatorial purposes let $C_m$ be the labeled cyclic set of $m$
sites. Its cycle potential has one loop when $m=1$ and two copies of the
edge when $m=2$. Every nonempty proper induced connected component is an
ordinary path, with an isolated site counted as a path of length one.
For a proper subset $S\subsetneq C_m$, let $\Runs(S)$ be its maximal
cyclic runs of selected sites. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weights}
 w_L(k)=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&L\equiv0,2\pmod4,\\
  k-1,&L\equiv1\pmod4,\\
  2k-1,&L\equiv3\pmod4,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 c_m(k)=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&4\nmid m,\\
  4k+1,&4\mid m.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Alternating-zero local length]\label{thm:length}
For every odd $k\geq3$ and every $m\geq1$, all alternating-zero
points of $\Acyc_{k,2m}$ have isomorphic local algebras, of length
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main_length}
 \ell_m(k)=c_m(k)+
  \sum_{S\subsetneq C_m}\ \prod_{R\in\Runs(S)}w_{|R|}(k).
\end{equation}
The empty subset contributes one. Their total contribution to the
length of $\Acyc_{k,2m}$ is $2\ell_m(k)$ when $m$ is odd and
$2k\ell_m(k)$ when $m$ is even.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Rational multiplicity series]\label{cor:series}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D}
 D_k(t)=1-t-(k-1)t^2-t^3-2kt^4.
\end{equation}
As an identity of formal power series,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Z}
 Z_k(t):=\exp\!\left(\sum_{m\geq1}\frac{\ell_m(k)}m t^m\right)
 =\frac1{(1-t)D_k(t)(1-t^4)^{k-1}}.
\end{equation}
Let $b_n$ be the total alternating-zero contribution at clock $n$, with
$b_n=0$ for odd $n$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:native}
 \exp\!\left(\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{b_n}n u^n\right)
 =Z_k(u^2)^{(k+1)/2}Z_k(-u^2)^{(k-1)/2}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

The theorem is proved by the local reductions in the next three sections;
the corollary is proved by an exact double count in \cref{sec:series}.
For orientation, the first four lengths are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:firstfour}
 \ell_1=2,\qquad \ell_2=2k,\qquad
 \ell_3=3k+2,\qquad \ell_4=2k(k+6).
\end{equation}
The weights in \eqref{eq:weights} are local gradient colengths, not numbers
of reduced branches. This is why the intersection argument below must
retain nilpotents.
