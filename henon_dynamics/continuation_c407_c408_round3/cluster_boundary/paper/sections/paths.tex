\section{Path gradient colengths}\label{sec:paths}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:paths}
For every $L\geq1$, the gradient ideal of $P_L$ in
\eqref{eq:pathpotential} has finite colength
\[
 \mu(P_L)=w_L(k).
\]
\end{proposition}

\subsection{The Hessian and the uncancelled kernel}

The quadratic part of $P_L$ is $\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}z_jz_{j+1}$.
Its Hessian $H_L$ is the ordinary path adjacency matrix. Expanding its
determinant at an endpoint gives
$\det H_L=-\det H_{L-2}$, with $\det H_0=1$ and $\det H_1=0$.
Consequently $H_L$ is invertible for even $L$. Formal implicit elimination
of the full gradient then gives $\mu(P_L)=1$.

For odd $L$, solving $H_Lv=0$ recursively from an endpoint shows that
the kernel is the one-dimensional space generated by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pathkernel}
 v=(1,0,-1,0,1,0,-1,\ldots).
\end{equation}
For the bilinear dot product $v^Tv=(L+1)/2\ne0$. Thus
$\C^L=\Span(v)\oplus v^\perp$, and the symmetric Hessian is invertible
on $v^\perp$. Write $z=av+u$ with $u\in v^\perp$ and solve the
complementary gradient equations for $u=u(a)$. By
\cref{lem:eliminate}, the gradient colength equals the colength of
$(p_L'(a))$, where $p_L(a)=P_L(av+u(a))$.

At $av$, every adjacent product is zero. The nonlinear gradient starts
in degree $k-1$, so $u(a)=O(a^{k-1})$, and elimination changes the
restricted potential $P_L(av)$ only in degree at least $2k-2$.
If $L=4r+1$, the nonzero entries of $v$ have one more $+1$ than $-1$.
Since $k$ is odd and $-\Li(-s)=s+O(s^2)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pathuncancelled}
 p_L(a)=a^k+O(a^{k+1}).
\end{equation}
Here $2k-2>k$ for every $k\geq3$, so complementary elimination cannot
change the displayed coefficient. Therefore $p_L'$ has order $k-1$,
and $\mu(P_L)=k-1$. This also proves the isolated-site case $L=1$.

\subsection{The cancelled kernel at lengths three modulo four}

It remains to treat $L=4r-1$, $r\geq1$. Reflection of the path sends
$v$ to $-v$ and preserves $v^\perp$. It preserves the potential, so the
uniqueness of the complementary solution implies $p_L(-a)=p_L(a)$.
The degree-$k$ restriction vanishes, since the $2r$ nonzero entries
of $v$ consist of equal numbers of the two signs.

Set $q=k-1$, which is even. The leading nonlinear gradient at $av$ is
$ka^q$ at every odd site and zero at every even site. Its projection
onto the kernel is zero. The first complementary correction that solves
the Hessian equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pathcorrection}
 (u_0)_{2j-1}=0,\qquad
 (u_0)_{2j}=
 \begin{cases}
  -ka^q,&j\text{ odd},\\
  0,&j\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Indeed, each odd site's adjacent even entries sum to $-ka^q$; the
missing entries at the two endpoints are zero. Each even site's adjacent
odd correction entries sum to zero. There are exactly $r$ nonzero even
entries, and $u_0$ is orthogonal to $v$.

We next bound the residual rather than assuming this first correction is
exact. The higher pair-potential gradient at an even site begins with
$u_{\rm even}a^2$, of degree $k+1$. At an odd site it begins with
$a u_{\rm even}^2$, of degree $2k-1$. The unary derivative at a corrected
even site has degree $q^2$, and the higher unary derivatives at odd sites
start in degree $2k-1$. For odd $k\geq3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residualinequalities}
 2k-1\geq k+1,\qquad (k-1)^2\geq k+1;
\end{equation}
the second inequality is an equality at $k=3$.
The linear Hessian contribution cancels the leading unary gradient by
\eqref{eq:pathcorrection}. Hence the full gradient residual, and in
particular its complementary part, has order at least $k+1$.
\Cref{lem:eliminate} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:patherror}
 u(a)-u_0(a)=O(a^{k+1}),\qquad
 p_L(a)-P_L(av+u_0(a))=O(a^{2k+2}).
\end{equation}
It therefore suffices to evaluate the displayed approximation through
degree $2k$.

Each nonzero even entry lies between $+a$ and $-a$, and the corresponding
pair terms satisfy the formal identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dilogeven}
 \Li(-ka^k)+\Li(ka^k)
   =\tfrac12\Li(k^2a^{2k}).
\end{equation}
This identity follows by cancelling the odd powers in the defining series.
With $r$ such entries, the total pair coefficient in degree $2k$ is
$rk^2/2$. Opposite odd sites give
\[
 -\Li(-a^k)-\Li(a^k)=-\tfrac12\Li(a^{2k});
\]
their $r$ pairs contribute $-r/2$ in that degree. The $r$ corrected even
sites each have leading unary term $(-ka^q)^k$. Its degree is $k(k-1)$,
which exceeds $2k$ if $k\geq5$. At $k=3$ it instead contributes
$-27a^6$ per corrected site. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pathcoefficient}
 p_{4r-1}(a)=
 \begin{cases}
  \dfrac{r(k^2-1)}2 a^{2k}+O(a^{2k+1}),&k\geq5\text{ odd},\\[4pt]
  -23r a^6+O(a^7),&k=3.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
No lower-degree term survives: the paired evaluations start in degree
$2k$, the corrected unary degree is at least $2k$, and the elimination
error starts in degree $2k+2$. Both displayed coefficients are nonzero
over $\C$. The derivative therefore has order $2k-1$ and finite
colength $2k-1$. Together with the previous cases, this proves
\cref{prop:paths}.
