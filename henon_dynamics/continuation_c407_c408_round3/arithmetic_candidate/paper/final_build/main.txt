       Zero-dimensional Cantor limit sets for hyperbolic
              finite-adelically distorted orbit counting
                                      Anonymous Authors

                                        6 September 2026
                                    Internal research series C407


                                              Abstract
          The accumulation set of normalized prime-orbit counting is a Cantor set of upper box
      dimension zero for every genuinely distorted hyperbolic finite-adelically distorted system
      of positive entropy. Here hyperbolic means that the multiplicative part of the fixed-point
      sequence has a unique dominant root Λ > 1, and the normalized count is N πf (N )/ΛN ,
      where πf (N ) counts primitive orbits of length at most N . The result allows any fixed
      finite set of distortion primes and the nonzero wild exponential factors in the fixed-point
      formula. More precisely, if d primes are active, the accumulation set can be covered by at
      most C(1 + log(1/ε))2d intervals of length ε, with constants depending on the fixed system.
      The proof starts from the known representation of this set as a continuous image of a
      compact finite-adelic group. An adaptive valuation partition gives the covering bound. A
      separate Fourier argument for positive sums of radial kernels proves nonconstancy on every
      admissible cylinder and excludes isolated image values. Both arguments allow nonnegative
      real exponent data. The theorem addresses the multiprime and wild hyperbolic regimes
      left open in the cited 2024 public source. Solenoid, additive-group and product examples
      illustrate the conclusion using classical fixed-point counts.

Keywords: arithmetic dynamics; prime-orbit counting; finite-adelic distortion; Cantor set;
box dimension; p-adic Fourier analysis.


1    Introduction and prior work
An orbit-counting function may have the expected exponential order of growth without having
a single asymptotic constant. For a map f with finitely many fixed points of every iterate,
let πf (N ) be the number of primitive periodic orbits of length at most the integer N . Given
an exponential growth base Λ > 1, the observable studied here is the set of accumulation
points of

Arithmetic factors in the fixed-point sequence can prevent this sequence from converging.
Describing all its subsequential limits is then a different question from determining its order
of magnitude.
    Byszewski, Cornelissen and Houben introduced finite-adelically distorted systems, abbre-
viated FAD systems, to treat fixed-point formulas with finitely many valuation-dependent
factors. Their detector theorem realizes the accumulation set in (1.1) as the image of an
explicit continuous function on a compact group [1, Definitions 7.1.1–7.1.2 and Theorem
12.4.3]. Their word hyperbolic means that the multiplicative part has a unique dominant root.
In that case the archimedean phase disappears, leaving a finite residue coordinate and finitely
many p-adic coordinates. For one distortion prime and no wild exponential factor, they prove

    The detector viewpoint already has close predecessors. For S-integer dynamics, Everest,
Miles, Stevens and Ward control normalized orbit counts through compact-group rotations
and obtain a quantitative one-prime injectivity result [3, Theorem 1.1, Lemma 2.4 and
Corollary 2.5]. For abelian varieties in positive characteristic, in the unique-dominant-root
setting and the branch that is not very inseparable, Byszewski and Cornelissen prove the
one-characteristic finite-set/Cantor-set conclusion through a one-variable detector and local
injectivity [2, Proposition 9.4 and Theorem 9.5]. These counting reductions and examples
are classical inputs to the present problem. The term hyperbolic in this article is always used
in the precise FAD sense of [1, Definition 10.3.9], not inferred from the terminology in the
title of [3].
    Two independent arguments are needed. A continuous image of a profinite space need
not remain totally disconnected, and even a zero-dimensional compact set may have isolated
points. The covering proof retains only the first L summands of the detector series and
refines a valuation tree only along balls containing one of their centers. At depth K this
needs O(LK) pieces, instead of all pK residue classes. Taking both L and K logarithmic in
1/ε proves (1.2).
    Perfectness comes from a positive radial-kernel lemma. Each active kernel has strictly
negative Fourier coefficients at every nontrivial conductor. Among finitely many exponent
types, one has the slowest coefficient decay. High-conductor characters approach one at each
fixed ordinary integer, and summability then prevents cancellation among positive translated
kernels. Local rescaling gives nonconstancy in every relevant p-adic ball. To apply this
argument in several coordinates, the other coordinates are fixed at negative integers. That
selection device already occurs in BCH’s proof of their cardinality dichotomy [1, proof of
Theorem 12.5.1, pp. 132–133]. The additional conclusion here follows from applying the

the detector series, only distances to a finite list of integer centers matter after truncation.
Refining the occupied branches of the residue tree gives a smaller partition.

7     Scope, limitations, and provenance
The two parts of the proof use different hypotheses. The adaptive cover needs finitely many
primes and finitely many exponent types, together with geometric decay of the detector
weights. It does not need exponent periods coprime to their own prime. In contrast, the
perfectness proof uses positive coefficients to prevent Fourier cancellation, and it uses coprime
exponent periods to put an active center in every coordinate ball. Dropping the latter
hypothesis can change the topological answer even for one active prime.

Proposition 7.1 (A forbidden-period singleton). Fix Λ > 1. Retain the definitions of D
and Φ, but allow S = {2}, w = 2, rn = 1, t2,n = 0, and s2,n = 1{n odd} . Then the exponent
sequence has forbidden own-prime period 2, and

                                    Φ(D) = {(1 − Λ−1 )−1 },

although the prime 2 is active.

Proof. Lemma 5.1, which does not use the exponent periods, gives D = {(a, x) : x ≡ a
(mod 2)}. In a term with a − ℓ odd, the argument x − ℓ is odd. Its valuation is therefore
zero and the active kernel H2;1,0 (x − ℓ) equals one. In a term with a − ℓ even, the kernel is
inactive and again equals one. Every summand of Φ is thus Λ−ℓ , independently of (a, x), and
summation proves the formula.

    This example belongs to the enlarged analytic data class, not to the FAD class in (6.1).
Its image still obeys the covering bound, but it is not perfect. It therefore separates the
metric and topological roles of the hypotheses without contradicting Corollary 6.1.
    No injectivity, local injectivity, or explicit parametrizing homeomorphism is proved for
the detector. Nor does perfectness alone imply that the pushforward of Haar measure has no
atoms; no such measure-theoretic assertion is made here. The exponent 2d in the logarithmic
cover is an upper bound, and its optimality or an exact Hausdorff gauge is left open. Signed
or complex weights would require a different noncancellation argument. Infinitely many
distortion primes would require a replacement for the finite-product cover.
    The theorem also excludes a nontrivial continuous archimedean phase. The nonhyperbolic
FAD result that the accumulation set contains a nondegenerate interval belongs to [1, Theorem
12.5.2(i)]; it is outside the present image theorem. For the hyperbolic case, the comparison
with open problems remains tied to the cited public v2 of that work. The forthcoming EMS
version listed in [4, reference 6] was not obtained for comparison, so an unqualified claim
about priority over the final book is not supported by the source audit.
