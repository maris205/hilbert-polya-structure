\section{Hyperbolic FAD systems and realized examples}\label{sec:dynamics}

We now state precisely the classical reduction that makes the image
theorem an orbit-counting result. No fixed-point realizability assertion
is made for arbitrary data in Theorem~\ref{thm:image}.

\subsection{The FAD convention and detector reduction}

A self-map $f:X\to X$ is \emph{confined} when $\Fix(f^n)$ is finite for
every $n\geq1$. Write $f_n=\#\Fix(f^n)$ and let $P_f(n)$ be the number
of primitive periodic orbits of length exactly $n$. Thus
\[
 f_n=\sum_{k\mid n}kP_f(k),\qquad
 \pi_f(N)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}P_f(n).
\]
All time parameters here are native integer iterates; no time change or
alternative normalization is introduced.

A sequence $(a_n)_{n\geq1}$ is a \emph{gcd sequence} if some $h\geq1$
satisfies $a_n=a_{\gcd(n,h)}$ for every $n\geq1$. It is periodic and can
be extended to all integer indices by periodicity.
Following~\cite[Definitions 7.1.1--7.1.2]{bch2024}, a FAD system is a
confined system whose fixed-point counts admit a representation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fad_counts}
 f_n=|\det(A^n-I)|c^n r_n
       \prod_{p\in S}|n|_p^{s_{p,n}}p^{-t_{p,n}|n|_p^{-1}},
 \qquad n\geq1.
\end{equation}
Here $A$ is an integral square matrix, $c>0$, $S$ is a finite prime set,
$(r_n)$ is a positive real gcd sequence, and for each $p\in S$ the
sequences $(s_{p,n})$ and $(t_{p,n})$ are nonnegative real gcd sequences
whose periods are coprime to $p$. Their least common period is therefore
coprime to $p$ as well. The matrix may have size zero, with determinant
factor interpreted as one. These are restrictions on the counts of a
genuine self-map, not a claim that every integer sequence of this form is
realizable.

Expand the multiplicative part as an exponential polynomial, collecting
distinct roots with nonzero coefficients:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dominant_roots}
 |\det(A^n-I)|c^n=\sum_j m_j\lambda_j^n,
 \qquad \Lambda=\max_j|\lambda_j|.
\end{equation}
For the systems considered below this expression is nonzero. A root of
modulus $\Lambda$ is dominant. The FAD meaning of \emph{hyperbolic} is
that there is exactly one dominant root
\cite[Definition 10.3.9]{bch2024}. It is then the positive real root
$\Lambda$; moreover the normalized leading factor is identically one,
not just an unspecified positive constant
\cite[Lemmas 10.3.2 and 10.3.10]{bch2024}.
The entropy in this convention is $\log\Lambda$
\cite[Definition 10.3.4]{bch2024}. We assume it is positive, equivalently
$\Lambda>1$, without asserting equality with topological entropy for an
arbitrary realization of the count sequence.

Remove inactive primes from the representation and let $d$ be the number
remaining. Take $w$ to be a common period of its data and form $\D$ and
$\Phi$ as in~\eqref{eq:detector_definition}--\eqref{eq:detector_function}.
The classical detector-image theorem specializes to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:classical_reduction}
 \LL_f:=\operatorname{Acc}_{N\to\infty}
       \frac{N\pi_f(N)}{\Lambda^N}=\Phi(\D).
\end{equation}
To specify the specialization, the general detector of
\cite[Definition 12.4.1]{bch2024} also includes the phases
$(\lambda_j/\Lambda)^n$ of the dominant roots. Hyperbolicity leaves only
$\lambda_1/\Lambda=1$, so this coordinate is trivial.
The factor denoted $u$ in that source is identically one. Its equation
(12.4) is consequently exactly~\eqref{eq:detector_function}, with the
continuous zero values in~\eqref{eq:kernel_zero}. Its Theorem 12.4.3(ii)
then gives~\eqref{eq:classical_reduction}. Thus the counting asymptotics
and the detector construction in this step are entirely prior input.

\begin{corollary}[Orbit-limit geometry]\label{cor:fad}
Let $(X,f)$ be a positive-entropy hyperbolic FAD system in the convention
above. If $d=0$, the accumulation set of $N\pi_f(N)/\Lambda^N$ is the
finite set in~\eqref{eq:finite_image}. If $d>0$, that accumulation set
is a Cantor set and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamical_cover}
 N_{\eps}(\LL_f)\leq C\bigl(1+\log(1/\eps)\bigr)^{2d}
 \qquad(0<\eps<\eps_0)
\end{equation}
for constants depending on the fixed system. In particular
$\boxdim\LL_f=\hausdim\LL_f=0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The data in~\eqref{eq:fad_counts} satisfy the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:image}; the gcd condition is stronger than the
periodicity used there. Apply that theorem to the classical equality
\eqref{eq:classical_reduction}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Two distortion primes on a solenoid}

Let $X$ be the Pontryagin dual of the discrete additive group $\Z[1/15]$,
and let $T:X\to X$ be dual to multiplication by $2$.
This is a classical $S$-integer system, and its fixed-point formula is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:solenoid_count}
 \#\Fix(T^n)=(2^n-1)|2^n-1|_3|2^n-1|_5
 \qquad(n\geq1)
\end{equation}
by~\cite[Definition 9.1.2 and Proposition 9.1.4]{bch2024}.
In this particular case the formula can also be seen from the finite
quotient $\Z[1/15]/(2^n-1)\Z[1/15]$: localization removes exactly the
$3$- and $5$-parts of $2^n-1$, and duality identifies its cardinality with
the fixed-point count.

The familiar lifting-of-valuations calculation gives
\begin{align*}
 v_3(2^n-1)&=\begin{cases}0,&2\nmid n,\\1+v_3(n),&2\mid n,\end{cases}
 &
 v_5(2^n-1)&=\begin{cases}0,&4\nmid n,\\1+v_5(n),&4\mid n.\end{cases}
\end{align*}
For example, in the first active case write $2^n=(2^2)^{n/2}$ and use
$v_3(2^2-1)=1$; in the second use $2^n=(2^4)^{n/4}$ and
$v_5(2^4-1)=1$. Consequently the FAD data are
\begin{align}
 &A=[2],\qquad c=1,\qquad \Lambda=2,\qquad w=4,\notag\\
 &r_n=3^{-\1_{2\mid n}}5^{-\1_{4\mid n}},\qquad
 s_{3,n}=\1_{2\mid n},\qquad s_{5,n}=\1_{4\mid n},\qquad
 t_{3,n}=t_{5,n}=0.\label{eq:solenoid_data}
\end{align}
The exponent periods $2$ and $4$ are coprime to $3$ and $5$ respectively,
and $r_n$ is a positive gcd sequence of period $4$. The multiplicative
part $2^n-1$ has the unique dominant root $2$. Both primes are active.
Corollary~\ref{cor:fad} shows that the accumulation set of
$N\pi_T(N)/2^N$ is a Cantor set with covering bound
$C(1+\log(1/\eps))^4$. The count~\eqref{eq:solenoid_count} is classical;
the conclusion being applied is the limit-set geometry.

\subsection{A genuinely wild additive example}

Fix an odd prime $p$, and consider $U(x)=x^p+x$ on
$\overline{\F}_p$, the points of the additive algebraic group.
The classical formula is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wild_count}
 \#\Fix(U^n)=p^{\,n-p^{v_p(n)}}
 \qquad(n\geq1).
\end{equation}
It is recorded in~\cite[Example 7.2.7]{bch2024}, which attributes it to
Bridy. The following calculation verifies this input directly without
assigning novelty to the formula.

Let $F(x)=x^p$ and write $n=p^a m$ with $p\nmid m$. In the commuting
composition ring generated by $F$ over $\F_p$,
\[
 U^n-I=(I+F)^n-I=(I+F^{p^a})^m-I.
\]
The first nonzero power of $F$ on the right is $F^{p^a}$, with coefficient
$m\neq0$ in $\F_p$, and the highest is $F^n$, with coefficient one.
The associated additive polynomial therefore has degree $p^n$ and can
be written as
\[
 (U^n-I)(x)=Q\bigl(x^{p^{p^a}}\bigr),
\]
where $Q$ is an additive polynomial of degree $p^{n-p^a}$ and derivative
$m\neq0$. Thus $Q$ has exactly $p^{n-p^a}$ distinct roots over the
algebraic closure. The map $x\mapsto x^{p^{p^a}}$ is bijective there,
which proves~\eqref{eq:wild_count}, including the case $n=p^a$.

Take the size-zero matrix $A$, with
\[
 c=\Lambda=p,\qquad r_n=1,\qquad S=\{p\},\qquad
 s_{p,n}=0,\qquad t_{p,n}=1,\qquad w=1.
\]
The multiplicative part is $p^n$, and the wild exponent is genuinely
nonzero. Corollary~\ref{cor:fad} yields a Cantor accumulation set for
$N\pi_U(N)/p^N$ and the bound $C(1+\log(1/\eps))^2$.
The cellular automaton $(x_i)_{i\in\Z}\mapsto(x_{i+1}+x_i)_{i\in\Z}$
on $\F_p^{\Z}$ has the same classical count
\cite[Example 7.2.6]{bch2024}. Equality of these counts is sufficient for
the same orbit-counting conclusion; it is not a claim of conjugacy between
the two systems.

\subsection{Multiprime and wild distortion together}

Take the preceding additive example at $p=7$ and the product map
$V=T\times U$ on $X\times\overline{\F}_7$. Fixed-point counts multiply
under products; FAD closure under this operation is also recorded
in~\cite[Example 7.1.3]{bch2024}. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product_count}
 \#\Fix(V^n)=(2^n-1)|2^n-1|_3|2^n-1|_5\,7^{\,n-7^{v_7(n)}}.
\end{equation}
Here $A=[2]$ and $c=7$, so the multiplicative part is $14^n-7^n$ and
has the unique dominant root $\Lambda=14$. The $3$- and $5$-data and
$r_n$ are those in~\eqref{eq:solenoid_data}; additionally
$s_{7,n}=0$ and $t_{7,n}=1$. The common period is $4$, and the active
prime set is $\{3,5,7\}$.
This single genuine system lies in both the multiprime and the wild
regimes. Its accumulation set for $N\pi_V(N)/14^N$ is a Cantor set with
\[
 N_{\eps}(\LL_V)\leq C\bigl(1+\log(1/\eps)\bigr)^6
\]
and zero upper box dimension. These examples are applications of one
image theorem, not separate fixed-point-count discoveries.
