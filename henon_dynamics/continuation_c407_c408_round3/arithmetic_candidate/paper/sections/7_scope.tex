\section{Scope, limitations, and provenance}\label{sec:scope}

The two parts of the proof use different hypotheses. The adaptive cover
needs finitely many primes and finitely many exponent types, together
with geometric decay of the detector weights. It does not need exponent
periods coprime to their own prime. In contrast, the perfectness proof
uses positive coefficients to prevent Fourier cancellation, and it uses
coprime exponent periods to put an active center in every coordinate
ball. Dropping the latter hypothesis can change the topological answer
even for one active prime.

\begin{proposition}[A forbidden-period singleton]\label{prop:forbidden_period}
Fix $\Lambda>1$. Retain the definitions of $\D$ and $\Phi$, but allow
$S=\{2\}$, $w=2$, $r_n=1$, $t_{2,n}=0$, and
$s_{2,n}=\1_{\{n\text{ odd}\}}$. Then the exponent sequence has forbidden
own-prime period $2$, and
\[
 \Phi(\D)=\{(1-\Lambda^{-1})^{-1}\},
\]
although the prime $2$ is active.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:detector_group}, which does not use the exponent periods,
gives $\D=\{(a,x):x\equiv a\pmod2\}$. In a term with $a-\ell$ odd,
the argument $x-\ell$ is odd. Its valuation is therefore zero and the
active kernel $\Hp{2}{1}{0}(x-\ell)$ equals one. In a term with
$a-\ell$ even, the kernel is inactive and again equals one. Every
summand of $\Phi$ is thus $\Lambda^{-\ell}$, independently of $(a,x)$,
and summation proves the formula.
\end{proof}

This example belongs to the enlarged analytic data class, not to the FAD
class in~\eqref{eq:fad_counts}. Its image still obeys the covering bound,
but it is not perfect. It therefore separates the metric and topological
roles of the hypotheses without contradicting Corollary~\ref{cor:fad}.

No injectivity, local injectivity, or explicit parametrizing homeomorphism
is proved for the detector. Nor does perfectness alone imply that the
pushforward of Haar measure has no atoms; no such measure-theoretic
assertion is made here. The exponent $2d$ in the logarithmic cover is an
upper bound, and its optimality or an exact Hausdorff gauge is left open.
Signed or complex weights would require a different noncancellation
argument. Infinitely many distortion primes would require a replacement
for the finite-product cover.

The theorem also excludes a nontrivial continuous archimedean phase.
The nonhyperbolic FAD result that the accumulation set contains a
nondegenerate interval belongs to
\cite[Theorem 12.5.2(i)]{bch2024}; it is outside the present image theorem.
For the hyperbolic case, the comparison with open problems remains tied
to the cited public v2 of that work. The forthcoming EMS version listed
in~\cite[reference 6]{cp2026} was not obtained for comparison, so an
unqualified claim about priority over the final book is not supported by
the source audit.
