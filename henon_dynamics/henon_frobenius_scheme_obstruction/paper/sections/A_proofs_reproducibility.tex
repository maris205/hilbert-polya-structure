\section{Proof details and reproducibility}

\subsection{The low-period normalized Jacobian norms}

For $n=1$, set $f=ax^2+2x-1$ and $j=2ax+2$.  Taking the norm of $j$ in
$\Q(a)[x]/(f)$ gives
\[
 \operatorname{Norm}(j)=-4(a+1).
\]
For $n=2$, the cyclic Jacobian determinant is
\[
 j_2=4a^2x_0x_1-4.
\]
Multiplication by $j_2$ on the standard basis
$1,x_0,x_1,x_0x_1$ has determinant
\[
 2^8(a+1)(a-3)^3.
\]
These $D_{a,n}$ are normalized transversality resultants/Jacobian norms, not
ordinary polynomial discriminants (the ordinary $n=1$ quadratic
discriminant is $4(a+1)$).  The factorization
\[
 f_0-f_1=(x_0-x_1)(a(x_0+x_1)-2)
\]
separates the fixed branch from the primitive period-two branch.  With
$u=ax+1$ and $v=ax_0-1$ the two quadratic relations are
\[
 u^2=a+1,\qquad v^2=a-3.
\]
The branch ideals are comaximal over $\Q(a)$, so this gives the
scheme-theoretic product there.  At $a=3$ the primitive branch collides with
the fixed branch; the splitting is not asserted at that specialization.

\subsection{The generic reversor-line marker}\label{app:marker}

The exact period-five marker before specialization is
\begin{align*}
G_a(q)={}&a^6q^6+2a^5q^5+(-3a^5+2a^4)q^4
 +(-4a^4+2a^3)q^3\\
&+(3a^4-4a^3+a^2)q^2+(2a^3-2a^2)q
 -a^3+2a^2-a-1.
\end{align*}
It is obtained as the greatest common divisor of the two equations defining
$\Fix(H_a^5)$ after setting $p=q$, divided by the fixed-point factor
$aq^2+2q-1$.  The producer derives this expression; it is not inserted as a
fit.

\subsection{Limitations}

We do not prove that $X_{a,n}$ is reduced in characteristic zero for every
fixed $a$ and $n$.  Scheme-level formal period requires extra care when the
characteristic divides $n$.  The global Dedekind factors discussed in the main
text can have nontrivial zeros and may contain $\zeta(s)$; what they do not
provide is a new H\'enon-specific gamma factor, exact Riemann divisor, or
self-adjoint realization required by Hilbert--P\'olya.
