\section{Route-A evaluation and residual question}

The local determinant in Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} is exact.  Its exactness
does not make it a candidate Riemann determinant: the determinant is universal
for finite schemes and has no target divisor.  Table~\ref{tab:routea} records
the resulting Route-A decision.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Scoped Route-A evaluation.}\label{tab:routea}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
layer & verdict & decisive reason\\
\midrule
A1 & \texttt{A1\_WEAK} & intrinsic cycles, but no prime-like clock or amplitude\\
A2 & \texttt{A2\_FAIL} & universal root-of-unity permutation determinant\\
A3 & \texttt{A3\_FAIL} & no completed Riemann divisor or counting law\\
A4 & \texttt{A4\_FAIL} & no natural operator lift of the arithmetic scheme factor\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The overall label is \texttt{ROUTE\_A\_REJECTED}; Route B is not authorized
for this registered candidate.  The failure is structural rather than
numerical.  This ruling does not deny the nontrivial zeros of the classical
global Dedekind/Artin factors, nor does it close every joint or
positive-dimensional mechanism.  It says that the fixed-$n$ local
rationality and finite trace decomposition are universal controls and hence
have no discriminating value for a new H\'enon-derived Riemann divisor.

A narrower arithmetic problem survives.  On exact-period points, absolute
Galois commutes with $H_a$ and with the reversor $R$.  Its image therefore lies
in the centralizer of a dihedral action, which is generally smaller than the
cyclic wreath-product bound.  Low-period polynomial and Galois calculations
are already well represented in the literature, so a new result would need to
determine a genuinely new higher-period image, a uniform proper-subgroup
obstruction, or the cohomology of a parameter-varying quotient.  None of these
claims follows from fixed-$n$ rationality.
