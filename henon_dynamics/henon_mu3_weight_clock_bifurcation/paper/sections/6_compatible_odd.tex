\section{Direct compatible systems and the odd survivor}
\label{sec:compatible}

\subsection{A scoped finite-rank obstruction}

The leading logarithmic factor in \eqref{eq:main-extraction} occurs with
exponent \(2/n\).  Suppose it were the standard \(L\)-function of a
semisimple direct source-native \(K\)-compatible system \(W_n\) that
preserved the same split-prime trace and retained the weight decomposition
\(E_n\oplus O_n\).  Degree-one primes have density one in \(K\).  Equality
of the leading traces there, Chebotarev density, and Brauer--Nesbitt give
\cite{Serre1981}
\[
 n[W_n]=2[E_n\oplus O_n]
\]
in the semisimple representation ring.  Purity separates the two weights,
so the weightwise dimensions of \(W_n\) would have to be
\[
 \frac2n\rank E_n,\qquad \frac2n\rank O_n.
\]
At \(n=3\), these are
\[
 \frac23(23,40)=\left(\frac{46}{3},\frac{80}{3}\right),
\]
and at \(n=4\), even the total dimension would be
\[
 \frac12(87+168)=\frac{255}{2}.
\]
Since the rank of an ordinary compatible system is an integer, the proposed
direct \(K\)-packets do not exist.

\begin{remark}[Restriction of scalars]
This is not a universal compatible-system no-go.  Restriction from \(K\)
to \(\QQ\) doubles ranks and changes the organization of primes and local
traces.  After that change the bare \(n=4\) parity obstruction disappears:
the half-multiplicities become \(87\) and \(168\).  At \(n=3\), the
corresponding dimensions \(92/3\) and \(160/3\) remain nonintegral.
Galois-orbit counterpackets, nonfactorwise identities, and the inherited
normalized-semifinite determinant are outside the theorem.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Denominator clearing}

The leading odd exponents for \(n=2,3,4\) are
\[
 1,\qquad \frac23,\qquad \frac12.
\]
Multiplication by their least common denominator \(6\) gives the integer
exponents \(6,4,3\).  Let \(\Lambda(O_n,u)\) denote a standard completed
weight-one factor whenever such a completion is known.  Define the formal
expected-completion skeleton
\begin{equation}\label{eq:odd-skeleton}
 \cO_6(s)=
 \Lambda(O_2,2s+1)^6
 \Lambda(O_3,3s+1)^4
 \Lambda(O_4,4s+1)^3.
\end{equation}
If all three factors satisfy their expected standard reflections
\[
 \Lambda(O_n,u)=\eps_n\Lambda(O_n,2-u),
\]
then
\[
 \cO_6(s)=\eps\,\cO_6(-s).
\]
This implication is exact, but its premise is proved here only for
\(O_2\).  Equation \eqref{eq:odd-skeleton} is therefore a
denominator-cleared formal skeleton, not a new Hasse--Weil theorem.

Product stability is natural in motivic \(\lambda\)-ring settings
\cite[Proposition 6.1]{Heinloth2007}, but it presupposes factors that
already possess the relevant rationality and functional equations.  It
does not construct the missing \(O_3,O_4\) analytic packages.

\subsection{Why a formal Gamma product is insufficient}

Serre's Hodge-theoretic Gamma ledger and Deligne's expected completion
specify which finite products would accompany an individual pure motive
\cite{Serre1970,Deligne1979}.  The complete H\'enon denominator tower,
however, produces an infinite list of shifted centers.  Turning that list
into a regularized product requires a spectral zeta function with a
meromorphic continuation at the regularization point and fixed branch and
multiplicity data.  These are part of the definition of a zeta-regularized
product \cite{Quine1993}.

Deninger expressed motivic Gamma and local factors through regularized
determinants \cite{Deninger1991,Deninger1992}.  This is an important
structural precedent, but it does not license a determinant for an
unconstructed H\'enon archimedean spectrum.  We therefore stop at the
finite odd skeleton \eqref{eq:odd-skeleton}.
