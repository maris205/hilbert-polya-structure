\section{The fivefold Hodge ledger and the next gate}\label{sec:hodge}

The primitive cubic Hodge pieces follow from the Jacobian ring, while the
odd complete-intersection pieces follow from weak Lefschetz and
Hirzebruch--Riemann--Roch.  After the twists in \eqref{eq:packets}, the
three rows are:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
E_2&(0,0)^7\\
O_2&(1,0)^4+(0,1)^4\\
E_3&(1,-1)^1+(0,0)^{21}+(-1,1)^1\\
O_3&(1,0)^{20}+(0,1)^{20}\\
E_4&(1,-1)^8+(0,0)^{71}+(-1,1)^8\\
O_4&(2,-1)^1+(1,0)^{83}+(0,1)^{83}+(-1,2)^1.
\end{array}
\]
At the unique complex place of \(K\), the corresponding expected
Deligne--Serre Gamma ledger in the present motivic normalization is
\[
 \Gamma_{\CC}(u)=2(2\pi)^{-u}\Gamma(u),
\]
and
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
E_2&\Gamma_{\CC}(u)^7\\
O_2&\Gamma_{\CC}(u)^8\\
E_3&\Gamma_{\CC}(u)^{21}\Gamma_{\CC}(u+1)^2\\
O_3&\Gamma_{\CC}(u)^{40}\\
E_4&\Gamma_{\CC}(u)^{71}\Gamma_{\CC}(u+1)^{16}\\
O_4&\Gamma_{\CC}(u)^{166}\Gamma_{\CC}(u+1)^2.
\end{array}
\]
This is an expected sector Gamma ledger obtained from the Hodge types
\cite{Serre1970,Deligne1979}.  It neither proves the \(n=3,4\)
functional equations nor defines an infinite Gamma product for the full
H\'enon denominator tower.

We give a finite independent derivation of the last line.

\subsection[The chi-y calculation]{The \(\chi_y\) calculation}

For \(X_4=(2,3)\subset\PP^7\), define the Hirzebruch characteristic
series
\[
 Q_y(x)=\frac{x(1+ye^{-x})}{1-e^{-x}}.
\]
Hirzebruch--Riemann--Roch \cite{Hirzebruch1995} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chiy-coefficient}
 \chi_y(X_4)=6[H^5]\,
 \frac{Q_y(H)^8}{(1+y)Q_y(2H)Q_y(3H)}.
\end{equation}
Only terms through degree five contribute.  The finite expansion
\[
 Q_y(x)=(1+y)+\frac{1-y}{2}x+\frac{1+y}{12}x^2
 -\frac{1+y}{720}x^4+O(x^6)
\]
in \eqref{eq:chiy-coefficient} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chiy-value}
 \chi_y(X_4)=1-82y^2+82y^3-y^5.
\end{equation}

Weak Lefschetz and Hodge symmetry leave two middle Hodge numbers
\[
 a=h^{4,1}=h^{1,4},\qquad b=h^{3,2}=h^{2,3}.
\]
In terms of \(a,b\), the same genus is
\[
 1+(a-1)y+(1-b)y^2+(b-1)y^3+(1-a)y^4-y^5.
\]
Comparing with \eqref{eq:chiy-value} gives
\[
 a=1,\qquad b=83.
\]
Twisting \(H^5(X_4)\) by \(2\) proves the displayed \(O_4\) row.

\subsection{The C52 projector gate}

The types \((2,-1)\) and \((-1,2)\) show that \(O_4\) is not itself the
\(H^1\) of an abelian variety.  Its Hodge ledger nevertheless suggests a
rank-two extreme part and a rank-166 level-one part:
\[
 O_4^{\mathrm{ext}}:\ (2,-1)+(-1,2),\qquad
 O_4^{\mathrm{lev1}}:\ (1,0)^{83}+(0,1)^{83}.
\]
A Hodge decomposition does not imply an algebraic splitting over \(K\).
Nor does a numerical projector at one prime define a compatible system.

\begin{proposition}[C52 gate]\label{prop:c52-gate}
The next positive route requires a \(K\)-rational algebraic correspondence
whose induced projectors on all \(\ell\)-adic realizations are compatible
and separate \(O_4^{\mathrm{ext}}\) from \(O_4^{\mathrm{lev1}}\).
Absent such a construction, the appropriate negative target is a
monodromy or endomorphism theorem obstructing that algebraic splitting.
\end{proposition}

\Cref{prop:c52-gate} is a research gate, not an existence theorem.  It is
large enough to change the analytic route: a rank-two extreme factor could
be tested for a Hecke or automorphic realization, while the level-one
factor could be compared with an abelian-type motive.  Additional
unstructured prime counting would not decide algebraicity.
