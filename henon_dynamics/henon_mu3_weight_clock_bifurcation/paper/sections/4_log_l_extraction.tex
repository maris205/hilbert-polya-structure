\section[The forced logarithmic L-extraction]
{The forced logarithmic \(L\)-extraction}\label{sec:logl}

Let
\[
 L_K^{(S)}(V,u)=\prod_{\mathfrak p\nmid S}
 \det(1-F_{\mathfrak p}N\mathfrak p^{-u}
 \mid V^{I_{\mathfrak p}})^{-1}
\]
be the incomplete standard cohomological Euler product for the frozen set
\(S\).  An omitted standard factor may be restored
only if it is holomorphic and nonzero on the stated domain; we do not
absorb arbitrary rational local factors.

For every good split \(p\notin S\), \eqref{eq:normalization} and
\eqref{eq:trace-main} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cp-denominator}
 c_{p,n}=-\frac{4(e_{p,n}+o_{p,n})}{p-1}.
\end{equation}
Let
\[
 \ell_n^{(S)}(s)=
 \sum_{\substack{p\equiv1(3)\\p\notin S}}c_{p,n}p^{-ns}.
\]
The finite source correction
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bad-source}
 B_{n,S}(s)=-\frac{\ell_n(s)-\ell_n^{(S)}(s)}n
 =-\frac1n\sum_{\substack{p\in S\\p\equiv1(3)}}
 c_{p,n}p^{-ns}
\end{equation}
is an entire Dirichlet polynomial.  For the good-prime sum,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ell-expand}
 -\frac{\ell_n^{(S)}(s)}n
 =\frac4n\sum_{\substack{p\equiv1(3)\\p\notin S}}
 (e_{p,n}+o_{p,n})
 \sum_{j\ge1}p^{-ns-j}.
\end{equation}
At a good split prime \(p\notin S\), the two degree-one primes of \(K\)
have equal traces.
The first Euler-logarithm term of
\[
 \frac2n\Logzero L_K^{(S)}(E_n\oplus O_n,ns+1)
\]
is hence
\[
 \frac4n(e_{p,n}+o_{p,n})p^{-ns-1},
\]
exactly the \(j=1\) term of \eqref{eq:ell-expand}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:residual}
The difference
\[
 R_n(s)=-\frac{\ell_n(s)}n
 -\frac2n\Logzero L_K^{(S)}(E_n\oplus O_n,ns+1)
\]
extends holomorphically to \(\RePart s>0\).  Thus
\(H_{n,S}(s)=\exp(R_n(s))\) is holomorphic and nonzero there.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(j\ge2\), the weight-one part left in
\eqref{eq:ell-expand} is
\[
 O(p^{-n\RePart s-3/2}),
\]
and the weight-zero part is smaller.  For a split-prime Euler-logarithm
power \(m\ge2\), the worst weight-one term is
\[
 O(p^{-mn\RePart s-m/2}).
\]
The \(m=2\) prime sum is normally convergent for \(\RePart s>0\), and
higher \(m\) are better.  At an inert rational prime outside \(S\), the
norm is \(p^2\), so even its degree-one term satisfies the same boundary.
Normal convergence proves the first assertion, and an exponential has no
zeros.  The remaining term \(B_{n,S}\) in \eqref{eq:bad-source} is entire,
and its exponential is also nonzero.
\end{proof}

The original term in \(\ell_n\) is
\[
 O(p^{-n\RePart s-1/2}),
\]
so \(\ell_n\) converges absolutely for \(\RePart s>1/(2n)\).
Exponentiating the identity in \Cref{prop:residual} proves
\eqref{eq:main-extraction}.

\begin{remark}[Meaning of the fractional powers]
For \(n=3,4\), the notation suggested by
\eqref{eq:main-extraction},
\[
 L_K^{(S)}(E_n\oplus O_n,ns+1)^{2/n},
\]
means only
\(\exp((2/n)\Logzero L_K^{(S)})\) on the nonvanishing Euler domain.  A local
divisor need not be divisible by the denominator, so no single-valued
meromorphic root or ordinary rational determinant is inferred.
\end{remark}

At \(n=2\), the exponent is integral and
\(E_2\simeq\Ql(0)^7\), while \(O_2=H^1(C)\).  This gives the exact
factorization \eqref{eq:n2-factor}.  It will provide a theorem-level
witness for the center obstruction in the next section.
