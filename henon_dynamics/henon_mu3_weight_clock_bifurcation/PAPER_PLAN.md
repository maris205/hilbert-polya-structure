# HCS-C51 paper plan

## Working title

**Weight--Clock Bifurcation in Cohomological Hénon Moments**

## One-sentence contribution

The unchanged Hénon prime clock turns the \(n=2,3,4\) moment geometry into
two pure-weight rails of total rank \(4^n-1\), but those rails have
incompatible standard centers except on the leading odd sector, where a
denominator-cleared expected-completion skeleton survives.

## Claim-to-section map

1. **Introduction:** why C50's continuation does not by itself provide a
   completion.
2. **Source and main theorem:** phase, clock, normalization, finite-bad
   scope, and theorem statement.
3. **Two weights and ranks:** projective cancellation, purity, and
   \(4^n-1\).
4. **Logarithmic extraction:** exponent \(2/n\), residual \(H_{n,S}\), and the
   \(\operatorname{Log}_0\) firewall.
5. **Center tower:** canonical \(j\ge1\), exact bifurcation, and twist
   invariance.
6. **Compatible systems and odd skeleton:** scoped rank no-go,
   restriction-of-scalars caveat, and exponents \(6,4,3\).
7. **Hodge gate:** \(\chi_y(X_4)\), the extreme rank-two piece, and C52.
8. **Route A:** inherited continuation/operator result and current limits.
9. **Declarations and limitations:** reproducibility and claim boundary.
10. **Appendix:** finite coefficient expansions and exact replay formulas.

## Required wording firewalls

- The \(n>4\) rank calculation is conditional on smoothness.
- The \(2/3\) and \(1/2\) powers are origin-normalized logarithmic germs.
- Only \(n=2\) has a proved component functional equation in this package.
- “No common center” means no common factorwise standard pure-motive center
  for the locked clock.
- The compatible-system no-go concerns a direct source-native
  \(K\)-packet preserving the split-prime trace.
- Restriction of scalars, Galois counterpackets, nonfactorwise identities,
  and normalized-semifinite determinants remain outside that theorem.
- C52 requires a \(K\)-rational algebraic projector whose realizations are
  \(\ell\)-compatible, not a numerical projector at one prime.
