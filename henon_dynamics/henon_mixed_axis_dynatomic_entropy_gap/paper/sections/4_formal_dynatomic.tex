\section{The formal primitive divisor}

Define the virtual zero-divisor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:formal-divisor}
\mathfrak D_n^{\mathrm{form}}
=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\,[F_d=0]
\end{equation}
for odd $n$.  This definition is unconditional in the divisor group; it does
not assert that every coefficient is nonnegative.

\begin{theorem}[Formal primitive degree and entropy]\label{thm:formal}
The degree of \eqref{eq:formal-divisor} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dn}
D_n=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)2^{(d+1)/2},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dn-asymptotic}
D_n=2^{(n+1)/2}+O(n2^{n/6+1/2}).
\end{equation}
In particular $D_n>0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:formal-entropy}
\lim_{\substack{n\to\infty\\ n\ \mathrm{odd}}}\frac1n\log D_n
=\frac12\log2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:Dn} follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:degree}.  Every proper
divisor of an odd $n$ is at most $n/3$, and there are at most $n$ divisors,
which proves \eqref{eq:Dn-asymptotic}.  The leading term dominates for
$n\ge11$ since $n2^{-n/3}<1$; direct substitution handles the smaller odd
periods.  Taking logarithms proves \eqref{eq:formal-entropy}.
\end{proof}

The adjective \emph{formal} is essential.  A point of smaller minimal period
can appear in a dynatomic divisor with positive excess multiplicity at a
degeneracy.  Standard dynatomic terminology makes precisely this distinction
\citep{MortonSilverman1994,Hutz2010}.
