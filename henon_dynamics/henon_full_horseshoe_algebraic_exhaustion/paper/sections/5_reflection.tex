\section{Mixed-axis effectivity}\label{sec:reflection}

We recall the exact reversible geometry.  Let
\[
R(q,p)=(p,q),\qquad J=R H_6.
\]
Then $R^2=J^2=1$ and $H_6=RJ$.  The fixed line of $J$ has the
parameterization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma}
\gamma(X)=\left(X,\frac{1-6X^2}{2}\right).
\end{equation}
For odd $n=2m+1$, write
\[
H_6^j\gamma(X)=(q_j(X),q_{j-1}(X)).
\]
The mixed-axis closure is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closure}
F_n(X)=q_{m+1}(X)-q_m(X).
\end{equation}
The predecessor divisibility theorem gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degree-closure}
\deg F_n=2^{m+1}=2^{(n+1)/2}
\end{equation}
and shows that every complex root of $F_n$ yields a complex point of
$\Fix(H_6^n)$.

\begin{theorem}[Total reality and squarefreeness]\label{thm:closure-real}
For every odd $n$, every root of $F_n$ is real and simple.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $F_n(X)=0$, the point $\gamma(X)$ is fixed by $H_6^n$.  Theorem
\ref{thm:algebraic-exhaustion} makes this point real, hence its first
coordinate $X$ is real.

For simplicity, the predecessor transversality lemma applies to the two
involution-fixed curves $\Fix(J)$ and
$\Fix(H_6^{-(m+1)}R H_6^{m+1})$.  A multiple closure root makes the two
curves tangent.  Their common tangent is fixed by both involution
derivatives, hence by their product $DH_6^n$, forcing multiplier $+1$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:no-neutral} excludes this.  Thus every root is simple.
\end{proof}

Define the monic primitive quotients recursively over odd integers by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:primitive-quotient}
\Psi_1=F_1,
\qquad
\Psi_n=F_n\bigg/\prod_{\substack{d\mid n\\d<n}}\Psi_d.
\end{equation}
The predecessor divisibility theorem proves that the quotient is a
polynomial over $\mathbb Q$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact primitive effectivity]\label{thm:primitive-effectivity}
For every odd $n$, $\Psi_n$ is squarefree and its roots are exactly the
mixed-axis points of least period $n$.  Consequently
$\mathbb Q[X]/(\Psi_n)$ is finite \emph{\'etale} and totally real.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $X$ be a root of $F_n$ and let $e$ be the least period of
$z=\gamma(X)$.  Then $e\mid n$, and $e$ is odd because $n$ is odd.  Since
$z\in\Fix(J)$ and $H_6^e z=z$, reversibility gives
\[
H_6^{(e+1)/2}z\in\Fix(R).
\]
Thus $X$ satisfies the mixed-axis closure $F_e(X)=0$.  Every lower-period
root is therefore removed by one of the proper-divisor factors in
\eqref{eq:primitive-quotient}.

Conversely, an exact-period-$n$ mixed-axis root lies in $F_n$ and in no
$F_d$ with proper $d\mid n$, so it remains in $\Psi_n$.  Theorem
\ref{thm:closure-real} makes the complete family of closure roots real and
simple.  Hence the quotient is reduced, its support is exactly the claimed
set, and the quotient algebra is finite \emph{\'etale} and totally real.
\end{proof}
