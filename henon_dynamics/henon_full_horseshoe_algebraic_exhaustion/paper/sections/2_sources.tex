\section{Parameter and source locks}\label{sec:sources}

We use the standard area-preserving convention
\begin{equation}\label{eq:standard-henon}
H_{a,-1}(x,y)=(a-x^2-y,x).
\end{equation}
Define $S(q,p)=(6q,6p)$.  A direct calculation gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaling}
S\circ H_6\circ S^{-1}(x,y)=(6-x^2-y,x)=H_{6,-1}(x,y).
\end{equation}
Thus every dynamical and algebraic statement can be transported through a
real linear conjugacy.

Three imported results are needed.  We state only the portions used here.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Arai proves that the chain recurrent set of $H_{a,-1}$ is uniformly
hyperbolic for
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arai-endpoint}
a\in\left[5.699951171875,\infty\right)
=\left[\frac{23347}{4096},\infty\right),
\end{equation}
and relates this hyperbolicity to $R$-stability on the plateau
\cite[Theorem~2]{Arai2007}.
\item Devaney and Nitecki use the normalization
$(x,y)\mapsto(1+y-Ax^2,Bx)$.  At $B=-1$, the scaling
$(X,Y)=(Ax,-Ay)$ gives $(X,Y)\mapsto(A-X^2-Y,X)$, exactly
\eqref{eq:standard-henon}.  Their threshold
$(5+2\sqrt5)(1+|B|)^2/4$ therefore reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DN-threshold}
a>5+2\sqrt5
\end{equation}
\cite{DevaneyNitecki1979}.
\item Friedland and Milnor prove that a cyclically reduced polynomial
automorphism of algebraic degree $d$ has $d$ fixed points over $\mathbb C$,
counted with algebraic multiplicity \cite[Theorem~3.1]{FriedlandMilnor1989}.
Applied to an iterate of a degree-two H\'enon map, the count is $2^n$.
\end{enumerate}

The numerical work of Sterling, Dullin, and Meiss places the horseshoe
boundary near $5.699$ and supplies useful context \cite{SterlingDullinMeiss1999},
but it is not used as a theorem here.  Likewise, general results on
reversible periodic points and dynatomic cycles provide context
\cite{Kang2014,Hutz2010}; the mixed-axis identities used below are proved in
the locked predecessor packages rather than imported from those papers.

The exact inequalities needed for the path are elementary:
\[
6>\frac{23347}{4096},\qquad
(10-5)^2=25>20=(2\sqrt5)^2.
\]
Consequently $[6,10]$ lies in Arai's connected hyperbolic region, and $10$
lies strictly in the Devaney--Nitecki full-shift region.
