# HCS-C54 proof package

## Claim

For the source forms

\[
C_n=\sum_{i=0}^{2n-1}x_i^3,
\qquad
Q_{n,\rho}=\sum_{i=0}^{2n-2}x_ix_{i+1}+\rho x_{2n-1}x_0,
\]

the following claims hold.

1. For every \(n\ge2\), the full projective monomial ideal stabilizer is
   \(\operatorname{Dih}(C_{3n})\) of order \(6n\).
2. For every \(n\ge2\), the HCS-C53 semilinear descent gives a nonconstant
   finite etale rational group scheme of rank \(6n\) with exactly two
   rational geometric elements.
3. For every packet-admissible row, an actual finite-rank rational
   compatible system realizes the prescribed split exponent \(4/n\), at
   trace level or at complete local-factor level, if and only if \(n\mid4\).
4. Over the common geometric source group at \(n=3\), no nonzero central
   source-isotypic sector clears the \(4/3\) multiplicity on both pure rails.
5. Every split-invisible virtual rational counterpacket restricts to zero over
   \(K\) and cannot change the ranks or multiplicities used in claims 3--4.

## Status

**PROVABLE AS STATED**, with the category and admissibility hypotheses written
above.  The stronger statements “full PGL automorphism group,” “all-\(n\)
smooth packet,” and “global or inert Euler root” are **NOT CURRENTLY
JUSTIFIED** and are not part of the claim.

## Assumptions

- \(n\ge2\), \(N=2n\), and \(K=\mathbf Q(\rho)\) with
  \(\rho^2+\rho+1=0\).
- “Full stabilizer” means full among projective monomial matrices stabilizing
  the homogeneous ideal.
- The group-form theorem uses the explicit HCS-C53 descent datum \(M_n\).
- The denominator theorem assumes packet-admissibility: smoothness and actual
  rational pure compatible realizations \(\mathsf E_n,\mathsf O_n\)
  with the stated ranks.  HCS-C53 verifies this only for \(n=2,3,4\).
- Split identities are imposed at every good rational prime split in
  \(K\), outside one finite bad set.
- The \(n=3\) two-rail character statement is first over the common
  geometric group over \(K\).

## Notation

- \(\operatorname{Dih}(C_m)\) has order \(2m\).
- \(\operatorname{PMonStab}\) denotes the projective monomial ideal
  stabilizer, not \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{PGL}}\).
- \(e_n=(4^n+5)/3\) and \(o_n=2(4^n-4)/3=2(e_n-3)\).
- \(\operatorname{Res}\) denotes restriction from \(G_{\mathbf Q}\) to
  \(G_K\) in a fixed-\(\ell\) semisimple Grothendieck group generated by
  finite-dimensional continuous \(E_\ell\)-representations from the
  in-scope compatible-system realizations and their semisimple subquotients,
  unramified outside one common finite set.  It is not the unrestricted
  category of all \(G_{\mathbf Q}\)-representations.
- Every Frobenius is geometric.

The characteristic-zero trace-rigidity lemma used below is the following:
if two finite-dimensional continuous semisimple \(E_\ell\)-representations
in this category have equal traces on a Chebotarev-dense set of Frobenius
classes, then their trace characters agree and their semisimple
Grothendieck classes are equal.  This is the only Brauer--Nesbitt
consequence required.

## Proof strategy

The proof has four independent mechanisms.

1. Grade the ideal, normalize a monomial matrix, and solve a closed edge-phase
   recurrence to enumerate the source group.
2. Transport the explicit generators through the semilinear reversal and
   solve two congruences for rational points.
3. At a fixed \(\ell\), semisimplify after restriction, turn split traces into
   a Grothendieck-group identity over \(K\), separate weights, and solve the
   resulting divisibility problem.
4. Compute the exact \(n=3\) Cayley and Fermat characters, then use
   multiplicity divisibility and restriction to close the source-sector and
   counterpacket escape hatches.

## Dependency map

1. Claim 1 depends on the ideal-grading lemma, cycle-support lemma, recurrence
   closure lemma, explicit generators, and presentation count.
2. Claim 2 depends on the HCS-C53 cocycle, the explicit generator transport,
   and the parity solutions of \(2k=0,1\pmod{3n}\).
3. Claim 3 depends on packet-admissibility, density-one degree-one primes,
   Chebotarev, Brauer--Nesbitt, purity, the rank identities, and a finite
   divisor check.
4. Claim 4 depends on the residue-corrected Cayley quotient, exact quotient
   traces, Fermat fixed-monomial traces, and dihedral character orthogonality.
5. Claim 5 depends on the same density/character theorem after restriction;
   it explicitly does not assume that restriction is injective on virtual
   classes.

## Proof

### Step 1. Reduce ideal stabilization to two equation lines

The degree-two ideal piece is \(KQ_{n,\rho}\), so every ideal stabilizer
scales \(Q_{n,\rho}\).  In degree three,

\[
g^*C_n=aC_n+LQ_{n,\rho}.
\]

A monomial pullback of \(C_n\) contains only pure cubes.  The product of a
linear form with the squarefree-edge quadric contains no pure cube.  Comparing
pure-cube coefficients gives \(g^*C_n=aC_n\), and the domain property of the
polynomial ring then gives \(L=0\).

Projectively normalize the diagonal coefficient at \(x_0\) to one.  The
cubic identity forces every other diagonal coefficient to lie in \(\mu_3\).
Thus every candidate is

\[
x_i\mapsto\rho^{e_i}x_{\sigma(i)},
\qquad e_i\in\mathbf F_3,\quad e_0=0.
\]

### Step 2. Enumerate every normalized candidate

The support graph of the quadric is the \(N\)-cycle, so \(\sigma\) is
dihedral.  If the quadric scale is \(\beta\), compare one normalized
transformed edge coefficient with the corresponding target coefficient.
Both lie in \(\mu_3\), so \(\beta\in\mu_3\) and
\(\beta=\rho^q\) for a unique \(q\in\mathbf F_3\).  Thus \(q\) is
derived from equation stabilization.  With the closing-edge indicator
\(c_{N-1}=1\), coefficient comparison at the edge \(E_j\) gives

\[
e_{j+1}=q+c_{\sigma(E_j)}-c_j-e_j\pmod3.
\]

For fixed \(\sigma,q,e_0\), the solution is unique.  Iteration around the
even cycle shows that closure is equivalent to the inverse image of the
closing edge having odd index.  This selects exactly the \(n\) even
rotations and the \(n\) odd reflections.  Every selected support admits all
three values of \(q\), hence the stabilizer contains exactly

\[
3(n+n)=6n
\]

elements.

The explicit lifts \(r,s\) in `THEOREM_PACKAGE.md` satisfy

\[
r^{3n}=s^2=1,\qquad srs=r^{-1},
\]

and \(r\) has exact order \(3n\) because \(r^n\) is the nontrivial
order-three alternating diagonal element.  Every element of
\(\langle r\rangle\) has rotation support, whereas \(s\) has reflection
support, so \(s\notin\langle r\rangle\).  Therefore
\[
r^k,\quad r^ks,\qquad 0\le k<3n,
\]
are \(6n\) distinct elements of the generated subgroup.  They lie in the
exhaustive \(6n\)-element stabilizer list, hence the subgroup is the full
stabilizer and the presentation is an isomorphism.  Its support kernel is
\(\langle r^n\rangle\cong C_3\), and its support image is
\(\operatorname{Dih}(C_n)\).  This proves claim 1.

### Step 3. Descend the group and count rational points

The semilinear reversal satisfies the cocycle identity.  Direct composition
with the explicit generators gives

\[
\delta(r)=r^{-1},\qquad\delta(s)=rs.
\]

These assignments preserve the dihedral presentation and define an
involutive descent action on the constant \(K\)-group and its action.  Thus
the group descends to a finite etale rational group scheme of geometric rank
\(6n\).

For rotations and reflections,

\[
\delta(r^k)=r^{-k},\qquad
\delta(r^ks)=r^{1-k}s.
\]

If \(n\) is even, \(2k=0\pmod{3n}\) has the two solutions
\(0,3n/2\), while \(2k=1\) has none.  If \(n\) is odd, the first
congruence has only \(0\), and the second has the unique solution
\((3n+1)/2\).  Hence exactly two geometric elements are rational.  Since
\(6n>2\), the group scheme is nonconstant.  This proves claim 2.

### Step 4. Prove the necessary denominator divisibilities

Let \(n\) be packet-admissible and suppose \(\mathsf V_n\) satisfies the
split trace identity.  Fix \(\ell\), multiply by \(n\), restrict the
\(\ell\)-adic realizations to \(G_K\), and take semisimplifications.
Semisimplification preserves traces, characteristic polynomials, ranks, and
purity.  Equality therefore holds at a density-one set of degree-one
Frobenius classes.  Apply Chebotarev and Brauer--Nesbitt to the resulting
semisimple representations.  In the semisimple Grothendieck group,

\[
n[(\operatorname{Res}V_{n,\ell})^{\mathrm{ss}}]
=4[(\operatorname{Res}E_{n,\ell})^{\mathrm{ss}}]
+4[(\operatorname{Res}O_{n,\ell})^{\mathrm{ss}}].
\]

The two right-hand classes have different pure weights and no common
irreducible constituent.  Since the Grothendieck group is free on irreducible
classes, equality separates rail by rail and gives

\[
n\mid4e_n,\qquad n\mid4o_n.
\]

Now \(o_n=2(e_n-3)\).  Therefore \(n\) divides \(8e_n\) and
\(8(e_n-3)\), and hence divides their difference \(24\).  Checking every
divisor \(n\ge2\) of 24 yields the residue table in `THEOREM_PACKAGE.md`;
only \(n=2,4\) satisfy both divisibilities.

### Step 5. Prove sufficiency at complete-factor level

If \(n\mid4\), define

\[
\mathsf V_n=\mathsf E_n^{\oplus4/n}
\oplus\mathsf O_n^{\oplus4/n}.
\]

This actual rational compatible system matches every Frobenius
power trace at every good prime.  The origin-normalized logarithm of a local
factor is the generating series of these power traces divided by their
degrees.  Therefore the full split-local logarithm is exactly \(4/n\) times
the source logarithm.  Complete-factor realization implies trace realization,
so the three conditions in Theorem C are equivalent.  This proves claim 3.

### Step 6. Compute and decompose the third-row characters

At \(n=3\), Step 2 gives \(G_3=\operatorname{Dih}(C_9)\).  In Cayley
bidegree \((1,-1)\), the 27 monomials \(zx_i\) and \(yx_ix_j\)
are quotiented by six derivative relations and \(yQ=0\).  Exact row
reduction over \(K\) gives relation rank seven.  The induced action on
residues is polynomial pullback multiplied by \(\det M_g/\det A_g\).
Exact quotient traces are

\[
(20,-1,-1,2,-1,-1,2,-1,-1)
\]

on rotations and \(-2\) on reflections.  Dihedral character inner products
give

\[
H^{2,1}=2\varepsilon+2U_1+2U_2+3U_3+2U_4.
\]

The real conjugate character doubles this to (D.1) on the odd rail.

On the Fermat rail, squarefree Jacobian monomials in degrees \(0,3,6\),
the residue determinant, and the extra trivial line give rotation traces

\[
(23,2,2,-4,2,2,-4,2,2)
\]

and reflection trace \(-1\).  Character inner products give (D.2).  The
six multiplicity pairs are

\[
(1,0),(2,4),(3,4),(3,4),(1,6),(3,4).
\]

Multiplication by \(4/3\) is integral on a multiplicity exactly when that
multiplicity is divisible by three.  No pair has both entries divisible by
three.  The coefficient-field orbit \(U_1,U_2,U_4\) retains pair
\((3,4)\), so orbit packaging does not help.  This proves claim 4 over the
common geometric group.  The rational-group-scheme caveat is handled by
twisting the Fermat descent datum, not by identifying two distinct rational
forms.

### Step 7. Close the split-invisible counterpacket loophole

Fix the same coefficient prime \(\ell\), a finite coefficient extension, and
a finite set containing \(\ell\), ramification, and all bad primes in
scope.  Let \(D\) lie in the Grothendieck group generated by the
finite-dimensional continuous semisimple fixed-\(\ell\) realizations of
the in-scope compatible systems and their semisimple subquotients, all
unramified outside this common set.  If its trace is zero at all but a
relative-Dirichlet-density-zero subset of the good rational primes split in
\(K\), its restriction has trace zero away from a density-zero subset of
the degree-one prime ideals of \(K\).  Those degree-one prime ideals form a
Dirichlet-density-one set.  Chebotarev and Brauer--Nesbitt give

\[
\operatorname{Res}(D)=0.
\]

An actual class with zero restriction has dimension zero and is the zero
representation.  A virtual class need not vanish over \(\mathbf Q\):

\[
\mathbf1-\chi_{K/\mathbf Q}
\]

is a nonzero kernel class.  More generally,
\(U-U\otimes\chi_{K/\mathbf Q}\) is nonzero only under the additional
condition \(U\not\simeq U\otimes\chi_{K/\mathbf Q}\).  Every such class has
restriction and rank zero, so it
cannot modify a \(K\)-rail dimension or \(G_3\)-multiplicity.  This proves
claim 5 without a false injectivity assertion.

### Step 8. Verify the global boundary

At an inert prime, geometric Frobenius on the base change is \(F_p^2\).
Therefore

\[
P_{K,v}(U^2)=P_p(U)P_p(-U),
\]

which need not be a square.  No step above constructs an inert or global
root.  The proof is complete. \(\square\)

## Corrections or missing assumptions

- The phrase “full stabilizer” was narrowed to “full projective monomial
  stabilizer of the ideal.”  Without this category, the full-PGL statement is
  unproved.
- The all-\(n\) packet theorem was narrowed to packet-admissible rows.  Only
  packet data in rows \(2,3,4\) are inherited as certified; semisimplicity is
  not inherited and enters only through fixed-\(\ell\) semisimplification.
- The \(n=3\) rational equivariance statement requires a common twisted
  descent datum.  The unconditional character theorem is over \(K\).
- Restriction is not injective on virtual rational classes; only its kernel's
  inability to alter \(K\)-data is used.

## Open risks

- A future claim that the monomial group is the full PGL automorphism group
  requires a separate automorphism theorem.
- A future row \(n\ge5\) must establish smoothness and construct both pure
  packets before Theorem C applies.
- A global analytic factor requires inert and bad-place data not present here.
