\section{The nonconstant rational group form}
\label{sec:rational-form}

Let \(\tau\) denote the nontrivial element of
\(\operatorname{Gal}(K/\Q)\), so that \(\tau(\rho)=\rho^2\).  Define
\[
\eta_0=0,\qquad
\eta_i=
\begin{cases}
1,&i\ne0\text{ is even},\\
0,&i\text{ is odd},
\end{cases}
\]
and let the inherited semilinear reversal have linear part
\begin{equation}
(M_nx)_i=\rho^{\eta_i}x_{-i}.
\label{eq:descent-matrix}
\end{equation}
Direct substitution gives
\begin{equation}
C_n(M_nx)=C_n(x),\qquad
Q_{n,\rho}(M_nx)=\rho Q_{n,\rho^2}(x),\qquad
M_n\tau(M_n)=I.
\label{eq:descent-identities}
\end{equation}
Thus \(M_n\circ\tau\) is a descent datum for the equation pair.  This
equation-level statement is available for every \(n\ge2\); it does not
assert smoothness or produce a motive in a new row.

\begin{theorem}[Rational form of the source group]
\label{thm:rational-form}
Transport through \eqref{eq:descent-matrix} acts on
\(G_n=\Dih(C_{3n})\) by
\begin{equation}
\delta(g)=M_n\tau(g)M_n^{-1},\qquad
\delta(r)=r^{-1},\qquad
\delta(s)=rs=sr^{-1}.
\label{eq:galois-action}
\end{equation}
Consequently \(G_n\) descends to a finite \({\rm etale}\)
\(\Q\)-group scheme \(\Gscheme_n\) of rank \(6n\), split by \(K\).
It is nonconstant and has exactly two rational geometric elements:
\[
\Gscheme_n(\Q)=
\begin{cases}
\{1,r^{3n/2}\},&n\text{ even},\\
\{1,r^{(3n+1)/2}s\},&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, \(\Gscheme_n(\Q)\cong C_2\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \(\tau\) to the phases in
\eqref{eq:r-generator}--\eqref{eq:s-generator}, conjugate the supports and
phases by \(M_n\), and compare with the same normalized generators.  The
phase identities give exactly \eqref{eq:galois-action}.  Since this is a
Galois action by group automorphisms, standard finite Galois descent produces
the asserted finite etale group scheme.

For rotations and reflections, respectively,
\[
\delta(r^k)=r^{-k},\qquad
\delta(r^ks)=r^{1-k}s.
\]
The fixed-point congruences are therefore
\[
2k\equiv0\pmod{3n},\qquad
2k\equiv1\pmod{3n}.
\]
If \(n\) is even, the first congruence has the two solutions
\(0,3n/2\) and the second has none.  If \(n\) is odd, the first has only
\(0\) and the second has the unique solution \((3n+1)/2\).  This proves
the displayed list.  Since \(6n>2\), not every geometric element is fixed;
the rational group scheme is nonconstant.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reynolds averaging and quadratic transfer}

Only on a certified smooth row do the automorphism graphs define the
correspondences used here.  Over \(K\), the geometric Reynolds idempotent is
\begin{equation}
e_{G_n}=\frac{1}{6n}\sum_{g\in G_n}\Gamma_g.
\label{eq:reynolds}
\end{equation}
It includes all \(6n\) graphs, not merely the rotation subgroup.  If
\(\pi\) denotes quadratic base change from the rational descended row, its
rational transfer is
\begin{equation}
e_{\Gscheme_n}=\frac12\pi_*e_{G_n},\qquad
\pi^*e_{\Gscheme_n}=e_{G_n}.
\label{eq:quadratic-transfer}
\end{equation}
The denominator \(6n\) in Reynolds averaging and the denominator \(2\)
in Galois transfer have different origins and must not be combined.  Neither
\eqref{eq:reynolds} nor \eqref{eq:quadratic-transfer} promotes the
equation-level all-\(n\) theorem to an all-\(n\) Chow statement.
