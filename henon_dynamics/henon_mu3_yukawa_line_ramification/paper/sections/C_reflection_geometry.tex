\section{Exact reflection geometry at the three large-support primes}
\label{app:reflection}

We give the instance part of the ordinary-double-point argument used in
\cref{sec:reflection}.  This appendix deliberately contains no local
factorization or $(e,f)$ row: the output is an inertia class obtained from
geometry.

\subsection{Singular loci on four disjoint pivot strata}

For $j=0,1,2,3$, let the $j$th pivot stratum be
\[
 u_0=\cdots=u_{j-1}=0,\qquad u_j=1,
\]
with $u_{j+1},\ldots,u_3$ as its remaining coordinates.  These strata are
disjoint and exhaust projective space.  Over $\F_p$ we specialize the four
homogeneous first derivatives of $F$ to each stratum and compute their
reduced Gr\"obner basis.  Since $p\ne3$, Euler's identity implies that a
common gradient zero also lies on $F=0$.  In every case below, the
stratum-$0$ ideal has the displayed reduced linear basis, whereas the ideals
on strata $1,2,3$ reduce to the unit ideal.  Thus the projective singular
locus consists of exactly one reduced point.

The coordinates in the third row are reduced to the interval $[0,q-1]$;

they are not overlapping affine-chart tests of the same point.

\subsection{Hessians and first Hensel corrections}

Put $f=f_0=F(1,x_1,x_2,x_3)$.  The affine Hessian matrices at the points of
\cref{tab:reflection-singular-points}, reduced modulo $p$, are
\begin{align*}
H_{283}&=
\begin{pmatrix}
146&136&91\\136&13&203\\91&203&263
\end{pmatrix},\\[3pt]
H_{1801}&=
\begin{pmatrix}
276&326&379\\326&700&902\\379&902&1796
\end{pmatrix},\\[3pt]
\end{align*}
Write $H_q=(h_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq3}$, with $h_{ij}=h_{ji}$.  Its six
independent entries are
\begin{align*}
h_{11}&=1311544089871005361766843220403139014755424019851,\\
h_{12}&=9978936465940142019654684549662337052767937590180,\\
h_{13}&=8612858348525205055038179080298823074941248581837,\\
h_{22}&=2137397871150289173057311598746005216526412381550,\\
h_{23}&=253346114695653176136051595749839977708789738244,\\
h_{33}&=14594568215694396621068754907779675489233126539611.
\end{align*}
Their determinants modulo $p$ are respectively
\begin{equation}
 228,\qquad 1387,\qquad
 6136116089260018682592250996037036352166217747437,
 \label{eq:reflection-hessian-dets}
\end{equation}
and are nonzero.  Consequently each singularity is an ordinary quadratic
singularity, and the gradient map has a unique zero lifting $P_p$ modulo
$p^2$.

For transparency, write
\[
 \mathbf g_p=\frac{\nabla f(P_p)}p\pmod p,\qquad
 H_p\mathbf c_p=-\mathbf g_p\pmod p.
\]
The exact reduced vectors are
\begin{align*}
\mathbf g_{283}&=(141,174,163),&
\mathbf c_{283}&=(222,71,239),\\
\mathbf g_{1801}&=(508,508,1167),&
\mathbf c_{1801}&=(495,192,1792).
\end{align*}

\begin{align*}

&\quad 8802808374986467697053812449802016955221345926655,\\[-2pt]

The critical lift is the exact residue class
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde P_p=[1,\mathbf x(P_p)+p\mathbf c_p]\pmod {p^2}.
 \label{eq:critical-lift-formula}
\end{equation}
For the two smaller primes this gives
\[
 \widetilde P_{283}=[1,62892,20248,67762],\qquad
 \widetilde P_{1801}=[1,892932,346330,3227903].
\]
\Cref{eq:critical-lift-formula}, together with the fully printed
$P_q$ and $\mathbf c_q$, is an exact (and more legible) carrier for the
$q^2$ lift than its three expanded $98$-digit coordinates.

\subsection{Critical value and transversality}

Direct integer evaluation gives the following unit residues:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
p&F(P_p)/p\pmod p&\det H_p\pmod p\\ \hline
283&212&228\\
1801&818&1387
\end{array}\\[4pt]
\begin{aligned}
F(P_q)/q&\equiv
11651769163508833344099877335703302197941640200357\pmod q,\\
\det H_q&\equiv
6136116089260018682592250996037036352166217747437\pmod q.
\end{aligned}
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:reflection-critical-values}
\end{equation}
Because $\nabla f(P_p)$ is divisible by $p$, Taylor expansion and
\cref{eq:critical-lift-formula} give
\[
 f(\widetilde P_p)\equiv f(P_p)\pmod {p^2}.
\]
The critical-value column of \cref{eq:reflection-critical-values} is
nonzero, hence
$v_p(f(\widetilde P_p))=1$.  After the unique critical-point translation,
the completed local equation has a nondegenerate quadratic term and a
smoothing parameter equal to $p$ times a unit.  It follows that the total
space is regular at the critical point and that the base trait meets the
discriminant divisor transversely.

All three residue characteristics are odd.  The Picard--Lefschetz formula
of SGA~7~II, Expos\'e XV \citep{sga7ii1973}, together with the
divided-discriminant bridge for cubic surfaces in
\citet[Proposition~2.3, p.~858; Theorem~3.5, pp.~864--866;
cubic-surface specialization, p.~870]
{saito2012discriminant}, therefore makes tame inertia act by a single root
reflection.  The exhaustive action comparison in
\cref{tab:app-order-two} identifies its subgroup as ToM $2$, with line type
$1^{15}2^6$, double-six type $1^{16}2^{10}$, fixed dimensions $(5,15)$,
and Artin pair $(1,5)$.  This conclusion uses no residue-degree assertion.
