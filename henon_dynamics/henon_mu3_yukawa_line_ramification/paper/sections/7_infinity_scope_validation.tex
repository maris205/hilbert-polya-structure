\subsection{Archimedean type}

The maximal-order field computation gives
\begin{equation}
 (r_1(E),r_2(E))=(3,12).
\label{eq:E-signature}
\end{equation}
Thus complex conjugation fixes three of the $27$ embeddings and exchanges
the remaining $24$ in pairs: its line type is $1^3 2^{12}$.
Independently, exact Sturm counting gives four real roots of
$\theta_{36}$, hence double-six type $1^4 2^{16}$.

The simultaneous order-two table is \cref{tab:order-two-profiles}.  The line
type alone would permit both \Tom{3} and \Tom{5}; the double-six type selects
\Tom{5}.  The finite-field class conventions in
\citet[Table 7.1]{banwaitfite2019delpezzo} provide useful context for
$W(E_6)$ labels, but the match here comes from the two exact real-root
counts and the reconstructed actions.

\begin{proposition}[Complex conjugation]
\label{prop:infinity}
The subgroup generated by complex conjugation is the Table-of-Marks subgroup
\Tom{5}.  Under CTblLib $1.3.1$, complex conjugation itself lies in
element-class index $17$ of \texttt{CharacterTable("U4(2).2")}; that class
has size $540$ and centralizer order $96$.  Its plus/minus dimensions are
\[
 V_6:(d^+,d^-)=(3,3),\qquad
 V_{20}:(d^+,d^-)=(11,9).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Among the four order-two subgroup profiles, only \Tom{5} has the pair of
carrier types $1^3 2^{12}$ and $1^4 2^{16}$.  Its fixed dimensions on
$(V_6,V_{20})$ are $(3,11)$.  Since complex conjugation has order two, these
are the plus dimensions; subtracting from $6$ and $20$ gives the minus
dimensions.  A separate reconstruction in the character table finds one
order-two element class with size $540$ and centralizer $96$, namely index
$17$.
\end{proof}

The first-use convention is therefore: \emph{Table-of-Marks subgroup
\Tom{5} / character-table element-class index $17$ (CTblLib $1.3.1$)}.  The
integers $5$ and $17$ belong to different indexing systems.  Neither is a
root number.

\subsection{What the theorem does not determine}

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.91\textwidth}
\small
\texttt{NO\_BAD\_EULER\_OR\_ROOT\_NUMBER}.  This paper proves no
decomposition Frobenius, bad Euler polynomial or factor, local epsilon
factor, local or global root number, Artin holomorphy, automorphy, analytic
continuation, or functional equation.  The alternatives
$(D,I)=(140,140)$ and $(206,140)$ at $3$ give identical filtered inertia,
Artin--Swan conductors, representation conductors, and field discriminants.
Even a future choice between them would not by itself authorize any of the
excluded conclusions.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

The firewall is a mathematical dependency statement.
\Cref{eq:swan-definition,eq:regular-disc-formula} use only the filtration
$I_i$, so the two possible $D_3$ overgroups are irrelevant to every quantity
computed here.  A bad Euler factor instead depends on a lift of residue
Frobenius through $D_3/I_0$, and epsilon factors require additional
representation-theoretic and additive-character data.  None of these inputs
is reconstructed by the present calculations.

Two other boundaries prevent local evidence from being silently enlarged.
First, $\theta_{36}$ is the only certified degree-$36$ local authority;
$\delta_{36}$ is a bounded nonresult and contributes neither a premise nor
corroboration.  Second, the reflection primes have geometric
ordinary-double-point/Picard--Lefschetz certification; no $(e,f)$
decomposition row is claimed.  The paper also proves no rational-point,
local-point, weak-approximation, Hasse-principle, or Brauer--Manin assertion
for $Y$, and no theorem for all cubic surfaces or all line fields.
