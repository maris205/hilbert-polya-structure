At $3$, the positive-layer contribution is
\[
 \frac12(6,16)+6\frac16(2,10)=(5,18),
\]
and adding the $I_0$ codimensions gives $(11,35)$.  At $5$,
\[
 3\frac14(4,16)=(3,12),
\]
and adding $(4,17)$ gives $(7,29)$.  The tame rows have no positive lower
group, so their Swan conductors vanish and their Artin conductors are the
final codimension column.

\begin{proposition}[Local conductor table]
\label{prop:local-conductors}
The local pairs are exactly those in \cref{thm:main}(c).  In particular,
every Swan conductor and every Artin conductor is a nonnegative integer
obtained layer by layer.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The wild calculations are the exact weighted sums above.  At each tame
$C_3$ prime, \cref{prop:tame-c3} supplies fixed dimensions $(0,8)$, hence
codimensions $(6,12)$.  At each reflection prime,
\cref{prop:reflection} supplies $(5,15)$, hence $(1,5)$.  Outside the
eight-prime support inertia is trivial.  These cases exhaust all finite
primes by \cref{prop:support}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Branchwise conductor--different closure}

For a finite separable extension, the Artin conductor of its permutation
representation is the discriminant exponent; see
\citet[Chapter VI, \S2, Corollary \(1'\), pp.~100--101, and \S3,
Proposition 6 and Corollary 1, pp.~103--104]{serre1979local}.  Applied
locally to each orbit of $D_p$ on the $27$ embeddings, this identifies the
branch permutation conductor with $f_{\mathfrak p}d_{\mathfrak p}$.

\begin{proposition}[Branchwise closure]
\label{prop:branchwise-closure}
For every local branch at $3,5,181,997,2346241$, the permutation conductor
computed from the proved lower filtration equals the direct maximal-order
different contribution.  Summing the branches gives $46$, $36$, and $18$
for the three prime types.  At a reflection prime, the six transpositions
give conductor $6$, equal to the global maximal-order valuation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the maximal-order primes, restrict the $27$-point permutation action to
each $D_p$ orbit and apply the local conductor--different identity.  The
orbit refinements used in
\cref{eq:p3-branch-equation,eq:p5-layer-equation} give the direct rows in
\cref{tab:local-rows}, rather than merely their totals.  For a tame
reflection the Artin conductor of a permutation is the codimension of its
fixed subspace: six disjoint transpositions have $21$ orbits on $27$ points,
so the codimension is $27-21=6$.
\Cref{eq:discE-valuation-vector} supplies the independent global-order value.
\end{proof}

The decomposition \cref{eq:perm-decomposition} now gives
\begin{equation}
 a_p(V_6)+a_p(V_{20})=v_p(\Disc E)
\label{eq:local-conductor-disc-check}
\end{equation}
at every $p$.  We emphasize the direction of use: the fixed-space
calculation proves the two summands, and
\cref{eq:local-conductor-disc-check} checks them.  The equality is not used
to guess one constituent from the other.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Global representation conductors and \(\Disc E\)}{Global representation conductors and the line-field discriminant}}

Multiplying the local powers in \cref{prop:local-conductors} yields:
\begin{corollary}[Global Artin conductors]
\label{cor:global-conductors}
With $A,B$ as in \cref{eq:qAB},
\[
 N(V_6)=3^{11}5^7A^6B,\qquad
 N(V_{20})=3^{35}5^{29}A^{12}B^5.
\]
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 N(V_6)N(V_{20})
 =3^{46}5^{36}A^{18}B^6
 =\Disc E.
\label{eq:global-conductor-disc}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
At $3$ and $5$ use the exponents $(11,35)$ and $(7,29)$.  Each of the three
factors of $A$ carries exponents $(6,12)$; each of the three factors of $B$
carries exponents $(1,5)$.  Addition of the two exponent vectors gives
$46,36,18,6$ by prime type.  The maximal-order discriminant has exactly
these valuations and is positive, proving \cref{eq:global-conductor-disc}.
\end{proof}

The exact field discriminant also closes the support argument.  No finite
prime outside the displayed eight can act nontrivially on the $27$ roots.
Because the $\WE$ action on those roots is faithful, no hidden inertia can
survive in $K$ while disappearing from $E$.

\subsection{The normal-closure discriminant}

For a Galois extension with group $G$ and lower inertia $I_i$, the exponent
of the regular permutation discriminant is
\begin{equation}
 v_p(\Disc K)=\frac{|G|}{|I_0|}
 \sum_{i\geq0}(|I_i|-1).
\label{eq:regular-disc-formula}
\end{equation}
The factor $|G|/|I_0|$ accounts for the regular representation, while the
sum is the local different of the completed Galois extension.  In particular,
$D_p$ does not occur.

\label{tab:discK-exponents}
\begin{tabular}{@{}c c c c c@{}}
\toprule
type&$(|I_i|)_i$&$\sum_i(|I_i|-1)$&$51840/|I_0|$&$v_p(\Disc K)$\\
\midrule
$p=3$&$(18,9,3,3,3,3,3,3,1)$&37&2880&106560\\
$p=5$&$(20,5,5,5,1)$&31&2592&80352\\
tame $C_3$&$(3,1)$&2&17280&34560\\
reflection $C_2$&$(2,1)$&1&25920&25920\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

For example, at $3$ the sum is
\[
 (18-1)+(9-1)+6(3-1)=37,
\]
and $2880\cdot37=106560$.  At $5$,
$(20-1)+3(5-1)=31$, and $2592\cdot31=80352$.

\begin{proposition}[Discriminant of the normal closure]
\label{prop:discK}
One has
\[
 \Disc K=3^{106560}5^{80352}A^{34560}B^{25920}.
\]
This formula is independent of the alternatives
$(D,I)=(140,140)$ and $(206,140)$ at $3$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Insert the four proved filtration types in
\cref{eq:regular-disc-formula}; \cref{tab:discK-exponents} lists every
arithmetic step.  The eight-prime support is exact by \cref{prop:support}.
Since only $I_i$ appears in \cref{eq:regular-disc-formula}, the order of the
decomposition overgroup at $3$ is irrelevant.  These calculations first give
$|\Disc K|$.  Because $K/\Q$ is Galois of degree $51840$, it is either totally
real or totally imaginary; in the latter case $r_2(K)=25920$ is even.  Hence
$\operatorname{sgn}(\Disc K)=(-1)^{r_2(K)}=1$, proving the displayed positive
formula.
\end{proof}

The prime-power formulas, rather than expanded decimal integers, are the
mathematical statements.  For transport checking, the decimal value of
$\Disc E$ has $586$ digits and that of $\Disc K$ has $1{,}931{,}353$
digits; their byte guards are recorded only in \cref{app:certificate}.
