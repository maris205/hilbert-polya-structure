# Narrative report

## Research question

The preceding solenoid round preserved chronology but produced the wrong
clock and no Hilbert--Pólya operator. HCS-C15 asked whether nonabelian graph
covers could do better by splitting periodic orbits into representation
sectors and deleting the obvious trivial branching sector.

## What changed during the round

The first hypothesis was optimistic: a finite Heisenberg voltage cover might
turn word order into magnetic area and yield an arithmetic twisted zeta.
Primary-source review killed that claim quickly. Finite Heisenberg graph
zetas, Artin factors, and Hofstadter blocks already have direct prior art.

The investigation then shifted from constructing one more finite graph to
asking what information the standard construction can retain. That change
produced the main obstruction.

## Main finding

There is a sharp tradeoff between aggregation and resolution.

The canonical quotient \(Z_Y/Z_X\) is representation-invariant, but each
primitive factor depends only on the order of its holonomy. The \(H_7\) words

\[
\mathrm{XXXyxxyxYY},\qquad \mathrm{XXXyxyxxYY}
\]

show that this is a real chronology loss, not a cosmetic character identity.
The words agree in every directed bigram count and are not related by cyclic
or dihedral symmetry, yet they return to different central elements. A fixed
Schrödinger sector distinguishes them, while the aggregate does not.

Resolution therefore repairs observability. It does not repair analytic
scale. A fixed finite-memory twisted graph determinant is a finite
exponential sum and has only \(O(T)\) zeros in a vertical strip. This remains
true for a finite nonlattice roof alphabet. It cannot reproduce the
\(T\log T\) Riemann--von Mangoldt divisor.

Growing towers are the obvious escape from fixed dimension. The Heisenberg
congruence tower shows why that escape is not automatic. A new
one-dimensional character approaches the trivial eigenvalue, and even after
all abelian sectors are deleted, a primitive Schrödinger block has top
eigenvalue tending to four. The corresponding nontrivial Bass poles return
toward the two trivial branching locations.

## What the evidence means

The result is not a universal no-go theorem for dynamical Hilbert--Pólya
programs. It closes the fixed finite-memory branches and rules out the
uniform Ramanujan/new-sector-gap version of one registered tower:

- finite voltage graphs with finite-memory roofs;
- canonical regular-minus-trivial aggregation;
- finite representation-resolved repairs;
- the natural amenable Heisenberg congruence tower under that frozen gap
  criterion.

It does not rule out a separately renormalized infinite determinant or
subtraction scheme for the same tower.

The surviving escape routes are structurally different: nonamenable
property-\(\tau\) towers, infinite-rank transfer operators with proved nuclear
determinants, or joint arithmetic flows with more than one intrinsic place.

## Research status

HCS-C15 is a meaningful negative paper node and is closed for further local
parameter search. The next broad candidate is a compact quaternionic
\(S\)-arithmetic real--\(p\)-adic product flow; if its two-place length or
joint determinant splits, the next fallback is Rauzy--Veech dynamics with a
Kontsevich--Zorich twist.
