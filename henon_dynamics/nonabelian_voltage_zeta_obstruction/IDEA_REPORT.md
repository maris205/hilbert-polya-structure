# Breadth-first idea report

## Question selected

Can a nonabelian regular graph cover retain chronological holonomy after its
trivial branching sector is deleted, and can the resulting twisted
determinant have a divisor dense enough for a Hilbert--Pólya interpretation?

This question was selected because it admits three theorem-sized gates:
observability under aggregation, analytic zero density at fixed complexity,
and behavior of a natural growing congruence tower.

## Cheap gates and decisions

### Direct Heisenberg graph proposal

**Decision: RED collision.** Finite Heisenberg graph zetas, their Artin
factorization, and their Hofstadter spectra were studied directly by DeDeo
et al. A new paper cannot be based on rediscovering those objects.

### Higher nilpotent / residue-symbol proposal

**Decision: RED as a generic proposal.** Higher Milnor and residue-symbol
holonomies already occur in arithmetic Ihara/Galois theory, and
noncommutative voltage towers now have an Iwasawa-theoretic literature. A
graph bridge would require a precise new sector theorem, not a naming
analogy.

### Canonical nontrivial aggregate

**Decision: structural obstruction.** Regular multiplicities turn every
primitive local factor into a function of holonomy order. The \(H_7\)
same-bigram witness proves loss of central conjugacy data without averaging
the chronological dynamics.

### Representation-resolved finite determinant

**Decision: structural obstruction.** Resolution repairs observability but
leaves a fixed exponential polynomial with only \(O(T)\) zeros.

### Heisenberg congruence tower

**Decision: structural obstruction.** Growing dimension evades the fixed
zero-density theorem, but primitive exact-conductor Schrödinger eigenvalues
approach the trivial spectral edge. Nontrivial Bass poles return toward both
branching locations.

## Why this counts as a large step

The round does not tune one finite graph. It gives a three-way decision tree:

\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{aggregate sectors}
\Rightarrow\text{conjugacy-to-order collapse},\\[2mm]
\text{resolve finitely}
\Rightarrow O(T)\text{ divisor},\\[2mm]
\text{take the natural amenable tower}
\Rightarrow\text{primitive branch-pole return}.
\end{array}
\]

This closes the intended HCS-C15 form rather than leaving another numerical
candidate in limbo.

## Next pivot: C15-S

The next system should be a compact quaternionic \(S\)-arithmetic quotient

\[
\Gamma\backslash(\mathbb H\times T_p)
\]

whose primitive regular conjugacy classes carry a joint length
\((\ell_\infty,\ell_p)\) and whose compact quotient offers commuting
self-adjoint archimedean and Hecke operators. The first gate is not numerical
root fitting. It is to prove that:

1. the joint primitive class and repetition multiplicity are canonical;
2. the two length coordinates do not collapse to one lattice clock;
3. the proposed joint zeta does not factor into already known Selberg and
   Ihara pieces;
4. the joint spectral counting law is structurally compatible with the
   target before any detailed computation.

If any gate fails, switch to the Rauzy--Veech/Kontsevich--Zorich candidate.
