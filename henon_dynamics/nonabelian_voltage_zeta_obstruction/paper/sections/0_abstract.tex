Nonabelian graph covers offer exact primitive orbits, representation-resolved
zeta functions, and finite transfer operators, but these features do not by
themselves produce a Hilbert--Pólya model. We isolate three obstructions.
First, the canonical product of all nontrivial Artin--Ihara sectors, with
regular-representation multiplicities, coarsens each primitive holonomy from
its conjugacy class to its element order. An explicit pair of primitive
length-ten words in \(H(\mathbb F_7)\) has identical cyclic directed-bigram
counts and is not related by rotation or time reversal, yet has distinct
central holonomies; the aggregate merges the pair while a Schrödinger sector
separates it. Second, every fixed finite-state, finite-memory determinant with
a constant finite-dimensional twist and finitely many positive roof values is
an exponential polynomial. A Jensen argument bounds its zero count in a
fixed vertical strip by \(O(T)\), incompatible with the
\(\Theta(T\log T)\) Riemann--von Mangoldt law. Third, growing dimension does
not rescue the natural Heisenberg congruence tower: primitive
exact-conductor Schrödinger blocks have top adjacency eigenvalue tending to
four, so nontrivial Bass poles return toward \(1/3\) and \(1\). At modulus
243, a rational Rayleigh bound already exceeds \(7/2>2\sqrt3\). These results
form a scoped exclusion theorem, not a new Artin factorization or a general
no-go result for infinite-rank dynamics.
