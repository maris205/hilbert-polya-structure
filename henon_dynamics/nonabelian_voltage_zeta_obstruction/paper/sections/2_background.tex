\section{Background and prior boundaries}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Finite graph zeta functions.}
Hashimoto's oriented-edge operator and Bass's determinant formula turn the
Ihara Euler product of a finite graph into a finite determinant
\citep{hashimoto1990zeta,bass1992ihara}. For a graph without degree-one
vertices, primitive objects are reduced closed oriented cycles modulo cyclic
rotation; immediate reversal and tails are excluded. This exact orbit ledger
is valuable in a dynamical search because orientation, cyclic multiplicity,
and repetition are fixed before computation. It is also restrictive: for a
unit roof, the inverse zeta is a polynomial in one variable and its pullback
under \(u=e^{-s}\) is vertically periodic.

\paragraph{Graph covers and Artin sectors.}
Regular graph covers admit Artin--Ihara factorizations parallel to finite
Galois theory \citep{stark1996finite,stark2000partii}. If a voltage
assignment \(\alpha:E^\pm(X)\to G\) satisfies
\(\alpha(\bar e)=\alpha(e)^{-1}\), then a primitive base cycle \(P\) carries
a conjugacy class \(g_P\). A representation \(\rho\) contributes the local
factor

\[
\det(I-\rho(g_P)u^{|P|})^{-1}.
\]

The full regular cover uses every irreducible representation with exponent
\(\dim\rho\). Sato's weighted covering formula makes the corresponding
prime-splitting law explicit \citep{sato2006weighted}. Our order-only
identity in Section~\ref{sec:aggregation} is a direct specialization of this
standard theory. It is not a new Artin factorization.

\paragraph{Heisenberg graph precedent.}
Finite Heisenberg Cayley graphs are a natural test because chronological
commutators become central area coordinates and the high-dimensional
representations produce Harper or Hofstadter matrices. These graph spectra,
their irreducible finite-ring Schrödinger blocks, and their Harper matrices
were studied directly by \citet{dedeo2003spectra}; the corresponding covers
and Artin factors appear in \citet{dedeo2005heisenberg}. The discrete
Heisenberg/Harper spectral edge also has direct precedent
\citep{beguin1997harper}. More recent work treats noncommutative
pro-\(p\) voltage towers and Iwasawa-theoretic special values
\citep{kleine2026iwasawa}. We therefore use Heisenberg groups as exact
controls, not as newly introduced zeta objects. The new question here is what
the canonical subtraction and conductor restrictions can observe.

\paragraph{Exponential sums and zero density.}
Zeros of exponential sums have a classical theory
\citep{langer1931zeros,heittokangas2021polya}. We require only an elementary
upper bound: a nonzero entire function of finite exponential type obtained
from a finite determinant has \(O(T)\) zeros in a disk of radius \(T\). We
include a self-contained Jensen proof to expose every hypothesis. The
comparison target is von Mangoldt's zero-count law
\citep{vonmangoldt1905zeros},

\[
N_\xi(T)=
\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}
-\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(\log T).
\]

The mismatch is unconditional and concerns the whole divisor, not the
location of a short list of zeros.

\paragraph{Scope of the obstruction.}
A general H{\"o}lder roof on a finite-symbol shift need not yield a finite
matrix. Nuclear Ruelle operators, infinite weighted graphs, and graph towers
can produce genuinely infinite determinants. Our finite-memory theorem does
not address those objects. Conversely, showing that every finite truncation
has linear density does not control a nonuniform diagonal limit. The
Heisenberg theorem separately rules out the uniform
Ramanujan/new-sector-gap version of one frozen amenable tower; it does not
rule out every renormalized determinant or subtraction scheme and is not
used to overextend the finite determinant argument.
