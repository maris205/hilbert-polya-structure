\section{Exact ledgers}
\label{app:ledgers}

\subsection{The cyclic bigram certificate}

For the ordered alphabet \((x,X,y,Y)\), the complete common cyclic-bigram
ledger of the two words in Proposition~\ref{prop:witness} is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
 & x & X & y & Y\\
\hline
x & 1&0&1&1\\
X & 0&2&1&0\\
y & 2&0&0&0\\
Y & 0&1&0&1
\end{array}
\]
and the entries sum to ten. The row and column marginals both recover the
common directed Parikh vector
\[
(n_x,n_X,n_y,n_Y)=(3,3,2,2).
\]
Thus neither symbol incidence nor a first-order empirical transition matrix
distinguishes the pair. The exact group product does.

\subsection{Resolved central phases}

With \(\rho_k(z)=e^{-2\pi ik/7}I\), the exponent ledgers modulo seven are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
k&1&2&3&4&5&6\\
\hline
-3k&4&1&5&2&6&3\\
-2k&5&3&1&6&4&2
\end{array}
\]
for \(P\) and \(Q\), respectively. Each row is a permutation of the nonzero
residues, so multiplying symmetrically over every primitive central character
collapses the distinction again. A single resolved sector distinguishes the
pair but requires a canonical rule selecting \(k\).

\subsection{D4 one-dimensional factors}

For \(\rho(r)=\varepsilon_r\), \(\rho(s)=\varepsilon_s\), the four unit-roof
inverse factors are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
\varepsilon_r&\varepsilon_s&D_{\varepsilon_r,\varepsilon_s}(u)\\
\hline
1&1&-(u-1)^2(u+1)(3u-1)\\
1&-1&-(u-1)(u+1)(3u^2+1)\\
-1&1&-(u-1)(u+1)(3u^2+1)\\
-1&-1&-(u-1)(u+1)^2(3u+1).
\end{array}
\]
Their product times the square of the two-dimensional factor equals the
direct regular-cover determinant coefficient by coefficient.

\subsection{A general amenable warning}

The explicit Heisenberg proof is a concrete instance of a broader mechanism.
For a finitely generated amenable group, Følner indicators have adjacency
Rayleigh quotient arbitrarily close to the degree. In sufficiently deep
finite residual quotients, their finite supports embed without collision.
After subtracting the constant component, the quotient still has
nontrivial spectrum near the trivial edge because the group order tends to
infinity. This observation explains why trivial-only subtraction is fragile
in amenable towers. The primitive Schrödinger construction in
Theorem~\ref{thm:schrodinger} is stronger for the registered family: it
identifies an exact-conductor nonabelian sector rather than only the full
orthogonal complement of constants.
