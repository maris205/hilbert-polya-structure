\section{A finite-memory zero-density obstruction}
\label{sec:zero-density}

Finite incommensurable roofs are often proposed as a way to break the
vertical lattice of a finite graph zeta. They do break exact periodicity, but
they do not change the growth order of the determinant.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-memory zero density]
\label{thm:zero-density}
Let
\[
B(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}e^{-s\tau_j}B_j,
\qquad
B_j\in M_d(\C),\qquad \tau_j>0,
\]
where \(r,d\) are fixed and the matrices do not depend on \(s\). More
generally, allow every entry of \(B(s)\) to be a finite sum with finitely many
positive real exponents and coefficients independent of \(s\). Then
\[
D(s)=\det(I-B(s))
\]
is a nonzero entire exponential polynomial. For fixed real \(a<b\), its
zero count with multiplicity satisfies
\[
\#\{D(s)=0:a\le\Re s\le b,\ |\Im s|\le T\}=O(T).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The determinant expansion contains only finitely many products of matrix
entries. Thus, for a finite set \(\Lambda\subset[0,\infty)\),
\[
D(s)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}c_\lambda e^{-\lambda s}.
\]
Because every nonconstant roof is positive, \(B(\sigma)\to0\) as
\(\sigma\to+\infty\), so \(D(\sigma)\to1\). In particular, \(D\) is not
identically zero.

Put \(L=\max\Lambda\) and choose real \(\sigma_0\) with
\(|D(\sigma_0)|\ge1/2\). There is a constant \(C\) such that, on
\(|z|=2R\),
\[
|D(\sigma_0+z)|\le C e^{2LR}.
\]
Apply Jensen's formula at admissible outer radii tending down to \(2R\);
these radii avoid the discrete set of circles meeting a zero. The bound
\(|D(\sigma_0+z)|\le Ce^{L|z|}\) and monotone convergence give
\[
\sum_{|s_k-\sigma_0|<2R}
\log\frac{2R}{|s_k-\sigma_0|}
\le \log C+2LR-\log|D(\sigma_0)|.
\]
Each zero in the inner disk of radius \(R\) contributes at least \(\log2\).
Consequently
\[
n_D(R)\le
\frac{\log(2C)+2LR}{\log2}=O(R).
\]
The fixed strip rectangle of height \(2T\) lies in an inner disk with
\(R=T+O(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Riemann divisor exclusion]
\label{cor:rvm}
No fixed determinant in Theorem~\ref{thm:zero-density}, no finite product or
quotient of such determinants, and no product with a zero-free entire factor
has the full nontrivial divisor of \(\xi\) under a fixed nonconstant complex
affine spectral identification.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A finite product or quotient, after cancelling common factors, has total
zero-plus-pole count \(O(T)\) in every disk of radius \(O(T)\).
Multiplication by a zero-free entire factor does not change the divisor. A
fixed nonconstant complex affine map sends the target critical rectangle of
height \(T\) into such a disk. In the standard positive-height normalization,
the Riemann--von Mangoldt formula gives
\[
N_\xi(T)=
\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}
-\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(\log T),
\]
which is not \(O(T)\).
The symmetric count for \(|\Im s|\le T\) is twice this quantity up to
\(O(1)\), so the normalization does not affect the contradiction.
\end{proof}

When all roofs lie in \(h\N\), the determinant is a polynomial in
\(e^{-hs}\). Each nonzero polynomial root generates an arithmetic vertical
sequence with spacing \(2\pi/h\). For a finite irrational roof alphabet,
\(D\) is no longer periodic, but the Jensen bound remains unchanged. Thus
nonlattice finite roofs remove a visible symptom, not the divisor-growth
obstruction.

\begin{remark}[Excluded objects]
The theorem assumes one fixed finite-state, finite-memory model with a
constant finite-dimensional twist. It does not cover a general H{\"o}lder
roof represented by an infinite-rank transfer operator; infinitely many roof
values; Gamma or Dirichlet structure inserted through \(s\)-dependent
coefficients; nonlinear reparameterization; or a tower whose dimension and
exponential type grow with the height at which zeros are counted. The next
section analyzes one such tower separately.
\end{remark}
