\section{Canonical aggregation and chronology}
\label{sec:aggregation}

Let \(Y\to X\) be a finite regular voltage cover with deck group \(G\).
For a primitive base cycle \(P\), let \(g_P\) be its holonomy and let
\(x_P\) be any multiplicative scalar cycle weight. The unweighted case is
\(x_P=u^{|P|}\), while a suspension roof gives
\(x_P=e^{-s\tau(P)}\).

\begin{theorem}[Regular-minus-trivial local factor]
\label{thm:order-collapse}
Put \(o_P=\ord(g_P)\). The local factor of the regular cover is
\[
\prod_{\rho\in\widehat G}
\det(I-\rho(g_P)x_P)^{-\dim\rho}
=(1-x_P^{o_P})^{-|G|/o_P}.
\]
After removing the trivial representation, the canonical local aggregate is
\[
\prod_{\rho\ne\one}
\det(I-\rho(g_P)x_P)^{-\dim\rho}
=(1-x_P)(1-x_P^{o_P})^{-|G|/o_P}.
\]
Hence this aggregate depends on the holonomy only through its order.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The regular representation decomposes as
\[
\Reg_G\simeq
\bigoplus_{\rho\in\widehat G}(\dim\rho)\rho.
\]
Left multiplication by \(g_P\) is a permutation of \(G\) with
\(|G|/o_P\) cycles, each of length \(o_P\). Therefore
\[
\det(I-x_P\Reg_G(g_P))
=(1-x_P^{o_P})^{|G|/o_P}.
\]
Taking inverse determinants and dividing by the trivial factor
\((1-x_P)^{-1}\) proves the two formulas.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What collapses]
Theorem~\ref{thm:order-collapse} does not say that the scalar zeta loses all
chronology. Ordered multiplication can change \(o_P\), and the full product
can retain the distribution of orders across primitive cycles. The theorem
says that two same-weight base cycles with same-order, nonconjugate
holonomies have identical canonical aggregate local factors.
\end{remark}

We now make that loss explicit beyond symbol incidence or first-order
transition statistics. Write \(H_7=H(\F_7)\) as triples with law
\[
(a,b,c)(a',b',c')=(a+a',b+b',c+c'+ab').
\]
Let \(x=(1,0,0)\), \(y=(0,1,0)\), and denote inverses by upper-case letters.

\begin{proposition}[Same-bigram chronology witness]
\label{prop:witness}
The cyclic words
\[
P=\mathrm{XXXyxxyxYY},\qquad
Q=\mathrm{XXXyxyxxYY}
\]
are reduced, primitive, and not related by cyclic rotation or time reversal.
They have identical cyclic directed-bigram counts, but
\[
g_P=(0,0,3)=z^3,\qquad g_Q=(0,0,2)=z^2.
\]
The two holonomies are distinct central conjugacy classes, neither equal nor
inverse, and both have order seven.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct inspection excludes adjacent inverse letters, including across the
cyclic boundary. Neither length-ten word is a proper power. The nonzero
bigram counts of each are
\[
xx:1,\ xy:1,\ xY:1,\ XX:2,\ Xy:1,\ yx:2,\ YX:1,\ YY:1.
\]
Enumerating the ten cyclic rotations of \(P\) and of \(P^{-1}\) does not
produce \(Q\). Chronological multiplication with the displayed group law
gives the two stated central elements. Since \(2\not\equiv\pm3\pmod7\), the
elements are distinct and noninverse. Every nonidentity central element has
order seven.
\end{proof}

The canonical aggregate assigns both words the local factor
\[
(1-x)(1-x^7)^{-49}.
\]
Here \(x=u^{10}\) for the common unit roof; the same conclusion holds for a
weight determined only by the matched one-letter or directed-bigram ledger.
We do not assert equality for an arbitrary longer-memory roof.
It therefore cannot recover the central coordinate even though the
chronological dynamics did. By contrast, in the seven-dimensional
Schrödinger sector with
\(\rho_k(z)=e^{-2\pi ik/7}I\), the local inverse determinants are
\[
(1-e^{-6\pi ik/7}x)^{-7}
\quad\text{and}\quad
(1-e^{-4\pi ik/7}x)^{-7}.
\]
A resolved sector repairs the observability loss. The next section shows why
no fixed finite set of such repairs supplies the required global divisor.
