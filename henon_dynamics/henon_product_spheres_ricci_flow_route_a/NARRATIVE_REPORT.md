For a round `d`-sphere scaled by `a`, the Ricci tensor as a `(0,2)` tensor is
`(d-1)g_round`.  Product curvature has no mixed contribution, so every scale
is affine in physical time.  This elementary observation becomes a complete
all-parameter theorem only after the degeneracies are kept: circles have zero
curvature and never collapse, several curved factors can hit zero together,
and the normalized time can be finite or infinite depending on the total
dimension of precisely that tied set.

The decisive invariant is not the number of factors but `D/n`, where `D` is
the total dimension of the first collapsing factors.  When `D<n`, normalized
time has an integrable endpoint singularity.  Collapsing normalized scales
vanish, survivor scales diverge, and curvature blows up at finite normalized
time.  When `D=n`, every factor is curved and all clocks coincide; this is
exactly the Einstein face.  The unnormalized solution shrinks homothetically,
the normalized metric is stationary, and normalized time runs to infinity.
All-circle products are the separate stationary flat face.

The parabolic blowup retains every tied sphere with scale
`-2(d_i-1)s`.  All noncollapsing factors expand under rescaling and converge
pointedly to Euclidean space, yielding an explicit product shrinker times
`R^(n-D)`.  This proves the Type-I statement rather than inferring it from a
single curvature scalar.
