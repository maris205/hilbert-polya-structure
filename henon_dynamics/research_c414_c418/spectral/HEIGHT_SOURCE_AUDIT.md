## Claim-level ownership assessment

The following comparison is an inference from the stated accessed scopes,
not a quotation from an author about this new candidate.

1. **Established/classical, excluded from novelty:** forward/backward canonical
   height limits, their scaling and good-reduction escape, supported by both
   Kawaguchi and Ingram. The one-orbit two-sided scaling is not a new result.
2. **Established neighboring program:** Hsia/Takehira's one-variable
   height-zeta method; vertical periodicity must not be suppressed in a
   leading asymptotic. The candidate retains the real-height fractional-part
   oscillation explicitly.
3. **Not supplied by those accessed statements:** a disjoint, exhaustive
   degree-valley census of every point in `F_q[t]^2`, coefficient-uniform exact
   weights for all degrees, and the aggregated simple/double pole divisor
   with a meromorphic natural boundary. These are the proposed increment,
   supported by the new self-contained proof, not by absence of search hits.
4. **Why a one-variable good-reduction shortcut fails:** at an edge with equal
   coordinate degree `m`, the two-sided canonical height is `2m`, whereas the
   naive height is `m`. Thus the additive discrepancy is unbounded even
   though all coefficients are constant. There is no finite constant
   height-discrepancy partition of this entire two-dimensional domain of the
   type used in the accessed one-variable theorem.

Two independent original authors support the classical local/global height
baseline; the recent one-variable paper and Hsia's independently verified
record support attribution of the neighboring program. The candidate's new
proof is not made “source verified” by those comparisons: it requires its
own non-author mathematical review. Every bibliographic entry above has an
existence check, while actual full-text scope is visibly narrower for Hsia
and the final Ingram/Takehira versions.

## Targeted repository collision check

Searches in both Hénon registries and relevant Markdown for `canonical height`,
`height zeta`, `bounded height`, `polynomial points`, `degree valley` and
related orderings found older attribution to Ingram but no existing package
for this fixed polynomial-point canonical-height series. C412 is about
periodic rational/integer points of integral conservative quadratic maps;
C401/C404 concern geometric return/Frobenius counts; C409 concerns finite
active-fibre return kernels. Those domains and observables differ from this
one and none of their theorems is being renamed. No frozen package was edited
or its tests rerun. The negative finite-filter/GCD/LCM/operator candidates
remain unadmitted; this height question does not revive them.
