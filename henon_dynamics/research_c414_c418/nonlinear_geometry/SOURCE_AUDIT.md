## Direct sources and access limits

1. **Kim–Krieger–Postolache–Szeto**, *Hénon maps with many rational periodic
   points*: [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01668),
   [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01668v2),
   [v2 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01668v2).
   The arXiv header gives 8 July 2025 for v2; the served HTML's internal
   Date field separately says August 24, 2026. Neither is silently substituted
   for the other, and no corrected final version is inferred from this
   discrepancy. Read the introduction, polynomial
   definition, Lemma 2.1, relevant statements/proof excerpts in §§4–5; PDF text
   corroborates the conflicting count statements discussed in the report.
   Two PDF screenshot requests failed, so visual page inspection is not
   claimed. An author-page search excerpt reports acceptance at JTNB, but
   opening that page failed and no journal final text was retrieved; the
   mathematical comparison is explicitly against arXiv v2. No unseen
   correction is inferred.

2. **Patrick Ingram**, *Canonical heights for Hénon maps*:
   [record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.3609),
   [primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.3609).
   Read the abstract and §7, especially Lemmas 7.1–7.2 and the discussion
   after them. These computations use $(y,x+y^2+b)$, not our conservative
   minus-$x$ quadratic. The denominator observation is a close mechanism
   owner, not ownership of a complete classification for the other sign.
   The paper discusses a genus-14 period-five curve as a future direction;
   its finite checks do not prove unrestricted rational uniformity.

3. **Julia Xénelkis de Hénon**, *Hénon maps: a list of open problems*:
   [journal-hosted full text](https://amj.math.stonybrook.edu/html-articles/Files-2015-2024/23-70/index.html).
   Read §11 and its named conjectures, plus the introduction for convention
   warnings. The page gives publication on 13 August 2024. Its varying
   coordinate/sign conventions were not conflated. It supplies the
   arithmetic problem boundary and attribution to Ingram, not a theorem
   excluding all rational higher periods in our candidate family.

4. **Seung uk Jang**, *Tropical Dynamics of Markov Surfaces*:
   [record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11357),
   [v2 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.11357v2).
   Read the introduction and Theorem A, including the holomorphic-parameter
   restriction in part (b). Metadata dates v2 to 18 December 2025, while
   the served HTML additionally displays a last-revised date of 24 August
   2026; that discrepancy is recorded, not silently harmonized. The record
   explicitly says the previous Theorem C was wrong and fixed. We do not
   use the old statement or claim to have checked all of the 64-page proof.
   The skeleton conjugacy is direct ownership of the proposed tropical
   mechanism, not by itself a classification of rational periodic points.

5. **Marc Abboud**, *Unlikely intersections problem for automorphisms of
   Markov surfaces*: [v4 text](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.05762v4).
   Read the introduction, Theorems A–B and the special $D=4$ discussion.
   Theorem A assumes $D=0$ or $D=2-2\cos(\pi/q)$, $q\ge2$.
   The result compares Zariski-dense common periodic sets and common
   iterates. It is a rigidity result, not a rational point census for all
   integral $D$, still less for all four character parameters.
