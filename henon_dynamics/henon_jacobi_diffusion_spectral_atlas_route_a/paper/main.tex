\section{Source lock and endpoint theorem}
For $\alpha,\beta>0$ let $X$ be the conservative solution on $[0,1]$ of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sde}
 dX_t=[\alpha-(\alpha+\beta)X_t]dt+
 \sqrt{2X_t(1-X_t)}\,dW_t,
\end{equation}
with generator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L}
 Lf=x(1-x)f''+[\alpha-(\alpha+\beta)x]f'.
\end{equation}
This is twice the clock in the neutral recurrent-mutation convention of
Song and Steinrücken~\cite{SS12}; losing that factor halves every eigenvalue.
When an endpoint is regular, the interior expression alone admits other
extensions.  We therefore freeze the canonical conservative/no-flux
realization studied in the Wright--Fisher semigroup literature~\cite{EM10}.

\paragraph{Theorem 1 (complete endpoint atlas).}
At zero the endpoint is regular reflecting for $0<\alpha<1$ and entrance for
$\alpha\ge1$.  At one it is regular reflecting for $0<\beta<1$ and entrance
for $\beta\ge1$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Up to positive constants the scale and speed densities are
\[
 s'(x)=x^{-\alpha}(1-x)^{-\beta},\qquad
 m'(x)=x^{\alpha-1}(1-x)^{\beta-1}.
\]
The speed integral is finite at zero for every $\alpha>0$, while the scale
integral is finite precisely when $\alpha<1$.  Feller's tests give regular
versus entrance.  The same calculation at one replaces $\alpha$ by $\beta$.
The SDE/no-flux lock selects instantaneous reflection in the regular cases.
In particular, equality belongs to the entrance side. \hfill$\square$

\section{Beta reversibility and the complete spectrum}
Normalize the speed law to
\[
 \pi(dx)=B(\alpha,\beta)^{-1}x^{\alpha-1}(1-x)^{\beta-1}\,dx.
\]
Direct differentiation gives the divergence identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:div}
 Lf=\pi^{-1}\frac d{dx}\{x(1-x)\pi f'\},\qquad
 -\langle f,Lf\rangle_\pi=\int_0^1x(1-x)|f'|^2\,d\pi .
\end{equation}
Thus the canonical closure is reversible and self-adjoint.  Define
\[
 J_n(x)=P_n^{(\beta-1,\alpha-1)}(2x-1),\qquad
 \lambda_n=n(n+\alpha+\beta-1).
\]
The Jacobi differential equation gives $LJ_n=-\lambda_nJ_n$; orthogonal
polynomial completeness gives an $L^2(\pi)$ basis~\cite{SS12,G79}.  Therefore
zero is simple, the sharp gap is $\lambda_1=\alpha+\beta$, and
\[
 \|P_tf-\pi(f)\|_2\le e^{-(\alpha+\beta)t}
 \|f-\pi(f)\|_2.
\]

For the orthonormal Jacobi basis $\phi_n$, the transition kernel relative to
$\pi$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heat}
 k_t(x,y)=\sum_{n\ge0}e^{-\lambda_nt}\phi_n(x)\phi_n(y).

Since $\lambda_n\asymp n^2$, $P_t$ is trace class for every $t>0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:det}
 \operatorname{tr}P_t=\sum_{n\ge0}e^{-\lambda_nt},\qquad
 \det(I-zP_t)=\prod_{n\ge0}(1-ze^{-\lambda_nt}).
\end{equation}
This is the full-semigroup source determinant, including the stationary

 Lx^k=k(k+\alpha-1)x^{k-1}-k(k+\alpha+\beta-1)x^k.

\[
 \dot m_k=k(k+\alpha-1)m_{k-1}-k(k+\alpha+\beta-1)m_k,

\section{Two meanings of recurrence}
The finite invariant speed measure and irreducibility make the sample process
positive recurrent: interior neighborhoods are revisited.  Thus the blanket
statement ``there is no recurrence'' is false.  A different, exact semigroup
statement follows from the spectrum.  If $P_Tf=f$ for $T>0$ and
$f=\sum c_n\phi_n$, then
$(1-e^{-\lambda_nT})c_n=0$.  Since $\lambda_n>0$ for $n\ge1$, only $c_0$
survives.  Therefore the periodic $L^2$ observables are exactly the constants.
Sample recurrence and absence of nonstationary semigroup cycles coexist.
