# HCS-C336 narrative report

The single-peak Crow--Kimura equation looks nonlinear because mean fitness is
subtracted from every coordinate.  Its first exact simplification is global:
the nonlinear flow is just the positive linear semigroup generated by the
mutation--selection matrix, divided by its total mass.

The larger step is spectral.  Walsh characters split mutation into `L+1`
Hamming levels.  A master-sequence fitness spike is rank one and sees only one
direction in each level.  Consequently almost the entire mutation spectrum
survives with explicit binomial multiplicities, while the remaining one
direction per level becomes a single `L+1` dimensional rank-one problem.  A
secular equation then locates every missing eigenvalue strictly between the
mutation poles and produces the exact relaxation gap of the normalized flow.

This is not one formula split across several papers: projectivization,
multiplicity accounting, secular interlacing, global convergence, sharp
generic rate and all reducible faces are one closed theorem.  The finite
evidence checks conventions and algebra independently; the analytic proof is
what establishes all `L` and all positive parameters.

Route A stops honestly.  Binary genotypes do not provide rational-prime local
data or a logarithmic prime clock, the flow has no isolated primitive-orbit
ledger, and its finite characteristic polynomial is not a target determinant.
The operator symmetry is only a formal hint, so Route B remains disabled.
