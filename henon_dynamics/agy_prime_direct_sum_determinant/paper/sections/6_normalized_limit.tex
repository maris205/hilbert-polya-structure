\section{Normalized-trace collapse on the positive monoid}
\label{sec:normalized}

The direct sum uses counting trace on every \(p^2\)-dimensional fibre.  A
natural alternative is to normalize that trace by \(p^{-2}\).  The limit
exists, but freeness makes it dynamically trivial on the one-sided positive
language.

\begin{theorem}[Vanishing normalized moments and determinant germ]
\label{thm:normalized-collapse}
For every fixed \(n\ge1\),

\begin{equation}
 \frac1{p^2}\Tr\cL_{s,p}^n\longrightarrow0
 \label{eq:moment-collapse}
\end{equation}

locally uniformly for \(s\in\halfplane\).  More precisely, for every compact
\(K\Subset\halfplane\), define

\begin{equation}
 M_K=\sup_{s\in K,\ p\ \odd}\Norm{\cL_{s,p}}<\infty.
 \label{eq:MK}
\end{equation}

Choose any \(r_K>0\) with \(r_K<M_K^{-1}\).  On
\(K\times\{|u|\le r_K\}\), let \(\Log_0\cD_p(s,u)\) be the Fredholm
logarithm whose value at \(u=0\) is zero.  Then, uniformly on this set,

\begin{equation}
 \frac1{p^2}\Log_0\cD_p(s,u)\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 \exp\!\left[p^{-2}\Log_0\cD_p(s,u)\right]\longrightarrow1.
 \label{eq:det-collapse}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Divide the absolute word trace \cref{eq:local-word-trace} by \(p^2\):

\begin{equation}
 \frac1{p^2}\Tr\cL_{s,p}^n
 =\sum_{w\in\Gamma^n}A_{s,w}
   \frac{\Theta_p(g_w)}{p^2}.
 \label{eq:normalized-word-sum}
\end{equation}

Every word in this sum is nonempty.  Freeness in
\cref{eq:decoder-consequences} gives \(g_w\ne I\), so each normalized
character tends to zero by \cref{cor:regular-character}.  Its modulus is at
most one.  The inherited absolute word majorant permits dominated
convergence.  Local uniformity follows by the same finite-head/uniform-tail
argument used in \cref{thm:sharp-local}: choose a finite word head whose
scalar tail is uniformly small on \(K\), and then use pointwise convergence
on that finite head.  This proves \cref{eq:moment-collapse}.

The operator-norm upper bound in \cref{thm:sharp-local} proves
\(M_K<\infty\), and the trace-norm branch estimate gives a constant \(C_K\)
such that

\begin{equation}
 \Norm{\cL_{s,p}}_{\Schatten1}\le C_Kp^2.
 \label{eq:local-S1-CK}
\end{equation}

Therefore

\begin{equation}
 \frac1{p^2}|\Tr\cL_{s,p}^n|
 \le \frac1{p^2}\Norm{\cL_{s,p}}_{\Schatten1}
       \Norm{\cL_{s,p}}^{n-1}
 \le C_KM_K^{n-1}.
 \label{eq:moment-domination}
\end{equation}

Because \(r_KM_K<1\), the Fredholm series

\begin{equation}
 \frac1{p^2}\Log_0\cD_p(s,u)
 =-\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{u^n}{n p^2}\Tr\cL_{s,p}^n
 \label{eq:normalized-log-series}
\end{equation}

converges on the indicated disc and is dominated, independently of \(p\) and
\(s\), by the summable series
\[
 C_K\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{r_K^nM_K^{n-1}}{n}.
\]
Apply \cref{eq:moment-collapse} term by term and then exponentiate.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact source lock]
\label{rem:normalized-scope}
The character limit is pointwise for each fixed integral matrix.  Its use in
\cref{thm:normalized-collapse} is rigorous for the positive C26 AGY monoid
because the fixed-start decoder proves that every nonempty positive word is
nonidentity, while the local trace theorem supplies the absolute word
majorant.  It is not automatically valid for a two-sided language, where
nonempty identity-holonomy words may occur.  Nor does
\cref{eq:det-collapse} assert convergence on the full \(u\)-plane or define
a product over primes.
\end{remark}

The exponential in \cref{eq:det-collapse} is the only normalized-root
notation used here.  A symbol such as \(\cD_p^{1/p^2}\) would require a
global branch choice and is deliberately avoided.  Also, normalized trace
\(p^{-2}\Tr\) is not operator scaling \(p^{-2}\cL_{s,p}\); after prime
summation the latter yields an \(\Schatten2\) but non-trace-class direct sum
and a \(\det_2\), hence a different object.
