\begin{abstract}
We prove the complete real-line spectrum of the degree-one Lam\'e operator in
a fixed Jacobi convention.  Three explicit band-edge modes, a skew
third-order commuting operator, and its cubic algebraic relation yield two
bands, one finite open gap, and pure absolute continuity.

\section{Operator convention and band-edge modes}
Fix $m=k^2\in(0,1)$ and write $K=K(m)$.  On $L^2(\RR)$ let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 \HH=-D^2+u(x),\qquad D=\frac{d}{dx},\qquad
 u(x)=2m\sn^2(x\mid m),
\end{equation}
with domain $H^2(\RR)$.  The real potential is smooth, bounded, and has
period $2K$.  Hence the stated realization is self-adjoint and bounded
below.  All Floquet multipliers below are measured over $2K$.

The Jacobi differential identities are
\begin{align}
 \sn''&=-(1+m)\sn+2m\sn^3,\label{eq:sn}\\
 \cn''&=(2m\sn^2-1)\cn,\qquad
 \dn''=(2m\sn^2-m)\dn.\label{eq:cndn}
\end{align}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edges}
 \HH\dn=m\dn,\qquad \HH\cn=\cn,\qquad
 \HH\sn=(1+m)\sn.
\end{equation}
Over one potential period, $\dn$ is periodic while $\cn$ and $\sn$ are
antiperiodic.  These formulas identify three edges, but by themselves do not
prove that all higher gaps close.

\section{Commuting operator and spectral curve}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A}
 A=-4D^3+\{6u-4(1+m)\}D+3u'.
\end{equation}
For $\theta\in[-\pi,\pi)$ define
\[
 H^j_\theta=\{f\in H^j([0,2K]):
 f^{(\ell)}(2K)=e^{i\theta}f^{(\ell)}(0),\ 0\leq\ell<j\}.
\]
The closed fiber realization of~\eqref{eq:A} has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-domain}
 D(A_\theta)=H^3_\theta([0,2K]).
\end{equation}
The coefficient of $D$ has derivative $6u'$.  Since all coefficients are
$2K$-periodic, every endpoint product in the Green boundary form cancels:
the common phase $e^{i\theta}$ cancels its conjugate.  Three integrations by
parts therefore show $A_\theta^*=-A_\theta$; equivalently, the adjoint
boundary form imposes the same three quasi-periodic conditions.  Direct use
of~\eqref{eq:sn} gives the two identities
\begin{align}
 u''&=3u^2-4(1+m)u+4m,\label{eq:stationary}\\
 (u')^2&=2u^3-4(1+m)u^2+8mu.\label{eq:first}
\end{align}
Differentiating~\eqref{eq:stationary} and expanding differential-operator
products, with~\eqref{eq:first} for the zeroth-order remainder, proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve}
 [A,\HH]=0,\qquad
 A^2=-16(\HH-m)(\HH-1)(\HH-1-m).
\end{equation}
The first integral is essential here; the stationary second-order equation
alone leaves an arbitrary integration constant and does not prove the second
identity in~\eqref{eq:curve}.

\begin{theorem}[complete Lam\'e spectrum]\label{thm:spectrum}
For every $0<m<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectrum}
 \sigma(\HH)=[m,1]\cup[1+m,\infty),
\end{equation}
and the spectrum is purely absolutely continuous.  Thus $(1,1+m)$ is the
only finite open gap and every higher folded-zone gap is closed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
 R(E)=(E-m)(E-1)(E-1-m).
\]
Floquet decomposition realizes $\HH$ as the direct integral of self-adjoint
quasi-periodic operators with domain $H^2_\theta$ on $[0,2K]$.  The operator
$A_\theta$ has domain~\eqref{eq:A-domain} and is skew-adjoint there.  A fiber
eigenfunction is smooth by its second-order ODE, lies in $H^3_\theta$, and
is therefore in $D(A_\theta)$.  If $\HH\psi=E\psi$ in a fiber,
then~\eqref{eq:curve} gives
\[
 -\|A\psi\|^2=-16R(E)\|\psi\|^2.
\]
Therefore a spectral energy must satisfy $R(E)\ge0$.

Conversely, suppose $R(E)>0$.  On the two-dimensional solution space of
$\HH\psi=E\psi$, equation~\eqref{eq:curve} gives distinct eigenvalues
$\pm4i\sqrt{R(E)}$ for $A$.  Because $A$ commutes with period monodromy,
its one-dimensional eigenspaces are Bloch lines.  Real coefficients make the
two Bloch solutions complex conjugates.  If one has multiplier $\lambda$,
the other has multiplier $\bar\lambda$.  Their Wronskian is nonzero and
constant; translating it by $2K$ multiplies it by
$\lambda\bar\lambda$.  Hence $|\lambda|=1$, placing $E$ in the spectrum.
The three roots of $R$ follow by closure and agree with~\eqref{eq:edges}.
The condition $R(E)\ge0$ is exactly~\eqref{eq:spectrum}.

For a real one-dimensional periodic Schr\"odinger operator, Floquet fibers
have analytic nonconstant band functions; the direct integral therefore has
neither eigenvalues nor singular-continuous spectrum.  Its spectrum is purely
absolutely continuous~\cite{Kuchment}.  Since the second allowed interval extends from
$1+m$ to infinity without another excluded chamber, all higher folded-zone
contacts are closed.  This is an analytic completeness proof, not a finite
coefficient inference.
\end{proof}

The argument through~\eqref{eq:curve} is the \emph{commuting spectral-curve
owner} of revision round zero.

\section{Endpoint and equivalence atlas}

spectrum $[0,\infty)$; the finite gap has closed.  This face also explains

\[
 u(x)\longrightarrow2\tanh^2x=2-2\operatorname{sech}^2x.
