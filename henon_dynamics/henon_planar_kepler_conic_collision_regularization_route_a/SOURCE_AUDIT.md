# Source and provenance audit

## Classical sources

The package uses standard classical results and labels them as such:

- T. Levi–Civita, collision regularization, DOI [`10.1007/BF02404404`](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02404404).
- J. Moser, fixed-energy regularization of Kepler's problem, DOI [`10.1002/cpa.3160230406`](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.3160230406).
- T. Ligon and M. Schaaf, global Kepler symmetry context, DOI [`10.1016/0034-4877(76)90061-6`](https://doi.org/10.1016/0034-4877(76)90061-6).

These citations establish historical ownership and context.  The package makes no priority claim, and it does not assert that its finite ledger is a new global regularization theorem.

## Scope and independent work

The code independently reconstructs exact rational probe rows, high-precision quadratures, symbolic identities, and adversarial checks.  The written theorem package supplies the universal quantifiers and proof sketches.  The finite rows do not prove a continuum statement by sampling.

The Levi–Civita section stops at the configuration map \(q=u^2\), the time density \(dt=|u|^2d\tau\), the fixed-energy equation \(u''=(E/2)u\), and the constraint \(2|u'|^2-E|u|^2=\mu\).  It explicitly does not claim a full Ligon–Schaaf symplectomorphism, global action-angle chart, or three-dimensional collision theorem.

## Prohibited material

No prime lists, Riemann-zero lists, Euler factors, root numbers, automorphy data, target divisors, fitted logarithmic clock, or Hilbert–Pólya operator enters the source, code, evidence, or paper.
