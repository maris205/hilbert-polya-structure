\begin{abstract}
We close a source-local theorem for the one-dimensional equal-well
Allen--Cahn reaction--diffusion equation.  For
$u_t=u_{xx}+\eps^{-2}(u-u^3)$, every monotone heteroclinic from $-1$ to $+1$
is a translate of $\tanh(\xi/(\sqrt2\eps))$, and the equal-well balance
forces its travelling speed to be zero.  The gradient-flow energy dissipates
exactly, with interfacial energy $2\sqrt2/(3\eps)$.  Linearization is the
factorized Pöschl--Teller operator: translation is the simple zero mode, the
shape mode has eigenvalue $-3/(2\eps^2)$, and the essential spectrum is
$(-\infty,-2/\eps^2]$.  Singular scaling faces are stated explicitly.  The
front is heteroclinic, not a primitive periodic orbit, so this is a rigorous
Route-A negative result rather than an arithmetic or Hilbert--Pólya claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Frozen equation and the heteroclinic}
We use the scaled family
\begin{equation}
 u_t=u_{xx}+\eps^{-2}(u-u^3),\qquad \eps>0,
 \label{eq:pde}
\end{equation}
whose $\eps=1$ member is the requested Allen--Cahn equation.  Put
$W(q)=(1-q^2)^2/4$; then \eqref{eq:pde} is the $L^2$ gradient flow of
\begin{equation}
 E_\eps[u]=\int_{\mathbb R}\left(\frac12u_x^2+\eps^{-2}W(u)\right)\dd x.
 \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
For a travelling ansatz $u(x,t)=U(\xi)$, $\xi=x-ct$, the profile equation is
\begin{equation}
 U''+cU'+\eps^{-2}(U-U^3)=0,qquad
 U(-\infty)=-1,\quad U(+\infty)=1.
 \label{eq:tw}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[front, speed selection, and translation uniqueness]\label{thm:front}
Every nonconstant monotone $C^2$ solution of \eqref{eq:tw} has $c=0$ and,
for some $\xi_0\in\mathbb R$,
\begin{equation}
 U(\xi)=U_\eps(\xi-\xi_0):=	anh\!\left(\frac{\xi-\xi_0}{\sqrt2\eps}\right).
 \label{eq:tanh}
\end{equation}
The reverse orientation is $-U_\eps$.  For the increasing orientation,
\begin{equation}
 \frac12(U_\eps')^2=\eps^{-2}W(U_\eps),\qquad
 \int_{\mathbb R}(U_\eps')^2\dd\xi
 =E_\eps[U_\eps]=\frac{2\sqrt2}{3\eps}.
 \label{eq:equip}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Multiply \eqref{eq:tw} by $U'$ and integrate from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$.
The endpoint derivatives and $W(\pm1)$ vanish, giving
\[
 c\int_{\mathbb R}(U')^2\dd\xi=0.
\]
Since a nonconstant front has positive integral, $c=0$.  A first integral is
then $(U')^2/2=\eps^{-2}W(U)$.  Monotonicity selects the positive square root,
and separation of variables gives \eqref{eq:tanh} with one free translation.
The identity $U_\eps'=(\sqrt2\eps)^{-1}\sech^2 y$,
$y=(\xi-\xi_0)/(\sqrt2\eps)$, and
$\int_{\mathbb R}\sech^4y\dd y=4/3$ prove \eqref{eq:equip}.
\end{proof}

\section{Dissipation and the exact interface ledger}
For smooth solutions with finite relative energy (subtracting the two pure
well constants outside a bounded transition region), differentiation of
\eqref{eq:energy} and integration by parts gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dd}{\dd t}E_\eps[u(t)]
 =\int_{\mathbb R}(-u_{xx}-\eps^{-2}(u-u^3))u_t\dd x
 =-\int_{\mathbb R}u_t^2\dd x\leq0.
 \label{eq:dissipation}
\end{equation}
Equality holds only for a stationary solution.  Along the front, the
equipartition in \eqref{eq:equip} splits the surface energy equally between
gradient and potential terms.  Fixing the zero crossing $U(\xi_0)=0$ removes
the sole translation freedom, so the monotone profile is unique under that
phase condition.  This is an energy statement, not a claim of a global
attractor or a quantitative nonlinear convergence rate.

The speed identity also explains why adding a nonzero $c$ by hand would change
the model: equal wells have equal potential values, so a dissipative front
cannot carry a net energy drop.  A tilted potential can select a moving front,
but it is not silently substituted for \eqref{eq:pde}.

\section{Pöschl--Teller linearization}
Write $u=U_\eps+v$ and $y=\xi/(\sqrt2\eps)$.  The linearized generator is
\begin{equation}
 L_\eps=\partial_\xi^2+\eps^{-2}(1-3U_\eps^2)
 =\frac1{2\eps^2}M,\qquad
 M=\partial_y^2-4+6\sech^2y.
 \label{eq:lin}
\end{equation}
Its positive Schrödinger partner has the exact factorization
\begin{equation}
 -M=B^*B,\qquad B=\partial_y+2\tanh y,
 \qquad B^*=-\partial_y+2\tanh y.
 \label{eq:factor}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[closed one-dimensional spectrum]\label{prop:spectrum}
On $L^2(\mathbb R)$ with domain $H^2(\mathbb R)$,
\begin{align}
 \sigma_{\mathrm{disc}}(L_\eps)&=\left\{0,-\frac3{2\eps^2}\right\},
 &\ker L_\eps&=\operatorname{span}\{U_\eps'\},
 \label{eq:disc}\\
 \sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(L_\eps)&=\left(-\infty,-\frac2{\eps^2}\right].
 \label{eq:ess}
\end{align}
The second bound state is $\phi_1(y)=\sech y\tanh y$; the translation mode
is $\phi_0(y)=\sech^2y$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct differentiation gives $M\phi_0=0$ and $M\phi_1=-3\phi_1$.  The
factorization \eqref{eq:factor} makes the zero mode nonnegative for $-M$;
$Bf=0$ has exactly the $L^2$ solution $f\propto\sech^2y$, proving simplicity.
The standard Pöschl--Teller factorization chain (or the one-dimensional Sturm
oscillation count) places the one-node $\phi_1$ as the second and last bound
state.  Since $6\sech^2y\to0$, Weyl's theorem compares $M$ with
$\partial_y^2-4$, whose spectrum is $(-\infty,-4]$; rescaling by
$1/(2\eps^2)$ gives \eqref{eq:ess}.
\end{proof}

\section{Singular faces and Route-A boundary}
As $\eps\downarrow0$, the width $\sqrt2\eps$ collapses while the surface
energy and the spectral scales diverge; this is a sharp-interface scaling, not
a finite-width limiting eigenproblem.  As $\eps\to\infty$, reaction, the
essential edge, and the discrete gap all collapse to zero and the front leaves
every fixed compact scale.  Setting the reaction coefficient to zero gives the
linear heat equation and removes the heteroclinic ODE; setting dimensional
diffusivity to zero removes the second derivative and likewise does not define
a smooth tanh front.  Equal wells exclude every nonzero-speed front in this
model.

The object has a physical PDE clock but no primitive periodic orbit or
cross-period repetition law.  Therefore the strict Route-A record is
