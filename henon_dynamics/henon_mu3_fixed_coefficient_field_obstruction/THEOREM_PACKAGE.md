# HCS-C44 theorem package

## 1. Frozen Hénon moment

Let \(p>3\) be prime with \(p\equiv1\pmod3\), write \(p-1=3m\), and choose
\(\rho\in\mathbf F_p^\times\) of order three.  Since \(p\equiv1\pmod6\),
\(m\) is even.  Put

\[
c=1+\rho=-\rho^2,
\qquad
f(x,y)=2x^3+2y^3+cxy.
\]

This definition is independent of which of the two order-three elements is
named \(\rho\).  Indeed,

\[
 f_{\rho}(\rho^2x,y)=f_{\rho^2}(x,y),
\]

so the two choices give the same value histogram and hence the same \(B_p\).

For \(r\in\mathbf F_p\), define

\[
N(r)=\#\{(x,y)\in\mathbf F_p^2:f(x,y)=r\},
\qquad H(r)=N(r)+N(-r).
\]

If \(\zeta_p=e^{2\pi i/p}\), the conjugate-paired first augmentation moment
inherited from HCS-C43 is

\[
B_p=\frac{2}{p}\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p}H(r)\zeta_p^r. \tag{1}
\]

The factor \(2/p\) is rational and nonzero, so it does not affect the generated
number field.

## 2. Galois stabilizer lemma

For \(a\in\mathbf F_p^\times\), let \(\sigma_a(\zeta_p)=\zeta_p^a\).  Then

\[
\sigma_a(B_p)=B_p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
H(ar)=H(r)\quad\text{for all }r\in\mathbf F_p. \tag{2}
\]

Indeed, equality in (2) says that the rational coefficient vector
\(H(a^{-1}r)-H(r)\) evaluates to zero at \(\zeta_p\).  The kernel of
\(\mathbf Q^p\to\mathbf Q(\zeta_p)\) is generated by
\((1,\ldots,1)\).  Both coefficient vectors have sum \(2p^2\), so the
constant difference is zero.

## 3. Two-moment stabilizer theorem

For even \(k\), let

\[
M_k=\sum_{r\in\mathbf F_p}r^kH(r)
    =2\sum_{x,y\in\mathbf F_p}f(x,y)^k
    \quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p. \tag{3}
\]

### Lemma 3.1

For every such prime,

\[
M_{2m}=2\binom{2m}{m}4^m\ne0\pmod p. \tag{4}
\]

When \(p\ge13\),

\[
M_{2m+2}
=2\frac{(2m+2)!}{(m-2)!^2\,6!}
  2^{2m-4}c^6\ne0\pmod p. \tag{5}
\]

Proof.  For \(e\ge0\), finite-field monomial orthogonality gives

\[
 \sum_{t\in\mathbf F_p}t^e=
 \begin{cases}
 -1,&e>0\text{ and }(p-1)\mid e,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]

In the multinomial expansion of \(f^k\), let
\((\alpha,\beta,\delta)\) be the multiplicities of
\(2x^3,2y^3,cxy\).  A term contributes only when

\[
 3\alpha+\delta=3mu,
 \qquad
 3\beta+\delta=3mv
\]

for positive integers \(u,v\).  Since \(\alpha+\beta+\delta=k\), adding
these equations gives

\[
 3m(u+v)=3k-\delta. \tag{5a}
\]

At \(k=2m\), equation (5a) forces \(u=v=1\), \(\delta=0\), and then
\(\alpha=\beta=m\).  At \(k=2m+2\) with \(p\ge13\), so \(m\ge4\), its
right-hand side is strictly below \(9m\).  Since \(u,v\ge1\), the left-hand
side is at least \(6m\).  Hence \(u+v=2\), so \(u=v=1\),
\(\delta=6\), and \(\alpha=\beta=m-2\).  The two surviving
one-variable sums are both \(-1\), so their product has positive sign and
gives (4)--(5).  Finally \(2m+2<p\), all displayed factorials are units
modulo \(p\), and \(c=-\rho^2\ne0\), proving nonvanishing.

### Theorem 3.2

The scaling stabilizer of \(H\) is exactly \(\{\pm1\}\).

Proof.  If \(a\) stabilizes \(H\), then (3) gives
\((a^k-1)M_k=0\).  Equations (4) and (5) imply
\(a^{2m}=a^{2m+2}=1\).  Hence \(a^2=1\).  For \(p=7\), (4) gives
\(M_4=3\ne0\), hence \(a^4=1\); Fermat gives \(a^6=1\), and therefore
\(a^{\gcd(4,6)}=a^2=1\).  Conversely, \(H\) is even, so \(\pm1\)
stabilize it.

## 4. Maximal real trace field

Combining (2) with Theorem 3.2 gives

\[
\boxed{\mathbf Q(B_p)=\mathbf Q(\zeta_p)^+},
\qquad
\boxed{[\mathbf Q(B_p):\mathbf Q]=(p-1)/2}. \tag{6}
\]

There are infinitely many primes congruent to one modulo three.  For an
elementary proof, suppose a finite proposed list has product \(P\), set
\(n=3P\), and choose a prime \(q\mid n^2+n+1\).  The congruences
\(n\equiv0\) modulo \(3\) and modulo every prime in the list show that
\(q\) is neither \(3\) nor a listed prime.  Also \(n^3\equiv1\pmod q\),
while \(n\not\equiv1\pmod q\), since that would force \(q=3\).  Thus
\(n\) has exact order three modulo \(q\), so \(q\equiv1\pmod3\), a
contradiction.  Hence the degrees in (6) are unbounded.

### Corollary 4.1

There is no number field \(E\) containing all paired moments \(B_p\).  In
particular, these moments cannot be the Frobenius traces of a finite-rank
\(E\)-compatible system with fixed coefficient field \(E\).

This corollary uses only the defining fixed-coefficient-field requirement; it
does not require a rank or conductor estimate.  Allowing a place-dependent
embedding or replacing \(B_p\) by a Galois conjugate does not evade the
argument, because every conjugate still generates a field of degree
\((p-1)/2\).  The conclusion concerns a conventional compatible system whose
frozen Frobenius traces are these exact moments.

## 5. Canonical additive descent control

The zero fiber has exactly

\[
N(0)=p-3. \tag{7}
\]

To see this, the origin is the only solution with \(x=0\) or \(y=0\).  For
\(x,y\ne0\), write \(t=x/y\).  Then

\[
 f(ty,y)=y^2\bigl(2(t^3+1)y+ct\bigr).
\]

For each \(t\in\mathbf F_p^\times\) with \(t^3\ne-1\), this gives exactly
one nonzero value \(y=-ct/[2(t^3+1)]\).  For \(t^3=-1\), it gives none.
The three excluded values are \(-1,-\rho,-\rho^2\).  Hence there are
\(p-4\) nonzero solutions plus the origin.

Let \(K_p=\mathbf Q(\zeta_p)\) and \(K_p^+=\mathbf Q(\zeta_p)^+\).
Since \(\operatorname{Tr}_{K_p/\mathbf Q}(\zeta_p^r)=-1\) for
\(r\ne0\), while the trace of \(1\) is \(p-1\), equation (1) gives

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_{K_p/\mathbf Q}B_p
 =\frac2p\left((p-1)H(0)-\sum_{r\ne0}H(r)\right)
 =2\bigl(H(0)-2p\bigr).
\]

Here \(\sum_rH(r)=2p^2\) and \(H(0)=2N(0)\).  Because \(B_p\in K_p^+\),
its trace from \(K_p\) is twice its trace from \(K_p^+\).  Equation (7)
therefore yields

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbf Q(\zeta_p)^+/\mathbf Q}B_p=-6}. \tag{8}
\]

Thus the additive rational descent forgets the maximal first-moment trace
field and returns a universal coefficient.  This is the handoff to C45, where
the multiplicative norm of the complete local determinant is tested.

## 6. Scope

The theorem rules out:

- one fixed finite coefficient field containing all paired first moments;
- a conventional fixed-field compatible system realizing those moments;
- repairing C43 merely by pairing \(\psi\) and \(\psi^{-1}\).

It does not rule out:

- prime-dependent fields, which are outside the compatible-system target;
- an infinite coefficient field;
- Galois trace, norm, restriction of scalars, or another invariant descent;
- a different Hénon system or a non-compatible analytic construction.
