\section{Scope, limitations, and conclusion}
\label{sec:scope-conclusion}

\subsection{Limitations and interpretation}

The decomposition group at $3$ remains unresolved.  This is not hidden by a
choice of notation: \cref{tab:c59-local-tom140} and
\cref{tab:c59-local-tom206} are logically separate inputs, and the theorem
was designed to be valid in both cases.  Resolving the branch would be a
different problem and is not necessary for the local separator proved here.

The exact minimal polynomials are supplied as orbit products, which define
them completely over $\Q$.  We neither print nor hash their expanded
coefficient vectors.  Likewise, the field discriminants follow from
permutation conductors.  We do not compute
an integral basis or maximal order of either degree-$320$ field, assert
monogenicity, or identify a polynomial discriminant with the field
discriminant.

The local rows record degree, ramification index, residue degree, and a lower-
filtration discriminant entry.  Their differing factor-degree multisets
separate the product algebras.  They do not determine an individual
high-degree completion up to isomorphism.  From the zeta identity alone we
infer none of integral permutation equivalence, isomorphic rings of integers,
equal class numbers, local equivalence, adelic equivalence, or isomorphic
idele groups.  The local and adelic nonisomorphisms proved in
\cref{cor:local-separation} are separate positive conclusions.

Finally, the prior-work conclusion is deliberately bounded.  Prior art
includes James's collision list, Gassmann--Perlis arithmetic equivalence,
relative resolvents, and locally inequivalent arithmetically equivalent
fields.
The precise source and priority boundary is the one stated in
\cref{sec:prior-definitions,app:source-scope}; no stronger priority claim is
made here.

\subsection{Conclusion}

The labelled $27$ lines turn a known abstract $W(E_6)$ collision into two
primitive explicit subfields of one geometric normal closure.  Exact support
stabilizers and the split prime $692717$ identify the fields; the complete
subgroup table and Artin formalism give nonisomorphic fields with the same
Dedekind zeta function; and the inherited ramification package---complex
conjugation together with the lower filtrations---yields the common signature
$(16,152)$ and signed discriminant
$+3^{624}5^{496}A^{192}B^{160}$.  The equal global data conceal a different
$3$-adic product in both the \Tom{140} and \Tom{206} alternatives.  The
construction therefore requires no branch selection.
