\section{Signature, conductors, and the exact global discriminant}
\label{sec:global}

The global invariants are computed on the two $320$-point coset carriers.
The equality of permutation characters predicts equality of their conductor
data, but the proof below uses the exact orbit counts supplied by the fixed
lower filtrations.

\subsection{Complex conjugation and the signature}

Let $c\in G$ be the fixed complex-conjugation element.  On each carrier
$G/H_\pm$, the cyclic group $\langle c\rangle$ has $168$ orbits.

\begin{lemma}[Signature]
\label{lem:signature}
Both fields have signature $(r_1,r_2)=(16,152)$, and their discriminants
have positive sign.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fixed cosets correspond to real embeddings and two-element orbits correspond
to conjugate pairs.  Therefore
\[
 r_1+2r_2=320,
 \qquad
 r_1+r_2=168.
\]
Subtracting gives $r_2=152$ and then $r_1=16$.  The sign of a number-field
discriminant is $(-1)^{r_2}=+1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Permutation conductors from orbit deficits}

Let $V_H=\Q[G/H]$.  If a subgroup $J\leq G$ has $o_H(J)$ orbits on
$G/H$, then
\begin{equation}
 \dim V_H^J=o_H(J),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{codim}V_H^J=320-o_H(J).
 \label{eq:orbit-codim}
\end{equation}
For a lower filtration $G_0\supseteq G_1\supseteq\cdots$ with inertia
$G_0$, the Artin conductor of the permutation representation is
\begin{equation}
 a(V_H)=\sum_{u\geq0}\frac{|G_u|}{|G_0|}
       \bigl(320-o_H(G_u)\bigr).
 \label{eq:permutation-conductor}
\end{equation}
The conductor--discriminant identity identifies this integer with the prime
exponent of $\Disc(K^H)$.  This is a field-discriminant computation; it does
not use a defining-polynomial discriminant.

The common exact orbit vector, in the order
$(I_3,P_3,Q_3,I_5,P_5,C_3,C_2,C_\infty)$, is
\begin{equation}
 (36,56,112,16,64,128,160,168).
 \label{eq:common-orbit-vector}
\end{equation}
Here $P_p$ denotes wild inertia, $Q_3$ the last nontrivial deep subgroup
needed below, $C_3$ a tame cubic inertia class, and $C_2$ the reflection
class.

\begin{proposition}[Exact conductor exponents]
\label{prop:conductor-exponents}
For either $H=H_+$ or $H=H_-$, the field $K^H$ has common conductor
exponents
\[
 v_3=624,
 \qquad v_5=496,
 \qquad v_A=192,
 \qquad v_B=160.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At $3$, the lower filtration has inertia order $18$, first wild group order
$9$, and six occurrences of the deep order-$3$ group.  Substituting
\eqref{eq:common-orbit-vector} in \eqref{eq:permutation-conductor} gives
\begin{align*}
 v_3
 &= (320-36)+\frac{9}{18}(320-56)
    +6\frac{3}{18}(320-112)\\
 &= (320-36)+\frac{320-56}{2}+(320-112)\\
 &=284+132+208=624.
\end{align*}
At $5$, inertia has order $20$ and the order-$5$ wild group occurs in three
positive lower grades.  Hence
\begin{align*}
 v_5
 &= (320-16)+3\frac{5}{20}(320-64)\\
 &= (320-16)+\frac34(320-64)\\
 &=304+192=496.
\end{align*}
At a tame cubic prime only $G_0=C_3$ contributes, and at a reflection prime
only $G_0=C_2$ contributes.  Formula \eqref{eq:orbit-codim} therefore gives
\[
 v_A=320-128=192,
 \qquad
 v_B=320-160=160.
\]
The orbit vector is identical on both coset carriers, so the calculation
applies to both fields.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Exact support and signed identity}

Let $q$ denote the certified prime below, and define
\begin{align}
 q&=14932047182473291995860108491583652133938007263719,
 \label{eq:q-definition}\\
 A&=181\cdot997\cdot2346241=423395612137,
 \label{eq:A-definition}\\
 B&=283\cdot1801\cdot q
 =7610610604104534884325967676315830570581925356194091077.
 \label{eq:B-definition}
\end{align}

\begin{corollary}[Signed field discriminant and exact support]
\label{cor:global-discriminant}
For both signs,
\[
 \Disc(F_\pm)=+3^{624}5^{496}A^{192}B^{160},
\]
and the finite ramified support is exactly
\[
 \{3,5,181,283,997,1801,2346241,q\}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The normal closure $K$ is unramified outside the displayed eight primes.
Every subfield of $K$ is therefore unramified outside the same set, giving
one inclusion.  Conversely, each exponent in
\cref{prop:conductor-exponents} is positive.  After expanding $A$ and $B$,
every one of the eight primes occurs with positive field-discriminant
valuation, hence ramifies in each $F_\pm$.  This gives the reverse inclusion.
The positive sign is \cref{lem:signature}.
\end{proof}

The positive discriminant is an $11{,}658$-digit integer.  As a
reproducibility check, its unsigned no-newline decimal expansion has SHA-256
\[
\hexsha{7f3ed0f731e5905f9af8254df2114ad15c2bb7d96cfa9a8b464a58ae8ea3ae70}.
\]
The decimal expansion itself is not needed in the paper.  Neither the
factorization nor its digest asserts an integral basis, a maximal order,
monogenicity, or equality between the discriminant of an expanded
degree-$320$ polynomial and the field discriminant.  This completes the
proof of \cref{thm:integrated}(iv).
