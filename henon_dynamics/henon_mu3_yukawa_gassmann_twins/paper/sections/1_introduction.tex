\section{Introduction and the integrated theorem}
\label{sec:introduction}

Equal Dedekind zeta functions conceal information at ramified primes.  They
force the same unramified residue-degree data, yet they need not determine
the products of local fields at the excluded primes.  The phenomenon is
classical; the problem addressed here is to realize it primitively and
exactly inside the line field of one cubic surface, while retaining every
decomposition-group branch left by the preceding ramification analysis.

Let $Y/\Q$ be the fixed smooth cubic surface, let $E$ be its degree-$27$
line field, and let $K$ be its fixed normal closure.  The released labelled
line action identifies
\begin{equation}
  G=\Gal(K/\Q)=\WE,
  \qquad |G|=51840.
  \label{eq:intro-G}
\end{equation}
The complete subgroup computation of James already records $350$ subgroup
classes, $339$ permutation-character profiles, and eleven Gassmann
collisions \citep[\S3.1, p.~22]{james2022bayesian}.  An abstract collision
$K^{H_+},K^{H_-}$, however, does not supply primitive elements in the
coordinates of the $27$ lines.  Nor does it determine whether the same
local separator works for both decomposition groups at $3$ that survive the
ramification theorem for $K$.

We now give the durable data needed to state the result.  In the fixed
$27$-point labelling, define $H_+$ and $H_-$ by the following two displays:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
H_+=\langle
 &(3,19,6)(4,21,17)(5,20,16)(7,24,27)(8,11,23)(12,15,22), \\
 &(1,16,23,10,7,15)(2,27,11,14,5,22)(3,13,20,19,9,24) \\
 &\qquad (4,12,18,17,8,25)(6,26,21), \\
 &(1,26,10)(2,13,25)(3,22,8)(4,20,27)(5,24,17)(6,15,23) \\
 &\qquad (7,21,16)(9,14,18)(11,19,12)
\rangle .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Hplus-cycles}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
H_-=\langle
 &(1,5,3,6,4,2)(7,10,17,18,20,13)(8,21,16,15,9,14) \\
 &\qquad (11,19,12)(22,26,24,27,25,23), \\
 &(1,7,16,11,22,10)(2,15,27)(3,13,9,20,24,19) \\
 &\qquad (4,12,8,18,25,17)(5,26,23,21,14,6), \\
 &(1,16,22)(2,23,21)(3,9,24)(4,8,25)(5,26,15)(6,27,14)
\rangle .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:Hminus-cycles}
\end{equation}
These arrays define the embedded groups.  Their package positions $301$ and
$303$ will be used only as frozen-version locators.
For the two retained C58 decomposition-group alternatives at $3$,
\Tom{140} and \Tom{206} likewise denote versioned Table-of-Marks locators;
the exact embedded arrays and filtration data, not these numbers, are the
durable definitions.

Write the nonmonic degree-$27$ line eliminant as $g(d)\in\Z[d]$, let
$L>0$ be its leading coefficient, and label its roots $d_1,\ldots,d_{27}$
as above.  Set
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_i=Ld_i,
  \qquad
  \cS_+=H_+\!\cdot\!\{1,2\}\sqcup H_+\!\cdot\!\{1,9\},
  \qquad
  \cS_-=H_-\!\cdot\!\{1,2\},
  \label{eq:intro-supports}
\end{equation}
where the two $H_+$-orbits are disjoint and have size $27$ each, while
$|\cS_-|=81$.  Our generators are always the scaled integral sums
\begin{equation}
  \eta_\pm=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\cS_\pm}\alpha_i\alpha_j.
  \label{eq:intro-eta}
\end{equation}
If an unscaled sum is mentioned, it is denoted $\widetilde\eta_\pm$ and
$\eta_\pm=L^2\widetilde\eta_\pm$.  The symbol $\eta_\pm$ never denotes
the unscaled object.

\begin{theorem}[Primitive $W(E_6)$ Gassmann twins]
\label{thm:integrated}
With the exact embedded subgroups in \eqref{eq:Hplus-cycles} and
\eqref{eq:Hminus-cycles} and the scaled sums in \eqref{eq:intro-eta}, put
\[
 R_\pm(T)=\prod_{gH_\pm\in G/H_\pm}(T-g\eta_\pm),
 \qquad F_\pm=\Q(\eta_\pm).
\]
Then the following five assertions hold as one integrated statement.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.6em]
\item The polynomials $R_\pm(T)$ are monic integral irreducible separable
polynomials of degree $320$, in exact product form, and
\[
  F_+=K^{H_+},\qquad F_-=K^{H_-}.
\]
\item Both subgroups have order $162$, index $320$, trivial core, and
normalizer order $324$.  Their SmallGroup identifiers are $[162,11]$ and
$[162,19]$, respectively.  Their full rational transitive permutation
characters agree, and $\{H_+,H_-\}$ is the unique minimum-index collision
within the complete $350$-class $W(E_6)$ subgroup table.
\item The fields are not isomorphic over $\Q$, have common normal closure
$K$, and satisfy
\[
   \zeta_{F_+}(s)=\zeta_{F_-}(s).
\]
\item Let $q$ be the certified prime
\[
 q=14932047182473291995860108491583652133938007263719
\]
and put
\[
 A=181\cdot997\cdot2346241=423395612137,
\]
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 B&=283\cdot1801\cdot q\\
  &=7610610604104534884325967676315830570581925356194091077.
 \end{aligned}
\]
Both fields have signature $(16,152)$, exact finite ramified support
\[
 \{3,5,181,283,997,1801,2346241,q\},
\]
and signed field discriminant
\[
 \Disc(F_+)=\Disc(F_-)=+3^{624}5^{496}A^{192}B^{160}.
\]
\item For each retained alternative $D_3=\Tom{140}$ and
$D_3=\Tom{206}$, the finite \etale{} $\Qthree$-algebras
\[
 F_+\otimes_\Q\Qthree
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 F_-\otimes_\Q\Qthree
\]
are nonisomorphic.  Hence $F_+$ and $F_-$ are arithmetically equivalent
but not locally equivalent, and their adele rings are nonisomorphic.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The theorem joins four instance-specific contributions.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.1em]
\item Exact integral quadratic sums in the labelled line roots generate both
fixed fields; the proof uses support stabilizers and $320$ distinct values
at the split prime $692717$.
\item The complete subgroup table identifies the table-restricted
minimum-index collision, while trivial cores, nonconjugacy, and Artin
formalism give common normal closure, field nonisomorphism, and equal zeta
functions.
\item The inherited ramification package---complex conjugation together with
the lower filtrations---yields signature $(16,152)$, exact eight-prime
support, and the signed discriminant in
\cref{thm:integrated}(iv).
\item Separate complete double-coset tables prove local-algebra
nonisomorphism for both $D_3$ alternatives; neither alternative is selected.
\end{enumerate}
