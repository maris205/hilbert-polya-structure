\section{Prior work, frozen input, and durable definitions}
\label{sec:prior-definitions}

\subsection{Arithmetic equivalence and its local boundary}

Gassmann's character condition and Perlis's number-field formulation identify
equality of Dedekind zeta functions with equality of the relevant induced
trivial characters \citep[Theorem~1]{perlis1977equation}.  This criterion is
the universal bridge used below; the present work does not propose a new
criterion for arithmetic equivalence.  Bosma and de Smit explain the common
global invariants forced by arithmetic equivalence and use permutation
characters computationally \citep[printed pp.~67--68]{bosmadesmit2002small}.
Our contribution is narrower: we give primitive line-coordinate generators
and exact ramified arithmetic for one fixed normal closure.

Equal zeta functions do not imply equality of all arithmetic attached to the
ramified completions.  Explicit common-normal-closure constructions with
different ramified local degrees occur in the literature
\citep[proof of Theorem~1.2]{mcreynolds2015spectra}, and explicit polynomial
pairs can have identical zeta data but different ramification tuples
\citep[Theorems~1.3 and~3.7]{mantillasoler2019question}.  Komatsu separates
arithmetic equivalence from equivalence of adele rings
\citep{komatsu1984adele}.  These works preclude any claim that local
inequivalence or nonisomorphic adele rings are new in general.  What is
specific here is that the same local obstruction is proved for each of the
two decomposition groups still possible in this cubic-surface instance.

We call two number fields \emph{locally equivalent} if their places can be
put in a bijection under which paired completions are isomorphic as
topological fields.  Equivalently, their adele rings are isomorphic as
topological rings; we use this standard equivalence in the sense discussed by
Komatsu \citep{komatsu1984adele}.

\subsection{Subgroup collisions and resolvents}

James's complete $W(E_6)$ computation is the prior source for the figures
$350$, $339$, and eleven collision pairs
\citep[\S3.1, p.~22]{james2022bayesian}.  We neither rediscover those
collisions nor call the pair in \cref{thm:integrated} a new Gassmann pair.
The phrase ``unique minimum-index'' will always mean unique among those
eleven collision buckets in the complete $350$-class table.  It is not a
minimum-degree assertion about arithmetically equivalent fields in general.

The resolvent method is likewise classical.  Stauduhar constructs resolvents
from ordered roots and subgroup invariants
\citep{stauduhar1973determination}; Fieker and Kl\"uners develop practical
relative invariants and stabilizer methods
\citep[\S\S1--2 and Remark~2.1]{fiekerklueners2014computation}.  Our orbit
products use this established framework.  Their special feature is the exact
quadratic support in the labelled $27$-line carrier and a split-prime
noncollision proof of primitivity, not a new resolvent algorithm.

Explicit realization of subgroups of $W(E_6)$ through lines on cubic
surfaces is also established.  Elsenhans and Jahnel give a moduli and inverse
Galois construction, including an algorithm that produces cubic surfaces
with prescribed line action
\citep[Theorem in the introduction, Algorithm~5.1, and
Algorithm~A.10]{elsenhansjahnel2015moduli}.  Here that geometric program is
an input boundary.  The paper studies two particular primitive subfields and
their global and local arithmetic inside one already fixed realization.

The source audit authorizes only the following bounded conjunction: for this one
cubic-surface line extension, exact integral quadratic orbit sums
primitively realize the table-restricted minimum-index $W(E_6)$ Gassmann
pair, and the resulting fields have an exact eight-prime global arithmetic
package and nonisomorphic finite \etale{} $\Qthree$-algebras in both
surviving decomposition-group branches.  This is a source boundary, not an
absolute-priority or ``first'' claim.

\begin{lemma}[Embedded subgroup carrier]
\label{lem:embedded-subgroups}
The arrays above generate embedded subgroups of the released labelled action
with the invariants in \cref{tab:subgroup-invariants}.  The pair supports in
\eqref{eq:intro-supports} have setwise stabilizers $H_+$ and $H_-$,
respectively.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The image arrays determine permutations without reference to a package
ordering.  Closure in the displayed $27$-point action gives order $162$ in
each case, hence index $51840/162=320$.  Exact enumeration gives the
remaining group invariants in \cref{tab:subgroup-invariants}.  Acting on
unordered pairs gives component sizes $27,27$ for $H_+$ and $81$ for $H_-$;
the two $H_+$ components are disjoint.  Enumerating the setwise stabilizer of
the resulting $54$- and $81$-element supports returns exactly the displayed
groups.  The full pair arrays and their certificate hashes are recorded in
\cref{app:split-graph}; the finite enumeration is independently reconstructed
as described in \cref{sec:reproducibility}.
\end{proof}

The fixed version used for the finite table is GAP~4.11.1 with TomLib~1.2.9,
whose table is named \texttt{U4(2).2}.  GAP and TomLib supply computational
conventions and versioned data, not the durable field definition
\citep{gap2021,tomlib2019}.
