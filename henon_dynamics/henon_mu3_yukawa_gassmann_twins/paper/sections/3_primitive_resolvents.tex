\section{Primitive integral quadratic resolvents}
\label{sec:primitive}

\subsection{Scaled roots and integrality}

The leading coefficient is essential.  Write
\begin{equation}
 g(x)=Lx^{27}+b_{26}x^{26}+\cdots+b_1x+b_0,
 \qquad b_i\in\Z.
 \label{eq:eliminant-general}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Integral scaling]
\label{lem:integral-scaling}
Every $\alpha_i=Ld_i$ is an algebraic integer.  Consequently
$\eta_\pm$ are algebraic integers and $R_\pm(T)\in\Z[T]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substitute $x=y/L$ in \eqref{eq:eliminant-general} and multiply by
$L^{26}$.  The result is the monic integral equation
\[
 y^{27}+b_{26}y^{26}+Lb_{25}y^{25}+\cdots
   +L^{25}b_1y+L^{26}b_0=0.
\]
It is satisfied by $y=Ld_i$, proving that $\alpha_i$ is integral.  Products
$\alpha_i\alpha_j$ and the finite sums \eqref{eq:intro-eta} are therefore
integral.

Because $H_\pm$ fix $\eta_\pm$, the products over $G/H_\pm$ are
well-defined.  Their coefficients are symmetric functions of a full
$G$-orbit, hence lie in the fixed field $K^G=\Q$.  They are also algebraic
integers.  A rational algebraic integer lies in $\Z$, so every coefficient
of $R_\pm$ is integral.
\end{proof}

\begin{convention}[Scaled and unscaled sums]
\label{conv:eta-scaling}
Throughout the paper, $\eta_\pm$ means exactly the scaled sum in
\eqref{eq:intro-eta}.  The optional unscaled quantity is
\[
 \widetilde\eta_\pm
   =\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\cS_\pm}d_id_j,
 \qquad
 \eta_\pm=L^2\widetilde\eta_\pm.
\]
All integral-polynomial and modular-coefficient statements refer to
$\eta_\pm$, never to $\widetilde\eta_\pm$.
\end{convention}

The finite product defining $R_\pm$ is an exact characteristic-zero object;
an expanded coefficient vector is neither needed nor supplied.  In
particular, no characteristic-zero coefficient hash is asserted.  This
distinction matters because the hashes below bind modular products only.

\subsection{Support stabilizers and formal orbits}

The setwise support computation in \cref{lem:embedded-subgroups} gives
\begin{equation}
 \Stab_G(\cS_+)=H_+,
 \qquad
 \Stab_G(\cS_-)=H_-.
 \label{eq:support-stabilizers}
\end{equation}
Thus each support has $320$ translates.  Equality
\eqref{eq:support-stabilizers} is a statement about formal supports.  It
does not by itself exclude an accidental equality between two evaluated
quadratic sums.  The next argument supplies exactly that missing bridge.

\subsection{The split-prime noncollision argument}

The prime used for the specialization is
\begin{equation}
 p=692717.
 \label{eq:split-prime}
\end{equation}
Exact arithmetic proves that $p$ is prime, is outside the ramified support,
and does not divide the eliminant or lexicographic reconstruction
denominators.  Modulo $p$, the normalized eliminant is squarefree and splits
as $1^{27}$.  Hence all labelled roots and all scaled values can be reduced
in one common residue field.

\begin{lemma}[Split-prime noncollision]
\label{lem:split-noncollision}
For each sign, the $320$ translated values $g\eta_\pm$, indexed by
$gH_\pm\in G/H_\pm$, are pairwise distinct in characteristic zero.
Consequently
\[
 \Stab_G(\eta_\pm)=H_\pm,
 \qquad
 \Q(\eta_\pm)=K^{H_\pm}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Reduce the labelled roots, their scaled coordinates, and the translated
support sums modulo a fixed prime of the integral model above $p$.  Complete
enumeration gives $320$ distinct residues for the $H_+$ family and $320$
distinct residues for the $H_-$ family.  If two characteristic-zero
conjugates were equal, their difference would be zero as an algebraic
integer and would reduce to zero at every common integral reduction.  Their
residues would then coincide, a contradiction.

It follows that the $G$-orbit of $\eta_\pm$ has size $320$.  Since
$H_\pm$ fixes $\eta_\pm$ and $[G:H_\pm]=320$, orbit--stabilizer forces
$\Stab_G(\eta_\pm)=H_\pm$.  Galois correspondence then gives
\[
 [\Q(\eta_\pm):\Q]=320,
 \qquad
 \Q(\eta_\pm)=K^{H_\pm}.
\]
\end{proof}

As an independently checkable summary of the specialization, the monic
degree-$320$ products modulo $p$ have $321$ coefficients and SHA-256 values
\begin{align}
 H_+\colon\quad &
 \hexsha{21b304679d3b77a7b1fae4182e203d8f2652588efffa4a160cccd98ac3e81257},
 \label{eq:modhash-plus}\\
 H_-\colon\quad &
 \hexsha{76fa8081c92e58839f60659fa7c9979d9b002fae5408cc30777341d21665acb2}.
 \label{eq:modhash-minus}
\end{align}
These are hashes of the complete modular coefficient arrays.  They are not
hashes of expanded characteristic-zero coefficients.

\begin{corollary}[Exact product-form minimal polynomials]
\label{cor:product-minimal}
The orbit products $R_\pm(T)$ are the monic integral irreducible separable
minimal polynomials of $\eta_\pm$, each of degree $320$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:integral-scaling}, the orbit products lie in $\Z[T]$.  By
\cref{lem:split-noncollision}, each product contains every distinct
$\Q$-conjugate of $\eta_\pm$ exactly once.  It is therefore the minimal
polynomial.  Characteristic zero gives separability.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Graph authority and labelling independence}

The labelled action is tied back to the cubic surface rather than accepted
as a free-standing abstract permutation representation.  At the split prime,
the exact lexicographic formulas reconstruct all $27$ lines.  Substitution
into each of four chart line equations gives zero for every line.  Pairwise
incidence then yields a graph with $27$ vertices, $135$ edges, and degree
$10$ at every vertex.  We denote this line-intersection graph by
$\Gamma_{\mathrm{meet}}$.  It is the complement of the graph conventionally
called the Schlaefli graph, whose edges join skew lines and which is therefore
$16$-regular; complementation does not change the automorphism group.

\begin{lemma}[Full graph-labelling bridge]
\label{lem:graph-bridge}
Inside the fixed labelled action,
\[
 \Aut(\Gamma_{\mathrm{meet}})=G
\]
as equality of the two complete $51{,}840$-element permutation sets.  Any
other graph identification transports $H_\pm$ and $\cS_\pm$ by one element
of $G$ and merely permutes the $320$ factors of $R_\pm$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The six fixed simple-reflection generators preserve the reconstructed graph,
so the released $G$ is contained in the graph automorphism group.  Exact
enumeration gives $|G|=51840$.  Independently, individualization followed by
equitable color refinement with base vertices $1,2,3,4,5$ gives cell-choice
sizes $27,16,10,6,2$ and a discrete final partition.  Their product is
$51840$, an upper bound for the graph automorphism group.  Thus containment
and the matching upper bound give equality of the complete permutation sets.

Two identifications of the reconstructed graph with the standard labelled
graph differ by an automorphism, hence by some $w\in G$.  They send
$(H_\pm,\cS_\pm)$ to $(wH_\pm w^{-1},w\cS_\pm)$.  Left multiplication by
$w$ permutes the full coset orbit, leaving its product and noncollision
property unchanged.  Literal equality with the particular fixed fields in
\cref{thm:integrated} uses the displayed labelling; a relabelling transports
the subgroups with it.
\end{proof}

Eight sampled graph isomorphisms would not prove
\cref{lem:graph-bridge}.  The argument uses all four chart equations, all
$27$ reconstructed lines, and equality of the full permutation sets.  This
completes the proof of \cref{thm:integrated}(i).
