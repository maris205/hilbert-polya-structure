\section[Both 3-adic branches and local separation]{Both
  \texorpdfstring{$3$}{3}-adic branches and local separation}
\label{sec:local}

The two decomposition alternatives in \eqref{eq:d3-alternatives} are now
treated separately.  We first give the common completion and lower-filtration
formulas, including the bridge between computational right-coset orbits and
the standard double-coset convention.

\subsection{Double cosets and completion factors}

Fix a prime $w$ of $K$ above $3$, with decomposition group $D$ and inertia
group $I$.  Let $F=K^H$.  For a representative $g$ of a double coset in
$D\backslash G/H$, put
\begin{equation}
 J=D\cap gHg^{-1}.
 \label{eq:local-J}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Completion factors]
\label{lem:double-coset-completions}
The factors of the finite \etale{} algebra $F\otimes_\Q\Qthree$ are indexed
by $D\backslash G/H$.  The factor attached to $DgH$ has
\begin{equation}
 n=[D:J],
 \qquad
 e=[I:I\cap J],
 \qquad
 f=[D:IJ],
 \qquad n=ef.
 \label{eq:nef-formulas}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Embeddings of $F$ into $K$ correspond to cosets $G/H$.  The decomposition
group $D$ acts on these embeddings, and its orbits are precisely the double
cosets $D\backslash G/H$.  One $D$-orbit therefore gives one prime of $F$
above $3$, equivalently one completion factor.  The stabilizer in $D$ of
$gH$ is $D\cap gHg^{-1}=J$, so the degree is $n=[D:J]$.

The inertia orbit inside that transitive factor has size
$[I:I\cap J]=e$.  Passing to the residue quotient gives
$f=[D:IJ]$.  Since $I\triangleleft D$, the product $IJ$ is a subgroup and
\[
 [D:J]=[D:IJ][IJ:J]
       =[D:IJ][I:I\cap J]=fe.
\]
Some computations enumerate right cosets and right orbits.  Inversion sends
$Hg^{-1}D$ to $DgH$ and preserves stabilizer indices, so it yields the same
completion factors rather than a second convention-dependent table.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Lower filtration and the local different entry}

Let
\[
 D=G_{-1}\supseteq G_0=I\supseteq G_1\supseteq G_2\supseteq\cdots
\]
be the lower ramification filtration.  The Artin conductor of the transitive
permutation representation on $D/J$ gives the discriminant contribution of
the corresponding factor.  A direct orbit calculation yields
\begin{equation}
 f d
 =\sum_{u\geq0}\frac{|G_u|}{|G_0|}
 \left([D:J]-[D:J]\frac{|G_u\cap J|}{|G_u|}\right).
 \label{eq:fd-artin}
\end{equation}
Using \eqref{eq:nef-formulas}, this is equivalent to the general formula
\begin{equation}
 d=\sum_{u\geq0}
 \frac{|G_u|-|G_u\cap J|}{|I\cap J|}.
 \label{eq:d-general}
\end{equation}
For every representative in the two certified tables, the stored
intersection quadruples in \cref{app:local-ledger} give
\begin{equation}
 |J|=\frac{|D|}{n},
 \qquad
 |I\cap J|=\frac{|I|}{e}.
 \label{eq:J-intersection-orders}
\end{equation}
These two quantities are equal row by row.  In the \Tom{140} table this is
immediate from $D=I$ and $n=e$.  In the \Tom{206} table, $|D|=36$,
$|I|=18$, and every displayed row has $n=2e$, so again
$36/n=18/e$.  Since $I\cap J\leq J$ and the two finite groups have equal
order, $J=I\cap J$ and therefore $J\leq I$.  Thus the displayed entry used
below is
\begin{equation}
 d(g,H)=\sum_{u\geq0}
 \frac{|G_u|-|G_u\cap J|}{|J|}.
 \label{eq:d-local}
\end{equation}
Formula \eqref{eq:d-local} explains, rather than merely labels, the fourth
coordinate of each $(n,e,f,d)$ row.  Summing $fd$ over all factors must
reproduce the global exponent $624$.

For this check, one must first reconcile the tables with the common Dedekind
zeta function.  The relevant datum is the multiset of residue degrees.  In
the \Tom{140} branch, both fields have
$36$ factors of residue degree $1$.  In the \Tom{206} branch, both have
$18$ factors of residue degree $2$.  Thus the different local-degree
multisets below do not contradict \cref{prop:zeta-equality}.  This is only a
Dedekind-zeta residue-degree reconciliation; no bad Artin Euler factor is
computed.

\subsection{One-way separation and branch-independent conclusion}

\begin{proposition}[Complete branchwise local tables]
\label{prop:complete-local-tables}
The tables in \cref{tab:c59-local-tom140,tab:c59-local-tom206} contain every
factor for the respective branch.  In each row $n=ef$; in each field and
branch the degrees sum to $320$ and the values $fd$ sum to $624$.
The residue-degree counts agree between $F_+$ and $F_-$ branchwise.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The $D$-orbits on $G/H_\pm$ exhaust the relevant coset carriers.  Exact
stabilizer intersections give \eqref{eq:nef-formulas}, and the complete lower
filtration gives \eqref{eq:d-local}.  Collecting identical rows produces
exactly the displayed multiplicities.  The explicit sums above verify the
three independent totals.  Finally, all rows in the first branch have
$f=1$ and there are $36$ of them, while all rows in the second have $f=2$
and there are $18$ of them.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Local and adelic inequivalence]
\label{cor:local-separation}
For either choice in \eqref{eq:d3-alternatives},
\[
 F_+\otimes_\Q\Qthree\not\cong F_-\otimes_\Q\Qthree.
\]
Consequently the fields are not locally equivalent and their adele rings are
not isomorphic.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
An isomorphism between finite products of finite extensions of $\Qthree$
permutes their field factors and preserves the factor-degree multiset.  In
the \Tom{140} branch, eight degree-one factors occur only for $F_+$.  In the
\Tom{206} branch, four degree-two factors occur only for $F_+$.  Each
difference is therefore sufficient for product-algebra nonisomorphism.  A
single nonisomorphic local product rules out local equivalence.  Moreover, an
isomorphism of adele rings induces a bijection of their local-field factors;
residue characteristic is preserved, so it would in particular identify the
two products above $3$.  Their nonisomorphism therefore also rules out an
isomorphism of adele rings.
\end{proof}

The argument uses the tuples $(n,e,f,d)$ only in the sufficient direction.
Such a tuple does not classify an individual high-degree local field, and no
classification of the completion factors is claimed.  Both branches have
been proved and neither has been selected.  This completes
\cref{thm:integrated}(v).
