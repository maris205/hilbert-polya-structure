## 1. Methodological principle

C59 is a proof-guided exact-computation project. Every theorem sentence must
decompose into:

1. a released predecessor object;
2. a universal theorem with a precise source boundary;
3. an exact instance computation;
4. a written bridge from the computation to the field theorem;
5. an independent replay; and
6. a negative mutation capable of falsifying the claimed bridge.

No layer may silently stand in for another.

## 4. Independence requirements

- Producer and checker theorem call graphs are disjoint.
- Each lane reconstructs subgroup/support families rather than sharing a
  generated family list.
- Each lane reconstructs modular lines and incidence from fixed upstream
  inputs.
- Common utilities are limited to canonical I/O, fingerprints, and backend
  preflight.
- Evidence carriers are immutable inputs during official certificate refresh.
- Source bytes are rebound before and after child processes.
- Staging and promotion are rollback-atomic and reject ABA/path substitution.

## 5. Notation and explicitness discipline

`eta` always means the scaled integral sum in (alpha_i=Ld_i). An optional
unscaled sum is `tilde_eta`, with `eta=L^2*tilde_eta`. The modular hashes and
$R(T)\in\mathbf Z[T]$ bind only scaled `eta`.

An exact finite product can be a complete characteristic-zero definition even
without expanded coefficients. The project must not claim an expanded vector,
coefficient height bound, integral basis, maximal order, monogenicity, or
polynomial-discriminant equality.

## 6. Kill-first discipline

The project stops for predecessor drift, non-durable subgroup definitions,
support-stabilizer failure, fewer than 320 values, incomplete character
comparison, another minimum collision, graph-automorphism mismatch, failed
global arithmetic, incomplete local tables, one-branch failure, or any source
or scope overclaim. No partial theorem replaces a failed integrated target.
