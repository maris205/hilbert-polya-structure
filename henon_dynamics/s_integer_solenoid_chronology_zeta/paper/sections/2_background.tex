\section{Background and positioning}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Area-preserving H\'enon dynamics.}
H\'enon's quadratic area-preserving mappings were introduced as tractable
models for two-degree-of-freedom conservative dynamics
\citep{Henon1969}.  Their shear--rotation structure, invariant islands, and
chaotic regions make them natural laboratories for orbit geometry.  The
source manuscript \citep{Wang2026AreaPreservingHenon} uses a common recurrence
form of this philosophy and then takes a formal continuous limit before
adding a quartic confinement and numerical spectral solvers.  Our use of that
work is narrower: it motivates invertibility and a genuine dynamical clock.
We do not transport its parameter, continuous limit, regularization, or
spectral matching into the present system.  This separation prevents a
numerical H\'enon hypothesis from becoming an unexamined premise of the exact
theorems below.

\paragraph{Periodic points on solenoids.}
Dualizing modules of \(S\)-integers produces compact abelian dynamical
systems whose periodic counts factor into archimedean and nonarchimedean
local terms.  Chothi, Everest, and Ward developed this mechanism for
\(S\)-integer systems and showed that even finite \(S\) can produce
irrational dynamical zeta functions \citep{ChothiEverestWard1997}.  Miles
gave higher-dimensional periodic-point formulas for solenoids and related
compact groups \citep{Miles2008}.  Lind and Ward's entropy theory explains
the corresponding decomposition into Euclidean and \(p\)-adic places
\citep{LindWard1988}.  The formula used here is a concrete matrix instance of
that theory; because \(\Z[1/2]\) is a localized principal ideal domain, we
also prove it directly by a cokernel and Smith-normal-form argument.
For rank-two solenoids specifically, \citet{HaLee2023} give explicit
subgroup-index and periodic-point formulas for rational matrix
endomorphisms.  These results place our fixed-index calculation firmly in
prior theory; the new claim below concerns chronological switching and its
local--global analytic split.

\paragraph{Dynamical zeta and natural boundaries.}
For a map with finite fixed sets, the Artin--Mazur series
\citep{ArtinMazur1965} packages the counts \(N_n=\#\Fix(F^n)\) as
\[
 Z_F(z)=\exp\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{N_n}{n}z^n\right).
 \tag{2.1}\label{2.1}
\]
Algebraic dynamical systems exhibit a sharp contrast between rational zeta
functions and zeta functions whose convergence circles are natural
boundaries.  Bell, Miles, and Ward prove a P\'olya--Carlson-type dichotomy
under finite-place and archimedean hyperbolicity hypotheses
\citep{BellMilesWard2014}.  We use their theorem only for a single fibre
return automorphism.  We do not apply it directly to the full switching skew
product, and we do not infer the analytic type of an infinite product from
one factor.
The finite-adelically-distorted framework of
\citet{ByszewskiCornelissenHouben2022} supplies a broader modern setting for
fixed-point sequences and zeta dichotomies, including \(S\)-integer systems.
It does not by itself determine the zeta of a noncommuting full-shift cocycle.

\paragraph{Ordered semigroup actions.}
For Ruelle-expanding generators, free-semigroup dynamics, full-shift skew
products, periodic-point growth, and associated zeta functions are established
structures
\citep{CarvalhoRodriguesVarandas2017}.  Consequently neither the use of a
two-symbol driver nor the observation that noncommuting products depend on
order is a novelty claim.  Our result concerns a particular interaction:
the mod-2 fixed-point correction selects a cyclic finite-type language; two
equal-Parikh primitive returns then acquire different zeta analytic types;
and the global zeta nevertheless crosses its first circle.  A directed source
audit did not locate this combination in one explicit solenoid cocycle, but
this is a scoped assessment rather than an exhaustive priority claim.

\paragraph{Hilbert--P\'olya scope.}
The broad physical motivation for a self-adjoint spectral realization of the
Riemann zeros is reviewed, for example, by Schumayer and Hutchinson
\citep{SchumayerHutchinson2011}.  A conservative classical map, a unitary
Koopman operator, or random-matrix-like statistics are not by themselves a
Riemann divisor.  Our evaluation therefore asks separately whether primitive
orbits have an arithmetic clock, whether a frozen determinant has the right
zeros, whether the analytic completion has the required global structure,
and whether a compatible discrete self-adjoint operator exists.  The present
system fails those later gates despite its exact zeta theorems.
