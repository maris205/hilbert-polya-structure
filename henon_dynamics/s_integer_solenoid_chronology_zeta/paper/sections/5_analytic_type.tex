\section{Chronology changes analytic type}
\label{sec:analytic-type}

The natural orbit decomposition is by primitive base sectors, not by a
dihedral quotient and not by letter incidence.  If \([w]\) is a primitive
base necklace of length \(\ell\), its invariant sector contains \(\ell\)
base phases.  For \(n=\ell r\), that sector contributes
\(\ell\#\Fix(\alpha_{M_w}^r)\) to \(\#\Fix(F^n)\), and it contributes zero
when \(\ell\nmid n\).  Consequently
\[
 \Zsol(z)=
 \prod_{[w]\ \mathrm{primitive\ base}}
 \zeta_{\alpha_{M_w}}(z^{|w|})
 \tag{5.1}\label{5.1}
\]
as an exact formal identity.  Analytically, we assert the product only in its
initial convergence disk.  Each fibre zeta in \eqref{5.1} contains its own
primitive fibre orbits; primitive base necklaces are therefore a complete
base-sector decomposition, not an enumeration of all primitive \(F\)-orbits.

Let \(t_w=\tr M_w\), \(d_w=\det M_w=2^{3\ell}\), and write the characteristic
polynomial as
\[
 f_w(x)=x^2-t_wx+d_w.
 \tag{5.2}\label{5.2}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Trace-parity analytic dichotomy]
\label{thm:dichotomy}
Let \(w\) be a primitive base word of length \(\ell\).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item If \(t_w\) is even, then every \(\det(I-M_w^r)\) is odd and
\[
 \zeta_{\alpha_{M_w}}(u)
 =\frac{1-t_wu+d_wu^2}{(1-u)(1-d_wu)}.
 \tag{5.3}\label{5.3}
\]
\item If \(t_w\) is odd, then \(\zeta_{\alpha_{M_w}}(u)\) has the circle
\(|u|=d_w^{-1}\) as a natural boundary.  The corresponding factor in the
base clock has natural boundary \(|z|=1/8\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If \(t_w\) is even, Cayley--Hamilton gives \(M_w^2\equiv0\pmod2\), so
\(\det(I-M_w^r)\) is odd for every \(r\).  The solenoid fixed count equals
the torus count \(1-\tr(M_w^r)+d_w^r\); exponentiating its three trace terms
gives \eqref{5.3}.

Suppose \(t_w\) is odd.  Modulo two, \(f_w(x)=x(x+1)\), with two simple
roots.  Hensel lifting gives one \(2\)-adic unit eigenvalue and one eigenvalue
of valuation \(3\ell\), hence one isometric finite-place direction.  More
explicitly, the dual module is the full \(\Z[1/2]\)-lattice
\(\Z[1/2]^2\).  After passage to each characteristic-field factor, its
finite place set in the Miles representation is precisely the set of places
above the inverted rational prime two: localization of a full lattice adds
no finite place away from two and includes every place above two.  The simple
factorization \(f_w=x(x+1)\pmod2\) and Hensel's lemma split the scalar
extension over \(\Q_2\) into a unit line and a valuation-\(3\ell\) line.
Equivalently, in the irreducible quadratic case, two splits in the
characteristic field and the unit root defines a place \(v\mid2\) in that
finite place set with \(|\xi|_v=1\).  In fact, the reducible case cannot occur
in this odd-trace branch: rational roots of the monic integral polynomial are
integers; positivity and expansion make both roots greater than one; and their
product \(2^{3\ell}\) then forces both roots, hence their sum \(t_w\), to be
even.  Thus the finite-place condition of the natural-boundary branch is
genuinely met.  We now audit the remaining
hypotheses of Theorem~15 of \citet{BellMilesWard2014}.  The group
\(\Xtwo\) is connected and two-dimensional; \(d_w\) is a unit of
\(\Z[1/2]\), so the return is an automorphism; Proposition~\ref{prop:expansion}
excludes roots of unity and makes it ergodic; and no archimedean eigenvalue
lies on the unit circle.  The theorem therefore gives a natural boundary at
\(e^{-h(\alpha_{M_w})}=d_w^{-1}\).  Here the entropy is \(\log d_w\): both
archimedean eigenvalues are expanding, while neither dyadic eigenvalue is
\(2\)-adically expanding.  Substitution \(u=z^\ell\) yields
\(|z|=d_w^{-1/\ell}=1/8\).
\end{proof}

The dichotomy becomes chronological at the first possible equal-incidence
witness used here.

\begin{theorem}[Equal-Parikh analytic-type split]
\label{thm:witness}
The primitive period-five words
\[
 w_0=\texttt{aabbb},\qquad w_1=\texttt{ababb}
\]
have the same Parikh vector \((2,3)\) and are neither cyclic nor dihedral
copies.  Their return data are those in Table~\ref{tab:witness}.  In
particular, \(w_0\) has rational fibre zeta
\[
 \zeta_{\alpha_{M_{w_0}}}(z^5)
 =\frac{1-2734z^5+32768z^{10}}
 {(1-z^5)(1-32768z^5)},
 \tag{5.4}\label{5.4}
\]
whereas the \(w_1\) sector has natural boundary \(|z|=1/8\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Direct chronological multiplication gives
\(M_{w_0}=B^3A^2\) and \(M_{w_1}=B^2ABA\), with the entries shown in
Table~\ref{tab:witness}.  Both words are nonconstant of prime length and are
therefore primitive.  Their rotation and reversal classes are disjoint by
direct inspection.  Theorem~\ref{thm:index} gives the fixed counts, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:dichotomy} gives the two analytic types.
\end{proof}

For the natural-boundary witness, the local fluctuation is also explicit.
If \(\xi\) is its \(2\)-adic unit eigenvalue, then
\(\vTwo(\xi-1)=1\) and \(\vTwo(\xi+1)=3\).  The other eigenvalue makes
\(1-\eta^r\) a unit, and the dyadic lifting-the-exponent identity gives
\[
 \vTwo\det(I-M_{w_1}^r)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&r\text{ odd},\\
 3+\vTwo(r),&r\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{5.5}\label{5.5}
\]
The term ``isometric direction'' here means absolute value one at the dyadic
place; it does not mean that an eigenvalue equals one.
