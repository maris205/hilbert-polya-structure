\section{Proof and convention details}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Dual composition, rotations, and repetitions}

If \(T\) is an endomorphism of a discrete abelian group, its dual acts on a
character \(x\) by \(\widehat T(x)=x\circ T\).  Hence
\(\widehat T\circ\widehat S=\widehat{S\circ T}\).  With
\(\alpha_M=\widehat{M^{\mathsf T}}\), this becomes
\(\alpha_M\circ\alpha_N=\alpha_{MN}\), which proves the order in
\eqref{3.3}.

For \(w=w_0\cdots w_{n-1}\) and its left rotation
\(\rho w=w_1\cdots w_{n-1}w_0\),
\[
 M_{\rho w}=M_{w_0}M_wM_{w_0}^{-1}
\]
in \(\GL_2(R)\).  Thus trace, determinant, and fixed-group order are cyclic
invariants.  Concatenating \(r\) copies gives \(M_{w^r}=M_w^r\).  Because
both generators are symmetric, reversing the multiplication convention
transposes every word product and leaves all data used in this paper
unchanged.

\subsection{Localized Smith normal form}

Let \(C=I-M_w^{\mathsf T}\in M_2(\Z)\).  Over \(\Z\), choose Smith factors
\(s_1,s_2\ne0\) with \(|s_1s_2|=|\det C|=D_w\).  Tensoring the cokernel with
\(R=\Z[1/2]\) makes every power of two invertible.  The finite localized
cokernel therefore has order
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^2\frac{|s_i|}{2^{\vTwo(s_i)}}
 =\frac{D_w}{2^{\vTwo(D_w)}}.
\]
Pontryagin duality preserves finite cardinality and converts this cokernel
into \(\ker(\alpha_{M_w}-I)\), proving Theorem~\ref{thm:index}.

\subsection{The dyadic unit direction for the period-five witness}

For \(M=M_{\texttt{ababb}}\),
\[
 f(x)=x^2-2727x+32768.
\]
The reduction \(f(x)\equiv x(x+1)\pmod2\) has simple roots.  If \(\xi\) is
the unit root, then
\[
 f(1)=30042,\quad \vTwo(f(1))=1,\qquad
 f(-1)=35496,\quad \vTwo(f(-1))=3,
\]
while \(f'(1)\) and \(f'(-1)\) are odd.  The strong Hensel estimate gives
\(\vTwo(\xi-1)=1\) and \(\vTwo(\xi+1)=3\).  The other eigenvalue has
valuation \(15\), so \(1-\eta^r\) is a unit.  For odd \(r\),
\(\vTwo(\xi^r-1)=\vTwo(\xi-1)\).  For even \(r\), dyadic
lifting-the-exponent gives
\[
 \vTwo(\xi^r-1)
 =\vTwo(\xi-1)+\vTwo(\xi+1)+\vTwo(r)-1,
\]
which proves \eqref{5.5}.

\subsection{Why the secondary boundary is open}

The lower bound \eqref{6.3} gives
\[
 \frac{\Delta_n}{n}R^n\ge \frac{c}{n}
\]
for all sufficiently large \(n\) and some \(c>0\).  Hence
\(G(r)\to+\infty\) along the positive real axis.  It follows that the power
series for \(G\) cannot be holomorphic across \(R\).  It does not follow that
\(e^{-G}\) is singular there: for example, exponentiating a logarithmic
singularity can yield a holomorphic zero.  Proving a boundary for \(\Zsol\)
would require more detailed control of the congruence layers than their
exponential growth rate.
