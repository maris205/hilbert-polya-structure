\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The area-preserving H\'enon viewpoint poses a useful structural question for
dynamical approaches to the Riemann zeros: after dissipation is removed, can
one define an intrinsic orbit zeta whose analytic structure carries more than
generic chaotic statistics?  A recent project manuscript pursued this
question numerically by passing from a dissipative logistic model to
\[
 H_a(x,y)=(1-a x^2-y,x),\qquad \det D H_a=1,
\]
then constructing regularized quantum and Fokker--Planck solvers
\citep{Wang2026AreaPreservingHenon}.  The manuscript carefully labels the
resulting zero comparisons as numerical rather than as an identification of a
Hilbert--P\'olya operator.  This leaves a basic mathematical gap: the
conservation law alone does not provide exact periodic weights, a determinant
identity, or a spectral divisor.

We address that gap by changing dynamical category.  The present system is
not derived from, conjugate to, or a quantization of the smooth H\'enon map.
It replaces nonlinear planar motion by a chronological cocycle of
compact-group automorphisms.  The fibre is the dyadic two-solenoid
\(\Xtwo=\widehat{\Z[1/2]^2}\), and a full two-shift chooses at each time
between the noncommuting matrices
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}3&2\\2&4\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{1.1}\label{1.1}
\]
Their real determinants are \(8\), so these matrices are not planar
area-preserving maps.  They are invertible over \(\Z[1/2]\), however, and
their dual maps are automorphisms preserving Haar measure on \(\Xtwo\).
This distinction is essential.

For a word \(w=w_0\cdots w_{n-1}\), later symbols multiply on the left:
\[
 M_w=M_{w_{n-1}}\cdots M_{w_0}.
 \tag{1.2}\label{1.2}
\]
We never replace this product by a power of an averaged transition matrix.
Only after every ordered word has been evaluated do we form the periodic
sum.  This convention preserves both primitive base necklaces and the correct
repetition law \(M_{w^r}=M_w^r\).

The main contribution is a local--global analytic split.  Chronology is
strong enough to change the analytic type of two primitive return zetas with
the same abelianized symbol data.  Yet after all base words are summed, the
full zeta crosses its leading convergence circle with no zeros.  More
precisely, we prove the following.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(\roman*)]
\item Every return is expanding and has an intrinsic finite fixed group with
\[
 \#\Fix(\alpha_{M_w})
 =\oddpart\bigl(8^n-\tr M_w+1\bigr).
\]
The corresponding archimedean comparison has the exact rational zeta
\[
 \Zinf(z)=\frac{1-13z+33z^2}{(1-2z)(1-16z)}.
\]

\item A word receives a nontrivial dyadic correction if and only if it has no
cyclic occurrence of \texttt{aa}.  The active words are therefore counted by
Lucas numbers.  Every fixed congruence depth is represented by a finite
chronological monoid recurrence.

\item The primitive period-five words \texttt{aabbb} and \texttt{ababb} have
the same Parikh vector \((2,3)\), but their return zetas are respectively
rational and natural-boundary.  The latter boundary occurs at \(|z|=1/8\) in
the base clock.

\item If \(\Delta_n\) is the total dyadic correction at time \(n\), then
\(\lim \Delta_n^{1/n}=8\golden\).  Consequently the full solenoid zeta
continues meromorphically to \(|z|<(8\golden)^{-1}\), where its only divisor
is a simple pole at \(z=1/16\).
\end{enumerate}

This theorem package is not a Riemann-zero construction.  Its natural clocks
are the lattice scales \(16\), \(8\golden\), and \(8\); no prime-period law,
Riemann divisor, functional equation, or discrete self-adjoint operator
appears.  We therefore retain the arithmetic-dynamics result and reject the
frozen object under the project's Hilbert--P\'olya evaluation.  This negative
conclusion is part of the result rather than a caveat added after the fact.

Section~\ref{sec:background} positions the construction.  Sections
\ref{sec:system}--\ref{sec:analytic-type} establish the fixed-point formula,
the parity language, and the chronology theorem.  Section~\ref{sec:global}
proves the continuation disk, and Section~\ref{sec:route-a} records the scoped
Hilbert--P\'olya ruling.  Full algebraic details and reproducibility checks
appear in the appendices.
