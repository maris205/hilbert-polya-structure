# C182 theorem package

## 1. State space, clock, and conserved data

Fix integers `L>=1` and `0<=M<=floor(L/2)`.  A state is a binary word on a ring of length `L` with `M` balls.  The capacity-`l` periodic carrier/combinatorial-`R` update is `T_l`, and one application is one unit of the physical discrete clock.  KTT prove that the maps `T_l` commute and preserve the soliton content

\[
 m=(m_j)_{j\ge1},\qquad \sum_{j\ge1}j m_j=M.
\]

Write `H={j:m_j>0}` and define the vacancy numbers

\[
 p_j=L-2\sum_{k\in H}\min(j,k)m_k\qquad(j\in H).
\]

The assumption `L>=2M` implies `p_j>=0`.  If `N=max H`, then `p_N=L-2M`; thus half filling is exactly the boundary `p_N=0`.

## 2. Precisely imported KTT/Takagi theorem

For each `j in H`, choose a positive internal-symmetry divisor

\[
 \alpha_j\mid\gcd(m_j,p_j),\qquad \gcd(m,0)=m.
\]

Order `H` increasingly.  Define the integral matrix with rows and columns indexed by `H`,

\[
 F^{(\alpha)}_{jk}
 =\frac{\delta_{jk}p_k+2\min(j,k)m_k}{\alpha_k},
\qquad
 h_l=(\min(j,l))_{j\in H}.
\]

Takagi's component theorem, specialized to the binary periodic box--ball system, states that every connected component of the symmetry-`alpha` part of the level set is equivariantly bijective to

\[
 \mathbb Z^{H}/F^{(\alpha)}\mathbb Z^{H},
 \qquad T_l:x\longmapsto x+h_l.
\]

Its cardinality is `det F^(alpha)>0`.  The number of such components is

\[
 c(\alpha)=\prod_{j\in H}
 \frac{|\Lambda^{(\alpha_j)}(m_j,p_j)|}{m_j/\alpha_j},
\]

where

\[
 |\Lambda^{(\alpha)}(m,p)|
 =\sum_{\substack{\beta\mid\gcd(m,p)\\\alpha\mid\beta}}
 \mu(\beta/\alpha)
 \binom{(p+m)/\beta-1}{m/\beta-1}.
\]

The factor is an integer: the cyclic shift on a sequence of exact internal period `m/alpha` has free orbits of that size.  The displayed Möbius formula is the inversion of the decomposition of all weakly increasing periodic rigging sequences by their exact period.

For `p=0`, every binomial in the sum equals one.  Möbius cancellation leaves a nonzero value only at `alpha=m`, where it equals one.  Therefore the half-filled boundary is included rather than divided by zero or silently discarded.

The unsymmetrized level size is

\[
 \Omega_L(m)=\det F^{(1)}
 \prod_{j\in H}\frac1{m_j}
 \binom{p_j+m_j-1}{m_j-1},
\]

and the sector identity is

\[
 \Omega_L(m)=\sum_\alpha c(\alpha)\det F^{(\alpha)}.
\]

Summing over every partition `m` of `M` yields `binom(L,M)`, because the KTT action variable partitions the binary state space.

## 3. Smith-normal-form order theorem

Fix a component and abbreviate `F=F^(alpha)`, `h=h_l`, `D=det F`.  Let `B=[F|h]`, the matrix obtained by adjoining `h` as one column.  If the Smith factors of `B` are `e_1,...,e_s`, then the sublattice `F Z^s+Z h` has index `e_1...e_s` in `Z^s`.  Hence

\[
 q_{\alpha,l}:=\operatorname{ord}([h])
 =\frac{D}{e_1\cdots e_s}.
\]

Proof: the quotient by the cyclic subgroup generated by `[h]` is

\[
 (\mathbb Z^s/F\mathbb Z^s)/\langle[h]\rangle
 \cong \mathbb Z^s/(F\mathbb Z^s+\mathbb Z h).
\]

Its size is the product of the nonzero Smith factors of `B`.  Since the original group has size `D`, the cyclic subgroup has the displayed quotient size.  Equivalently, if `UFV=diag(d_i)` is a Smith decomposition, then

\[
 q_{\alpha,l}=\operatorname{lcm}_i
 \frac{d_i}{\gcd(d_i,(Uh)_i)}.
\]

The empty `H` convention gives `D=q=1`.  Also `h_l=(j)_(j in H)` once `l>=max H`, proving saturation of all larger carriers on the level set.

## 4. Fixed points and all-state aggregation

Translation by an element of order `q` on a finite group has no fixed point at iterate `n` unless `q|n`; if `q|n`, it is the identity.  Thus one component contributes

\[
 \#\operatorname{Fix}(T_l^n)=
 \begin{cases}
 D,&q_{\alpha,l}\mid n,\\
 0,&q_{\alpha,l}\nmid n.
 \end{cases}
\]

Multiplying by `c(alpha)` and summing gives the level law

\[
 F_{m,l}(n)=\sum_\alpha
 c(\alpha)\det F^{(\alpha)}
 \mathbf 1_{q_{\alpha,l}\mid n}.
\]

The complete fixed-mass and positive-weight laws are respectively

\[
 F_{L,M,l}(n)=\sum_{m:\sum jm_j=M}F_{m,l}(n),
 \qquad
 F_{L,l}^{+}(n)=\sum_{M=0}^{\lfloor L/2\rfloor}F_{L,M,l}(n).
\]

These are exact finite sums for every allowed parameter, not cutoff formulas.

## 5. Primitive cycles, zeta, and Koopman determinant

Let `P_n` count points of exact period `n` and `C_n=P_n/n` primitive cycles.  Möbius inversion gives

\[
 P_n=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)F(d),\qquad C_n=P_n/n.
\]

More directly, each symmetry-`alpha` component consists of `D/q` cycles of length `q`; with multiplicity it contributes `c(alpha)D/q` such cycles.  After any desired aggregation, let `C_q` be the resulting number of length-`q` cycles.  Then

\[
 \zeta_{T_l}(z)
 =\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{F(n)}n z^n\right)
 =\prod_q(1-z^q)^{-C_q}.
\]

On the finite Hilbert space `ell^2(X)` with counting measure, `U_l f=f after T_l` is a unitary permutation.  A `q`-cycle contributes every `q`-th root of unity once and

\[
 \det(I-zU_l)=\prod_q(1-z^q)^{C_q}=\zeta_{T_l}(z)^{-1}.
\]

This is an ordinary finite determinant with the same discrete clock.  It is not a Hilbert--Pólya operator, and no infinite-volume limit is asserted.

## 6. Boundary and Route-A decision

The vacuum `M=0` is the one-point zero-dimensional torus; every `T_l` is the identity.  At `L=2M`, the exact-period cancellation above resolves `p_max=0`.  For `l>=max H`, carrier saturation is explicit.  These three cases prevent the common hidden exclusions at vacuum, half filling, and infinite carrier capacity.

The primitive cycles are complete but carry neither intrinsic prime labels nor stability/von-Mangoldt weights.  The clock is an unweighted iterate, not `log p`.  Every fixed-parameter zeta is a finite rational function with no target functional equation, counting law, continuation theorem, or Weil compression.  The natural finite Koopman unitary is retained.
