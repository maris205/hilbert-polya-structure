\section{The third abscissa and two trace categories}

The logarithm of the normalized Euler product is
\[
 -\sum_{p\equiv1(3)}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{c_{p,n}}n p^{-ns}.
\]
The exact first moment \(c_{p,1}=-12/(p-1)\) is summable for
\(\RePart s>0\).  By \eqref{eq:weil}, the \(n=2\) contribution is summable
for \(\RePart s>1/4\).  For \(n\ge3\), the inherited estimate
\(|c_{p,n}|\le4\cdot4^n\) gives the large-prime majorant, but a finite-prime
step is needed to sum over \(n\).  Fix a compact set with
\(\RePart s\ge\sigma_0>1/3\), and choose \(P_0\) so that
\(4p^{-\sigma_0}\le1/2\) for \(p>P_0\).  Then
\[
 \sum_{p>P_0}\sum_{n\ge3}\frac{|c_{p,n}|}{n}p^{-n\sigma_0}
 \ll\sum_{p>P_0}p^{-3\sigma_0}<\infty.
\]
For the finitely many \(p\le P_0\), the unitary-block estimate
\(|c_{p,n}|\le\tau_p(I)=(8p+4)/3\) makes the logarithmic series converge
geometrically because \(p^{-\sigma_0}<1\).  This proves local normal
convergence and the Euler assertion in \cref{thm:main}.

The operator interpretation is equally exact, but the trace category must be
specified.  Let \((\mathcal M,\tau)\) be the inherited product algebra with
its normalized faithful semifinite trace, and let \(X_s\) be the
Galois-sector block.  Since each local block is unitary,
\[
 \tau(|X_s|^q)
 =\sum_{p\equiv1(3)}\frac{8p+4}{3}p^{-q\RePart s},
 \qquad
 X_s\in L^q(\mathcal M,\tau)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q\RePart s>2.
\]
In particular, \(X_s\) is \(\tau\)-trace class exactly for
\(\RePart s>2\); only there does the unregularized
\(\tau\)-associated analytic graded determinant exist.  On the larger
half-plane \(\RePart s>1/3\), \(L^6(\mathcal M,\tau)\) is the first fixed
integer ideal covering every point.  With
\[
 \ell_n(s)=\sum_pc_{p,n}p^{-ns},\qquad1\le n\le5,
\]
the \(n=1\) and \(n=2\) estimates above, together with the uniform bound for
\(3\le n\le5\), show that every counterterm converges there.  Removing the
first five logarithmic terms produces the trace-associated graded
regularized determinant
\[
 \Det_{6,\tau,\mathrm{gr}}(I-X_s)
 =\exp\!\left(-\sum_{n\ge6}\frac{\str(X_s^n)}n\right),
\]
equivalently the quotient of the \(\tau\)-associated sixth-order regularized
determinants in the positive and negative grades
\cite[Chapter~9]{Simon2005}.  This gives the factorization in
\cref{thm:main}.  Order six is the least fixed integer order forced by
positive \(L^q(\mathcal M,\tau)\)-membership on the whole half-plane; the
claim does not classify unrelated regularization schemes.

The ordinary Hilbert trace gives a different threshold and a different local
object.  Let \(\mathcal H=\bigoplus_p\mathcal H_p\) be the underlying Hilbert
direct sum and \(d_p=(p-1)/2\).  Since
\[
 \dim\mathcal H_p
 =d_p\tau_p(I)=\frac{(p-1)(4p+2)}3,
\]
one has
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal H}(|X_s|^q)
 =\sum_{p\equiv1(3)}\frac{(p-1)(4p+2)}3p^{-q\RePart s},
 \qquad
 X_s\in S^q(\mathcal H)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q\RePart s>3.
\]
At equality the series is a positive constant multiple of the divergent
split-prime harmonic series; the two strict inequalities follow by the same
prime-series comparison.  Thus classical Hilbert trace class begins only at
\(\RePart s>3\).  Furthermore,
\[
 \operatorname{Str}_{\mathcal H_p}(W_p^n)
 =d_pc_{p,n}=C_{p,n},
\]
Writing \(G_p\) for the local normalized root and
\(N_p=G_p^{d_p}\) for its ordinary Galois norm, the canonical local
logarithm satisfies
\[
 \exp\operatorname{Str}_{\mathcal H_p}\operatorname{Log}_0(I-zW_p)
 =G_p(z)^{d_p}=N_p(z).
\]
The classical Fredholm determinant therefore encodes the ordinary Galois
norm \(N_p\), rather than the normalized root \(G_p\) used in
\(\mathcal G\).  The larger regularization order in the normalized category
is the transparent price of reaching a larger half-plane; it is not a loss
of H\'enon chronology.
