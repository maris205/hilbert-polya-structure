# Source audit

## Source lock

1. J. Douglas Jr. and H. H. Rachford Jr., “On the numerical solution of heat
   conduction problems in two and three space variables,” *Transactions of the
   AMS* 82 (1956), 421–439. DOI:
   `10.1090/S0002-9947-1956-0084194-4`.
2. H. H. Bauschke, J. Y. Bello Cruz, T. T. A. Nghia, H. M. Phan and X. Wang,
   “The rate of linear convergence of the Douglas–Rachford algorithm for
   subspaces is the cosine of the Friedrichs angle,” *Journal of Approximation
   Theory* 185 (2014), 63–79. DOI: `10.1016/j.jat.2014.06.002`.

The first source owns the classical splitting ancestry.  The second source
owns the two-subspace convergence location and sharp Friedrichs-angle rate.
C197 claims neither discovery nor priority for these results.  Its package
contribution is a convention-locked synthesis across every real relaxation,
including scalar intersection blocks, generic principal planes, endpoint and
instability boundaries, finite determinant/trace consequences, and executable
cross-validation.

## Verification record

- The AMS publisher record fixes the 1956 title, volume, pages and DOI.
- The Journal of Approximation Theory publisher record fixes the 2014 title,
  authors, volume, pages and DOI and states the cosine-rate theorem.
- The formulas used here are derived again from orthogonal projections; no
  formula is accepted solely because it appears in a source.

## Literature limitations

This is a narrow source audit, not an exhaustive novelty search.  It does not
claim global literature priority or external peer review.  Affine inconsistent
sets, nonlinear/nonconvex feasibility, infinite-dimensional nonclosed sums,
and optimized generalized reflection schemes are outside the frozen owner.
