\begin{abstract}
We close a source-level theorem for one-dimensional Brownian motion with a
Poisson reset to its starting point.  The free process has an absolutely
continuous Laplace stationary law, while the separately killed search process
has an exact first-passage renewal transform.  The mean hitting time, all
Laplace moments, the unique positive reset optimum, and singular boundaries
follow in one dimensionless calculation.  The denominator is a renewal
resolvent, not a dynamical zeta or a Fredholm determinant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Two realizations}
Fix $D,r,a>0$.  In the free realization on $\R$,
$dX_t=\sqrt{2D}\,dW_t$ between independent rate-$r$ resets to $0$.
In the search realization the same process starts at $0$ and is killed at
$T_a=\inf\{t\geq0:X_t=a\}$; its law on $(-\infty,a)$ is therefore
sub-Markov after absorption.  The stationary statement below belongs only to
the free realization.  The clock is physical elapsed time.

\section{Free renewal theorem}
Let
\[
 G_D(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi Dt}}\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{4Dt}\right).
\]
Conditioning on the last reset gives
\begin{equation}
p_r(x,t\mid0)=e^{-rt}G_D(x,t)+r\int_0^t e^{-ru}G_D(x,u)\,du .
\label{eq:renewal}
\end{equation}
For $x\ne0$, the integral equals
\[
\frac{e^{-|x|\sqrt{r/D}}\operatorname{erfc}(\frac{|x|}{2\sqrt{Dt}}-\sqrt{rt})
-e^{|x|\sqrt{r/D}}\operatorname{erfc}(\frac{|x|}{2\sqrt{Dt}}+\sqrt{rt})}{4\sqrt{Dr}},
\]
and its continuous $x=0$ value is $\operatorname{erf}(\sqrt{rt})/(2\sqrt{Dr})$.
For every $t>0$ this is an absolutely continuous Lebesgue density.  Letting
$t\to\infty$ gives the normalized free stationary law
\begin{equation}
p_{\rm st}(x)=\frac12\sqrt{\frac rD}\,e^{-|x|\sqrt{r/D}},
\qquad \int_{\R}p_{\rm st}(x)\,dx=1.
\label{eq:stationary}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:stationary} is not a stationary claim for the killed search.

\subsection*{A realization check}
The distinction is operational, not cosmetic.  At a fixed positive time the
free law is a Lebesgue density: a reset at an exactly prescribed instant has
probability zero, so the renewal integral does not hide a point mass.  For the
search process the state space is $(-\infty,a)$ with a Dirichlet boundary at
$a$; probability that has reached the boundary is removed.  Thus
\eqref{eq:stationary} can be used to describe long-time resetting on $\R$,
but never to normalize the killed survival law.

\section{Killed first passage}
The no-reset transform from $0$ to $a$ is
$f_0(q)=e^{-a\sqrt{q/D}}$.  A first-reset decomposition yields
\begin{equation}
F_r(s):=\E[e^{-sT_a}]
 =\frac{(s+r)e^{-a\sqrt{(s+r)/D}}}
 {s+r e^{-a\sqrt{(s+r)/D}}},
\qquad s\geq0,
\label{eq:fpt}
\end{equation}
and the survival transform
\begin{equation}
S_r(s):=\int_0^\infty e^{-st}\Pr(T_a>t)\,dt
 =\frac{1-e^{-a\sqrt{(s+r)/D}}}
 {s+r e^{-a\sqrt{(s+r)/D}}}.
\label{eq:survival}
\end{equation}
For $s>0$, $1-F_r(s)=sS_r(s)$; continuity at zero gives
\[
\E[T_a]=-F_r'(0)=S_r(0)=\frac{e^{a\sqrt{r/D}}-1}{r}.
\]
All moments are finite for positive $D,r,a$.  For every $n\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
(-1)^nF_r^{(n)}(0)=\E[T_a^n],\qquad
(-1)^nS_r^{(n)}(0)=\frac{\E[T_a^{n+1}]}{n+1}.
\label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}

One direct way to see \eqref{eq:fpt} is to write $f=f_0(s+r)$ for the
no-reset transform during one exponentially distributed attempt.  A reset
before absorption contributes the factor $r(1-f)/(s+r)$ and starts an
independent copy, hence
\[
F_r(s)=f+\frac{r(1-f)}{s+r}F_r(s).
\]
This scalar renewal equation also shows why its denominator is a resolvent of
attempts rather than a periodic-orbit product.

\section{Universal optimum and boundaries}
Put $z=a\sqrt{r/D}$.  Then
\[
\frac D{a^2}\E[T_a]=g(z):=\frac{e^z-1}{z^2}.
\]
The derivative has the sign of
$h(z)=z-2(1-e^{-z})$: $g(z)\to\infty$ at both endpoints,
$h$ has exactly one root in $(0,\infty)$ other than the limiting root at zero,
and
\[
z_*=1.5936242600400400923\ldots,
\qquad r_*=D(z_*/a)^2.
\]
At $r=0$ there is no normalizable stationary density and the half-line
MFPT is infinite; at $a=0$, $T_a=0$; at $D=0$, a reset path cannot reach a
positive target.  These are boundaries, not substitutions into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fpt}.
