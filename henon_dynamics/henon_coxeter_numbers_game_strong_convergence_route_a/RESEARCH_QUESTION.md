# Research question

For a dominant position in an arbitrary finite crystallographic Coxeter/Weyl
system, does every sequence of legal positive-coordinate firings terminate at
one common position after one common number of moves, including all wall,
zero, reducible, and rank-one faces?  Can the terminal group element and the
common length be identified intrinsically from the stabilizer parabolic?

The answer is yes.  For
`J={i:<lambda,alpha_i^vee>=0}`, legal plays are precisely left weak-order
ascents through the minimal right-coset representatives `W^J`.  Every maximal
play reaches `w_0w_J`, has length
`|Phi^+|-|Phi_J^+|`, and sends `lambda` to the unique anti-dominant point
`w_0lambda` in its orbit.

This is a finite-type strong-convergence question.  It is not a claim for
affine, indefinite, noncrystallographic, or arbitrary generalized Cartan
systems, and it supplies no prime clock, primitive periodic orbit, target
determinant, analytic target bridge, or same-clock quantum operator.
