# Narrative report

## Result

One side-coupled degree of freedom changes the free one-dimensional chain in a
way that is simultaneously elementary and easy to mishandle.  The free local
Green function lives on a two-sheeted square-root surface.  Its physical branch
forces one pole below and one pole above the band for every nonzero coupling.
Squaring that pole equation produces a convenient quartic, but sometimes also
two additional real roots.  The paper's central discipline is to separate
algebraic candidates from physical-sheet eigenvalues.

The same Schur function gives the impurity spectral density and residue
weights.  Reflection parity then turns the full operator into one free odd
half-line and one cyclic, eventually-free even-plus-impurity Jacobi matrix.
With the convention used here, the scalar resolvent is anti-Herglotz and its
negative is Herglotz.  Uniform boundary convergence on compact open-band
subintervals and Stone inversion identify the full local measure with the
displayed density.  Exterior meromorphy leaves only the two simple atoms, and
the explicit edge atom limits vanish.  This closes the band multiplicity and
singular-continuous exclusion without an appeal to a finite chain.

Finally, eliminating the impurity in the stationary equation produces an
energy-dependent on-site potential.  Matching the two plane-wave halves gives
unitary reflection/transmission probabilities and exact destructive
interference at the impurity energy precisely when that energy lies in the
open band.
