\section{The coarse quotient retains the invariant sector}\label{sec:chronology}

Let $\ell$ be any prime and set
\begin{equation}
\Vn=(\pi_n)_*\Q_\ell.
\end{equation}
On a geometric fiber, $\Vn$ is the vector space of functions on the finite
exact-period set, and $\Dih$ permutes its coordinate basis.  After spreading
the cover and the action over $\Z[1/N]$ for a suitable $N$, every good closed
point of residue characteristic $p\nmid N\ell$ has a geometric Frobenius
action on the corresponding stalk.

\begin{theorem}[invariant-sector projection]\label{thm:chronology}
Let $\mathcal C_n=\Pn/\Dih$ be the coarse orbit-set cover over $U_n$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:invariants}
(\mathcal C_n\to U_n)_*\Q_\ell\simeq\Vn^{\Dih}.
\end{equation}
The generator $H$ acts identically on the right-hand side.  If $F^r$ is any
endomorphism commuting with $\Dih$---including geometric Frobenius at a good
closed point---then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:average}
\Tr(F^r\mid\Vn^{\Dih})
=\frac1{2n}\sum_{g\in\Dih}\Tr(F^rg\mid\Vn).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Functions on a finite orbit set are exactly the functions on the original set
that are constant on every group orbit.  This proves
\eqref{eq:invariants}.  Every vector in $\Vn^{\Dih}$ is fixed by every group
element, including $H$.  Since $\Q_\ell$ has characteristic zero, the Reynolds
projector is
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{inv}}=\frac1{2n}\sum_{g\in\Dih}g.
\end{equation}
Commutation of $F^r$ with this projector gives
\begin{align}
\Tr(F^r\mid\Vn^{\Dih})
&=\Tr(F^rP_{\mathrm{inv}}\mid\Vn)\\
&=\frac1{2n}\sum_{g\in\Dih}\Tr(F^rg\mid\Vn),
\end{align}
which is \eqref{eq:average}.
\end{proof}

The theorem distinguishes unmarked orbit identity from equivariant data.  The
intermediate invariants $\Vn^{\langle H\rangle}$ remember cyclic H\'enon
orbits while distinguishing a chiral orbit from its reversal partner.  Taking
the remaining reversal invariants identifies that pair:
\begin{equation}
\Vn^{\Dih}=\bigl(\Vn^{\langle H\rangle}\bigr)^{\langle R\rangle}.
\end{equation}
The final quotient therefore does not remember a marked phase, reversal
orientation, any non-trivial $\Dih$ representation, or an unaveraged joint
Frobenius--H\'enon trace.

This statement does not invalidate ordinary autonomous dynamical zeta
functions.  Such zeta functions intentionally sum cycles modulo cyclic
starting phase, and the period $n$ remains an external label.  They do,
however, count both members of a chiral pair.  An unweighted squarefree
dihedral marker counts that pair once; a scalar orbit zeta built from it must
restore multiplicity two or an equivalent representation weight.

Ordinary constant-$\Q_\ell$ cohomology of the quotient stack also computes the
invariant sector: higher finite-group cohomology vanishes in characteristic
zero.  This does not mean that the stack forgets all equivariant information.
Its sheaf category can retain the omitted sectors after one specifies
non-trivial equivariant coefficients or an inertia-type construction.  A
candidate invoking non-trivial joint H\'enon--Frobenius action must make that
additional structure explicit.
