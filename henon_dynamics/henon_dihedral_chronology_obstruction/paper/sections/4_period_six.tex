\section{The first chiral quotient has genus zero}\label{sec:period6}

The all-period counting formulas of \citet{gallas2007} provide a useful
consistency check.  Let $M_n=\nu(n)/n$ and define
\begin{equation}
A_n=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)2^{\lfloor(d+1)/2\rfloor},\qquad
Q_n=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)2^{\lfloor(d+2)/2\rfloor}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
D_n=\begin{cases}A_n,&n\text{ odd},\\A_n/2,&n\text{ even},\end{cases}
\qquad
N_n=\begin{cases}0,&n\text{ odd},\\Q_n/2,&n\text{ even},\end{cases}
\end{equation}
and the number of chiral cyclic orbits is $C_n=M_n-D_n-N_n$.
Since chiral orbits occur in reversal pairs, the coarse quotient degree is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:burnside-degree}
D_n+N_n+C_n/2=\frac{M_n+D_n+N_n}{2}.
\end{equation}

At $n=6$, the source factorization is
\begin{equation}
S_6(\sigma)=C_6(\sigma)^2D_6(\sigma)N_6(\sigma),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
C_6=\sigma-2,\qquad D_6=\sigma^2+4\sigma-4A
\end{equation}
and
\begin{align}
N_6={}&\sigma^5+2\sigma^4-4(5A+4)\sigma^3+8A\sigma^2\notag\\
&+4(16A^2+12A+9)\sigma+128A^2-96A+72.
\label{eq:N6}
\end{align}
The squarefree marker has degrees $1+2+5=8$, agreeing with
Table~\ref{tab:counts}.

\begin{lemma}[generic period-six marker bridge]\label{lem:marker-bridge}
Shrink the period-six parameter open set so that the source orbital
polynomials are separable and reconstruct the cycles they label.  The map
$\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^6x_i$ then induces a generically bijective map from the
coarse dihedral orbit-set cover to the squarefree marker
$C_6D_6N_6=0$.  Their componentwise normalizations are birational.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The orbital reconstruction theorem associates one cyclic cycle to each simple
root of $D_6N_6$ and two distinct cyclic cycles to the double root selected by
$C_6$; the latter two are exchanged by $R$
\citep{endler2004,endler2006chiral}.  Thus the nine cyclic cycles map to eight
distinct squarefree markers, exactly one for each of the eight dihedral
classes in Table~\ref{tab:counts}.  A finite generically degree-one map of
curves induces an equality of function fields after normalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:genus0}
The smooth projective normalizations of the three affine components
$C_6=0$, $D_6=0$, and $N_6=0$ all have genus zero.  In particular, their
weight-one $H^1$ vanishes.  Under Lemma~\ref{lem:marker-bridge}, this is also
the componentwise genus statement for the generic coarse period-six quotient.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first component is the parameter line $\sigma=2$.  The second is rational
because $A=(\sigma^2+4\sigma)/4$.  For the third, regard \eqref{eq:N6} as a
quadratic $c_2(\sigma)A^2+c_1(\sigma)A+c_0(\sigma)$.  Exact factorization gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disc}
\disc_A(N_6)=16(\sigma-6)(\sigma+2)
(3\sigma^2-8\sigma-12)^2.
\end{equation}
On the generic locus, the substitution
\begin{equation}
Y=\frac{2c_2(\sigma)A+c_1(\sigma)}
{4(3\sigma^2-8\sigma-12)}
\end{equation}
identifies its function field with that of
\begin{equation}
Y^2=(\sigma-6)(\sigma+2).
\end{equation}
This conic is rational; one parametrization is
\begin{equation}
\sigma=2+2(t+t^{-1}),\qquad Y=2(t-t^{-1}).
\end{equation}
The inverse parameter is $t=(\sigma-2+Y)/4$, while
$A=(4(3\sigma^2-8\sigma-12)Y-c_1)/(2c_2)$.  The discriminant in
\eqref{eq:disc} is not a square in $\Q(\sigma)$, so this is the generic
quadratic function field rather than a collapsed linear component.  All three
normalizations therefore have genus zero.
\end{proof}

Singular gluing among rational components can contribute weight-zero
dual-graph cohomology.  It cannot supply the proposed weight-one curve
spectrum.  Proposition~\ref{prop:genus0} is a low-period witness, not a claim
that every higher-period component is rational.
