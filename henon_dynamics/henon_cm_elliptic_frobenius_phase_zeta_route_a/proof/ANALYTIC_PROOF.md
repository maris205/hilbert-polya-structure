# Proof package: primary CM phases and all-degree Frobenius dynamics

## Claim

For every odd prime $p$, let $E_p$ be the smooth projective completion of
$y^2=x^3-x$ over $\mathbf F_p$, and let $F(x,y)=(x^p,y^p)$ on its geometric
points. Put $t_p=p+1-\#E_p(\mathbf F_p)$. If $p\equiv3\pmod4$, then
$t_p=0$. If $p\equiv1\pmod4$, there are unique integers $a,b$ with
$b>0$, $a$ odd, $b$ even, $a^2+b^2=p$ and $a+b\equiv1\pmod4$, and
$t_p=2a$. The unordered eigenvalue pair is $\{a+bi,a-bi\}$ in the latter
case and $\{i\sqrt p,-i\sqrt p\}$ in the former. For every $n\geq1$,
the fixed-point count, primitive orbit count, and native zeta are determined
by this pair as stated in Steps 5--7 below.

## Status

PROVABLE AS STATED. This is a source-specific synthesis and independently
audited implementation of classical CM and finite-field geometry, not a
claim of a new CM theorem or a Riemann-target determinant.

## Assumptions

1. $p$ is prime and odd; characteristic two is excluded because the model
   has discriminant $64$ and is singular there.
2. Geometric Frobenius terminology is avoided: $F$ means the explicitly
   written $p$-power morphism. On cohomology use its pullback, whose degree
   two eigenvalue is $p$.
3. The standard elliptic-curve endomorphism theorem is used: the rational
   endomorphism algebra is $\mathbf Q$, an imaginary quadratic field, or
   a definite quaternion algebra; endomorphisms are integral and the
   characteristic polynomial has degree as constant term. See Milne,
   *Elliptic Curves*, II §6, especially Theorem 6.2 and the classification
   following it. The elliptic finite-field trace formula is IV §9,
   Theorems 9.4 and 9.10 and Remark 9.11.
4. Ordinary means that the geometric $p$-torsion has rank one; for an
   elliptic curve over $\mathbf F_p$ this is equivalent to $p\nmid t_p$.
   The complementary case is supersingular. This standard criterion is
   invoked, not inferred from a finite data table.

## Notation

$N_{p,n}=\#E_p(\mathbf F_{p^n})$, $B_{p,n}$ is the number of closed points
of degree $n$, $S_{p,n}=\alpha_p^n+\bar\alpha_p^n$, and $u$ is a formal
variable or a complex variable in a stated convergence domain. Conjugation
always swaps the eigenvalues and never selects a literal prime ideal.

## Proof strategy and dependency map

The inert chamber uses a character involution. The split chamber uses the
centralizer of an intrinsic order-four automorphism, then explicit rational
torsion to determine the otherwise ambiguous Gaussian unit. The all-degree
claims use the standard Frobenius trace theorem, finite Galois orbits,
Möbius inversion, and formal logarithms. The source-target separation is a
direct zero-count obstruction.

## Proof

### Step 1. Smoothness, involution, and the inert chamber

The polynomial $x^3-x$ has distinct roots $0,1,-1$ in odd characteristic;
the projective point at infinity is smooth. If $\chi$ is the quadratic
character with $\chi(0)=0$, then
$$N_{p,1}=p+1+\sum_{x\in\mathbf F_p}\chi(x^3-x).$$
For $p\equiv3\pmod4$, replacing $x$ by $-x$ multiplies every summand
by $\chi(-1)=-1$. The sum equals its negative in $\mathbf Z$ and is
zero. Thus $t_p=0$, including $p=3$.

### Step 2. The split Frobenius lies in the Gaussian order

For $p\equiv1\pmod4$ choose $j\in\mathbf F_p$ with $j^2=-1$ and define
$I(x,y)=(-x,jy)$. Then $I^2=[-1]$, so $\mathbf Q(I)\simeq\mathbf Q(i)$
embeds in $\operatorname{End}^0(E_{\overline{\mathbf F}_p})$. Since $I$
is defined over $\mathbf F_p$, $F$ commutes with it. In a quadratic field
the centralizer is the field itself. In a quaternion algebra, the
centralizer of a noncentral element is its quadratic field: writing the
algebra as $K\oplus Kv$ with $vi=-iv$, a commuting element has zero
$v$ coefficient. Therefore $F\in\mathbf Q(i)$. Integrality of
endomorphisms gives $F=a+bi$ with $a,b\in\mathbf Z$, because the maximal
order of $\mathbf Q(i)$ is $\mathbf Z[i]$. The degree equals the norm,
hence $a^2+b^2=p$, and its characteristic trace is $2a=t_p$.

All three nonzero points of $E[2]$ are rational. Thus $F-[1]$ kills $E[2]$.
Because $[2]$ is separable, an isogeny killing its kernel factors through
$[2]$; consequently $(F-[1])/2$ is an endomorphism. It still lies in
$\mathbf Q(i)$ and is integral, so $a$ is odd and $b$ even.

### Step 3. The Gaussian sign is forced by rational four-torsion

The point $Q=(j,j-1)$ belongs to $E(\mathbf F_p)$ since
$(j-1)^2=-2j=j^3-j$. Its tangent slope is
$m=(3j^2-1)/(2(j-1))=j+1$. The doubling formula gives
$x(2Q)=m^2-2j=0$ and $y(2Q)=m(j-0)-(j-1)=0$.
Thus $2Q=(0,0)$, and $Q$ has order four. The point $(1,0)$ is not in
$\langle Q\rangle$, whose unique element of order two is $(0,0)$.
The subgroup generated by these points has order eight. Hence
$$8\mid N_{p,1}=p+1-2a,\qquad a\equiv(p+1)/2\pmod4.$$
For odd $a$ and even $b$, $a^2\equiv1\pmod8$. If $b\equiv0\pmod4$,
then $p\equiv1\pmod8$ and $a\equiv1\pmod4$; if $b\equiv2\pmod4$,
then $p\equiv5\pmod8$ and $a\equiv3\pmod4$. These statements are
equivalent to $a+b\equiv1\pmod4$. Unique factorization in $\mathbf Z[i]$
gives a unique norm-$p$ pair with the stated parity, up to signs. The
congruence fixes the sign of $a$, and $b>0$ fixes the display convention.
Equivalently $a+bi\equiv1\pmod{(1+i)^3}$. Both conjugates satisfy this
primary condition; it does not distinguish the two primes above $p$.

### Step 4. Phase and ordinary/supersingular distinction

The characteristic polynomial is $X^2-t_pX+p$. In the split chamber its
roots are $a\pm bi$, both of norm $\sqrt p$. Since $0<|a|<\sqrt p$,
$p\nmid2a$, and the curve is ordinary. The phase in the upper half-plane
is the unique $\theta_p\in(0,\pi)$ with
$\cos\theta_p=a/\sqrt p$ and $\sin\theta_p=b/\sqrt p$.
In the inert chamber the roots are $\pm i\sqrt p$, $\theta_p=\pi/2$,
and $p\mid t_p$, so the curve is supersingular. The phase is recorded
algebraically; no transcendental decimal precision is needed.

In fact the split normalized eigenvalue is never a root of unity. If
$\alpha/\sqrt p$ were a root of unity, its square
$\alpha/\bar\alpha$ would be a root of unity in $\mathbf Q(i)$, hence
one of $1,-1,i,-i$. These four cases require, respectively, $b=0$, $a=0$,
$a=b$, or $a=-b$. None is possible for an odd prime norm with $a$ odd and
$b$ even. Thus $\theta_p/\pi$ is irrational in the ordinary chamber.
Every extension has strict Hasse inequality there. In the supersingular
chamber the normalized eigenvalues have exact order four, and the Hasse
bound is saturated exactly for even extension degree. At degree $n=2m$,
the count is $(p^m-(-1)^m)^2$. This distinguishes quasiperiodic CM phase
from a genuinely periodic phase at every degree without numerical angles.

### Step 5. All extension counts and genuine primitive closed points

The Frobenius trace theorem yields, for every $n\geq1$,
$$S_{p,0}=2,\quad S_{p,1}=t_p,\quad
S_{p,n}=t_pS_{p,n-1}-pS_{p,n-2},\quad
N_{p,n}=p^n+1-S_{p,n}.$$
In the split chamber $S_{p,n}=2\operatorname{Re}(a+bi)^n$; in the inert
chamber it is zero for odd $n$ and $2(-p)^{n/2}$ for even $n$.
Every geometric point has coordinates in a finite extension, so its orbit
under $F$ is finite. Closed points are exactly these Galois orbits. A
degree-$d$ orbit has $d$ points fixed by $F^n$ exactly when $d\mid n$.
Consequently
$$N_{p,n}=\sum_{d\mid n}dB_{p,d},\qquad
B_{p,n}=\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)N_{p,n/d}\in\mathbf Z_{\geq0}.$$
This proves completeness for all degrees; finite enumeration is only an
implementation check. Orientation is forward $F$, repetitions are powers
$F^n$, and primitive weights are one. The derivative of the characteristic
$p$ morphism is zero. It is not a real differentiable return-map stability
multiplier. The CM cohomological phase is not an attached primitive weight.
Moreover $F^n-[1]$ has differential $-\mathrm{id}$, so this isogeny is
separable and its kernel is reduced. Each geometric fixed point has scheme
multiplicity one. This is an algebraic multiplicity statement, not a
positive-Lyapunov stability statement.

### Step 6. Native zeta and the full graded determinant

Formal logarithms give
$$Z_p(u)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{N_{p,n}}n u^n\right)
=\prod_{d\geq1}(1-u^d)^{-B_{p,d}}
=\frac{1-t_pu+pu^2}{(1-u)(1-pu)}.$$
The sum and product converge absolutely for $|u|<1/p$, since
$N_{p,n}\leq p^n+1+2p^{n/2}$. This defines the same rational function
elsewhere by continuation. On $\ell$-adic cohomology, $\ell\ne p$,
the eigenvalues are $1$ on $H^0$, $\alpha_p,\bar\alpha_p$ on $H^1$,
and $p$ on $H^2$. Thus
$$Z_p(u)=\prod_{r=0}^2\det(1-uF^*\mid H^r)^{(-1)^{r+1}}.$$
In particular, the numerator is the $H^1$ determinant and the zeta is the
graded ratio; neither may be substituted for its reciprocal unnoticed.
The two numerator roots are distinct and have modulus $p^{-1/2}$.
They cannot cancel the poles at $1$ or $1/p$, since their moduli differ.

### Step 7. Functional equation and a source-target obstruction

Direct substitution gives $Z_p(1/(pu))=Z_p(u)$. With $u=p^{-s}$,
$Z_p(s)=Z_p(1-s)$ and conjugation symmetry hold. Its zeros lie on
$\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$ and form two arithmetic progressions of imaginary
parts modulo $2\pi/\log p$. Their number for $0<\operatorname{Im}s\leq T$
is $(\log p/\pi)T+O(1)$. The completed Riemann zeta has the classical
Riemann--von Mangoldt count of order $T\log T$. A zero-free entire
prefactor cannot change a divisor, so no fixed-$p$ source determinant can
equal $e^{g(s)}\xi(s)$. Taking an unanalysed product over $p$ does not
remove this obstruction for the frozen fixed-fibre object and is not a
construction established here. This is obstruction HEN-O366.

### Step 8. Arithmetic controls and their limits

For a nonsquare $d\in\mathbf F_p$, the quadratic twist $dy^2=x^3-x$
has trace $-t_p$: the quadratic-character sum acquires the factor $-1$.
The simpler parent $\mathbf P^1$ has $N_n=p^n+1$ and no $H^1$ factor.
These analytically exact controls separate CM phase from generic finite-field
rationality. Mixed composite characteristic cannot be a field, since a
field has prime characteristic; prime-power cardinalities retain the same
prime characteristic and correspond to iterates, not new prime owners.
These are three A0 controls. The six required A1 perturbation controls are
not all completed. Strict evaluation therefore records A1_WEAK despite
the complete native closed-point formula. A2_FAIL and A3_FAIL refer to the
Riemann target. Normalized $H^1$ eigenvalues give A4_FORMAL_HINT only;
no Hilbert-space/domain/quantization theorem is proved.
