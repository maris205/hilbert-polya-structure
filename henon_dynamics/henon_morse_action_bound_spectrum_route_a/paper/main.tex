\begin{abstract}
We solve the classical and quantum one-dimensional Morse Hamiltonian under
one convention lock.  Every bounded classical orbit has an elementary
action and period, while canonical quantization has a finite, explicitly
exhausted generalized-Laguerre point spectrum.

The proof derives both formulas and fixes the strict square-integrability
cutoff.
\fi
\ifnum\CRevisionRound>1

\fi
\end{abstract}

\section{Frozen Hamiltonian and result}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
 V(x)=D(e^{-2ax}-2e^{-ax}),\qquad
 H_{\rm cl}(x,p)=\frac{p^2}{2m}+V(x),
\end{equation}
where $m,D,a>0$.  The potential has minimum $-D$ at zero, tends to zero
from below as $x\to+\infty$, and tends to $+\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$.
For a closed regular energy curve we use the normalized action
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actiondef}
 J(E)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint p\,dx.
\end{equation}
For $\hbar>0$, let $H_q$ be the self-adjoint operator associated with the
lower-bounded closed form of
\[
 -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dx^2}+V(x)
 \quad\hbox{on }L^2(\R),
 \qquad \lambda=\frac{\sqrt{2mD}}{a\hbar}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Classical action and quantum closure]\label{thm:main}
For every $-D<E<0$, the classical energy curve is periodic and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:JT}
 J(E)=\frac{\sqrt{2mD}}a\left(1-\sqrt{-E/D}\right),\qquad
 T(E)=\frac{2\pi}{a\sqrt{-2E/m}},\qquad
 J'(E)=\frac{T(E)}{2\pi}.
\end{equation}
The essential spectrum of $H_q$ is $[0,\infty)$.  Its point spectrum is
exactly the finite set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:levels}
 E_n=-\frac{a^2\hbar^2}{2m}(\lambda-n-\tfrac12)^2,\qquad
 n\in\mathbb N_0,\quad n<\lambda-\tfrac12,
\end{equation}
and every eigenvalue is simple.  With $z=2\lambda e^{-ax}$, a corresponding
eigenfunction is, up to normalization,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eigenfunction}
 \psi_n(x)=z^{\lambda-n-1/2}e^{-z/2}
 L_n^{(2\lambda-2n-1)}(z).
\end{equation}
There are $\max(0,\lceil\lambda-1/2\rceil)$ bound states.  Equality in the
cutoff produces a zero-energy constant tail, not an $L^2$ eigenfunction;
there are no nonnegative eigenvalues.
\end{theorem}

The phrase \emph{Morse classical action owner} is the round-zero
certificate: it fixes the model, the $2\pi$ normalization, and the strict
spectral statement before any finite computation.

\section{Classical proof}
Put $y=e^{-ax}$ and $\delta=\sqrt{1+E/D}\in(0,1)$.  The two turning values
of $y$ are $1-\delta$ and $1+\delta$; their order in $x$ is reversed.  A
full oscillation has period
\begin{align*}
 T(E)&=\sqrt{2m}\int_{x_-}^{x_+}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{E-V(x)}}\\
 &=\frac{\sqrt{2m}}{a\sqrt D}
 \int_0^\pi\frac{d\theta}{1+\delta\cos\theta}
 =\frac{\pi\sqrt{2m}}{a\sqrt{-E}}.
\end{align*}
The last integral follows directly from the tangent-half-angle
substitution.  Differentiating~\eqref{eq:actiondef} under the integral gives
$J'(E)=T(E)/(2\pi)$; endpoint terms vanish because $p=0$ at both turning
points.  Since the curve contracts to the equilibrium at $E=-D$, we have
$J(-D)=0$.  Integrating the period from $-D$ to $E$ proves all of
\eqref{eq:JT}.  This derivation also detects an erroneous use of the
unnormalized closed integral as the action.

\section{Quantum reduction and exhaustion}
Write a prospective negative eigenvalue as
$E=-(a^2\hbar^2/2m)s^2$ with $s>0$.  The change
$z=2\lambda e^{-ax}$ turns the Schr\"odinger equation into
\begin{equation}\label{eq:whittaker}
 z^2\psi_{zz}+z\psi_z+(-z^2/4+\lambda z-s^2)\psi=0.
\end{equation}
Substitution $\psi=z^se^{-z/2}F$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laguerre}
 zF''+(2s+1-z)F'+(\lambda-s-\tfrac12)F=0.
\end{equation}
At $z\to\infty$, square integrability selects the recessive Whittaker
solution.  Its small-$z$ nonintegrable coefficient is proportional to
$1/\Gamma(s-\lambda+1/2)$, and hence vanishes precisely when
\[
 \lambda-s-\tfrac12=n\in\mathbb N_0.
\]
Then~\eqref{eq:laguerre} terminates as
$L_n^{(2s)}(z)$.  Since $dx=-(az)^{-1}dz$, the remaining endpoint test is
$\int_0 z^{2s-1}\,dz<\infty$, exactly when $s>0$.  Thus the inequality in
\eqref{eq:levels} is strict and the displayed list exhausts all negative
eigenvalues.  Sturm oscillation supplies simplicity and node count $n$.
The special-function conventions agree with the official references
\cite{DLMFL,DLMFW}.

\section{Threshold, essential spectrum, and all faces}
Weyl sequences supported increasingly far to the right see the free
half-line and give $[0,\infty)\subset\sigma_{\rm ess}(H_q)$.  On a left
half-line, $V\to+\infty$ gives compact resolvent.  On a right half-line,

Dirichlet bracketing therefore yields
$\sigma_{\rm ess}(H_q)=[0,\infty)$.  Standard one-dimensional Volterra
asymptotics for this short-range tail make every nonzero positive-energy
solution oscillatory with nonvanishing amplitude and every zero-energy

terminating equality $s=0$, formula~\eqref{eq:eigenfunction} directly has

The complete boundary atlas is now transparent.  At $E=-D$ lies the

energy shell.  Quantum mechanically, $\lambda\leq1/2$ gives no bound state;
every equality $n=\lambda-1/2$ is excluded.  Nonpositive $m,D,a$, or
$\hbar$ lies outside the frozen owner.
