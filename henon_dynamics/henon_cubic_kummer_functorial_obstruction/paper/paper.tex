\begin{abstract}
The homogeneous cubic H\'enon phase gives a natural scalar scaling cocycle,
but that cocycle is an exact gauge coboundary.  We test the smallest proposed
nonscalar escape: the direct three-channel Kummer grading.  We prove a general
representation-stability theorem: every finite-dimensional functor applied
to a scalar coboundary is again a coboundary, and therefore has identity
holonomy on every closed orbit and every repetition.  The result covers
tensor products, exterior powers, duals, parity shifts, and virtual
supertraces.  A type-strict exact certificate checks 10,985 component
identities in a thirteen-state model and an independent eleven-state replay.
Thus a direct \(\mathbb Z/3\)-graded lift cannot supply new prime weights or a
H\'enon determinant.  A surviving successor must add nonfunctorial channel
permutation or projective central-extension data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The area-preserving H\'enon program begins with a nonlinear symplectic map and
asks whether its periodic, arithmetic, or quantized structures can enter a
Hilbert--P\'olya-style determinant.  The foundational map and its exact
generating-function structure are described in \cite{Wang2026}.  In the
adelic scaling realization, the homogeneous cubic phase
\(\phi(x)=\psi(2x^3)\) produces the cocycle
\[
 c(a,x)=\phi(ax)\phi(x)^{-1}.
\]
The scalar line is gauge trivial.  A tempting response is to retain the three
cubic characters as a graded vector bundle.  This paper shows that this
change is insufficient whenever it is performed functorially.

Our contribution is one sharp statement.  \emph{Representations do not turn
a coboundary into monodromy.}  This elementary fact has a consequential
application: every direct Kummer channel, every finite tensor construction,
and every associated virtual supertrace has identity closed holonomy.  The
obstruction is all-orbit and independent of a prime cutoff.

The paper distinguishes three evidence levels.  The representation theorem
is \texttt{PROVED}.  The finite programs are
\texttt{NUMERICALLY\_CERTIFIED} implementation checks.  The possibility of a
nonfunctorial monomial or projective extension remains \texttt{OPEN}.

\section{The homogeneous cubic cocycle}
Let a group \(G\) act on a set \(X\) on the left, and let \(A\) be a group.
For a map \(\phi:X\to A\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocycle}
 c(g,x)=\phi(gx)\phi(x)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Then
\[
 c(gh,x)=c(g,hx)c(h,x).
\]
In the H\'enon application, \(X\) is the nonzero adelic scaling site,
\(G\) is the scaling group, and \(A\) is the circle group generated by the
global additive character.  The phase \(2x^3\) is fixed by the source map;
no prime or Riemann-zero data enter its definition.  Tate harmonic analysis
supplies the ambient normalization \cite{Tate1950}.

Let \(\mu_3=\langle\zeta\rangle\).  The direct three-channel model records
the characters \(1,\chi,\chi^2\).  If the scalar cocycle is represented by a
\(\mu_3\)-valued label, its fibre map is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direct}
 U(g,x)=\operatorname{diag}(1,c(g,x),c(g,x)^2).
\end{equation}
The determinant of \eqref{eq:direct} is one, but determinant one alone would
not exclude nontrivial matrix holonomy.  The theorem below does.

\section{Representation-stable gauge triviality}
\begin{theorem}[Functorial coboundary theorem]\label{thm:main}
Let \(G\curvearrowright X\), let \(A\) be a group, and let \(c\) be given by
\eqref{eq:cocycle}.  For every representation
\(\rho:A\to\mathrm{GL}(V)\), the represented cocycle satisfies
\[
 \rho(c(g,x))=F(gx)F(x)^{-1},\qquad F=\rho\circ\phi.
\]
For a closed word \(g_n\cdots g_1\) based at \(x\), its chronological
holonomy on \(V\) is the identity.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first identity follows by applying the homomorphism \(\rho\) to
\eqref{eq:cocycle}.  Write \(x_0=x\) and
\(x_j=g_jx_{j-1}\).  Chronological multiplication gives
\[
 \prod_{j=n}^{1}F(x_j)F(x_{j-1})^{-1}
 =F(x_n)F(x_0)^{-1}.
\]
If the word closes, \(x_n=x_0\), so the product is the identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite tensor closure]\label{cor:tensor}
The conclusion of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} survives finite direct sums, tensor
products, duals, exterior and symmetric powers, parity shifts, and formal
differences in the representation ring.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each listed construction is a functor on finite-dimensional
representations.  It sends \(F(gx)F(x)^{-1}\) to the corresponding quotient
of the transformed gauges.  Formal differences alter traces and
determinants but not the identity of each closed holonomy.
\end{proof}

For the three Kummer characters, take
\(F(x)=\operatorname{diag}(1,\phi(x),\phi(x)^2)\).  Equation
\eqref{eq:direct} is precisely \(F(gx)F(x)^{-1}\).  Thus its full matrix
holonomy, not only its determinant, is one.

\section{Prime repetitions and determinant consequences}
Let \(C_p\) denote an inherited scaling-site primitive orbit with length
\(\log p\).  The \(r\)-fold repetition has holonomy \(I\) by
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.  Hence the H\'enon increment to any orbit trace is
just the dimension or superdimension of a fixed representation.  It does
not depend on \(p\), on chronology, or on the cubic dynamics.

\begin{proposition}[No new functorial prime weight]
Any orbit Euler factor obtained solely by applying a finite representation
functor to the scalar homogeneous cocycle is identical to the corresponding
factor for the undecorated scaling system, up to a fixed multiplicity and
an exactly removable gauge.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All closed holonomies equal the identity.  Every character evaluation is
therefore its value at the identity, which is independent of the orbit.  A
graded character replaces the dimension by a superdimension but introduces
no H\'enon-dependent coefficient.
\end{proof}

This proposition does not say that all three-channel cocycles are trivial.
It says that those obtained functorially from the scalar phase are trivial.
A monomial cocycle of the form
\[
 F(gx)P(g)F(x)^{-1}
\]
can retain the channel permutation \(P(g)\).  Such data are not generated by
changing representation category alone and form the next candidate.

\section{Exact finite certificate}
The certificate works in \(X=\mathbb Z/13\mathbb Z\) with translation action
and phase label
\[
 f(x)=x^3+2x+1\pmod 3.
\]
It exhausts all \((g,h,x)\), verifies the cocycle identity, checks the three
represented gauges, and checks the graded determinant.  A closed path with
increments \((2,5,1,5)\) starts at \(4\) and has channel exponents
\((0,0,0)\).  The totals are shown in Table~\ref{tab:checks}.

\section{Route-A and Route-B audit}
The primitive scaling orbits and \(\log p\) clock remain exact, so A1 is
weak rather than absent.  A2 fails: there is no new zeta or determinant.
The grading is analytically coherent but inherits all divisor data, giving
only partial A3.  The quantization is natural, giving A4.  The resulting
tuple is
\[
 (A1_{\rm WEAK},A2_{\rm FAIL},
 A3_{\rm PARTIAL\ ANALYTIC\ STRUCTURE},A4_{\rm NATURAL\ QUANTIZATION}).
\]
Route B is not authorized because Route A is not ready and no candidate
operator domain or spectral determinant has been constructed.

\section{Conclusion}
The direct cubic Kummer lift does not cross the bridge left by C37.
Functorial operations preserve the exact gauge, so every closed holonomy is
the identity at the matrix level.  The obstruction is reusable well beyond
this model: whenever a proposed nonscalar lift is obtained only by applying
a representation functor to a scalar coboundary, it cannot create periodic
monodromy.  The smallest honest successor must add intrinsic permutation or
projective data and then face a separate all-prime determinant test.
