# Proof Package: Fixed-Discriminant Gauss Reduction

## Claim

Fix a positive nonsquare integer \(\Delta\equiv0,1\pmod4\). Let \(\mathcal R_\Delta\) consist of all

\[
\alpha=\frac{P+\sqrt\Delta}{Q}
\]

such that \(Q>0\) is even,

\[
A=\frac Q2,\qquad B=-P,\qquad
C=\frac{P^2-\Delta}{2Q}
\]

are integers with \(\gcd(A,B,C)=1\), and

\[
\alpha>1,\qquad -1<\bar\alpha=\frac{P-\sqrt\Delta}{Q}<0.
\]

Define \(G(\alpha)=1/(\alpha-\lfloor\alpha\rfloor)\) and \(R(\alpha)=-1/\bar\alpha\). Then:

1. \(\mathcal R_\Delta\) is finite, and \(G\) is a bijection of it.
2. Every state belongs to one primitive cycle. If its minimal cycle has states \(\alpha_j\), digits \(a_j=\lfloor\alpha_j\rfloor\), and length \(\ell\), then
   \[
   M=A(a_0)\cdots A(a_{\ell-1}),
   \qquad A(a)=\begin{pmatrix}a&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
   \]
   fixes \(\alpha_0\), has \(\det M=(-1)^\ell\), and is the primitive positive inverse-branch stabilizer. Up to \(\{\pm I\}\), the determinant-one stabilizer is generated by \(M\) when \(\ell\) is even and by \(M^2\) when \(\ell\) is odd.
3. Writing \(M=\begin{psmallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{psmallmatrix}\), there is a positive integer \(k\) such that
   \[
   (c,d-a,-b)=k(A,B,C),\qquad
   (\operatorname{tr}M)^2-4\det M=k^2\Delta.
   \]
   Moreover \(\lambda=c\alpha_0+d>1\), and
   \[
   \left|(G^\ell)'(\alpha_0)\right|=\lambda^2.
   \]
4. \(R\) is an involution of \(\mathcal R_\Delta\) and \(RGR=G^{-1}\). It therefore pairs cycles with their reversed digit words.
5. If \(c_m\) is the number of cycles of length \(m\), then
   \[
   \#\operatorname{Fix}(G^n)=\sum_{m\mid n}m c_m,
   \quad
   \zeta_{G,\Delta}(z)=\prod_{m\ge1}(1-z^m)^{-c_m},
   \quad
   \det(I-zU_G)=\prod_{m\ge1}(1-z^m)^{c_m},
   \]
   where \(U_Gf=f\circ G\) on \(\ell^2(\mathcal R_\Delta)\).

The claim excludes square, zero, negative, invalid-congruence, and imprimitive discriminant faces exactly as recorded below.

## Assumptions

- \(\Delta>0\), \(\Delta\) is nonsquare, and \(\Delta\equiv0,1\pmod4\).
- Forms are integral and primitive with the displayed \(Q=2A\), \(P=-B\) normalization.
- Reduction means exactly \(\alpha>1\) and \(-1<\bar\alpha<0\).
- The clock is one regular Gauss shift.

## Notation

- \(H_a(x)=a+1/x\) is the inverse branch of \(G\) with matrix \(A(a)\).
- The “positive stabilizer” is the subgroup whose eigenvalue on the ray \((\alpha,1)^T\) is positive.
- \(c_m\) counts source cycles, not rational primes or prime powers.

## Proof Strategy

Use exact radical inequalities to bound the reduced representatives. Prove closure directly from the conjugate inequalities and primitivity under a unimodular substitution. Construct the unique predecessor from the conjugate. Then use inverse-branch matrices, the cyclic stabilizer theorem for a real quadratic irrational, conjugate reciprocity, and elementary permutation determinants.

## Dependency Map

1. Finiteness uses only the two strict reduction inequalities.
2. Integrality and primitivity of the update use the associated primitive form and a determinant-minus-one substitution.
3. Bijectivity uses an explicit unique predecessor.
4. The stabilizer statement uses minimal pure periodicity and the cyclicity of a real-quadratic integral stabilizer.
5. Reversal uses the reversed purely periodic continued fraction, equivalently transposition of the symmetric digit matrices.
6. Fixed counts and determinants use only the complete finite cycle decomposition.

## Proof

### Step 1: the convention really has discriminant \(\Delta\)

The associated polynomial is

\[
Ax^2+Bx+C=\frac Q2x^2-Px+\frac{P^2-\Delta}{2Q}.
\]

Its discriminant is

\[
B^2-4AC=P^2-(P^2-\Delta)=\Delta,
\]

and its two roots are \((P\pm\sqrt\Delta)/Q\). Thus the displayed pair represents a primitive integral form of the claimed discriminant, not of \(4\Delta\).

### Step 2: finiteness

The inequalities \(\bar\alpha<0\) and \(\alpha>1\) imply

\[
P<\sqrt\Delta,\qquad Q<P+\sqrt\Delta.
\]

The inequality \(\bar\alpha>-1\) gives \(P+Q>\sqrt\Delta\), while \(Q>0\) and \(Q<P+\sqrt\Delta\) imply \(P>-\sqrt\Delta\). Hence

\[
|P|<\sqrt\Delta,\qquad 0<Q<2\sqrt\Delta.
\]

Only finitely many integer pairs satisfy these bounds, and the integrality and gcd conditions select a finite subset.

### Step 3: the Gauss update preserves the phase space

Let \(a=\lfloor\alpha\rfloor\ge1\), put

\[
P_1=aQ-P,\qquad Q_1=\frac{\Delta-P_1^2}{Q}.
\]

Rationalizing gives

\[
G(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\alpha-a}
=\frac{P_1+\sqrt\Delta}{Q_1}.
\]

Because \(0<\alpha-a<1\), the new real root is greater than one. Since \(-1<\bar\alpha<0\),

\[
-a-1<\bar\alpha-a<-a,
\]

and therefore

\[
-1<\frac1{\bar\alpha-a}<0.
\]

Thus the new root is reduced. Further, \(P_1^2\equiv P^2\pmod Q\), and, because \(Q\) is even, the difference \(P_1^2-P^2=a^2Q^2-2aPQ\) is divisible by \(2Q\). Consequently \(Q_1\) is a positive even integer and the new third form coefficient is integral. The change \(x\mapsto a+1/x\) is represented by a matrix of determinant \(-1\); the induced integral change of variables preserves the content of the associated form. Primitivity is therefore preserved.

### Step 4: the predecessor is unique

Given a reduced \(\beta\), its conjugate \(\bar\beta\) lies in \((-1,0)\). Set

\[
t=-\frac1{\bar\beta}>1,\qquad a=\lfloor t\rfloor,\qquad
\alpha=a+\frac1\beta.
\]

The number \(t\) is irrational, so \(a-t\in(-1,0)\). Hence

\[
\alpha>1,\qquad \bar\alpha=a+\frac1{\bar\beta}=a-t\in(-1,0).
\]

The inverse unimodular substitution preserves integrality, discriminant, and primitivity, and \(G(\alpha)=\beta\). The condition \(-1<\bar\alpha<0\) determines the integer \(a\) uniquely. Thus every state has exactly one predecessor, so \(G\) is a bijection of the finite set.

### Step 5: primitive cycles and their matrices

Finiteness and bijectivity put every state on a cycle. If \(\ell\) is the least return time, then

\[
\alpha_j=H_{a_j}(\alpha_{j+1}),\qquad
H_a(x)=a+\frac1x,
\]

with indices modulo \(\ell\). Therefore the product \(M=A(a_0)\cdots A(a_{\ell-1})\) fixes \(\alpha_0\). The word is primitive: a shorter digit period would, by uniqueness of an infinite simple continued fraction, give a shorter state return. Since every digit matrix has determinant \(-1\), \(\det M=(-1)^\ell\).

The stabilizer of a real quadratic irrational in \(PGL_2(\mathbb Z)\) maps,
after choosing the representative for which \(c\alpha+d>0\), by
\(\begin{psmallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{psmallmatrix}\mapsto c\alpha+d\)
into the units of its quadratic order that are positive at the
\(\alpha\)-embedding. The logarithm of this image is a nonzero discrete
subgroup of \(\mathbb R\), hence cyclic; its elements with logarithm greater
than zero are the expanding ones.

We use the classical reduction--automorph cross-section lemma in the following
precise form. An expanding positive projective stabilizer preserves the
oriented geodesic from \(\bar\alpha\) to \(\alpha\). Its translation carries a
reduced continued-fraction cross-section hit forward by an integer
\(r\geq 1\) of complete Gauss digits. Consequently its projective matrix is
\[
[A(a_0)A(a_1)\cdots A(a_{r-1})]
\]
and \(G^r(\alpha_0)=\alpha_0\); conversely, every such complete return gives a
positive projective stabilizer. This is the standard
reduction--automorph correspondence recorded in the sources of Uspensky and
Buell listed in SOURCE_AUDIT.md.

Because \(\ell\) is the least state-return time, every such \(r\) is a positive
multiple \(n\ell\), while \(M\) realizes \(r=\ell\). The digit word repeats
with period \(\ell\), so the product for \(r=n\ell\) is \(M^n\). Thus no smaller
stabilizer, including one first written with mixed signs, can precede \(M\):
after passage to its positive projective representative the lemma expresses it
as a complete digit block. Hence \(M\) generates the positive projective
stabilizer. Finally
\[
\det(M^n)=(-1)^{\ell n}.
\]
Therefore the determinant-one stabilizer, up to \(\pm I\), is generated by
\(M\) for even \(\ell\) and by \(M^2\) for odd \(\ell\).

### Step 6: fixed polynomial and multiplier

Write \(M=\begin{psmallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{psmallmatrix}\). The fixed-point equation is

\[
cx^2+(d-a)x-b=0.
\]

It has the same irrational root as the primitive polynomial \(Ax^2+Bx+C\). Therefore its integer coefficient vector is an integer multiple \(k(A,B,C)\); positivity of the digit matrices gives \(k>0\). Taking discriminants yields

\[
(d-a)^2+4bc=(a+d)^2-4(ad-bc)=k^2\Delta.
\]

The eigenvalue of \(M\) on \((\alpha,1)^T\) is \(\lambda=c\alpha+d\). Positive digit matrices and \(\alpha>1\) give \(\lambda>1\). Since

\[
M'(\alpha)=\frac{\det M}{(c\alpha+d)^2}
\]

and \(M\) is the local inverse branch of \(G^\ell\), reciprocal differentiation gives \(|(G^\ell)'(\alpha)|=\lambda^2\).

### Step 7: reversal

For a purely periodic continued fraction

\[
\alpha=[\overline{a_0,a_1,\ldots,a_{\ell-1}}],
\]

transposition of the product of the symmetric matrices \(A(a_j)\) shows that

\[
-\frac1{\bar\alpha}
=[\overline{a_{\ell-1},a_{\ell-2},\ldots,a_0}].
\]

In pair coordinates rationalization gives

\[
R(P,Q)=\left(P,\frac{\Delta-P^2}{Q}\right).
\]

This expression immediately gives \(R^2=1\). Shifting a word and reversing it in the two possible orders gives opposite shifts, so \(RGR=G^{-1}\). Thus reversal pairs complete cycles and reverses their digit words.

### Step 8: fixed counts, zeta, and determinant

A cycle of length \(m\) contributes all its \(m\) states to \(\operatorname{Fix}(G^n)\) exactly when \(m\mid n\), proving the fixed-count formula. The finite Artin--Mazur definition gives

\[
\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\#\operatorname{Fix}(G^n)}n z^n\right)
=\prod_m(1-z^m)^{-c_m}.
\]

On one \(m\)-cycle the Koopman matrix is a cyclic permutation, so its contribution to \(\det(I-zU_G)\) is \(1-z^m\). Multiplying over all cycles proves the last identity. Therefore every part of the claim follows. \(\square\)

## Degenerate and excluded boundaries

- If \(\Delta\) is a positive square, the roots are rational and their regular continued fractions terminate; a periodic reduced-irrational permutation is unavailable.
- At \(\Delta=0\) the polynomial has a repeated rational root.
- For \(\Delta<0\) there is no ordered pair of real conjugates satisfying the reduced chart.
- Integers congruent to \(2\) or \(3\pmod4\) are not discriminants of integral binary quadratic forms.
- Without primitivity, \([2,-2,-2]\) at discriminant \(20\) divides to \([1,-1,-1]\) at discriminant \(5\); the filter is structural.
- Odd \(\ell\) means \(\det M=-1\); the orientation-preserving generator is \(M^2\), not \(M\).
