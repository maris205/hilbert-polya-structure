# Narrative report

P72 ended with a tempting but ambiguous instruction: cancel every essential
singularity.  P74 shows why that instruction does not by itself define a new
determinant.

The singular part is rigid.  Every circle in the ladder belongs to one and
only one scalar channel, so a channel-log multiplier has no way to trade one
coefficient against another.  Its coefficient must be the exact P72
coefficient with the opposite sign to the relative logarithm.  The negative
source boundary is equally rigid and repeats the P71 pair `3/4, 1/2`.

What survives is a holomorphic gauge.  Once the singular pieces have been
removed, any nowhere-zero holomorphic factor may be inserted.  Two natural
primary-factor conventions already give different answers: one annihilates
the channel sector, while the next genus preserves one source coefficient
per channel and leaves an explicit analytic residual.  Both products are
normally convergent and independent of pole ordering.

Thus P74 is a rigidity theorem and a canonicity warning at once.  It proves
what cancellation forces, but it also proves that cancellation alone cannot
own a nontrivial operator or arithmetic determinant.
