\begin{abstract}
The reflection-packet Euler product of the full H\'enon horseshoe has an exact
scalar-channel logarithm, and its locally normalized ratio to the reverse-flip
Lind zeta has one exponential essential singularity on each of infinitely many
complex pole orbits.  We classify all-channel counterterms in a normally
convergent exponential channel class.  With
\(\Phi(x)=2x/(1-2x^2)\) and
\(c_m=m^{-1}\prod_{p\mid m,\ p\text{ odd}}(1-p)\), the relative channel
logarithm is \(-\sum_{m\ge2}c_m\Phi(t^m)\).  A multiplier
\(\exp(\sum_{m\ge2}d_m\Phi(t^m)+G(t))\) can make every channel singularity
meromorphic only if \(d_m=c_m\) coefficient by coefficient.  At the remaining
negative source boundary, nonzero holomorphic extension forces the unique
power--exponential pair \((a,\beta)=(3/4,1/2)\).  These singular coefficients
are rigid, but the renormalization is not absolutely unique: the remaining
counterterms form a torsor under the nowhere-zero holomorphic functions on the
unit disk.  Two normally convergent, pole-order-independent primary products
make this freedom explicit.  Genus \(m-1\) annihilates the channel sector and,
after the forced scalar normalization, trivializes the full relative object;
genus \(m\) preserves the first source monomial and leaves
\(\exp(-2\sum_{m\ge2}c_mt^m)\).  Every finite Taylor-jet normalization still
admits nonconstant gauges.  The result is an analytic classification, not an
operator determinant or an arithmetic trace formula.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The full H\'enon horseshoe is conjugate to a two-shift in the classical
horseshoe regime \cite{DevaneyNitecki1979}.  Retaining marked reflection
packets produces an Euler product different from the full infinite-dihedral
Lind zeta.  The reverse-flip source formula of \citet{KimLeePark2003} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lind-source}
 \zflip(t)=(1-2t^2)^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left(\frac{2t+3t^2}{1-2t^2}\right).
\end{equation}
Earlier exact packet calculations give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packet-source}
 \log\Zorb(t,1)=\sum_{m\ge1}c_m\Phi(t^m),\qquad
 \Phi(x)=\frac{2x}{1-2x^2},
 \quad
 c_m=\frac1m\prod_{\substack{p\mid m\\p\ \text{odd}}}(1-p).
\end{equation}
The first positive boundary can be cancelled uniquely in the relative ratio,
but all later channels survive.  Their poles lie on distinct circles and
accumulate at the unit circle.

The natural next instruction is ``cancel every remaining singularity.''  It
contains two logically separate questions.  First, which singular
coefficients are forced?  Second, after they have been forced, which
holomorphic part remains?  Confusing these questions can turn a valid local
rigidity theorem into an unjustified claim of a canonical determinant.

This paper answers both questions in a declared channel-log class.  The
singular coefficients are completely rigid.  The regular part is a free
nowhere-zero holomorphic gauge.  We also construct two explicit primary
products, both absolutely normally convergent and independent of the ordering
of individual complex poles, that choose different gauges.  One gives a
normalized constant; the other leaves a nonconstant M\"obius--cyclotomic
residual.  Thus all-channel renormalization exists analytically, but
canonicity requires an additional source-native axiom.

The distinction matters for determinant language.  A Fredholm determinant
comes with an operator, a trace ideal, and a fixed normalization
\cite{Simon1977}.  A freely chosen holomorphic gauge supplies none of these.
Our result neither constructs nor rules out an infinite-rank operator; it
isolates what such an operator would still have to select.

\section{The relative continuation and its domains}

Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:u}
 u=1-\sqrt2t.
\end{equation}
The uniquely normalized positive-boundary relative germ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Crel-def}
 \Crel(t)=u^{1/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{3}{4u}\right)
 \frac{\zflip(t)}{\Zorb(t,1)},
\end{equation}
with the power chosen on a local branch.  The exact continuation ledger is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-log}
 \log\Crel(t)=H_{\rm rel}(1-\sqrt2t)
 -\sum_{m\ge2}c_m\Phi(t^m),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 H_{\rm rel}(u)=-\frac12\log(2-u)
 -\frac{3(2u-3)}{4(u-2)}.
\end{equation}
The sign in \cref{eq:relative-log} is fundamental.  Our counterterms are
multipliers of \(\Crel\), so their channel coefficients must enter with the
positive sign.

For \(m\ge2\) and \(0\le k<2m\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poles}
 \rho_m=2^{-1/(2m)},\qquad
 \alpha_{m,k}=\rho_m e^{\pi i k/m}.
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S2}
 S_2=\{\alpha_{m,k}:m\ge2,\ 0\le k<2m\},\qquad
 D_2=\D\setminus S_2.
\end{equation}
The set \(S_2\) is locally finite in \(\D\), because \(\rho_m\nearrow1\).
The logarithmic source term in \cref{eq:H} is single-valued on the additional
slit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slit}
 \D_{\rm slit}=\D\setminus(-1,-2^{-1/2}],
\end{equation}
using the principal branch in \(w=1+\sqrt2t\).  Channel statements live on
\(D_2\); statements involving \(\Crel\) live on
\(D_2\cap\D_{\rm slit}\).  This separation prevents branch choices from
being mistaken for channel monodromy.

\begin{lemma}[Normal convergence of channel logs]\label{lem:channel-normal}
Suppose a sequence \((d_m)_{m\ge2}\) satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:d-growth}
 \sum_{m\ge2}|d_m|r^m<\infty\qquad(0<r<1).
\end{equation}
Then \(\sum_{m\ge2}d_m\Phi(t^m)\) converges normally on compact subsets of
\(D_2\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a compact \(K\subset D_2\), and choose \(r<1\) with \(|t|\le r\) on
\(K\).  For all sufficiently large \(m\),
\(2r^{2m}\le1/2\), and hence
\[
 |\Phi(t^m)|\le \frac{2r^m}{1-2r^{2m}}\le4r^m.
\]
The corresponding tail is controlled by \cref{eq:d-growth}.  Only finitely
many earlier rational functions remain, and their denominators are bounded
away from zero on \(K\).  The Weierstrass theorem then gives normal
convergence and holomorphy \cite{Conway1978,Remmert1991}.
\end{proof}

For the source sequence, \(|c_m|\le1\): the absolute numerator is at most
the odd radical of \(m\), hence at most \(m\).  Thus \cref{eq:d-growth}
holds for \(d_m=c_m\).

\section{The exact complex principal parts}

The positive P72 ladder is only one point from each complete pole orbit.  We
need the complete orbit to discuss order-independent pole products.

\begin{lemma}[Weighted partial fractions]\label{lem:partial}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bmk}
 b_{m,k}=\frac{c_m(-1)^k}{\sqrt2\,m}.
\end{equation}
Then, as rational functions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partial}
 c_m\Phi(t^m)=\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}
 \frac{b_{m,k}}{1-t/\alpha_{m,k}}.
\end{equation}
In particular, the principal coefficient at \(\alpha_{m,k}\) in the
relative channel logarithm is \(-b_{m,k}\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write \(x=t/\rho_m\) and \(\zeta=e^{\pi i/m}\).  For \(|x|<1\), expand
the right side of \cref{eq:partial} into a geometric series.  The coefficient
at degree \(j\) contains
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filter}
 \sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}(-1)^k\zeta^{-kj}
 =\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}e^{\pi i k(m-j)/m}
 =\begin{cases}
 2m,&j\equiv m\pmod{2m},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Since \(\rho_m^m=2^{-1/2}\), the surviving series is
\[
 2c_mt^m+4c_mt^{3m}+8c_mt^{5m}+\cdots
 =\frac{2c_mt^m}{1-2t^{2m}}.
\]
Equality in a neighborhood of zero proves the rational identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Unique ownership of each pole]\label{lem:ownership}
If \(\alpha_{m,k}\) is a pole of channel \(j\), then \(j=m\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The modulus of every pole in channel \(j\) is \(\rho_j\).  The map
\(j\mapsto2^{-1/(2j)}\) is strictly increasing, so
\(\rho_j=\rho_m\) implies \(j=m\).
\end{proof}

This elementary radial separation is the source of coefficient rigidity.
It also rules out a cancellation between arithmetically unrelated channel
indices.

\section{Coefficientwise rigidity}

Let \(G\in\Ohol(\D)\), and let \((d_m)\) satisfy
\cref{eq:d-growth}.  Define the multiplier
\begin{equation}\label{eq:W}
 W_{d,G}(t)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{m\ge2}d_m\Phi(t^m)+G(t)\right)
 \qquad(t\in D_2).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Channel coefficient rigidity]\label{thm:rigidity}
Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:combined-channel}
 W_{d,G}(t)\exp\!\left(-\sum_{m\ge2}c_m\Phi(t^m)\right)
\end{equation}
extends meromorphically across every point of \(S_2\).  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:d=c}
 d_m=c_m\qquad(m\ge2).
\end{equation}
Consequently \cref{eq:combined-channel} extends holomorphically and nowhere
zero to \(\D\), where it equals \(e^{G(t)}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(m\ge2\) and \(0\le k<2m\).  By
\cref{lem:channel-normal,lem:ownership}, every channel with index different
from \(m\) is holomorphic near \(\alpha_{m,k}\), including the normally
convergent infinite tail.  By \cref{lem:partial}, the logarithm of
\cref{eq:combined-channel} has principal part
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rigidity-principal}
 \frac{(d_m-c_m)(-1)^k}
 {\sqrt2\,m(1-t/\alpha_{m,k})}.
\end{equation}
If \(d_m-c_m\ne0\), exponentiation produces an essential singularity.  It
cannot be meromorphic.  Hence \(d_m=c_m\).  The pole orbit was arbitrary,
so this holds for every \(m\ge2\).  Substitution leaves \(e^G\), proving the
last assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why meromorphic extension is enough]
The theorem does not assume a nonzero holomorphic extension in order to force
\cref{eq:d=c}.  A nonzero log pole exponentiates to an essential singularity,
so even meromorphic continuation is impossible.  The conclusion becomes
nonzero holomorphic because the declared gauge is an exponential of a disk
holomorphic function.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the class]
The theorem classifies the channel-log multipliers in \cref{eq:W}.  One may
write more exotic slit-dependent expressions, add algebraic zero factors, or
allow counterterms without a global logarithm.  Those are extra
regularization data and are not silently included in the uniqueness claim.
\end{remark}

\section{The negative source boundary}

The positive source boundary was already removed in the definition of
\(\Crel\).  The negative point \(t=-2^{-1/2}\) survives.  Its exact ledger
is particularly simple.

\begin{proposition}[Exact negative-source ledger]\label{prop:negative-ledger}
Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:w}
 w=1+\sqrt2t=2-u.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-w}
 H_{\rm rel}(2-w)=\frac{3}{4w}-\frac12\log w-\frac32.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitute \(u=2-w\) in \cref{eq:H}.  Since
\(2-u=w\), \(u-2=-w\), and \(2u-3=1-2w\), one obtains
\[
 -\frac12\log w-
 \frac{3(1-2w)}{-4w}
 =\frac{3}{4w}-\frac12\log w-\frac32.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Negative source-pair rigidity]\label{thm:source-rigidity}
Choose a local slit at \(w=0\).  Let \(a,\beta\in\mathbb C\).  After the
channel coefficients have been cancelled, the multiplier
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-factor}
 w^\beta\exp(-a/w)
\end{equation}
makes the relative object extend holomorphically and nonvanishingly across
\(w=0\) if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forced-source}
 (a,\beta)=\left(\frac34,\frac12\right).
\end{equation}
For this pair the source contribution is \(e^{-3/2}\).  Including the
holomorphic channel gauge, the exact total residual is
\(e^{-3/2}e^{G(t)}=e^{-3/2}A(t)\), where \(A(t):=e^{G(t)}\).  In the
distinguished \(G=0\) gauge this reduces to the constant \(e^{-3/2}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After channel cancellation, the relevant logarithm is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-combined}
 \frac{3/4-a}{w}+(\beta-1/2)\log w-\frac32+G(t).
\end{equation}
A nonzero coefficient of \(1/w\) yields an exponential essential
singularity, forcing \(a=3/4\).  The remaining factor
\(w^{\beta-1/2}\) has a nonzero holomorphic limit only when its exponent is
zero, forcing \(\beta=1/2\).  Conversely these values reduce
\cref{eq:source-combined} to \(-3/2+G(t)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Nonvanishing is used]
If the target were merely a meromorphic function allowed to vanish or have a
pole at \(w=0\), the conclusion would weaken to
\(a=3/4\) and \(\beta-1/2\in\mathbb Z\).  The exact pair
\cref{eq:forced-source} belongs to the stated nonzero extension problem.
\end{remark}

\section{The holomorphic gauge torsor}

Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Rch}
 R_{\rm ch}(t)=\exp\!\left(-\sum_{m\ge2}c_m\Phi(t^m)\right).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Gauge-torsor classification]\label{thm:gauge}
The channel-log multipliers \(W\) for which \(WR_{\rm ch}\) extends
holomorphically and nowhere zero to \(\D\) form a torsor under
\(\Ohol(\D)^\times\).  More precisely, they are exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge-form}
 W(t)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{m\ge2}c_m\Phi(t^m)\right)A(t),
 \qquad A\in\Ohol(\D)^\times,
\end{equation}
and \(WR_{\rm ch}=A\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Necessity of the singular series follows from \cref{thm:rigidity}.  Define
\[
 A=W\exp\!\left(-\sum_{m\ge2}c_m\Phi(t^m)\right).
\]
This is precisely the extended product \(WR_{\rm ch}\), hence belongs to
\(\Ohol(\D)^\times\).  Conversely every
such \(A\) yields a valid multiplier.  Since the disk is simply connected,
every nowhere-zero holomorphic function on it has a holomorphic logarithm
\cite{Conway1978}; therefore \(A=e^G\) for some \(G\in\Ohol(\D)\), and the
description agrees exactly with \cref{eq:W}.  Multiplication by \(A\) acts
freely and transitively.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Source-normalized form]\label{cor:full-gauge}
Insert the forced source factor \(w^{1/2}e^{-3/(4w)}\).  Every fully
removable relative object in the class then
has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-gauge}
 e^{-3/2}A(t),\qquad A\in\Ohol(\D)^\times.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

The singular data therefore determine a torsor, not a distinguished point of
that torsor.  An operator model could distinguish a point, but cancellation
alone cannot.

\section{Two primary products and the residual sign}

We now construct two explicit points of the torsor.  Besides proving
existence, they make the nonuniqueness visible without inserting an arbitrary
function by hand.

For \(z=t/\alpha_{m,k}\) and an integer \(g\ge0\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:primary}
 \PP_{m,k}^{[g]}(t)
 =\exp\!\left(
 b_{m,k}\left[\frac1{1-z}-\sum_{j=0}^{g}z^j\right]\right)
 =\exp\!\left(\frac{b_{m,k}z^{g+1}}{1-z}\right).
\end{equation}
Its logarithm has principal part
\(b_{m,k}/(1-t/\alpha_{m,k})\), the positive sign needed to cancel the
relative coefficient \(-b_{m,k}\).

\begin{lemma}[Normal convergence of the primary products]\label{lem:primary-normal}
Both double products
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-products}
 \prod_{m\ge2}\prod_{k=0}^{2m-1}\PP_{m,k}^{[m-1]}(t),
 \qquad
 \prod_{m\ge2}\prod_{k=0}^{2m-1}\PP_{m,k}^{[m]}(t)
\end{equation}
converge through absolutely normally convergent logarithms on compact subsets
of \(D_2\).  Their values are independent of every ordering of the individual
pole factors.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(K\subset D_2\) be compact and choose \(r<q<1\) with
\(|t|\le r\) on \(K\).  Since \(\rho_m\to1\), for all sufficiently large
\(m\) and every \(k\),
\(|t/\alpha_{m,k}|\le q\).  Using \(|c_m|\le1\),
\begin{align}
 \sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}|\log\PP_{m,k}^{[m-1]}(t)|
 &\le 2m\frac{1}{\sqrt2m}\frac{q^m}{1-q}
 =\frac{\sqrt2q^m}{1-q},\label{eq:majorant-minus}\\
 \sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}|\log\PP_{m,k}^{[m]}(t)|
 &\le\frac{\sqrt2q^{m+1}}{1-q}.
 \label{eq:majorant-plus}
\end{align}
Both majorants are summable in \(m\).  The finitely many earlier factors are
holomorphic on \(K\).  Absolute normal convergence of the logarithms gives
nonzero holomorphic products and unconditional, hence arbitrary-order,
convergence \cite{Remmert1991}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Genus \(m-1\): annihilating gauge]\label{thm:genus-minus}
The first product in \cref{eq:two-products} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wminus}
 W_-(t)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{m\ge2}c_m\Phi(t^m)\right).
\end{equation}
Hence \(W_-R_{\rm ch}=1\).  After the forced source factor, the result is
\(e^{-3/2}\); imposing final value one multiplies by the forced scalar
\(e^{3/2}\) and gives the identity function.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The compensating polynomial in \cref{eq:primary} contains degrees
\(0,\ldots,m-1\).  Every corresponding weighted root sum in
\cref{eq:filter} vanishes.  Thus for each \(m\),
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}\log\PP_{m,k}^{[m-1]}(t)
 =c_m\Phi(t^m).
\]
Normal convergence permits summation over \(m\), giving
\cref{eq:Wminus}.  The remaining assertions follow from
\cref{eq:Rch,eq:H-w}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Genus \(m\): source-preserving gauge]\label{thm:genus-source}
The second product in \cref{eq:two-products} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wplus}
 W_+(t)=\exp\!\left(
 \sum_{m\ge2}c_m\Phi(t^m)-2\sum_{m\ge2}c_mt^m
 \right).
\end{equation}
Consequently its channel residual is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
 W_+(t)R_{\rm ch}(t)
 =\exp\!\left(-2\sum_{m\ge2}c_mt^m\right).
\end{equation}
The minus sign in \cref{eq:residual} is forced by treating \(W_+\) as a
multiplier of the negative relative channel logarithm.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The genus-\(m\) compensating polynomial adds degree \(m\) to the terms that
were present in the preceding proof.  By \cref{eq:filter},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-retained}
 \sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}b_{m,k}
 \left(\frac{t}{\alpha_{m,k}}\right)^m=2c_mt^m.
\end{equation}
It is subtracted in \cref{eq:primary}.  Therefore
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}\log\PP_{m,k}^{[m]}(t)
 =c_m\Phi(t^m)-2c_mt^m.
\]
Sum over \(m\) and use \cref{eq:Rch}.
\end{proof}

The residual has a useful M\"obius product description.

\begin{proposition}[M\"obius--cyclotomic residual]\label{prop:mobius-product}
For \(|t|<1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mobius-product}
 \exp\!\left(-2\sum_{m\ge2}c_mt^m\right)
 =e^{2t}\prod_{\substack{d\ge1\\d\ \text{odd}}}
 (1-t^d)^{2\mu(d)},
\end{equation}
where the product is defined by its normally convergent logarithm.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Using
\(c_m=m^{-1}\sum_{d\mid m,\ d\ \text{odd}}d\mu(d)\), absolute convergence
in \(|t|<1\) permits the regrouping
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{m\ge1}c_mt^m
 &=\sum_{\substack{d\ge1\\d\ \text{odd}}}\mu(d)
   \sum_{r\ge1}\frac{t^{dr}}r\\
 &=-\sum_{\substack{d\ge1\\d\ \text{odd}}}\mu(d)\log(1-t^d).
\end{align*}
Since \(c_1=1\), separating \(m=1\), multiplying by \(-2\), and
exponentiating gives \cref{eq:mobius-product}.
\end{proof}

The two products are equally successful at singularity cancellation and
order independence, yet they differ by the nonconstant holomorphic gauge in
\cref{eq:residual}.  This is an explicit obstruction to claiming absolute
canonicity from those two properties alone.

\section{Finite jets and monodromy}

One might try to fix the gauge by prescribing the value and several
derivatives at the origin.  No finite number suffices.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-jet nonuniqueness]\label{thm:finite-jet}
Fix \(N\ge0\), \(t_0\in\D\), and an admissible multiplier \(W\).  For every
\(\lambda\in\mathbb C\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jet-gauge}
 W_\lambda(t)=W(t)\exp\!\left(\lambda(t-t_0)^{N+1}\right)
\end{equation}
has the same cancellation data as \(W\), and its residual agrees with that
of \(W\) through Taylor order \(N\) at \(t_0\).  When \(\lambda\ne0\), the
gauge quotient is nonconstant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The added exponential is entire and nowhere zero, so it changes no principal
part or removability statement.  Its Taylor expansion is
\(1+\lambda(t-t_0)^{N+1}+O((t-t_0)^{2N+2})\).  Thus all derivatives through
order \(N\) agree, while the derivative at order \(N+1\) changes when
\(\lambda\ne0\).
\end{proof}

An infinite jet at one point would fix a holomorphic gauge by the identity
theorem, but that is equivalent to prescribing the entire germ rather than
deriving it from the singularities.

The primary factors in \cref{eq:primary} are exponentials of single-valued
rational functions on \(D_2\).  They therefore have trivial monodromy.  On a
slit neighborhood of one puncture one can append
\(\lambda\log(1-t/\alpha_{m,k})\).  Its exponential glues to the punctured
disk only for integral \(\lambda\); it then introduces a zero or pole unless
\(\lambda=0\).  Hence nontrivial slit monodromy is additional data, and the
requirement of a nowhere-zero globally exponential counterterm removes it.

\section{What the classification does not construct}

The genus-\(m-1\) convention gives a striking normalized identity, but it
must be interpreted correctly.  It says that a counterterm carrying the
entire channel logarithm can cancel that logarithm.  This is a valid analytic
construction and an important order-independence theorem.  It does not turn
the identity function into a new spectral invariant.

The genus-\(m\) convention keeps a nontrivial function, but its nontriviality
does not by itself make it canonical.  The torsor theorem supplies infinitely
many other residuals, all with the same singular cancellation and any fixed
finite jet.  To promote one to a determinant requires independent data such
as:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item a specified operator or complex;
 \item a trace ideal or a proved regularized-trace construction;
 \item a normalization functorial under the relevant dynamics; and
 \item a theorem identifying its determinant with the selected gauge.
\end{enumerate}
None is supplied here.  In particular, the integer channel label \(m\) has
not acquired rational-prime or von-Mangoldt semantics.  The theorem does not
produce a self-adjoint spectrum, a functional equation, or an explicit
formula.  It also does not rule out a future infinite-rank construction that
selects one gauge for source-native reasons.

\section{Executable certificate}

The accompanying exact-arithmetic package performs four independent types
of finite verification.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item It replays
 \(c_m=m^{-1}\sum_{d\mid m,\ d\ \text{odd}}d\mu(d)\) against the odd-radical
 Euler product.
 \item It checks the root filter \cref{eq:filter}, both primary residuals, and
 the product formula \cref{eq:mobius-product} through degree (96) using
 rational arithmetic.
 \item It constructs explicit finite-jet witnesses through order (12), and
 an independent program reconstructs all coefficient rows without importing
 the producer.

\section{A general exponential-removability lemma}

The channel proof used only a simple special case of the following local
fact.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:general-exp}
Let \(F\) be single-valued and holomorphic on a punctured disk.  If \(e^F\)
extends meromorphically across the puncture, then its zero or pole order is
zero.  If the extension is finite there, then it is nonzero and \(F\) extends
holomorphically after addition of a constant in \(2\pi i\mathbb Z\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write the meromorphic extension as \(z^nU(z)\), where \(n\in\mathbb Z\) and
\(U(0)\ne0\).  On the punctured disk,
\[
 F'(z)=\frac nz+\frac{U'(z)}{U(z)}.
\]
The residue of the derivative of a single-valued Laurent series is zero, so
\(n=0\).  A local holomorphic logarithm \(L\) of \(U\) then exists.  Since
\(e^{F-L}=1\), the holomorphic function \(F-L\) takes values in the discrete
set \(2\pi i\mathbb Z\), hence is constant.  Thus (F) extends.
\end{proof}

This lemma explains why globally exponential counterterms cannot create
hidden zeros at the removed channel points.  Algebraic zero factors require a
counterterm without a single-valued global logarithm and hence lie outside the
declared class.
