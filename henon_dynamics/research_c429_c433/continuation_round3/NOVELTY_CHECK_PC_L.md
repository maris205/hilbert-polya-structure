## Proposed theorem and three claims

For every odd prime p, every c in the algebraic closure of F_p, and
f(x)=x²+c, a polynomial has zero sum on every ordinary primitive f-cycle
if and only if it is Q(f(x))−Q(x) for a polynomial Q. The native one-step
clock and cycles of length divisible by p are retained. The proof uses
two consecutive periodic algebras and a particular stable coefficient;
it does not assume reduced periodic schemes or generic parameters.

## Closest primary sources

**Kalinin, Livšic theorem for matrix cocycles.** The coordinator read
the abstract, introduction and Theorem 1.1 in the
[primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/0808.0350v2). Its base is a
topologically transitive homeomorphism of a compact metric space with
the closing property, and its cocycle takes Hölder values in GL(m,R).
The conclusion is a Hölder transfer. Neither polynomial regularity over
an algebraic closure of a finite field nor applicability to every
quadratic parameter follows by specialization. The Livšic problem
template is classical and must be acknowledged.

**Veech, Periodic points and invariant pseudomeasures for toral
endomorphisms (1986).** The coordinator verified the
[publisher abstract and bibliographic record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/ergodic-theory-and-dynamical-systems/article/periodic-points-and-invariant-pseudomeasures-for-toral-endomorphisms/5AF4A715A07BF8E0AC967645A282DA24).
It gives a smooth coboundary result for ergodic toral endomorphisms and
also records a lower-regularity failure. This check is abstract-level,
not full-proof access. Toral smooth regularity is not the target's
arbitrary-parameter positive-characteristic polynomial conclusion.

**Cattani–Dickenstein–Sturmfels, Computing Multidimensional Residues.**
The coordinator read §4, Lemma 4.2, Theorem 4.3 and Algorithm 4.8, and
Remark 1.6(iii), in the [primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/alg-geom/9404011).
Monic pure-power initial forms yield the corresponding monomial basis;
residues recover normal-form coefficients and the Jacobian supplies a
trace formula. The cyclic quadratic leading ideal has this structure.
The paper initially works over subfields of C and discusses extensions
to other fields; this is not blanket permission to specialize every
analytic argument. All this machinery is subtracted. The inspected
statements do not establish the specified stabilization as the number
of cyclic variables changes.

**Cvitanović–Hansen–Rolf–Vattay, Beyond the periodic orbit theory (1998).**
The coordinator read relevant §§2–4, especially equations (25)–(28),
in the [author-hosted primary text](https://cns.gatech.edu/~predrag/papers/contourNonl.pdf).
The source already relates adjacent quadratic multiplier-weighted
traces by finite matrices with binomial entries. Thus neither
“cross-period comparison” nor “finite binomial transfer” alone is a
new method. Its inspected complex residue identities do not state the
specific short-support stabilization or ordinary additive polynomial
coboundary test. A direct implication to the proposed theorem would
require an additional argument, not a change of vocabulary.

**Levin–Sodin–Yuditskii, Ruelle operators with rational weights for
Julia sets (1994).** The 1998 paper explicitly credits this antecedent
for its finite matrices. X2 obtained the
[publisher abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF03008428),
but not full theorem text. The matrix formulas are therefore cited
through the actually inspected 1998 source, with the 1994 priority
credited and the original-text access limitation retained. No statement
that the original paper cannot imply the present result is warranted.
