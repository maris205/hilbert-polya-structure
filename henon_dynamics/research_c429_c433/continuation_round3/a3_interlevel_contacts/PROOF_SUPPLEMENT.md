# An interlevel contact-degree criterion and a discriminant-only pair proof

2026-09-09 UTC. New proof-only work. All round-2 certificate and review files remain unchanged.

## Claims and status

Let $p$ be an odd prime, $k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p$, $K=k((s))$, $R=k[[s]]$, $v(s)=1$, and $P=P_s=(1+s)z+z^2$. For every integer $e\ge1$, let $M_e$ denote the accepted canonical degree-$p^e$ small-cycle factor and put $r=(p-1)/p$.

**Theorem A (contact-degree criterion).** Let $e\ge2$, let $\alpha$ be a root of $M_e$, and let $\beta$ be a root of $M_1$. Fix an integer $\nu$ with $2\le\nu\le e$. If $d=v(\alpha-\beta)$ satisfies

$$
p^\nu d\in\mathbb Z\setminus p\mathbb Z,
\qquad
d<2r-\frac{p-1}{p^\nu},
\tag{1}
$$

then $[K(\alpha):K]\ge p^\nu$.

**Theorem B (bounded discriminant-only control).** At $(p,e)=(3,2)$, suppose that the canonical small factor has polynomial discriminant valuation $144$. Then $M_2$ is irreducible of degree $9$ over $K$, without assuming or using the value or nonvanishing of its level-two AS class.

**Theorem C (uniform second-layer degree).** For every odd prime $p$, every $e\ge2$, every root $\alpha$ of $M_e$, and every root $\beta$ of $M_1$,

$$
v(\alpha-\beta)=\frac{2(p-1)^2}{p^2},
\qquad [K(\alpha):K]\ge p^2.
\tag{C}
$$

In particular, $M_2$ is irreducible of degree $p^2$ for every odd prime $p$. This theorem has **no discriminant-computation or higher-level AS premise**.

**Status: PROVABLE AS STATED from the accepted small-cycle/prime-level inputs and, for the separate Theorem B only, the accepted discriminant certificate.** Theorems A--C are author proofs awaiting independent review. Theorem C makes Theorem B unnecessary for the pair conclusion; its separate proof is retained to expose the original discriminant-only control and check the same contact by another derivation.

The actual first-level contact in (C) also shows that condition (1) with $\nu=e$ fails for every $e\ge3$: its denominator remains exactly $p^2$. Thus that particular uniform first-level-contact route is refuted, not silently assumed; the original all-level irreducibility question remains open.

## Assumptions and dependencies

The accepted dynamical inputs are:

1. Each $M_j$ is monic, finite étale over $K$, and has exactly one native orbit of $p^j$ roots, all of valuation $r$.
2. For a root $\gamma$ of $M_j$, $K(\gamma)$ is the cyclic splitting field of that orbit. If its degree is $p^h$, its actual Galois subgroup acts by the rotations generated by $P^{\circ p^{j-h}}$, with $1\le h\le j$.
3. The first-level field $F=K(\beta)$ has degree $p$ and unique lower/upper break $p-1$.
4. In the formal germ at $(s,z)=(0,0)$, the first dynatomic quotient has the factorization $Q_1=M_1V_1$, where $V_1(0,0)\ne0$. Thus

$$
P^{\circ p}(z)-z=z(z+s)M_1(z)V_1(z).
\tag{2}
$$

The first three inputs do not assume full degree at higher levels. The fourth is the accepted coprime Hensel factorization, with the factor $P(z)-z=z(z+s)$ restored exactly. No AS value at level $2$ or higher is a premise.

The proof proceeds through a native displacement bound, a prime-valuation ramification lemma, a compositum contradiction, and then an exact distance calculation for Theorem B. Quotient compatibility and the first lower/upper break conventions are reviewed in Elder--Keating, [Section 2 before Lemma 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.16830v1). Their setup permits arbitrary perfect residue fields, as required here. The elementary displacement arguments are included below.

## 1. Native displacements and proper rotation subgroups

All small-orbit points have valuation $r$. For two such points $x,y$,

$$
P(x)-P(y)=(x-y)(1+s+x+y).
\tag{3}
$$

The factor is a unit, and its difference from $1$ has valuation at least $r$. Iteration gives, for every nonnegative integer $a$,

$$
v(P^{\circ a}(x)-P^{\circ a}(y))=v(x-y),\qquad
\frac{P^{\circ a}(x)-P^{\circ a}(y)}{x-y}
\in1+\{u:v(u)\ge r\}.
\tag{4}
$$

The second assertion follows by multiplying the $a$ factors from (3); products of elements in the displayed set remain in that set.

For any small periodic point $x$, the two terms in $P(x)-x=sx+x^2$ have different valuations $1+r>2r$, so

$$
v(P(x)-x)=2r.
\tag{5}
$$

If $a>0$ is prime to $p$, the telescoping sum for $P^{\circ a}(x)-x$, divided by $P(x)-x$, has residue $a\ne0$ by (4). Hence its valuation is $2r$. For $a=p$, the same sum of $p$ terms cancels its constant part in characteristic $p$, and every remaining term has gained at least $r$:

$$
v(P^{\circ p}(x)-x)\ge3r.
\tag{6}
$$

The inequality includes $+\infty$ when the difference is zero. A displacement by any positive multiple of $p$ is a sum of $p$-step displacements, all of the same valuation by (4), and therefore also has valuation at least $3r$.

It follows in particular that if $L=K(\alpha)$ has degree $p^h$ with $h<e$, every nonidentity automorphism of $L/K$ moves $\alpha$ by valuation at least $3r>2r$. Its native rotation index is divisible by $p$, by input 2. By contrast, every nonidentity automorphism of the prime-level field $F/K$ moves $\beta$ by valuation exactly $2r$, by (5) and the prime-to-$p$ telescoping argument.

## 2. Prime-valuation ramification lemma

**Lemma.** Let $B/K$ be a finite totally ramified Galois $p$-extension with residue field $k$. Normalize $v_B$ to have value group $\mathbb Z$. Let $x\in B$ satisfy $a=v_B(x)>0$ and $p\nmid a$. For a nonidentity $\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(B/K)$, put $b_\tau=v_B(\tau(t)-t)-1$, where $t$ is a uniformizer. Then

$$
v_B(\tau x-x)=a+b_\tau.
\tag{7}
$$

**Proof.** The field is complete, has fixed coefficient field $k$, and has residue field $k$, so $O_B=k[[t]]$. Since the automorphism has $p$-power order, its leading multiplier on $t$ is a $p$-power root of unity in $k^\times$, hence is $1$. Consequently $b_\tau\ge1$, and

$$
\tau(t)=t(1+u),\qquad v_B(u)=b_\tau.
$$

For every integer $j>0$, write $j=p^v j_0$ with $p\nmid j_0$. The identity $(1+u)^j-1=(1+u^{p^v})^{j_0}-1$ gives

$$
v_B(\tau(t^j)-t^j)=j+p^{v_p(j)}b_\tau.
$$

Expand $x=c_at^a+\sum_{j>a}c_jt^j$, $c_a\ne0$. Its leading term has difference order $a+b_\tau$ because $p\nmid a$. Every later term has difference order at least $j+b_\tau>a+b_\tau$. Those bounds tend to infinity, so the entire later tail has strictly greater order and cannot cancel the leading coefficient. This proves (7). $\square$

In particular, the first lower break of $B/K$ is

$$
\min_{\tau\ne1}\{v_B(\tau x-x)-v_B(x)\}.
\tag{8}
$$

It equals the first upper break. Any nontrivial Galois quotient has first upper break at least that of $B/K$: up to the latter break the full upper ramification group is still the whole group, and its image in the quotient is whole by Herbrand's quotient compatibility.

## 3. Proof of Theorem A

Write $L=K(\alpha)$ and $[L:K]=p^h$. Put $F=K(\beta)$ and $B=LF$. These are finite totally ramified extensions: their residue fields are finite extensions of the algebraically closed field $k$, hence equal $k$. Their Galois groups are $p$-groups; $L/K$ and $F/K$ are cyclic by the accepted inputs. The compositum has

$$
[B:K]\le p^{h+1}.
\tag{9}
$$

The first condition in (1) says that $d=v(\alpha-\beta)$ has exact denominator $p^\nu$. Because $\alpha-\beta\in B$, its denominator divides the ramification index $[B:K]$. Thus (9) implies $h\ge\nu-1$.

Suppose for contradiction that $h=\nu-1$. Then the denominator condition and (9) force $[B:K]=p^\nu=p[L:K]$, so $L\cap F=K$ and

$$
\operatorname{Gal}(B/K)\cong C_{p^{\nu-1}}\times C_p.
\tag{10}
$$

Since $\nu\le e$, we have $h<e$. Section 1 therefore shows that any automorphism moving $\alpha$ moves it by valuation at least $3r$, whereas any automorphism moving $\beta$ moves it by valuation exactly $2r$.

Let $\eta=\alpha-\beta$. The two fields are linearly disjoint, so the restrictions to $L$ and $F$ in (10) can be chosen independently. Every automorphism whose restriction to $F$ is nontrivial satisfies

$$
v(\tau\eta-\eta)=
v\bigl((\tau\alpha-\alpha)-(\tau\beta-\beta)\bigr)=2r,
\tag{11}
$$

because the two displayed contributions have different valuations, or the first is zero. An automorphism acting trivially on $F$ and nontrivially on $L$ has $\eta$-displacement valuation at least $3r$. Thus the minimum of all nonidentity displacements in (11) is exactly $2r$.

Normalize $v_B=p^\nu v$. We have $v_B(\eta)=p^\nu d>0$, prime to $p$ by (1). Apply (8) to obtain the first lower/upper break

$$
b_B=p^\nu(2r-d)>p-1.
\tag{12}
$$

But $F/K$ is a Galois quotient of $B/K$ with first upper break $p-1$, contradicting the quotient inequality after (8). The case $h=\nu-1$ is impossible. Combined with $h\ge\nu-1$, this gives $h\ge\nu$ and proves Theorem A. $\square$

The exact-denominator condition is indispensable to this proof: without it, the integer-normalized value of $\eta$ may be divisible by $p$, and (7) need not hold.

## 4. The pair's three-step distance from the total discriminant

Now set $p=3$, $e=2$ and $r=2/3$. No irreducibility of $M_2$ is assumed. Its nine roots form one native cycle, so Section 1 gives valuation $4/3$ for the six differences with rotation indices $1,2,4,5,7,8$. The differences at indices $3$ and $6$ have a common valuation $D$: after dividing their telescoping relation by the three-step displacement, the two summands have residue $1$, so their sum has nonzero residue $2$.

The derivative $M_2'(\alpha)$ is the product of these eight root differences. By (4), all nine roots have the same derivative valuation, even if they are not all Galois conjugate. The total polynomial discriminant certificate therefore implies

$$
144=9\bigl(6\cdot(4/3)+2D\bigr),\qquad D=4.
\tag{13}
$$

This argument uses neither a field different nor the level-two AS class. It is valid for a disconnected finite étale cyclic-nine torsor.

## 5. Exact cross-contact with the period-three cycle

Let $\beta_0,\beta_1,\beta_2$ be the roots of $M_1$. Their pairwise differences have valuation $4/3$, by Section 1. The formal factorization (2) is, at this prime,

$$
P^{\circ3}(z)-z=z(z+s)M_1(z)V_1(z),
\qquad V_1(0,0)\ne0.
\tag{14}
$$

It is an exact identity in $R[z]$: the quotient defining $Q_1$ is polynomial; its coprime Hensel factors are $M_1,V_1$; and $P(z)-z=z(z+s)$. Since $v(\alpha)=2/3>0$, evaluating the complementary factor gives a unit. Also $v(\alpha+s)=2/3$, as the two terms have different valuations. Equation (13), evaluated in (14), gives

$$
\sum_{i=0}^2v(\alpha-\beta_i)=4-\frac23-\frac23=\frac83.
\tag{15}
$$

All three contacts are finite because the ordinary periods $9$ and $3$ differ. If all three were at least $4/3$, their sum would be at least $4$, contrary to (15). Choose one contact below $4/3$. The strong triangle inequality and the fact that every $v(\beta_i-\beta_j)=4/3$ force the other two contacts to equal that smaller value. Hence (15) yields

$$
v(\alpha-\beta_i)=\frac89\qquad(i=0,1,2).
\tag{16}
$$

The numerator $8$ is prime to $3$, and

$$
\frac89<\frac43-\frac{2}{9}=\frac{10}{9}.
$$

Thus (1) holds with $\nu=2$. Theorem A gives $[K(\alpha):K]\ge9$. The accepted orbit bound gives degree at most $9$, so the degree is exactly $9$, and $M_2$ is irreducible. This proves Theorem B. $\square$

For clarity, the contrary degree-$3$ alternative would produce $B/K$ of degree $9$, $v_B(\eta)=8$, and minimum nonidentity $\eta$-displacement $12$. Formula (8) would force first break $4$, while its period-three quotient has break $2$. This is the contradiction, not a previously known AS conclusion inserted into the proof.

## 6. Uniform exact contact from the prime-level multiplier

This section proves Theorem C from accepted small-cycle and prime-level inputs alone. Neither (13) nor the level-two AS result is used.

Let $\beta$ be any root of $M_1$, and put

$$
\mu=(P^{\circ p})'(\beta).
$$

Differentiate the exact factorization (2) and evaluate at $\beta$. Since $M_1(\beta)=0$, only the derivative of $M_1$ contributes:

$$
\mu-1=\beta(\beta+s)M_1'(\beta)V_1(\beta).
\tag{18}
$$

The complementary factor is a unit. The two initial factors have valuation $r$ each. Each of the $p-1$ differences in the derivative product for $M_1'(\beta)$ has valuation $2r$, because the first-level roots differ by prime-to-$p$ native steps. Hence (18) gives the finite exact valuation

$$
v(\mu-1)=2r+(p-1)2r=2(p-1).
\tag{19}
$$

In particular, $\mu-1\ne0$.

Fix $e\ge2$, and write $n=p^e$, $m=p^{e-1}$. Both iterate differences $P^{\circ n}(z)-z$ and $P^{\circ m}(z)-z$ vanish at $\beta$. Differentiate the polynomial identity

$$
P^{\circ n}(z)-z=(P^{\circ m}(z)-z)Q_e(z)
$$

and then evaluate at $\beta$. Chain rule along the period-$p$ orbit gives

$$
Q_e(\beta)=
\frac{\mu^{p^{e-1}}-1}{\mu^{p^{e-2}}-1}
=(\mu-1)^{p^{e-1}-p^{e-2}}.
\tag{20}
$$

The denominator is nonzero by (19); the last equality is the characteristic-$p$ identity $x^{p^a}-1=(x-1)^{p^a}$. Thus (20) is a legitimate consequence of differentiating a polynomial identity, not an undefined substitution into a $0/0$ quotient.

The accepted Hensel factorization $Q_e=M_eV_e$ has $V_e(0,0)\ne0$, so $V_e(\beta)$ is a unit. By (19)--(20),

$$
\sum_{\alpha_i:M_e(\alpha_i)=0}v(\beta-\alpha_i)
=v(M_e(\beta))
=2(p-1)^2p^{e-2}.
\tag{21}
$$

Every pair of level-$e$ roots has distance valuation at least $2r$: their difference is a sum of native one-step displacements of valuation $2r$, as in Section 1. The average of the $p^e$ finite contacts in (21) is

$$
d_0=\frac{2(p-1)^2}{p^2}<2r.
$$

There is therefore a root $\alpha_i$ with contact below $2r$. Since all other level-$e$ roots are at mutual distance valuation at least $2r$, the strong triangle inequality forces every contact with this fixed $\beta$ to equal that value. Their average is $d_0$, so every contact equals $d_0$. The same argument applies to every prime-level $\beta$. This proves the exact equality in (C), for every $e\ge2$.

Because $p$ is odd, the integer $2(p-1)^2$ is not divisible by $p$, so this contact has exact denominator $p^2$. Furthermore,

$$
p^2(2r-d_0)=2(p-1)>p-1.
\tag{22}
$$

Theorem A applies with $\nu=2$, proving $[K(\alpha):K]\ge p^2$. At $e=2$, the degree is also at most $p^2$ by the orbit bound, hence equals $p^2$, and $M_2$ is irreducible. This proves Theorem C. $\square$

An additional exact displacement follows by evaluating (2) at a level-$e$ root $\alpha$, now using the proven contact formula:

$$
v(P^{\circ p}(\alpha)-\alpha)
=2r+p d_0=2(p-1)
\qquad(e\ge2).
\tag{23}
$$

It is the same at all higher native levels. At $(p,e)=(3,2)$, (C) gives $8/9$ and (23) gives $4$, agreeing with the independent route through the existing discriminant value in (13)--(16).

## 7. Exact remaining all-level obligation and a refuted shortcut

A sufficient all-level contact law is the following statement:

> **(CL), initially proposed but now refuted for $e\ge3$.** For every odd prime $p$, every $e\ge2$, and a root $\alpha$ of the actual canonical $M_e$, there is a root $\beta$ of the actual $M_1$ such that $d=v(\alpha-\beta)$ satisfies
> $$
> p^ed\in\mathbb Z\setminus p\mathbb Z,
> \qquad d<2(p-1)/p-(p-1)/p^e.
> $$

Theorem A with $\nu=e$ would prove the original local full-degree claim from (CL), without changing the native clock or adjoining an unspecified auxiliary extension as part of the conclusion. For $e=1$ the accepted prime-level theorem already applies. However, the now-proved formula (C) makes $p^ed$ divisible by $p$ whenever $e\ge3$, so (CL) is actually false there.

This refutes only the proposed sufficient contact law, not the original full-inertia claim. The first-level comparison supplies the complete second layer and no automatic induction beyond it. The exact-denominator requirement cannot be discarded from Theorem A.

For completeness, the general relation first used to seek the contact law is available directly from (2). Defining $D_e=v(P^{\circ p}(\alpha)-\alpha)$, it says

$$
\sum_{\beta_i:M_1(\beta_i)=0}v(\alpha-\beta_i)=D_e-2r.
\tag{24}
$$

If $D_e<2r(p+1)$, the equalization argument gives all contacts equal to $(D_e-2r)/p$. Section 6 now proves $D_e=2(p-1)$, showing precisely why that first-level strategy saturates at degree $p^2$.

The remaining exact induction problem is to compare a level-$e$ root with an already understood level $d\ge2$, account for the now nonconstant within-cycle distance profile, and produce a ramification contradiction for every proposed $p^h$ with $2\le h<e$. A sufficient version would construct, in the relevant compositum, an explicit element of positive valuation prime to $p$ whose displacements force a first upper break exceeding a known quotient break. Neither such an element nor the needed higher-level contact/quotient data is asserted here. B4's separate single-field upper-bound ledger is not used as an upper bound on this compositum.

The global native-cycle quotient transitivity gap remains separate in every case. No new paper admission, mathematical execution or target arithmetic conclusion is claimed.

`NO_BAD_EULER_OR_ROOT_NUMBER` remains unconditional.
