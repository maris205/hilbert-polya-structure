## 3. Exact incidence, inverse maps, and open exclusions

I independently derived the rank-one description before reading the author's
completed proof. Writing $T(x,y,z)=(u,v,w)$, the first return gives
$$
A=x+u-yz,\quad B=y+v-uz,\quad C=z+w-uv.
$$
The second return is exactly
$$
yz=vw,\quad uz=xw,\quad uv=xy,
$$
the minors of $M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&v&z\\u&y&w\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Substitution in the level equation gives
$D=xyz+uvw-xu-yv-zw$. These are scheme identities: subtraction of the two
return equations yields the minors, and insertion of the minors recovers
the second return. No saturation by a coordinate was used. Thus coordinate
zeros cannot conceal another dominating component.

The cone is the image of rows $a(p_1,p_2,p_3)$ and $b(p_1,p_2,p_3)$.
Its prime determinantal coordinate ring and dimension four are correct.
The report's displayed projection Jacobian at $(x,y,z,r)=(1,0,0,2)$ has
determinant $5/2$, confirming dominance without a counting assumption.
Over the generic base its coordinate algebra is consequently a finite
field, separable in characteristic zero. These statements remain true
over algebraically closed constants.

The phase involution swaps the two rows, exactly the action of $T$ on
the ordered pair. The coordinates $q,X,Y,Z$ and the formulas
$$
A=X-qYZ,\quad B=Y-qXZ,\quad C=Z-qXY,
\quad D=q(XYZ-X^2-Y^2-Z^2)
$$
were checked by substitution. The report's half-coordinate signs in (14)
are necessary for the ordered word and are correct. With
$\delta=(r-1)/(r+1)$ they give $\delta^2=1-4q$ and interchange the two
phases when $\delta$ changes sign. The valuation at $q=1/4$ proves that
the quadratic extension is nontrivial. It follows that the invariant field
is precisely $E=\mathbb Q(q,X,Y,Z)$, not a proper subfield of the cycle field.

The initial chart $xu\ne0$ and subsequent $r\ne-1$ restriction are dense
opens in the full integral cone. Their complements have dimension at most
three, hence cannot dominate a four-dimensional base. This is enough for
the generic field claim. At the selected special fibres the missing charts
are addressed separately; the generic-open argument is not improperly used
to discard special points.

Finally, $T$ fixes a point if and only if each individual coordinate update
fixes it. The equations are exactly $\nabla K=0$, a three-dimensional
incidence over the four-dimensional base after the level is included.
Thus $P_1$ is empty generically and the entire generic $T^2$ incidence is
ordinary exact period two. No formal dynatomic subtraction is needed.

## 5. Full local sheet inventory and the missing-axis risk

For $A=B=C=0$ and $D\ne0$, a nonzero rank-one point has $a,b\ne0$:
if one row factor vanishes, the coefficient equations force the remaining
row to vanish as well, contradicting $D\ne0$.
If $a+b\ne0$, the finite-$q$ chart is valid and the fibre equations force
$X,Y,Z$ all nonzero. They then give precisely
$$
(X,Y,Z)=(\alpha/q,\beta/q,\gamma/q),\qquad
\alpha\beta\gamma=1,\qquad
Dq^2+3q-1=0.
$$
If $a+b=0$, the equations force all pairwise products $p_ip_j$ to vanish,
leaving exactly the three axis cycles with coordinates $\pm\sqrt D$.
Thus the inventory is exhaustive, not a torus-only count.

For an independent boundary calculation, use rows $(a,b,c)$ and
$t(a,b,c)$. The base equations are
$$
A=(1+t)a-tbc,\quad B=(1+t)b-tac,\quad
C=(1+t)c-tab,
\quad D=t((1+t)abc-a^2-b^2-c^2).
$$
At $t=-1,b=c=0,a^2=D\ne0$, their differential is
$$
dA=a\,dt,\quad dB=a\,dc,\quad dC=a\,db,
\quad dD=2a\,da-a^2\,dt.
$$
It is invertible. Column permutation proves the other two axes as well;
this is a symmetry of the incidence, not an illicit reordering of the
forced dynamical word. The phase action is free at these axis points, so
their cycle quotients are also unramified.

The initially flagged problem was that the four finite-$q$ folds alone
account for only eight of eleven cycle sheets. Report Sections 3.5–3.7
explicitly include the missing three axis cycles and resolve that issue.

## 6. The two isolated inertia elements

Let $s=(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ with product one. The fixed-$q$ coefficient

$$
d(q)=q^{-2}-3q^{-1},\qquad
d'(q)=(3q-2)/q^3,\qquad d''(2/3)=81/8,

and
$$
\nabla_{A,B,C}\mathcal D_s(q,0,0,0)

Each sign point at $q=2/3$ is a simple fold. Its critical point continues
uniquely, and the branch values have linear terms
$\tfrac12(\alpha A+\beta B+\gamma C)$. The ordered phase extension
is unramified because $1-4q=-5/3$ is nonzero.

The report does more than assert distinct tangents: it selects a coefficient
direction outside the six equality hyperplanes, so for sufficiently small
nonzero displacement the four actual analytic branch values are distinct.
A circle in the remaining $D$-line can then surround only one of them.

The four local degree-two cycle maps plus three axis sections supply all
eleven sheets. Local simple-fold neighborhoods can be chosen finite over

they already supply the full generic degree, so an additional sheet cannot

### 6.2 Phase branch at $D=4$

At $q=1/4$, $d'=-80\ne0$, so all four corresponding cycle maps are
unramified, while the ordered cover has simple equation
$\delta^2=1-4q$. Its branch values have linear terms
$-2(\alpha A+\beta B+\gamma C)$ and can be separated by the same
explicit perturbation argument.

The other root is $q=-1$, where $d'=5$ and $1-4q=5$, so the four
corresponding cycles and their eight ordered points are unramified.
The six axis points are unramified as checked above. The four phase
folds plus these other sheets have local degree

A meridian about exactly one phase branch acts trivially on all cycle
labels, swaps two phases in one cycle, and fixes the other twenty points.
It is a **pure single flip**, not an unknown lift of a cycle permutation.
Using a singular central cubic is legitimate: the meridian lies in nearby

## 7. Group lifting and constant field

Geometric integrality of $E$ gives transitivity on eleven cycle labels.
Prime degree gives primitivity. The graph consisting of supports of all
conjugates of the transposition has a group-invariant component partition;
primitivity makes this graph connected. Its edge transpositions generate
$S_{11}$. Thus no stronger permutation theorem is being silently assumed.

For the ordered group $G$, commuting with the native involution gives
$G\subseteq C_2^{11}\rtimes S_{11}$. The pure flip lies in its kernel
on cycle labels. Conjugation by lifts of the transitive cycle action gives
each coordinate flip, so the full $C_2^{11}$ lies in $G$.
Surjectivity onto $S_{11}$ then proves equality with the wreath product;
no independent choice of splitting of the extension is required.

The complex analytic calculation gives the geometric function-field group.
Extension of algebraically closed characteristic-zero constants from
$\overline{\mathbb Q}$ to $\mathbb C$ does not change this finite
geometric monodromy. The field generated by the full Galois-stable finite
point set is normal and separable. Its arithmetic group contains the
geometric group and is contained in the same native centralizer, so the
groups coincide. The usual arithmetic/geometric exact sequence then makes
the algebraic constant extension trivial. This applies to $L_2$, not an
unproved all-period compositum.
