## 5. Exact applicability boundaries

The following elementary observations clarify the missing bridge; they are
not borrowed as a new theorem from S1 or S2.

* Since $f'=0$, $F_n=f^{\circ n}-x$ satisfies $F_n'=-1$ for every $n$.
  Thus all periodic points are simple, including $p$-divisible return lengths.
* Put $H_n=\prod_{i<n}A(f^{\circ i})-\prod_{i<n}B(f^{\circ i})$.
  Ordinary CP is equivalent to existence of a nonzero $S$ with
  $F_n\mid S H_n$ for every $n\ge1$. Forward, put into $S$ the finitely many
  bad admissible cycles and all cycles meeting the finite zero/pole support.
  Reverse, an admissible cycle disjoint from the finite zero set of $S$ has
  $H_r=0$ at its native period $r$, hence product one. Only finitely many
  cycles meet either finite support. A cycle meeting both a zero and pole may
  have both cleared products zero; it is a support exception, not divided
  through or counted as a good admissible cycle.
* Finite exceptional **native cycles** do not mean finite support of the
  word/return series: one exceptional cycle can recur at arbitrarily large
  return lengths. A finite-support automaton theorem is not a direct bound
  on those native periods.
* All coefficients lie in some finite field, but its degree is unbounded.
  For example, the one-dimensional matrix $[\zeta]$ can have arbitrarily
  large finite order as $\zeta\in k^*$ varies. Finite-state or finite-field
  existence alone does not provide the requested coefficient-independent
  native-period bound.
* The derivative-zero class is not just the pure-Frobenius class. For
  $s\ge2$ with $p\nmid s$, $(x^s+x)^p$ has $s$ distinct generic inverse
  branches, whereas a pure Frobenius power has one. Thus the full class
  includes mixed inseparability with genuine multiple inverse branches.
* For monic degree-$d$ $f$, the products $\prod_i f^{\circ i}(x)^{\epsilon_i}$,
  $0\le\epsilon_i<d$, have distinct base-$d$ degrees and unit leading
  coefficients. They form the required degree-ordered polynomial bases.
  This elementary observation does not by itself prove the bounded Laurent
  representation or its boundary contraction.

The substantive bridge to be checked is therefore: construct that representation
for general $f$, combine balanced evaluation rank with arbitrarily long
nonzero returns to remove the unknown exceptional-period bound, and use
$F_n'=-1$ to recover exactly ordinary CP. All three parts must occur in the
actual complete proof; none is supplied by the word-test citation alone.

$$
F_n\mid S_*H_n\quad(1\le n\le N),\qquad

$$
b=\left\lceil\frac{m}{d-1}\right\rceil+1,
\qquad D=2b^2,\qquad N=3D+1=6b^2+1.
$$

The proof-bearing steps actually present are:

1. Section 3 uses a genuine scaling conjugacy, not a scalar multiple of $f$.
   Section 4 proves the exact ordinary-CP interface from $F_n'=-1$, retaining
   support cycles and both whole cleared products.
2. Sections 5–6 construct the coefficient extractor for a general monic $f$.
   The coefficient sum is proved locally finite; its annihilation of the
   cyclic ideal and value on every digit basis vector are checked. The
   two-block Laurent matrices obey $ds\le r+m$ at ordinary sites and
   $ds\le r+m+\deg S$ at the exceptional site. This yields the actual
   fixed-$S$ horizon $k_S+4b^2$, including contraction of high states.
3. Section 7 factors each balanced-cut coefficient matrix through the
   $D$-dimensional two-block matrix algebra. The same matrix is
   $V\operatorname{diag}(-H_n(x))W^{\mathsf T}$ on the visible return set.
   Both evaluation matrices have full column rank once their degree ranges
   cover that finite set. No positive-definite or nondegenerate trace-pairing
   assumption is used.
4. Under a finite total visible set, any already visible native cycle occurs
   at arbitrarily long nonzero returns: use $q\equiv1$ modulo the orders of
   the nonzero numerator/denominator products. A one-sided zero is treated
   explicitly. Balancing a sufficiently long return then bounds that
   cycle's native period by $D$, not the cardinality of the entire union.
5. Section 8 consequently replaces the unknown annihilator by $S_*$.
   Since $\deg S_*<d^{D+1}$, its carry horizon is at most $D+1$, giving
   the advertised $N$. This is the removed unknown bound, not a hypothesis
   inserted into the finite test.
