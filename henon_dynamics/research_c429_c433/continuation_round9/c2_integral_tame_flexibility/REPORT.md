## 5. A precise all-word coordinate interface

The following characterization retains **all** tame word lengths. It is not a new bounded-shear construction class.

**Proposition.** A lift in Section 1 exists if and only if there is an actual integral tame coordinate pair $M=(U,V)$ with these two properties:

1. $U(P_j)=U(P_{\kappa(j)})$ for every label $j$.
2. For every distinct integer level $u$ occurring among the $U(P_j)$, the numbers
   $V(P_j)+V(P_{\kappa(j)})$ are equal for all labels with $U(P_j)=u$; write the common number as $s(u)$. The unique rational interpolation polynomial of degree smaller than the number of distinct levels, taking value $s(u)$ at each such level, has integer coefficients.

Here an **actual integral tame coordinate pair** means that $(U,V)$ is already proved to be an element of $\operatorname{TA}_2(\mathbb Z)$, not just two polynomials having the prescribed values or unit Jacobian.

**Proof, necessity.** The global identity $MK=\rho_QM$ gives

$$
U(P_{\kappa(j)})=U(P_j),\qquad
V(P_{\kappa(j)})=Q(U(P_j))-V(P_j).
$$

Thus the common values in property 2 are well defined and are values of $Q\in\mathbb Z[t]$. If the distinct levels are $u_1,\ldots,u_r$, divide $Q$ by the monic integer polynomial

$$
B(t)=\prod_{\ell=1}^{r}(t-u_\ell).
$$

Monic division gives an integer remainder of degree less than $r$ with those values. Uniqueness of interpolation over $\mathbb Q$ identifies it with the polynomial in property 2.

**Proof, sufficiency.** Take the integral interpolation polynomial supplied by property 2 as $Q$. Then

$$
\rho_Q(M(P_j))=M(P_{\kappa(j)})
$$

for every $j$. The already existing integral tame inverse of $M$ gives $K=M^{-1}\rho_QM$ with the exact required action. $\square$

For the fixed $\kappa$, the first condition says that $U$ is constant on each of

$$
\{P_1\},\ \{P_2\},\ \{P_4\},\qquad
\{P_5,P_9\},\ \{P_3,P_8\},\ \{P_6,P_7\}.
$$

There are at most six distinct levels; repeated levels across these sets require the compatibility in property 2. No distinctness of all six levels is assumed. C4 excludes the first-coordinate condition only for its frozen $L V_Q H_P$ class; it supplies no induction that covers every tame coordinate pair in this proposition.

This proposition exposes the unproved existence step rather than solving it. Integer interpolation in its second condition is a complete test **after** a genuine coordinate pair has been supplied. No such pair is constructed in this R9 investigation.

## 6. Why the exact evaluation ring still does not complete coordinates

Let

$$
J_C=\bigcap_{P\in C}(x-P_x,y-P_y)\subseteq\mathbb Z[x,y].
$$

The earlier evaluation result says that $\mathbb Z[x,y]/J_C$ is the explicitly known ring of nine-tuples with the three within-fiber parity constraints. Consequently the prescribed finite action of $\kappa$ defines an automorphism of this quotient ring. It does **not** follow that this quotient automorphism is the restriction of an ambient tame automorphism, much less one of the required conjugate-reflection form.

The loss of coordinate information under evaluation has a direct example using this same $C$, without introducing any new point set. Put

$$
b(t)=(t+1)t(t-1)(t-2)(t-3),\qquad
U_0(x,y)=x,\qquad U_1(x,y)=x+b(x).
$$

Every first coordinate of a point of $C$ is one of $-1,0,1,2,3$, so $U_0$ and $U_1$ have exactly the same nine evaluations. The polynomial $U_0$ is a tame coordinate. The polynomial $U_1$ is not even a polynomial coordinate over $\mathbb Q$: for any candidate companion $V$,

$$
\det D(U_1,V)=(1+b'(x))\,\frac{\partial V}{\partial y}.
$$

The first factor is a nonconstant polynomial of degree four, with leading coefficient five. This product cannot be a nonzero constant in $\mathbb Q[x,y]$. But a polynomial automorphism with polynomial inverse has nonzero constant Jacobian determinant, by the chain rule and the fact that polynomial-ring units are constants. Hence no such companion $V$ exists.

This example does not exclude a different coordinate representative having particular evaluations. It proves only that the exact evaluation-ring calculation cannot, by itself, certify that its chosen polynomial representatives are coordinates. The missing lifting assertion must impose and establish a global coordinate condition. It cannot be replaced by the Chinese remainder theorem for scalar functions.

## 7. The applicable local-to-global theorem does not patch different maps

Theorem 3 of Berson–Dubouloz–Furter–Maubach, in the actually inspected original text, applies to $\mathbb Z$ and yields

$$
\operatorname{TA}_2(\mathbb Z)
=\bigcap_{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}
\operatorname{TA}_2(\mathbb Z_{\mathfrak p}),
$$

with the groups viewed inside the automorphism group over $\mathbb Q$. The theorem's condition holds because $\mathbb Z$ is a principal ideal domain. Its proof descends the degree of a **common** automorphism using coefficients that lie in every localization. [Original theorem and proof](https://arxiv.org/html/1011.0976v1).

Accordingly, it can certify the tameness of a single candidate whose required localization properties have all been proved. It does not exchange

$$
\exists M\ \forall\mathfrak p
$$

with

$$
\forall\mathfrak p\ \exists M_{\mathfrak p}.
$$

This investigation has not even established the latter local existence assertion for the full specified lift. It therefore cannot infer the former. Exact finite-field permutations, separately chosen congruence approximations or local coordinate pairs would still need a common-map existence argument.

There is also no supplied bound forcing a sequence of approximate constructions to stabilize to one polynomial word. A limit in a completion, if separately justified, would not by itself be an element of the discrete group $\operatorname{TA}_2(\mathbb Z)$. No such limit construction is claimed here.
