## 1. Frozen full question

Let $A,B,C,D$ be algebraically independent over $\mathbb Q$, let
$k=\mathbb Q(A,B,C,D)$, and let
$$
S/k:\quad K=x^2+y^2+z^2-xyz-Ax-By-Cz=D.
$$
Use precisely $T=s_zs_ys_x$, with the rightmost factor first and
$s_x(x,y,z)=(A+yz-x,y,z)$, and cyclic analogues. One $T$ is one native
time unit. For each $n\ge1$, let $P_n$ be all ordinary geometric affine
points of exact least period $n$; do not substitute scheme lengths,
integer points, group orbits, a phase lift, or finite-field Frobenius.
Let $L_n/k$ be the field generated by all coordinates in $P_n$, and
$L_{\le N}=L_1\cdots L_N$.

**FGT (one full question).** Determine for every $N\ge1$ the actual
permutation representation
$$
G_N=\operatorname{Gal}(L_{\le N}/k)
\curvearrowright\coprod_{1\le n\le N}P_n,
$$
its geometric subgroup after base extension to $\overline{\mathbb Q}$,
and its restriction maps as $N$ increases. A solution must give and prove
one structural description, including permitted components, group
attainment, constant fields and inter-period intersections. A collection
of isolated specializations or ever-larger Galois computations is not
that description. The field is generic in the full four-parameter family;
no claim about every rational specialization is included.

The elementary centralizer containment
$G_N\subseteq\prod_{n\le N}(C_n\wr S_{|P_n|/n})$ is only an upper bound.
It is not the answer, and maximality is not assumed. Applying Chebotarev
after supplying an arbitrary extension is background, not FGT.

**Success criterion:** actual structural image, proved uniformly in $N$,
with all compatibility claims. **Failure boundary:** exhibit a precise
unproved global-generation/intersection mechanism, or a counterexample to
a proposed structural answer, without changing FGT into a low-period
paper. Finite-level helpers remain auxiliaries unless separately admitted
by the coordinator.

## 3. Auxiliary theorem: the complete first nonempty primitive layer

**Claim FG2.** In the full generic family of Section 1, $P_1$ is empty,
$P_2$ consists of $22$ ordinary points in $11$ native two-cycles, and
the geometric and arithmetic Galois groups on $P_2$ are both the full
natural wreath product
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(L_2\overline{\mathbb Q}/\overline{\mathbb Q}(A,B,C,D))
=\operatorname{Gal}(L_2/k)=C_2^{11}\rtimes S_{11}.
\tag{FG2}
$$
In particular the constant field of $L_2$ is $\mathbb Q$. The equalities
are equalities of permutation groups on the actual affine points,
after choosing labels for their native cycles. No rational specialization
is asserted to retain that group.

### 3.1 Assumptions, strategy and dependencies

All algebra below is in characteristic zero. For local analytic monodromy
we extend constants to $\mathbb C$. The only substantive external
dynamical input is the classical generic degree-$22$ fixed-intersection
calculation for the Coxeter square, justified and subtracted in Section 4.
We use that calculation as an intersection count, not as ordinary
cardinality at collision parameters.

The proof has five dependencies:

1. The universal $T^2$ incidence is a rational rank-one determinantal
   variety, and its projection to the four-parameter base is dominant.
2. Quotienting by the native phase swap gives a rational degree-$11$
   cover; the ordered-point cover is a specified quadratic extension.
3. A smooth-fibre simple collision supplies a single transposition on
   those $11$ cycles.
4. A singular-fibre degeneration supplies a single phase flip, supported
   in one two-cycle and no others.
5. Elementary permutation-group arguments attain the entire wreath
   product; arithmetic cannot enlarge its geometric image.

The holomorphic implicit-function theorem, the one-variable simple-fold
normal form, and the identification of complex algebraic-cover monodromy
with geometric splitting-field Galois action are classical tools. Here the
fold derivatives and all other local sheets are explicitly checked.

### 3.2 Fixed points are singular-fibre data

Suppose $T(x,y,z)=(x,y,z)$. The first coordinate is changed only by
$s_x$, so $s_x$ must fix it. Then the second-coordinate equality makes
$s_y$ fix its coordinate, and the third does the same for $s_z$. Thus,
scheme-theoretically,
$$
\operatorname{Fix}(T):\quad
A=2x-yz,\quad B=2y-xz,\quad C=2z-xy.
\tag{1}
$$
Conversely these equations fix all three involutions. They are exactly
$\nabla K=0$. Adding $K=D$ gives
$D=-x^2-y^2-z^2+2xyz$. This incidence has dimension three; its image
cannot contain the generic point of the four-dimensional parameter base.
Therefore $P_1=\varnothing$. All generic $T^2$-fixed points have exact
native period two.

### 3.3 Exact incidence is a rank-one cone, including zero coordinates

Write $T(x,y,z)=(u,v,w)$. The first return determines the parameters
$$
A=x+u-yz,\quad B=y+v-uz,\quad C=z+w-uv.
\tag{2}
$$
The second return is then equivalent to
$$
yz=vw,\quad uz=xw,\quad uv=xy.
\tag{3}
$$
These are the three $2\times2$ minors of
$$
M=\begin{pmatrix}x&v&z\\u&y&w\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{4}
$$
On this determinantal variety, the remaining level equation is precisely
$$
D=xyz+uvw-xu-yv-zw.
\tag{5}
$$
Indeed substituting (2) into $K$ gives
$-xu-yv-zw+uyz+uvz$; (3) identifies its last two terms with
$uvw+xyz$. Hence (2) and (5), together with (3), give a scheme
isomorphism from the universal $T^2$ incidence to the affine rank-at-most-one
$2\times3$ determinantal cone $V$. This is not just a parametrization of
a selected nonzero branch.

For completeness, $V$ is geometrically integral and has dimension four.
The rank-one parametrization has rows
$$
(x,v,z)=a(p_1,p_2,p_3),\qquad
(u,y,w)=b(p_1,p_2,p_3).
\tag{6}
$$
Its coordinate ring is the subring generated by the six products
$ap_i,bp_i$ in the polynomial ring on $a,b,p_1,p_2,p_3$. The relations
are generated by the minors: two products of these generators have the
same exponent vector exactly when their row assignments can be changed
by successive pairwise swaps, each swap being one minor relation.
This proves primeness, over any constant field. On a chart with a
nonzero entry, one row ratio and three entries are free, giving dimension
four and rationality. This also shows why coordinate-zero strata are not
separate generic components.

On the chart $xu\ne0$, put $r=u/x$. Equations (3) give
$$
u=rx,\qquad v=y/r,\qquad w=rz,
\tag{7}
$$
with explicit inverse $r=u/x$. The base projection becomes
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=(r+1)x-yz,\\
B&=(1+r^{-1})y-rxz,\\
C&=(r+1)z-xy,\\
D&=-r(x^2+z^2)-r^{-1}y^2+(r+1)xyz.
\end{aligned}
\tag{8}
$$
At $(x,y,z,r)=(1,0,0,2)$ its Jacobian, with columns in that order, is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
3&0&0&1\\
0&3/2&-2&0\\
0&-1&3&0\\
-4&0&0&-1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det=5/2\ne0.
\tag{9}
$$
Thus the projection is dominant and generically finite. Its generic
coordinate algebra is a field, and is separable in characteristic zero.
The argument remains valid after extending constants to $\mathbb C$.

### 3.4 Rational cycle coordinates and the exact quadratic phase cover

The native map acts on $V$ by swapping the two rows in (4). On (7) it is
$$
\tau:(x,y,z,r)\longmapsto(rx,y/r,rz,r^{-1}).
\tag{10}
$$
On the dense chart $r\ne-1$, define invariant coordinates
$$
X=(1+r)x,\quad Y=(1+r^{-1})y,\quad Z=(1+r)z,
\quad q=\frac{r}{(1+r)^2}.
\tag{11}
$$
They give the base projection
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&=X-qYZ,& B&=Y-qXZ,& C&=Z-qXY,\\
D&=q(XYZ-X^2-Y^2-Z^2).
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
$$
The cycle function field is exactly
$E=\mathbb Q(q,X,Y,Z)$. To verify this assertion, set
$$
\delta=\frac{r-1}{r+1},\qquad \delta^2=1-4q.
\tag{13}
$$
The birational inverse reconstructs both phases as
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x,y,z)&=\left(\frac{1-\delta}{2}X,
                    \frac{1+\delta}{2}Y,
                    \frac{1-\delta}{2}Z\right),\\
(u,v,w)&=\left(\frac{1+\delta}{2}X,
                    \frac{1-\delta}{2}Y,
                    \frac{1+\delta}{2}Z\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$
Direct substitution in the three ordered involutions, using
$(1-\delta^2)/4=q$, sends the first triple to the second and back.
Substitution in $K$ gives (12). Conversely (11) and (13) recover the
coordinates from a generic ordered point. The element $1-4q$ is not a
square in the rational field $E$, because it has valuation one at
$q=1/4$. Hence the ordered-point function field is
$$
F=E(\delta),\quad [F:E]=2,\quad F^{\langle\tau\rangle}=E.
\tag{15}
$$
All these field identities also hold over $\mathbb C$. In particular the
generic cycle cover is geometrically connected, not merely defined over
$\mathbb Q$.

### 3.5 Degree 22/11 and a complete unforced local sheet inventory

The classical generic fixed-intersection degree is $22$ (Section 4).
Since the entire generic incidence is the separable field of Section 3.3,
this is the number of ordinary generic points, not a nonreduced generic
length. Consequently
$$
[F:k]=22,\qquad [E:k]=11.
\tag{16}
$$
Here is an explicit sheet inventory that will control the two local
degenerations. Set $A=B=C=0$ and keep $D\ne0$. In the finite-$q$ chart,
(12) forces all $X,Y,Z$ nonzero: if one vanishes, the remaining equations
force them all to vanish, contradicting $D\ne0$. The solutions are
$$
(X,Y,Z)=(\alpha/q,\beta/q,\gamma/q),\qquad
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\{1,-1\},\quad\alpha\beta\gamma=1,
\tag{17}
$$
where
$$
D=d(q):=q^{-2}-3q^{-1},\qquad Dq^2+3q-1=0.
\tag{18}
$$
There are four sign triples, two generic roots $q$, and two phases per
cycle, giving $16$ points. The chart omitted by $r=-1$ has row sum zero
in (6). Equations (2) with $A=B=C=0$ then require pairwise products
$p_ip_j=0$. Since $D\ne0$, exactly one $p_i$ is nonzero. These give the
three axis two-cycles
$$
\{(\sqrt D,0,0),(-\sqrt D,0,0)\},
\quad\{(0,\sqrt D,0),(0,-\sqrt D,0)\},
\quad\{(0,0,\sqrt D),(0,0,-\sqrt D)\}.
\tag{19}
$$
This exhausts the fibre, including all zero coordinates. At an ordered
point $(x,0,0)$ with $x^2=D\ne0$ and $r=-1$, (8) has Jacobian
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0&x\\
0&0&x&0\\
0&-x&0&0\\
2x&0&0&-x^2
\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det=-2x^4\ne0.
\tag{20}
$$
The other two axes have the same conclusion: in (6) the base map is
$A_i=(a+b)p_i-abp_jp_k$ and
$D=ab((a+b)p_1p_2p_3-\sum p_i^2)$, so permuting columns permutes
$A,B,C$ and preserves the local incidence problem. This uses a symmetry
of the incidence presentation, not an asserted reversal of arbitrary
ordered forcing. The phase action on (19) is free; their quotient sheets
are also unramified.

At each solution (17), write $s=(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)^t$. The Jacobian
of $(A,B,C)$ with respect to $(X,Y,Z)$ at fixed $q$ is
$$
J=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\gamma&-\beta\\
-\gamma&1&-\alpha\\-\beta&-\alpha&1\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{21}
$$
Conjugation by $\operatorname{diag}(s)$ turns this into the matrix with
diagonal $1$ and off-diagonal $-1$, whose eigenvalues are $-1,2,2$.
Thus $J$ is invertible and $Js=-s$. Near each sign branch, the implicit
function theorem solves $X,Y,Z$ holomorphically as functions of
$(q,A,B,C)$. Write the remaining map as $D=\mathcal D_s(q,A,B,C)$.
At $A=B=C=0$,
$$
\mathcal D_s(q,0,0,0)=d(q),\quad
d'(q)=\frac{3q-2}{q^3},\quad
d''(2/3)=81/8,
\tag{22}
$$
and differentiation through $J^{-1}$ gives
$$
\nabla_{A,B,C}\mathcal D_s(q,0,0,0)
=-\frac{1-2q}{q}\,s.
\tag{23}
$$
For example $D=1$ has two distinct $q$-roots avoiding $q=1/4,2/3$;
(20)--(22) and (13) show that all $22$ displayed points are unramified.
This independently checks generic reducedness against the imported degree.

### 3.6 A single cycle transposition, not four inseparable collisions

Take the base point
$$
b_f=(A,B,C,D)=(0,0,0,-9/4).
$$
The two roots in (18) coincide at $q_f=2/3$. For each of the four
sign triples, the cycle-space map has
$\partial_q\mathcal D_s=0$ and
$\partial_q^2\mathcal D_s=81/8\ne0$. Its critical point therefore
continues uniquely as $q=q_s(A,B,C)$ by the implicit-function theorem.
Its branch hypersurface is a graph
$$
D=B_s(A,B,C):=\mathcal D_s(q_s(A,B,C),A,B,C),
$$
with first-order expansion, by (23),
$$
B_s(A,B,C)=-9/4+\tfrac12(\alpha A+\beta B+\gamma C)
+\text{terms of total order at least two}.
\tag{24}
$$
The local map has degree two with simple branch along this graph:
expand around the critical $q_s$; its nonzero quadratic coefficient
has a holomorphic square root, giving a local coordinate whose square
is $D-B_s(A,B,C)$. A small transverse meridian exchanges precisely
the two local cycle sheets.

It remains to justify that this meridian can act on only one pair.
The four linear forms in (24) are distinct. Choose a vector
$v\in\mathbb C^3$ outside their six pairwise equality hyperplanes.
For sufficiently small nonzero $\varepsilon$, their values at
$(A,B,C)=\varepsilon v$ are pairwise distinct, because each difference
has a nonzero linear leading term. A sufficiently small circle in the
$D$-line around exactly one value $B_s(\varepsilon v)$ encloses none
of the other three branch values. The critical points and their
quadratic coefficients stay distinct and nonzero for this perturbation.

There are no unaccounted local cycle sheets: at $b_f$ there are four
degree-two local cycle maps from (17), plus the three unramified axis
sheets from (19), totaling $4\cdot2+3=11$, the full generic degree.
The phase discriminant is $1-4q_f=-5/3\ne0$, so it contributes no
phase ramification in these neighborhoods. Shrinking neighborhoods,
the eleven sheets for every nearby generic base point are precisely
these local ones; no remaining generic sheets are available to escape
to infinity or introduce an extra local permutation. Thus the stated
meridian fixes the other nine cycle labels and is a single transposition
in the cycle monodromy group $H\subseteq S_{11}$.

The cubic surface at $b_f$ is smooth: for $A=B=C=0$, the equations
$\nabla K=0$ give the origin on $D=0$ and the four points with coordinate
absolute values $2$ and product $8$ on $D=4$. Neither level is $-9/4$.
This example also warns that smoothness alone does not imply reduced
periodic incidence. At $b_f$ the ordinary fixed-point set has $14$
points: eight doubled nonzero points and six simple axis points,
with total length $22$.

### 3.7 A single phase flip, supported on one native two-cycle

Now take
$$
b_p=(A,B,C,D)=(0,0,0,4).
$$
The roots in (18) are $q_p=1/4$ and $q=-1$.
At $q_p$, $d'(q_p)=-80\ne0$. Thus each of the four cycle maps
(17) is unramified over the parameter base near its point, while its
ordered-point cover is $\delta^2=1-4q$. Since $q$ is a local base
coordinate transverse to $q=1/4$, this is a simple two-sheet branch.
Its branch graph is
$$
D=C_s(A,B,C):=\mathcal D_s(1/4,A,B,C)
=4-2(\alpha A+\beta B+\gamma C)
+\text{terms of total order at least two}.
\tag{25}
$$
Again these four tangent linear forms are distinct. The same small
generic $(A,B,C)$ perturbation and a meridian around just one graph
isolate one phase branch from the other three.

All other local sheets are unramified. At $q=-1$, one has
$d'(-1)=5\ne0$ and $1-4q=5\ne0$, giving four further cycle sheets
and eight ordered-point sheets. The three axis cycles remain unramified
by (20). At $q_p$ there are four degree-two ordered-point maps. Their
total local degree is $4\cdot2+8+6=22$, so the same degree-exhaustion
argument excludes unseen nearby sheets. The isolated meridian therefore
exchanges exactly the two phases of one cycle and fixes the other twenty
points. Its image on cycle labels is the identity.

The central degeneration lies on a singular cubic surface. This is
intentional: the generic field problem permits branching around the
singular-fibre discriminant. The nearby points on the meridian have
ordinary exact native period two; no singular point has been inserted
into the generic fibre or counted twice as an ordinary point.

### 3.8 Group attainment and constants

Because $E$ is a geometrically integral degree-$11$ field cover, its
geometric monodromy $H$ is transitive on eleven labels. A transitive
permutation group of prime degree is primitive: all translates of a
block partition the eleven labels into blocks of equal size, so a block
has size one or eleven. By Section 3.6, $H$ contains a transposition.

Here is the needed elementary group argument in full. Draw a graph on
the eleven labels whose edges are the supports of all $H$-conjugates
of that transposition. Its connected components form an $H$-invariant
block system. At least one edge exists, so primitivity makes the graph
connected. Transpositions along the edges of a connected graph generate
the full symmetric group (along a path, successive conjugations produce
the transposition of its endpoints). Hence $H=S_{11}$.

Let $G$ be the geometric ordered-point monodromy. Since it commutes with
$T$, it lies in $W=C_2^{11}\rtimes S_{11}$ and maps onto $H=S_{11}$.
Section 3.7 supplies an element of $G$ that is one coordinate vector
in the normal elementary abelian phase-flip group $C_2^{11}$. Conjugating
it by elements of $G$ whose cycle permutations carry its supporting
cycle to each other cycle produces all eleven coordinate flips.
Therefore $C_2^{11}\subseteq G$, and surjectivity to $S_{11}$ gives
$G=W$.

The arithmetic group also commutes with $T$, so it too is contained
in the same $W$. It contains the geometric group already equal to
$W$, and hence equals $W$. For a finite normal extension of the rational
function field, the quotient of its arithmetic group by its geometric
group is the Galois group of its algebraic constant field over
$\mathbb Q$. The equality just proved makes that quotient trivial.
Thus $L_2\cap\overline{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb Q$, completing FG2. $\square$

## 5. What remains missing for the unchanged FGT question

FG2 does not determine $P_n$-cover components for $n\ge3$, branch
generators on those components, their phase-rotation subgroups, or
intersections $L_n\cap L_2\cdots L_{n-1}$. Its two local branch
constructions are specific to the exact rank-one incidence at $n=2$;
no analogous all-$n$ parametrization or separating-degeneration theorem
has been proved. This is a missing global mechanism, not a no-go theorem
for Fricke arithmetic.

The exact bridge one would need next is an all-$n$ geometric
connectedness/component classification plus controlled degenerations
that realize enough cycle permutations and independent phase rotations,
with a separate inertia/intersection argument across period fields.
Even full monodromy of each individual layer would not by itself prove
independence of their compositum. GM3 is a concrete local warning against
that implication, not a counterexample to FGT for this different map.
