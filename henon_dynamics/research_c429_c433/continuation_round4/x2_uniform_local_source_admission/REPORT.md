## 2. Exact theorem being assessed

For every odd prime \(p\), let

\[
k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p,\quad K=k((s)),\quad v(s)=1,
\qquad P_s(z)=(1+s)z+z^2.
\]

For each integer \(e\geq1\), let \(M_e\in k[[s]][z]\) be the canonical monic
degree-\(p^e\) Hensel factor, reducing to \(z^{p^e}\), of

\[
Q_e(z)=\frac{P_s^{\circ p^e}(z)-z}
                 {P_s^{\circ p^{e-1}}(z)-z}.
\]

The roots form the unique small ordinary native \(p^e\)-cycle. The main theorem
asserts that \(M_e\) is irreducible over this fixed \(K\), and its root field
\(L_e\) is its splitting field, totally ramified cyclic of degree \(p^e\).
The designated generator is one application of \(P_s\), not a Frobenius or a
relabelled prime-power clock.

In a single fixed separable closure, the corollary identifies the oriented
degree-\(p\) character of every \(L_e\), \(e\geq2\): with the author's
trace-one resolvent normalized by \(\sigma_e y_e-y_e=+1\),

\[
[y_e^p-y_e]=[y_2^p-y_2]\quad\text{in }K/\wp(K),
\qquad F_e=F_2,\qquad L_1\cap L_e=K.
\]

Here \(F_e\) is the unique degree-\(p\) subfield of \(L_e\). The equality is
of oriented characters/classes and of embedded fields, not necessarily of raw
resolvent Laurent series. For \(p=3\), the previously accepted pair certificate
then propagates the reduced class \(2s^{-4}+s^{-2}\) to all \(e\geq2\).
There is no general-prime coefficient or first-break formula in this claim.

That addendum is not an input to this report's recommendation or to the

The object is natural within the quadratic family: translating by

parabolic fixed point, with a specific local base field. This coordinate

## 4. Closest primary sources and exact subtraction

### 4.1 Lindahl--Rivera-Letelier: the essential geometric input

Primary text: *Optimal cycles in ultrametric dynamics and minimally ramified
power series*, arXiv:1311.4478v3, 26 May 2015. Read: introduction, Theorems
A--E, relevant minimal-ramification criteria, Proposition 5.1 with proof,
Lemma 5.9's characteristic-\(p\) case, and Theorem C's proof.
[Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1311.4478v3)

Theorem C concerns an algebraically closed ultrametric field of odd residue
characteristic, an irrationally indifferent multiplier whose reduction has
finite order \(q\), and a minimally ramified reduction. It gives one optimal
unit-disk cycle of period \(qp^n\) at each level; a further multiplier
transcendence condition applies in characteristic zero. The quadratic with
reduction \(z+z^2\) lies in the \(q=1\) case. Its proof uses Weierstrass
degrees and exclusion of parabolic cycles to establish the exact cycle count.
These existence, uniqueness, ordinary-period, and radius statements are
source-owned. Formal-iteration ramification indices are not asserted as
breaks of \(L_e/K\).

**Our applicability analysis.** The imported radius here is
\(v(\alpha_e)=(p-1)/p\), independent of \(e\). Its denominator forces only
a factor \(p\) in the ramification degree, not \(p^e\). A single geometric
cycle implies that the parameter-field Galois image commutes with its native
rotation, hence is a subgroup of \(C_{p^e}\). Such a subgroup can be proper.
Thus uniqueness plus the radius does not contain the missing transitivity
argument. Indeed, geometric cycle facts survive extending the base field,
whereas its Galois image can decrease. No fixed-base irreducibility conclusion
was located in the examined source argument. This is an inference from the
actual theorem and proof, not an assertion that every conceivable consequence
of that paper has been exhausted.

### 4.2 Keating: a close method antecedent, with different hypotheses

Primary text: *Extensions of local fields and truncated power series*,
arXiv:math/0312391, 16 March 2005 revision. Read: §6 Theorem 6.1 and its
periodic-field argument; §5 Proposition 5.1 with proof.
[Primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0312391)

Theorem 6.1 assumes \(p>3\), a finite \(K/\mathbb Q_p\) of ramification
index at most \(p-1\), and a reduction-generated \(\mathbb Z_p\)-group of
index \(1\leq d\leq p-2\). After finite tame base extension it compares
periodic-point fields with a norm-field tower, obtaining a large intersection.
Its proof, in the \(n\geq3\) argument, identifies the orbit's Galois action
with a cyclic subgroup and uses valuation denominator \(p^n\) to prove
fullness. Credit this method antecedent.

The separate characteristic-\(p\) Proposition 5.1 assumes two already cyclic
degree-\(p^n\) extensions and sufficiently matching uniformizer jets. It
obtains an isomorphism allowing a base-field automorphism; its local-field
convention has finite residue field. It does not supply irreducibility of
our factors or fixed-\(K\), native-oriented equality of their AS classes.
**Our applicability analysis.** The candidate supplies neither those jet
hypotheses nor a norm-field identification. Equal characteristic and its
fixed root denominator \(p\) prevent applying §6 unchanged.

## 5. What mathematical work remains after source subtraction?

The decisive work is not the final assertion that an irreducible single-cycle
factor has cyclic splitting field. That implication is elementary: every
root is a polynomial iterate of one root, so the root field already splits
the factor, and its conjugates are native rotations.

The candidate's nontrivial content occurs before that implication:

1. The uniform second-layer argument uses exact interlevel contact and a
   ramification obstruction to establish degree at least \(p^2\), together
   with \(v(P_s^{\circ p}(\alpha_e)-\alpha_e)=2(p-1)\) for all \(e\geq2\).
   This is not inferred from the old \((p,e)=(3,2)\) certificate. The contact
   denominator at this stage remains \(p^2\), so it does not directly force
   full degree at arbitrarily high levels.
2. The new proof identifies the \(p\) top clusters by native indices modulo
   \(p\). It evaluates a higher dynatomic quotient at a level-two point by
   a valid derivative ratio. The mean cross-level contact is
   \(2(p-1)^2(2p-1)/p^3>2(p-1)/p\), uniformly for every odd \(p\).
3. A deeper pair, native isometry, and strict ultrametric separation give
   a canonical Galois- and native-equivariant matching of the two sets of
   \(p\) clusters. Nontrivial action on this quotient forces the whole
   subgroup of \(C_{p^e}\) to be full. The cluster argument supplies separate
   evidence beyond the root-denominator bound.
4. Native equivariance preserves the designated \(+1\) generator, so the
   first quotient characters agree without a scalar twist. Exact contacts
   and the classical break congruence then exclude \(L_1\subset L_2\),
   and character stabilization excludes \(L_1\subset L_e\) for every
   later level.

These steps concern the specific equal-characteristic arithmetic deformation.
No corresponding level-two cluster-transfer or oriented stabilization theorem
was found in the primary sources above. This is finding-level separation;
ultrametric clustering, commuting Galois actions, AS resolvents, and
Hasse--Arf are not themselves new methods.
