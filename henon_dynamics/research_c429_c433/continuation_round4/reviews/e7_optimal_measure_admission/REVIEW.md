## 2. Exact whole question and frozen claim scope

For every odd prime $p$, every complete algebraically closed ultrametric
field $K$ of characteristic $p$, and every $\lambda\in K$ with
$0<|\lambda-1|<1$, put $P_\lambda(z)=\lambda z+z^2$. For every
$e\ge1$, let $\Pi_e$ be its unique small ordinary cycle of least period
$p^e$. One application of $P_\lambda$ is one native tick. Define the
real probability

$$
\mu_e=p^{-e}\sum_{z\in\Pi_e}\delta_z.
$$

The complete success criterion is convergence of the full sequence
against every continuous real test function on
$\mathbb P^{1,\mathrm{an}}_K$. A subsequence, a single formal field,
a finite list of levels, or top-cluster convergence would not suffice.
These are precisely the parameters and selected measures in
[LRL Problem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1311.4478v3#S1.SS3).

The reviewed integrated conclusion answers that question affirmatively
and additionally establishes:

- A compact subset of the classical field $K$ containing every cycle
  and their limit support; compactness is proved in the absolute-value
  metric, not inferred from compactness of a Berkovich disk.
- Hausdorff convergence to a compact invariant set $A$, and convergence
  of the probabilities with an explicit $W_\infty$ upper bound.
- The system $P_\lambda|_A$ is topologically conjugate to addition by
  $1$ on $\mathbb Z_p$. Its unique invariant probability is the image
  of Haar probability, hence is nonatomic and has exactly $A$ as
  support. The support contains only classical/type-I points.

No characteristic-zero case, $p=2$ case, root-of-unity multiplier,
all-normalized-germ theorem, canonical equilibrium measure, explicit
analytic conjugacy, optimal convergence rate, or higher Galois-field
nesting is included. The theorem concerns each fixed allowed multiplier;
it does not assert uniform convergence in $\lambda$ as $|\lambda-1|$
approaches $1$.

## 3. What survives after subtracting the shared machinery

Put $s=\lambda-1$, $v(s)=1$ and $r=(p-1)/p$. At an anchor
$\beta\in\Pi_d$, write

$$
\delta_j=v(P_\lambda^{\circ p^j}(\beta)-\beta),
\qquad
A_d=v((P_\lambda^{\circ p^d})'(\beta)-1).
$$

The operative all-anchor statement is

$$
\frac1{p^e}\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_e}v(\beta-\alpha)
=c_d:=\frac{p-1}{p^{d+1}}A_d
\ge d r^3+r^2(1+1/p)\longrightarrow+\infty
\qquad(e>d).
$$

The source-owned cycle geometry, Hensel factorization, derivative
products, characteristic-$p$ binomial identities, and the previously
proved weighted displacement estimate are not new general theories.
The derivative-ratio mechanism was already used at low anchors in
UL4's proof. The new work is its all-anchor deployment, the divergent
sum using $\delta_j\ge(p^j+1)r$, and its uniform cross-level
consequence. An individual identity of formal derivatives is not
being promoted to a separate substantial result.

In particular, a maximum at least equal to the finite average gives a
pair at distance at most $|s|^{c_d}$. Native isometry transports this
pair to a coupling of the whole original cycles. The $p^e$ iterates
give each lower-cycle point multiplicity exactly $p^{e-d}$, so the
marginals are the actual probabilities. This proves the same bound
uniformly for every $e>d$, rather than selecting unrelated favorable
subsequences.

With $b_d=d r^3+r^2(1+1/p)$, the integrated estimate is

$$
d_H(\Pi_d,A),\quad W_\infty(\mu_d,\mu)
\le |s|^{c_d}\le |s|^{b_d}\longrightarrow0.
$$

The topology after this estimate is classical. The existence of a
finite net for every tail proves total boundedness; completeness gives
compactness. Couplings make the continuous-function integrals Cauchy,
and Riesz representation gives the probability. Finite cyclic clopen
quotients give the odometer/Haar description. These conclusions count
as the structure of this same limit, not independent paper contracts.

The finite equality, not only its divergent lower bound, also excludes
the finite-cycle alternative. A fixed anchor contact $h$ recurs on a
fraction $p^{-d}$ of every higher cycle, yielding

$$
h\le p^d c_d-(p^d-1)r<\infty.
$$

Thus $A$ avoids every old $\Pi_d$. The only remaining finite
possibility would be a fixed point, but $0$ and $-s$ are off the
limiting sphere. This is the genuine polynomial input for the
nonatomic refinement; generic odometer theory alone does not supply it.

A1 constructs the coefficient copy $k_0((t))\hookrightarrow K$ by
$t\mapsto s$: the constants algebraic over $\mathbb F_p$ have norm
one, completeness gives series evaluation, and a unique leading term
proves the isometry and injectivity. It does not assume the residue

The multiplier denominator is proved nonzero from the differentiated
factorization before higher quotients are evaluated, including
$e=d+1$. Real valuation averages and real probability masses are
never divided by $p$ inside $K$.

conclusion, not an inference that limits of type-I atoms must always
remain type I. No assumption that the entire Berkovich line is

## 6. Is this independent enough from UL4?

The strongest case for merging must be acknowledged. Both studies use
$P_s(z)=(1+s)z+z^2$, the same small cycles and Hensel factors, native
isometry, and differentiated dynatomic quotients. The principal new
arithmetic portion here is relatively short once those inputs are
available; many lines in both packages expose standard foundations.
Line count, a topological vocabulary change, or the existence of two
author lanes would not justify two papers.

The reason I nevertheless favor a separate measure contract is the
complete mathematical question that survives this overlap:

| Aspect | Admitted UL4 | Proposed single measure contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Object retained by the conclusion | Embedded root fields over the fixed $\overline{\mathbb F}_p((s))$ | Actual cycle sets and real measures over every allowed complete algebraically closed field |
| Main observable | Full cyclic Galois action and native-oriented first AS character | Full-sequence weak distribution, compact support and native limit dynamics |
| Crucial contact depth | A fixed level-two comparison beyond the top-cluster threshold | Arbitrarily deep contacts as the anchor level tends to infinity, uniformly over all higher levels |
| New conclusion | Irreducibility, first-character stabilization and first-field separation | Quantitative convergence to a classical nonatomic $\mathbb Z_p$ minimal system |
| Main conclusion needed from the other contract | No measure-convergence premise | No full-inertia, AS-character or root-field-nesting premise |

UL4's exact conclusions do not contain an estimate tending to zero
for distances between all sufficiently late cycles. Its fixed-depth
cluster comparison suffices for its Galois subgroup argument but not
for total boundedness at every metric scale. D1's explicitly checked
abstract control shows that single cycles, stable top-$p$ matching and
strong internal return estimates alone can coexist with alternating
measure limits. That control is not an arithmetic counterexample or a
formal independence theorem for UL4; it identifies the missing
inference in precisely the proposed generic shortcut.

Conversely, a compact nonatomic limit does not determine irreducibility
over the fixed coefficient field, its oriented AS character, or the
claimed embedded-field intersections. The present proof deliberately
avoids those assertions. Over the algebraically closed ambient field,
the measure problem retains substance even though those fixed-base
Galois questions are not its observable.

The all-anchor identity, its divergent estimate and the limit theorem
therefore close an identifiable full distribution question, rather than
merely computing another invariant of UL4's field contract. The
classical-support and aperiodic-Haar description give the resulting
measure concrete structure. This is sufficient independent content
for **one** paper under the batch's complete-question standard, even
after crediting the shared contact algebra and general topology.

This is not a claim that short proofs cannot be corollaries, or that
every named question merits a paper. The decisive distinction is the
new unbounded-depth implication and the full original distribution
conclusion. If an exact prior theorem is found to imply that implication
or conclusion, substantiality must be reopened; a quota cannot preserve

option, but merging is not mathematically forced by the reviewed scope
