## Claim, conditional dependency, and notation

For every odd prime $p$, retain $K=\overline{\mathbb F}_p((s))$, $v(s)=1$, $P_s(z)=(1+s)z+z^2$, and the canonical small-cycle fields $L_1,L_2$ inside one fixed separable closure. Put $B=L_2L_1$ and normalize $v_{L_2}=p^2v$, $v_B=p^3v$.

Then the actual upper and lower breaks of $L_2/K$ are

$$
\begin{aligned}
(u_1,u_2)&=\bigl(2(p-1),\ 2p(p-1)\bigr),\\
(b_1,b_2)&=\bigl(2(p-1),\ 2(p-1)(p^2-p+1)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

The actual upper and lower breaks of $B/K$ are

$$
\begin{aligned}
(v_0,v_1,v_2)&=\bigl(p-1,\ 2(p-1),\ 2p(p-1)\bigr),\\
(c_0,c_1,c_2)&=\bigl(p-1,\ p^2-1,\ p^2-1+2p^2(p-1)^2\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
$$

Their field different exponents, in the respective integer-normalized extension valuations, are

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{L_2/K}&=(p-1)(2p^3-2p^2+3p-1),\\
d_{B/K}&=p^2(p-1)(2p^2-2p+3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}
$$

These are field differents, not discriminants of the unnormalized order generated by a periodic root.

## 1. The complete filtration in the compositum

Let $\sigma,\gamma\in\operatorname{Gal}(B/K)=C_{p^2}\times C_p$ act by one native step on $\alpha$ and $\beta$, respectively, while fixing the other root. Put $\tau=\sigma^p$, $N=\langle\tau\rangle$, and $\eta=\alpha-\beta$.

The cyclic extension $L_2/K$ has exactly two upper breaks $u_1,u_2$, where the first-quotient theorem gives $u_1=2(p-1)>p-1$, and classical cyclic ramification gives $u_2\ge p u_1>u_1$. The first-quotient proof establishes the first two upper groups of $B$. For completeness, the full groups, including the last interval, are

$$
G^u=
\begin{cases}
G,&0\le u\le p-1,\\
\langle\sigma\rangle,&p-1<u\le u_1,\\
N,&u_1<u\le u_2,\\
1,&u>u_2.
\end{cases}
\tag{5}
$$

Here is why the last two lines introduce no unproved independence rule for ramification. For $u>p-1$, upper quotient compatibility makes the image on $L_1$ trivial. The image on $L_2$ is exactly its cyclic upper ramification group. In the direct product $G$, the subgroup with trivial $L_1$ projection and this specified $L_2$ image is uniquely that image in the first factor. Therefore it is exactly $N$ for $u_1<u\le u_2$, and trivial thereafter. The same reasoning gives the second line, while the first line is the already proved first-break statement. Thus the nontrivial successive indices in $G$ are precisely $1,p,p^2$.

The first two corresponding lower breaks are

$$
c_0=p-1,\qquad
c_1=(p-1)+p\bigl(u_1-(p-1)\bigr)=p^2-1.
\tag{6}
$$

Since $\tau$ generates the nontrivial group in the last interval of (5), its lower ramification value is the final lower break $c_2$.

## 2. The exact last displacement determines the last break

The first-quotient proof gives

$$
a=v_B(\eta)=2p(p-1)^2,\qquad
n=a+(p-1)^2,
\tag{7}
$$

and shows that the first nonzero exponent of $\eta$ prime to $p$ in a uniformizer expansion is $n$. Its cancellation lemma gives, for every automorphism with lower break greater than $p-1$,

$$
v_B(g\eta-\eta)=n+\ell_B(g).
\tag{8}
$$

Equation (8) is a proved later-break rule, not a prime-valuation rule applied incorrectly to $\eta$, whose value $a$ is divisible by $p$.

The automorphism $\tau$ fixes $\beta$. The exact displacement (4) therefore gives

$$
v_B(\tau\eta-\eta)
=p^3v(P_s^{\circ p}(\alpha)-\alpha)
=2p^3(p-1).
\tag{9}
$$

Apply (8), valid because $c_2>c_1>p-1$. Then

$$
c_2=2p^3(p-1)-n.
\tag{10}
$$

The native generator $\sigma$ has displacement $A=2p^2(p-1)$ and lower break $c_1=A-n$, as already established in the first-quotient proof. Subtract this identity from (10):

$$
c_2-c_1=2p^3(p-1)-2p^2(p-1)=2p^2(p-1)^2.
\tag{11}
$$

The index on the last interval of (5) is $p^2$. Herbrand's formula consequently gives

$$
u_2-u_1=\frac{c_2-c_1}{p^2}=2(p-1)^2,
\qquad u_2=2p(p-1).
\tag{12}
$$

Equations (6), (11), and (12) prove (2). Upper quotient compatibility identifies $u_1,u_2$ with the two upper breaks of $L_2/K$. Its lower breaks satisfy

$$
b_1=u_1=2(p-1),\qquad
b_2=b_1+p(u_2-u_1)=2(p-1)(p^2-p+1),
$$

proving (1).

## 3. Field different exponents

Use the classical Hilbert different formula

$$
d_{D/K}=\sum_{i\ge0}(|\operatorname{Gal}(D/K)_i|-1).
\tag{13}
$$

Its normalization here can also be checked directly: a uniformizer of a finite totally ramified extension of the complete base generates its integer ring; its monic minimal polynomial is Eisenstein. The different is generated by that polynomial's derivative, whose valuation is the sum of all nonidentity uniformizer-conjugate distances. Counting the contribution of each lower group gives (13). The derivative formula is the monogenic instance of the complete-intersection different formula in [Stacks, Lemmas 49.12.2--49.12.3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BWD), with the [comparison to the Kähler different](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BWG). These classical identities are subtracted.

For $L_2/K$, group orders are $p^2$ through lower index $b_1$, $p$ from $b_1+1$ through $b_2$, and one thereafter. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{L_2/K}
&=(b_1+1)(p^2-1)+(b_2-b_1)(p-1)\\
&=(2p-1)(p^2-1)+2p(p-1)^3\\
&=(p-1)(2p^3-2p^2+3p-1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{14}
$$

For $B/K$, the corresponding three group orders are $p^3,p^2,p$. Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{B/K}
&=(c_0+1)(p^3-1)+(c_1-c_0)(p^2-1)+(c_2-c_1)(p-1)\\
&=p(p^3-1)+p(p-1)(p^2-1)+2p^2(p-1)^3\\
&=p^2(p-1)(2p^2-2p+3).
\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
$$

This proves (3). As a normalization check, the displayed formulas give $d_{B/K}-p\,d_{L_2/K}=p(p-1)$, matching the order-$p$ subgroup $\langle\gamma\rangle$ whose nonidentity elements have lower break $p-1$ in $B$.
