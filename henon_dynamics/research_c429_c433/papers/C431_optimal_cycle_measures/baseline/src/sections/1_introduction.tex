\section{Introduction and main theorem}
\label{sec:introduction}

The location of a periodic orbit does not determine how a sequence of
such orbits is distributed. This distinction is particularly concrete
for the polynomial $P_\lambda(z)=\lambda z+z^2$ in positive
characteristic: its small cycles occupy one sphere, although their
ordinary periods tend to infinity. We prove that these cycles converge
to a classical compact adding-machine system, and give a quantitative
estimate for their supports and uniform probability measures.

Throughout the article, $p$ is an odd prime, $(K,|\cdot|)$ is a
complete algebraically closed ultrametric field of characteristic $p$,
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:setup}
  s=\lambda-1,\qquad 0<|s|<1,\qquad
  P(z)=(1+s)z+z^2,\qquad r=\frac{p-1}{p}.
\end{equation}
We normalize the real valuation by
$v(x)=\log|x|/\log|s|$ for $x\ne0$ and $v(0)=+\infty$.
For $e\ge1$, let $\Pi_e$ be the unique cycle in the open unit disk
of ordinary least period $p^e$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycle-measures}
  \mu_e=p^{-e}\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_e}\delta_\alpha.
\end{equation}
The existence, uniqueness, distinctness, and common radius
$|\alpha|=|s|^r$ are the optimal-cycle results of
Lindahl--Rivera-Letelier~\cite[Theorem C and the $q=1$ discussion]{lindahl2016optimal}.
All masses in \eqref{eq:cycle-measures} are real numbers.

Distances between classical points are $d(x,y)=|x-y|$. For nonempty
compact sets $E,F$ we use the Hausdorff distance
\[
 d_H(E,F)=\max\left\{\sup_{x\in E}\inf_{y\in F}d(x,y),
                         \sup_{y\in F}\inf_{x\in E}d(x,y)\right\}.
\]
A coupling of probabilities $\sigma,\tau$ is a probability on the
product with those marginals. Write $W_1(\sigma,\tau)$ for the
infimum of $\int d(x,y)\,d\pi$ over couplings, and
$W_\infty(\sigma,\tau)$ for the infimum of their essential supremum
of $d(x,y)$. All measures used in these distances below are carried
by a common compact metric space. Weak convergence on $\Ber$ means
convergence against every continuous real-valued function there.

\begin{theorem}[Compact adding-machine limit of the optimal cycles]
\label{thm:compact-adding-machine}
Under \eqref{eq:setup}, the following conclusions hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item The classical closure
\[
 C=\cl_K\!\left(\bigcup_{e\ge1}\Pi_e\right)
\]
is compact. The entire sequence $\Pi_e$ has a nonempty Hausdorff limit
$\A\subset C$, with $P(\A)=\A$ and $|a|=|s|^r$ for $a\in\A$.
\item For $d\ge1$ and any $\beta\in\Pi_d$, the number
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-cd}
 c_d=\frac{p-1}{p^{d+1}}
       v\!\left((P^{\circ p^d})'(\beta)-1\right)
\end{equation}
is finite and independent of $\beta$. For every $e>d$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-contact}
 \frac1{p^e}\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_e}v(\beta-\alpha)
 =c_d\ge d r^3+r^2\left(1+\frac1p\right).
\end{equation}
\item The entire sequence $\mu_e$ converges weakly on $\Ber$ to a
probability $\mu$ supported exactly on $\A$. For each $d\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-rate}
 d_H(\Pi_d,\A)\le |s|^{c_d},\qquad
 W_1(\mu_d,\mu)\le W_\infty(\mu_d,\mu)\le |s|^{c_d}.
\end{equation}
\item There is a homeomorphism $\theta:\Zp\to\A$ such that
$P(\theta(t))=\theta(t+1)$. The measure $\mu$ is the pushforward
of Haar probability under $\theta$, is nonatomic, and is the unique
$P$-invariant probability on $\A$. In particular $\A$ has no
periodic points and consists entirely of type-I points of $\Ber$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

This answers Problem~1.3 in the 26 May 2015 version of
Lindahl--Rivera-Letelier~\cite{lindahl2016optimal}, with its original
field and multiplier quantifiers. The assertion concerns each fixed
allowed $\lambda$; it does not give convergence uniform as
$|\lambda-1|\to1$. The set $C$ contains the old finite cycles and
their invariant atomic measures. Unique ergodicity in the theorem is
asserted only on $\A$, not on $C$.

The main arithmetic step is \eqref{eq:main-contact}. Differentiating
a small-cycle factorization at a level-$d$ anchor expresses its
multiplier through within-cycle distances. Differentiating every
higher return quotient at the same anchor then gives its mean contact
with $\Pi_e$. A characteristic-$p$ displacement estimate makes that
mean diverge as $d$ grows. Because $P$ is isometric on the relevant
disk, a single close pair couples the complete cycles. The finite
value of the same mean also separates every fixed old cycle from
the limit, which is the additional input needed for aperiodicity.

\paragraph{Relation to other measures and classical inputs.}
Jacobs proves convergence of Rumely's crucial measures to the
canonical dynamical measure~\cite[Theorem 2]{jacobs2017crucial}.
His reduction-dependent weights are supported at type-II points;
they are not the uniform measures \eqref{eq:cycle-measures}.
This distinction is one of measures, not of characteristic.
The periodic-point norm bounds of
Nordqvist--Rivera-Letelier~\cite[Theorem 3]{nordqvist2020residue}
concern ramified multiplier-one series and do not provide the
cross-level estimate used here. We use classical seminorm topology
for the Berkovich line~\cite{baker2007berkovich}. The inverse-limit
description and invariant measure of compact minimal equicontinuous
systems are also classical; see
Hurder--Lukina~\cite[author version, Sections 2.2--2.3]{hurder2025essential}.
We give the required cyclic-partition proof, including the finite
alternative, in full.

The argument is independent of any full-inertia theorem for local
periodic fields, of Artin--Schreier character stabilization, or of a
nested sequence of splitting fields. The coefficient-field construction
in Section~\ref{sec:coefficients} is a proof device inside arbitrary
$K$, not a restriction of the theorem to a Laurent-series field.
Sections~\ref{sec:displacements}--\ref{sec:contacts} prove the contact
estimate; Sections~\ref{sec:compact}--\ref{sec:adding-machine} prove
the compact, measure, and adding-machine conclusions.
