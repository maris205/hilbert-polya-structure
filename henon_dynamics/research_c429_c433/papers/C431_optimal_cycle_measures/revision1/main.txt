                            Haar limits of optimal wild cycles
                                           Anonymous Authors


                                                    Abstract
          For a complete algebraically closed ultrametric field of odd characteristic p, we prove that
      the uniform measures on the small cycles of Pλ (z) = λz + z 2 converge whenever 0 < |λ − 1| < 1.
      The conclusion concerns the entire sequence of cycles of least periods pe , over every field and
      multiplier in this range. Their union has compact closure in the classical field, and their Hausdorff
      limit is a compact invariant set on which Pλ is conjugate to addition by one on Zp . The limiting
      probability is the corresponding nonatomic Haar measure, with support consisting entirely of
      type-I points. The main estimate is an exact average-contact identity between any fixed cycle
      and every higher cycle. With s = λ − 1 and r = (p − 1)/p, its normalized average cd satisfies
      cd ≥ dr3 + r2 (1 + 1/p); the Hausdorff and essential-supremum transport errors are at most |s|cd .
      A finite upper bound on contacts with each old cycle excludes periodic points from the limit.
      The proof answers the selected-cycle convergence question posed by Lindahl and Rivera-Letelier
      without a discrete-valuation or field-nesting assumption.


1    Introduction and main theorem
The location of a periodic orbit does not determine how a sequence of such orbits is distributed.
This distinction is particularly concrete for the polynomial Pλ (z) = λz + z 2 in positive characteristic:
its small cycles occupy one sphere, although their ordinary periods tend to infinity. We prove that
these cycles converge to a classical compact adding-machine system, and give a quantitative estimate
for their supports and uniform probability measures.

original field and multiplier quantifiers. The assertion concerns each fixed allowed λ; it does not
give convergence uniform as |λ − 1| → 1. The set C contains the old finite cycles and their invariant

    The main arithmetic step is (1.4). Differentiating a small-cycle factorization at a level-d anchor
expresses its multiplier through within-cycle distances. Differentiating every higher return quotient
at the same anchor then gives its mean contact with Πe . A characteristic-p displacement estimate
makes that mean diverge as d grows. Because P is isometric on the relevant disk, a single close pair
couples the complete cycles. The finite value of the same mean also separates every fixed old cycle
from the limit, which is the additional input needed for aperiodicity.

6     Aperiodicity and the Haar adding-machine limit
We first establish the general structure of the compact limit. The polynomial contact identity will
then exclude the finite possibilities in that structure theorem.

Proposition 6.1 (Finite cyclic quotients). Under the hypotheses of Proposition 5.2, T |A is minimal
and uniquely ergodic, and supp σ = A. If |Ce | = pe for a prime p, the system is conjugate either to
addition by one on Z/pk Z for some k ≥ 0, or to addition by one on Zp . Its invariant probability is
the corresponding Haar probability.

Proof. Fix x, y ∈ A and η > 0. Choose a cycle Ce with dH (Ce , A) < η and points u, v ∈ Ce satisfying

by vanishing diameter. This proves surjectivity. The map is a homeomorphism and conjugates T to
addition by one.
    If kj is bounded, it eventually stabilizes and the inverse limit is one finite p-power cycle, including
a singleton. Otherwise pkj is cofinal among all powers of p, and (6.1) is Zp . In each case the invariant
probability is uniform on every finite quotient and hence is Haar probability. In the infinite case,
the mass of a point is at most p−kj for every j, proving non-atomicity.

7     Consequences and scope
The theorem describes two distinct compact invariant sets and their associated measures. Keeping
them separate prevents a false unique-ergodicity assertion on a space containing finite cycles.
    For any real continuous function φ on C, the proof also gives the explicit modulus-of-continuity
estimate                           Z         Z

In particular a Lipschitz test function of constant L has error at most L|s|cd . The bound is for each
fixed allowed multiplier; neither its optimality nor uniformity near the boundary |s| = 1 is asserted.
    The proof uses the source’s optimal-cycle geometry, coprime Hensel factorization, derivative
products, and the elementary characteristic-p substitution identity. Its specific distribution step is
the all-anchor contact average with a bound diverging in the anchor level. Compactness and the
cyclic inverse-limit argument then identify the resulting measure. No Galois irreducibility, oriented
character, or splitting-field nesting conclusion is needed or obtained here. The adding-machine
conjugacy is topological, not an asserted analytic linearization.
    The theorem does not address characteristic zero, characteristic two, all normalized germs, or
counting all periodic points of a polynomial. The comparison with existing measure and ramification
results is bounded to the specified sources and does not certify worldwide priority or the present
status of every later or unpublished work. The assertion proved here is the positive answer to the
explicitly identified version of the selected-cycle question.
