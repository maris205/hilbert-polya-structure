\section{Compact supports and convergence of measures}
\label{sec:compact}

We record the metric argument at the generality used here. It does
not require local compactness of the ambient space, and it separates
the closure containing every old cycle from the limiting support.

\begin{lemma}[Exact finite-cycle distances]\label{lem:exact-distances}
Let $T$ be an isometry of a metric space. For finite single $T$-cycles
$E,F$ with uniform probabilities $\sigma_E,\sigma_F$, put
$a=\min_{x\in E,y\in F}d(x,y)$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-distances}
 d_H(E,F)=W_1(\sigma_E,\sigma_F)
        =W_\infty(\sigma_E,\sigma_F)=a.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose a nearest pair $x,y$, let the cycle lengths be $m,n$, and put
$L=\lcm(m,n)$. The probability
\[
 \pi=\frac1L\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\delta_{(T^jx,T^jy)}
\]
has the required marginals, since each coordinate point occurs
$L/m$ or $L/n$ times. Isometry makes every paired distance equal
to $a$. Every pair in $E\times F$ has distance at least $a$, so no
coupling has smaller mean or essential-supremum cost. The iterates
also cover both finite sets, proving both directed Hausdorff
distances equal to $a$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Compact limit of uniformly close cycles]
\label{prop:compact-limit}
Let $(X,d)$ be a complete ultrametric space, let $T:X\to X$ be an
isometry, and let $C_e$ be a nonempty finite single $T$-cycle for
each $e\ge1$. Let $\sigma_e$ be its uniform probability, and suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contact-criterion}
 a_{d,e}=\min_{x\in C_d,y\in C_e}d(x,y),\qquad
 \varepsilon_d=\sup_{e>d}a_{d,e}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Then $B=\cl_X\bigl(\bigcup_e C_e\bigr)$ is compact and $T(B)=B$.
There is a nonempty compact set $A\subset B$ with $T(A)=A$ such that
$C_e\to A$ in Hausdorff distance. The probabilities $\sigma_e$
converge weakly on $B$ to an invariant probability $\sigma$ with
$\supp\sigma\subset A$. For every $d\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-limit-bound}
 d_H(C_d,A)\le\varepsilon_d,\qquad
 W_1(\sigma_d,\sigma)\le W_\infty(\sigma_d,\sigma)
 \le\varepsilon_d.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix $\eta>0$ and choose $d$ with $\varepsilon_d<\eta$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-distances} makes $C_d$ a finite $\eta$-net
for every later cycle. Adding all earlier cycle points gives a finite
net for the entire union. That union is totally bounded, as is its
closure $B$. The latter is complete because it is closed in $X$.
A complete totally bounded metric space is compact: successive finite
covers of radii tending to zero provide a Cauchy subsequence of any
sequence, and completeness supplies its limit. Thus $B$ is compact.

The map $T$ sends the union of cycles onto itself. By continuity it
maps $B$ into $B$, and its compact image is closed and contains the
dense union. Hence $T(B)=B$. No surjectivity on $X$ was assumed.

By \eqref{eq:exact-distances}, the cycle sets are Hausdorff-Cauchy.
Define their nonempty compact tail intersection by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-set}
 A=\bigcap_{N\ge1}\cl_B\!\left(\bigcup_{e\ge N}C_e\right).
\end{equation}
We verify Hausdorff convergence directly. Given $\eta>0$, choose
$N$ so that $d_H(C_e,C_f)<\eta/2$ whenever $e,f\ge N$. Fix
$e\ge N$ and $x\in C_e$. Points in arbitrarily late $C_f$ can be
chosen within $\eta/2$ of $x$; a subsequence converges in $B$ to
a point of $A$ at distance at most $\eta/2$ from $x$.
Conversely, each $y\in A$ is the limit of points in cycles with
indices tending to infinity. Choose nearby points of the fixed
compact set $C_e$ and pass to a convergent subsequence there. This
gives a point of $C_e$ within $\eta/2$ of $y$. Both bounds are
uniform, proving $d_H(C_e,A)\to0$. The same argument with $d$
fixed and $d_H(C_d,C_e)\le\varepsilon_d$ for all $e>d$ gives
the first inequality in \eqref{eq:general-limit-bound}.

Continuity implies $T(A)\subset A$. For the reverse inclusion,
write $y\in A$ as a limit of $y_j\in C_{e_j}$, $e_j\to\infty$.
Choose a predecessor $x_j\in C_{e_j}$ with $T(x_j)=y_j$.
A convergent subsequence of $x_j$ has limit $x\in A$, and
$T(x)=y$. Therefore $T(A)=A$.

For $\varphi\in C(B,\R)$ let
\[
 \omega_\varphi(t)=\sup\{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|:
                      x,y\in B,\ d(x,y)\le t\}.
\]
Compactness gives uniform continuity, so $\omega_\varphi(t)\to0$
as $t\downarrow0$. The coupling in Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-distances}
yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:modulus-bound}
 \left|\int\varphi\,d\sigma_e-\int\varphi\,d\sigma_d\right|
 \le\omega_\varphi(\varepsilon_d)\qquad(e>d).
\end{equation}
Thus the integrals converge for every such $\varphi$. Their limits
define a positive norm-one linear functional taking the constant
one to one. The Riesz representation theorem on the compact
Hausdorff space $B$ gives a regular Borel probability $\sigma$.
This is convergence of the full sequence. Applying it to
$\varphi\circ T$ proves invariance. The continuous nonnegative
function $x\mapsto d(x,A)$ has integrals tending to zero by
Hausdorff convergence; hence $\supp\sigma\subset A$.

For the transport bound, fix $d$ and consider the finite-cycle
couplings with $\sigma_e$ for $e>d$. Probabilities on the compact
metric space $B\times B$ admit a weakly convergent subsequence.
One proof takes a countable uniformly dense subset of continuous
functions, extracts a diagonal subsequence of their integrals, and
uses Riesz representation for the resulting positive functional.
The limiting coupling has marginals $\sigma_d,\sigma$. The
nonnegative continuous function $(x,y)\mapsto
\max\{d(x,y)-\varepsilon_d,0\}$ has integral zero along these
couplings and in their limit. The limit is therefore supported on
$d(x,y)\le\varepsilon_d$. This proves the remaining inequalities
in \eqref{eq:general-limit-bound}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Application to the optimal cycles}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-limit} to the complete disk
$\mathcal D$, the isometry $P$, and the cycles $\Pi_e$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:coupling} gives
\[
 \varepsilon_d\le |s|^{c_d}
 \le |s|^{d r^3+r^2(1+1/p)}\longrightarrow0.
\]
It follows that $C=\cl_K\bigl(\bigcup_e\Pi_e\bigr)$ is compact,
that the full sequence has Hausdorff limit $\A$, and that
$\mu_e$ has an invariant weak limit $\mu$ on $C$ with
$\supp\mu\subset\A$. The norm is continuous, so $C$ and $\A$
remain on $|z|=|s|^r$. The inequalities
\eqref{eq:main-rate} have also been proved.

To interpret the limit on $\Ber$, send $a\in K$ to the evaluation
seminorm at $a$. This map is continuous: each defining coordinate
$a\mapsto|H(a)|$, $H\in K[z]$, is continuous. The Berkovich
projective line is Hausdorff; see the seminorm and analytification
background in~\cite[Sections 2.3--2.4]{baker2007berkovich}.
The restriction to compact $C$ is therefore a homeomorphism onto
a closed compact image. Pushing $\mu$ forward gives a Radon
probability on $\Ber$. Every continuous real test function on
$\Ber$ restricts to one on $C$, so convergence on $C$ proves
the required weak convergence on $\Ber$. Neither compactness of
an ambient classical disk nor metrizability of the entire Berkovich
line has been used.
