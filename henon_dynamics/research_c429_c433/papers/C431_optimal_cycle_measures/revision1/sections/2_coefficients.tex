\section{Small factors over an arbitrary complete field}
\label{sec:coefficients}

We first construct the factorization used in the contact calculation
without assuming irreducibility or local inertia. Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:returns}
 F_e(z)=P^{\circ p^e}(z)-z\quad(e\ge0),\qquad
 Q_e(z)=F_e(z)/F_{e-1}(z)\quad(e\ge1).
\end{equation}
These indices label $p$-power returns; the superscript $\circ$ always
denotes ordinary iteration. The minimal ramification of $g(z)=z+z^2$
gives the source input
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimal-ramification}
 \ord_z\bigl(g^{\circ p^e}(z)-z\bigr)
 =1+\frac{p^{e+1}-1}{p-1}\qquad(e\ge0).
\end{equation}
This is the $q=1$ reduction underlying the optimal-cycle
theorem~\cite[Theorem C and the $q=1$ discussion]{lindahl2016optimal}.
The other input is the already established set of $p^e$ distinct
points $\Pi_e$, each of valuation $r$. We will use degree to identify
the whole small factor, so no further separability premise is needed.

\begin{lemma}[Coefficient-field realization]\label{lem:coefficient-field}
Let $k_0\subset K$ be the subfield of elements algebraic over $\Fp$.
There is an isometric embedding
\[
 \iota:k_0((t))\longrightarrow K,\qquad t\longmapsto s,
\]
where $k_0((t))$ has norm $|s|^{\ord_t}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Algebraic closedness of $K$ makes $k_0$ an algebraic closure of
$\Fp$. Every nonzero element of $k_0$ lies in a finite field and
has norm one, since it is a root of unity. For a Laurent series
$a(t)=\sum_{n\ge n_0}a_nt^n$, set
\[
 \iota(a)=\sum_{n\ge n_0}a_ns^n.
\]
Its terms tend to zero, so the ultrametric inequality and completeness
of $K$ give convergence. If $n_*$ is its first nonzero coefficient
index, the leading term has norm $|s|^{n_*}$ and the remaining tail
has norm at most $|s|^{n_*+1}$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:embedding-norm}
 |\iota(a)|=|s|^{n_*}\qquad(a\ne0).
\end{equation}
Addition and multiplication are preserved on finite Laurent
polynomials. Approximating arbitrary series by finite truncations
and using continuity of both operations proves the same for series.
Equation~\eqref{eq:embedding-norm} gives injectivity, and preservation
of $1$ and multiplication gives preservation of inverses. The norm
identity proves the isometry. No equality between the residue field
of $K$ and $k_0$ is assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Canonical small factor]\label{lem:small-factor}
For every $e\ge1$ there are monic $M_e,V_e\in K[z]$ with integral
coefficients such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:small-factor}
 F_e=F_{e-1}M_eV_e,\qquad
 M_e(z)=\prod_{\alpha\in\Pi_e}(z-\alpha),\qquad
 v(V_e(x))=0\quad\text{if }v(x)>0.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Work first over the complete discrete valuation ring $R=k_0[[t]]$
with $P_t(z)=(1+t)z+z^2$, and attach a subscript $t$ to the returns
in \eqref{eq:returns}. For a polynomial $h$, the relation $h(z)=z$
in the quotient by $h(z)-z$ implies $h^{\circ p}(z)=z$ there.
Taking $h=P_t^{\circ p^{e-1}}$ proves divisibility of $F_{e,t}$ by
$F_{e-1,t}$. Monic division gives a monic quotient $Q_{e,t}$ in
$R[z]$, and reduction commutes with this division.

Successive orders in \eqref{eq:minimal-ramification} differ by
$p^e$. Consequently
\[
 \overline Q_{e,t}(z)=z^{p^e}\overline V_e(z),
 \qquad \overline V_e(0)\ne0.
\]
The displayed monic factors are coprime. Coprime Hensel factorization
over $R$ gives monic polynomials $M_{e,t},V_{e,t}\in R[z]$ with
\[
 Q_{e,t}=M_{e,t}V_{e,t},\qquad
 \deg M_{e,t}=p^e,\qquad
 \overline M_{e,t}=z^{p^e},\qquad
 \overline V_{e,t}(0)\ne0.
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:coefficient-field} coefficientwise. The resulting
$M_e,V_e$ have integral coefficients, and $V_e(0)$ is a unit.
At an input of positive valuation, every positive-degree term of
$V_e$ has positive valuation. Hence $v(V_e(x))=0$ there.

For $\alpha\in\Pi_e$, its least period implies
$F_{e-1}(\alpha)\ne0$. The transported factorization therefore
forces $M_e(\alpha)=0$. There are $p^e$ distinct such points and
$M_e$ is monic of degree $p^e$. This proves its exact root product
in \eqref{eq:small-factor} and completes the argument.
\end{proof}

The construction takes place over one coefficient field that embeds
in every allowed $K$. It uses neither a generic-specialization
argument nor any conclusion about the Galois group of $M_e$.
