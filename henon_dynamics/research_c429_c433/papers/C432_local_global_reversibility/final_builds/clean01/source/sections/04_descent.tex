\section{Scalar twisting and descent over the original field}
\label{sec:descent}

We now prove Theorem~\ref{thm:family}. Fix a number field $L$ and
$a\in L^\times$. Choose an embedding $L\hookrightarrow\C$ and
$u\in\C$ with $u^8=a$, and set
$A_u=\diag(u,u^{-1})$. These choices are proof devices for a
geometric classification; the statement remains over $L$.

The identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alternating-twist}
  A_u^{-1}H_dA_u^{-1}=H_{u^{d+1},d},
  \qquad
  A_uH_dA_u=H_{u^{-(d+1)},d}
\end{equation}
follow by evaluating the two coordinates. Inserting alternating
$A_u$ and $A_u^{-1}$ between the four factors gives
\begin{align}
  A_u^{-1}F_0A_u
  &=(A_u^{-1}H_7A_u^{-1})(A_uH_{15}A_u)
    (A_u^{-1}H_{15}A_u^{-1})(A_uH_7A_u)\notag\\
  &=H_{a,7}H_{a^{-2},15}H_{a^2,15}H_{a^{-1},7}
   =F_a. \label{eq:family-conjugation}
\end{align}
The conjugated swap is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twisted-swap}
  A_u^{-1}\tau A_u=R_{t_0},\qquad t_0=u^2,\qquad t_0^8=a^2.
\end{equation}
Since $A_u$ commutes with all $S_\zeta$, the preceding full
centralizer and reversing-coset equalities conjugate to the same
equalities for $F_a$. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-geometric-twists}
  \Rev_{\C}(F_a)
  =\{F_a^jR_t:j\in\Z,\ t^8=a^2\}.
\end{equation}
Indeed,
$S_\zeta R_{t_0}=R_{\zeta^{-1}t_0}$, and
$\zeta^{-1}t_0$ runs through all eight roots of $a^2$.
Thus roots with $t^4=-a$ are retained; the necessary scalar
equation is not the stronger equation $t^4=a$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:family}]
Let $R\in\Rev_L(F_a)$. After the chosen embedding,
\eqref{eq:all-geometric-twists} writes $R=F_a^jR_t$ for some
$j\in\Z$ and $t\in\C^\times$ with $t^8=a^2$.
For either sign of $j$, the map $F_a^{-j}$ is a polynomial
automorphism over $L$. Therefore
\[
  F_a^{-j}R=R_t\in\Aut_L(\A^2).
\]
The coefficient of $x$ in the second coordinate of $R_t$ is $t$,
so $t\in L^\times$. This proves necessity in every integer
component, regardless of the degree of the original $R$.
Conversely, a root $t\in L^\times$ gives $R_t$ by
\eqref{eq:all-geometric-twists}, and each $F_a^jR_t$ is then
defined over $L$ and reverses $F_a$.
This proves the full equality \eqref{eq:family-classification}
and its existence criterion.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:local-construction}
Let $E$ be any characteristic-zero field, let $a,t\in E^\times$,
and suppose $t^8=a^2$. Then the affine involution $R_t$ reverses
$F_a$ over $E$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In an algebraic closure of $E$, choose $u^8=a$ and put $t_0=u^2$.
The coordinate identities
\eqref{eq:alternating-twist}--\eqref{eq:twisted-swap} hold over
this closure. Put $\zeta=t_0/t$; then $\zeta^8=1$.
The identity $H_d^{-1}S_\zeta H_d=S_{\zeta^{-1}}$ for
$d=7,15$ proves that $S_\zeta$ commutes with $F_0$.
It also commutes with $A_u$, and hence with $F_a=A_u^{-1}F_0A_u$.
Thus $S_\zeta R_{t_0}=R_t$ reverses $F_a$.
Both sides of the reversal identity have coefficients in $E$,
so the identity already holds over $E$.
Finally, $R_t^2=\id$ by its two-coordinate formula.
\end{proof}

The lemma constructs the witnesses over completions without requiring
an embedding of a completion into $\C$.
Only the global classification uses such an embedding for a number
field. Removing the original-field native power in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:family} is also essential: coefficient cancellation
in a high-degree expression $F_a^jR_t$ cannot evade that operation.
