\section{The eventual native quotient tower}\label{sec:tower}

This section combines the local Galois fields with an independent
compact-limit theorem. We state the imported interface explicitly
before proving the Galois continuity and quotient transfer needed
for Theorem~\ref{thm:tower}.

Choose an algebraic closure of \(K\) containing \(K^\sep\), and let
\(\mathcal C\) be its completion. It is complete and algebraically
closed by the classical completion theorem
\cite[Theorem 1.1]{conradcompletion}. Fix \(0<|s|<1\) and use the
associated absolute value. The sets \(S_e\) are still the unique
small ordinary cycles in \(\mathcal C\), by Lemma~\ref{lem:small}.

For nonempty compact subsets \(E,F\subset\mathcal C\), let \(d_H(E,F)\)
denote their Hausdorff distance for the ordinary metric
\(d(x,y)=|x-y|\); explicitly,
\[
 d_H(E,F)=\max\left\{
 \sup_{x\in E}\inf_{y\in F}|x-y|,
 \sup_{y\in F}\inf_{x\in E}|x-y|
 \right\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Compact adding-machine input]\label{thm:compact-input}
For these cycles, the ordinary metric closure
\[
 C=\overline{\bigcup_{e\ge1}S_e}^{\,\mathcal C}
\]
is compact. The set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-limit}
 \Omega=\bigcap_{N\ge1}
 \overline{\bigcup_{e\ge N}S_e}^{\,C}
\end{equation}
is nonempty and compact, \(P(\Omega)=\Omega\), and
\(d_H(S_e,\Omega)\to0\) for the full sequence.
There is a homeomorphism \(h:\Omega\to\Zp\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:native-conjugacy}
 h(Px)=h(x)+1.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

This is precisely the compactness, Hausdorff and conjugacy
part of the unpublished companion
\cite[Theorem 1.1, \emph{Compact adding-machine limit of the optimal
cycles}]{companion2026haar}, applied to \(\mathcal C\) and
\(\lambda=1+s\). The companion proves it for every complete
algebraically closed nonarchimedean field of characteristic \(p\)
and every \(0<|\lambda-1|<1\).
Its proof includes the all-anchor identity
\[
 \frac1{p^e}\sum_{\alpha\in S_e}v(\beta-\alpha)
 =c_d:=\frac{p-1}{p^{d+1}}
 v\bigl((P^{\circ p^d})'(\beta)-1\bigr)
 \ge d r^3+r^2(1+1/p),\qquad e>d,\quad\beta\in S_d.
\]
The finite value of this average excludes the finite-cycle
alternative for the compact limit. Thus no unproved isolation
assumption, nor merely weak convergence of measures, is being
used in \eqref{eq:native-conjugacy}. The companion proves its
factor, contact and compactness assertions without using full
local inertia or the first AS character.
Theorem~\ref{thm:compact-input} is the only additional
model-specific input in this section; no part of the preceding
sections depends on it.

\subsection{Continuity of the Galois action on the compact closure}

The characters
\(\rho_e:G_K\to\Z/p^e\Z\) were defined in
Proposition~\ref{prop:as-stable}. By Theorem~\ref{thm:main}
they are continuous and surjective, with one native step
labeled \(+1\).
Every \(g\in G_K\) extends uniquely to the algebraic closure:
purely inseparable roots have uniquely determined images.
The extension of the valuation from complete \(K\) is unique,
so the resulting action is isometric. It therefore extends
to an isometric automorphism of \(\mathcal C\), with inverse
the extension of \(g^{-1}\).
Since \(gS_e=S_e\) at every level and \(g\) commutes with \(P\),
it preserves \(C\), every tail closure in \eqref{eq:tail-limit},
and \(\Omega\).

We need continuity in \(g\), not just individual isometries.
Fix \(\varepsilon>0\).
Compactness of \(C\) and density of the algebraic cycle union
give a finite set \(A_\varepsilon\subset\bigcup_e S_e\) such that
every \(x\in C\) is within distance strictly less than
\(\varepsilon\) of some \(a\in A_\varepsilon\).
Each \(a\) is separable algebraic over \(K\), so its stabilizer
in \(G_K\) is open. Let \(U_\varepsilon\) be the intersection
of these finitely many stabilizers. For \(g\in U_\varepsilon\),
\[
 |gx-x|\le
 \max\{|gx-ga|,|ga-a|,|a-x|\}<\varepsilon
 \qquad(x\in C).
\]
Thus elements of \(G_K\) near the identity move all of \(C\)
uniformly little. If \(g=g_0u\), \(u\in U_\varepsilon\), and
\(|x-x_0|<\varepsilon\), then
\[
 |gx-g_0x_0|=|ux-x_0|
 \le\max\{|ux-x|,|x-x_0|\}<\varepsilon.
\]
This proves joint continuity of \(G_K\times C\to C\), and
of its restriction to \(\Omega\).
It requires no assertion that a limit point is algebraic.

\subsection{A canonical translation character}

For a homeomorphism \(h\) satisfying \eqref{eq:native-conjugacy},
the continuous map \(f_g=h\circ g\circ h^{-1}:\Zp\to\Zp\)
commutes with addition by \(1\).
Any continuous \(f\) with \(f(z+1)=f(z)+1\) is a translation:
induction gives \(f(m)=f(0)+m\) on nonnegative integers,
and these integers are dense in \(\Zp\).
Hence there is a unique \(\chi_\infty(g)\in\Zp\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:translation-character}
 h(gx)=h(x)+\chi_\infty(g)\qquad(x\in\Omega).
\end{equation}
Composition adds translation amounts, so \(\chi_\infty\)
is a homomorphism.
For a fixed \(\omega_0\in\Omega\),
\[
 \chi_\infty(g)=h(g\omega_0)-h(\omega_0),
\]
and the joint continuity just proved shows that it is continuous.

The character is independent of the origin and of the chosen
native conjugacy: if \(h'\) also satisfies
\eqref{eq:native-conjugacy}, then \(h'\circ h^{-1}\) commutes
with addition by \(1\), so is itself a translation.
Conjugating a translation by a translation does not change
its amount. Multiplication of coordinates by a unit
\(u\in\Zp^\times\) would instead change the native step to
\(+u\), and is compatible with \eqref{eq:native-conjugacy}
only if \(u=1\). There is therefore no sign or scalar
ambiguity at any finite depth.

\subsection{Transfer every finite quotient to all late cycles}

Fix \(j\ge1\) and define the continuous surjection
\[
 q_j:\Omega\longrightarrow\Z/p^j\Z,\qquad
 q_j(x)=h(x)\bmod p^j.
\]
Its finitely many fibers are compact and clopen.
They are permuted natively by \(+1\) and by Galois
translation by \(\chi_\infty(g)\bmod p^j\).
This quotient exists at every \(j\); there is no assumption
that a particular family of metric partitions attains every
cardinality \(p^j\). Equivalently, a cofinal inverse system
of cyclic partitions suffices, since any partition of size
\(p^k\), \(k\ge j\), maps to the quotient modulo \(p^j\).

Different fibers of \(q_j\) have positive minimum distance:
the distance function attains its minimum on their compact
product, and disjointness makes that minimum nonzero.
Choose \(\delta_j>0\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-separation}
 x,y\in\Omega,\ |x-y|<\delta_j
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad q_j(x)=q_j(y).
\end{equation}
Full-sequence Hausdorff convergence supplies \(E_j\ge j\)
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-threshold}
 d_H(S_e,\Omega)<\delta_j\qquad(e\ge E_j).
\end{equation}
This threshold is chosen before, and independently of, \(g\).

For each such \(e\), define \(q_{e,j}:S_e\to\Z/p^j\Z\)
by choosing \(x\in\Omega\) with \(|\alpha-x|<\delta_j\)
and setting \(q_{e,j}(\alpha)=q_j(x)\).
Two choices of \(x\) are at mutual distance less than
\(\delta_j\), by the ultrametric inequality; hence
\eqref{eq:delta-separation} makes the definition independent
of the choice. The other directed Hausdorff bound in
\eqref{eq:uniform-threshold} proves surjectivity.

All points in \(C\) lie on \(|z|=|s|^r<1\), by continuity
of the norm and Lemma~\ref{lem:small}. On the open unit disk,
\[
 P(x)-P(y)=(x-y)(1+s+x+y),\qquad |1+s+x+y|=1.
\]
Thus \(P\) is an isometry there. If \(x\) is an allowed
choice for \(\alpha\), then \(Px\) is an allowed choice for
\(P\alpha\). Likewise \(gx\) is an allowed choice for
\(g\alpha\), since Galois acts isometrically and preserves
\(\Omega\). Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-equivariances}
 \begin{aligned}
 q_{e,j}(P\alpha)&=q_{e,j}(\alpha)+1,\\
 q_{e,j}(g\alpha)&=q_{e,j}(\alpha)+\chi_\infty(g)\bmod p^j.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
There is no need to choose nearby points globally consistently;
choice independence was proved first.
On the other hand, the definition of \(\rho_e\) and the
first identity give
\[
 q_{e,j}(g\alpha)
 =q_{e,j}(P^{\circ\rho_e(g)}\alpha)
 =q_{e,j}(\alpha)+\rho_e(g)\bmod p^j.
\]
Comparison with the second identity in
\eqref{eq:two-equivariances} proves
\eqref{eq:eventual-intro} simultaneously for every \(g\in G_K\).
It is exact equality of characters, not just an abstract
isomorphism of finite cyclic fields or a subsequence limit.

\subsection{Surjectivity and the algebraic kernel fields}

The image \(H=\chi_\infty(G_K)\) is compact and hence closed
in \(\Zp\), since \(G_K\) is profinite.
Taking \(j=1\) and a sufficiently late \(e\) in
\eqref{eq:eventual-intro} shows that its reduction modulo
\(p\) is the nonzero native first character.
Thus \(H\) contains a \(p\)-adic unit \(u\).
It contains all integer multiples of \(u\), which are
dense in \(\Zp\), and closedness yields \(H=\Zp\).

For every \(j\), \(\chi_\infty\bmod p^j\) is a continuous
surjection onto the cyclic group of order \(p^j\). Its
kernel is open and normal, so Galois correspondence
\cite[Section 9.22]{stacksgalois} gives
\[
 K_j=(K^\sep)^{\ker(\chi_\infty\bmod p^j)},\qquad
 [K_j:K]=p^j.
\]
For \(e\ge j\), the unique degree-\(p^j\) subfield of
\(L_e\) is
\[
 F_{e,j}=L_e^{\langle\sigma_e^{p^j}\rangle}
 =(K^\sep)^{\ker(\rho_e\bmod p^j)}.
\]
The proved character equality therefore gives
\[
 K_j=F_{e,j}\qquad(e\ge E_j)
\]
as actual subfields of the chosen separable closure.
The kernels for \(j+1\) lie in the kernels for \(j\), so
\(K_j\subset K_{j+1}\).
Restrictions on Galois groups are the ordinary reductions
modulo \(p^j\), induced by the same character. Consequently
\[
 \Gal\left(\bigcup_{j\ge1}K_j\,/\,K\right)
 \simeq\varprojlim_j\Z/p^j\Z=\Zp,
\]
with quotient map \(\chi_\infty\).
If a second continuous character satisfied
\eqref{eq:eventual-intro}, choose \(e\) beyond both thresholds
at each \(j\). Their reductions both equal
\(\rho_e\bmod p^j\); equality at every \(j\) gives equality
in \(\Zp\). This proves uniqueness and completes
Theorem~\ref{thm:tower}.

In particular \(K_1=F_2\), by choosing \(e\) beyond \(E_1\)
and using Proposition~\ref{prop:as-stable}.
It is not \(L_1\), since \(L_1\cap L_2=K\).
For larger \(j\), the theorem fixes the quotient degree
first and only then takes the cycle level sufficiently
large. It does not imply containment of a prescribed
full \(L_j\) in all later fields.

Finally, the points of \(\Omega\) are classical elements
of the completed algebraic closure, not necessarily
algebraic over \(K\). No finite Galois correspondence has
been applied to such a coordinate, and no theorem
identifying fixed fields inside the completion is used.
Every field above is defined by a continuous character
kernel inside \(K^\sep\).
