\section{Normal forms and full periodic algebras}
\label{sec:algebra}

We first separate two statements that need not coincide at a
multiple return root: a polynomial can vanish at every ordinary
root of $F_n$ without being zero in $k[x]/(F_n)$. The coefficient
arguments will take place in the latter, full algebra.

\begin{lemma}[Polynomial normal form]
\label{lem:normal}
For
\[
 \Vd=k\oplus\bigoplus_{\substack{r\ge1\\d\nmid r}}kx^r
\]
one has the direct sum of $k$-vector spaces
$k[x]=\Bf\oplus\Vd$. Thus every polynomial has a unique normal
part $v$ in a decomposition $h=\Deltaf Q+v$. The elimination
does not increase degree, and $Q$ is unique up to a constant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $t\ge1$, the polynomial
$\Deltaf x^t=(x^d+c)^t-x^t$ is monic of degree $dt$.
Eliminate the largest positive exponent divisible by $d$ using
the corresponding multiple of this polynomial, and continue.
Each subtraction introduces only smaller exponents, so the
process terminates without increasing degree. This proves existence.
If $Q$ is nonconstant, then $\deg\Deltaf Q=d\deg Q$,
whereas a nonzero element of $\Vd$ has degree zero or a positive
degree not divisible by $d$. Their intersection is therefore zero.
The same degree argument shows that the kernel of $\Deltaf$ on
$k[x]$ is the constants. For $h=0$, take $Q=v=0$.
\end{proof}

Telescoping gives $\Bf\subseteq\Kf$. It also shows that on a
root $a$ of $F_n$ of native period $r$,
\begin{equation}
 H_n(h)(a)=(n/r)S_h(O_a),
 \label{eq:ordinary-repetition}
\end{equation}
where $n/r$ is an integer counting repetitions. This formula is
not division in $k$. In particular, $h\in\Kf$ implies ordinary-root
vanishing of every $H_n(h)$.

\begin{lemma}[Cyclic digit basis]
\label{lem:basis}
For $n\ge1$, with indices taken modulo $n$, set
\[
 \cA_n=k[X_0,\ldots,X_{n-1}]/
       (X_i^d+c-X_{i+1}:0\le i<n).
\]
The substitutions $X_i\mapsto f^{\circ i}(x)$ and
$x\mapsto X_0$ identify $\cA_n$ with $k[x]/(F_n)$. The
monomials
\begin{equation}
 X^{\mathbf e}=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}X_i^{e_i},\qquad
                  0\le e_i<d,
 \label{eq:digit-basis}
\end{equation}
are a basis, including when $F_n$ is not squarefree.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first $n-1$ relations eliminate all variables except $X_0$;
the last becomes $F_n(X_0)=0$. The substitutions are inverse,
and the dimension is $d^n$. Replacing a factor $X_i^d$ by
$X_{i+1}-c$ strictly lowers total degree in each resulting term,
including at wraparound. These replacements terminate at the
$d^n$ monomials in \eqref{eq:digit-basis}. They span and their
number equals the dimension, proving linear independence.
\end{proof}

Write $[M]U$ for the coefficient of a digit-basis monomial $M$
in $U\in\cA_n$. We use the notation
\begin{equation}
 \cH_n(v)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}v(X_i),\qquad
 \Pi_n=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}X_i^{d-1}.
 \label{eq:class-notation}
\end{equation}
The first is the class of $H_n(v)$. Telescoping and the chain
rule give, respectively,
\begin{equation}
 \cH_n(\Deltaf Q)=0,\qquad [F_n']=d^n\Pi_n-1.
 \label{eq:telescoping-jacobian}
\end{equation}
Integer coefficients such as $d^n$ are interpreted in $k$;
digits and reduction exponents remain integers.

\begin{lemma}[Leading-digit detection]
\label{lem:leading}
Let $v\in\Vd$ have positive leading degree $D$ and leading
coefficient $a_D\ne0$. Put $m=\lfloor\log_dD\rfloor$, and
write $D=\sum_{j=0}^m e_jd^j$ in base $d$. For $n>2m$,
the digit monomial $M_D=\prod_{j=0}^mX_j^{e_j}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 [M_D]\cH_n(v)=a_D.
 \label{eq:leading-coefficient}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Reduce a term $X_i^r$, where $r\le D<d^{m+1}$, in the forward
window $X_i,\ldots,X_{i+m}$, with local weights
$1,d,\ldots,d^m$. Choosing the next variable in
$X_{i+j}^d=X_{i+j+1}-c$ preserves weight; choosing the constant
strictly lowers it. No term can reach $X_{i+m}^d$, whose weight
would exceed $r$. The reduction stays inside a window of distinct
variables. For a fixed reduction order, the unique branch choosing
no constants produces the base-$d$ digits of $r$ with coefficient
one and weight $r$. Every other branch has smaller weight.

Normality gives $e_0>0$, and positivity gives $e_m>0$.
For $m\ge1$, only the circular window starting at zero can
contain both endpoints $0,m$: the other directed distance
$n-m$ exceeds $m$. If $m=0$, the same assertion holds for
singleton windows. Thus only source zero can reach $M_D$.
Its target weight is $D$, so only $a_DX_0^D$ contributes,
and it contributes exactly $a_D$. Constants have empty support.
This proves \eqref{eq:leading-coefficient}, regardless of any
cancellation among smaller-weight terms.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Multiplicity-safe certificates]
\label{lem:root-certificates}
Let $G\ne0$ and $U$ belong to $k[x]$. If $U$ vanishes at
every ordinary root of $G$, then for every integer $E\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 G\mid D^{[E]}G\,U^E.
 \label{eq:hasse-necessary}
\end{equation}
In particular $G\mid G'U$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At a root $a$ of multiplicity $e$, write $G=(x-a)^eV$ with
$V(a)\ne0$. The coefficient-of-$z^E$ product rule gives
\[
 \ord_a D^{[E]}G\ge\max(e-E,0).
\]
The factor $U^E$ has order at least $E$, so the product has
order at least $e$. Check this at each root of $G$ to obtain
the divisibility. Vanishing Hasse coefficients only increase
the relevant order and cause no exception.
\end{proof}

Neither this lemma nor \cref{lem:leading} assumes reducedness.
The first gives a necessary passage from ordinary roots to the
full algebra. Its converse at one level is not required; the
two-level arguments below supply the stronger finite equivalence.
