\section{Eliminating unknown exceptions}\label{sec:decision}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
The equivalence of $\CP$ and $I_g\ne(0)$ is
Proposition~\ref{prop:ideal}. Suppose $I_g\ne(0)$. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:period}, every point of $\Bcal$ is a root
of some $F_r$ with $1\le r\le D$. Hence $S_*$ vanishes on
$\Bcal$, and~\eqref{eq:annihilator} gives $S_*\in I_g$.
Conversely, $S_*\in I_g$ implies $I_g\ne(0)$ because $S_*$
is nonzero.

The degree of this specified polynomial is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:star-degree}
 \ell_* =\sum_{r=1}^{D}d^r
        =\frac{d^{D+1}-d}{d-1}<d^{D+1}.
\end{equation}
Thus the integer in~\eqref{eq:ks} satisfies $k_{S_*}\le D+1$.
Its fixed-annihilator horizon from Theorem~\ref{thm:fixed} is at
most
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finalhorizon}
 k_{S_*}+4b^2\le D+1+2D=3D+1=N.
\end{equation}
If the stated tests through $N$ pass, they include the entire
fixed-$S_*$ horizon, so $S_*\in I_g$. That membership plainly
implies every finite test. This proves all four equivalences.
Proposition~\ref{prop:period} gives the asserted visible-period
bound. Finally, Lemma~\ref{lem:monic} transports the conclusions
back to every nonmonic input without changing the stated tests.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Support, constants, and what the bound measures}

The numerator and denominator remain whole products throughout
the argument. No individual factor is required to satisfy $\CP$,
and no support multiplicity is reduced modulo $p$. A support cycle
containing both a numerator zero and a denominator zero has both
products equal to zero at every return. It belongs to no $\Bcal_n$
and is a permissible finite exception to admissibility. Its period
is not bounded by Proposition~\ref{prop:period}. A cycle containing
only one type of zero is product-visible, so its period is bounded
by $D$ when $\CP$ holds. Both cases were retained in
Proposition~\ref{prop:ideal} and in the persistence proof; the
universal polynomial $S_*$ does not silently impose a period bound
on every support cycle.

For $m=0$, the formulas give $a=0$, $b=1$, $D=2$, and $N=7$.
The low state range is the single state zero; no separate
classification of constant weights is needed. In characteristic
two, $-1=1$ is still nonzero, so the simple-root and two-product
arguments continue unchanged. All return integers $1,\ldots,N$
are tested, not only prime periods or periods prime to $p$.

This is a finite decision rather than only a necessary condition:
failure of a specified remainder proves that $\CP$ fails, and
passing all of them proves $\CP$. Computation can use composition,
multiplication, and Euclidean remainders in a finite field
containing the finitely many input coefficients. No such tests or
mathematical experiments are needed for the proof here. The
polynomial $S_*$ and the return polynomials can have large degree;
no field-size-independent running time, optimal cutoff, or
practical census claim is made.
