## 1. Claim, status and exact scope

Let $\operatorname{TA}_2(\mathbb Z)$ denote the subgroup generated by integral affine automorphisms and integral polynomial shears. Every element has an integral polynomial inverse. Define

$$
\mathcal R=\{M^{-1}\rho_QM:M\in\operatorname{TA}_2(\mathbb Z),\ Q\in\mathbb Z[t]\},
\qquad \rho_Q(u,v)=(u,Q(u)-v).
$$

The two claims proved here are:

1. Every $R\in\mathcal R$ preserving the fixed set $C$ below induces one of exactly 27 **candidate permutations**, described explicitly in Section 5. This is an upper set, not a claim that all 27 lift.
2. If an integral tame automorphism induces a nine-cycle on $C$, at least nine different candidate permutations are realized by members of $\mathcal R$ preserving $C$.

**Status of these two claims: PROVABLE AS STATED.**

The additional bounded task tests preservation of point-difference gcd ideals as a necessary condition for a genuine integral tame lift. Every candidate passes. Section 8 also proves that ordinary integral polynomial interpolation cannot distinguish them here. A particular candidate would immediately give a native integer nine-cycle if it lifted, but that global tame lift is **NOT CURRENTLY JUSTIFIED**. No candidate permutation is substituted for an actual automorphism.

## 4. A local fixed-curve constraint

**Lemma.** Three fixed points of $\rho_Q$ that are congruent modulo two cannot be affinely independent after subtracting one of them, dividing their differences by two, and reducing modulo two.

**Proof.** Take the first fixed point $(u_0,v_0)$. Every other point in its modulo-two class is $(u_0+2s,v_0+2t)$ with $s,t\in\mathbb Z$. Expand

$$
Q(u_0+X)=Q(u_0)+Q'(u_0)X+q_2X^2+q_3X^3+\cdots,
$$

where all $q_j$ are integers. The fixed-point equations $2v=Q(u)$ give

$$
2t=sQ'(u_0)+2q_2s^2+4q_3s^3+\cdots.
$$

If $Q'(u_0)$ is odd, reduction modulo two forces $s=0$ modulo two, so all normalized fixed points lie on that line. If $Q'(u_0)$ is even, division by two and reduction modulo two give

$$
t=\bigl(Q'(u_0)/2+q_2\bigr)s\pmod2,
$$

using $s^2=s$ in $\mathbb F_2$. They again lie on one line. In both cases the first normalized point is the origin, so two other normalized differences cannot be independent. $\square$

For any integral polynomial automorphism $M$ and any $P,z\in\mathbb Z^2$, Taylor expansion gives

$$
\frac{M(P+2z)-M(P)}2\equiv DM(P)z\pmod2.
$$

The matrix $DM(P)$ is invertible modulo two, by differentiating the identity for the integral polynomial inverse. Thus this normalized affine independence is preserved by $M$.

In each of the three actual fibers of $C$, it holds: taking the first listed label as anchor, the two normalized difference columns are respectively

$$
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

All three matrices have determinant $\pm1$. Therefore **no member of $\mathcal R$ can fix all three points of any one $B_j$**: its conjugating coordinates would contradict the lemma.

## 5. The 27 candidates

Let $R=M^{-1}\rho_QM\in\mathcal R$ preserve $C$. Modulo three, $\rho_Q$ has exactly three fixed points, because each of the three values of $u$ admits exactly one solution of $2v=Q(u)$. Its polynomial-automorphism conjugate $\overline R$ has the same count. The bijection $C\to\mathbb F_3^2$ intertwines the permutations, so $R|_C$ has exactly three fixed points, not merely at least three.

Modulo two, $R$ permutes the three nonempty fibers and induces an involution on them. If it exchanged two fibers, all its fixed points in $C$ would have to lie in the remaining fiber. The count of three would force it to fix that entire fiber, contradicting Section 4. Hence $R$ preserves each $B_j$ individually.

An involution on a three-element set has either three fixed points or one fixed point and one exchanged pair. The total fixed-point count is three, so every fiber must have exactly one fixed point and one exchanged pair. Define

$$
\mathcal K=\{\kappa_{f_0,f_1,f_2}:f_j\in B_j\},
$$

where $\kappa_{f_0,f_1,f_2}$ fixes $f_j$ and swaps the other two labels in $B_j$. These are $3^3=27$ distinct permutations. We have proved

$$
\{R|_C:R\in\mathcal R, R(C)=C\}\subseteq\mathcal K.
$$

This inclusion does not assert equality or supply a tame lift for an arbitrary member of $\mathcal K$.

## 6. A nine-cycle requires nine different reflection restrictions

Suppose $T\in\operatorname{TA}_2(\mathbb Z)$ preserves $C$ and $\tau=T|_C$ is a nine-cycle. Its induced action on the three modulo-two fibers must be a three-cycle: if it fixed each fiber, each point's orbit would have length at most three. Thus $\tau^3$ is a three-cycle within each $B_j$.

For $j=0,\ldots,8$, set

$$
R_j=T^j I T^{-j}\in\mathcal R.
$$

These actual tame conjugates preserve $C$ and have restrictions $\tau^j i\tau^{-j}$. If two restrictions were equal, $i$ would commute with $\tau^d$ for some $1\le d\le8$. In the cyclic group of order nine, $\langle\tau^d\rangle$ contains $\tau^3$, whether $\gcd(d,9)$ is one or three. Hence $i$ would commute with $\tau^3$.

On a fiber, $i$ fixes one point. Commutation would make it fix the other two points obtained by applying the three-cycle $\tau^3$, contradicting its nontrivial transposition there. Therefore the nine restrictions are all different. Section 5 puts them in $\mathcal K$.

Consequently a native nine-cycle on this $C$ requires at least nine liftable candidates, and therefore at least six beyond the three known restrictions in Section 2. This is a necessary condition, not a construction of the nine lifts.

## 7. Selected arithmetic test: point-difference gcd ideals

For distinct integer points $P,Q$, define

$$
\mathfrak d(P,Q)=(P_x-Q_x,P_y-Q_y)\subseteq\mathbb Z.
$$

An integral polynomial map sends each coordinate difference into this ideal, by expanding differences of monomials. If the map has an integral polynomial inverse, applying that inverse gives the reverse inclusion. Thus every integral tame automorphism preserves $\mathfrak d(P,Q)$ exactly.

For this specific $C$, direct differences give

$$
\mathfrak d(P_j,P_k)=
\begin{cases}
2\mathbb Z,&j,k\text{ in the same }B_s,\ j\ne k,\\
\mathbb Z,&j,k\text{ in different fibers}.
\end{cases}
$$

The nine within-fiber pairs have differences twice primitive vectors, as follows from the three matrices in Section 4 and their column differences. To verify all 27 cross-fiber pairs explicitly, the following matrices list the absolute coordinate differences, with rows and columns in the fixed fiber orders:

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
B_0\text{ versus }B_1&(1,0)&(1,2)&(1,0)\\
&(1,2)&(1,4)&(1,2)\\
&(1,2)&(3,4)&(3,2)
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
B_0\text{ versus }B_2&(1,1)&(3,1)&(1,1)\\
&(1,1)&(3,1)&(1,3)\\
&(1,1)&(1,1)&(1,3)
\end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
B_1\text{ versus }B_2&(0,1)&(2,1)&(0,1)\\
&(2,3)&(4,3)&(2,1)\\
&(2,1)&(4,1)&(2,1)
\end{array}.
$$

Every displayed pair has gcd one. Every member of $\mathcal K$ preserves the fibers, so **all 27 candidates pass this necessary test**. It excludes none and proves no tame lift.

## 8. Why ordinary integral polynomial interpolation also fails to discriminate

In fact the exact image of evaluation $\mathbb Z[x,y]\to\mathbb Z^C$ is

$$
\mathcal L=\{(v_1,\ldots,v_9):v_j\equiv v_k\pmod2
\text{ whenever }j,k\in B_s\}.
$$

Necessity is polynomial congruence preservation. For sufficiency, on each fiber the two normalized difference columns form a unimodular matrix. Given three values congruent modulo two, subtract the anchor value and divide the two other value differences by two. Solving the resulting unimodular two-by-two system gives an integral affine polynomial realizing those three values on that fiber.

These three affine polynomials can be joined exactly over $\mathbb Z$. For a point $P$, let $\mathfrak m_P=(x-P_x,y-P_y)$. For points in different fibers, Section 7 gives $\mathfrak m_P+\mathfrak m_Q=\mathbb Z[x,y]$. Put $J_s=\bigcap_{P\in B_s}\mathfrak m_P$. The ideals $J_s$ are pairwise comaximal: if ideals $U_1,U_2$ are each comaximal with $J$, multiplying representatives congruent to one modulo $J$ shows that $U_1U_2$, and hence $U_1\cap U_2$, is comaximal with $J$; iterate this argument over the finite intersections on both sides.

For each $s$ and each $t\ne s$, choose a polynomial representative equal to one modulo $J_s$ and zero modulo $J_t$, and multiply these representatives over $t\ne s$. This gives an integral selector $e_s$ equal to one at all points of $B_s$ and zero at the other fibers. Multiplying each fiber's affine interpolant by $e_s$ and summing proves sufficiency and the displayed characterization of $\mathcal L$.

It follows that any permutation of $C$ preserving the fiber partition, including every candidate in $\mathcal K$, can be realized on $C$ by a pair of integral polynomials: apply the characterization separately to its target $x$ and $y$ coordinate values. Its inverse permutation can also be interpolated. These separately interpolated maps need not be global inverses, need not have constant unit Jacobian, and need not be tame automorphisms. This calculation pinpoints why finite polynomial-value interpolation cannot supply the missing global lift.

$$

It fixes labels $1,2,4$ and differs from each of the three known reflection restrictions. It passes the gcd test and has the ordinary integral polynomial interpolation just proved. Direct permutation multiplication gives

$$

\kappa i a=(1\ 2\ 3\ 9\ 7\ 8\ 5\ 6\ 4).

Therefore, **if** an actual $K\in\mathcal R$ preserving $C$ and restricting to $\kappa$ can be constructed, the complete integral tame word $KIA$ has a native least-period-nine orbit on the nine displayed integer points. No factor-step or augmented clock is involved.

That existence is not proved here. The required additional result is an explicit integral tame coordinate map $M$ and $Q\in\mathbb Z[t]$ realizing $M^{-1}\rho_QM|_C=\kappa$, or a valid global obstruction ruling out this lift. A nine-element permutation and a coordinatewise interpolating polynomial map do neither. No candidate has been established tame-liftable or nonliftable beyond the earlier three explicit examples.
