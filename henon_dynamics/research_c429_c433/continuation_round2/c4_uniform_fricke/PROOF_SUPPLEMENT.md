# NI: uniform separation of nodal inertia from higher native periods

2026-09-09. Author proof, submitted for independent review. The first-pass
FG2 theorem is an accepted input; none of its incidence, Galois-image or
constant-field arguments is rerun here. The original all-level FGT question
remains open.

## Status

**PROVABLE AS STATED — author proof; independent whole-proof review
pending.** NI survives unchanged from its freeze. It is a uniform
auxiliary theorem, not a closure of the larger FGT claim.

## Assumptions

The base parameters are algebraically independent in characteristic
zero, the map and native-period convention are those fixed in §1,
and the generic geometric points are considered in one algebraic
closure of the base function field. The accepted FG2 local theorem
and the classical generic intersection-length theorem are the two
substantive inputs stated precisely in §1. No higher-layer group or
constant-field maximality is assumed.

## Proof strategy

Degenerate the fixed incidence to the Cayley fibre. Account separately
for its smooth periodic points and its four nodes, then saturate the
generic intersection degree. Pass from the resulting complete set of
higher exact-period étale germs to the normal splitting field.

## Dependency map

1. Section 2 proves that the singular-fibre locus is one geometrically
   irreducible divisor; this is used only in the global-divisor passage.
2. The classical torus quotient plus hyperbolicity gives all smooth
   Cayley point counts and nondegenerate returns in §3.
3. The parameter-family iterate identity and accepted FG2 give the
   complete lower-period contribution of the nodes in §4.
4. The classical length bound and §§3–4 give all-sheet exhaustion and
   reducedness in §5; no properness of the affine fixed incidence is
   assumed.
5. Uniqueness of the nondegenerate returns gives native-period
   persistence in §6. Integral closure under étale base change then
   gives the entire splitting-cover statement in §7.
6. Closedness of finite-cover branch images, §2 and FG2 give the
   generic-divisor and finite-joint inertia statement in §8.

## 1. Statement, fields and imported inputs

Put
$$
B=\mathbb A^4_{\mathbb Q},\quad k=\mathbb Q(A,B,C,D),\qquad
K=x^2+y^2+z^2-xyz-Ax-By-Cz.
$$
The reuse of $B$ as the name of the base and its second coordinate is
traditional; a parameter is always written $(A,B,C,D)$. Let
$\mathcal S=\{K=D\}\to B$ and $T=s_zs_ys_x$, rightmost first, with
$$
s_x(x,y,z)=(A+yz-x,y,z)
$$
and the analogous $s_y,s_z$. Each involution fixes all base parameters.
For $n\ge1$, $P_n$ means the ordinary geometric affine points of exact
least native $T$-period $n$ on the generic fibre; $L_n/k$ is their splitting
field, generated by every coordinate of every point. An empty layer has
splitting field $k$. Write $\bar k=\overline{\mathbb Q}(A,B,C,D)$ and
$\bar L_n=\bar kL_n$. Let $\Delta\subset B$ be the reduced singular-fibre
discriminant and $b_C=(0,0,0,4)$.

**Theorem NI.** For every integer $n\ge3$, the normalization of
$B_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}$ in $\bar L_n$ is étale over $b_C$ and is
unramified at the generic point of $\Delta_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}$.
Consequently, for every finite $N\ge2$, an inertia group at $\Delta$ in
$L_2L_3\cdots L_N/k$ has order two. Its nonidentity element flips exactly
the two phases of one native two-cycle, and fixes every point in every
$P_n$ with $3\le n\le N$.

The claim includes all sheets of each finite splitting cover. The open
étale neighborhood is allowed to depend on $n$ or $N$. No simultaneous
neighborhood for an infinite tower, higher-layer maximal group, or
linear disjointness assertion is included.

Two accepted/source-owned inputs are used:

**FG2 input.** The [accepted first-pass proof](../../lanes/c4_fricke_galois/REPORT.md),
§§3.7–3.8, gives $P_1=\varnothing$, $|P_2|=22$ and
$\operatorname{Gal}(L_2/k)=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar L_2/\bar k)
=C_2^{11}\rtimes S_{11}$. At each Cayley node, its ordered-point
incidence has exactly two generic local sheets, of native period two,
with simple phase ramification. Their cycle quotient is étale. The four
local branch graphs have distinct tangent forms, and a meridian of one
graph acts as a single phase flip on the entire $P_2$ layer.

**Intersection-length input.** Set
$$
u_n=(2+\sqrt5)^n+(2-\sqrt5)^n,\qquad
a_n=u_n+4(-1)^n.                                      \tag{1}
$$
The generic affine fixed scheme of $T^n$ is zero-dimensional of length
$a_n$. This is the graph-intersection calculation of
[Iwasaki–Uehara, Lemmas 8.1–8.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0512583v3),
pp. 21–23: the projective intersection is $a_n+2$, with two boundary
contributions of one. Their coordinate convention changes by
$(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(-x,y,z)$ and
$(\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3,\theta_4)=(-A,B,C,-D)$.
Reversing the Coxeter word transposes the graph and preserves the count.
Only length is imported, not reducedness on every smooth fibre; their
Painlevé return has twice our native clock.

In particular, the ordinary generic counts used below will be proved
by producing distinct sheets that exhaust (1), not assumed from a
formal Möbius degree or from the source's cardinality notation.

## 2. The singular-fibre divisor is geometrically irreducible

The universal critical incidence has the parametrization
$$
(x,y,z)\longmapsto
\left(2x-yz,\ 2y-xz,\ 2z-xy,
      -x^2-y^2-z^2+2xyz\right).                        \tag{2}
$$
Its source is $\mathbb A^3$. The family compactifies to
$$
W(x^2+y^2+z^2)-xyz-W^2(Ax+By+Cz)-DW^3=0
\quad\text{in }B\times\mathbb P^3.
$$
At $W=0$ a singular point would satisfy
$xy=xz=yz=0$ and $x^2+y^2+z^2=0$. These equations have no projective
solution in characteristic zero. Thus the relative projective singular
locus is closed and is entirely affine; it is precisely (2).

Its map to $B$ is proper. Each geometric fibre is a projective closed
subvariety contained in an affine variety, hence is zero-dimensional;
therefore the map is finite. Its image is closed and, because its source
is geometrically integral of dimension three, is a geometrically
irreducible hypersurface. This image is $\Delta$.

There is a dense open part of $\Delta$ whose fibre has exactly one
ordinary node. To see the nonemptiness explicitly, the parameter
$(0,0,0,0)$ has only the singular point $(0,0,0)$: the critical equations
with $A=B=C=0$ give either the origin on $D=0$ or the four points with
coordinates of absolute value two and product eight, on $D=4$.
The Hessian at the origin is $2I$. The finite critical map is locally
one-to-one there, since the derivative of its first three coordinates
is $2I$. This proves the stated dense-open claim.

At $b_C$ there are the four nodes
$$
p_s=(2\alpha,2\beta,2\gamma),
\qquad \alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\{1,-1\},\quad
\alpha\beta\gamma=1.                                  \tag{3}
$$
They are four branches of this one global irreducible divisor, not
four global discriminant components. The FG2 phase branch has
$\delta=0$, hence $x=X/2,y=Y/2,z=Z/2$ in its accepted coordinates.
Its equations then give exactly (2), so the four phase branch graphs
in accepted FG2 are the four local branches of $\Delta$ at $b_C$.

## 3. Every smooth Cayley periodic return is nondegenerate

The classical Cayley presentation is
$$
\pi:(\mathbb G_m)^2\longrightarrow S_{b_C},\qquad
\pi(u,v)=\left(u+u^{-1},v+v^{-1},uv+(uv)^{-1}\right).
                                                               \tag{4}
$$
It identifies $S_{b_C}$ with the quotient by
$\eta(u,v)=(u^{-1},v^{-1})$; the map is étale away from the four
two-torsion points, whose images are (3). This presentation and the
monomial action are source-owned
[Cantat–Loray, §2.1, pp. 12–13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0711.1579).
Negating all three trace coordinates changes their $+xyz$ convention
to ours. The native lift, with our fixed word order, is
$$
M=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-2\\-2&-3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\widetilde T(u,v)=(u^{-1}v^{-2},u^{-2}v^{-3}).            \tag{5}
$$
The exact lift is also an accepted input from the
[D2 proof package](../../lanes/d2_frobenius_native_bridge/PROOF_PACKAGE.md),
Step 1; it is not a Frobenius map. Here $\det M=-1$, $M\equiv I\pmod2$,
and its eigenvalues are
$$
\mu_1=-2-\sqrt5,\qquad \mu_2=-2+\sqrt5.
$$
Neither eigenvalue has complex absolute value one.

Suppose that $p=\pi(t)$ is smooth and $T^n(p)=p$. Exactly one of
$M^nt=t$ and $M^nt=t^{-1}$ holds. In logarithmic coordinates near $t$,
the tangent return on the quotient is respectively $M^n$ or $-M^n$.
Indeed, in the second case one composes the lift with $\eta$ to return
to the original local chart. All return eigenvalues therefore have
the form $\varepsilon\mu_i^n$, with $\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}$, and
none equals one. Thus
$$
\det\left(D(T^n|_{S_{b_C}})_p-I\right)\ne0.             \tag{6}
$$

Since $\mathcal S\to B$ is smooth of relative dimension two at $p$,
the algebraic implicit-function criterion applied to its relative
fixed-point equations says that the fixed incidence of $T^n$ is
étale over $B$ at $(b_C,p)$. Equivalently, $p$ has a unique étale
continuation as a fixed point of $T^n$ over the strict henselization
of the base at $b_C$.

We can count these smooth Cayley points without multiplicity. The
kernels of the torus homomorphisms $M^n-I$ and $M^n+I$ have orders
$$
A_n=|\det(M^n-I)|,\qquad B_n=|\det(M^n+I)|.
$$
This is the elementary Smith-normal-form count for an isogeny of
$(\mathbb G_m)^2$ in characteristic zero. Their intersection is
exactly the four two-torsion points, since $M\equiv I\pmod2$.
The other quotient fibres have size two. Therefore the number of
ordinary Cayley fixed points is $(A_n+B_n)/2$.

Now $\operatorname{tr}(M^n)=(-1)^nu_n$ and
$\det(M^n)=(-1)^n$. For odd $n$, $A_n=B_n=u_n$; for even $n$,
$A_n=u_n-2$ and $B_n=u_n+2$. We have proved
$$
\#\operatorname{Fix}(T^n|_{S_{b_C}})=u_n,
\qquad
\#\operatorname{Fix}(T^n|_{S_{b_C}^{\rm sm}})=u_n-4.      \tag{7}
$$
All four omitted nodes are fixed already by $T$. Every point counted
in the second expression gives one distinct étale branch by (6).

## 4. A uniform iterate-ideal lemma at the nodes

We require an identity for the whole parameter family, not merely a
calculation on the central fibre.

**Lemma 4.1.** Let $f_a$ be a polynomial or analytic map in state
variables $z=(z_1,\ldots,z_r)$, with parameters $a$ fixed by iteration,
and suppose $f_{a_0}(z_0)=z_0$. For every $m\ge1$ there is a matrix of
local functions $Q_m(a,z)$ such that
$$
f_a^m(z)-z=Q_m(a,z)\bigl(f_a(z)-z\bigr),\qquad
Q_m(a_0,z_0)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(Df_{a_0,z_0})^j.           \tag{8}
$$
If the displayed matrix at the central point is invertible, the ideals
generated by $f_a^m(z)-z$ and by $f_a(z)-z$ coincide in the local ring
there, including the parameter variables.

**Proof.** Write
$$
f_a^m(z)-z=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}
\left(f_a^j(f_a(z))-f_a^j(z)\right).
$$
For each coordinate of $f_a^j$, divide its difference at two arguments
successively by the coordinate differences. This gives a matrix
$Q_{m}$ as in (8). At two equal arguments its value is the sum of the
Jacobians of $f_a^j$, namely the matrix shown. If its determinant is a
unit, multiplication by $Q_m$ does not change the generated ideal.
$\square$

At the node $(2,2,2)$, the state derivative of $T$ is
$$
J=\begin{pmatrix}
-1&2&2\\-2&3&6\\-6&10&15
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\det(tI-J)=(t+1)(t^2-18t+1).                            \tag{9}
$$
The matrix follows by multiplying the three involution derivatives
at their common fixed point. Conjugacy by an even number of sign
changes gives the same spectrum at all four nodes. Thus the state
eigenvalues are $-1,\lambda,\lambda^{-1}$, with
$\lambda=9+4\sqrt5>1$.

For odd $n$, the geometric sum in (8), with $f=T,m=n$, has nonzero
values on each of these three eigenvalues. At $-1$ it equals one.
For even $n=2m$, apply the lemma to $f=T^2$: its eigenvalues are
$1,\lambda^2,\lambda^{-2}$, and the corresponding sum is nonzero
at every eigenvalue (its value at one is $m$). Consequently, at each
$(b_C,p_s)$ the full relative fixed-incidence germs satisfy
$$
\operatorname{Fix}(T^n)=
\begin{cases}
\operatorname{Fix}(T),& n\text{ odd},\\
\operatorname{Fix}(T^2),& n\text{ even}.
\end{cases}                                             \tag{10}
$$
Here the identity first holds in the state variables and $(A,B,C)$;
adjoining $D$ and imposing $K=D$ preserves the equality of ideals.
Thus it genuinely controls parameter deformations through the node.

The fixed locus of $T$ is the critical incidence (2): equality of the
first, then second, then third updated coordinates forces the three
critical equations. It has no component dominating $B$. Hence the odd
case in (10) contributes no generic point sheets. In the even case,
the accepted FG2 local model contributes precisely two generic sheets
at each node, both of native period two. No higher native period is
created by iteration at any node, uniformly in $n$.

## 5. Degree exhaustion rules out remote sheets

Fix $n\ge1$. The $u_n-4$ smooth Cayley points in (7) give that many
distinct generic geometric points, by their disjoint étale local
branches. When $n$ is even, (10) and FG2 give eight additional distinct
generic points specializing to the four nodes; when $n$ is odd, they
give none. These constructed points are distinct because their central
specializations are distinct, except for the two phases at each node,
which FG2 proves are distinct generically. Their total number is
$$
u_n-4+8\,\mathbf 1_{2\mid n}=u_n+4(-1)^n=a_n.           \tag{11}
$$
Each geometric point of a finite characteristic-zero fixed scheme
has length at least one. The generic fixed scheme has total length
$a_n$ by (1). Therefore (11) exhausts its entire length: it is reduced,
has exactly $a_n$ ordinary points, and has no additional generic point
or generic nilpotent contribution.

This also addresses possible escape to infinity. A hypothetical
generic sheet not approaching one of the exhibited affine branches
would add a geometric point to the already exhausted generic degree.
The argument does not rely on affine incidence being proper and does
not infer global exhaustion just from the local no-resonance lemma.
It uses all $u_n-4$ smooth branches and the eight lower-period sheets,
as well as the independently known total length.

For precision about a moving parameter: the smooth branches are
algebraic étale germs, not just numeric solutions on one nearby fibre.
Take a common pointed étale neighborhood for these finitely many
smooth germs. Then place the finitely many ramified FG2 algebraic
phase germs in an algebraic closure of its fraction field. The phase
germs themselves are not étale at $b_C$. This exhibits all the points
in one geometric generic fibre, where the degree comparison (11)
is made. It holds separately for each $n$.

## 6. Exact native periods persist, and all higher sheets are étale

Let a smooth Cayley point $p$ have least native period $d\mid n$.
By (6), it has a unique étale continuation as a fixed point of $T^d$.
That continuation is fixed by $T^n$ as well, so uniqueness for $T^n$
identifies the two local sections. None of its proper positive
iterates below $d$ equals it at the central point. Removing the
finitely many corresponding equality loci preserves these
inequalities. The continued section has exact native period $d$.

Conversely, every generic fixed point of $T^n$ is one of the branches
exhausted in §5. Branches from smooth Cayley points have the same
least period as their specializations, and node branches have least
period two. It follows immediately that for $n\ge3$ every generic
$P_n$ point extends to a smooth Cayley point of exact native period
$n$, and these specializations are all distinct.

As a count check, ordinary Möbius inversion is now justified by
reducedness and least-period decomposition. If $p_n=|P_n|$, then
$$
p_n=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)a_d.
$$
The elementary identity
$$
\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)(-1)^d
=2\mathbf 1_{n=2}-\mathbf 1_{n=1}
$$
follows from $(-1)^d=2\mathbf 1_{2\mid d}-1$. Consequently
$$
p_1=0,\qquad p_2=22,\qquad
p_n=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)u_d\quad(n\ge3).              \tag{12}
$$
For $n\ge3$, the last expression is also the ordinary number of exact
native period-$n$ Cayley points; all such points are smooth. Formula
(12) is a consequence of the sheet argument, not its substitute.

## 7. From all étale point germs to the entire splitting cover

We record carefully why ordinary local branches control the normal
splitting cover. It would be incorrect to argue that one unramified
root of a nonnormal finite field extension excludes other ramified
roots above the same parameter.

Work over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$, set
$R=\mathcal O_{B_{\overline{\mathbb Q}},b_C}$ and let $R^{\rm sh}$ be
its strict henselization, with fraction field $K^{\rm sh}$.
$R^{\rm sh}$ is a normal domain
([Stacks, Lemma 15.46.6](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/06DI)). By §6,
all $p_n$ points of the geometric generic exact layer, for $n\ge3$,
occur as distinct sections with coordinates in $R^{\rm sh}$. This is
the algebraic étale lifting property at their distinct smooth central
points. Their coordinate functions generate the splitting field.
Thus this splitting field embeds in $K^{\rm sh}$ and all its conjugates
split there. Its normality is essential here.

Let $S$ be the integral closure of $R$ in $\bar L_n$; it is finite,
since $R$ is excellent. Integral closure in an algebra commutes with
filtered étale base change
([Stacks, Lemma 10.147.5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CBF)).
Also $\bar k\otimes_R R^{\rm sh}=K^{\rm sh}$: the localization on
the left is an algebraic domain over $\bar k$, hence is a field and
equals the fraction field of $R^{\rm sh}$. Since $\bar L_n/\bar k$
is finite Galois and splits over $K^{\rm sh}$, the normalized base
change is
$$
S\otimes_R R^{\rm sh}\simeq
\prod_{[\bar L_n:\bar k]} R^{\rm sh}.                   \tag{13}
$$
Indeed, its total generic algebra is the corresponding product of
$K^{\rm sh}$, and the integral closure of the normal domain
$R^{\rm sh}$ in each factor is $R^{\rm sh}$ itself. Formula (13)
is finite étale. By faithfully flat descent, $S/R$ is finite étale.
This proves that every point of the finite splitting normalization
above $b_C$ is étale, not just a selected unramified place.

The argument is unchanged if the generic incidence initially included
vertical lower-period components: §6 selects only the closure of the
generic exact-$n$ layer, and the splitting field is generated only by
those generic points. Such components do not enter (13).

## 8. The divisor conclusion and finite-joint inertia

For a finite normalization over $B_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}$, the image
of its non-étale locus is closed: the locus is closed and the map is
finite. If $\bar L_n$ ramified at the generic point of the irreducible
$\Delta_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}$, this closed image would contain all
of $\Delta_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}$, including $b_C$. Section 7 rules
this out for every $n\ge3$.

The same horizontal ramification index is one over $\mathbb Q$.
One may either descend étaleness from algebraically closed constants
or pass to the discrete valuation at the generic point of $\Delta$;
characteristic-zero constant extensions are unramified there and
do not alter its ramification index. Thus $L_n/k$ is unramified at
$\Delta$ for all $n\ge3$, without a claim that its constant field is
$\mathbb Q$.

FG2 gives simple ramification of $L_2$ along one local branch of
$\Delta$, with an inertia generator equal to a single phase flip.
Section 2 identifies that branch with the irreducible divisor, so the
generic-$\Delta$ inertia in $L_2$ has this conjugacy class. A finite
compositum of extensions unramified at a characteristic-zero discrete
valuation remains unramified. Therefore adjoining $L_3,\ldots,L_N$
neither enlarges this inertia nor changes its action on any higher
layer. Its restriction to $L_2$ is still nontrivial. The inertia group
of the compositum has exactly order two, with the asserted support.
This proves Theorem NI. $\square$

## 9. Interfaces, residual obligation and ownership

Let $V_2=C_2^{11}$ be the phase subgroup in accepted FG2. The NI
generator has phase-parity character $-1$ and cycle-sign character
$+1$. Its conjugates in the full finite tower project to the eleven
coordinate phase flips in $L_2$ while acting trivially on every higher
layer. In particular, for
$$
H_N=L_3\cdots L_N,\qquad J_N=L_2\cap H_N,
$$
one obtains the unconditional interface
$$
J_N\subseteq L_2^{V_2}.                                \tag{14}
$$
The [parallel D2 lane](../d2_fricke_layer_intersection/REPORT.md)
owns the subsequent normal-quotient and quadratic-intersection
analysis. NI does not prove that its remaining cycle-sign quadratic
is absent. It also does not supply higher native-cycle transitivity,
connectedness of the higher incidence, a higher-layer rotation, or
maximal higher-layer Galois groups. Those remain genuine FGT gaps.

The Cayley quotient, hyperbolic monomial model and intersection-length
sequence are prior work. FG2 and D2's exact lift are accepted earlier
batch inputs. The new deduction here is the uniform combination of
the parameter-family iterate identity, all-sheet degree exhaustion,
least-period persistence and finite-normalization argument giving NI.
It is not presented as a replacement paper contract or a worldwide
priority claim. Targeted searches for Fricke/Markoff periodic parameter
monodromy and discriminant ramification located no exact NI statement
in the inspected primary passages; that limited read scope is not a
novelty certificate.

The bounded author-side local audit passed §7's abstract lemma, and
specifically rejected the stronger non-Galois shortcut: a simple root
of $t=z^3-z^2$ at $z=1$ embeds $\mathbb C(z)$ in a strict henselian
fraction field at $t=0$, although the same field ramifies at $z=0$.
The full Galois splitting argument in (13) avoids that error. The audit
did not review the Cayley geometry, degree input or the complete NI
theorem, and is not the independent whole-proof acceptance.

Execution ledger: zero mathematical programs, old certificate reruns,
