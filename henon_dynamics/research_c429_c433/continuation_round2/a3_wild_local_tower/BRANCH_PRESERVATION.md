# Separate proof attack: branch preservation at the parabolic germ

2026-09-09 UTC. This is a new proof-only attack allocated by the coordinator. It does not modify or extend the two-run computational certificate under independent review. No additional mathematical execution is allocated or performed.

## Frozen exact claim

For every odd prime $p$ and every $e\ge1$, retain $R=k[[s]]$, $K=k((s))$, $k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p$, $n=p^e$, $m=p^{e-1}$, and the canonical small factor $M_e$ of [REPORT.md](REPORT.md). Put

$$
A=k[[s,z]],\qquad B_e=A/(M_e),\qquad
\Phi^*(s)=s,\qquad \Phi^*(z)=(1+s)z+z^2.
$$

The exact proposed lemma is:

> **(BP)** The local formal automorphism $\Phi^*$ preserves every minimal prime of $B_e$.

This is an all-odd-prime, all-level statement about this exact quadratic family and its native one-step clock. Success requires a proof of (BP), or a verified counterexample for this exact family. A generic counterexample for a different formal automorphism only refutes a proposed proof principle.

**Status: NOT CURRENTLY JUSTIFIED.** The elementary equivalence and intersection interfaces below are proved; branch preservation itself is not. In particular this file does not claim another proof of the certified pair or of the global dynatomic component question.

## Assumptions, strategy and dependency map

The accepted one-small-cycle theorem supplies $n$ distinct generic roots, one native orbit, and root valuation $(p-1)/p$. No local irreducibility is assumed. The first-pass torsor criterion identifies irreducibility with the desired full AS nonvanishing.

1. Identify the formal automorphism and its action on branches without assuming it fixes a branch.
2. Translate (BP) exactly into the missing local transitivity statement.
3. Test whether intersection with the two coordinate axes distinguishes a proper branch permutation.
4. Any subsequent successful proof must use additional information about the exact quadratic coefficients or higher intersections; tangency alone cannot establish (BP).

This geometric reformulation is a proof device, not a stronger replacement for the original (AS-full) claim. The already accepted existence, uniqueness and root slope are source-owned, and ordinary local intersection algebra is not claimed as a new general theorem.

## 1. Formal automorphism and branch action

The linear part of the displayed substitution has invertible determinant. More concretely, its coefficient of $z$ is the unit $1+s$, so recursive formal inversion in $R[[z]]$ constructs a unique inverse. Thus it is an automorphism of $A$. It is not asserted to have a polynomial inverse or to be a global automorphism of the affine plane.

The ring $B_e$ is free over $R$ of rank $n$, by the monic equation and formal division, and its generic fibre is the accepted finite étale algebra $E_e$. Evaluation of $M_e(P_s(z))$ at every generic root vanishes. Monic division by $M_e$ therefore has zero remainder over $K$, and hence also over $R$. The substitution descends to $B_e$; its $n$-th power is the identity, since this holds on every generic root and $B_e$ is $R$-torsion-free. Consequently the induced map on $B_e$ is an automorphism.

The ring $B_e$ is reduced: a nilpotent maps to zero in its reduced generic fibre and hence is $R$-torsion, which is impossible. Its minimal primes correspond to irreducible factors of $M_e$ over $K$ (or to their monic integral factors over $R$). The cyclic action is transitive on those factors because its action on all $n$ geometric roots is transitive.

Let their number be $t$, and let $q$ be their common degree. Then

$$
tq=n,\qquad q=p^h,\qquad t=p^{e-h},\qquad1\le h\le e.
\tag{1}
$$

The accepted slope forces $p\mid q$. The root-field Galois group is cyclic of order $q$, and its roots form one coset of the rotation subgroup generated by $P_s^{\circ t}$. These facts do not make $P_s$ itself a field automorphism when $t>1$.

Since the action on the set of minimal primes is transitive, it fixes each such prime if and only if $t=1$. Therefore

$$
(\mathrm{BP})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M_e\text{ irreducible over }K
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{red}_{\mathrm{AS}}(a_e)\ne0.
\tag{2}
$$

Equation (2) supplies an exact alternative formulation, not a proof of any of its three conditions.

## 2. Coordinate-axis intersections do not decide the branch count

For a reduced branch with prime ideal $(D)\subset A$, write $I(D,s)$ and $I(D,z)$ for the lengths of $A/(D,s)$ and $A/(D,z)$, respectively. Neither coordinate axis is a component: the reduction $M_e(z,0)=z^n$ and the nonzero constant term below ensure the lengths are finite.

At $z=0$, use the coefficient of $z$ in the iterate difference to obtain

$$
Q_e(0,s)=
\frac{(1+s)^n-1}{(1+s)^m-1}
=s^{n-m}.
$$

The complementary Hensel factor is a unit at $(s,z)=(0,0)$. Hence

$$
I(M_e,s)=n,\qquad I(M_e,z)=n-m.
\tag{3}
$$

Both axis ideals are preserved by $\Phi^*$, since $\Phi^*(s)=s$ and $\Phi^*(z)=z(1+s+z)$ with unit second factor. Its transitive action on the branches therefore makes both intersection lengths equal across branches. Additivity of intersection multiplicity in a product of distinct branch equations and (1)--(3) give, for each branch,

$$
I(D,s)=q=p^h,\qquad
I(D,z)=\frac{q(p-1)}p=p^{h-1}(p-1).
\tag{4}
$$

Thus their gcd is $p^{h-1}$. The initial Newton edge and these two axis intersections recover the already accepted divisibility by $p$, but do not force $h=e$. A higher-intersection or coefficient obstruction is genuinely missing.

## 3. Counterexample to a generic tangency shortcut

For any integer $a\ge2$, consider the different formal automorphism

$$
\Psi^*(s)=s,\qquad\Psi^*(z)=z+s^a.
$$

It is tangent to the identity and preserves the reduced germ

$$
F(s,z)=\prod_{c\in\mathbb F_p}(z-cs^a)
      =z^p-s^{a(p-1)}z.
$$

Its $p$ branches have the same tangent, but the automorphism permutes them cyclically. This explicit control was also independently pointed out by B4. It shows that an origin-preserving formal automorphism, identity linear part, invariant reduced curve and common tangent do not imply individual branch preservation. It is not the quadratic $P_s$, does not have the required slope, and is not a counterexample to (BP) or (AS-full).

## Remaining exact gap

One must exclude $t=p^{e-h}>1$ for the actual invariant germ $M_e$ using information beyond (3)--(4). The independently checked displacement inequality in B4's [proof supplement](../b4_local_period_degree/PROOF_SUPPLEMENT.md) gives a necessary different/conductor cost for such a subgroup, but currently no incompatible upper bound. The exact local branch-preservation lemma remains open; the global native-cycle quotient problem would remain separate even if it were proved.
