**One full question, FI2.** Determine the increasing family
$J_N=L_2\cap K_N$ for every $N\ge3$, and its geometric analogue
after extending constants to $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. In particular,
is every $J_N=k$? The infinite-tower intersection means the union
$\bigcup_NJ_N$ inside a fixed separable closure, not an unqualified
infinite product of finite Galois groups. A complete answer must
separate arithmetic intersections, geometric intersections, and any
constant extension arising in a compositum.

This is the layer-two-versus-all-higher-layers interface of the unchanged
FGT contract, not a substitute low-period paper or a claim about rational
specializations. Full wreath monodromy in each individual layer does not
by definition imply linear disjointness. The first-pass Cayley
single-closed-point obstruction is not rerun or used as this answer.

**Success or failure boundary.** Prove one actual all-$N$ description,

## Proved reduction package

The complete proofs, hypotheses and arithmetic/geometric conventions are
in [PROOF_PACKAGE.md](PROOF_PACKAGE.md). They yield the following exact
interfaces; the word “conditional” applies to the displayed geometric or
monodromy input, not to an unstated independence assumption.

Write $V_2=C_2^{11}$ and $Q_2=L_2^{V_2}$, the **cycle splitting field**
with group $S_{11}$. It is not FG2's degree-$11$ non-Galois cycle field
$E$. From the accepted presentation $F=E(\delta)$,
$\delta^2=1-4q$, define intrinsic squareclasses

$$
p_2=\operatorname{Norm}_{E/k}(1-4q),\qquad
d_2=\operatorname{disc}(E/k)\quad\bmod k^{\times2}.
$$

Here the discriminant means the trace-form discriminant, equivalently
the discriminant of any primitive-element minimal polynomial modulo
squares. FG2 proves that the two characters are independent. Therefore

$$
L_2^{\rm ab}=k(\sqrt{p_2},\sqrt{d_2}),\qquad
Q_2^{A_{11}}=k(\sqrt{d_2}).
$$

They detect total phase parity and the sign of the permutation of the
eleven cycles, respectively. This identifies possible common quotients;
it does not identify an actual common subfield of distinct layers.

The wreath-product assertion includes the joint, not just pairwise,
statement. Its key calculation is that

$$
(C_m\wr S_r)'=\{a\in C_m^r:\textstyle\sum a_i=0\}\rtimes A_r
$$

is perfect for $r\ge5$. Distinct alternating composition factors then
force common Goursat quotients to be solvable; perfectness kills them.
Thus the joint Galois image contains the product of all these derived
subgroups. All remaining relations are exactly relations on
$\prod_i(C_{m_i}\times C_2)$. The proof does not infer the hypotheses
from a centralizer upper bound or from formal periodic degrees.

Proposition 7 adds a hand-only count refinement. C4's actual
ordinary-sheet count gives $r_2=11,r_3=24$; its recurrence
$u_{j+1}=4u_j+u_{j-1}$ bounds the sum of all possible proper-divisor
contributions and proves $r_n>r_{n-1}$ for every $n\ge4$.
Thus, on acceptance of that ordinary all-period count, the Fricke
application's distinct-count and $r_n\ge5$ conditions are discharged.
Only the actual higher full-wreath hypotheses remain unproved there.
This does not substitute a formal count for incidence connectedness
or Galois attainment.

## Constants are not silently removed

Every $J_N\subseteq L_2$ has constant field $\mathbb Q$, because FG2
already proves that $L_2$ is regular. This fact does not assert that
$K_N$ or $L_2K_N$ is regular, and arithmetic and geometric intersections
must still be distinguished.

Under the explicit full-wreath/distinct-count and individual-regularity
hypotheses of Proposition 5, let $R_{\rm ar}$ be the relations among all
layer abelian characters and $R_{\rm geo}$ the relations after restricting
to the geometric Galois group. For the constant field
$C=(\prod_iL_i)\cap\overline{\mathbb Q}$ the proof gives

$$
\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Gal}(C/\mathbb Q),\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)
\simeq R_{\rm geo}/R_{\rm ar}.
$$

An exact elementary control is
$\mathbb Q(t,\sqrt t)$ and $\mathbb Q(t,\sqrt{2t})$.
They are individually regular and arithmetically linearly disjoint,
but their compositum acquires $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ and they coincide
geometrically. This is a logical counterexample to a general inference,
**not** an actual Fricke entanglement.

## Source subtraction and transfer boundary

The closest directly accessed source is Andrew Bridy and Derek Garton,
*Dynamically distinguishing polynomials*, Research in the Mathematical
Sciences **4** (2017), article 13. Its Theorem 2.3 proves simultaneous
geometric disjointness for suitable integer translates of $x^d+c$ at a
fixed finite period level. The proof separates finite branch supports.
Corollary 2.4 gives joint dynatomic splitting-field disjointness and
arithmetic/geometric full wreath groups. The relevant theorem, proof and
corollary were read in the [primary published HTML](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40687-017-0103-3).
These are one-variable noninvertible polynomial results. The needed
Fricke branch separation has not been deduced from them. The source
attributes its single-family input to Morton (1998); that original
full text was not directly accessed, so no separate claim of reading
Morton's proof is made here.

Najmuddin Fakhruddin, *The algebraic dynamics of generic endomorphisms
of $\mathbb P^n$*, Algebra & Number Theory **8** (2014), recalls the
one-variable direct-product theorem as Theorem 3.2 and proves generic
projective-endomorphism transitivity in Proposition 3.3. Those statements
and the relevant proof passage were read in the
[primary published paper](https://msp.org/ant/2014/8-3/ant-v8-n3-p03-p.pdf).
Genericity there is in the full morphism parameter space. It is not the
genericity of an invertible four-parameter Fricke return and does not
supply its full tower Galois image.

Goursat's lemma, inertia normal closures, trace discriminants and the
arithmetic/geometric exact sequence are classical. This note supplies
self-contained bookkeeping proofs to prevent an incorrect transfer;
it does not claim a new general independence theorem. The accepted
Fricke-specific FG2 construction is cited from
[C4's first-pass proof](../../lanes/c4_fricke_galois/REPORT.md).

## The exact remaining sign-field test

Under NI, the independence subquestion “$J_N=k$ for every $N\ge3$”
is equivalent to

$$
k(\sqrt{d_2})\not\subseteq L_3\cdots L_N
\quad\text{for every }N\ge3,
\tag{SF2}
$$

with a separate geometric version. Indeed the only nontrivial
alternative larger than that quadratic is $Q_2$, which contains it.
If SF2 fails, the complete FI2 description still requires distinguishing
$k(\sqrt{d_2})$ from $Q_2$ and determining the relevant finite stages;
failure of the independence test does not provide that description.
One sufficient mechanism is a divisor whose inertia on the eleven
cycle labels is a transposition and which is unramified in every higher
splitting field. FG2 already supplies the transposition locally at a
smooth two-cycle fold; it does **not** prove absence of ramification
among all remote higher-period cycles along its divisor.

The branch-character parities are exact: the accepted nodal branch
has odd $p_2$ valuation and even $d_2$ valuation, while the accepted
smooth fold has even $p_2$ valuation and odd $d_2$ valuation. Thus NI
cannot itself eliminate $d_2$. Shared availability of an abstract $C_2$
quotient in wreath groups also cannot prove that their associated
quadratic fields coincide.
