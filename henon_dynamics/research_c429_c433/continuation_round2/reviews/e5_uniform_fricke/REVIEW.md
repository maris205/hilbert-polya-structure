## 3. Critical incidence and irreducibility of the divisor

The equations for a critical point give the exact parametrization
$$
(x,y,z)\longmapsto
(2x-yz,2y-xz,2z-xy,-x^2-y^2-z^2+2xyz).
$$
Its total source really is $\mathbb A^3$: the four parameter coordinates
are solved polynomially, so no extra scheme component is omitted.

I checked the compactification used to prove properness of this particular
map. At infinity, a relative singular point would satisfy all three pairwise
products $xy,xz,yz$ equal to zero, and also $x^2+y^2+z^2=0$. There is no
projective solution in characteristic zero. Thus the projective relative
singular locus is closed and lies entirely in the affine chart. It is the
displayed critical incidence and is proper over the base. Each fibre is
both proper and affine, hence zero-dimensional; proper quasi-finiteness
makes the map finite. Its reduced image is therefore closed, geometrically
irreducible, and three-dimensional, as required for a divisor in the base.

At parameter $(0,0,0,0)$ the only critical point on the fibre is the origin,
with Hessian $2I$. The derivative of the first three parameter coordinates
is invertible there. Finiteness and this single nondegenerate point give an
open part of $\Delta$ with one ordinary node. At Cayley the four nodes are
the four sign points $(2\alpha,2\beta,2\gamma)$ with product of signs one.
The accepted FG2 phase equations at $\delta=0$ reproduce the critical
parametrization, so the four local phase graphs are branches of this one
global divisor, not four globally separate components.

This properness argument concerns the critical map only. The proof does not
claim that the affine higher fixed-point incidence is proper.

## 4. All-period Cayley count and smooth nondegeneracy

The source-owned quotient is
$$
\pi(u,v)=(u+u^{-1},v+v^{-1},uv+(uv)^{-1}),
\qquad (u,v)\sim(u^{-1},v^{-1}).
$$
The four two-torsion points are exactly the quotient's singular images;
elsewhere it is étale. For the fixed native word, the accepted matrix is
$$
M=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-2\\-2&-3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\det M=-1,\quad M\equiv I\pmod2.
$$
Its eigenvalues $-2\pm\sqrt5$ have absolute values different from one.

If a smooth point is fixed by $T^n$, exactly one of $M^nt=t$ and
$M^nt=t^{-1}$ holds for a lift $t$. If both held, the lift would be a
two-torsion point and the image would not be smooth. In logarithmic
coordinates the actual quotient return derivative is $M^n$ in the first
case and $-M^n$ in the second. The minus sign correctly accounts for the
deck identification. Neither derivative has eigenvalue one, for any
$n\ge1$. The relative fixed incidence is consequently étale over the
four-dimensional base at every smooth central fixed point.

Set $u_n=(2+\sqrt5)^n+(2-\sqrt5)^n$. The two torus kernels have sizes
$A_n=|\det(M^n-I)|$ and $B_n=|\det(M^n+I)|$. Their intersection is exactly
the four two-torsion points. Burnside counting, including those four fixed
points of inversion, gives $(A_n+B_n)/2$ quotient points, not the size of a
disjoint union divided by two. The determinant evaluations are
$$
(A_n,B_n)=
\begin{cases}(u_n,u_n),&n\text{ odd},\\
(u_n-2,u_n+2),&n\text{ even}.
\end{cases}
$$
Thus there are exactly $u_n$ ordinary Cayley fixed points and $u_n-4$
smooth ones. All four nodes are already fixed by $T$. No cutoff in $n$
or finite numerical census enters this calculation.

## 5. Nodal iterate ideals: full family, odd and even

The telescoping identity in Lemma 4.1 is valid with the parameters retained:
$$
f_a^m(z)-z=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}
[f_a^j(f_a(z))-f_a^j(z)]=Q_m(a,z)(f_a(z)-z).
$$
Coordinatewise divided differences are local functions. At a central fixed
point, $Q_m$ specializes to $I+J+\cdots+J^{m-1}$. If its determinant is a
unit, the generated ideals are equal in the local ring containing both
state and parameter variables. This is stronger than a central-fibre
multiplicity comparison and is the relevant implication here.

I independently checked the nodal derivative
$$
J=\begin{pmatrix}-1&2&2\\-2&3&6\\-6&10&15\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det(tI-J)=(t+1)(t^2-18t+1).
$$
Even sign changes transport it to the other nodes. If
$\lambda=9+4\sqrt5$, the eigenvalues are $-1,\lambda,\lambda^{-1}$.
For odd $n$, the sum for $T$ is nonzero on all three eigenvalues, including
the value one at $-1$. For $n=2m$, use $T^2$: the sum is nonzero on
$1,\lambda^2,\lambda^{-2}$, including the value $m$ at one. Therefore
the full local ideals agree with the $T$-fixed ideal for odd $n$ and with
the $T^2$-fixed ideal for even $n$. Adding $D$ and imposing the level
equation preserves equality of ideals.

The odd nodal germs are critical-incidence germs with no generic sheet
over the four-dimensional base. The even germs have precisely the two
generic native-period-two sheets of accepted FG2 at each node. Resonance
does not produce a higher native period at a node; the one eigenvalue one
for $T^2$ is handled by the invertible sum, not ignored.

## 6. Length exhaustion, remote sheets and exact periods

The classical generic affine fixed-scheme length is
$a_n=u_n+4(-1)^n$. The $u_n-4$ smooth étale germs and the eight nodal
phase sheets for even $n$ produce
$$
u_n-4+8\mathbf1_{2\mid n}=a_n
$$
distinct points of one geometric generic fibre. Distinct central points
give distinct sections. The two phases at each node are generically
distinct by the accepted FG2 model.

The repaired Section 5 formulation is correct: only the finitely many
smooth germs are placed in a common pointed étale neighborhood. The
ramified phase germs are placed in an algebraic closure of its fraction
field. It would be false to call those phase germs étale at Cayley.
The corrected formulation puts all points into one geometric generic
fibre without making that false claim.

Every geometric point contributes at least one to the length of a finite
scheme. The constructed distinct points already reach the full source
length, so the generic scheme is reduced and has no other point or
nilpotent contribution. An additional sheet with no specialization in the
listed local branches would violate that length equality. This excludes
remote/escaping generic sheets without assuming properness or deducing
global completeness from the nodal lemma alone.

If a smooth Cayley point has least period $d\mid n$, its unique étale
$T^d$ continuation is also $T^n$-fixed and hence is the unique $T^n$
continuation. Equalities with a smaller iterate fail at the central point,
so after shrinking they still fail. Its least period remains $d$.
Consequently every generic exact-$n$ point for $n\ge3$ comes from a
distinct smooth Cayley point of that same least period. No higher-period
root can hide in one of the period-two nodal germs.

Ordinary Möbius inversion is valid only after this reducedness argument.
The correction term $4(-1)^n$ contributes only to primitive levels one
and two, giving the report's stated count formula for $n\ge3$.

## 7. Strict henselian splitting and the entire normalization

Let $R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb A^4_{\overline{\mathbb Q}},b_C}$,
$R^{\rm sh}$ its strict henselization, and
$K^{\rm sh}=\operatorname{Frac}(R^{\rm sh})$. The ring $R$ is an excellent
normal local domain. Its strict henselization is a normal domain.
All the finitely many generic exact-$n$ point branches, not just one root,
have sections with coordinates in $R^{\rm sh}$ for $n\ge3$.

Let $S$ be the integral closure of $R$ in the finite Galois field
$\bar L_n$. It is finite by excellence. The localization
$\bar k\otimes_RR^{\rm sh}$ is an algebraic domain over $\bar k$,
hence a field, and equals $K^{\rm sh}$. All coordinates of the full
periodic point set lie in that field, so $\bar L_n$ splits there as a
Galois extension. This is not the invalid shortcut from one unramified
root of a nonnormal extension to all its places.

The integral closure base-change lemma applies because $R^{\rm sh}$ is
a filtered colimit of étale, thus smooth, $R$-algebras. It identifies
$S\otimes_RR^{\rm sh}$ with the integral closure of $R^{\rm sh}$ in
$\bar L_n\otimes_{\bar k}K^{\rm sh}$. The latter generic algebra is
the product of $[\bar L_n:\bar k]$ copies of $K^{\rm sh}$. Normality
of $R^{\rm sh}$ gives
$$
S\otimes_RR^{\rm sh}
\cong\prod_{[\bar L_n:\bar k]}R^{\rm sh}.
$$
Faithfully flat descent now proves that $S/R$ is finite étale. This proves
étaleness at every point of the normalization over $b_C$. Vertical
fixed-incidence components do not enter this field normalization, which
is generated solely by the generic exact-period points.

The cited Stacks statements and their proofs were independently read in
full: [0CBF](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CBF) supplies integral
closure under filtered smooth base change, and
[06DI](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/06DI) supplies normality of
the strict henselization. Their hypotheses match this application.

## 8. Generic divisor and joint-field inertia

The non-étale locus of a finite normalization is closed, and its image in
the base is closed. If a higher layer ramified generically along the
irreducible $\Delta$, that image would contain all of $\Delta$, including
$b_C$. Section 7 excludes this. This is a valid special-point-to-generic-
divisor implication; it is not an inference from an arbitrary unramified
point on a reducible discriminant.

Horizontal ramification indices do not change after an algebraic constant
extension in characteristic zero. Equivalently, one may descend the
geometric étaleness after accounting for the conjugate geometric components.
Thus the arithmetic fields $L_n/k$ are unramified at $\Delta$ for $n\ge3$,
without asserting that their constant fields are trivial.

Accepted FG2 supplies a simple phase branch along $\Delta$ with inertia a
single phase flip. The local branch is identified with the one global
irreducible divisor above, so it determines the generic inertia conjugacy
class. A finite compositum of the higher unramified fields remains
unramified at the associated characteristic-zero discrete valuation.
In the full joint field the inertia restricts injectively and surjectively
to the order-two inertia in $L_2$: the kernel fixes every constituent
field, while surjectivity is the usual inertia restriction in a finite
Galois tower. This proves both its exact order and its asserted support.

If $H_N=L_3\cdots L_N$ and $J_N=L_2\cap H_N$, conjugates in the full
tower act trivially on $H_N$ and project to all eleven individual FG2
phase flips. Hence the unconditional exported inclusion
$$
J_N\subseteq L_2^{C_2^{11}}
$$
is valid. It does not show $J_N=k$.
