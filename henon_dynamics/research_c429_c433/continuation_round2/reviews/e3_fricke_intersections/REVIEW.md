## 1. The original question has not been changed into a group exercise

The frozen field is k = Q(A,B,C,D), with four algebraically independent
parameters and native return T = s_z s_y s_x, rightmost factor first.
L_n is generated by coordinates of ordinary exact-native-period-n
points. The question asks for the actual increasing fields

\[
J_N=L_2\cap K_N,\qquad K_N=L_3\cdots L_N,
\]

and their geometric analogues, with constants treated separately.
The accepted FG2 theorem supplies the actual arithmetic and geometric
group of L2, namely C2 wr S11, and regularity over Q. It does not
supply the higher groups or their intersections.

The new package gives exact reductions of possible intersections under
specific additional hypotheses. This is useful proof progress, but the
original demand concerns actual field inclusions. Two extensions sharing
an abstract C2 quotient need not share the same quadratic subfield.
Neither maximal individual monodromy nor transitivity implies joint
linear disjointness. The author correctly preserves these distinctions
throughout the propositions.

## 2. Proposition 1: the two actual FG2 characters

The accepted FG2 input was checked against the first-pass C4 report:
its equations (11)–(15) identify the cycle field E and ordered-point
field F = E(delta), with delta squared equal to 1−4q. Sections 3.6–3.8
give the separated fold and phase-flip inertia and the full wreath
group. Those established inputs are imported, not re-certified here.

For the eleven embeddings of E, choose compatible delta_i. Then

\[
\left(\prod_i\delta_i\right)^2
=\operatorname{Norm}_{E/k}(1-4q)=p_2.
\]

An element (a,sigma) of C2^11 semidirect S11 acts on that product by
the total phase sign. Permuting scalar factors introduces no extra
sign. Independently, the Vandermonde product of the eleven conjugates
of a primitive element of E transforms by sign(sigma). Its square
is the discriminant d2. Replacing the primitive element changes that
discriminant by a square, by the trace-form change-of-basis formula.

The two characters are independent because the actual group is full:
a single phase flip and a pure cycle transposition are available as
abstract elements of that realized group. The sum/sign map is onto
C2 × C2. Its kernel is V0 semidirect A11, where V0 is the even-sum
phase module, and this is the commutator subgroup. Thus the maximal
abelian field is exactly k(sqrt(p2),sqrt(d2)), not merely a field
contained in it. All three quadratic squareclasses are nontrivial.

The report correctly distinguishes E, a non-Galois degree-eleven
cycle field, from Q2 = L2^V2, its cycle **splitting** field with
group S11. Writing a normal-subgroup argument for E would be invalid;
the author instead applies it to Q2.

## 3. Propositions 2–3: inertia, normality and the trichotomy

If L and K are finite Galois over k, their intersection J is Galois
over k. If a divisor is unramified in K, it is unramified in J.
Inertia in L restricts trivially to J. Therefore Gal(L/J), a normal
subgroup of Gal(L/k), contains the entire normal closure of that
inertia subgroup. This proves the stated containment in the fixed
field of the normal closure.

The compositum clause is also correct. After henselizing the valuation
ring, finite unramified extensions are finite étale; a finite
compositum stays unramified. The hypothesis must concern the whole
splitting field, not just an analytic branch near one selected orbit.

Under NI, conjugating one coordinate phase flip in the actual FG2
group supplies all eleven flips. Its normal closure is exactly V2:
conjugation does not leave V2, and the conjugates generate V2. Hence
J_N is contained in Q2. Since J_N/k is Galois, it corresponds to a
**normal** subgroup of S11. The only possibilities are S11, A11 and
the trivial subgroup, giving

\[
J_N\in\{k,\ k(\sqrt{d_2}),\ Q_2\}.
\]

No assumption about higher-layer group sizes is needed for this
trichotomy. It rules out the phase quadratic and phase-times-sign
quadratic, but does not rule out the sign field. The same proof works
over Qbar(A,B,C,D), provided the corresponding geometric NI is
available. The independently verified geometric FG2 group is the same.

The branch-character parity claims are correct at the stated
residue-characteristic-zero divisors. A single phase flip has phase
character −1 and cycle-sign character +1. A simple smooth cycle fold
has cycle-sign −1 and phase character +1: on the ordered points it
exchanges two cycle labels in two phase-matched transpositions, with
no local phase ramification. A quadratic squareclass has nontrivial
inertia precisely when its valuation is odd. Thus NI cannot remove
d2 through the nodal valuation alone.

The optional completion test is logically sufficient: if a divisor
whose inertia projects to a cycle transposition is unramified in all
higher fields, its image normally generates S11 after the V2 quotient,
and every J_N is k. Only the inertia **image** is needed at this
second step; the lift need not be a specially labelled pure permutation.
FG2 establishes the local transposition, not the required all-higher
unramifiedness.

NI itself is not adjudicated here. The current C4 continuation report
was read for its exact proposed input, but its new normalization and
degree-exhaustion proof is outside this assigned nonauthor audit. No
author status label is being converted into independent closure.

## 4. Proposition 4: all moduli, module coinvariants and joint products

### 4.1 The derived group really is perfect

Write V = (Z/mZ)^r and V0 = ker(sum). The onto map

\[
V\rtimes S_r\longrightarrow C_m\times C_2,
\qquad (a,\sigma)\longmapsto
(\textstyle\sum a_i,\operatorname{sgn}\sigma)
\]

has kernel V0 semidirect A_r. Commutators with permutations generate
all differences a(e_i−e_j), hence V0, while S_r' = A_r. This proves
the asserted abelianization and exact derived subgroup.

The potential modular obstruction is dealt with correctly. For four
distinct indices i,j,k,l and u = a(e_i−e_l), a 3-cycle (i j k) gives

\[
(i\ j\ k)u-u=a(e_j-e_i).
\]

These differences generate V0 over Z/mZ, so [A_r,V0] = V0 and the
coinvariants (V0)_{A_r} vanish. Since A_r is perfect for r ≥ 5,
(V0 semidirect A_r)' is the whole group. No division by m, r, 2
or 3 was used. Thus composite m, m divisible by r, and nontrivial
diagonal vectors lying in V0 create no exception. The trivial-module
case m = 1 also works. This is stronger coverage than m ≥ 5;
the author's stated range m ≥ 1 is valid.

### 4.2 The composition-factor argument needs subdirectness, and has it

Each W_i has only A_{r_i} as a nonabelian simple composition factor.
For a subdirect product, project away its last coordinate. The kernel
lies in that last factor. It is normal there because every element of
the last factor has a lift in the subdirect product, and conjugating
by such a lift preserves the kernel. Thus the kernel's composition
factors occur in the last factor; induction handles the image.

This justifies the author's observation for arbitrary finite
**subdirect** products. It would not be true for arbitrary subgroups
of a product. The actual compositum Galois group has surjective
restrictions to every individual layer, supplying precisely the
required hypothesis.

For r ≥ 5, distinct r give nonisomorphic A_r, since their orders
r!/2 strictly increase. The exceptional outer automorphism of S6
does not identify A6 with a differently indexed alternating group
and creates no omitted case here.

### 4.3 Goursat gives a joint statement, not just pairwise independence

In the induction, the common Goursat quotient of the last W_t and
the preceding subdirect image H0 can have no nonabelian simple factor:
it would have to belong to both disjoint alternating-factor lists.
The quotient is therefore solvable. Every image of a perfect group
in a solvable group is trivial. It follows that W_t' lies in the last
kernel and the already contained product of earlier W_i' lies in
the other kernel. Hence

\[
D=\prod_iW_i'\subseteq H,\qquad
H=\pi^{-1}(\pi(H)).
\]

In fact H' = D: H/D is abelian, giving H' contained in D, while
D is perfect and contained in H, giving the reverse containment.
This also identifies the maximal abelian field of the preceding
compositum as the compositum of the individual abelian fields.
The field equality in equation (10) follows in both directions.

Joint linear disjointness is thus exactly full product image on
the abelianizations. Pairwise tests are insufficient. For example,
an abelian image in C2^3 defined by a1+a2+a3 = 0 projects onto
each pair, but is not the full product. Taking its inverse image
under the wreath abelianizations preserves the same warning even
when the alternating degrees are distinct. This is a logical control,
not an asserted Fricke field realization.

For application to J_N, the distinct-count hypothesis must range over
**every n in {2,...,N}, including r2 = 11**. It is not enough for
r3,...,rN to be mutually distinct if one equals 11. If r_i = r_j,
the fibre product over the common quotient S_{r_i}, or a diagonal
subgroup when the whole groups coincide, can carry nonabelian
entanglement. The claim as formally stated in Proposition 4 excludes
this; the Fricke specialization should make the index range explicit.

## 5. Proposition 5: arithmetic and geometric constants

Individual regularity makes both H_ar and H_geo subdirect in the
same product of actual full groups. Proposition 4 therefore places
D in both. The standard finite constant-field exact sequence is

\[
1\longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{geo}}
\longrightarrow H_{\mathrm{ar}}
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(C/\mathbb Q)
\longrightarrow1.
\]

Here C = L intersect Qbar is finite Galois over Q; the base is a
purely transcendental Q-field. Its quotient group is abelian because
both groups contain D and H_ar/D is abelian. This conclusion concerns
the compositum constants under the full Proposition 5 hypotheses,
not all composita of individually regular extensions.

Let A be the product of the layer abelianizations. Characters of its
subgroup H_ar/D extend to A because Q/Z is divisible. Thus the
ambient character group modulo R_ar is exactly the character group
of H_ar/D. Requiring such a character to vanish on H_geo/D takes
the kernel R_geo/R_ar, proving

\[
\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Gal}(C/\mathbb Q),\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)
\simeq R_{\mathrm{geo}}/R_{\mathrm{ar}}.
\]

There is no lost dual or reversed quotient here. A proper subgroup
of a finite abelian group has a nonzero annihilator. Together with
Proposition 4 this proves arithmetic joint disjointness iff R_ar = 0
and geometric joint disjointness iff R_geo = 0. The arithmetic test
alone does not imply geometric disjointness or constant-free composita.

The control Q(t,sqrt(t)) and Q(t,sqrt(2t)) is exact: both are rational
function fields over Q; they are distinct quadratic extensions of
Q(t), but their compositum is Q(sqrt(2))(sqrt(t)). Its constants are
exactly Q(sqrt(2)), and over Qbar the two quadratic fields coincide.
These groups do not satisfy r ≥ 5; the control is used only to
refute an unrestricted inference from individual regularity, not as
an instance or counterexample of Proposition 5.

Every actual J_N has constants Q simply because it is a subfield of
the regular L2. That does not identify the geometric intersection
with the constant extension of J_N, nor control constants in K_N or
L2 K_N. The author retains this necessary separation.

## 6. Proposition 6 and the exact SF2 boundary

An element of L2 intersect union(K_N) belongs to some finite K_N,
so the intersection is union(J_N). The increasing J_N correspond
to a decreasing chain of subgroups of the finite group Gal(L2/k).
There can be only finitely many strict changes, and the chain
stabilizes at one finite stage. The same argument applies after
extending constants. There is no unjustified infinite direct-product
assertion, and no effective bound on the last change follows.

Under NI, for each finite N the trichotomy implies the equivalence

\[
J_N=k\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k(\sqrt{d_2})\not\subseteq K_N.
\]

Thus SF2 is exactly the remaining test for the **all-intersections-
trivial answer**, and it must hold at every finite N. If the sign
field does occur, NI alone does not distinguish J_N equal to that
quadratic from J_N equal to Q2. With the additional full-wreath and
distinct-count hypotheses, the Q2 alternative is excluded and only
the quadratic remains. None of these occurrence tests is performed
or proved for the actual higher Fricke fields.

### 10.1 Count implication independently checked

Use nu_n to denote the integer |P_n| in this review, reserving p2 for
the norm squareclass throughout the field argument. C4's input comprises
both the ordinary generic count and its ordinary Cayley interpretation:

\[
\nu_2=22,\qquad
\nu_n=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)u_d\quad(n\ge3),
\qquad
u_n=(2+\sqrt5)^n+(2-\sqrt5)^n.
\]

The recurrence is u_{j+1} = 4u_j + u_{j-1}, with u0 = 2 and
u1 = 4. Positivity implies u_{j+1} > 4u_j for j ≥ 1. Bounding
the preceding terms by a finite geometric series proves

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_j<\frac43u_m<\frac13u_{m+1}
\qquad(m\ge1).
\]

Every proper positive divisor of n ≥ 3 is at most n−2. Replacing
all its Möbius coefficients by the worst possible negative sign gives

\[
\nu_n\ge u_n-\sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\d<n}}u_d
\ge u_n-\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}u_j
>u_n-\frac13u_{n-1}
>\frac{11}{3}u_{n-1}.
\]

Thus the lower bound holds even at n = 3, though the monotonicity
comparison using the previous ordinary Cayley count starts at n = 4.
For j ≥ 3, the Cayley interpretation gives nu_j ≤ u_j because
exact-period-j points are a subset of the points fixed by T^j. Hence

\[
r_n=\frac{\nu_n}{n}
>\frac{11}{3n}u_{n-1}
>\frac{u_{n-1}}{n-1}\ge r_{n-1}\qquad(n\ge4),
\]

where 11(n−1) > 3n. Finally u2 = 18 and u3 = 76 give r2 = 11
and r3 = (76−4)/3 = 24. This explicitly handles the exceptional
generic two-cycle correction: it would be wrong to use nu2 ≤ u2,
since 22 > 18. The author does not make that mistake.

The conclusion is r2 < r3 < r4 < ..., all at least 11. In particular,
the index-two value cannot collide with a higher value. No bounded
census, asymptotic extrapolation, or new computation enters this proof.
