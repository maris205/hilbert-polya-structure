\section{Exact resummation of the second logarithm}\label{sec:resummation}

The second moment (2.3) is too large for absolute convergence on every
\(\RePart s>1/5\): the Weil bound gives only
\(a_p=O(\sqrt p)\).  The elliptic decomposition permits an exact global
resummation instead of a nonexistent pointwise improvement.

For \(u\in\CC\), use the cohomological convention
\[
 L(H^1(C/K),u)=
 \prod_{\mathfrak p}
 \det\!\left(
 1-\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak p}\Norm\mathfrak p^{-u}
 \mid H^1(C_{\overline K},\QQ_\ell)
 \right)^{-1}.                                         \tag{4.1}
\]
The finitely many bad factors use their standard inertia-invariant
definition.  Set
\[
 \ell_2(s)=\sum_{p\equiv1(3)}c_{p,2}p^{-2s},
 \qquad F_2(s)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\ell_2(s)}2\right)     \tag{4.2}
\]
on \(\RePart s>1/4\).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:second-extraction}
There is a canonical holomorphic nonvanishing function \(H_2\) on
\(\RePart s>0\) such that
\[
 F_2(s)=
 \zeta_K(2s+1)^7L(H^1(C/K),2s+1)H_2(s)                 \tag{4.3}
\]
on \(\RePart s>1/4\).  The right-hand side continues \(F_2\)
holomorphically to \(\RePart s>0\), but need not be nonvanishing there.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(u=2s+1\).  Equation (2.3) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \log F_2(s)
 &=\sum_{p\ {\rm split}}
   \frac{14+2a_p}{p-1}p^{-2s}\\
 &=\sum_{p\ {\rm split}}(14+2a_p)p^{-u}
   +R_{\mathrm{den}}(s).
\end{aligned}                                           \tag{4.4}
\]
where
\[
 R_{\mathrm{den}}(s)
 =\sum_{p\ {\rm split}}(14+2a_p)p^{-u}
   \sum_{j\ge1}p^{-j}.                                 \tag{4.5}
\]
Every split rational prime gives two degree-one prime ideals of \(K\).
The first logarithmic coefficient of \(\zeta_K(u)^7\) is therefore \(14\).
The reductions of \(C\) at the two primes are isomorphic, so the first
coefficient of \(L(H^1(C/K),u)\) is \(2a_p\).  The sign and powers in
(4.3) are consequently forced.

On the common absolute-convergence half-plane, define
\[
 \log H_2(s)=
 \log F_2(s)-7\log\zeta_K(u)-\log L(H^1(C/K),u).
                                                               \tag{4.6}
\]
We verify that the right side converges locally absolutely for
\(\RePart u>1\).  Since \(g(C)=4\),
\[
 |a_p|\le8\sqrt p.
\]
The extra factor \(p^{-1}\) in (4.5) makes the denominator correction
summable.  At a split prime, an \(m\)-th curve Euler term is
\[
 O\!\left(p^{m/2}p^{-m\RePart u}\right);
\]
the terms \(m\ge2\) are summable for \(\RePart u>1\).  At an inert
rational prime, \(\Norm\mathfrak p=p^2\), and the first curve term is
\[
 O\!\left(p^{1-2\RePart u}\right),
\]
with the same threshold.  Higher zeta powers are smaller, while ramified
and bad primes form a finite set.  Thus (4.6) defines a holomorphic
logarithm, and \(H_2=\exp(\log H_2)\) is holomorphic and nonzero on
\(\RePart s>0\).

By \cref{thm:jac-decomposition} and isogeny invariance,
\[
 L(H^1(C/K),u)=L(E_+/K,u)^2L(E_-/K,u)^2.                \tag{4.7}
\]
Caraiani--Newton's theorem applies to all elliptic curves over
\(K=\QQ(\sqrt{-3})\) \cite[Theorem~1.1 and the examples immediately
following it]{CaraianiNewton2025}.  Hence both elliptic \(L\)-functions are
entire.  Since \(\zeta_K(u)\) is holomorphic for \(\RePart u>1\), equation
(4.3) gives the asserted holomorphic continuation.  The elliptic factors
may vanish, so the continued product is not zero-free.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Functional-equation firewall]\label{rem:fe-firewall}
The modular elliptic factors possess standard automorphic completions and
functional equations \cite[Global Theory]{GodementJacquet1972}.  Neither
the residual \(H_2\) nor the remaining chronological product is identified
with an automorphic \(L\)-function.  We therefore do not multiply their
individual Gamma factors into a conjectural completion of the H\'enon
object.

The original \(\operatorname{Log}_0\) is also not continued through zeros.
Equation (4.3) continues its exponential \(F_2\), which is the
single-valued object needed below.
\end{remark}
