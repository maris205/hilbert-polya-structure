     The count is independent of all lower coefficients of g and of its nonzero leading coefficient.
     The proof computes the unique leading term of every iterate of the difference of two
     commuting linear pullback operators; a characteristic-p factorization then resolves the
     cancellation at every period, including all levels of the p-divisibility tower. Coprime
     leading monomials and a nonvanishing Jacobian convert this degree calculation into an
     ordinary point count. These are precisely the fixed counts of the single map S = H −1 ◦ Φq .

     integer power has |t| = q −2 as a natural boundary for meromorphic continuation. We
     distinguish this nonlinear count from established algebraic-group mechanisms, give a direct
     vector-group conjugacy obstruction, and exhibit a counterexample when the coprimality
     hypothesis is removed.

1    Introduction
In positive characteristic, equality of the dominant degrees in a Frobenius coincidence problem
can conceal a substantial cancellation. For the Hénon automorphism

                                   H(x, y) = (y, y q + g(y) − ax)                                      (1.1)

over Fq , both H n and the nth iterate of q-power Frobenius have degree q n . In their second
coordinates the dominant monomials cancel. The remaining degree, rather than the original
degree, controls the affine intersection. A calculation at periods prime to the characteristic
does not settle this problem: additional cancellations appear when the period acquires
successive factors of p.
   We compute the remaining degree for all periods and all coefficients in the following
family. Throughout the paper,

           q = pe ,   K = Fq ,    a ∈ F∗q ,   g ∈ Fq [y],   2 ≤ m := deg g < q,       p ∤ m.           (1.2)

    The algebraic point of the paper is the all-period cancellation formula (1.4), not a new
general principle relating distorted counts to natural boundaries. That principle has important
antecedents. Byszewski, Cornelissen, and Houben study endomorphisms of algebraic groups
with finite fixed sets at every iterate, develop finite-adelically distorted counting sequences,
and prove corresponding zeta dichotomies [3, Theorems A and C, and §5.2]. Their vector-
group theorem applies to additive group endomorphisms, whereas (1.1) yields a genuinely
nonlinear affine-plane problem. Proposition 6.1 gives a fixed-cardinality obstruction to direct
vector-group conjugacy in any dimension. It does not exclude every possible group quotient
presentation.
    One-dimensional polynomial dynamics also has well-established positive-characteristic
zeta phenomena. In the version of Bridy’s paper specified in the bibliography, Theorem 1
gives rationality when the derivative of a polynomial on A1 is zero, and Theorem 2 gives
transcendental examples [1]. These statements concern one variable, so neither determines the

Postcomposing both morphisms in an equalizer with an isomorphism preserves that equalizer.
Applying H n to the pair (S n , id) therefore gives an equality of closed subschemes

                                            Fix(S n ) = En .                               (2.3)

This is not only a bijection of geometric point sets.
    The map S is finite and radicial, since it is the composition of a polynomial automorphism
with q-power Frobenius. Its function-field degree is q 2 , entirely inseparable. The degree here
is not the cardinality of a geometric fiber: a radicial map has at most one point in such a
fiber. Neither that degree nor radiciality by itself computes the fixed-point count.
    Let R = Fq [x, y]. We use the commuting Fq -linear ring endomorphisms

             T (f ) = f ◦ H,        U (f ) = f ◦ Φ = f (xq , y q ) = f q ,   δ = T − U.    (2.4)

The equality U (f ) = f q holds because every coefficient of f is fixed by q-power Frobenius.
Arbitrary elements of K[x, y] do not satisfy that identity, which is why the operator calculation
is performed in R before extending scalars for the zero scheme.
    Although T and U are ring endomorphisms, their difference δ is in general only a linear
operator. It is neither a ring endomorphism nor a derivation. Its iterates below mean
composition as a linear operator, and every binomial identity takes place in the algebra of
commuting linear operators on R. In particular,

                               δ(y) = g(y) − ax,          δ(x) = y − xq .                  (2.5)

There is no binomial identity asserted for a difference of nonlinear maps of the affine plane.

4     From actual degrees to geometric points
We now prove Theorem 1.1. Put Q = q n and d = dn = q n−r Dr , and write the literal fixed
equations as
                      F1 = T n (x) − xQ ,   F2 = T n (y) − y Q .
Proposition 3.3 gives unique highest homogeneous terms −xQ and scr y d , respectively. In
particular, their leading monomials for any graded monomial order are relatively prime.

4.1   The quotient length
The coprime-leading-monomial criterion and the standard-monomial basis are classical
polynomial-ideal tools; see [4]. Their application here can be made explicit. Normalize
the polynomials to be monic and write

                                f = xQ + f0 ,          h = y d + h0 ,

where deg f0 < Q and deg h0 < d. Their sole S-polynomial has the representation

                                     y d f − xQ h = f0 h − h0 f.

Both products on the right have leading monomial strictly smaller than xQ y d . This is
the standard representation in Buchberger’s criterion, so f, h form a Gröbner basis. The
monomials not in the initial ideal (xQ , y d ) are exactly

                              xi y j ,    0 ≤ i < Q,        0 ≤ j < d.                    (4.1)

                                                 5
Their residue classes form a K-basis of the quotient. Thus En is finite and

                                 dimK K[x, y]/(F1 , F2 ) = Qd.                               (4.2)

    This calculation also shows why there is no unaccounted correction at infinity. Homog-
enizing each equation to its actual degree yields the forms −xQ and scr y d on the line at
infinity. They cannot vanish at the same point of that projective line: simultaneous vanishing
would require both homogeneous coordinates to be zero. Plane Bézout consequently gives the
same length Qd. Homogenizing the second equation as if it still had degree Q would retain a
spurious infinity contribution; resolving the resonant cancellation must precede the count.

6     Model boundaries and bounded exact checks
6.1   A direct additive-model obstruction
The first coordinate of S contains the nonzero term a−1 bxmq , where mq is not a power of p.
Thus S is not additive for the standard vector-group law on A2 . That coordinate observation
alone would not exclude a different coordinate system. The first-period count gives a stronger
but still limited statement.

Proposition 6.1. The point dynamical system S : A2 (K) → A2 (K) is not conjugate, even
by a set-theoretic bijection, to an algebraic-group endomorphism of any vector group Gda (K)
having finite fixed sets at every iterate.

Proof. For a vector-group endomorphism τ , its fixed points form the subgroup ker(τ − id)(K)
of Gda (K). If finite, this is a finite-dimensional vector space over Fp , and therefore has
cardinality a power of p. On the other hand, Theorem 1.1, or the first-period elimination
in Section 4, gives N1 = qm. Since m > 1 and p ∤ m, this is not a power of p. A conjugacy
preserves fixed-set cardinalities, giving a contradiction.

    This elementary obstruction distinguishes the family from the direct vector-group setting
of [3, Theorem 5.2.5]. It says nothing about finite quotients, other algebraic groups, or a
birational presentation restricted to a smaller domain. In particular, it is not a theorem
excluding every dynamically affine presentation. Such a classification is not needed for the
count proved here and is not supplied by it.

6.2   Why the coprimality hypothesis cannot be dropped
Example 6.2. Take q = 8, a = 1, g(y) = y 6 + y 3 + 1, and n = 2, so p | m. The geometric
count is 2816, whereas extending (1.3) to these parameters would give 2944.

Proof. Work in characteristic two and put h = y 6 + y 3 + 1 + x. The commuting-operator
identity still gives T 2 − U 2 = δ 2 , and direct expansion gives

                  δ 2 (y) = y 32 h2 + y 16 h4 + h6 + y 16 h + y 8 h2 + h3 + y + x8 .

The unique top term is y 44 : the first three displayed products have degrees 44, 40, 36, and
the remaining degrees are smaller. The first equation has unique top term x64 . The quotient-
length and Jacobian arguments of Section 4, here with determinant one, therefore give

64 · 44 = 2816 reduced points. The unjustified extension of (1.3) gives (5 · 84 + 2 · 82 )/7 = 2944,
as claimed.

    This is a nonlinear failure example, not an additive degeneration. It shows that deleting
p ∤ m is invalid; it does not classify every degree divisible by p.

6.4   What is, and is not, being counted
The clock is the positive integer n for the one fixed map S on A2 (K). Equivalently, both H
and Φ are iterated n times in the coincidence condition. This is not the count of ordinary
H-periodic points over a fixed finite field, and it is not the Hasse–Weil point count of a fixed
variety under Frobenius alone. Accordingly, the natural boundary does not assert a failure of
Hasse–Weil rationality.
    All lower coefficients of g are permitted, but the monic y q term, the coefficient field Fq ,
a ̸= 0, and 2 ≤ m < q with p ∤ m are structural hypotheses. The result is not a classification
of all resonant Hénon maps, of independently varying two-clock resonances, or of the case
p | m.

7      Conclusion and reproducibility
The all-period resonance in (1.1) is resolved by a coefficient-uniform leading-term recurrence
in a polynomial ring. The characteristic-p factorization isolates the largest p-power dividing
the period, and the remaining factor contributes a nonzero scalar. Together with the coprime
leading monomials and Jacobian, this proves an exact geometric count for every period. The
same count then gives one explicit dynamical zeta function and its natural boundary; the
analytic consequence is not a separate counting theorem.
    Several questions remain outside this result: a complete treatment of p | deg g, nonmonic
Frobenius-degree terms, independently varying clocks, and possible presentations through
other groups or finite quotients. Nonrationality excludes a finite-dimensional characteristic-
zero determinant formula for this exact zeta, since a finite product or quotient of polynomial
determinants is rational. It does not exclude infinite-dimensional realizations. No target
arithmetic Euler factors, zero correspondence, root numbers, automorphy statement, or
Hilbert–Pólya realization are obtained.
