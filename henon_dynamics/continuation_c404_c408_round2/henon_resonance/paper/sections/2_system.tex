\section{One dynamical system and its pullbacks}\label{sec:system}

The inverse of \eqref{eq:H-intro} is the polynomial map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse}
 H^{-1}(x,y)=\bigl(a^{-1}(x^q+g(x)-y),x\bigr).
\end{equation}
All coefficients lie in $\F_q$, so $H$ commutes with $\Phi$. It follows
that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:S}
 S^n=H^{-n}\Phi^n,\qquad
 S(x,y)=\bigl(a^{-1}(x^{q^2}+g(x^q)-y^q),x^q\bigr).
\end{equation}
Postcomposing both morphisms in an equalizer with an isomorphism
preserves that equalizer. Applying $H^n$ to the pair $(S^n,\id)$
therefore gives an equality of closed subschemes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equalizer}
 \Fix(S^n)=E_n.
\end{equation}
This is not only a bijection of geometric point sets.

The map $S$ is finite and radicial, since it is the composition of a
polynomial automorphism with $q$-power Frobenius. Its function-field
degree is $q^2$, entirely inseparable. The degree here is not the
cardinality of a geometric fiber: a radicial map has at most one point
in such a fiber. Neither that degree nor radiciality by itself computes
the fixed-point count.

Let $R=\F_q[x,y]$. We use the commuting $\F_q$-linear ring endomorphisms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:operators}
 T(f)=f\circ H,\qquad
 U(f)=f\circ\Phi=f(x^q,y^q)=f^q,
 \qquad \delta=T-U.
\end{equation}
The equality $U(f)=f^q$ holds because every coefficient of $f$ is fixed
by $q$-power Frobenius. Arbitrary elements of $K[x,y]$ do not satisfy
that identity, which is why the operator calculation is performed in
$R$ before extending scalars for the zero scheme.

Although $T$ and $U$ are ring endomorphisms, their difference $\delta$
is in general only a linear operator. It is neither a ring endomorphism
nor a derivation. Its iterates below mean composition as a linear
operator, and every binomial identity takes place in the algebra of
commuting linear operators on $R$. In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-basic}
 \delta(y)=g(y)-ax,\qquad \delta(x)=y-x^q.
\end{equation}
There is no binomial identity asserted for a difference of nonlinear
maps of the affine plane.
