\section{Model boundaries and bounded exact checks}\label{sec:boundaries}

\subsection{A direct additive-model obstruction}

The first coordinate of $S$ contains the nonzero term
$a^{-1}b x^{mq}$, where $mq$ is not a power of $p$.
Thus $S$ is not additive for the standard vector-group law on $\A^2$.
That coordinate observation alone would not exclude a different
coordinate system. The first-period count gives a stronger but still
limited statement.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:no-vector}
The point dynamical system $S\colon\A^2(K)\to\A^2(K)$ is not conjugate,
even by a set-theoretic bijection, to an algebraic-group endomorphism
of any vector group $\Ga^d(K)$ having finite fixed sets at every iterate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a vector-group endomorphism $\tau$, its fixed points form the
subgroup $\ker(\tau-\id)(K)$ of $\Ga^d(K)$. If finite, this is a
finite-dimensional vector space over $\F_p$, and therefore has
cardinality a power of $p$. On the other hand,
Theorem~\ref{thm:count}, or the first-period elimination in
Section~\ref{sec:count}, gives $N_1=qm$. Since $m>1$ and $p\nmid m$,
this is not a power of $p$. A conjugacy preserves fixed-set
cardinalities, giving a contradiction.
\end{proof}

This elementary obstruction distinguishes the family from the direct
vector-group setting of \cite[Theorem 5.2.5]{bch2024groups}.
It says nothing about finite quotients, other algebraic groups, or a
birational presentation restricted to a smaller domain. In particular,
it is not a theorem excluding every dynamically affine presentation.
Such a classification is not needed for the count proved here and is
not supplied by it.

\subsection{Why the coprimality hypothesis cannot be dropped}

\begin{example}\label{ex:failure}
Take $q=8$, $a=1$, $g(y)=y^6+y^3+1$, and $n=2$, so $p\mid m$.
The geometric count is $2816$, whereas extending
\eqref{eq:count} to these parameters would give $2944$.
\end{example}

\begin{proof}
Work in characteristic two and put $h=y^6+y^3+1+x$.
The commuting-operator identity still gives $T^2-U^2=\delta^2$, and
direct expansion gives
\begin{align*}
 \delta^2(y)
 &=y^{32}h^2+y^{16}h^4+h^6
   +y^{16}h+y^8h^2+h^3+y+x^8.
\end{align*}
The unique top term is $y^{44}$: the first three displayed products
have degrees $44,40,36$, and the remaining degrees are smaller.
The first equation has unique top term $x^{64}$. The quotient-length
and Jacobian arguments of Section~\ref{sec:count}, here with determinant
one, therefore give $64\cdot44=2816$ reduced points.
The unjustified extension of \eqref{eq:count} gives
$(5\cdot8^4+2\cdot8^2)/7=2944$, as claimed.
\end{proof}

This is a nonlinear failure example, not an additive degeneration. It
shows that deleting $p\nmid m$ is invalid; it does not classify every
degree divisible by $p$.

\subsection{Exact checks and their evidentiary role}

The accompanying bounded calculation literally composes $H$ and forms
the two fixed polynomials. It does not construct them from the degree
recurrence. Exact coefficient arithmetic then determines their degrees,
highest homogeneous terms, and Jacobian. Table~\ref{tab:checks} records
the five archived cases. These values are supplementary checks of the
implementation and hypotheses, not the proof of a universally
quantified statement.

For the genuine extension-field examples, we use
$\F_4=\F_2[\alpha]/(\alpha^2+\alpha+1)$ and
\[
 a=\alpha+1,\qquad
 g(y)=\alpha y^3+(\alpha+1)y^2+y+\alpha.
\]
Thus their leading and lower coefficients do not all lie in the prime
field. In the archived encoding, the integers $2$ and $3$ mean
$\alpha$ and $\alpha+1$, respectively; arithmetic is not performed in
the nonfield ring $\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z$.

For the three prime-subfield coefficient cases, an additional F5B
Gr\"obner calculation with SymPy computes the quotient length from a
returned basis. That secondary method was not run for the two genuine
$\F_4$ cases; no extension-field SymPy Gr\"obner check is claimed.
The counts inferred from the coprime leading terms use the same
elementary length principle as the proof and are not an independent
proof of that principle. The checks include a prime-to-$p$ period,
a wild period over a nonprime base size, non-prime-field coefficients,
a second $p$-power level, and an out-of-hypothesis control. They are
not a large census or an empirical extrapolation to all periods.

\subsection{What is, and is not, being counted}

The clock is the positive integer $n$ for the one fixed map $S$ on
$\A^2(K)$. Equivalently, both $H$ and $\Phi$ are iterated $n$ times
in the coincidence condition. This is not the count of ordinary
$H$-periodic points over a fixed finite field, and it is not the
Hasse--Weil point count of a fixed variety under Frobenius alone.
Accordingly, the natural boundary does not assert a failure of
Hasse--Weil rationality.

All lower coefficients of $g$ are permitted, but the monic $y^q$ term,
the coefficient field $\F_q$, $a\ne0$, and $2\le m<q$ with $p\nmid m$
are structural hypotheses. The result is not a classification of all
resonant H\'enon maps, of independently varying two-clock resonances,
or of the case $p\mid m$.
