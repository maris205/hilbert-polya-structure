\section{From actual degrees to geometric points}\label{sec:count}

We now prove Theorem~\ref{thm:count}. Put $Q=q^n$ and
$d=d_n=q^{n-r}D_r$, and write the literal fixed equations as
\[
 F_1=T^n(x)-x^Q,\qquad F_2=T^n(y)-y^Q.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:period-degree} gives unique highest homogeneous
terms $-x^Q$ and $s c_r y^d$, respectively. In particular, their
leading monomials for any graded monomial order are relatively prime.

\subsection{The quotient length}

The coprime-leading-monomial criterion and the standard-monomial basis
are classical polynomial-ideal tools; see \cite{cox2015ideals}.
Their application here can be made explicit. Normalize the polynomials
to be monic and write
\[
 f=x^Q+f_0,\qquad h=y^d+h_0,
\]
where $\deg f_0<Q$ and $\deg h_0<d$. Their sole $S$-polynomial has
the representation
\[
 y^d f-x^Q h=f_0h-h_0f.
\]
Both products on the right have leading monomial strictly smaller
than $x^Qy^d$. This is the standard representation in Buchberger's
criterion, so $f,h$ form a Gr\"obner basis. The monomials not in the
initial ideal $(x^Q,y^d)$ are exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rectangle}
 x^i y^j,\qquad 0\le i<Q,\quad 0\le j<d.
\end{equation}
Their residue classes form a $K$-basis of the quotient. Thus $E_n$
is finite and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:length}
 \dim_K K[x,y]/(F_1,F_2)=Qd.
\end{equation}

This calculation also shows why there is no unaccounted correction at
infinity. Homogenizing each equation to its actual degree yields the
forms $-x^Q$ and $s c_r y^d$ on the line at infinity. They cannot
vanish at the same point of that projective line: simultaneous
vanishing would require both homogeneous coordinates to be zero.
Plane B\'ezout consequently gives the same length $Qd$. Homogenizing
the second equation as if it still had degree $Q$ would retain a
spurious infinity contribution; resolving the resonant cancellation
must precede the count.

\subsection{Reducedness and the first period}

The derivatives of $x^Q$ and $y^Q$ vanish in characteristic $p$.
Moreover,
\[
 DH=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-a&g'(y)\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \det DH=a.
\]
The chain rule therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobian}
 \det\frac{\partial(F_1,F_2)}{\partial(x,y)}
 =\det D(H^n)=a^n\ne0.
\end{equation}
Each point of the finite zero scheme has zero-dimensional regular
local ring. Over the algebraically closed field $K$, that ring is $K$.
The scheme is therefore reduced, and its length is its number of
geometric points. Substituting \eqref{eq:degree-closed} into
\eqref{eq:length} gives
\[
 N_n=Qq^{n-r}D_r
 =q^{2n-r}\frac{(m-1)q^r+(q-m)}{q-1},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:count}. This completes the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:count}.

At $n=1$, the equations are simply $y=x^q$ and $g(y)-ax=0$.
Eliminating $y$ gives $g(x^q)-ax=0$, of degree $qm$ and derivative
$-a$. Thus $N_1=qm$ with simple roots, in agreement with the full
argument. No exceptional convention is required at the first period.
In particular, neither reducedness nor the passage from ideal length
to point count has been inferred from finite computational agreement.
