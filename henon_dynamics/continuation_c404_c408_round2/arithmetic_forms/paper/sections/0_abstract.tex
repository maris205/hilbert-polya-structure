Normalized divisibility Gram matrices at the critical coefficient scale
$a(k)=k^{-1/2}L(k)$ can have a nonzero entrywise limit and a zero
strong-resolvent limit on $\ell^2(\mathbb N)$. We establish the complete
summability dichotomy for positive measurable slowly varying $L$, without
assuming multiplicativity or monotonicity. If
$F(N)=\sum_{k\le N}L(k)^2/k$ diverges, the entries tend to
$\gcd(m,n)/\sqrt{mn}$, while the normalized Gram operators converge to zero
in strong resolvent sense and their norms tend to infinity. The limiting
quadratic form on finitely supported sequences is purely singular: every
nonnegative closable minorant vanishes. An explicit average of prime shifts
proves this statement and supplies low-energy recovery vectors for arbitrary
bounded positive approximants with the same entrywise limit. If $F$ is
bounded, the strong-resolvent limit is instead the nonzero operator
$C^*C/F(\infty)$, where $C$ is rowwise Dirichlet convolution on its maximal
domain. Its proof uses finite-row weak lower bounds and does not assume a
finitely supported graph core. For
$L(x)=(\log(ex))^{-\beta}$, the two branches meet at $\beta=1/2$.
The conclusions concern a fixed Hilbert space and normalization, and
distinguish matrix-entry limits from closed-operator limits.
