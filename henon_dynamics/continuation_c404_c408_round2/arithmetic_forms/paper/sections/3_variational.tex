\section{A variational consequence for positive approximants}
\label{sec:variational}

The recovery vectors in Proposition~\ref{prop:singularity} carry more
information than a single failure of closability. They approximate any
specified finite vector with arbitrarily small energy, which determines
the resolvent limit of every positive entrywise approximation.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:collapse}
Assume $\sum_p r_p^2=\infty$. Let $B_N$ be bounded nonnegative
self-adjoint operators on $\HH$ such that
\[
 \ip{e_m}{B_Ne_n}\longrightarrow K_r(m,n)
 \qquad\text{for every fixed }m,n.
\]
Then $(B_N+\lambda I)^{-1}\to\lambda^{-1}I$ strongly for every
$\lambda>0$, and $\norm{B_N}\to\infty$.
No monotonicity or uniform norm bound on the approximants is required.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each \emph{fixed} $v\in\cc$, finite summation gives
\begin{equation}
 \ip{v}{B_Nv}\longrightarrow q_r[v].
 \label{eq:fixed-energy}
\end{equation}
Let $g\in\HH$. Choose finite $f_j\to g$ and then, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:singularity}, choose finite $v_j$ with
$\norm{v_j-f_j}<1/j$ and $q_r[v_j]<1/j$.
For each $j$, \eqref{eq:fixed-energy} supplies an integer $N_j$
such that
\[
 \abs{\ip{v_j}{B_Nv_j}-q_r[v_j]}<1/j
 \qquad\text{for all }N\ge N_j.
\]
Choose the $N_j$ strictly increasing and tending to infinity.
For $N\ge N_1$, let $j(N)=\max\{j:N_j\le N\}$.
The finitely many smaller $N$ can be assigned any finite vector.
Then
\begin{equation}
 w_N:=v_{j(N)}\longrightarrow g,\qquad
 \ip{w_N}{B_Nw_N}\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:diagonal-recovery}
\end{equation}
This diagonal choice is what permits a moving support; entrywise
convergence alone was used only for each fixed $v_j$.

Fix $\lambda>0$ and $f\in\HH$ and put
$x_N=(B_N+\lambda I)^{-1}f$.
The unique minimizer of
\[
 J_N(x)=\ip{x}{B_Nx}
       +\lambda\norm{x-\lambda^{-1}f}^2
\]
is $x_N$, as follows by expanding the square and using the resolvent
equation. Apply \eqref{eq:diagonal-recovery} to $g=\lambda^{-1}f$.
Positivity and minimality imply
\[
 0\le\lambda\norm{x_N-\lambda^{-1}f}^2
 \le J_N(x_N)\le J_N(w_N)\longrightarrow0.
\]
This proves the strong-resolvent assertion.

Finally $q_r$ is unbounded on the finite unit vectors: otherwise it
would extend to a bounded nonnegative form on $\HH$, making its
restriction a nonzero closable minorant of itself.
For each $M>0$ choose a fixed finite unit vector $u$ with $q_r[u]>2M$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:fixed-energy} gives
$\norm{B_N}\ge\ip{u}{B_Nu}>M$ for all sufficiently large $N$.
Thus the norms tend to infinity, not merely along a subsequence.
\end{proof}

The proposition is a direct relaxation and minimization argument.
It does not apply Simon's monotone form-convergence
theorems~\citep[Section~3]{simon1978canonical} to a nonmonotone family.
It also makes no assertion about arbitrary signed approximants:
nonnegativity is used in the decisive minimizer estimate.
