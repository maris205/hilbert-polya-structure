\section{Conclusion and reproducibility}
\label{sec:conclusion}

At the critical Dirichlet coefficient exponent, the normalization
$F(N)$ separates two different closed-operator limits.
Divergence gives a universal nonzero entrywise GCD form, but the
explicit prime-tail vectors annihilate every closable minorant and
force positive approximants to converge to zero in strong resolvent
sense. Square summability gives a nonzero maximal-convolution limit.
The distinction is between types of limiting objects, not between
two constants in a common norm-convergence formula.

The complete arguments are contained in this article.
The accompanying \texttt{exact\_checks.py} uses Python rational
arithmetic to verify only the finite identities in
Section~\ref{sec:examples}. Its command, outputs and failed-control
history appear in \texttt{BOUNDED\_RECEIPTS.md}.
No random sampling, GPU computation or numerical resolvent extrapolation
is part of the proof.
