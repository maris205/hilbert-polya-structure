\section{Introduction and the critical dichotomy}
\label{sec:introduction}

A positive limit of finite matrix entries need not define a closed
quadratic form on the ambient Hilbert space. Critical divisibility Grams
give an arithmetic instance of this distinction: their first diagonal
entry stays equal to one, but their resolvents can converge strongly to
the resolvent of zero. The issue is therefore not only whether the
limiting matrix is bounded, but whether any nonzero closable part survives.

Throughout, $\HH=\ell^2(\N)$ with its usual coordinate basis $(e_m)$.
Inner products are conjugate-linear in the first argument.
Let $L:[1,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ be measurable, bounded above and away
from zero on compact intervals, and slowly varying:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{L(tx)}{L(x)}\longrightarrow1
 \qquad(x\to\infty),\quad t>0.
 \label{eq:slow}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
 a(k)=k^{-1/2}L(k),\qquad
 F(x)=\sum_{k\le \lfloor x\rfloor}a(k)^2,
 \label{eq:coefficients}
\end{equation}
with an empty sum equal to zero. For an integer $N\ge1$, let
\begin{equation}
 T_N(r,m)=
 \begin{cases}
 a(r/m),&m\mid r\le N,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 A_N=F(N)^{-1}T_N^*T_N.
 \label{eq:gram-definition}
\end{equation}
These are finite matrices extended by zero on $\HH$, so $A_N$ is bounded,
nonnegative and self-adjoint. No uniform bound in $N$ is assumed.

For clarity, a nonnegative form $q$ on $\cc$ is \emph{closable} if
$u_j\to0$ in $\HH$ and $q[u_j-u_k]\to0$ imply $q[u_j]\to0$.
A \emph{closable minorant} is a nonnegative closable form $b$ on $\cc$
such that $b[u]\le q[u]$ there. We call $q$ \emph{purely singular} when
every such minorant is zero. This refers to form relaxation, not to a
singular spectral measure of an already defined self-adjoint operator.

\begin{theorem}[Critical summability dichotomy]
\label{thm:main}
Under \eqref{eq:slow}--\eqref{eq:gram-definition}, exactly one of the
following alternatives holds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $F(N)\to\infty$, then for every fixed $m,n\in\N$,
\begin{equation}
 \ip{e_m}{A_Ne_n}\longrightarrow
 K(m,n):=\frac{\gcd(m,n)}{\sqrt{mn}}.
 \label{eq:critical-kernel}
\end{equation}
The nonnegative form
$q_K[u]=\sum_{m,n}\overline{u(m)}K(m,n)u(n)$ on $\cc$ is nonzero
and purely singular. It has no nonnegative closed extension agreeing
with it on $\cc$. Nevertheless, for every $\lambda>0$,
\begin{equation}
 (A_N+\lambda I)^{-1}f\longrightarrow\lambda^{-1}f
 \quad\text{for every } f\in\HH,
 \qquad \norm{A_N}\longrightarrow\infty.
 \label{eq:divergent-limit}
\end{equation}
\item If $F(N)\to F_\infty<\infty$, define the maximal rowwise
Dirichlet convolution operator by
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 (Cx)(r)&=\sum_{m\mid r}a(r/m)x(m),\\
 D(C)&=\left\{x\in\HH:
  \sum_{r=1}^{\infty}
  \abs{\sum_{m\mid r}a(r/m)x(m)}^2<\infty\right\}.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:maximal-C}
\end{equation}
Then $C$ is densely defined and closed. The operator
$A_\infty=F_\infty^{-1}C^*C$ is nonnegative, self-adjoint and nonzero,
and for every $\lambda>0$ and $f\in\HH$,
\begin{equation}
 (A_N+\lambda I)^{-1}f
 \longrightarrow(A_\infty+\lambda I)^{-1}f.
 \label{eq:summable-limit}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Here and below, strong-resolvent convergence for nonnegative operators is
expressed by the resolvent limits at $-\lambda$, $\lambda>0$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is one endpoint statement with two alternatives.
Its arithmetic ingredient is an explicit prime-tail approximation for
the limiting form. The square-summable alternative uses the maximal domain
in \eqref{eq:maximal-C}, rather than an unproved identification with the
graph closure of finitely supported sequences.

\paragraph{Position relative to earlier work.}
The GCD kernel and its finite positivity are classical.
Aistleitner, Berkes and Seip~\citep{aistleitner2015gcd} give the
finite-product Poisson representation and study finite GCD sums and
spectral norms; the role of the prime-square summability threshold
already appears there. Hilberdink's arbitrary-coefficient Gram identity
\citep[Proposition~2.1]{hilberdink2017singular} supplies the finite algebra
underlying \eqref{eq:gram-definition}. We rederive that algebra, and do
not claim either identity as new.

For power coefficients below the critical line,
Hilberdink and Pushnitski~\citep{hilberdink2023spectral} obtain compact
LCM operators and rescaled-Gram convergence in Schatten classes in their
stated range. The comparison here concerns Sections~2.2--2.3 of their
2021 preprint, where the Gram result assumes $\sigma<1/2$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} addresses what replaces a closed limiting
operator at the critical coefficient exponent, including slowly varying
weights that need not be multiplicative. It does not supply new finite
GCD norm asymptotics or a new diagonalization of LCM operators.

General regular/singular form decomposition and the largest closable
minorant belong to Simon~\citep[Section~2]{simon1978canonical}.
Our prime-tail vectors identify the purely singular case for the present
arithmetic kernel; their resolvent consequence is a direct variational
argument within that classical framework. Simon's monotone convergence
theorems are not invoked as an entrywise convergence theorem.
Yafaev~\citep{yafaev2017semibounded} also emphasizes the distinction
between a semibounded matrix form and its closed-operator realization.
His one-circle Toeplitz closability criterion is not transferred here
to an infinite-prime space.

Section~\ref{sec:product} proves the prime-product form criterion.
Section~\ref{sec:variational} converts its recovery vectors into
resolvent convergence. Sections~\ref{sec:critical} and
\ref{sec:summable} prove the two branches of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
The final sections give a boundary example, finite verification scopes,
and the limits of the fixed-space conclusion.
