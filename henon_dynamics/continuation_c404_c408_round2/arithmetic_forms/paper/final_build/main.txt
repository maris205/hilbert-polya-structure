1    Introduction and the critical dichotomy
A positive limit of finite matrix entries need not define a closed quadratic form on the ambient
Hilbert space. Critical divisibility Grams give an arithmetic instance of this distinction: their
first diagonal entry stays equal to one, but their resolvents can converge strongly to the resolvent
of zero. The issue is therefore not only whether the limiting matrix is bounded, but whether any
nonzero closable part survives.

Position relative to earlier work. The GCD kernel and its finite positivity are classical.
Aistleitner, Berkes and Seip [1] give the finite-product Poisson representation and study finite
GCD sums and spectral norms; the role of the prime-square summability threshold already
appears there. Hilberdink’s arbitrary-coefficient Gram identity [3, Proposition 2.1] supplies the
finite algebra underlying (1.3). We rederive that algebra, and do not claim either identity as
new.
    For power coefficients below the critical line, Hilberdink and Pushnitski [4] obtain compact
LCM operators and rescaled-Gram convergence in Schatten classes in their stated range. The
comparison here concerns Sections 2.2–2.3 of their 2021 preprint, where the Gram result assumes
σ < 1/2. Theorem 1.1 addresses what replaces a closed limiting operator at the critical coefficient
exponent, including slowly varying weights that need not be multiplicative. It does not supply
new finite GCD norm asymptotics or a new diagonalization of LCM operators.
    General regular/singular form decomposition and the largest closable minorant belong to
Simon [5, Section 2]. Our prime-tail vectors identify the purely singular case for the present

3    A variational consequence for positive approximants
The recovery vectors in Proposition 2.3 carry more information than a single failure of closability.
They approximate any specified finite vector with arbitrarily small energy, which determines the
resolvent limit of every positive entrywise approximation.

7    Conclusion and reproducibility
At the critical Dirichlet coefficient exponent, the normalization F (N ) separates two different
closed-operator limits. Divergence gives a universal nonzero entrywise GCD form, but the explicit
prime-tail vectors annihilate every closable minorant and force positive approximants to converge
to zero in strong resolvent sense. Square summability gives a nonzero maximal-convolution limit.
The distinction is between types of limiting objects, not between two constants in a common
norm-convergence formula.

outputs and failed-control history appear in BOUNDED_RECEIPTS.md. No random sampling, GPU
computation or numerical resolvent extrapolation is part of the proof.
