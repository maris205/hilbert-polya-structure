                       The critical second Weyl coefficient
                           of a harmonic delta chain
                                        Anonymous Authors


                                               Abstract
          We determine the second high-energy eigenvalue-counting   Pn coefficient for a half-line
      harmonic chain with positive point interactions at xn = π j=1 j −1 . If the couplings are
      fixed independently of the spectral parameter and satisfy bn /n → κ ∈ (0, ∞), then the
      inclusive count obeys Nb (k 2 ) = k log k + C(κ)k + o(k) as real k → ∞. The coefficient is a
      convergent regularized integral of the integrated density of states of a periodic comparison
      problem with cell length π and coupling κ. Its derivation requires control of a growing
      transition region in which finite delta transmission contributes at order k. We prove a
      uniform finite-chain counting estimate and a local-periodic reduction using geometric blocks,
      an explicit Rayleigh-quotient comparison, and a telescoping bound on the accumulated
      freezing error. The function C is continuous and strictly decreasing, with range (2γ − 1, ∞),
      where γ is Euler’s constant. The formula is stable under arbitrary positive o(n) perturbations
      of the couplings. If bn /n → ∞, the second coefficient is 2γ − 1. If bn /n → 0 and nbn → ∞,
      we prove only that [Nb (k 2 ) − k log k]/k → ∞; no general soft-regime leading asymptotic is
      asserted.


1    Introduction and main results
Point interactions on a chain of shrinking intervals can remain visible in the second term of
a high-energy counting law. On the harmonic chain, the critical balance occurs when the
interaction at the nth vertex has strength comparable to n. We prove that the resulting second
coefficient is determined by the full periodic-cell integrated density of states, rather than by its
free or Dirichlet endpoint.
    The harmonic-chain model is classical. Egger né Endres and Steiner [3] considered constant
positive coupling, proved discreteness, and analyzed in detail the fully decoupled Dirichlet limit.
Its eigenvalues are the squares of positive integers with divisor multiplicities, so its counting
law is an instance of Dirichlet’s divisor problem. General realization and discreteness results
for shrinking-gap point interactions are developed by Kostenko and Malamud [4]. We include
a direct form argument because its quantitative tail bound is needed for the counting proof,
not because compactness itself is new.
    The periodic comparison uses the usual delta-jump and free-propagation matrices; their
constant-coupling specialization and band structure are recalled, for example, in Drabkin,
Kirsch, and Schulz-Baldes [2, Secs. 2.1–2.3]. The spectral comparison work of Bifulco and
Kerner [1] also revisits the harmonic chain. Its modified local Weyl theorem in arXiv v1,
Section 5, Theorem 11 assumes fully Dirichlet coupling. That local theorem does not provide
the global critical second coefficient considered here. These comparisons identify the background
used below; we make no general priority claim concerning all slowly varying periodic-operator
asymptotics.

Lemma 2.2 (Finite-codimension count comparison). Impose zero traces at r specified internal
vertices of the chain. Let NbD (E) be the inclusive eigenvalue count of the restricted form. For
every real E,
                                 Nb (E) − r ≤ NbD (E) ≤ Nb (E).                             (2.3)
The restricted form is the orthogonal sum of the Dirichlet-ended block forms and, if present,
the remaining tail form.
Proof. Each point trace is a bounded linear functional on the form domain. The joint kernel
therefore has codimension at most r. If VE is the span of the original eigenvectors of eigenvalues
at most E, its intersection with that kernel has dimension at least dim VE − r. The Rayleigh
quotient on this intersection is at most E. Conversely, every subspace of the restricted form
domain is a subspace of the original one. The variational characterization of the inclusive
count proves (2.3), including at eigenvalue thresholds. At a cut the corresponding delta term
vanishes; continuity and the zero traces identify the restricted form with the stated direct
sum.

Remark 2.3. An infinite set of cuts is not a finite-codimension perturbation. When the fully
Dirichlet operator is used below, its comparison with Hb is by form-domain inclusion, not by a
finite count error.

6     Asymptotic couplings and the hard and soft sides

order of the limiting operations is important: the spectral frequency tends to infinity before
the comparison couplings approach their limiting values.

7     Scope and reproducibility
The critical two-term law concerns a fixed source quadratic form and its eigenvalue count. Its
main input beyond the classical comparison machinery is the local-periodic reduction with
cumulative o(k) error. The coefficient’s range, its rate-free stability, and the hard and soft
statements are consequences within the same argument. The theorem does not cover arbitrary
cell geometries or give a sharp oscillatory remainder.
    The harmonic-chain Dirichlet benchmark has an exact divisor interpretation, but no
corresponding target arithmetic identification is obtained for the coupled operator. In particular,
a two-term mean counting law does not establish target Euler factors, root numbers, individual
Riemann-zero correspondence, or a Hilbert–Pólya realization. Nor does an o(k) counting
remainder alone imply meromorphic continuation of a spectral zeta function.
    All mathematical arguments needed for the stated theorems are given in the article.
No numerical eigenvalue data, simulation, or experimental validation is used. The source
manuscript is supplied as modular LATEX with a four-entry bibliography; the build receipt
records the initial source hashes, compiler invocation, and actual PDF and log hashes.
