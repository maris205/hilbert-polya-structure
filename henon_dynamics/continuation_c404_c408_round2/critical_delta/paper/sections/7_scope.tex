\section{Scope and reproducibility}
\label{sec:scope}

The critical two-term law concerns a fixed source quadratic
form and its eigenvalue count. Its main input beyond the
classical comparison machinery is the local-periodic
reduction with cumulative $o(k)$ error. The coefficient's
range, its rate-free stability, and the hard and soft
statements are consequences within the same argument.
The theorem does not cover arbitrary cell geometries or
give a sharp oscillatory remainder.

The harmonic-chain Dirichlet benchmark has an exact
divisor interpretation, but no corresponding target
arithmetic identification is obtained for the coupled
operator. In particular, a two-term mean counting law
does not establish target Euler factors, root numbers,
individual Riemann-zero correspondence, or a
Hilbert--P\'olya realization. Nor does an $o(k)$ counting
remainder alone imply meromorphic continuation of a
spectral zeta function.

All mathematical arguments needed for the stated theorems
are given in the article. No numerical eigenvalue data,
simulation, or experimental validation is used. The
source manuscript is supplied as modular \LaTeX{} with
a four-entry bibliography; the build receipt records
the initial source hashes, compiler invocation, and
actual PDF and log hashes.
