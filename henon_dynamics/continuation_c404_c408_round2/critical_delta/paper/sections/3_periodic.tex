\section{The periodic cell and a uniform finite-chain estimate}
\label{sec:periodic}

The cell forms \eqref{eq:fiber-form} are closed on their stated
domains, have compact resolvent, and depend continuously on their
parameters. We first record bounds with the normalization needed
for the infinite-chain calculation.

\begin{lemma}[Cell bounds and band bottom]
\label{lem:cell-bounds}
For every $\kappa>0$, $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$, and $z\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 \lfloor z\rfloor\le\nu_\kappa(z,\theta)
                         \le\lfloor z\rfloor+1,
 \qquad
 \lfloor z\rfloor\le J_\kappa(z)\le z.
 \label{eq:cell-bounds}
\end{equation}
There is a unique $r(\kappa)\in(0,1)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 r(\kappa)\tan\frac{\pi r(\kappa)}2=\frac\kappa2.
 \label{eq:band-bottom}
\end{equation}
It is continuous and strictly increasing in $\kappa$, and
\begin{equation}
 J_\kappa(z)=0\quad(0\le z<r(\kappa)),
 \qquad J_\kappa(z)>0\quad(z>r(\kappa)).
 \label{eq:ids-gap}
\end{equation}
The function $J_\kappa$ is nondecreasing in $z$ and nonincreasing
in $\kappa$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Dirichlet domain $H^1_0(0,\pi)$ is the kernel of the single
trace $u\mapsto u(0)$ on $\D_\theta$. It is therefore a
codimension-one restriction, with eigenvalues $j^2$, $j\ge1$.
The same variational argument as in Lemma~\ref{lem:cuts} proves
the first two-sided bound in \eqref{eq:cell-bounds}.
Increasing $\kappa$ increases the form on its fixed domain, so
all fiber eigenvalues are nondecreasing in $\kappa$.
At $\kappa=0$, the eigenfrequencies are
$\abs{2j+\theta/\pi}$, $j\in\Z$. Tonelli's theorem gives
\begin{align*}
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
    \sum_{j\in\Z}\ind_{\{\abs{2j+\theta/\pi}\le z\}}\dd\theta
 &=\frac12\sum_{j\in\Z}\int_0^2
                     \ind_{\{\abs{2j+s}\le z\}}\dd s\\
 &=\frac12\int_\R\ind_{[-z,z]}(s)\dd s=z.
\end{align*}
Comparison with these free forms and integration of the Dirichlet
lower bound prove the remaining inequalities in
\eqref{eq:cell-bounds}. Monotonicity in energy is immediate from
the definition of the count.

The endpoint estimate on a cell gives, uniformly in $\theta$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{u}_2^2\le2\pi\abs{u(0)}^2+2\pi^2\norm{u'}_2^2
       \le\left(\frac{2\pi}{\kappa}+2\pi^2\right)
                         q_{\kappa,\theta}[u].
 \label{eq:fiber-gap-estimate}
\end{equation}
There is consequently a positive lower bound for all fiber
eigenvalues. A quasiperiodic function $u$ has periodic modulus
$\abs{u}$, with the same $L^2$ norm and endpoint penalty and with
no larger derivative energy. Thus the bottom over all fibers is
the periodic ground eigenvalue.

In the periodic fiber, the positive symmetric solution of the
interior eigenvalue equation is a multiple of
$\cos(z(x-\pi/2))$. Its endpoint derivative condition is
\[
 u'(0)-u'(\pi)=\kappa u(0),
\]
which gives $2z\sin(\pi z/2)=\kappa\cos(\pi z/2)$.
The function $z\mapsto z\tan(\pi z/2)$ increases continuously
from zero to infinity on $(0,1)$, so it has the unique solution
\eqref{eq:band-bottom}. The corresponding eigenfunction is
strictly positive, and hence is the ground state by the
one-dimensional variational/Sturm characterization. This proves
the vanishing in \eqref{eq:ids-gap} and the asserted dependence
of $r$ on $\kappa$.

For completeness, continuity of the fiber eigenvalues in $\theta$
follows on writing $u(x)=e^{\ii\theta x/\pi}v(x)$ with $v$
periodic. The form becomes
\[
 \int_0^\pi\abs{v'(x)+\ii\theta v(x)/\pi}^2\dd x
       +\kappa\abs{v(0)}^2
\]
on a fixed domain. If $z>r(\kappa)$, the periodic ground
eigenvalue is strictly below $z^2$, so the same is true on an
open set of nearby phases. The phase integral is therefore
positive, proving the second statement in \eqref{eq:ids-gap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Phase equation and continuity of the cell count]
\label{lem:phase}
At a fixed positive frequency $z$, the phases for which $z^2$
is a fiber eigenvalue satisfy
\begin{equation}
 D_\kappa(z):=\cos(\pi z)+\frac{\kappa}{2z}\sin(\pi z)
                   =\cos\theta.
 \label{eq:discriminant}
\end{equation}
There are at most two such phases modulo $2\pi$.
The integrated count $J_\kappa(z)$ is continuous in $z\ge0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the state vector $(u,u')^{\mathsf T}$, propagation across
one interval and passage through its terminal delta interaction
are represented by
\begin{equation}
 P_z=\begin{pmatrix}
       \cos(\pi z)&z^{-1}\sin(\pi z)\\
       -z\sin(\pi z)&\cos(\pi z)
     \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 K_\kappa=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\\kappa&1\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:transfer-matrices}
\end{equation}
Both matrices have determinant one. The quasiperiodic value
and derivative conditions obtained from \eqref{eq:fiber-form}
state that $e^{\ii\theta}$ is an eigenvalue of $K_\kappa P_z$.
Taking the trace proves \eqref{eq:discriminant}.

At an integer $z=m\ge1$, $P_z=(-1)^m I$, but
$K_\kappa\ne I$. Thus the transfer matrix is a nontrivial
shear with the sole eigenphase $0$ or $\pi$, and its eigenspace
has dimension one. No additional phase or double free eigenspace
is lost at those frequencies. At zero, the continuous matrix
limit has half-trace $1+\kappa\pi/2>1$, consistently with
\eqref{eq:fiber-gap-estimate}.

Fix $z_0>0$. Except for the at most two phases in
\eqref{eq:discriminant} at $z_0$, the fiber count is constant
for $z$ sufficiently close to $z_0$. On a bounded interval of
frequencies the first bound in \eqref{eq:cell-bounds} provides
a common integrable bound on the phase counts. Dominated
convergence proves continuity of $J_\kappa$ at $z_0$; the
uniform gap gives continuity at zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform finite-chain counting error]
\label{prop:finite-chain}
Let $N^D_{M,\kappa}(z^2)$ be the inclusive count on a chain
of $M$ cells of length $\pi$, with coupling $\kappa$ at every
internal junction and Dirichlet conditions at both ends.
For all integers $M\ge1$, all $\kappa>0$, and all $z\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 \abs{N^D_{M,\kappa}(z^2)-M J_\kappa(z)}\le5.
 \label{eq:finite-chain-error}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At fixed $z>0$, the fiber count is locally constant in $\theta$
away from the phases in \eqref{eq:discriminant}. This follows
from continuity of the eigenvalues after the fixed-domain gauge
used above. By \eqref{eq:cell-bounds}, the difference
\[
 h(\theta)=\nu_\kappa(z,\theta)-\lfloor z\rfloor
\]
takes only the values zero and one. With at most two exceptional
phases, it is the indicator of a circle arc or its complement,
or a constant function, up to values at the exceptional points.
The number of points of a uniform $M$-point phase mesh in a
circle arc differs from $M$ times its normalized length by at
most two. Altering values at at most two endpoints changes the
sum by at most two more. Hence
\begin{equation}
 \abs{\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\nu_\kappa(z,2\pi j/M)
                              -M J_\kappa(z)}\le4.
 \label{eq:phase-mesh}
\end{equation}
The loose constant covers inclusive endpoint counts as well.

Join the ends of an $M$-cell chain into a ring, with one delta
interaction of strength $\kappa$ at each of its $M$ junctions.
Translation by one cell is unitary, has order $M$, and commutes
with the form. Its finite Fourier decomposition gives exactly
the fibers at phases $2\pi j/M$. On each sector the norm and
energy are both $M$ times their one-cell counterparts, so
neither the eigenvalue nor the coupling is rescaled.
The sum in \eqref{eq:phase-mesh} is therefore the ring count.

Imposing zero trace at one specified ring junction has
codimension one and cuts the ring to the Dirichlet-ended
chain. The delta term at that junction vanishes, and the
restricted form is exactly the chain form. Its count lies
between the ring count minus one and the ring count.
Combining this fact with \eqref{eq:phase-mesh} gives
\eqref{eq:finite-chain-error}. At $z=0$, all counts vanish
by the positive gap, so the same estimate holds.
\end{proof}

The periodic ingredients above are included to fix normalization
and threshold conventions. The essential property for the
nonperiodic problem is that the error in
\eqref{eq:finite-chain-error} is independent of the number of
cells and of the test frequency.
