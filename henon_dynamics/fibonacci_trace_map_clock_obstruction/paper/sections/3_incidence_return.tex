\section{Marked spectral incidence and closed return}

The length-\(q_k\) Floquet discriminant is \(d_k(E)\).  Let \(\theta\) denote
the total Floquet phase across the entire \(q_k\)-site cell, so that a Bloch
solution satisfies \(M_k(E)\psi=e^{i\theta}\psi\).  Since
\(\det M_k=1\), its spectral equation is
\(d_k(E)=2\cos\theta\).  Band edges have \(\theta=0,\pi\), while
\(\theta=\pi/2\) gives the discriminant-zero condition \(d_k(E)=0\).
By \eqref{eq:iterate-line}, these are one-coordinate section
incidences:
\begin{equation}
T^k\ell_\lambda(E)\in\{x=-1,0,1\}.
\label{eq:section}
\end{equation}
A return after \(m\) trace-map steps instead requires all three equations
\begin{equation}
d_m=d_0,\qquad d_{m-1}=d_{-1},\qquad d_{m-2}=2.
\label{eq:return}
\end{equation}
Neither \(m=k\) nor \(m=q_k\) turns \eqref{eq:section} into
\eqref{eq:return}.

\begin{lemma}[Escape region]
If three consecutive half traces satisfy
\begin{equation}
1<|x_{n-2}|<|x_{n-1}|<|x_n|,
\label{eq:escape-region}
\end{equation}
then
\[
|x_{j+1}|>|x_j||x_{j-1}|
\]
for every \(j\ge n\).  In particular, there is \(c>0\) such that
\(\log|x_{n+r}|\ge cF_{r+2}\) for all \(r\ge0\); the orbit grows
super-exponentially in trace-map time.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The reverse triangle inequality and the trace recursion give
\[
|x_{n+1}|\ge2|x_n||x_{n-1}|-|x_{n-2}|
>|x_n||x_{n-1}|.
\]
The last inequality follows from
\(|x_n||x_{n-1}|>|x_{n-2}|\).  It implies the strict ordering needed to
propagate \eqref{eq:escape-region}.  Set
\(a_r=\log|x_{n+r}|\).  Then \(a_{r+1}>a_r+a_{r-1}\).  Taking
\(c=\min(a_{-1},a_0)>0\), induction gives
\(a_r\ge cF_{r+2}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two exact nonreturns]
At \(\lambda=1\), the first nontrivial discriminant is
\begin{equation}
d_1(E)=E(E-1)-2.
\end{equation}
The band edge \(E=0\), for which \(d_1=-2\), and the discriminant-zero root
\(E=-1\),
for which \(d_1=0\), have unbounded trace-map orbits.  In particular, neither
point satisfies \eqref{eq:return} for any \(m\ge1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Starting at index \(-2\), the two half-trace sequences are respectively
\[
1,0,-\tfrac12,-1,1,-\tfrac32,-2,5,-\tfrac{37}{2},-183,\ldots
\]
and
\[
1,-\tfrac12,-1,0,\tfrac12,1,1,\tfrac32,2,5,\ldots.
\]
Each contains the strict escape triple
\((3/2,2,5)\) in absolute value.  The escape lemma applies.
\end{proof}

These are exact spectral-section energies of the first finite approximant.
Their escaping orbits mean, in particular, that they are not being asserted
to lie in the spectrum of the infinite Fibonacci Hamiltonian.

\subsection{Exact factor audit}

For \(c\in\{0,2,-2\}\), define \(f_{k,c}=d_k-c\).  At coupling one the
registered calculation forms
\begin{equation}
\gcd\bigl(f_{k,c},d_m-d_0,d_{m-1}-d_{-1},d_{m-2}-2\bigr)
\label{eq:simultaneous-gcd}
\end{equation}
in \(\F_{1000003}[E]\), for \(1\le k\le8\) and
\(m\in\{k,q_k\}\).  All \(8\cdot3\cdot2=48\) gcds have degree zero.

Because \(f_{k,c}\) is monic, any common factor over \(\Q[E]\) can be chosen
monic in \(\Z[E]\).  Division of the other integral polynomials by this monic
factor stays integral, and reduction modulo a prime preserves its positive
degree.  Hence gcd one in \eqref{eq:simultaneous-gcd} excludes every common
root over \(\overline{\Q}\) for the frozen four equations.  A separate
implementation, using a different polynomial representation, verifies all 48
rows at \(p=1000033\) and 30 systems directly in \(\Q[E]\) for \(k\le5\).

This certificate concerns the return equations \eqref{eq:return}.  It cannot
exclude a zero of a different energy-dependent Fredholm determinant arising
from cancellations among global trace coefficients.

\subsection{An explicit boundary/closed comparison}

Casdagli proves a Markov coding in his \(V_{\rm C}\ge8\) convention
\citep[Secs.~2--3]{casdagli1986}.  Centering the potential gives
\(E_{\rm C}=E-\lambda/2\), \(V_{\rm C}=\lambda/2\), with an exchange of the
first two seed coordinates; hence this source regime corresponds to
\(\lambda\ge16\) for positive coupling.  Its spectral-band language uses the
source-faithful ten-state graph.  With rows as current states and columns as
next states, define \(A_{10}\) by
\begin{align}
1&\to\{3,7,10\},&2&\to\{4,8,9\},&
3&\to6,&4&\to5,\nonumber\\
5&\to1,&6&\to2,&7&\to1,&8&\to1,&
9&\to2,&10&\to2.
\label{eq:casdagli-data}
\end{align}
The endpoint vectors obtained from Casdagli's equation~(3.1) are
\[
u_{10}=e_1+e_6,\qquad v_{10}=e_1+e_2+e_3+e_4.
\]
Casdagli uses the convention \(F_0=F_1=1\).  Translated to our standard
convention, the endpoint-constrained band words of \(n\) symbols (thus
\(n-1\) edges) selected by \(u_{10},v_{10}\) have count \(F_{n+1}\).
Setting \(n=k+1\) gives the project convention
\(q_k=F_{k+2}\), and exact algebra gives
\begin{equation}
u_{10}^\top A_{10}^kv_{10}=F_{k+2},\qquad
u_{10}^\top(I-zA_{10})^{-1}v_{10}=\frac{1+z}{1-z-z^2}.
\label{eq:boundary-series}
\end{equation}
The closed paths instead give the Bowen--Lanford zeta
\begin{align}
Z_{\AM}(z)
&=\exp\left(\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{z^n}{n}\Tr A_{10}^n\right)
=\frac1{\det(I-zA_{10})},\\
\det(I-zA_{10})
&=(1+z)^2(1-z+z^2)(1-z-z^2).
\label{eq:closed-zeta}
\end{align}
Thus the marked spectral-band path count in this large-coupling coding and
the closed-return zeta share the Fibonacci
factor \(1-z-z^2\), but they carry different boundary information.  After
rational continuation to \(z=-1\), the boundary function has a simple zero
whereas \(Z_{\AM}\) has a double pole.

Casdagli also gives a six-symbol quotient.  The certificate constructs its
class-incidence matrix \(Q\) and verifies
\[
A_{10}Q=QA_6,\qquad u_{10}^\top Q=u_6^\top,\qquad Qv_6=v_{10}.
\]
For the marked language the initial quotient symbol \(6\) must be decorated
with its lift to the old geometric state \(R_6\), rather than \(R_9\) or
\(R_{10}\).  Arbitrary energy-dependent weights need not descend: the full
operator-quotient argument is valid when
\(L_{10}(E)Q=QL_6(E)\).  We therefore keep the ten-state presentation as the
primary spectral object.

Equation \eqref{eq:boundary-series} counts marked admissible paths; it is not
an identity for the energy polynomial \(d_k(E)\).  This distinction motivates,
rather than proves, an energy-dependent boundary operator.
