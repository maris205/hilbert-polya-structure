# Selected-prime good reduction and point-count comparison

## Theorem

Let (p\in\{5,11,13\}), (R=\mathbb Z_p), and let (E) be the
ordered-edge (D_7)-curve of the frozen H\'enon septic.  Put

\[
B=E/\langle\tau\rangle,
\qquad C=E/\langle J\rangle,
\qquad J(x,y)=(x,a-x^2-y).
\]

Then (B,E,C) have good reduction over (R).  If \(\mathscr X/R\) is the
integral projective plane closure of (P(\sigma,x)=0), the smooth proper
model \(\mathcal C/R\) obtained as \(\mathcal E/\langle J\rangle\) is the
normalization of \(\mathscr X\), and its special fibre is the normalization
of \(\mathscr X_{\mathbb F_p}\).  Consequently, for every (r\geq1),

\[
\#\mathcal C(\mathbb F_{p^r})
=A_{p,r}+7+\epsilon_{p,r},
\]

where (A_{p,r}) is the affine solution count for (P=0), and

\[
\epsilon_{p,r}=
\begin{cases}
+1,&-7\text{ is a square in }\mathbb F_{p^r},\\
-1,&-7\text{ is a nonsquare in }\mathbb F_{p^r}.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus the three degree-six polynomials recorded in this project are the
genuine Hasse--Weil local numerators of (C), and

\[
L_{E,p}(T)=L_{B,p}(T)L_{C,p}(T)^2
\]

gives the genuine local numerator of (E), for these three primes only.

## 1. The smooth genus-two base

The rotation quotient is

\[
\mathcal B:\quad w^2=Q_6(\sigma).
\]

For the three selected primes,

\[
p\ne2,
\qquad
p\nmid\operatorname{Disc}(Q_6)=2^{63}\cdot97.
\]

Hence (Q_6\bmod p) is squarefree.  The second standard hyperelliptic chart,

\[
t=\sigma^{-1},\qquad v=w/\sigma^3,
\qquad v^2=t^6Q_6(1/t),
\]

has (v^2=64) at (t=0), so its two infinity points (v=\pm8) are
separated and smooth.  These charts give a smooth proper, geometrically
integral genus-two model \(\mathcal B/R\).  In particular, the algebraic
constant field of
\(\mathbb F_p(\mathcal B_{\mathbb F_p})\) is exactly \(\mathbb F_p\).

## 2. Excluding vertical inertia in the cyclic cover

In characteristic zero, (E\to B) is a connected, unramified cyclic
degree-seven cover.  Normalize \(\mathcal B\) in \(\mathbb Q_p(E)\), and
consider the henselization of the DVR at the generic point of the special
fibre.  Its residue field is

\[
k=\mathbb F_p(\mathcal B_{\mathbb F_p}).
\]

The decomposition and inertia groups are subgroups of (C_7).  If inertia
were nontrivial, then it would be all of (C_7); the local extension would
have (e=7), (f=1), and would be totally tamely ramified because (p\ne7).
The tame inertia character would then embed (C_7) into (k^\times), forcing
\(\mu_7\subset k\).  Since (k\) has constant field \(\mathbb F_p\), this
would imply \(\mu_7\subset\mathbb F_p\), or (7\mid p-1).  This is false for
(p=5,11,13).  Hence the vertical inertia is trivial.

There is no horizontal inertia: all characteristic-zero inertia of
(E\to\mathbb P^1_\sigma) is generated by reflections, and a reflection
meets the rotation subgroup trivially; the normalized node and infinity
branches are unramified as well.  The base \(\mathcal B\) is regular and
excellent, its normalization is finite and normal, and the finite map is
unramified at every codimension-one point.  Purity of the branch locus
therefore gives a finite \(\acute{e}\)tale cyclic cover

\[
\mathcal E\longrightarrow\mathcal B
\]

of degree seven.  Thus \(\mathcal E/R\) is smooth and proper.  The use of
purity here is precisely under the regular-target, normal-source,
quasi-finite, codimension-one-unramified hypotheses of Stacks Project,
Tag 0BMB.

The geometric connected-component count of a proper smooth family is locally
constant.  Since the generic fibre (E_{\mathbb Q_p}) is geometrically
connected, so is \(\mathcal E_{\overline{\mathbb F}_p}\).  Smoothness then
implies that the special fibre is geometrically integral.

## 3. Extending the reflection and taking the quotient

The reflection (J) normalizes \(\langle\tau\rangle\) and induces the
hyperelliptic involution (w\mapsto-w) on (B); this formula extends on both
charts of \(\mathcal B\).  Functoriality of integral closure then extends
(J) uniquely to \(\mathcal E\).  Define

\[
\mathcal C=\mathcal E/\langle J\rangle.
\]

The quotient is proper.  Away from the fixed locus it is \(\acute{e}\)tale
locally smooth.  At a geometric fixed point, strict henselization and
completion give a relative parameter (z) with

\[
J(z)=-z,
\]

because (2\in R^\times).  The invariant completed local ring is

\[
R^{\mathrm{sh}}[[z]]^{\langle J\rangle}
=R^{\mathrm{sh}}[[z^2]],
\]

which is again a smooth relative one-dimensional local ring.  Therefore
\(\mathcal C/R\) is smooth and proper, proving good reduction of (C).

## 4. Total-space normalization in two charts

On the affine chart (Z\ne0), let

\[
A=R[\sigma,x]/(P).
\]

The polynomial (P) is monic in (x), so (A) is finite integral over
(R[\sigma]).  If (M=\mathbb Q_p(C)), the coordinate ring of the preimage
of \(\mathbb A^1_R\) in \(\mathcal C\) is the integral closure of
(R[\sigma]) in (M).  Transitivity of integrality shows that this is also
the integral closure of (A) in (M).

On the chart (S\ne0), put

\[
t=1/\sigma,qquad y=x/\sigma,qquad
F(t,y)=t^7P(1/t,y/t).
\]

The polynomial (F) is monic of degree seven in (y), and the same argument
identifies \(\mathcal C\) with the normalization of
(R[t,y]/(F)).  Finally,

\[
P^h(S,X,0)=(X-S)^4(X+S)^3,
\]

so every point at infinity has (S\ne0); there is no omitted point with
(S=0).  The two normalizations glue and prove

\[
\mathcal C\simeq\operatorname{Norm}(\mathscr X).
\]

## 5. Comparison after reduction

The following exact specializations are irreducible:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
p&\sigma_0&P(\sigma_0,x)\bmod p\\ \hline
5&0&x^7-x^4+x^3-2x^2+2x-2,\\
11&0&x^7+4x^4+x^3-2x^2+2x+3,\\
13&1&x^7-x^6+5x^5-x^4-3x^3-5x+1.
\end{array}
\]

Because (P) is monic in (x), Gauss's lemma shows that one irreducible
specialization rules out a factorization over
\(\mathbb F_p(\sigma)[x]\).  Thus the special plane curve is integral and
has degree seven over \(\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb F_p}\).  The smooth quotient
special fibre also has degree seven over that line.  The finite comparison
map therefore has function-field degree (7/7=1); it is birational.  Since
\(\mathcal C_{\mathbb F_p}\) is normal,

\[
\mathcal C_{\mathbb F_p}
=\operatorname{Norm}(\mathscr X_{\mathbb F_p}).
\]

Notice the exact scope: irreducibility over \(\mathbb F_p(\sigma)\) proves
that the plane special fibre is integral.  Geometric integrality of the plane
model is neither inferred from that statement nor needed for the comparison.

## 6. The branch correction

The affine singular-projection calculation leaves only
(4\sigma-9=0).  Direct residual gcds are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
p&\gcd_x(P(9/4,x),P_x(9/4,x))\\ \hline
5&x+1,\\
11&x-3,\\
13&x+3.
\end{array}
\]

Each is (x-1/4) in its field.  The tangent-cone discriminant is
(-7\ne0), so this is the unique affine ordinary node.  The affine count
includes it once; its normalization has two rational preimages if (-7) is
a square and none if it is a nonsquare.  Its correction is therefore
\(\epsilon_{p,r}\).

The two plane points at infinity have four and three branches.  The exact
successive initial forms are

\[
\begin{aligned}
&8z(t+z)^2(2t+z),
&&16(-2t+z)(-t+z)^2,\\
&-4t^2w(t^2+2w),
&&-8t(t^2-2w)(t^2-w).
\end{aligned}
\]

All coefficients that separate the seven final branch labels are units at
(p=5,11,13).  Hence all seven normalization points at infinity are already
defined over \(\mathbb F_p\) and contribute seven points over every
extension.  There are no other affine or infinite defects, proving the
point-count formula.

## Claim boundary

- The theorem is proved only for (p=5,11,13); it is not a blanket
  good-reduction theorem away from (2,7,97).
- The proof uses the genuine chronological (C_7)-cover and never replaces
  it by an averaged transition matrix.
- The resulting local factors remain fixed-period arithmetic data.  They do
  not define a cross-period dynamical zeta function or a Hilbert--P\'olya
  operator.
