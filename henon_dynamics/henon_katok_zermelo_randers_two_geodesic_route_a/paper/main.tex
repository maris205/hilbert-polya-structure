We give a cutoff-free closed-geodesic classification for rotational Zermelo
navigation on the unit round two-sphere.  An irrational wind leaves exactly
the two oriented equators, with periods $2\pi/(1\pm\epsilon)$ and explicit
transverse Poincare determinants.

\section{Navigation convention}
Let $h$ be the round metric of curvature one on $S^2$, with longitude of
period $2\pi$.  Let $W=\epsilon\partial_\phi$ and
$0<|\epsilon|<1$.  Since $|W|_h\le |\epsilon|<1$, translating the
$h$-unit circle in each tangent plane by $W$ defines a strongly convex
Randers metric $\FF$.  We orient its geodesics and use the clock
$\FF(\dot\gamma)=1$.  The signs $+$ and $-$ refer to the two directions of
the round equator before adding the wind.

On the nonzero cotangent bundle $T^*S^2$, the dual navigation Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual}
 \FF^*(x,\xi)=|\xi|_h+\xi(W).
\end{equation}
The convention in~\eqref{eq:dual} is equivalent to the translated
indicatrix convention above and fixes every sign in the period formulas.

\begin{lemma}[commuting flow factorization]\label{lem:factor}
Let $\Phi_t$ be rotation generated by $W$.  Every unit $\FF$-geodesic,
and no other curve, has the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:navigation}
 \gamma(t)=\Phi_t(\rho(t)),
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is a unit-speed round great circle.  Moreover, $\FF$ has
constant flag curvature one.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The cotangent lift of $W$ preserves the round cometric because $W$ is
Killing.  Hence the two homogeneous Hamiltonians in~\eqref{eq:dual} Poisson
commute.  Their flows compose in either order: the first projects to round
unit cogeodesic motion and the second to $\Phi_t$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:navigation} and its converse.  The navigation curvature theorem
for a Killing wind on a curvature-one background gives flag curvature one.
\end{proof}

\section{The complete irrational ledger}

\begin{theorem}[two-geodesic theorem]\label{thm:main}
If $\epsilon\notin\mathbb Q$, the complete oriented prime closed-geodesic
ledger of $(S^2,\FF)$ consists of exactly the two equators.  Their least
periods are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:periods}
 T_+=\frac{2\pi}{1+\epsilon},\qquad
 T_-=\frac{2\pi}{1-\epsilon}.
\end{equation}
In canonical transverse Jacobi coordinates their Poincare maps have
multipliers $e^{\pm iT_+}$ and $e^{\pm iT_-}$, respectively, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:det}
 \det(I-P(T))=2-2\cos T=4\sin^2(T/2).
\end{equation}
Both prime orbits are nondegenerate.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The oriented round unit tangent bundle is $SO(3)$; represent a frame by
$g$.  If $R_z(\alpha)$ is rotation through $\alpha$ about $e_3$, round
geodesic motion is $gR_z(t)$ and the wind acts by $R_z(\epsilon t)g$.
Consequently a return at $T>0$ is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:return}
 R_z(\epsilon T)gR_z(T)=g,
 \qquad R_z(\epsilon T)=gR_z(-T)g^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The rotation on the left of the second equality has axis $e_3$; the
conjugated right rotation has axis $ge_3$.  At a non-equatorial frame these
unoriented axes are distinct.  Thus equality forces both rotations to be
the identity, including at angle $\pi$, where a nontrivial rotation still
determines its unoriented axis.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closure}
 T=2\pi m,\qquad \epsilon T=2\pi n,\qquad m>0,\quad m,n\in\ZZ.
\end{equation}
This is impossible for irrational $\epsilon$.

The exceptional frames are precisely the two equators, for which
$ge_3=\pm e_3$ and the unoriented axes coincide.  Their signed angular
velocities are $1+\epsilon$ and
$-1+\epsilon$.  Since $|\epsilon|<1$, their positive least periods are
exactly~\eqref{eq:periods}.

Flag curvature one makes a scalar transverse Jacobi coefficient satisfy
$j''+j=0$.  In the returning canonical normal frame its fundamental matrix
is
\[
 P(T)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos T&\sin T\\-\sin T&\cos T\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This gives the characteristic polynomial
$z^2-2(\cos T)z+1$ and~\eqref{eq:det}.  If an irrational-wind equator were
degenerate, $T_\pm/(2\pi)=1/(1\pm\epsilon)$ would be an integer, forcing
$\epsilon$ to be rational.  Hence both determinants are nonzero.
\end{proof}

The phrase ``exactly two'' counts orientation.  The Randers metric is not
reversible, so quotienting the two equators by direction would change the
dynamical object.

\section{Complete boundary atlas}

\begin{theorem}[rational and singular faces]\label{thm:boundary}
Write $\epsilon=p/q$ in lowest terms, where $0<|p|<q$.  Every
non-equatorial geodesic has least period $2\pi q$.  The two equators instead
have least periods
\[
 \frac{2\pi q}{q+p},\qquad \frac{2\pi q}{q-p}.
\]
At $\epsilon=0$ the metric is the round Zoll metric and all great circles
have least period $2\pi$.  Longitude reflection conjugates $\epsilon$ to
$-\epsilon$ and exchanges the oriented equators.  At $|\epsilon|=1$ global
strong convexity fails; $|\epsilon|\ge1$ is outside the theorem.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a non-equatorial frame, equations~\eqref{eq:closure} are necessary and
sufficient.  Substitution of $p/q$ shows that $q$ divides $m$, so the least
positive return is $2\pi q$.  On the equator the shared-axis calculation
used in~\eqref{eq:periods} gives the two displayed values; they must not be
replaced by the generic period.  The zero-wind and reflection claims follow
directly from the indicatrix.  Finally, at the equator, where $|W|_h$
attains its maximum, the strict navigation inequality fails when
$|\epsilon|=1$.
\end{proof}

An equator on the rational face is degenerate exactly when
$q/(q\pm p)$ is an integer.  This is a source resonance statement, not an
arithmetic-prime correspondence.  This section is the \emph{rational
boundary owner} added in revision round one.
