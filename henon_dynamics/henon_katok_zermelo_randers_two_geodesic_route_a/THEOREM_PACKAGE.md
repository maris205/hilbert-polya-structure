# HCS-C339 theorem package

## Frozen convention

Let (h) be the unit round metric on (S^2), with longitude of period
(2\pi), and let (W=\epsilon\partial_\phi).  Assume
(0<|\epsilon|<1), so (|W|_h<1) everywhere.  The navigation Randers metric
(F_\epsilon) is defined by declaring that its unit indicatrix at (x) is
(W(x)+\{v:|v|_h=1\}).  Geodesics are oriented and parametrized with
(F_\epsilon(\dot\gamma)=1).  The labels (+) and (-) mean the two
round-equatorial directions before the wind is added.

## Main theorem

Let (\Phi_t) be the rotation generated by (W).

1. Every unit (F_\epsilon)-geodesic has the form

   \[
   \gamma(t)=\Phi_t(\rho(t)),
   \]

   for a unit-speed round great circle (\rho), and every such curve is a
   unit (F_\epsilon)-geodesic.  The metric has constant flag curvature one.
2. If (\epsilon\notin\mathbb Q), the complete oriented prime closed-
   geodesic ledger consists of exactly the two equators.  Their least periods
   are

   \[
   T_+=\frac{2\pi}{1+\epsilon},\qquad
   T_-=\frac{2\pi}{1-\epsilon}.
   \]
3. In canonical transverse Jacobi coordinates, the linear Poincare map of a
   closed orbit of period (T) is

   \[
   P(T)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos T&\sin T\\-\sin T&\cos T\end{pmatrix}.
   \]
   Consequently its multipliers are (e^{\pm iT}) and

   \[
   \det(I-P(T))=2-2\cos T=4\sin^2(T/2).
   \]
   Both irrational-wind equators are nondegenerate.
4. If (\epsilon=p/q\) in lowest terms, (0<|p|<q), every
   non-equatorial oriented geodesic has least period (2\pi q).  The two
   equatorial least periods remain (2\pi q/(q+p)) and
   (2\pi q/(q-p)), which need not equal the generic period.

## Proof

On (T^*S^2\setminus0), the dual navigation Hamiltonian is

\[
F_\epsilon^*(x,\xi)=|\xi|_h+\xi(W).
\]

The two summands Poisson commute because (W) is Killing: its cotangent lift
preserves the round cometric.  Their Hamiltonian flows therefore commute.
The first is the unit round cogeodesic flow and the second is the cotangent
lift of (\Phi_t).  Projecting their product proves the formula in part 1,
including its converse.  The navigation curvature theorem for a Killing wind
on a curvature-one background gives flag curvature one.

Identify the oriented round unit tangent bundle with \(SO(3)\), and represent
a frame by \(g\).  With \(R_z(\alpha)\) denoting rotation through \(\alpha\)
about \(e_3\), round geodesic motion is \(gR_z(t)\), while the wind acts by
\(R_z(\epsilon t)g\).  Thus return at \(T>0\) is exactly

\[
R_z(\epsilon T)gR_z(T)=g,
\qquad
R_z(\epsilon T)=gR_z(-T)g^{-1}.                         \tag{3}
\]

The left rotation has axis \(e_3\), whereas the conjugated right rotation
has axis \(ge_3\).  For a non-equatorial frame these unoriented axes are
distinct.  Equality of rotations about distinct axes forces both rotations
to be the identity; this remains true at angle \(\pi\), where a nontrivial
rotation still determines its unoriented axis.  Hence

\[
T=2\pi m,\qquad \epsilon T=2\pi n
\]

for integers (m>0,n).  An irrational (\epsilon) makes this impossible.
The exceptional frames are precisely the two oriented equators, where
\(ge_3=\pm e_3\) and the two circle actions share an unoriented axis.  Their
angular speeds are (1+\epsilon)
and (-1+\epsilon), giving the positive periods in part 2.  If
(\epsilon=p/q) is reduced, the two closure equations force (q\mid m);
therefore the non-equatorial least return is (2\pi q).  This also proves
the rational face without confusing its generic period with the shorter
equatorial periods.

For a Finsler surface of flag curvature one, a transverse Jacobi coefficient
(j) along a unit geodesic satisfies (j''+j=0).  The fundamental matrix in
the returning canonical normal frame is the displayed rotation.  Its
characteristic polynomial and determinant follow by direct calculation.  If
(\epsilon) is irrational, neither (1/(1+\epsilon)) nor
(1/(1-\epsilon)) is an integer, so neither (T_+\) nor (T_-\) is a
multiple of (2\pi); the determinant is nonzero.

## Boundary atlas

- At (\epsilon=0), the metric is the reversible round metric: every great
  circle is closed with least period (2\pi).  This is a clean Zoll family,
  not a continuation of the two-isolated-orbit statement.
- For rational (p/q), the non-equatorial period is (2\pi q), while the
  two equators retain their separately reduced periods.  Degeneracy of an
  equator is decided by whether (q/(q\pm p)) is an integer.
- Longitude reflection conjugates (\epsilon) to (-\epsilon) and swaps the
  two oriented equators.
- At (|\epsilon|=1), the navigation indicatrix loses global strong
  convexity.  Values (|\epsilon|\ge1) are outside this global Randers
  theorem.
- Orientation cannot be quotiented away: (F_\epsilon) is nonreversible for
  nonzero wind.
