\section{Introduction}

The coefficient-field obstruction for the full $\mu_3$-graded H\'enon kernel
is severe: at every split prime $p$, the conjugate-paired first moment
generates $\Q(\zeta_p)^+$, of degree $(p-1)/2$.  A conventional compatible
system over one fixed number field is therefore impossible.  Rather than
choosing a different field independently at every prime, we test Galois trace
and norm, the two minimal invariant descents.

The additive trace collapses the first moment to $-6$.  The multiplicative
norm preserves the complete local chronological determinant and restores
rational coefficients, but its virtual degree grows linearly with $p$.
The central observation of this paper is that dividing the canonical
logarithm by the trace-field degree preserves the ordered moments while
adding exactly enough first-order decay to move the Euler convergence boundary.

\begin{theorem}[Critical-boundary germ]\label{thm:main}
Let $G_p(z)$ be the origin-normalized field-degree root of the Galois norm of
the conjugate-paired H\'enon augmentation factor, as defined in
\cref{sec:normalized}.  Then
\[
 \mathcal G(s)=\prod_{p\equiv1\ (3)}G_p(p^{-s})
\]
converges locally uniformly and is holomorphic and nonzero on
$\RePart s>1/2$.
\end{theorem}

The statement is both stronger and more limited than an ordinary Euler
product theorem.  It reaches the critical boundary from the right, but the
local factors are defined by a normalized logarithm on the unit disk.  Their
divisor orders need not be integral after division by $(p-1)/2$.  Thus this
paper proves an analytic determinant germ, not a finite-dimensional rational
determinant or a Fredholm determinant.

Normalized determinants are natural in finite and von Neumann algebras
\cite{FugledeKadison1952,DeLaHarpe2000}; our construction does not yet claim
such an operator realization.  That distinction becomes the next theorem
gate rather than a semantic choice.
