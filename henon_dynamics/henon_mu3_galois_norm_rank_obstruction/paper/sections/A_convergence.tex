\section{Normal convergence}\label{sec:convergence}

Let $K$ be compact in $\RePart s>1/2$, and choose
$\sigma_0>1/2$ with $\RePart s\ge\sigma_0$ on $K$.  Every fixed local factor
is analytic because $|p^{-s}|<1$.  We may remove finitely many primes so that
$4p^{-\sigma_0}\le1/2$ in the remaining tail.

The $n=1$ contribution to the logarithm is bounded uniformly on $K$ by
\[
 \sum_p\frac{12p^{-\sigma_0}}{p-1},
\]
which converges by comparison with $\sum_pp^{-1-\sigma_0}$.  Using
\eqref{eq:bound}, the higher repetitions satisfy
\begin{align*}
 \sum_p\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{|c_{p,n}|}{n}p^{-n\sigma_0}
 &\le4\sum_p\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{(4p^{-\sigma_0})^n}{n}\\
 &\ll\sum_pp^{-2\sigma_0}<\infty.
\end{align*}
Thus the logarithmic series converges normally.  Its exponential is
holomorphic and nonzero, proving \cref{thm:main}.
