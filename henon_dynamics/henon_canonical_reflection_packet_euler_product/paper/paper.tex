\begin{abstract}
Odd reflection packets in the full H\'enon horseshoe admit a unique normalized
linear sampler invariant under symbolic coboundaries.  We promote that sampler
to a canonical packet-mean Euler product.  Its logarithmic derivative has an
exact primitive/repetition divisor law, and the product converges in an
explicit disk.  In the unweighted case its radius is $2^{-1/2}$ and
\[
 \log\Zpack_0(z)
 =\frac{1}{\sqrt2(1-\sqrt2z)}+G(z),
\]
where $G$ is analytic near the positive boundary point.  Consequently the
Euler product has an exponential essential singularity there, rather than a
meromorphic pole.  We distinguish this restricted packet object from the
standard Lind zeta of the full infinite-dihedral action and from an
orbit-resolved weighted determinant.  The result is an exact Route-A analytic
germ and an exact obstruction to one naive Fredholm promotion; it supplies no
rational-prime trace or operator.
\end{abstract}

\section{Canonical data and source boundary}

Consider the area-preserving H\'enon map
$H(x,y)=(6-x^2-y,x)$.  The inherited hyperbolic coding identifies its real
chain-recurrent set with the full two-shift and intertwines a reversor with
sequence reversal \cite{DevaneyNitecki1979,Arai2007}.  For odd $n$, let $A_n$
denote the primitive marked reflection packet.  Its exact cardinality is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dn}
 D_n=|A_n|=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)2^{(d+1)/2}.
\end{equation}
The preceding canonical-sampler theorem proves that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bn}
 b_n(f)=\frac{1}{nD_n}\sum_{\omega\in A_n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
 f(\sigma^j\omega)
\end{equation}
is the unique normalized real linear sampler on these cycles that annihilates
every coboundary.  In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gauge}
 b_n(f+u-u\circ\sigma)=b_n(f)
\end{equation}
exactly at each finite period.

There is already a standard zeta theory for flip systems.  A flip and the
shift generate an action of the infinite dihedral group, and the Lind zeta is
defined by fixed-point counts for all finite-index subgroups.  The exact
decomposition in \citet{KimLeePark2003} combines a square root of the ordinary
shift zeta with odd and even flip fixed-point series; matrix extensions appear
in \citet{Ryu2019}.  Our object below is deliberately narrower: it uses only
the odd primitive packet in \eqref{eq:Dn} and the aggregate mean
\eqref{eq:bn}.  We do not identify it with the Lind zeta.

\section{The packet-mean Euler product}

For bounded continuous $f$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product}
 \Zpack_f(z,s)=\prod_{\substack{n\ge1\\n\ \mathrm{odd}}}
 \left(1-z^n e^{-sn b_n(f)}\right)^{-D_n}.
\end{equation}
One sufficient absolute-convergence condition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:safedisk}
 \sqrt2\,|z|e^{|s|\lVert f\rVert_\infty}<1,
\end{equation}
because $D_n\le2^{(n+1)/2}$.  Thus \eqref{eq:product} defines a nonzero
analytic germ.  Equation \eqref{eq:gauge} makes it exactly cohomology
invariant in this disk.

\begin{theorem}[Primitive and repeated packets]\label{thm:ledger}
In the disk of absolute convergence,
\begin{align}
 \log\Zpack_f(z,s)
 &=\sum_{\substack{n\ge1\\n\ \mathrm{odd}}}D_n
   \sum_{r\ge1}\frac{z^{nr}e^{-srn b_n(f)}}{r},\label{eq:log}\\
 [z^m]\,z\partial_z\log\Zpack_f(z,s)
 &=\sum_{\substack{n\mid m\\n\ \mathrm{odd}}}
 nD_ne^{-sm b_n(f)}.\label{eq:logder}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expand $-\log(1-w)=\sum_{r\ge1}w^r/r$ in \eqref{eq:product}; absolute
convergence justifies rearrangement.  Differentiation multiplies the
$(n,r)$ term by $nr$, and collecting $m=nr$ gives \eqref{eq:logder}.
\end{proof}

The divisor condition in \eqref{eq:logder} distinguishes a primitive packet
of length $n$ from its $r$-fold repetition.  It does not retain the
distribution of individual orbit weights within $A_n$: all factors at one
period receive the common mean $b_n(f)$.  This mean-field firewall becomes
the next research gate.

\section{The unweighted entropy boundary}

Set $s=0$ and write $\Zpack_0(z)=\Zpack_f(z,0)$.  The primitive generating
series can be summed exactly.

\begin{proposition}[M\"obius generating function]\label{prop:Dseries}
For $|z|<2^{-1/2}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dseries}
 D(z):=\sum_{\substack{n\ge1\\n\ \mathrm{odd}}}D_nz^n
 =\sum_{\substack{k\ge1\\k\ \mathrm{odd}}}
 \mu(k)\frac{2z^k}{1-2z^{2k}}.
\end{equation}
Its radius is $R=2^{-1/2}$, and near the positive point $R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dprincipal}
 D(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt2(1-\sqrt2z)}+G_1(z)
\end{equation}
with $G_1$ analytic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitute \eqref{eq:Dn}, put $n=km$, and interchange the absolutely
convergent sums.  Since $m$ is odd,
\[
 \sum_{m\ \mathrm{odd}}2^{(m+1)/2}z^{km}
 =\frac{2z^k}{1-2z^{2k}},
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:Dseries}.  The $k=1$ term satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partialfraction}
 \frac{2z}{1-2z^2}
 =\frac{1}{\sqrt2(1-\sqrt2z)}
  -\frac{1}{\sqrt2(1+\sqrt2z)}.
\end{equation}
Every $k\ge3$ term is analytic in a neighborhood of $R$.  Positivity of
$D_n$ and the pole in \eqref{eq:partialfraction} give the radius.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Essential boundary singularity]\label{thm:essential}
There is a function $G$, analytic near $R=2^{-1/2}$, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:logprincipal}
 \log\Zpack_0(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt2(1-\sqrt2z)}+G(z).
\end{equation}
Consequently $\Zpack_0$ has an essential singularity at $R$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The $r=1$ part of \eqref{eq:log} is $D(z)$.  For $r\ge2$, the series
\[
 \sum_{n\ \mathrm{odd}}D_n\sum_{r\ge2}\frac{z^{nr}}r
\]
is analytic for $|z|<2^{-1/4}$, which strictly contains a neighborhood of
the positive point $R$.  Combine this with \cref{prop:Dseries}.  Exponentiating
\eqref{eq:logprincipal} gives a nonvanishing analytic factor times the
exponential of a simple pole, and hence an essential singularity.
\end{proof}

This theorem is an analytic obstruction to calling \eqref{eq:product} a
meromorphic Fredholm determinant at its entropy boundary.  It is not a no-go
theorem for a separately renormalized relative determinant.

\section{Exact audit and route status}

The accompanying implementation computes $D_n$ through period $41$ by
M\"obius inversion and independently by divisor subtraction.  It expands the
Euler product through order $41$ and recovers \eqref{eq:logder} from the
coefficient identity $zZ'= (zZ'/Z)Z$.  Eight tests pass in normal and
optimized modes; five upstream artifacts are hash locked and 25 hostile
mutations are rejected.

The strongest positive result is the exact canonical Euler germ and its
repetition law.  The strongest obstruction is the essential boundary
singularity.  Route A records an A2 certified prefix and partial A3 analytic
structure, but no prime semantics.  No rational-prime labels, von Mangoldt
amplitudes, transfer operator, self-adjoint generator, or completed Riemann
determinant has been constructed; Route B is not authorized.  The next
minimal theorem is to compare \eqref{eq:product} with the orbit-resolved
exponential moment.  Its first missing invariant is within-period variance.
