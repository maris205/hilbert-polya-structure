# Research question

For the continuous-time branching chain on the nonnegative integers,

\[
(Gf)(n)=\lambda n[f(n+1)-f(n)]+\mu n[f(n-1)-f(n)],
\qquad \lambda,\mu\ge 0,
\]

can one close, in one theorem package and for every initial population
`z>=0`, all of the following without hiding singular or degenerate cases?

1. the exact one-ancestor M\"obius probability-generating semigroup;
2. the arbitrary-`z` transition law with its survivor-binomial layer;
3. exact first two moments;
4. subcritical quasi-stationarity, critical Yaglom scaling, and the
   supercritical martingale atom/gamma mixture; and
5. critical, pure-birth, pure-death, zero-rate, zero-population and zero-time
   boundaries.

The finite exact ledger uses `delta=exp(-(lambda-mu)t)` off criticality and
`tau=lambda*t` at criticality. Long-time claims are proved symbolically and
analytically rather than extrapolated from those rational sentinels. The
arithmetic evaluation occurs only after the source theorem is closed.
