# Narrative report

HCS-C288 takes a singular operator that is often treated by formal notation
and closes every convention in one place.  The jump sign fixes the attractive
chamber, the resolvent denominator fixes the physical pole, scattering fixes
the factor two, and the heat trace checks the same normalization in physical
time.  The result is a substantial standalone spectral-dynamics theorem even
though the model is classical and explicitly owned in the literature.

The attractive/repulsive transition changes both the discrete spectrum and
large-time heat behavior: a negative coupling produces one exponentially
growing heat-semigroup component because its energy is negative, while a
nonnegative coupling has no bound state.  This is not an instability claim
for the unitary Schrödinger flow.

The odd sine and even Robin transforms close the spectral type: the
continuum is purely absolutely continuous, the singular-continuous sector is
empty, and the attractive pole is the only discrete atom.  The heat receipt
is independently reconstructed from the resolvent transform rather than
checked by a second copy of the producer's closed formula.

The package earns only a candidate-local natural quantization coordinate.
It does not obtain arithmetic primitive orbits or a target spectral bridge.
