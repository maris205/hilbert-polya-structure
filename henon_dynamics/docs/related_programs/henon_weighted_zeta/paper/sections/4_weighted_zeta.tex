\section{Weighted cycle expansions and error language}
\label{sec:zeta}

\subsection{Definitions}

Let $\cP_*$ denote the primitive periodic orbits in the certified survivor
$\Lam_*$.  For $p\in\cP_*$, write $n_p$ for its primitive period and
$\Lambda_{u,p}=\sigma_pL_p$ for its unstable multiplier, where
$\sigma_p\in\{-1,+1\}$ and $L_p=|\Lambda_{u,p}|>1$.  For $\beta\geq0$ define
the formal weighted dynamical zeta function and its Euler determinant by
\begin{equation}
 Z_\beta(z)=
 \prod_{p\in\cP_*}\left(1-z^{n_p}L_p^{-\beta}\right)^{-1},
 \qquad
 D_{E,\beta}(z)=Z_\beta(z)^{-1}.
 \label{eq:zeta}
\end{equation}
Two different truncations occur and must be distinguished.  The
primitive-period cutoff retains all repetitions of orbits with $n_p\leq N$:
\begin{equation}
 \log Z_\beta^{(\mathrm{prim}\leq N)}(z)=
 \sum_{\substack{p\in\cP_*\\n_p\leq N}}
 \sum_{r\geq1}
 \frac{z^{r n_p}}{rL_p^{\beta r}}.
 \label{eq:log-zeta}
\end{equation}
The root calculations instead use the degree-$N$ coefficient section
\begin{equation}
 D_{E,\beta}^{[N]}(z)=
 \left[\prod_{\substack{p\in\cP_*\\n_p\leq N}}
 \left(1-z^{n_p}L_p^{-\beta}\right)\right]_{\leq N},
 \label{eq:euler-coefficient-section}
\end{equation}
where $[\cdot]_{\leq N}$ discards powers above $z^N$.  Orbits of primitive
period greater than $N$ cannot affect these first $N$ coefficients, but the
polynomial in \eqref{eq:euler-coefficient-section} is not the same object as
the all-repetition expression in \eqref{eq:log-zeta}.
At $\beta=0$ this is the topological periodic-point zeta on the selected
symbolic system; positive $\beta$ weights unstable expansion.  The notation
does not imply that the finite catalogue is globally complete.

\subsection{Periodic-point flat determinant and algebraic dependence}

The second cycle polynomial is built from smooth fixed-point traces.  If
$M_p=DH_6^{n_p}(z_p)$, define
\begin{equation}
 T_n=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Fix}(H_6^n|_{\Lam_*})}
 \frac{1}{|\det(I-DH_6^n(x))|}
 =\sum_{\substack{p\in\cP_*\\n_p\mid n}}
 \frac{n_p}{|\det(I-M_p^{n/n_p})|}.
 \label{eq:flat-traces}
\end{equation}
The associated periodic-point flat determinant is the formal power series
\begin{equation}
 D_F(z)=\exp\left(-\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{T_nz^n}{n}\right).
 \label{eq:flat-determinant}
\end{equation}
Its degree-$N$ coefficient section is computed from $f_0=1$ and
\begin{equation}
 f_n=-\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^nT_jf_{n-j},
 \qquad D_F^{[N]}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^Nf_nz^n.
 \label{eq:flat-recurrence}
\end{equation}
This definition is called ``Fredholm'' in the historical result fields, but no
specific continuous operator or Banach space is inferred from that name here.

The Euler and flat determinants are not algebraically independent.  Since the
eigenvalues of $M_p$ are $\sigma_pL_p$ and $\sigma_pL_p^{-1}$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac1{|\det(I-M_p^r)|}
 =\sum_{k\geq0}(k+1)
 \left(\sigma_p^kL_p^{-(k+1)}\right)^r.
 \label{eq:flat-factor-expansion}
\end{equation}
Substitution into \eqref{eq:flat-determinant} gives the formal factorization
\begin{equation}
 D_F(z)=\prod_{p\in\cP_*}\prod_{k\geq0}
 \left(1-\sigma_p^kL_p^{-(k+1)}z^{n_p}\right)^{k+1}.
 \label{eq:flat-factorization}
\end{equation}
The $k=0$ factor is exactly $D_{E,1}$.  This formal algebraic dependence
motivates comparing the leading roots of $D_{E,1}^{[N]}$ and $D_F^{[N]}$ as an
empirical finite-section and implementation consistency check, not as
independent cycle evidence.  Degree truncation does not preserve the zero set
of the full product, however, and the factorization supplies no bound on the
finite-section root discrepancy.

\subsection{Conditional operator statement}

For a compact locally maximal uniformly hyperbolic basic set with a compatible
Markov coding, standard thermodynamic formalism associates a weighted Ruelle
operator to the potential
\begin{equation}
 \phi_\beta=-\beta\log \norm{DH_a|_{E^u}}.
 \label{eq:potential}
\end{equation}
Corollary~\ref{cor:basic-set} supplies the basic-set properties for $\Lam_*$.  Under
the regularity assumptions of thermodynamic formalism, the corresponding
dynamical determinant is related to the weighted periodic expansion
\citep{Ruelle1976Zeta,Ruelle1978Thermodynamic,Bowen1975Equilibrium,
Baladi2000,BlankKellerLiverani2002,BaladiTsujii2016}.  We use this statement
only conditionally.  The absorbing finite-volume matrix in Sections
\ref{sec:operator}--\ref{sec:r061} is not identified with that symbolic
operator by numerical proximity.

\subsection{A finite-period tail bound}

The following elementary bound records what can and cannot be inferred from a
finite orbit table.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional truncation bound]
\label{lem:tail}
Assume that the number of primitive orbits of period $n$ is at most
$C e^{hn}$ with $h\geq0$, and that $L_p\geq e^{\chi n_p}$ for all included
orbits, with $\chi>0$.  If
\[
 t=|z|e^{-\beta\chi}<1,\qquad q=e^h t<1,
\]
then
\begin{equation}
 \left|\log Z_\beta(z)-
 \log Z_\beta^{(\mathrm{prim}\leq N)}(z)\right|
 \leq
 \frac{Cq^{N+1}}{(1-q)(1-t^{N+1})}.
 \label{eq:tail-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a primitive orbit of period $n$, the geometric series in
\eqref{eq:log-zeta} is bounded by $t^n/(1-t^n)$.  Summing over the omitted
periods and using the assumed orbit count gives
\[
 \sum_{n>N} C e^{hn}\frac{t^n}{1-t^n}
 \leq \frac{C}{1-t^{N+1}}
 \sum_{n>N}q^n,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:tail-bound}.  The assumptions, rather than the finite table,
are what make the estimate valid.
\end{proof}

The lemma is useful as an accounting device for an absolutely convergent branch
of the logarithm.  It does not control a zero of a degree-$N$ determinant
section.  In fact, the explicit bounds $h=\log\varphi$ and
$\chi=\log(773/224)$ give the $\beta=1$ domain
$|z|<(773/224)/\varphi\approx2.133$, while the reported reciprocal root is
$z_*\approx3.429$.  The period-8--12 root behavior below is therefore empirical
finite-section stabilization, not a consequence of Lemma~\ref{lem:tail}.

\subsection{What the zeta language excludes}

The determinants above belong to the certified local survivor.  The finite
matrices below remain separately defined estimators; numerical proximity does
not identify them with a continuous Ruelle operator or extend the determinant
to the full H\'enon plane.
