# Exact validation plan

This is a proof package.  Code supplies deterministic regression and hostile
audits; it does not replace any unbounded theorem.

## Frozen inputs

- Coprime integers \(a>b\geq1\).
- Every \(N\geq2\) coprime to \(ab\), with
  \(R_N(x)=ab^{-1}x\) on \(U_N\), marked at \(1\).
- One multiplication is one discrete step.
- Unweighted fixed-point cardinality and ordinary finite permutation
  determinant conventions.
- The disjoint union and inverse limit remain distinct.
- Exact arithmetic only and no target data.

## Gates

1. **Primitive-return gate:** reconstruct all factorization rows and verify
   primitive divisor, order, and first-return equivalence.
2. **Attribution gate:** require the exact Zsigmondy exception set and label
   that existence theorem external and not new.
3. **Lift gate:** independently compute valuations and orders on every
   recorded \(p^k\), including minimality.
4. **Finite-fiber gate:** enumerate every unit, orbit, fixed prefix, zeta
   factor, Koopman determinant factor, and inversion identity.
5. **Globalization gate:** reconstruct the divisor-totient fixed ledger,
   Möbius primitive-cycle count, and an explicit nonfixing profinite witness.
6. **Symbolic gate:** use SymPy for homogeneous cyclotomic values, all
   recorded prime-power orders, formal zeta algebra, and representative
   permutation matrices.
7. **Adversarial gate:** reject repaired-hash semantic mutations and a
   stale-hash mutation.
8. **Release gate:** byte replay, two fresh fixed-epoch PDF builds, embedded
   fonts, visual inspection, and 27-file payload closure.

## Finite sentinels

The producer covers all 63 coprime pairs with \(2\leq a\leq14\), times
through 10, all 31 coprime pairs through \(a=10\), admissible moduli through
120, fixed prefixes through time 12, primitive primes through 257, and
powers through \(k=4\).  The 4,927 rows consist of 567 primitive-divisor,
630 globalization, 1,650 finite-fiber, and 2,080 lift rows.  These bounds
detect regression only.  Theorems 1–4 carry all unbounded quantifiers.

## Kill and pivot rules

- Any primitive-return mismatch kills the source identification.
- Any lift mismatch kills the tower theorem and forces a weaker paper.
- Any nonuniform finite-fiber cycle or failed reversor kills the complete
  fiber claim.
- Equality of the two global fixed ledgers would kill the owner
  nonselection theorem.
- Any need for target data, logarithmic prime time, or a prime-weighted
  product kills scope compliance.

All source identities survive.  The correct pivot is therefore not a model
replacement but a strict limitation: retain weak A0/A1 progress and stop at
A2/A3.
