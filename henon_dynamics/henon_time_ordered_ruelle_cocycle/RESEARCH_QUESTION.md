## Single research question

For

\[
H_a(q,p)=(1-aq^2-p,q),
\qquad
a_-=\frac{59}{10},\quad a_+=\frac{61}{10},
\]

let

\[
F(\omega,z)=(\sigma\omega,H_{a_{\omega_0}}z)
\]

on the full two-shift.  What chronological information survives after joint
parameter--state primitive orbits are passed to an intrinsic
stability-weighted dynamical determinant?

Here "chronological information" means dependence on the order of symbols
after quotienting only the symmetries forced by the dynamics: cyclic choice
of starting phase and reversal through the common reversor.  It does not mean
a preferred orientation of time.

## Frozen conventions

The state is

\[
z_i=(q_i,q_{i-1}),
\qquad
z_{i+1}=H_{a_{\omega_i}}z_i.
\]

For a word \(w=w_0\ldots w_{n-1}\), later symbols act on the left:

\[
F_w=H_{a_{w_{n-1}}}\circ\cdots\circ H_{a_{w_0}}.
\]

The periodic equations are

\[
a_{w_i}q_i^2+q_{i-1}+q_{i+1}-1=0,
\qquad i\pmod n.
\]

A primitive object is a **joint** parameter--state orbit under one
microstep of \(F\).  Parameter necklaces and state necklaces may not be
canonicalized separately.  The clock is the microstep period \(n\).

The primary potential is the intrinsic unstable length

\[
\ell(\Gamma)=\log|\Lambda_u(M_\Gamma)|,
\]

where \(M_\Gamma\) is the ordered fibre monodromy along the complete joint
orbit.  The proposed Euler factor is initially

\[
1-z^{n(\Gamma)}e^{-s\ell(\Gamma)},
\]

with repetitions generated by the same primitive monodromy.  Signed flat
trace weights and bare orbit counts are controls, not substitutes for this
potential.

## Forced equivalences

With \(R(q,p)=(p,q)\),

\[
RH_aR=H_a^{-1}.
\]

If \(w^{\mathrm{rev}}\) is the reversed protocol, then

\[
F_{w^{\mathrm{rev}}}=RF_w^{-1}R.
\]

Since every derivative monodromy has determinant one,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(M^{-1})=\operatorname{tr}(M),
\quad
\det(I-M^{-1})=\det(I-M),
\quad
|\Lambda_u(M^{-1})|=|\Lambda_u(M)|.
\]

Consequently cyclic rotations and reversals are mandatory equality controls.
A difference inside one of these classes is a code/convention error, not a
discovery.
