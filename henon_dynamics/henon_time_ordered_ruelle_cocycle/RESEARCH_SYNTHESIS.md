# Research synthesis after T4 and the orbitwise scalar-T5 gate

## Main conclusion

The two-letter Paper-5-coordinate Hénon skew product has a rigorously
certified common local survivor.  Its intrinsic instability weights
distinguish the certified period-seven and period-eight protocol pairs after
matching their complete cyclic parameter-bigram and parameter-trigram
ledgers.  The resulting pure-instability Euler determinant now has an exact
repetition law, fixed-point trace identity, and explicit nonzero convergence
domain.  However, the unit-numerator all-complex signed residue construction
collapses exactly to one, and the natural orbitwise scalar pinning
realization of the pure instability trace is impossible.

This is a meaningful split result:

\[
\boxed{
\text{local real instability remembers order;}
\quad
\text{its Euler product exists locally;}
\quad
\text{orbitwise scalar denominator cancellation cannot realize it.}
}
\]

This is a finite parameter-memory obstruction through cyclic word length

\[

## T5 orbitwise scalar obstruction

The common complex geometry does not rescue the frozen orbitwise scalar
pinning construction.  Its periodic trace contains a fixed-point
denominator.  For an area-preserving saddle with trace \(t\),

\[
\det(I-M)=2-t,
\qquad
\det(I-M^2)=4-t^2.
\]

Scalar multiplicativity would require

\[
|4-t^2|=|2-t|^2,
\]

which fails for every \(|t|>2\).  This closes orbit-by-orbit geometric scalar
denominator cancellation before any spectrum computation.  It does not
exclude compensation among distinct same-period orbits in an unmarked
aggregate scalar trace, nor a graded exterior complex whose alternating
supertrace can cancel the denominator.

## Methodological lessons

- Joint parameter--state cyclic phase is the primitive object.  Separate
  canonicalization loses relative chronology.
- Reversal is equality metadata, not an Euler quotient.  Removing reversed
  oriented sectors changes multiplicity.
- A branch-level difference is insufficient; complete marked-state
  aggregation is mandatory.
- Local real, global complex, signed, absolute, and instability-weighted sums
  are genuinely different mathematical objects.
- The correct global proof uses the cyclic orbit scheme, Hill identity, and
  Grothendieck residue.  Naive \(\mathbb P^2\) homogenization produces false
  points at infinity.
- Euler weights, flat traces, and Fredholm traces have different repetition
  laws; matching one primitive return is not enough.
- A complex instability weight requires an oriented nonvanishing factor and
  a frozen logarithm branch.  A real modulus cannot simply be inserted into
  a holomorphic kernel.
- Projectivizing the whole line bundle would count stable and unstable fixed
  lines.  A strict unstable slope domain and uniqueness theorem are
  mandatory.

## Decision

C22 is complete under its frozen orbitwise geometric scalar trace
convention.  T4 passes; the common base and projective/logarithm domains pass;
the termwise scalar denominator-cancellation gate fails by a theorem.  This
is not a global nonexistence theorem for every aggregate scalar
representation.  No additional Ulam resolution, finite-memory matrix, or
isolated short-orbit search is warranted.

The only authorized continuation changes dynamical form to the lifted
projective exterior-degree Ruelle--Lefschetz complex.  The next round must
either prove common nuclear branch operators and the exact supertrace, or
close the entire C22 operator lineage.  Since projective lifting and exterior
cancellation are standard prior art, a routine noneffective specialization
does not meet the paper-promotion threshold.
