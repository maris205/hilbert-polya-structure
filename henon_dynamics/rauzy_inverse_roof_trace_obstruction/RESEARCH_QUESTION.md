## Subquestions

1. Do any cyclic phases of the C29 C25 and C26 identity-holonomy words possess
   an orbit whose every genuine \(B^{-\mathsf T}\) length prefix stays in the
   positive Rauzy cone, and does the independent contravariant transfer action
   pass the same domain gate?
2. Can the AGY logarithmic normalizer be strictly positive on both an arrow
   and its inverse while retaining the additive time-cocycle law?
3. Can faithful forward and inverse edge operators act on one
   infinite-dimensional space and produce an ordinary nuclear determinant or
   the standard isolated-fixed-point flat trace?

All three inherit the same scope: the literal source-locked C25/C26 matrices,
the C29 words, later-on-the-left chronology, and no fitted clock or averaged
transition matrix.

## Scope

In scope:

- exact raw Rauzy matrices rather than only fixed-frame symplectic images;
- the two C25 length-six words and the derived C26 length-twenty-four word;
- all cyclic phases in the positive real length cone and in the separate
  transfer/projective action;
- the raw covariant homology action as a negative convention control, never as
  a substitute for the length action;
- ordinary compact/nuclear ideals and the standard isolated-fixed-point
  holomorphic/flat-trace formula;
- the distinction between a signed groupoid time cocycle and a positive
  symmetric graph length.

Out of scope:

- longer word searches, additional primes, Riemann-zero data, or fitted
  normalizations;
- a universal impossibility theorem for every distributional
  clean-fixed-set regularization;
- a claim that the finite C29 Hashimoto determinant is algebraically wrong;
- Route B or any Hilbert--Pólya/RH conclusion.

## Stop/go rule

- Continue the C29-to-AGY promotion only if at least one cyclic phase is a
  genuine positive-cone orbit and both the positive roof and trace theorem
  survive without changing the dynamics.
- Otherwise close the lane, retain its finite combinatorial determinant, and
  pivot to a genuinely hyperbolic positive-time system.
