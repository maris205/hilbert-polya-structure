\section{The finite group-trace determinant that survives}
\label{sec:grouptrace}

The negative geometric conclusion does not erase the preceding finite
calculation.  This section states exactly what remains and why it is not in
conflict with \Cref{thm:same-space}.

\subsection{A finite edge matrix over a group algebra}

Let $G$ be the discrete group generated by the frozen matrix holonomies and
let $\lambda:G\to\mathcal B(\ell^2(G))$ be the left regular representation.
The canonical group trace is
\begin{equation}
 \tau_G\!\left(\sum_{g\in G}a_g\lambda(g)\right)=a_e
 \label{eq:group-trace}
\end{equation}
on the group algebra and extends to its group von Neumann algebra.  Let
$\mathcal H$ be the finite non-backtracking matrix whose $(f,e)$ block is
$x_f\lambda(g_f)$ when $e\to f$ is legal and zero otherwise.  Chronological
composition gives
\begin{equation}
 (\operatorname{tr}_{E^\pm}\otimes\tau_G)(\mathcal H^n)
 =\sum_{\substack{e_1\cdots e_n\ {\rm closed}\\
                   {\rm nonbacktracking}}}
 x_{e_1}\cdots x_{e_n}\,
 \mathbf 1_{\{g_{e_n}\cdots g_{e_1}=e\}}.
 \label{eq:group-moment}
\end{equation}
Thus the trace selects based closed non-backtracking words with identity
holonomy.  With unit marking $x_e=u$, write the number of such based words as
$N_n$.  On a sufficiently small disk the trace-log germ is
\begin{equation}
 D_\infty(u)
 =\exp\!\left[-\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{N_nu^n}{n}\right].
 \label{eq:group-determinant}
\end{equation}
This is the group-trace analogue of a finite Hashimoto determinant; compare
the original edge-zeta framework of
\citet{Hashimoto1989,StarkTerras1996} and the von Neumann algebra setting in
\citet{LenzPogorzelskiSchmidt2019}.

\subsection{The frozen normalized finite-Weil limit}

The preceding phase of the experiment supplied finite Weil representations
$\rho_p$ over odd primes and finite twisted Hashimoto matrices $\mathcal H_p$.
Its normalized trace input is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1}{p^2}\operatorname{Tr}\rho_p(g)
 \longrightarrow \mathbf 1_{\{g=e\}}
 \label{eq:weil-delta}
\end{equation}
for every holonomy element occurring in a fixed finite moment.  Together with
the uniform degree bound $\|\mathcal H_p\|\leq M$, this defines, on the common
zero-free disk $|u|<1/M$, the normalized determinant root
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_p(u):=
 \exp\!\left[p^{-2}\operatorname{Log}_0
       \det(I-u\mathcal H_p)\right]
 =
 \exp\!\left[-\sum_{n\geq1}
   \frac{u^n}{np^2}\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal H_p^n)\right]
 \longrightarrow D_\infty(u).
 \label{eq:finite-weil-limit}
\end{equation}
Here $\operatorname{Log}_0$ is the holomorphic logarithm fixed by value zero
at $u=0$.  The convergence in \eqref{eq:finite-weil-limit} is coefficientwise
and locally uniform.
The finite-head limit follows from \eqref{eq:weil-delta}; the geometric bound
\[
 \left|p^{-2}\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal H_p^n)\right|\leq CM^n
\]
gives a prime-independent summable tail on every closed disk
$|u|\leq r<1/M$.  It also supplies a common zero-free disk and hence a common
logarithm branch through $u=0$.  The frozen degree bounds are $M=3$ for the
C25 oriented graph and $M=5$ for the C26 rose.

For the C25 graph, exact enumeration gives
\begin{equation}
 N_6=24,\qquad
 [u^6]\log D_\infty(u)=-\frac{24}{6}=-4.
 \label{eq:C25-coefficient}
\end{equation}
For the C26 graph, the relation and its certified inverse class give
\begin{equation}
 N_{24}\geq48,\qquad
 [u^{24}]\log D_\infty(u)=-\frac{N_{24}}{24}\leq-2.
 \label{eq:C26-coefficient}
\end{equation}
Here $N_n$ counts based closed identity-holonomy words, exactly as in
\eqref{eq:group-moment}; it is not a count of positive AGY periodic orbits.
The inequality in \eqref{eq:C26-coefficient} is a lower-bound statement for
the frozen combinatorial census, not an exact primitive-orbit
classification.

\subsection{Why this does not contradict the operator obstruction}

The trace in \eqref{eq:group-moment} is the finite edge-matrix trace tensored
with $\tau_G$.  It is not the ordinary Hilbert-space trace of $\mathcal H$
acting on $\ell^2(G)^{E^\pm}$.  In particular, $\lambda(g)$ is unitary and,
for infinite $G$, is not compact; nevertheless its group trace is perfectly
defined.  Theorem~\ref{thm:same-space} rules out an ordinary compact or
nuclear same-space realization with faithful inverses.  It does not rule out
a finite matrix over a finite von Neumann algebra equipped with a different
trace.

Likewise, \eqref{eq:group-determinant} should not be renamed an ordinary
Fredholm determinant.  No positivity statement identifying it with a
Fuglede--Kadison determinant is needed here.  Its exact and sufficient status
is a small-$u$ group-trace exponential that records identity-holonomy words
of the symmetric graph.

\begin{proposition}[Survival with a strict semantic boundary]
\label{prop:finite-survives}
Equations \eqref{eq:group-determinant}--\eqref{eq:C26-coefficient} remain
valid after the cone and roof obstructions.  What fails is only the
identification of their counted words with positive-time periodic orbits of
the original AGY natural extension.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The finite moments depend on legal graph adjacency, chronological group
products, and the group trace.  None of those algebraic statements requires a
positive Rauzy length vector or a groupoid-positive roof.  Conversely, the
geometric identification would require the cone and roof gates already shown
to fail.
\end{proof}
