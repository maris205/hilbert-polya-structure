\section{Operator ideals and the flat-trace dichotomy}
\label{sec:operator}

The failure of a common positive cone does not by itself forbid every transfer
operator.  Hyperbolic dynamics is routinely represented on anisotropic
spaces, and stable and unstable branches can be treated by different
polarizations.  The correct obstruction is narrower: faithful forward and
inverse actions cannot both appear as compact blocks on one
infinite-dimensional space.

\subsection{A same-space nuclearity obstruction}

Let $X$ be an infinite-dimensional Banach space and let $E^\pm$ be a finite
oriented edge set with a fixed-point-free involution $e\mapsto e^{-1}$.  A
transition $e\to f$ is legal when endpoints match and $f\neq e^{-1}$.  Assume
each oriented edge has at least one legal predecessor.  This holds for the
two frozen symmetric graphs.

Choose bounded operators $U_e\in\mathcal B(X)$.  For nonzero transition
weights $c_{f,e}$, define the non-backtracking block operator on
\[
 X^{E^\pm}=\bigoplus_{e\in E^\pm}X
\]
by the blocks
\begin{equation}
 (\mathbb B)_{f,e}=
 \begin{cases}
 c_{f,e}U_f,& e\to f\text{ legal},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:block-hashimoto}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Faithful same-space inverses obstruct compactness]
\label{thm:same-space}
Suppose
\begin{equation}
 U_{e^{-1}}U_e=I_X\qquad(e\in E^\pm).
 \label{eq:faithful-inverses}
\end{equation}
Then the block operator $\mathbb B$ in \eqref{eq:block-hashimoto} is neither
compact nor nuclear.  In a Hilbert realization it is, in particular, not
trace class.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $f\in E^\pm$ and choose a legal predecessor $e$.  Let
$J_e:X\to X^{E^\pm}$ be coordinate injection and
$P_f:X^{E^\pm}\to X$ coordinate projection.  Then
\begin{equation}
 P_f\mathbb BJ_e=c_{f,e}U_f.
 \label{eq:block-compression}
\end{equation}
Compact and nuclear operators are two-sided operator ideals.  If $\mathbb B$
belonged to either ideal, \eqref{eq:block-compression} would put every $U_f$
in that ideal.  Multiplying by the bounded $U_{f^{-1}}$ and using
\eqref{eq:faithful-inverses} would put $I_X$ in the same ideal.  The identity
on an infinite-dimensional Banach space is not compact, while every nuclear
operator is compact.  This is a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The proof also covers two spaces $X_+,X_-$ if compact arrows in both
directions compose to the identity on either infinite-dimensional space.  Its
hypothesis is not satisfied by a construction in which forward and backward
branches act between genuinely different anisotropic completions and are not
bounded inverse operators on one common realization.  This is how the
hyperbolic methods of \citet{GouezelLiverani2006}, the microlocal approach of
\citet{DyatlovZworski2016}, and stable--unstable pinning constructions such as
\citet{Fried1995,BaladiPujalsSambarino2005} escape the elementary ideal
argument.  Their escape requires hyperbolicity or a cross-map geometry; it is
not supplied by matrix identity alone.

\subsection{Why cancellation in a sum is not a word theorem}

It may be tempting to hope that noncompact blocks cancel in the unmarked sum
$\mathbb B$.  A word-resolved determinant claim needs more.

\begin{corollary}[Marked coefficient isolation]
\label{cor:marked-isolation}
Let $\mathbb B(\mathbf z)$ be an edge- or occurrence-marked family that is
holomorphic in nuclear norm near $\mathbf z=0$.  Every multivariate Cauchy
coefficient is nuclear.  If a layer marking isolates a relation-word
coefficient equal to $I_X$, or to a boundedly invertible multiplication
operator on an infinite-dimensional function space, then such a nuclear-norm
holomorphic family cannot exist.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The Cauchy coefficient is a Bochner integral of the nuclear-valued map around
a small polytorus, hence nuclear.  Neither $I_X$ nor a boundedly invertible
operator on an infinite-dimensional Banach space is compact.  Therefore the
isolated coefficient cannot be nuclear.
\end{proof}

The corollary is conditional on a marking that genuinely isolates the ordered
word.  It does not assert that every unmarked formal sum has such an
isolation.  Its purpose is to close a specific loophole in claims that already
require coefficientwise periodic-word semantics.

\subsection{Empty source domain or neutral fixed family}

Standard flat traces for hyperbolic maps and flows are built from isolated or
cleanly controlled fixed data; the familiar local denominator contains the
transverse factor $\det(I-Df)$
\citep{Fried1995,GiuliettiLiveraniPollicott2013,DyatlovZworski2016}.  For the
present identity words there is a sharp alternative.

\begin{theorem}[Positive-domain/flat-trace dichotomy]
\label{thm:flat-dichotomy}
Let $W\in\{C_1,C_2,W_{24}\}$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item On the frozen source positive domain, no cyclic phase of $W$ defines
  the tested complete length or transfer orbit.
\item If the projective domain is enlarged so that every prefix is defined,
  then $B_W=I$ implies
  \begin{equation}
   h_W=\operatorname{Id},\qquad
   Dh_W=I,\qquad
   \det(I-Dh_W)=0.
   \label{eq:neutral-family}
  \end{equation}
  The fixed set is the entire enlarged domain, not an isolated hyperbolic
  periodic point.
\end{enumerate}
The same conclusion holds for every power $W^m$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Part (i) is
\Cref{thm:C1-all-phases,thm:C2-all-phases,thm:W24-all-phases}.  For (ii), the
projectivization of $I$ is the identity map.  Its derivative is the identity
on every tangent space, so the transverse determinant vanishes.  Powers
retain both the matrix identity and the identity derivative.
\end{proof}

Thus the standard isolated fixed-point atom
\begin{equation}
 \frac{w_W(x_W)}{\left|\det(I-Dh_W(x_W))\right|}
 \label{eq:flat-atom}
\end{equation}
is unavailable for the very words responsible for the finite group-trace
signal.  This statement is deliberately not a universal no-go theorem for
all distributional traces.  A clean-fixed-manifold regularization, a quotient
that removes neutral directions, or another renormalized trace may be
possible under additional hypotheses.  Any such construction would define a
new zeta prescription and would require its own invariance, repetition, and
analytic-continuation theorem; it cannot be inferred from
\eqref{eq:flat-atom}.
