\section{Groupoid roofs, projective normalization, and repetitions}
\label{sec:roof}

The cone obstruction already rules out the proposed words on the frozen
positive domain.  There is a second, more structural obstruction to declaring
both orientations to have positive intrinsic time.  It follows directly from
the cocycle axioms and is independent of the particular matrices.

\subsection{Inverse arrows have opposite additive time}

Let $\mathcal G\rightrightarrows X$ be a groupoid.  We write composable arrows
as $gh$, with $h$ applied first.  A real one-cocycle is a function
$\tau:\mathcal G\to\mathbb R$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \tau(gh)=\tau(g)+\tau(h)
  \label{eq:additive-cocycle}
\end{equation}
whenever $gh$ is defined.

\begin{theorem}[Sign obstruction for a two-sided groupoid roof]
\label{thm:roof-sign}
Every real additive groupoid one-cocycle satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \tau(1_x)=0,\qquad \tau(g^{-1})=-\tau(g).
  \label{eq:inverse-sign}
\end{equation}
Consequently, if $e$ and $e^{-1}$ are categorical inverses, no such cocycle
can obey $\tau(e)>0$ and $\tau(e^{-1})>0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
From $1_x1_x=1_x$ and additivity,
$\tau(1_x)=2\tau(1_x)$, so $\tau(1_x)=0$.  Since
$g^{-1}g=1_{s(g)}$, additivity gives
$\tau(g^{-1})+\tau(g)=0$.
\end{proof}

Classical suspension-flow zeta functions instead begin with a positive roof
over a chosen base transformation or subshift
\citep[Chapter~6]{ParryPollicott1990}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:roof-sign} does not
contradict that construction.  It says that if the base alphabet is
simultaneously required to be a groupoid with literal cancellation, its
additive arrow cocycle cannot be positive on both members of an inverse pair.

\subsection{Three meanings of an identity word}

An identity matrix product is often enough for a finite group trace, but it is
not enough to conclude either path cancellation or vanishing of every time
cocycle.

\begin{proposition}[Identity trichotomy]
\label{prop:identity-trichotomy}
Let $w$ be a closed formal word.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item If $w$ is the unit arrow of $\mathcal G$, then every additive cocycle on
  $\mathcal G$ vanishes on $w$.
\item If only the represented matrix product $B_w$ equals $I$, an arbitrary
  antisymmetric edge-constant cocycle need not vanish on $w$.
\item If the cocycle is the logarithmic projective normalizer of the matrix
  action and all prefix maps are defined, then $B_w=I$ does force the signed
  sum to telescope to zero.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Part (i) is \eqref{eq:inverse-sign}.  For (ii), a matrix representation may
have a nontrivial kernel.  An edge-constant antisymmetric cocycle factors
through the signed abelianization of the free path groupoid, not necessarily
through the matrix quotient.  For the present words those abelianizations are
\begin{equation}
 [C_1]_{\mathrm{ab}}=1b+3t,\qquad
 [C_2]_{\mathrm{ab}}=4t+5b.
 \label{eq:signed-abelianizations}
\end{equation}
Choosing a nonzero value on one surviving generator proves the assertion.
Part (iii) is established explicitly in
\Cref{prop:normalizer-telescoping}.
\end{proof}

The length-twenty-four word has zero signed count in each of the frozen
$A,B,C$ generators.  Therefore every antisymmetric edge-constant cocycle in
that three-generator alphabet vanishes on $W_{24}$.  This is a property of
that particular signed word; it does not identify the path with the groupoid
unit.

\subsection{Conditional telescoping of the projective normalizer}

Let
\[
 \ell(x)=x_1+x_2+x_3+x_4.
\]
On any cone domain where $Mx$ has positive $\ell$-mass, set
\begin{equation}
 h_M(x)=\frac{Mx}{\ell(Mx)},\qquad
 r_M(x)=\log\frac{\ell(Mx)}{\ell(x)}.
 \label{eq:projective-normalizer}
\end{equation}
On the normalized simplex $\ell(x)=1$, this reduces to
$r_M(x)=\log\ell(Mx)$.  The domain qualification in
\eqref{eq:projective-normalizer} is essential: it is precisely what fails for
the frozen words on the positive cone.

\begin{proposition}[Projective normalizer telescoping]
\label{prop:normalizer-telescoping}
Where the indicated maps are defined,
\begin{align}
 r_{MN}(x)&=r_N(x)+r_M(h_Nx),
 \label{eq:normalizer-cocycle}\\
 r_{M^{-1}}(h_Mx)&=-r_M(x).
 \label{eq:normalizer-inverse}
\end{align}
If $w=e_1\cdots e_n$, $x_k=h_{B(e_k)}x_{k-1}$, every prefix is defined, and
$B_w=I$, then
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{k=1}^{n}r_{B(e_k)}(x_{k-1})
 =\log\frac{\ell(B_wx_0)}{\ell(x_0)}=0.
 \label{eq:normalizer-zero}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Homogeneity of $\ell$ gives
\[
 \ell\bigl(Mh_N(x)\bigr)=\frac{\ell(MNx)}{\ell(Nx)}.
\]
Taking logarithms and accounting for the initial normalization yields
\eqref{eq:normalizer-cocycle}.  Set $N=M^{-1}$, or evaluate at $h_Mx$, to
obtain \eqref{eq:normalizer-inverse}.  Iterating
\eqref{eq:normalizer-cocycle} gives the first equality in
\eqref{eq:normalizer-zero}; $B_w=I$ gives the second.
\end{proof}

This proposition is conditional, not a repair of the cone obstruction.  For
$C_1,C_2,W_{24}$,
\Cref{thm:C1-all-phases,thm:C2-all-phases,thm:W24-all-phases} say that no
positive starting point makes all source prefixes legal in any cyclic phase.
If one enlarges the domain to force the algebraic telescoping, one has changed
the geometric setting and must separately justify the new transfer theory.

\subsection{A positive symmetric clock is a different suspension}

\begin{corollary}[The graph-clock alternative]
\label{cor:graph-clock}
An assignment
\begin{equation}
 L(e)=L(e^{-1})>0
 \label{eq:symmetric-clock}
\end{equation}
defines a positive roof over the finite non-backtracking edge shift.  It
cannot at the same time be an additive cocycle on the path groupoid in which
$ee^{-1}=1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the standard suspension construction over a subshift:
sum $L$ along the successive symbols.  The second follows because
$L(e)+L(e^{-1})=2L(e)>0$, whereas an additive groupoid cocycle must take the
unit word $ee^{-1}$ to zero by \Cref{thm:roof-sign}.
\end{proof}

Thus \eqref{eq:symmetric-clock} is mathematically legitimate, but its inverse
label is an oriented graph symbol rather than positive-time inverse motion in
the original AGY groupoid.  We refer to the resulting object as the
\emph{symmetric Hashimoto suspension}.  Calling it a natural extension of the
AGY return map would erase the distinction proved above.

\subsection{Repetitions do not commute with changing clocks}

Periodic-orbit zeta functions organize a primitive orbit together with all of
its repetitions.  For a positive flow period $T_\gamma$, the logarithmic
expansion has terms $e^{-smT_\gamma}/m$; see, for example,
\citet[Section~2.2]{DyatlovZworski2016}.  The identity words expose a singular
boundary.

\begin{proposition}[Repetition dichotomy]
\label{prop:repetition-dichotomy}
Suppose a projective matrix-normalizer cocycle is defined along an
identity-holonomy word $w$.  Then
\[
 T(w^m)=mT(w)=0\qquad(m\geq1).
\]
Consequently a flow-style Euler factor formally contains
$(1-e^{-s\cdot0})^{-1}$, and the logarithmic repetition series contains
$\sum_{m\geq1}1/m$.  By contrast, under unit symmetric graph length,
\[
 T(C_1^m)=T(C_2^m)=6m,\qquad T(W_{24}^m)=24m,
\]
and the corresponding small-$u$ Hashimoto trace-log is regular.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The zero projective period is \eqref{eq:normalizer-zero}; additivity gives the
same conclusion for powers.  Substitution into the primitive flow factor and
its logarithm gives the divergence.  Graph periods are symbolic word lengths,
which multiply under repetition.
\end{proof}

One may use either clock when its hypotheses are satisfied.  One may not use
zero signed projective time to certify the primitive orbit and positive graph
length to regularize its repetitions.  That hybrid would not be the
trace-log of a single dynamical system.
