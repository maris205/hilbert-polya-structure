\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Transfer-operator constructions often benefit from passing from a one-sided
symbolic dynamics to a two-sided or groupoid language.  In a hyperbolic
system, past and future coordinates can encode stable and unstable directions,
and periodic-orbit trace formulae may be organized by primitive cycles and
their repetitions.  The passage is not formal, however.  A symbol denoted
$e^{-1}$ can mean at least three different things: the inverse of an arrow in
a path groupoid, a branch label in a natural extension, or merely the
oppositely oriented edge of a finite graph.  Those meanings impose different
positivity, time, and trace requirements.

This note examines that distinction in a concrete source-locked experiment.
Two length-six cyclically non-backtracking words and a length-twenty-four
relation have exact identity holonomy in frozen Rauzy--AGY matrices.  In a
finite symmetric graph they yield nonzero coefficients in a normalized
finite-group trace determinant.  That is an interesting algebraic
phenomenon: the group trace detects closed words whose chronological cocycle
product lies in the kernel.  The question is whether the same words can be
interpreted as positive-time periodic orbits of the original
Rauzy--Veech/AGY dynamics.

The main conclusion is a sharp boundary statement.

\begin{quote}
For the three certified words, identity of the chronological matrix product is
not sufficient for a positive Rauzy orbit, a positive two-sided groupoid roof,
or an ordinary same-space nuclear transfer operator.  The finite group-trace
determinant survives, but only as the determinant germ of a newly declared
symmetric graph system.
\end{quote}

There are four logically independent gates behind this conclusion.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(G\arabic*),leftmargin=2.6em]
\item \emph{Cone gate.}  The length vector of a forward Rauzy path evolves by
  $B^{-\mathsf T}$, while a transfer inverse branch is naturally
  contravariant and uses $B^{\mathsf T}$ in reversed application order.
  Exact rational feasibility, not a floating-point orbit search, is required
  for each cyclic phase.
\item \emph{Roof gate.}  An additive real cocycle on a groupoid satisfies
  $\tau(g^{-1})=-\tau(g)$.  Hence a strictly positive assignment to both
  orientations is not an intrinsic groupoid time.
\item \emph{Operator gate.}  If both orientations act as faithful bounded
  inverses on one infinite-dimensional Banach space, the associated finite
  non-backtracking block cannot be compact or nuclear.  Modern anisotropic
  and microlocal theories avoid this premise; they do not invalidate the
  theorem.
\item \emph{Flat-trace gate.}  On an enlarged domain, identity projective
  holonomy gives the identity return map, a neutral fixed family rather than
  an isolated hyperbolic periodic point.
\end{enumerate}

The cone gate is completely finite and exact.  Let chronological products
use the convention that later events multiply on the left.  The two
length-six words are
\begin{align*}
C_1&=(0t,1b,0t^{-1},0b,3t,0b^{-1}),\\
C_2&=(4t,6b^{-1},6t,5b,6t^{-1},6b).
\end{align*}
The third word, denoted $W_{24}$, is the frozen length-twenty-four expansion
of the earlier $A,B,C$ relation.  Exact replay gives
\[
B_{C_1}=B_{C_2}=B_{W_{24}}=I_4.
\]
Nevertheless, all $6+6+24$ cyclic phases fail the positive length-cone gate,
and all $6+6+24$ cyclic phases separately fail the transfer gate.  The proof
uses only integer row certificates: either a required prefix-coordinate row
is nonpositive, or positive integer combinations of required rows sum to
zero.  Two raw covariant homology witnesses remain positive for $C_1$ and
$C_2$, which is a useful control that the length and covariant conventions
were not silently exchanged.

The conceptual distinction can be summarized as follows.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{0.96\textwidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.22\textwidth}
  >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.31\textwidth}X@{}}
\toprule
Object & Meaning of $e^{-1}$ & Consequence for time and trace \\
\midrule
Rauzy/AGY positive branch & Exact inverse matrix tested against the source
positive cone & May fail to be a legal branch; the AGY return time is positive
only on its actual domain. \\
Path groupoid & Categorical inverse of $e$ & Additive time is antisymmetric;
$ee^{-1}$ is a unit of time zero. \\
Symmetric graph shift & A second oriented edge symbol & A positive symmetric
edge length is legal, but defines a new non-backtracking suspension. \\
Finite group-trace operator & Group-labelled finite edge matrix & Identity
holonomy contributes through the group trace without ordinary compactness on
$\ell^2(G)$. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

This paper is deliberately an obstruction paper.  It does not claim that
two-sided symbolic dynamics, anisotropic spaces, or group von Neumann traces
are generally unsuitable for dynamical zeta functions.  Positive-roof
suspension theory is classical \citep{ParryPollicott1990}, and anisotropic and
microlocal constructions are powerful precisely when genuine hyperbolicity
supplies the required contraction and wavefront geometry
\citep{GouezelLiverani2006,GiuliettiLiveraniPollicott2013,
GiuliettiLiveraniPollicottErratum2022,DyatlovZworski2016}.  The local result
here says that the certified kernel words cannot be inserted into those
theories by identifying formal inverse edges with positive-time AGY inverse
dynamics.

The argument is organized as follows.  \Cref{sec:setting} fixes chronology and
separates the three matrix actions.  \Cref{sec:cone} gives the exact all-phase
cone certificates.  \Cref{sec:roof} proves the groupoid roof obstruction and
tracks repetitions.  \Cref{sec:operator} gives the same-space nuclearity and
flat-trace dichotomy.  \Cref{sec:grouptrace} isolates the finite group-trace
object that remains valid.  \Cref{sec:routea} records the resulting
\RouteA{} decision and the nonduplicative next gate.  The appendices specify
the reproducible certificate protocol and collect the scope boundaries in a
single table.
