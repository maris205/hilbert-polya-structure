\begin{abstract}
For the positive-quadrant map
$F(x,y)=(y,(1+y)/x)$, direct rational simplification gives $F^5=I$.
There is one fixed point, $(\phi,\phi)$ with
$\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, and every other point has exact period five.  Hence
$\operatorname{Fix}(F^n)$ is a singleton when $5\nmid n$ and the entire
positive quadrant when $5\mid n$.  Thus the classical Artin--Mazur series
is undefined.  The measure $dx\,dy/(xy)$ is invariant, and the resulting
Koopman operator is a natural unitary of order five.  All five root-of-unity
eigenspaces have infinite multiplicity; thus the operator is noncompact and
not trace class, so the ordinary Fredholm determinant is unavailable as
well.  These exact obstructions reject the model as a periodic-orbit zeta
candidate; no regularized substitute or target-spectrum claim is made.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Lyness map; birational dynamics; exact
periodicity; fixed-point obstruction; Artin--Mazur zeta; Route A.

\section{Exact five-cycle identity}
On $X=(0,\infty)^2$, every denominator below is nonzero.  Direct substitution
gives
\begin{align*}
F(x,y)&=\left(y,\frac{1+y}{x}\right),&
F^2(x,y)&=\left(\frac{1+y}{x},\frac{1+x+y}{xy}\right),\\
F^3(x,y)&=\left(\frac{1+x+y}{xy},\frac{1+x}{y}\right),&
F^4(x,y)&=\left(\frac{1+x}{y},x\right),\qquad F^5(x,y)=(x,y).
\end{align*}
Thus $F^5=I$ globally, not merely on a finite sample.

\paragraph{Proposition.}
The point $(\phi,\phi)$ is the unique fixed point, and every other point of
$X$ has exact least period five.

\paragraph{Proof.}
A fixed point has $y=x$ and $x^2=x+1$.  Only the root
$\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ is positive.  Every least period divides the prime
number five, so deleting the unique period-one point leaves period five.
\hfill$\square$

\section{The fixed-set obstruction}
For every positive integer $n$,
\[
\operatorname{Fix}(F^n)=
\begin{cases}
\{(\phi,\phi)\},&5\nmid n,\\
X,&5\mid n.
\end{cases}
\]
The classical definition
\[
\zeta_{\rm AM}(z)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge1}
\#\operatorname{Fix}(F^n)\frac{z^n}{n}\right)
\]
requires every fixed set to be finite.  Here $\operatorname{Fix}(F^5)=X$
is uncountable.  Replacing it by a component count, a quotient count, or a
regularized trace would define a different object and is not done here.

\section{Invariant geometry and natural lift}
The Jacobian determinant is $(1+y)/x^2$, while the product of the image
coordinates is $y(1+y)/x$.  Therefore
\[
 \frac{|\det DF(x,y)|}{F_1(x,y)F_2(x,y)}=\frac1{xy},
\]
so $d\mu=dx\,dy/(xy)$ is invariant.  Moreover
\[
F^{-1}(x,y)=\left(\frac{1+x}{y},x\right),\qquad
R(x,y)=(y,x),\qquad RFR=F^{-1}.
\]
On $H=L^2(X,\mu)$ define $Uf=f\circ F$.  Then $U$ is unitary and
$U^5=I$.  If $\omega=e^{2\pi i/5}$, cyclic Fourier orthogonality gives
\[
P_j=\frac15\sum_{r=0}^4\omega^{-jr}U^r,
\qquad \operatorname{ran}P_j=\ker(U-\omega^jI).
\]
The negative sign is fixed by $UP_j=\omega^jP_j$.

Off $(\phi,\phi)$ the action is free.  Inductively choose a point outside
the finite compact union of the earlier orbit tubes and then a relatively
compact open set $A_m$ whose five translates are mutually disjoint and
avoid all earlier tubes.  Each $A_m$ has positive finite measure.  If
$0\ne h_m\in L^2(A_m)$, the five terms of $P_jh_m$ have disjoint supports,
so $\|P_jh_m\|^2=\|h_m\|^2/5>0$.  Different $m$ give orthogonal vectors.
Thus every fifth-root eigenspace is infinite-dimensional.  Hence $U$ is
noncompact, belongs to no finite Schatten class, and is not trace class;
the ordinary trace-class determinant $\det(I-zU)$ does not exist.  It is
also not self-adjoint: self-adjoint unitarity and $U^5=I$ would force $U=I$,
contrary to the nontrivial composition action.

Let $V_Rf=f\circ R$ and let $K$ be complex conjugation.  The antiunitary
$\Theta=V_RK$ satisfies
$\Theta U\Theta^{-1}=U^{-1}$.  This is a natural reversal for the Koopman
lift, not a self-adjoint Hamiltonian construction.
