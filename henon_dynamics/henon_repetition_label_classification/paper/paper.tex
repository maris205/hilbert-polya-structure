\begin{abstract}
Every periodic multiplier of the integral area-preserving H\'enon map is an
algebraic unit.  Could a natural scalar transform nevertheless turn these
units into rational prime labels while preserving all orbit repetitions?  We
classify the functorial rational category.  If
$R\in\mathbb Q(X)^\times$ satisfies $R(X^r)=R(X)^r$ identically for every
$r\ge1$, then $R(X)=X^k$ for an integer $k$.  The divisor proof is global and
uses no orbit cutoff.  Hence every rational repetition-compatible label of a
H\'enon multiplier remains an algebraic unit and cannot be a rational prime.
We also classify continuous positive labels on the unstable ray $X>1$
under the repetition law: they are exactly $X^c$ with $c\in\mathbb R$.  The
pressure-normalized label $X^{h_*}$ from the preceding prime-orbit theorem is
therefore the precise nonrational survivor.  A finite
adversarial scan of 2,187 Laurent polynomials and explicit trace/determinant
mutations independently checks the theorem's boundaries.
\end{abstract}

\section{The scalar repetition problem}
For a primitive H\'enon orbit with multiplier $\lambda$, the $r$th repetition
has multiplier $\lambda^r$.  A scalar Euler label $R(\lambda)$ preserves the
prime-power law only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:law}
R(\lambda^r)=R(\lambda)^r.
\end{equation}
We first impose functoriality: \eqref{eq:law} is an identity on the
multiplicative group $\mathbb G_m$, not a coincidence on a finite orbit
table.  This is the minimal source-native rational category.

\section{Rational classification}
\begin{theorem}[Rational repetition classification]\label{thm:rational}
Let $R\in\mathbb C(X)^\times$.  If
\[
R(X^r)=R(X)^r
\]
as rational functions for every integer $r\ge1$, then
\[
\boxed{R(X)=X^k}
\]
for a unique $k\in\mathbb Z$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
It is enough to use $r=2$.  Let $D=\operatorname{div}(R)$ on
$\mathbb P^1$.  The identity gives
\[
[2]^*D=2D,
\qquad [2](X)=X^2.
\]
Suppose a point $a\in\mathbb C^\times$ lies in the support of $D$.  Every
$2^m$th root of $a$ then lies in the support of $[2]^{m*}D=2^mD$.  The
union of these root sets has unbounded cardinality as $m$ grows,
contradicting the finite support of a rational divisor.  Hence $D$ is supported on
$\{0,\infty\}$, so $R(X)=cX^k$ for $c\ne0$ and $k\in\mathbb Z$.  Substitution
into $R(X^2)=R(X)^2$ gives $c=c^2$, hence $c=1$.
\end{proof}

The same proof works over $\mathbb Q$ and any characteristic-zero field.
The statement does not assume that the actual H\'enon multiplier set is
Zariski dense; it explicitly classifies rules functorial on all of
$\mathbb G_m$.

\section{H\'enon unit obstruction}
The preceding all-period theorem proves that every $H_6$ periodic multiplier
$\lambda$ and its inverse are algebraic integers.

\begin{corollary}[No rational functorial prime label]\label{cor:noprime}
For every $R$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:rational}, $R(\lambda)$ is an algebraic
unit.  If it is rational, it is $\pm1$.  It can never be a rational prime.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:rational} gives $R(\lambda)=\lambda^k$, which is a unit.
The only units in $\mathbb Q$ integral over $\mathbb Z$ are $\pm1$.
\end{proof}

Two obvious alternatives fail before this corollary is applied.  Trace uses
\[
T(X)=X+X^{-1},
\qquad T(X^2)=T(X)^2-2,
\]
not $T(X)^2$.  The fixed-point determinant uses
\[
F(X)=2-X-X^{-1}
\]
and also fails the square law.  Their repetition sequences are Chebyshev or
cyclic-resultant data, not powers of one primitive scalar label.

\section{Continuous positive unstable-ray classification}
The rational theorem must not be overextended to the pressure normalization.

\begin{theorem}[Continuous positive labels]\label{thm:continuous}
Let $L:(1,\infty)\to\mathbb R_{>0}$ be continuous and suppose
\[
L(X^r)=L(X)^r
\]
for every $X>1$ and integer $r\geq1$.  Then there is a unique
$c\in\mathbb R$ such that
\[
L(X)=X^c.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $f(t)=\log L(e^t)$ for $t>0$.  The repetition law says
$f(rt)=rf(t)$ for every positive integer $r$.  If $q=m/n$ is a positive
rational, then
\[
n f(qt)=f(nqt)=f(mt)=m f(t),
\]
so $f(qt)=qf(t)$.  Taking $t=1$ and approximating any positive real by
positive rationals, continuity gives $f(t)=t f(1)$.  Put $c=f(1)$ and
exponentiate.
\end{proof}

For $c\in\mathbb Z$ this agrees with Theorem~\ref{thm:rational}.  The
pressure-normalized label from the entropy-one H\'enon suspension has
\[
c=h_*,\qquad L(|\lambda|)=|\lambda|^{h_*}.
\]
No theorem in this batch says that $h_*$ is integral, rational, algebraic, or
transcendental.  Thus this label is a genuine survivor, not a loophole that
Corollary~\ref{cor:noprime} already closes.

\section{Finite adversarial certificate}
The code scans all $3^7=2{,}187$ Laurent polynomials supported on exponents
$-3,\ldots,3$ with coefficients in $\{-1,0,1\}$.  Exactly eight satisfy the
square law: zero and the seven monomials $X^k$ with coefficient one.  The
zero polynomial is retained only as a test sentinel and is excluded from
Theorem~\ref{thm:rational}.  Separate mutations verify that trace and
$2-X-X^{-1}$ fail.  C46's certificate and README are hash locked.

\section{Conclusion}
Exact repetition is highly rigid.  In the rational category it permits only
integer powers, and the all-period H\'enon unit theorem then forbids rational
prime labels.  Pressure normalization survives precisely because it exits
that category.  The batch therefore ends with one sharply isolated positive
candidate rather than an open-ended list of scalar repairs.
