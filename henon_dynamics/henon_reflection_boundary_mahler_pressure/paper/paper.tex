\begin{abstract}
For the area-preserving H\'enon map
$H(x,y)=(6-x^2-y,x)$, previous full-horseshoe and algebraic-exhaustion
results make every odd primitive mixed-axis coordinate divisor reduced,
totally real, and of degree
$D_n=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)2^{(d+1)/2}$.  Ordinary coordinate Weil
height is uniformly bounded, so its fixed-parameter pressure is flat.  We
identify the correct extensive packet limit.  Primitive coordinates sampled
at their marked reflection axis converge to a non-invariant
reflection-boundary Bernoulli measure, while time-averaging the same selected
orbits converges to the invariant maximal-entropy Bernoulli measure.  The
marked limit implies convergence of normalized logarithmic Mahler measures
and a nonconstant linear packet pressure
\[
 \lim_{\substack{n\to\infty\\ n\ \mathrm{odd}}}
 \frac1n\log\!\left(D_ne^{-snD_n^{-1}\log M(\widetilde\Psi_n)}\right)
 =\frac12\log2-s\kappa_J,
\]
where $0<\kappa_J\le\log(1+\sqrt7)$.  Exact symbolic enumeration through
period $21$ and independent primitive-polynomial diagnostics through period
$11$ accompany the theorem.  The result is a packet-level thermodynamic
statement; it does not prove an individual height pressure, a prime trace,
or a Hilbert--P\'olya operator.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Consider the scaled H\'enon automorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H}
 H(x,y)=(6-x^2-y,x).
\end{equation}
It is linearly conjugate to the repository normalization
$(q,p)\mapsto(1-6q^2-p,q)$.  Arai's certified hyperbolic plateau
\cite{Arai2007} transports the Devaney--Nitecki full-shift horseshoe
\cite{DevaneyNitecki1979} to parameter $6$.  Combining this with the
Friedland--Milnor complex fixed-point count \cite{FriedlandMilnor1989}
exhausts every complex periodic point by the real full two-shift.  In
particular, the odd mixed-axis primitive coordinate polynomial is reduced,
effective and totally real.

The integral recurrence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrence}
 x_{j+1}=6-x_j^2-x_{j-1}
\end{equation}
gives the sharp periodic bound $|x_j|\le1+\sqrt7$.  Thus an ordinary
fixed-parameter height weight is non-extensive.  The first natural repair is
the packet-average Mahler mass
\begin{equation}\label{eq:an-intro}
 a_n=\frac1{D_n}\log M(\widetilde\Psi_n),
\end{equation}
multiplied by the period clock $n$.

A subtlety appears before pressure: mixed-axis roots are marked at a
reflection axis.  They are sparse, symmetry-centered points rather than
generic marked periodic points.  We prove that their axis distribution is a
reflected one-sided Bernoulli process, not the invariant two-sided Bernoulli
measure.  Time-averaging the same orbits does recover maximal entropy.  This
separation is the main structural result and prevents a false identification
of the two Mahler slopes.

\section{Equivariant full-shift coding}

Define the involution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
 J(x,y)=(x,6-x^2-y).
\end{equation}
Then $J^2=1$ and $JHJ=H^{-1}$.  Let $\sigma$ be the left shift on
$\SigmaTwo$ and let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
 (\rho\omega)_k=\omega_{-k}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Equivariant coding]\label{lem:equivariant}
There is a homeomorphism $\pi:\SigmaTwo\to\Lambda$, where $\Lambda$ is the
real chain recurrent set of \eqref{eq:H}, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equivariant}
 \pi\sigma=H\pi,\qquad \pi\rho=J\pi.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At a Devaney--Nitecki large-parameter anchor, the sign itinerary is a
one-to-one full-shift coding.  Since $J$ preserves the first coordinate and
$H^kJ=JH^{-k}$, that coding intertwines $J$ with \eqref{eq:rho}.

Continue the basic set and its conjugacy along Arai's connected uniformly
hyperbolic plateau from the anchor to parameter $6$.  Choose the structural
continuation $h_a$ in the standard small-neighborhood normalization.  Both
$h_a$ and $J_a h_aJ_{10}$ conjugate the anchor dynamics to the parameter-$a$
dynamics and are close to the identity.  Expansivity gives uniqueness in
this normalization, so $J_a h_aJ_{10}=h_a$.  Composing the anchor itinerary
with $h_6$ proves \eqref{eq:equivariant}.
\end{proof}

This is a specialization of standard symbolic/hyperbolic continuation
machinery; \citet{LindMarcus1995} gives symbolic background, and
\citet{Kang2018} gives broader reversible-periodic context.  Neither source
is used to assert the counting or limit theorem below.

\section{Reflection half-words and primitive removal}

Fix an odd integer $n=2m+1$.  The intersection
$\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n)\cap\operatorname{Fix}(\rho)$ consists of the
words
\begin{equation}\label{eq:palindrome}
 (\omega_0,\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_m,
  \omega_m,\ldots,\omega_1),
\end{equation}
so it has cardinality $2^{m+1}$.  Under \cref{lem:equivariant}, these are
exactly the marked points in
$\operatorname{Fix}(H^n)\cap\operatorname{Fix}(J)$.  The mixed-axis closure
has degree $2^{m+1}$ and consists of simple points, so no symbolic point or
algebraic root is missing.

Let $\Omega_n^{\mathrm{prim}}$ denote the exact-period subset and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dn}
 D_n=\#\Omega_n^{\mathrm{prim}}
 =\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)2^{(d+1)/2}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Primitive subtraction]\label{lem:primitive}
If $U_n=\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n)\cap\operatorname{Fix}(\rho)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:primitive-bound}
 \frac{\#(U_n\setminus\Omega_n^{\mathrm{prim}})}{\#U_n}
 \le \tau(n)2^{-n/3}.
\end{equation}
Consequently uniform measure on $U_n$ and on
$\Omega_n^{\mathrm{prim}}$ differ in total variation by a quantity tending
exponentially to zero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every proper divisor $d$ of an odd integer $n$ satisfies $d\le n/3$.
Bounding all lower-period contributions by
$\sum_{d\mid n,d<n}2^{(d+1)/2}$ and dividing by $2^{(n+1)/2}$ gives
\eqref{eq:primitive-bound}.  The total variation identity follows because
the primitive family is a subset of the full family.
\end{proof}

In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy}
 \lim_{\substack{n\to\infty\\n\ \mathrm{odd}}}\frac1n\log D_n
 =\frac12\log2.
\end{equation}

\section{Two different weak-star limits}

Let $\eta_n$ be uniform measure on $\Omega_n^{\mathrm{prim}}$, with the
reflection axis marked at coordinate zero.  Let $(\xi_k)_{k\ge0}$ be iid
fair signs and form a two-sided sequence by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-process}
 \omega_k=\omega_{-k}=\xi_k\qquad(k\ge0).
\end{equation}
Denote its law by $\eta_J$.

\begin{theorem}[Reflection-boundary limit]\label{thm:axis}
As odd $n$ tends to infinity,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:axis-limit}
 \eta_n\stackrel{*}{\longrightarrow}\eta_J.
\end{equation}
The limit is not shift invariant and is not the fair two-sided Bernoulli
measure $\mu_B$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed cylinder radius $r$ and $n>2r$, the free coordinates
$\omega_0,\ldots,\omega_r$ in \eqref{eq:palindrome} are independent fair
signs.  The negative coordinates are their reflected copies.  This proves
cylinder convergence for the full family; \cref{lem:primitive} transfers it
to the primitive family.

Under $\eta_J$, the event $\{\omega_{-1}=\omega_1\}$ has probability one,
whereas it has $\mu_B$-probability $1/2$.  After applying one shift, the same
event pulls back to $\{\omega_0=\omega_2\}$, which has $\eta_J$-probability
$1/2$.  Hence $\eta_J$ is not shift invariant.
\end{proof}

Now erase the distinguished axis by averaging over each cyclic orbit:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit-average}
 \bar\eta_n=\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(\sigma^j)_*\eta_n.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Orbit-averaged limit]\label{thm:orbit}
For odd $n\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit-limit}
 \bar\eta_n\stackrel{*}{\longrightarrow}\mu_B.
\end{equation}
More precisely, for a cylinder $C$ depending on coordinates $[-r,r]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cylinder-bound}
 |\bar\eta_n(C)-\mu_B(C)|
 \le \frac{4r+1}{n}+\tau(n)2^{-n/3}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First use all palindromes.  A window centered at $j$ contains two positions
paired by reflection only if
$2j\equiv-(a+b)\pmod n$ for some $a,b\in[-r,r]$.  Because $n$ is odd,
each value of $a+b\in[-2r,2r]$ produces at most one bad center, so there are
at most $4r+1$.  At every other center the window reads distinct free
half-word coordinates and has exactly the fair Bernoulli law.  Average over
centers and then apply \cref{lem:primitive}.
\end{proof}

\section{Extensive packet pressure}

Write $x(z)$ for the first scaled coordinate and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi}
 \phi(z)=\logp|x(z)|.
\end{equation}
This is continuous on the compact horseshoe and satisfies
$0\le\phi\le\log(1+\sqrt7)$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappas}
 \kappa_J=\int\phi\,d(\pi_*\eta_J),\qquad
 \kappa_{\max}=\int\phi\,d(\pi_*\mu_B).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:kappa-positive}
One has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa-bound}
 0<\kappa_J\le\log(1+\sqrt7).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound is immediate.  The constant negative itinerary represents
the fixed point with first coordinate $-1-\sqrt7$.  By continuity, a finite
cylinder around this sequence has $\phi>0$.  That cylinder has positive
$\eta_J$-measure, proving strict positivity.
\end{proof}

Let $\widetilde\Psi_n\in\Z[T]$ be the monic integral primitive coordinate
polynomial.  Total reality and squarefreeness give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mahler}
 a_n:=\frac1{D_n}\log M(\widetilde\Psi_n)
 =\frac1{D_n}\sum_{\widetilde\Psi_n(\alpha)=0}\logp|\alpha|.
\end{equation}
The right side is the $\phi$-average under $\pi_*\eta_n$.

\begin{theorem}[Packet-Mahler pressure]\label{thm:pressure}
For every fixed $s\in\R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packet-pressure}
 \lim_{\substack{n\to\infty\\n\ \mathrm{odd}}}
 \frac1n\log\left(D_n\exp(-sn a_n)\right)
 =\frac12\log2-s\kappa_J.
\end{equation}
This pressure is nonconstant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:axis} and continuity, $a_n\to\kappa_J$.  Combine this with
\eqref{eq:entropy}.  Nonconstancy follows from
\cref{prop:kappa-positive}.
\end{proof}

Similarly, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bn}
 b_n=\frac1{nD_n}\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}
 \phi(H^jz_\alpha),
\end{equation}
where $z_\alpha\in\operatorname{Fix}(J)$ is the point with first coordinate
$\alpha$.  Then \cref{thm:orbit} gives $b_n\to\kappa_{\max}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit-pressure}
 \lim\frac1n\log\left(D_n\exp(-sn b_n)\right)
 =\frac12\log2-s\kappa_{\max}.
\end{equation}

\section{Claim boundary and next theorem}

The pressure in \eqref{eq:packet-pressure} weights the complete packet by
its average Mahler mass.  It is not the individual partition
$\sum_\alpha e^{-sn h(\alpha)}$.  If $\widetilde\Psi_n$ factors, different
factors may have different Weil heights; weak convergence of all roots does
not control that finer distribution.

The finite rows suggest $\kappa_J\ne\kappa_{\max}$, but a theorem requires
certified cylinder enclosures and a tail estimate for \eqref{eq:phi}.  No
prime-power family, von Mangoldt amplitude, determinant continuation,
self-adjoint operator, zeta-zero correspondence, or Riemann-hypothesis
statement is obtained here.

\section{Conclusion}

The reflection slice supports a genuine extensive packet pressure, but its
natural marked measure is a boundary process rather than an invariant
equilibrium state.  Orbit averaging restores maximal entropy.  This
distinction turns a false shortcut into two exact limit theorems and isolates
a concrete next task: rigorously separate the two H\'enon Mahler slopes.
