\begin{abstract}
We give an exact, bounded computation for one released Gassmann pair in the labelled action of
$G=W(E_6)$, $|G|=51840$.  If $H_+$ and $H_-$ are the nonconjugate subgroups of order $162$
and index $320$, then the three finite etale algebras
\[
 F_+\otimes F_+,\qquad F_+\otimes F_-,\qquad F_-\otimes F_-
\]
are pairwise distinct in the Burnside ring while their rational linearizations, and hence their
formal products of Dedekind zeta functions, agree.  We enumerate all 36 double-coset factors,
separate the 160 conjugate mixed positions into 12 factors and 8 rational field types, and give
the complete 160/12/8 dictionary.  A normalized Fourier decomposition of a released integral
carrier has rank three and identifies an exact degree-40 mixed base through an embedded stabilizer
equality.  The resulting $A/B/M/F_+$ diamond has degrees $40,80,160,320$.  Global arithmetic
for all advertised fields and both retained local branches is recorded.  The computation is
reproducible from the released source and result inventories; it makes no claim about bad Euler
factors, root numbers, automorphy, RH, or a Hilbert--Polya operator.
\end{abstract}

\section{Tensor products from double cosets}
For subgroups $H,J\leq G$, the diagonal action gives
\begin{equation}
 (G/H)\times(G/J)\simeq\coprod_{HgJ\in H\backslash G/J}G/(H\cap gJg^{-1}).
\label{eq:doublecoset}
\end{equation}
For a representative $g$ write $I_{ab,g}=H_a\cap gH_bg^{-1}$ and
$J_{ab,g}=\langle H_a,gH_bg^{-1}\rangle$.  Core-freeness and the common normal closure
identify the factor and base fields as $K^{I_{ab,g}}=F_aF_b^g$ and
$K^{J_{ab,g}}=F_a\cap F_b^g$.

\begin{lemma}[Tensor and fixed-field dictionary]\label{lem:dictionary}
For $H,J\leq G$,
\[
K^H\otimes_\Q K^J\cong\prod_{HgJ\in H\backslash G/J}K^{H\cap gJg^{-1}},
\quad K^HK^J=K^{H\cap J},\quad K^H\cap K^J=K^{\langle H,J\rangle}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The orbit through $(H,gJ)$ in $(G/H)\times(G/J)$ has stabilizer
$H\cap gJg^{-1}$, and two such points share an orbit precisely when their representatives
share a double coset.  Passing from finite $G$-sets to finite etale algebras gives the first
identity.  The compositum is fixed by $H\cap J$ and the intersection by the subgroup generated
by $H$ and $J$, proving the remaining identities by Galois correspondence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Core-free isomorphism criterion]\label{lem:corefree}
If $I_1,I_2\leq G$ have trivial core, then $K^{I_1}$ and $K^{I_2}$ are
$\Q$-isomorphic if and only if $I_1$ and $I_2$ are conjugate in $G$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Trivial core makes $K$ the normal closure of either fixed field.  A $\Q$-isomorphism extends
to an automorphism of an algebraic closure and restricts on the common normal closure to some
$g\in G$.  For $\alpha\in K^{I_1}$ and $h\in I_1$,
$(ghg^{-1})(g\alpha)=g(h\alpha)=g\alpha$.  Thus $gI_1g^{-1}$ fixes $K^{I_2}$,
and equal degrees force equality.  Conversely a conjugator maps one fixed field to the other.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Burnside and zeta conclusions]\label{prop:burnside}
Equation~\eqref{eq:main} holds.  The three finite etale algebras are pairwise nonisomorphic,
while their products of Dedekind zeta functions agree.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}

so $xy$ differs from $x^2$ and $y^2$.  The self spectra agree, but their two diagonal
degree-320 constituents have $H_+$ and $H_-$ type respectively.  The released subgroups are
nonconjugate and core-free; Lemma~\ref{lem:corefree} separates these constituents, proving
$x^2\ne y^2$.

Rational linearization $\ell:B(G)\to R_\Q(G)$ is a ring homomorphism.  From the released
Gassmann identity $\ell(x)=\ell(y)$ we obtain
$\ell(x^2)=\ell(xy)=\ell(y^2)$.  In particular $x(x-y)$, $y(x-y)$, and
$(x+y)(x-y)$ are explicit nonzero kernel elements.  The tensor decompositions turn Burnside
inequality into nonisomorphism of the finite etale algebras.  Finally $\zeta_{K^H}$ is the Artin
$L$-function of $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\mathbf1$; multiplicativity over direct sums gives the
three equal zeta products.  This formal consequence computes no forbidden bad Euler data.
\end{proof}
