# Argument Blueprint

## Central thesis

This paper argues that a human-directed, AI-assisted exploration of arithmetic dynamical systems is most useful when it is organized as a source-bound obligation map rather than a generator of attractive conjectural matches: the P1 case record shows that explicit object identity, negative controls, provenance separation, and stop conditions turn local mathematics and failure modes into reusable knowledge while preventing their aggregation into an unsupported Hilbert--Pólya or RH claim.

## Sub-arguments

### A1. A roadmap must encode dependencies, not scores

- **Claim:** Route A and Route B are a dependency map for one candidate, not a collection of badges that can be assembled across systems.
- **Evidence:** The P1 roadmap states A0--A4 and B1--B5 as ordered obligations, while the status vocabulary prohibits cross-object credit transfer ([P1-RM](../rh_roadmap0.png), [P1-CL](../01-status-and-claim-vocabulary.md)).
- **Reasoning:** If clock, orbit, determinant, and operator owner do not belong to the same object, an apparent chain contains an unlicensed transfer rather than a proof step.
- **Counterargument:** A broad corpus may still provide convergent support even if no single object closes every gate.
- **Response:** It can guide hypothesis selection, but cannot supply a theorem-level implication; the paper names this distinction rather than denying the corpus’s heuristic value.

### A2. The six lines make different kinds of progress visible

- **Claim:** Phase I contains mathematically meaningful local results and controls, but the meaning of progress differs across free exploration, analytic determinant construction, classification, finite-system work, and continuous-flow diagnostics.
- **Evidence:** The six conclusions pages and controlling records preserve restricted results and their nonclaims: local \(P^*_{\rm loc}\), the LOG-0001 determinant chain, C424--C428 classifications, Batch07 interfaces, P211--P215 finite theorems, and P24--P28 controls/obstacles.
- **Reasoning:** A common evidence taxonomy distinguishes local mathematics from process status and unresolved routes, so heterogeneous records can be compared without being falsely equated.
- **Counterargument:** A synthesis can flatten differences and merely re-label existing documents.
- **Response:** The paper’s contribution is deliberately organizational and methodological: every summary is coupled to its object, source type, and explicit non-transfer boundary.

### A3. Failure modes are first-class search outputs

- **Claim:** Negative transfer theorems, nonfactorization, owner obstructions, and explicit `UNASSIGNED`/`NOT_APPLICABLE` records reduce the search space and improve later evaluation.
- **Evidence:** The Flow P24--P28 record separates unit-roof and physical-roof determinants, finite-level and inverse-limit claims, and positive controls from arithmetic credit; other Sessions preserve similar boundaries.
- **Reasoning:** A failed or scope-limited bridge identifies an exact prerequisite that a future candidate must meet, rather than merely contributing a narrative of unsuccessful attempts.
- **Counterargument:** Negative results can be overgeneralized into “the programme cannot work.”
- **Response:** The draft labels every negative result by object, hypothesis, and transfer mechanism, and explicitly rejects universal impossibility inferences.

### A4. Human--AI collaboration requires a division of epistemic responsibility

- **Claim:** AI can accelerate corpus navigation, source indexing, relation checking, and draft organization, while human researchers must retain control over research questions, source locks, mathematical acceptance, authorship, and release.
- **Evidence:** The P1 corpus is organized around source locks, route records, receipts, and claim boundaries; the draft’s own provenance package makes those roles auditable.
- **Reasoning:** This separation makes automated assistance useful without treating an AI output, a clean build, or an internal review as mathematical validation.
- **Counterargument:** The case record does not measure AI effectiveness against a human-only baseline.
- **Response:** The paper makes no comparative-performance claim. It proposes a disciplined workflow and documents its application to this source-bound corpus.

## Logical flow

```text
Research risk: plausible local clues are easy to over-read
  -> source-bound evidence model
  -> A0--A4 / B1--B5 obligation map
  -> six object-specific case records
  -> recurring transfer errors and scoped negative results
  -> human--AI candidate-admission protocol
  -> limitations and nonclaims
```

## Drafting constraints

1. Use “reports,” “records,” “establishes in the stated scope,” and “does not document” for source-bound claims.
2. Never say that the corpus “proves” RH, “constructs” a completed Hilbert--Pólya operator, or “solves” another major open problem.
3. State a source key at each material factual claim; use a canonical manuscript or ledger when the statement is theorem-level.
4. Keep the methodological conclusion stronger than the mathematical conclusion: the paper organizes evidence and failure modes; it does not add a new theorem.
