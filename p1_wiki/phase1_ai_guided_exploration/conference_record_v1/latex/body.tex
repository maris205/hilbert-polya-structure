\section{Introduction}

The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) remains an unusually demanding target for
human--AI mathematical exploration. As of 13 September 2026, the Clay
Mathematics Institute continues to classify RH as an unsolved Millennium Prize
Problem \citep{CMI2026}. The challenge is not simply to generate a numerical
resemblance or an appealing operator. In the candidate class and
evidence-obligation architecture adopted here, a credible Hilbert--P\'olya
(HP)-oriented candidate is required to connect arithmetic information,
periodic dynamics, analytic structure, and an operator-theoretic trace identity
without changing the mathematical object along the way.

This difficulty is timely in light of recent, but carefully bounded, advances
in AI-assisted mathematics. Machine learning has helped human researchers
formulate and investigate conjectures in selected areas of mathematics
\citep{Davies2021}; evaluator-coupled language-model search has generated new
constructions in constrained combinatorial and algorithmic settings
\citep{RomeraParedes2024}; neuro-symbolic systems have solved 25 of 30
translated IMO-AG-30 Euclidean-geometry problems \citep{Trinh2024}; and
reinforcement learning has been used to search for Lean-verified proofs in a
specialized olympiad-level setting \citep{Hubert2026}. These examples show
that AI can contribute to workflows with clear representations, evaluators,
and verification boundaries. They do \emph{not} constitute a proof of RH, a
solution of any other active Millennium Prize Problem, or evidence that a
language model can replace mathematical validation.

The question addressed here is therefore methodological:

\begin{quote}
\small How can the search for an arithmetic dynamical structure relevant to
the HP programme be reformulated as a broad, falsifiable, and auditable
human--AI candidate-search problem?
\end{quote}

The answer developed from the Phase-I record is \emph{candidate engineering}.
Rather than optimize a loosely specified analogy to the Riemann zeros, the
researcher freezes a candidate's identity and tests an ordered set of
obligations: endogenous arithmetic source, primitive-orbit and repetition
ownership, a dynamical zeta or determinant belonging to that same object, and
only then the stronger analytic and operator-theoretic requirements. AI can
help enumerate, compare, document, and challenge candidates; human researchers
remain responsible for definitions, proof acceptance, claim authorization, and
external communication.

This paper makes five contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item It puts a human-governed, AI-executed research framework first:
  mathematicians choose the prime-symbolic origin and scientific constraints;
  a jointly maintained \texttt{AGENTS.md} makes the search protocol explicit;
  and AI returns control at stated route or budget boundaries.
  \item It presents the updated programme roadmap as an \emph{obligation
  architecture}, not a cumulative progress chart.
  \item It compresses the Phase-I six-direction record into an evidence
  landscape that distinguishes useful local output from a missing bridge or a
  non-transfer boundary.
  \item It formulates the \sameobject{} invariant and a candidate card that
  makes hidden ownership changes visible early.
  \item It motivates a transition from family scanning to a wider portfolio of
  candidate architectures, screened first at A0--A2 rather than repeatedly
  polishing unrelated A0/A1-limited systems.
\end{enumerate}

The detailed summary of each direction is placed in \cref{app:record}; the
repository remains the canonical navigation layer for the underlying source
materials \citep{PhaseIRepository}. The present manuscript is a research
record and methodology paper, not an independent re-proof of those local
results.

\section{A human-governed framework for AI-assisted arithmetic-dynamical search}
\label{sec:framework}

\Cref{fig:framework} is the paper's core methodological contribution: a
human-governed framework for bounded AI search. The mathematician selects the
prime-symbolic research origin, chooses the scientific question, and decides
what counts as a valid connection; no generic chaotic or quantum system is
admitted and decorated with primes later. Mathematician and AI jointly draft a
versioned \texttt{AGENTS.md}; after human approval, AI executes only named
tasks and cannot enlarge the admissible class, replace a missing preservation
mechanism, or authorize a mathematical claim.

The contract has four irreducible parts. First, it fixes the search constraints.
For this programme, a main candidate must trace to a prime-symbolic ancestor
and name a preservation, deformation, dimensional lift, or geometric-realization
mechanism. It must also state the permitted system families, branches,
dimensions, parameter sources, and arithmetic inputs before the search starts.
A one-dimensional prototype, a non-autonomous deformation, and a conservative
or symplectic lift are therefore named alternatives in a constrained genealogy,
not interchangeable invitations to search every available dynamical system.

Second, the contract contains a route that can be decided at its stated scope.
It specifies the evidence required for a gate, the controls that can falsify a
proposed mechanism, and the possible dispositions. Here \texttt{GO} means only
that a candidate may proceed to the next declared screen; \texttt{END} archives
a bounded result; \texttt{FORK} creates a new object identity and candidate
card; and \texttt{HOLD} returns an unresolved scientific decision to the
mathematician. These labels cannot be used as substitutes for A0--A4 or Route-B
evidence.

Third, the default search strategy is breadth first. AI can efficiently compare
several permitted architectures, generate positive and negative controls, and
keep source and ownership ledgers synchronized. That breadth is disciplined:
each architecture must pass the lineage admission test and the cheap
same-object checks before deeper work. The strategy is not a claim that breadth
is universally optimal; it is a practical response to the Phase-I finding that
local successes from distinct objects do not add to one candidate.

Finally, the contract makes stopping explicit. A search may terminate because
it reaches a named roadmap boundary, violates lineage or same-object ownership,
finds a scoped negative result, exhausts its specified token/compute budget, or
requires a scientific decision outside its authorization. Budget exhaustion is
an operational stopping condition, not a mathematical negative result. At any
such stop, the AI hands the mathematician a compact record containing the
candidate card, evidence and controls, exact scope, decision label, and any
question that must be resolved before work can resume. An \texttt{AGENTS.md}
therefore provides reproducible research governance; it is not mathematical
evidence and cannot validate a proof by itself.

The framework is prospective. The prior-work guide records a research
trajectory and explicitly does not claim to have been a historical candidate
selection manual. The framework uses that trajectory to govern future
candidate engineering without retroactively assigning a uniform AI workflow to
the six Phase-I directions \citep{PhaseIRepository}.

\section{From Hilbert--P\'olya intuition to an obligation architecture}
\label{sec:roadmap}

The framework in \cref{fig:framework} governs how a candidate is admitted,
screened, stopped, and returned to human judgment. The roadmap in
\cref{fig:roadmap} answers a separate question: which mathematical obligations
an admitted candidate must earn. Neither diagram substitutes for the other.

The HP programme is often motivated by the expectation that a suitable spectral
or trace structure could illuminate the nontrivial zeros of the zeta function.
That motivation has inspired important connections among spectral asymptotics,
trace formulae, and arithmetic \citep{BerryKeating1999,Connes1999}. Dynamical
zeta functions also have a well-established role in the study of expanding
maps and Anosov flows \citep{Ruelle1976}. None of these conceptual links
licenses a candidate-specific conclusion by itself. The central practical
question is which obligations must be tied to \emph{one} object before an
analogy can be treated as a serious candidate.

\subsection{Route A and Route B}

\Cref{fig:roadmap} turns a diffuse analogy into a sequence of evidence
obligations. Route A begins with A0, an endogenous arithmetic source. A1
requires primitive, reproducible orbit ownership together with a stated
repetition convention. A2 asks for a stable dynamical zeta or Fredholm
determinant whose operator, domain, normalization, and orbit expansion belong
to the same candidate. A3 asks for the relevant global analytic or
Weil-compatible structure, and A4 for a natural classical-to-quantum lift.

Route B is deliberately more demanding. It concerns a fixed operator and
domain (B1), genuine self-adjointness (B2), the required spectral type (B3),
an exact prime-power/Weil-compatible trace identity (B4), and a global
completed-\(\Xi\) determinant or divisor equality (B5). It is not a second
source of credit that can repair missing arithmetic or orbit ownership in a
weak Route-A object.

The roadmap's key word is \emph{same}. A symbolic clock, physical closed
orbit, determinant borrowed from a different transfer system, and separately
constructed operator cannot be concatenated into one HP candidate. This is
the \sameobject{} invariant:

\begin{center}
\fcolorbox{analyticpurple}{softpurple}{\parbox{.84\linewidth}{\centering
\small\textbf{Same-object invariant}\\[-.15em]
Arithmetic source, clock/repetition, orbits, determinant, and operator
must have one owner.}}
\end{center}

The Round-2 chain in the figure---prime arithmetic \(\rightarrow\) symbolic
structure \(\rightarrow\) geometric carrier \(\rightarrow\) closed-orbit /
trace structure \(\rightarrow\) quantum spectrum---is a proposed, constrained
new-object design hypothesis. It is not a theorem-dependency graph, a
declaration that the links already hold, or a request to attach results from
separate Phase-I systems after the fact: each connection requires a new
preservation ledger and \sameobject{} validation.

\subsection{Why the workflow does not lower mathematical obligations}

The practical value of AI in this setting is not an ungrounded ``guess the
answer'' mode. It is most credible where the representation and rejection
condition are exposed: candidate enumeration, source retrieval, object-ledger
checking, adversarial control generation, proof-dependency review, and
reproducible documentation. This perspective is close to the
generator--evaluator separation demonstrated in program-search work
\citep{RomeraParedes2024}, but the evaluator for a mathematical research
candidate is partly formal and partly human. It includes source identity,
mathematical scope, and whether a purported bridge preserves the object.

Human authority remains indispensable. A fluent synthesis can make
nonidentical constructions look continuous; an automated retrieval system can
surface a nearby theorem under a misleading normalization; a numerical fit can
conceal target leakage. The human researcher must decide whether definitions
are genuinely the same, whether a proof is valid, which claims have been
earned, and whether a record may be released.

\section{Phase-I record and source-bound evidence design}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The Phase-I corpus and its status vocabulary}

The Phase-I knowledge base separates one early free-exploration line,
\texttt{zeta\_mvp0}, from five roadmap-driven sessions: Logistic Dynamics,
H\'{e}non Dynamics, Symplectic Map, Symbolic Dynamics, and Flow Systems
\citep{PhaseIRepository}. The five P24--P28 continuous-time forms belong to
the Flow session; they are not an additional top-level direction. The detailed
historical synthesis is bounded to the stated source-corpus snapshot
\texttt{419ee36c1e310469209f7b83c096ec8aea448386} (13 September 2026 UTC).
The new paper and the updated roadmap are later documentation artifacts and do
not retroactively alter that historical record.

Four kinds of statements are kept distinct throughout this paper:

a scoped negative control when it does \emph{not} close the full route.


\subsection{Guided search space: prime-symbolic lineage}
\label{sec:lineage}

For the forward-looking Round-2 candidate-engineering protocol proposed here,
the admissible main-candidate space is lineage-constrained. It turns the
project's prior-work trajectory into an explicit admission rule:

\begin{center}
\fcolorbox{localblue}{softblue}{\parbox{.88\linewidth}{\small
\textbf{Prime-symbolic candidate lineage}\\[-.25em]
\(\text{prime/composite observables}\)
\(\longrightarrow\) symbolic coding and admissibility\\
\(\longrightarrow\) justified sequential / non-autonomous deformation\\
\(\longrightarrow\) Logistic prototype and one-dimensional obstructions\\
\(\longrightarrow\) area-preserving H\'{e}non dimensional/geometric lift\\
\(\longrightarrow\) higher-dimensional conservative / symplectic carrier\\
\(\longrightarrow\) later prime-side explicit-formula / operator-geometry benchmark.}}
\end{center}

The prior-work guide records a sequence of ideas rather than a completed theory
or historical candidate-selection manual. This paper's admission rule is an
author-proposed methodological use of that sequence. The arrows in the
displayed lineage chain are therefore a prospective admission constraint and a
search genealogy, not completed mathematical implications. For any proposed
candidate, the record must identify its ancestral node or nodes, the
transformation class being used, and the exact mechanism that is preserved,
deformed, lifted, or refuted. The mechanism might be a specified prime-symbolic
observable, admissibility rule, return structure, coding, clock, or a
rigorously defined deformation thereof. If that relation is not yet proved, it
must be marked \texttt{OPEN}; it cannot be silently replaced by a narrative
similarity.

This adds a second protection to the \sameobject{} invariant. A generic
area-preserving map, fashionable arithmetic space, trace formula, or operator
does not become an arithmetic-dynamical candidate simply because primes can be
attached to it later. Without a documented prime-symbolic lineage and
preservation ledger, it is an \emph{external control}, not an admitted main
candidate. Likewise, a sequential/non-autonomous model must state its evolution
law and show why it is more than a time-dependent fitting schedule. Prime-side
explicit-formula and finite operator-geometry results are a benchmark for
keeping prime powers visible, not permission to insert prime tables, von
Mangoldt weights, or zero data by hand \citep{PhaseIRepository}.

The portfolio supports two conclusions. First, Phase I did \emph{not} identify
an already formed candidate that can simply be extended to a proof. More
precisely, within the source-bound record, no top-level direction is documented
as carrying an integrated A0+A1+A2 chain in one natural candidate. This is a
statement about the record and its named objects, not an impossibility theorem
about every system in the corresponding family.

Second, Phase I did not amount to empty failure. It preserved reusable
structures: a same-object analytic determinant chain in the Logistic line,
periodic classification and geometric interfaces in the H\'{e}non and
Symplectic lines, exact finite-system controls in the Symbolic line, and
continuous-time ownership tests in the Flow line. The methodological result
is the identification of bridges that cannot be filled by prose alone:
arithmetic source, clock/repetition convention, analytic owner, and operator
owner must be frozen together.

\section{From family scan to candidate engineering}
\label{sec:strategy}

The principal Phase-I recommendation is not that breadth is universally
superior to depth. The record is neither an optimization benchmark nor a
controlled comparison of research strategies. Rather, repeated early
boundaries, combined with the non-additivity of evidence across objects,
motivate a change in emphasis: explore a broader portfolio of \emph{candidate
architectures}, but subject every architecture to low-cost, early,
same-object tests before investing deeply in an A0/A1-limited system.

Here, broader does not mean a random sweep over generic dynamical forms. It
means a portfolio of lineage-compatible descendants of the prime-symbolic
starting point, each with a documented deformation, dimensional lift, geometric
realization, or stated preservation failure. A system that lacks that entry
condition remains useful as an external positive or negative control, but it
does not enter the main candidate portfolio.

The card makes a crucial research economy possible. It places cheap,
disqualifying ownership tests before expensive operator construction, long
numerical searches, or a polished narrative. A Logistic-style analytic chain
is then valuable as a control rather than as an invitation to resume closed
root hunting; H\'{e}non and Symplectic structures become possible geometric
ingredients to test inside a new object, not inherited Route credit; and
Symbolic coding is admissible only when it arises from the same candidate's
geometry and clock.

The same card also prevents a common historical reversal: select a generic
high-dimensional or conservative system first, then decorate it with primes.
For a non-autonomous proposal, the deformation law itself must be specified;
for a dimensional or geometric lift, the card must name a projection,
semiconjugacy, invariant, return relation, or explicitly open preservation
claim. The words high-dimensional, area-preserving, or symplectic are not by
themselves a prime-symbolic mechanism.

\subsection{Why the next search should be architecturally broader}

The roadmap's Round-2 structural-synthesis direction gives a concrete shape to
the proposal: seek a single candidate in which arithmetic structure, symbolic
coding, a geometric carrier, closed-orbit/trace data, and any later quantum
realization are derived from one construction. This does \emph{not} mean
splicing together a symbolic model, a symplectic map, a physical flow, and an
operator with unrelated owners. It means designing a new object so that the
relations can be checked rather than narrated.

There is an important distinction between wider search and lower standards.
Candidate diversity is useful because Phase I exposed several orthogonal
failure modes. The screening standard becomes stricter, not looser: every new
architecture must first survive A0, then A1, then A2 with the same identity.
An early scoped negative result is a successful search output when it rules out
a named construction under named conventions; it prevents the next round from
repeating the same ownership transfer in new vocabulary.

\section{Discussion, limitations, and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The distinctive contribution of this work is methodological rather than a
claim of mathematical closure. Its first contribution is the framework in
\cref{fig:framework}: the mathematician fixes a prime-symbolic research origin
and scientific authority; a jointly drafted, human-approved \texttt{AGENTS.md}
contract makes the permissible search, decision labels, breadth-first strategy,
and stopping conditions explicit; and AI returns a source-bound handoff rather
than extending the project autonomously. The obligation roadmap then treats the
HP programme as non-compensatory. A collection of impressive local features does
not add up to a candidate. Negative controls, stops, and inapplicability become
informative because they identify exactly where a bridge is absent. The
versioned repository preserves provenance, source identity, and the distinction
between historical research materials and this later synthesis.

Several limitations follow. The paper is not an independent proof audit of the
component results; its detailed claims remain constrained by their named source
records. It is not a benchmark comparing AI systems or research strategies. It
does not establish that a broader portfolio is optimal, only that the Phase-I
record supports a broader, gate-aware next search more strongly than repeated
unbounded deepening of previously limited objects. Finally, it is not a proof
of RH, a complete HP realization, an exact prime-power trace formula, a
completed-\(\Xi\) determinant identity, or a spectral identification of
Riemann zeros.

Within those limits, Phase I supplies a useful research result: it turns
failure modes into design constraints. The next step is not to pick a winner
from six incomparable lines. It is to construct and screen new \sameobject{}
candidate architectures that begin from a documented prime-symbolic lineage,
then state an explicit preservation/deformation/lift mechanism, arithmetic
origin, clock/repetition ownership, determinant ownership, controls, and a
route decision. If a future candidate survives those constraints, its positive
claims will be easier to audit. If it fails, the recorded failure will still
improve the next search.

\subsection{Early free exploration: \texttt{zeta\_mvp0}}

The early \texttt{zeta\_mvp0} programme preserves local counting structures,

does not become native evidence merely by being stored in the same directory
\citep{PhaseIRepository}.



\subsection{Session 1: Logistic Dynamics}

The Logistic line supplies the clearest preserved example of a \sameobject{}
analytic chain. At a frozen exact algebraic parameter, its record connects
first-return structure, an intrinsic polar roof, complex inverse branches,
boundary/partition bookkeeping, a nuclear transfer operator, a Fredholm
determinant, and growth/order information. This is valuable precisely because
the objects and normalizations are retained together. The same record also
draws its boundary: the chain does not provide the Riemann-target arithmetic
source, target-level global closure, or a natural quantization. Root hunting
and Riemann-zero comparison are therefore stopped for that object. The
methodological use of the line is as a seed/control for object ownership, not
as an unfinished RH proof \citep{PhaseIRepository}.

\subsection{Session 2: H\'{e}non Dynamics}

The H\'{e}non record studies restricted families through periodic
classification and related low-/higher-dimensional geometric diagnostics. It
preserves scoped rational/integer, affine-model, and period-spectrum results.
Its Route-A interpretations remain object-specific: arithmetic relevance is
weak; primitive-orbit information may be weak or fail for the particular
object; and later analytic or lift obligations are absent or not testable in
the named scope. Later frozen/admitted contracts were retained without being
misrepresented as completed papers. This makes H\'{e}non a potential Round-2
geometric bridge or Poincar\'{e}-section ancestor for a new construction, not an
inheritance mechanism for a different candidate's analytic or arithmetic
credit \citep{PhaseIRepository}.

\subsection{Session 3: Symplectic Map}

The Symplectic session produced five scoped interfaces and diagnostic tools
around conservative structures, periodic schemes, and qPI-related contexts.
Its formal disposition is \texttt{NOT\_APPLICABLE} for the route schema used
elsewhere. That status is not a scientific failure, and it is not a pass. It
means that no fixed candidate has been supplied to own simultaneously an
endogenous prime-to-orbit mechanism, a logarithmic clock/repetition law, a
target determinant, and a natural operator lift. The session is consequently
valuable as a carrier/diagnostic resource, but it cannot be displayed as
partial gate closure on a shared HP scorecard \citep{PhaseIRepository}.

\subsection{Session 4: Symbolic Dynamics}

The Symbolic session provides exact, finite, autonomous deterministic-system
results with a particularly audit-friendly evidence pattern. Its
exact-five internal-complete and external-hold labels are process states,
not a claim of global analytic continuation or an operator realization. The
important structural boundary is that a finite symbolic system, however clean
its algebra, does not automatically own a geometric carrier, physical clock,
primitive closed-orbit convention, global determinant, or quantum operator.
For Round 2, symbolic structure is therefore most promising when it is derived
from a candidate's geometry rather than attached to a distinct object as an
independent unit-roof model \citep{PhaseIRepository}.

\subsection{Session 5: Flow Systems}

The Flow session turns the ownership issue into an explicit continuous-time
stress test. Across its P24--P28 forms, it records local trace results,
negative controls, and obstructions involving roofs, inverse limits,
factorization, and operator ownership. Its bounded final ledger reports
positive arithmetic A2 = 0/5 and Route-B invocation = 0/5 for these five
forms. The scope is crucial: this is neither a verdict on all flows nor a
seventh top-level Phase-I direction. Its reusable conclusion is the strongest
form of the \sameobject{} rule: one candidate must own clock, primitive orbit,
repetition law, determinant, trace regime, and operator, rather than combining
a symbolic clock, physical orbit, borrowed determinant, and separate operator
\citep{PhaseIRepository}.


\section{Candidate-card template and non-transfer checks}
\label{app:card}

The compact candidate card in \cref{sec:strategy} is the operational
template. For the archival research record, its non-transfer checks are:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.},leftmargin=1.45em]
\item \textbf{Identity.} Freeze phase space, map/flow, parameters, measure,
and normalization. A changed object receives a new card.
\item \textbf{Lineage.} Name the prime-symbolic ancestor and the precise
deformation, dimensional lift, geometric realization, or preservation failure.
Without this ledger, classify the system as an external control before A0.
\item \textbf{Arithmetic and clock.} State permitted data, the endogenous
arithmetic mechanism, primitive convention, time/roof/action, and repetition
law. Do not replace a physical clock with a distinct symbolic clock.
\item \textbf{Analytic and operator owners.} Name the transfer operator,
zeta/determinant, domain, normalization, and any later Hilbert-space/operator
pair. Do not borrow these from a proxy.
\item \textbf{Controls and decision.} Record positive and negative controls,
direct evidence, and an explicit \texttt{OPEN}, \texttt{STOP}, \texttt{FORK},
or \texttt{EXTERNAL CONTROL} decision. No neighboring Session supplies credit.
\end{enumerate}
