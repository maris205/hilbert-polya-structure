\section{Introduction}

The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) remains an unusually demanding target for
human--AI mathematical exploration. As of 14 September 2026, the Clay
Mathematics Institute continues to classify RH as an unsolved Millennium Prize
Problem \citep{CMI2026}. The challenge is not simply to generate a numerical
resemblance or an appealing operator. Under the proposed candidate class and
evidence-obligation architecture used in this paper, an HP-oriented candidate
admitted for screening is expected to connect arithmetic information, periodic
dynamics, analytic structure, and an operator-theoretic trace identity without
changing the mathematical object along the way.

The urgency of this design question has become unusually visible in recent
AI-assisted mathematics. In a public announcement on 8 September 2026, OpenAI
reported what it describes as an analytical proof and a Lean formalization for
a finite-time-singularity formulation of the Navier--Stokes Millennium Prize
Problem. Its account describes coordinated agents exploring explicitly
specified variants, comparing intermediate approaches, and passing a resulting
argument to formal verification \citep{OpenAI2026NavierStokes}. This paper does
not independently adjudicate that report; at the present access date, the Clay
Mathematics Institute continues to list Navier--Stokes as active
\citep{CMI2026}. A guest post by Andreas Thom, hosted by Terence Tao, also
describes a reported result concerning the existence of non-sofic groups while
locating it within prior human mathematical work \citep{Thom2026NonSofic}.
These current events motivate a research-design question; they are not used
here as settled theorems or as evidence about RH.

Their useful commonality is methodological rather than theorem-level. A hard
mathematical endpoint becomes more suitable for high-throughput AI assistance
when a human researcher reformulates it as a bounded construction and
candidate-search problem: a mathematically motivated candidate class, explicit
variants, discriminating evaluators, and a verification or falsification
boundary. Earlier peer-reviewed work supplies related, narrower precedents in
human--AI conjecturing \citep{Davies2021}, evaluator-coupled program search
\citep{RomeraParedes2024}, specialized Euclidean-geometry reasoning
\citep{Trinh2024}, and Lean-grounded proof search \citep{Hubert2026}. Most
directly relevant to the arithmetic setting, a 2026 preprint reports an
unconditional critical-line proportion bound for zeta zeros together with Lean
4 formal verification \citep{AlpogeFurman2026}. This is a
search-and-construction design pattern, not a claim that arbitrary mathematical
questions reduce to blind enumeration or that a language model replaces proof,
peer scrutiny, or conceptual mathematical work.

A complementary governance point is made by a declaration initially signed by
25 Fields Medallists and republished by Tao: success on problem-solving
benchmarks should not displace human understanding, interpretation, attribution,
or transmission \citep{Tao2026Misalignment}. We use that declaration as a
position on mathematical governance rather than as empirical evidence. It
explains why the present framework leaves the selection of a meaningful
mathematical origin, the acceptance of a proof, the authorization of a claim,
and the stewardship of provenance under human responsibility.

These precedents do not by themselves prescribe how a long-horizon
arithmetic-dynamical candidate should inherit a mathematical origin, retain one
object identity across orbit and determinant constructions, or preserve a
scoped failure for later reuse. The present paper addresses that narrower
design problem rather than proposing a general account of mathematical
intelligence.

The question addressed here is therefore methodological:

\begin{quote}
\small How can the search for an arithmetic dynamical structure relevant to
the HP programme be reformulated as a broad, falsifiable, auditable, and
cumulatively reusable human--AI construction-and-candidate-search problem?
\end{quote}

The answer developed from the Phase-I record is \emph{candidate engineering}.
For RH, the HP intuition is not treated as a license to search arbitrary
dynamical systems and attach prime data after the fact. It is a human-selected
construction architecture: a prime-symbolic research origin defines a
lineage-constrained candidate space, and each candidate carries a visible
preservation or transformation ledger. Rather than optimize a loosely specified
resemblance to the Riemann zeros, the researcher freezes a candidate's identity
and tests an ordered set of obligations: endogenous arithmetic source,
primitive-orbit and repetition ownership, a dynamical zeta or determinant
belonging to that same object, and only then the stronger analytic and
operator-theoretic requirements. AI can help enumerate, compare, document, and
challenge a broad portfolio of candidates; human researchers remain responsible
for mathematical origin, definitions, proof acceptance, claim authorization,
and external communication.

This paper makes six contributions.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item It puts a three-layer, human-governed framework first: the
  mathematician owns the research origin and scientific authority; a jointly
  drafted, human-approved \texttt{AGENTS.md} makes the search contract
  explicit; and AI returns a bounded evidence packet for review.
  \item It places the updated programme roadmap beside that framework as an
  \emph{obligation architecture}, not a cumulative progress chart or a
  workflow score.
  \item It makes the prime-symbolic origin concrete through a two-lane
  intellectual genealogy: five internal prior-work threads constrain the
  admissible transformation class, while a prime-side operator result supplies
  an external acceptance benchmark rather than inherited credit.
  \item It inventories the heterogeneous Phase-I portfolio as one free entry
  point and five system-family sessions, separating catalogue scale, bounded
  focal records, reusable local outputs, and unresolved bridges.
  \item It formulates the \sameobject{} invariant and a candidate card that
  make hidden ownership changes, post-hoc prime attachment, and premature
  operator claims visible early.
  \item It treats the versioned Wiki as a provenance and navigation resource
  designed to preserve successful local work, controls, scoped failures, and
  reuse boundaries for future candidate engineering.
\end{enumerate}

The roles of these contributions are distinct. The primary contribution is a
prospective candidate-engineering framework; the Phase-I material is a
source-bound retrospective design case from which its constraints are
distilled; and the Wiki is a navigation-and-provenance resource designed to
support later assessment and possible reuse. The paper does not claim that
Phase I was a controlled evaluation of the framework or that its six directions
followed one historical AI protocol.

The detailed summary of each direction is placed in \cref{app:record}; the
repository remains the canonical navigation layer for the underlying source
materials \citep{PhaseIRepository}. The present manuscript is a source-bound
methodological case study, supported by a research record rather than an
independent re-proof of those local results.

\section{A human-governed framework for AI-assisted arithmetic-dynamical search}
\label{sec:framework}

\Cref{fig:framework} is the paper's core methodological contribution: a
human-governed framework for bounded AI search, organized in three layers.
\emph{Layer 1} gives the mathematician responsibility for the prime-symbolic
research origin, the scientific question, permitted arithmetic data, and the
admissible search envelope. \emph{Layer 2} turns those choices into a jointly
drafted, human-approved \texttt{AGENTS.md} contract. \emph{Layer 3} requires
bounded AI execution, an explicit evidence handoff, and mathematician review
before another round can be authorized.

The prime-symbolic origin shown in \cref{fig:framework} is the present
case-study instantiation of Layer 1, not a permanent restriction on the
framework. If a mathematician introduces a different basic mathematical idea,
that change creates a new Layer-1 origin record and requires a new or revised,
human-approved and version-frozen Layer-2 contract before AI-assisted search
can begin. It is not an undocumented broadening of an existing candidate
portfolio.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth,height=.58\textheight,keepaspectratio]{../assets/research_framework.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Three-layer human--AI research framework for
  arithmetic-dynamical search.} \emph{Layer 1}: the mathematician fixes the
  prime-symbolic origin and permissible search envelope. \emph{Layer 2}: a
  jointly drafted, human-approved \texttt{AGENTS.md} fixes constraints, a
  testable route, a breadth-first portfolio with controls and near misses, and
  stopping conditions. \emph{Layer 3}: bounded AI execution, evidence handoff,
  and mathematician review; continuation needs a new authorization or revised
  contract. \texttt{GO}, \texttt{HOLD}, \texttt{FORK}, and \texttt{END} are

  prospective Round-2 framework, not a claim that Phase-I sessions used one
  uniform agent protocol.}
\label{fig:framework}
\end{figure}

This structure has a restrictive admission rule: no generic chaotic or quantum
system may be admitted and decorated with primes later; nor may AI enlarge the
admissible class, replace a missing preservation mechanism, or authorize a
mathematical claim.

The Layer-2 contract has four irreducible parts; Layer 3 operationalizes them
through bounded execution, evidence handoff, and mathematician review. First,
the search-constraints card fixes the admissible class. For this programme, a
main candidate must trace to a prime-symbolic ancestor and name a preservation,
deformation, dimensional lift, or geometric-realization mechanism. It must
also state the permitted system families, branches, dimensions, parameter
sources, and arithmetic inputs before the search starts. The illustrative
one-dimensional or low-dimensional focus in \cref{fig:framework} is a declared
family restriction, not a license to attach prime language to any available
dynamical system. Any later non-autonomous deformation or conservative lift
must preserve, transform, or explicitly refute a named piece of that lineage.

Second, the route-map card contains a multi-stage route that can be decided at
its stated scope. It specifies the evidence required for a gate, the controls
that can falsify a proposed mechanism, and a non-ordered set of possible
workflow dispositions: \texttt{GO} permits the next declared screen;
\texttt{HOLD} returns an unresolved scientific decision to the mathematician;
\texttt{FORK} creates a new object identity and candidate card; and
\texttt{END} archives a bounded result. Every stage must be testable. These
labels cannot be used as substitutes for A0--A4 or Route-B evidence.

Third, the search-strategy card makes breadth first the default. AI can compare
several permitted architectures, including controls and near-miss objects, and
keep source and ownership ledgers synchronized before deeper work begins. That
breadth is disciplined: each architecture must pass the lineage admission test
and cheap same-object checks. The strategy is not a claim that breadth is
universally optimal; it is a practical response to the Phase-I finding that
local successes from distinct objects do not add to one candidate.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
Fourth, the stop-conditions card makes operational boundaries explicit. A
search may stop at a named roadmap gate, a hard contradiction or declared route
closure, a loss of lineage or same-object ownership, a scoped negative result,
or exhaustion of its specified token, compute, or time budget. Budget
exhaustion is an operational stopping condition, not a mathematical negative
result. In Layer 3, AI may perform only contract-permitted candidate screening,
experiments, proof attempts, and audits. At a stop it hands the mathematician a
compact evidence packet containing the candidate ID, lineage ledger, direct
evidence and controls, route disposition, exact stopping trigger, and resource
status. The mathematician then validates scope and mathematics, accepts or
rejects the bounded conclusion, and may issue a new authorization or a revised
contract for a further round. There is no autonomous continuation. A versioned
\texttt{AGENTS.md} therefore supports traceable, reproducibility-oriented
research governance; it is not mathematical evidence and cannot validate a
proof by itself.

The framework is prospective. The prior-work guide records a research
trajectory and explicitly does not claim to have been a historical candidate
selection manual. The framework uses that trajectory to govern future
candidate engineering without retroactively assigning a uniform AI workflow to
the six Phase-I directions \citep{PhaseIRepository}.

\subsection{The companion mathematical obligation roadmap}
\label{sec:roadmap}

\Cref{fig:framework} answers who fixes the search space, who executes bounded
work, and when scientific authority returns to the mathematician. The roadmap
in \cref{fig:roadmap} answers a different question: which mathematical
obligations an admitted candidate must earn. Neither diagram substitutes for
the other.

  release-local mirror of the author-supplied roadmap (SHA-256

  \label{fig:roadmap}
\end{figure}

Route A begins with A0, an endogenous arithmetic source. A1 requires
primitive, reproducible orbit ownership and a stated repetition convention.
A2 asks for a stable dynamical zeta or Fredholm determinant whose operator,
domain, normalization, and orbit expansion belong to that same candidate. A3
and A4 add the relevant global analytic/Weil-compatible structure and a
natural classical-to-quantum lift. Route B is stricter still: it concerns a
fixed operator and domain (B1), self-adjointness (B2), the target spectral type
(B3), an exact prime-power/Weil-compatible trace identity (B4), and a global
completed-\(\Xi\) determinant or divisor equality (B5). It cannot repair a
weak Route-A object.

The roadmap's key word is \emph{same}. Under the \sameobject{} screening
criterion adopted here, a symbolic clock, a physical closed orbit, a
determinant borrowed from a different transfer system, and a separately
constructed operator do not count as components of one candidate unless common
ownership, or a precisely established identification or intertwining relation,
is supplied:

\begin{center}
\fcolorbox{analyticpurple}{softpurple}{\parbox{.84\linewidth}{\centering
\small\textbf{Same-object invariant}\\[-.15em]
Arithmetic source, clock/repetition, orbits, determinant, and operator
must have a common owner or an explicitly established identification.}}
\end{center}

Thus \texttt{GO}, \texttt{HOLD}, \texttt{FORK}, and \texttt{END} are workflow
dispositions under the human-approved contract; A0--A4 and B1--B5 are
candidate-specific mathematical obligations. The remainder of the paper
instantiates this distinction through the three layers of
\cref{fig:framework}.

\section{From Hilbert--P\'olya intuition to an obligation architecture}
\label{sec:method}

The HP programme is often motivated by the expectation that a suitable spectral
or trace structure could illuminate the nontrivial zeros of the zeta function.
That motivation has inspired important connections among spectral asymptotics,
trace formulae, and arithmetic \citep{BerryKeating1999,Connes1999}. Dynamical
zeta functions also have a well-established role in the study of expanding
maps and Anosov flows \citep{Ruelle1976}. None of these conceptual links
licenses a candidate-specific conclusion by itself. The central practical
question is how one turns that broad intuition into an origin-constrained,
contract-governed, and source-bound research programme. The following
subsections follow the three layers of \cref{fig:framework}: mathematical
origin, executable research contract, and historical evidence/handoff.

\subsection{Layer 1: mathematical origin and an admissible transformation class}
\label{sec:lineage}

meaningful. In this case study, the starting point is not an arbitrary chaotic
or quantum model; it is the published P1 article on prime/composite
observables, symbolic coding, and low-dimensional deterministic chaos
\citep{Wang2026PrimeChaos}. P2--P5 are subsequent project source records that,
together with P1, form the conceptual genealogy constraining admissible
deformations and lifts \citep{PhaseIRepository}. The sixth paper is
intentionally shown separately: it is a prime-side arithmetic/operator
benchmark, not a mathematical descendant of the first five.

For any candidate, the card must identify its ancestral node or nodes, the
transformation class being used, and the exact mechanism that is preserved,
deformed, lifted, geometrically realized, or refuted. The mechanism may be a
specified prime-symbolic observable, admissibility rule, return structure,
coding, clock, or rigorously defined deformation thereof. If the relation is
unproved, it is recorded as \texttt{OPEN}; it cannot be replaced by a narrative
similarity. Thus a generic area-preserving map, fashionable arithmetic space,
trace formula, or operator becomes an external control unless it carries this
ledger. A non-autonomous model must likewise state an evolution law rather than
merely fit a time-dependent parameter.

The P6 benchmark has a different role. It keeps prime powers and the
explicit-formula side visible as a scoped acceptance reference, but it supplies
no transfer of prime tables, von Mangoldt weights, or zero data into a distinct
dynamical object. This distinction is designed to make the common reversal of
choosing a generic system first and attaching arithmetic language only after
the fact visible at candidate admission.

\subsection{Layer 2: a versioned research contract}
\label{sec:contract}

Layer 2 makes Layer 1 executable without allowing the executable plan to
silently redefine the mathematics. A jointly drafted, human-approved
\texttt{AGENTS.md}, paired with a plan and candidate cards, records four
contract cards before bounded work begins. It is a research-governance
artifact: it specifies what the AI may screen and report, but never certifies a
mathematical conclusion.

For a round to be replayable as a governance record, the operative contract
must be fixed by a repository commit or tag and linked to its \texttt{plan.md},
candidate card, declared inputs, and evidence ledger. A mutable working
\texttt{AGENTS.md} is useful navigation and planning material, but it is not a
frozen evidence package by itself.

For this project, the contract requires that each candidate state one owner for
phase space, map or flow, roof or clock, primitive-orbit/repetition convention,
arithmetic mechanism, coding, transfer/determinant object and domain, controls,
and any later quantum operator. A loss of that identity is a \texttt{FORK} or
\texttt{END}, not an invitation to borrow a missing component. The contract
also makes the default portfolio breadth-first after the lineage screen; this
is a strategy proposal motivated by the case study, not a retrospective claim
that Phase I was a controlled breadth-first experiment.

The practical value of AI in this setting is not an ungrounded ``guess the
answer'' mode. It is most credible where the representation and rejection
condition are exposed: candidate enumeration, source retrieval, object-ledger
checking, adversarial control generation, proof-dependency review, and
reproducible documentation. This perspective is close to the
generator--evaluator separation demonstrated in program-search work
\citep{RomeraParedes2024}, but the evaluator for a mathematical research
candidate is partly formal and partly human. It includes source identity,
mathematical scope, and whether a purported bridge preserves the object.

Human authority remains indispensable. A fluent synthesis can make
nonidentical constructions look continuous; an automated retrieval system can
surface a nearby theorem under a misleading normalization; a numerical fit can
conceal target leakage. The human researcher must decide whether definitions
are genuinely the same, whether a proof is valid, which claims have been
earned, and whether a record may be released.

\subsection{Layer 3: bounded execution, evidence handoff, and source-bound record}
\label{sec:phase1-record}

\subsubsection{Case-study and evidence-synthesis protocol}

This paper is a source-bound case-study synthesis, not a systematic review, a
benchmark of research strategies, or an experimental comparison of AI systems.
Its unit of analysis is a top-level research direction, rather than an
individual file, PDF, build, or package identity. The case set is fixed by the
Phase-I repository organization at the historical evidence snapshot: one early
free-exploration entry and five subsequently organized system-family sessions.
It is therefore a complete six-direction portfolio at that stated scope, not a
random or statistically representative sample of arithmetic dynamics.

The synthesis uses three deliberately non-interchangeable information layers.
First, direction-level mathematical statements are bounded to the historical
source-corpus snapshot \texttt{419ee36c1e3...}. Second, navigation and
catalogue-size statements use the later P1 Wiki conversion snapshot
\texttt{afe747a194b9...}. The full identifiers are recorded in
\cref{fig:landscape,app:provenance}. Third, external references provide
motivation and context only. For every portfolio row, the paper records the
focal object or batch, a bounded reusable output, and a named discriminating
boundary, then links the reader to the controlling direction pages in
\cref{app:record}. File counts are never used to infer theorem strength,
novelty, resource use, or Route progress.

The principal coding rule is non-compensation. A direction is described as
having an integrated A0+A1+A2 chain only if its own source-bound record
documents all three obligations for one natural candidate with one object
identity. A local result, an internal process state, an external archive, or a
result in another direction cannot supply a missing obligation. Conversely,
the absence of such a documented chain in this synthesis is not an
impossibility claim about the whole family. This rule permits negative or
inapplicable outcomes to remain useful without turning them into a common
score.

\subsubsection{Layer 3 portfolio: six-track source-bound evidence}
\label{sec:results}

The six lines produced different kinds of useful output. They cannot be ranked
as six positions along one common route, because their objects, evaluation

The Phase-I conversion count of 1,126 is deliberately an inventory fact, not a

Phase I was organized as one exploratory entry point and five family-specific
sessions, rather than as a prospectively balanced breadth-first candidate
portfolio. It consequently does not benchmark breadth against depth, measure
time-to-progress, or establish that one search policy is globally optimal. Its
defensible portfolio-level lesson is narrower: repeated work around early
arithmetic-origin and ownership boundaries did not yield a documented
same-object A0+A1+A2 chain. That observation motivates, but does not validate,
the breadth-first policy in the prospective Layer-2 contract.

The portfolio supports two conclusions. First, Phase I did \emph{not} identify
an already formed candidate that can simply be extended to a proof. More
precisely, within the source-bound record, no top-level direction is documented
as carrying an integrated A0+A1+A2 chain in one natural candidate. This is a
statement about the record and its named objects, not an impossibility theorem
about every system in the corresponding family.

Second, Phase I did not amount to empty failure. It preserved reusable
structures: a same-object analytic determinant chain in the Logistic line,
periodic classification and geometric interfaces in the H\'{e}non and
Symplectic lines, exact finite-system controls in the Symbolic line, and
continuous-time ownership tests in the Flow line. The methodological result
is the identification of bridges that cannot be filled by prose alone:
arithmetic source, clock/repetition convention, analytic owner, and operator
owner must be frozen together.

\section{From family scan to candidate engineering}
\label{sec:strategy}

The principal Phase-I recommendation is not that breadth is universally
superior to depth. The record is neither an optimization benchmark nor a
controlled comparison of research strategies. Rather, repeated early
boundaries, combined with the non-additivity of evidence across objects,
motivate a change in emphasis: explore a broader portfolio of \emph{candidate
architectures}, but subject every architecture to low-cost, early,
same-object tests before investing deeply in an A0/A1-limited system.

Here, broader does not mean a random sweep over generic dynamical forms. It
means a portfolio of lineage-compatible descendants of the prime-symbolic
starting point, each with a documented deformation, dimensional lift, geometric
realization, or stated preservation failure. A system that lacks that entry
condition remains useful as an external positive or negative control, but it
does not enter the main candidate portfolio.

ingredients to test inside a new object, not inherited Route credit; and
Symbolic coding is admissible only when it arises from the same candidate's

The same card is designed to make a common historical reversal detectable
early: selecting a generic high-dimensional or conservative system first, then
decorating it with primes.

for a dimensional or geometric lift, the card must name a projection,
semiconjugacy, invariant, return relation, or explicitly open preservation
claim. The words high-dimensional, area-preserving, or symplectic are not by
themselves a prime-symbolic mechanism.

\subsection{Why the next search should be architecturally broader}

The roadmap's Round-2 structural-synthesis direction gives a concrete shape to
the proposal: seek a single candidate in which arithmetic structure, symbolic
coding, a geometric carrier, closed-orbit/trace data, and any later quantum
realization are derived from one construction. This does \emph{not} mean
splicing together a symbolic model, a symplectic map, a physical flow, and an
operator with unrelated owners. It means designing a new object so that the
relations can be checked rather than narrated.

There is an important distinction between wider search and lower standards.
Candidate diversity is useful because Phase I exposed several orthogonal
failure modes. The screening standard becomes stricter, not looser: every new
architecture must first survive A0, then A1, then A2 with the same identity.
An early scoped negative result is a successful search output when it rules out
a named construction under named conventions; it can reduce the risk that the
next round repeats the same ownership transfer in new vocabulary.

\section{Discussion, limitations, and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The distinctive contribution of this work is methodological rather than a
claim of mathematical closure. Its first contribution is the framework in
\cref{fig:framework}: the mathematician fixes a prime-symbolic research origin
and scientific authority; a jointly drafted, human-approved \texttt{AGENTS.md}
contract makes the permissible search, decision labels, breadth-first strategy,
and stopping conditions explicit; and AI returns a source-bound handoff rather
than extending the project autonomously. The obligation roadmap then treats the
HP programme as non-compensatory. A collection of impressive local features does
not add up to a candidate. Negative controls, stops, and inapplicability become
informative because they identify exactly where a bridge is absent. The
versioned repository preserves provenance, source identity, and the distinction
between historical research materials and this later synthesis.

\subsection{Extensibility beyond the present case study}

\paragraph{Extending a fixed mathematical origin across dynamical forms.}
The framework does not prescribe one privileged map, flow, or dimension.
Starting from the prime-symbolic origin in \cref{fig:lineage}, a future
portfolio may examine a non-autonomous deformation, a dimensional or
conservative lift, a symplectic/geometric realization, a suspension flow, or a
quantum-dynamical counterpart. The extension is admissible only when its card
names what is preserved, transformed, lifted, or shown to fail. This makes
system-family diversity a disciplined expansion of a stated mathematical
origin, rather than a post-hoc search for a system to which primes can be
attached.

\paragraph{Extending to a new human-defined basic idea.}
The framework is also not permanently tied to prime-symbolic dynamics. A
mathematician may initiate a different basic mathematical idea based on
judgement, conceptual reframing, intuition, imagination, creative structural
insight, or a new observation;
Layer 1 then records the new origin, permitted data, and admissible
transformations before AI-assisted search begins. The assignment of problem
selection, conceptual reframing, and scientific authorization to the
mathematician is a governance choice: the framework does not assume a reliable
autonomous procedure for recognizing a mathematically fertile new starting
idea. It is not an empirical claim that AI systems lack every form of
mathematical creativity. Its purpose is to make changes of research origin
explicit, reviewable, and attributable rather than implicit in a generated
narrative.

\paragraph{Extending through cumulative knowledge infrastructure.}
The P1 Wiki is designed to retain a candidate's source lineage, positive local
results, external and negative controls, scoped failures, stopping conditions,
and links back to canonical evidence. Such records make a failed construction
useful to a later agent or mathematician without turning it into transferable
Route credit. Preserving the relation among inputs, versions, procedures, and
outputs follows general reproducibility guidance \citep{Sandve2013}; the
findability and reusability aspiration is aligned with the FAIR principles
\citep{Wilkinson2016}. The Wiki itself is a navigation and
provenance layer, not a proof checker, archival guarantee, or assertion that
all included materials satisfy FAIR criteria. Its precise reuse hierarchy and
reader path are given in \cref{app:architecture}.

Several limitations follow. The paper is not an independent proof audit of the
component results; its detailed claims remain constrained by their named source
records. It is not a benchmark comparing AI systems or research strategies. It
does not establish that a broader portfolio is optimal. Rather, the Phase-I
record motivates a broader, gate-aware design recommendation in place of
unbounded deepening of previously limited objects. Finally, it is not a proof
of RH, a complete HP realization, an exact prime-power trace formula, a
completed-\(\Xi\) determinant identity, or a spectral identification of
Riemann zeros.

Within those limits, Phase I supplies a useful research result: it turns
failure modes into design constraints. The next step is not to pick a winner
from six incomparable lines. It is to construct and screen new \sameobject{}
candidate architectures that begin either from a documented prime-symbolic
lineage or from a newly approved Layer-1 mathematical origin, then state an
explicit preservation/deformation/lift mechanism, arithmetic origin,
clock/repetition ownership, determinant ownership, controls, and a route
decision. If a future candidate survives those constraints, its positive claims
should be easier to audit. If it fails, the recorded failure may still inform
the next search.

\subsection{Early free exploration: \texttt{zeta\_mvp0}}

The early \texttt{zeta\_mvp0} programme preserves local counting structures,
a fixed self-adjoint compact-resolvent operator convention, and a certified
local relative-trace construction. These are meaningful local operator and
trace results. Its global claim ledger nevertheless leaves the endogenous
prime-time mechanism open: no licensed internal rule supplies the relevant
\(r\log p\)-type timing with von Mangoldt weights. The corresponding
zero-spectral relation and RH remain unauthorized. Thus an internal local
label must not be translated into P1 Route-A credit merely because the
vocabulary resembles a later route. The line also includes an imported RH
archive whose source-preserving status is itself instructive: nearby material
does not become native evidence merely by being stored in the same directory
\citep{PhaseIRepository}.



\subsection{Session 1: Logistic Dynamics}

The Logistic line supplies the clearest preserved example of a \sameobject{}
analytic chain. At a frozen exact algebraic parameter, its record connects
first-return structure, an intrinsic polar roof, complex inverse branches,
boundary/partition bookkeeping, a nuclear transfer operator, a Fredholm
determinant, and growth/order information. This is valuable precisely because
the objects and normalizations are retained together. The same record also
draws its boundary: the chain does not provide the Riemann-target arithmetic
source, target-level global closure, or a natural quantization. Root hunting
and Riemann-zero comparison are therefore stopped for that object. The
methodological use of the line is as a seed/control for object ownership, not
as an unfinished RH proof \citep{PhaseIRepository}.

\subsection{Session 2: H\'{e}non Dynamics}

The H\'{e}non record studies restricted families through periodic
classification and related low-/higher-dimensional geometric diagnostics. It
preserves scoped rational/integer, affine-model, and period-spectrum results.
Its Route-A interpretations remain object-specific: arithmetic relevance is
weak; primitive-orbit information may be weak or fail for the particular
object; and later analytic or lift obligations are absent or not testable in
the named scope. Later frozen/admitted contracts were retained without being
misrepresented as completed papers. This makes H\'{e}non a potential Round-2
geometric bridge or Poincar\'{e}-section ancestor for a new construction, not an
inheritance mechanism for a different candidate's analytic or arithmetic
credit \citep{PhaseIRepository}.

\subsection{Session 3: Symplectic Map}

The Symplectic session produced five scoped interfaces and diagnostic tools
around conservative structures, periodic schemes, and qPI-related contexts.
Its formal disposition is \texttt{NOT\_APPLICABLE} for the route schema used
elsewhere. That status is not a scientific failure, and it is not a pass. It
means that no fixed candidate has been supplied to own simultaneously an
endogenous prime-to-orbit mechanism, a logarithmic clock/repetition law, a
target determinant, and a natural operator lift. The session is consequently
valuable as a carrier/diagnostic resource, but it cannot be displayed as
partial gate closure on a shared HP scorecard \citep{PhaseIRepository}.

\subsection{Session 4: Symbolic Dynamics}

The Symbolic session provides exact, finite, autonomous deterministic-system
results with a particularly audit-friendly evidence pattern. Its
exact-five internal-complete and external-hold labels are process states,
not a claim of global analytic continuation or an operator realization. The
important structural boundary is that a finite symbolic system, however clean
its algebra, does not automatically own a geometric carrier, physical clock,
primitive closed-orbit convention, global determinant, or quantum operator.
For Round 2, symbolic structure is therefore most promising when it is derived
from a candidate's geometry rather than attached to a distinct object as an
independent unit-roof model \citep{PhaseIRepository}.

\subsection{Session 5: Flow Systems}

The Flow session turns the ownership issue into an explicit continuous-time
stress test. Across its P24--P28 forms, it records local trace results,
negative controls, and obstructions involving roofs, inverse limits,
factorization, and operator ownership. Its bounded final ledger reports
positive arithmetic A2 = 0/5 and Route-B invocation = 0/5 for these five
forms. The scope is crucial: this is neither a verdict on all flows nor a
seventh top-level Phase-I direction. Under the \sameobject{} criterion used
here, its reusable design lesson is that clock, primitive orbit, repetition
law, determinant, trace regime, and operator require common ownership or an
explicitly established identification, rather than an unsupported combination
of a symbolic clock, physical orbit, borrowed determinant, and separate
operator \citep{PhaseIRepository}.
