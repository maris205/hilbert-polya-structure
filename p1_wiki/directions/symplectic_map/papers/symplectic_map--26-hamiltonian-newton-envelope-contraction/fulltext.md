# Introduction, local related work, and contributions

## Degree growth as an exact polynomial problem

For a polynomial automorphism, ordinary total degree records how the four coordinate polynomials expand under iteration. Its exponential scale is the first dynamical degree, but the exact degree sequence contains more information than the exponential limit alone. Algebraic entropy packages the degree growth of iterates as a complexity invariant for rational dynamics [@BellonViallet1999AlgebraicEntropy]. In broader birational dynamics, dynamical degrees also admit spectral interpretations [@DangFavre2021SpectralInterpretations].

The maps studied here admit an elementary-looking max-plus description: one takes a support function of the exponent set and alternates it with a diagonal power map. That description is initially only an upper bound. Two leading coordinate polynomials can share a whole exposed face, and arbitrary nonzero coefficients allow apparent leading terms to cancel. The central polynomial task is therefore to certify the entire exposed face before using any tropical transport as an equality.

## The three coupled obstacles

Three difficulties must be resolved in a fixed order. First, on a Newton wall several monomials tie, so selecting one maximizer cannot justify a leading form. Second, $F=T\circ S$ and $F^{-1}=S^{-1}\circ T^{-1}$ have opposite phase chronologies: the forward state is visible in the position block, whereas the inverse state is visible in the momentum block. Third, a selector may keep switching even though the projective state converges, so a chamber calculation by itself does not control all tails.

These obstacles are coupled. Exact transport requires cancellation control; projective contraction requires exact transport; and the scalar recurrence statements require both contraction and a separate visibility argument for each direction. The shifted forward--inverse bridge compares the two vector states, but it cannot replace the inverse recurrence proof because a diagonal rescaling need not preserve which coordinate realizes an ordinary maximum.

## Local related-work synthesis

Cluster-generated maps provide a separate setting in which invariant symplectic structures and tropical degree recurrences coexist [@FordyHone2011SymplecticMaps]. For mutation loops, stable tropical sign data can control algebraic entropy [@IshibashiKano2021AlgebraicEntropy]. The literature on polynomial symplectomorphisms supplies family-level structural background for the ambient map class [@JaneczkoJelonek2008PolynomialSymplectomorphisms]. Hamiltonian dynamics may be approximated by compositions of nonlinear shears depending only on position or only on momentum [@BergerTuraev2025GeneratorsHamiltonianMaps]. Affine-triangular automorphisms form an adjacent structured class in which dynamical degrees obey arithmetic restrictions [@BlancVanSanten2022DynamicalDegrees].

These comparisons are contextual. None is used to transfer a theorem or a proof step to the present family. All cancellation, phase-order, contraction, selector, spectral, and recurrence statements below are proved directly.

## Contributions and proof roadmap

The article makes five assumption-bounded contributions.

-   It proves a coefficient-uniform, full-face Hessian certificate, including arbitrary multi-point ties, and converts it into algebraic independence of every exposed gradient pair.

-   It derives cancellation-free forward and inverse half-step carries, visible ordinary degrees, exact $B\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{A}B$ transports, and the shifted vector bridge with its precise scalar boundary.

-   It proves one global contraction constant in logarithmic projective distance, including branch derivatives, endpoint control, finite-envelope uniformity, wall patching, and the scaling conjugacy for the inverse map.

-   It classifies selector tails by the unique fixed ray and obtains an interior quadratic-at-most Perron value or a wall multiplier that is a positive integer.

-   It proves forward and inverse scalar recurrence upper bounds separately and supplies two exact hand-checkable fixtures together with the boundaries of every mechanism.

The dependency chain is consequently rigid: symplectic phase order, full-face noncancellation, exact degree transport, projective contraction, selector classification, spectral arithmetic, and only then scalar recurrences. The theorem is confined to finite collected support in $\mathbf{Z}_{\geq2}^2$, a separated $W$, the ordinary coordinate seed, and characteristic zero. Global claim-level novelty remains unresolved. We make no genericity substitution, no extension to arbitrary Hamiltonian shears, and no higher-dimensional classification.

# Setting, symplecticity, and inverse phases

## Map class, collected support, and ordinary degree

Fix a field $K$ of characteristic zero. Let $$\varnothing\neq E\subset\mathbf{Z}_{\geq2}^2$$ be finite and let $$V(q_1,q_2)=\sum_{(x,y)\in E}c_{x,y}q_1^xq_2^y,
\qquad c_{x,y}\in K^\times.$$ The polynomial is collected: each exponent occurs once, and $E$ is the support of the resulting polynomial. The coefficients are otherwise arbitrary elements of $K^\times$. Let $$W(p_1,p_2)=\alpha p_1^{e+1}+\beta p_2^{f+1},
\qquad \alpha,\beta\in K^\times,\qquad e,f\geq2.$$ On coordinates ordered as $(q_1,q_2,p_1,p_2)$, define $$S(q,p)=(q,p+\nabla V(q)),\qquad
T(q,p)=(q+\nabla W(p),p),\qquad F=T\circ S.$$ All degrees below are ordinary total degrees in the four initial coordinates. Thus each initial coordinate has degree one. For any polynomial automorphism $G$, degree under composition satisfies $$\deg(G^{m+n})\leq\deg(G^m)\deg(G^n).$$ Thus $\log\deg(G^n)$ is subadditive, and Fekete's lemma gives the well-defined first dynamical degree $$\lambda_1(G)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\deg(G^n)^{1/n}
=\inf_{n\geq1}\deg(G^n)^{1/n}.$$

## Symplectic block calculation

Let $$J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_2\\-I_2&0\end{pmatrix}.$$

[\[prop:symplectic\]]{#prop:symplectic label="prop:symplectic"} The maps $S,T,F$, and $F^{-1}$ are polynomial symplectomorphisms. The inverse is $F^{-1}=S^{-1}\circ T^{-1}$, so $T^{-1}$ acts first.

Writing $H_V=\mathop{\mathrm{Hess}}V(q)$ and $H_W=\mathop{\mathrm{Hess}}W(p)$, symmetry of the two Hessians gives $$DS=\begin{pmatrix}I_2&0\\H_V&I_2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
DT=\begin{pmatrix}I_2&H_W\\0&I_2\end{pmatrix}.$$ Direct block multiplication yields $$(DS)^{\mathsf T}JDS=J,\qquad
(DT)^{\mathsf T}JDT=J.$$ Hence $S$ and $T$ preserve the standard symplectic form, and so do their composition and inverses. The displayed inverse order follows from reversing a composition.

## Subtraction inverses and phase chronology

The inverse shears are literal subtractions: $$S^{-1}(q,p)=(q,p-\nabla V(q)),\qquad
T^{-1}(q,p)=(q-\nabla W(p),p).$$ Consequently, $$F^{-1}(q,p)
=S^{-1}\bigl(q-\nabla W(p),p\bigr).$$ Every inverse iterate therefore applies the upper subtraction $T^{-1}$ before the lower subtraction $S^{-1}$. The signs change nonzero scalar factors in leading forms but do not change their degrees; the phase order, by contrast, changes the state transport and cannot be ignored.

## Support function and theorem staging

For $u=(u_1,u_2)^{\mathsf T}\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}^2$, set $$H(u)=\max_{(x,y)\in E}(xu_1+yu_2),\qquad
\mathcal{A}(u)=
\begin{pmatrix}H(u)-u_1\\H(u)-u_2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
B=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(e,f).$$ The map $\mathcal{A}$ is continuous and positively homogeneous. We shall use the ordinary seeds $$u^+_0=v^-_0=\mathbf{1}=(1,1)^{\mathsf T}.$$ The symbols $u_n^+$ and $v_n^-$ will become the visible forward position-degree and inverse momentum-degree states. Before asserting their transports, we must prove that the entire exposed face survives substitution and that no carried coordinate can tie a fresh block. The precise main theorem is therefore stated only after the cancellation certificate in the next section.

# Full exposed-face Hessian and cancellation

## Positive exposed support and full face polynomial

For $u\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}^2$, define $$E_u=\{(x,y)\in E:xu_1+yu_2=H(u)\}$$ and retain the entire exposed-face polynomial $$P_u(X,Y)=\sum_{(x,y)\in E_u}c_{x,y}X^xY^y.$$ On a wall, $E_u$ may contain arbitrarily many support points. All of them remain in $P_u$; choosing one tied monomial would discard exactly the cancellations that must be controlled.

## Unique minimal first coordinate

[\[lem:minimal-x\]]{#lem:minimal-x label="lem:minimal-x"} Every positive exposed face $E_u$ has a unique point with minimal first coordinate.

Finiteness gives existence. If $(x,y),(x,y')\in E_u$, then $$xu_1+yu_2=xu_1+y'u_2.$$ Since $u_2>0$, one has $y=y'$. Thus two distinct face points cannot have the same first coordinate, and the minimizer is unique.

## The isolated face-Hessian coefficient

[\[lem:hessian\]]{#lem:hessian label="lem:hessian"} Let $(x_0,y_0)$ be the point from Lemma [\[lem:minimal-x\]](#lem:minimal-x){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:minimal-x"}. The coefficient of $$X^{2x_0-2}Y^{2y_0-2}$$ in $\det\mathop{\mathrm{Hess}}P_u$ is $$c_{x_0,y_0}^2x_0y_0(1-x_0-y_0).$$ In particular, $\det\mathop{\mathrm{Hess}}P_u\neq0$.

Expand $$\det\mathop{\mathrm{Hess}}P_u=(P_u)_{XX}(P_u)_{YY}-(P_u)_{XY}^2.$$ An ordered pair $(x_a,y_a),(x_b,y_b)\in E_u^2$ can contribute to the target monomial only if $$x_a+x_b-2=2x_0-2.$$ Minimality gives $x_a,x_b\geq x_0$, so equality forces $x_a=x_b=x_0$. Lemma [\[lem:minimal-x\]](#lem:minimal-x){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:minimal-x"} then forces $y_a=y_b=y_0$. Hence the self-pair is the only contributor, even when the exposed face has many points.

Its coefficient in $(P_u)_{XX}(P_u)_{YY}$ is $$c_{x_0,y_0}^2x_0(x_0-1)y_0(y_0-1),$$ and its coefficient in $(P_u)_{XY}^2$ is $$c_{x_0,y_0}^2x_0^2y_0^2.$$ Their difference is $$c_{x_0,y_0}^2x_0y_0
\bigl((x_0-1)(y_0-1)-x_0y_0\bigr)
=c_{x_0,y_0}^2x_0y_0(1-x_0-y_0).$$ The factors $c_{x_0,y_0}\neq0$, $x_0,y_0\geq2$, and characteristic zero separately ensure that this coefficient is nonzero. Only nonvanishing of coefficients has been used.

## Jacobian independence

[\[lem:jacobian\]]{#lem:jacobian label="lem:jacobian"} If $R,S\in K[X,Y]$ and $$\det\frac{\partial(R,S)}{\partial(X,Y)}\neq0,$$ then $R$ and $S$ are algebraically independent over $K$.

If $R,S$ were algebraically dependent, then $K(R,S)$ would have transcendence degree at most one. In characteristic zero the Kähler differentials $dR,dS$ would be linearly dependent over $K(X,Y)$. Therefore $$dR\wedge dS
=\det\frac{\partial(R,S)}{\partial(X,Y)}\,dX\wedge dY$$ would vanish, contradicting the hypothesis.

Taking $R=(P_u)_X$ and $S=(P_u)_Y$, Lemmas [\[lem:hessian\]](#lem:hessian){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:hessian"} and [\[lem:jacobian\]](#lem:jacobian){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:jacobian"} show that the two derivatives of every full exposed face are algebraically independent.

## Injective substitution and pure-power survival

[\[lem:substitution\]]{#lem:substitution label="lem:substitution"} If $L_1,L_2$ are algebraically independent elements of a polynomial ring over $K$, then the substitution $$K[X,Y]\longrightarrow K[L_1,L_2],\qquad X\longmapsto L_1,\quad
Y\longmapsto L_2$$ is injective.

Its kernel consists precisely of the polynomial relations between $L_1$ and $L_2$, and algebraic independence says that this kernel is zero.

[\[lem:powers\]]{#lem:powers label="lem:powers"} If $L_1,L_2$ are algebraically independent and $a,b\geq1$, then $L_1^a,L_2^b$ are algebraically independent. Nonzero scalar multiples and sign changes preserve this property.

The map $K[U,V]\to K[X,Y]$ given by $U\mapsto X^a$, $V\mapsto Y^b$ is injective because distinct monomials have distinct images. Compose it with the injective substitution $X\mapsto L_1$, $Y\mapsto L_2$. Nonzero coordinate scalings are automorphisms of the generated polynomial algebra.

[\[prop:survival\]]{#prop:survival label="prop:survival"} Suppose $Q_1,Q_2$ have ordinary degrees $u_1,u_2>0$ and algebraically independent leading homogeneous forms $L_1,L_2$. Then $$\deg V_{q_1}(Q_1,Q_2)=H(u)-u_1,\qquad
\deg V_{q_2}(Q_1,Q_2)=H(u)-u_2,$$ their leading forms are $$(P_u)_X(L_1,L_2),\qquad (P_u)_Y(L_1,L_2),$$ and this pair is algebraically independent. The conclusion remains true for every multi-point tie, after nonzero scalar changes and signs, and after applying the separated pure powers arising from $W$.

The top ordinary degree of $Q_1^xQ_2^y$ is $xu_1+yu_2$. Injectivity from Lemma [\[lem:substitution\]](#lem:substitution){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:substitution"} prevents distinct monomials in $L_1,L_2$ from canceling, so the top part of $V(Q_1,Q_2)$ is the whole $P_u(L_1,L_2)$. Differentiating before substitution gives the displayed degrees and leading forms. If a polynomial relation held between those two forms, injectivity would pull it back to a relation between $(P_u)_X$ and $(P_u)_Y$, contrary to Lemmas [\[lem:hessian\]](#lem:hessian){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:hessian"}--[\[lem:jacobian\]](#lem:jacobian){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:jacobian"}. Lemma [\[lem:powers\]](#lem:powers){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:powers"} handles pure powers, nonzero scalars, and subtraction signs. The proof uses the whole tied face and is uniform in all permitted coefficients.

## Dependency-ordered Main Theorem

[\[thm:main\]]{#thm:main label="thm:main"} Let $K$ have characteristic zero. Let $\varnothing\neq E\subset\mathbf{Z}_{\geq2}^2$ be finite and collected, let every $c_{x,y}$ be an arbitrary element of $K^\times$, and let $$V=\sum_{(x,y)\in E}c_{x,y}q_1^xq_2^y,\qquad
W=\alpha p_1^{e+1}+\beta p_2^{f+1}$$ with $\alpha\beta\neq0$ and $e,f\geq2$. For $F=T\circ S$ and ordinary total degree from the coordinate seed $\mathbf{1}$, the following statements hold in the listed dependency order.

1.  The shears and their composition are symplectic, and $F^{-1}=S^{-1}\circ T^{-1}$ acts through $T^{-1}$ first.

2.  Every positive exposed face has nonzero Hessian determinant; its two gradient forms are algebraically independent, and injective substitution plus separated powers makes all forward and inverse leading pairs survive without a genericity assumption.

3.  With $B=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(e,f)$, the exact visible states satisfy $$u^+_{n+1}=B\mathcal{A}(u^+_n),\qquad
    v^-_{n+1}=\mathcal{A}(Bv^-_n),\qquad u^+_0=v^-_0=\mathbf{1},$$ and $$\deg(F^n)=\left\lVert u^+_n\right\rVert_{\infty},\qquad
    \deg(F^{-n})=\left\lVert v^-_n\right\rVert_{\infty}.$$

4.  For $c_\star=H(\mathbf{1})-1$, $$u^+_{n+1}=c_\star Bv^-_n\qquad(n\geq0).$$ This shifted vector identity yields a constant comparison and equality of forward and inverse exponential rates, but neither termwise equality of the scalar degree sequences nor an inverse scalar recurrence.

5.  The forward ratio map $$\phi(r)=\kappa\frac{\Phi(r)-r}{\Phi(r)-1},\qquad
    \Phi(r)=\max_{(x,y)\in E}(xr+y),\qquad\kappa=e/f,$$ is a global strict contraction in logarithmic distance. The inverse ratio map is scaling-conjugate to it and selects chambers at $\kappa s$.

6.  There is a unique positive fixed ray. If it is interior to a Newton chamber, every selector is eventually stationary. If it is on a wall, the fixed trajectory uses the full tied face, while every strict trajectory eventually alternates the adjacent labels and its ratios converge to the wall. Multiple ties are governed by the two extreme adjacent exponents off the wall and by the full face on it. There is no nontrivial numerical periodic orbit.

7.  In the interior case the common exponential rate is the Perron root of a positive integral $2\times2$ chamber matrix and has algebraic degree at most two. In the wall case the adjacent matrices share a primitive positive integral ray with a positive integer multiplier, which is the per-step rate. Thus $$[\mathbf{Q}(\lambda_1(F)):\mathbf{Q}]\leq2.$$

8.  Forward and inverse scalar degree tails are proved separately. Interior tails have recurrence order at most two, strict wall tails have interleaved recurrence order at most four, and fixed-ray cases are geometric. These are upper bounds only.

The remaining sections prove the conclusions after item two. In particular, no exact degree transport has been used in establishing the face certificate.

# Exact forward/inverse transports and bridge

## Weighted gradient transform

Let $Q_1,Q_2$ have leading forms $L_1,L_2$ and degree vector $u$. Proposition [\[prop:survival\]](#prop:survival){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:survival"} identifies the full top forms of $\nabla V(Q_1,Q_2)$ and gives the exact transform $$u\longmapsto\mathcal{A}(u).$$ Because the statement is made after differentiating $V$ and then substituting, its two entries are $H(u)-u_1$ and $H(u)-u_2$, not two independently chosen maxima. Their algebraic independence is what allows the next separated power phase to remain injective.

## Lower carry inequalities

[\[lem:carries\]]{#lem:carries label="lem:carries"} For every $u\in\mathbf{R}_{>0}^2$, $$H(u)-u_1\geq u_1+2u_2>\max(u_1,u_2),$$ $$H(u)-u_2\geq2u_1+u_2>\max(u_1,u_2).$$ If $A=\mathcal{A}(u)$, then $$2A_1-A_2\geq3u_2>0,\qquad
2A_2-A_1\geq3u_1>0.$$ Consequently, both components of $BA$ strictly dominate both components of $A$ and every component of $u$.

Every support point has $x,y\geq2$, hence $$H(u)\geq2u_1+2u_2.$$ Subtracting $u_1$ or $u_2$ proves the first two inequalities.

## Cross-coordinate upper carry

Writing $A_1=H(u)-u_1$, $A_2=H(u)-u_2$, direct subtraction gives $$2A_1-A_2=H(u)-2u_1+u_2\geq3u_2>0,$$ $$2A_2-A_1=H(u)+u_1-2u_2\geq3u_1>0.$$ Thus $2A_1>A_2$ and $2A_2>A_1$. Since $e,f\geq2$, $$eA_1\geq2A_1>\max(A_1,A_2),\qquad
fA_2\geq2A_2>\max(A_1,A_2).$$ The lower carries already make each $A_i$ exceed every component of $u$, completing the claim.

The cross-coordinate step is essential: multiplying each component by at least two does not, by itself, compare a small component with the other, possibly larger component.

## Forward half-step induction

At full forward time $n$, let $u_n^+$ be the position-degree vector. After the next $S$-phase, let $w_{n+1}^+$ be the fresh momentum-degree vector.

[\[prop:forward\]]{#prop:forward label="prop:forward"} For every $n\geq0$, the forward leading position pair is algebraically independent and $$w^+_{n+1}=\mathcal{A}(u^+_n),\qquad
u^+_{n+1}=Bw^+_{n+1}=B\mathcal{A}(u^+_n).$$ For every $n\geq1$, after a full iterate, every position degree exceeds every momentum degree. For every $n\geq0$, $$\deg(F^n)=\left\lVert u^+_n\right\rVert_{\infty}.$$

At $n=0$, the pair $(q_1,q_2)$ is algebraically independent and both coordinate blocks have degree one. Applying $S$ produces $p+\nabla V(q)$. The lower carries make both gradient degrees exceed one, so the old momentum terms cannot alter the top forms. Proposition [\[prop:survival\]](#prop:survival){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:survival"} gives an algebraically independent fresh pair and $w_1^+=\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{1})$.

Applying $T$ adds the separated pair $$\bigl(\alpha(e+1)p_1^e,\ \beta(f+1)p_2^f\bigr).$$ Lemma [\[lem:powers\]](#lem:powers){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:powers"} preserves algebraic independence, while Lemma [\[lem:carries\]](#lem:carries){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:carries"} makes both new position degrees dominate both fresh momentum degrees and the old position degrees. Thus $u_1^+=Bw_1^+$, and the position block is visible.

For the induction step, assume the position leading pair at time $n$ is independent and dominates the carried momentum block. The next $S$-phase has exact degree vector $\mathcal{A}(u_n^+)$; its two components exceed every old position degree, hence every old momentum degree. The next $T$-phase has vector $B\mathcal{A}(u_n^+)$, whose two components exceed both components of $\mathcal{A}(u_n^+)$ by the cross carries. Algebraic independence survives both phases. This proves the transport and makes the position maximum the ordinary degree after every full iterate.

## Inverse half-step induction

At full inverse time $n$, let $v_n^-$ be the momentum-degree vector. The next phase is $T^{-1}$, and its fresh position vector is denoted $z_{n+1}^-$.

[\[prop:inverse\]]{#prop:inverse label="prop:inverse"} For every $n\geq0$, the inverse leading momentum pair is algebraically independent and $$z^-_{n+1}=Bv^-_n,\qquad
v^-_{n+1}=\mathcal{A}(z^-_{n+1})=\mathcal{A}(Bv^-_n).$$ For every $n\geq1$, after a full inverse iterate, every momentum degree exceeds every position degree. For every $n\geq0$, $$\deg(F^{-n})=\left\lVert v^-_n\right\rVert_{\infty}.$$

At $n=0$, $(p_1,p_2)$ is algebraically independent. The first phase is $T^{-1}$, so subtraction of the two separated pure powers produces $$z^-_1=B\mathbf{1}.$$ The factors and signs are nonzero, and Lemma [\[lem:powers\]](#lem:powers){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:powers"} preserves independence. Since $e,f\geq2$, these fresh terms dominate the initial position coordinates. The following $S^{-1}$-phase subtracts $\nabla V$ evaluated at that fresh position pair. Proposition [\[prop:survival\]](#prop:survival){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:survival"} and the lower carries give $$v^-_1=\mathcal{A}(B\mathbf{1}),$$ whose two components exceed both components of $z^-_1$ and therefore the old momentum degrees. The final momentum block is visible.

Now assume the momentum block at time $n$ is independent and each of its degrees exceeds every current position degree. The next $T^{-1}$-phase gives $Bv^-_n$. Each old position degree is below each component of $v_n^-$, while each new component is at least twice its corresponding positive momentum degree; hence the fresh position block dominates the carried position block. The following $S^{-1}$-phase gives $\mathcal{A}(Bv^-_n)$, and each of its entries dominates both entries of $Bv^-_n$. In particular, it dominates the old momentum block as well. Full-face independence and harmless subtraction signs complete the induction.

## Exact vector states and total degrees

Propositions [\[prop:forward\]](#prop:forward){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:forward"} and [\[prop:inverse\]](#prop:inverse){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:inverse"} establish the oppositely ordered transports $$u^+_{n+1}=B\mathcal{A}(u^+_n),\qquad
v^-_{n+1}=\mathcal{A}(Bv^-_n).$$ The order $B\mathcal{A}$ belongs to the forward position state, while $\mathcal{A}B$ belongs to the inverse momentum state. Their visible scalar degrees are $$d_n^+=\deg(F^n)=\max(u^+_{n,1},u^+_{n,2}),$$ $$d_n^-=\deg(F^{-n})=\max(v^-_{n,1},v^-_{n,2}).$$ These are ordinary degrees of the full four-coordinate maps, not merely degrees of selected intermediate pairs.

## Shifted bridge induction

[\[prop:bridge\]]{#prop:bridge label="prop:bridge"} Let $c_\star=H(\mathbf{1})-1$. Then $$u^+_{n+1}=c_\star Bv^-_n\qquad(n\geq0).$$ This is a shifted vector identity for the ordinary seed. It is not an equality between $d_{n+1}^+$ and $d_n^-$, and it does not prove an inverse scalar recurrence.

Since both entries of $\mathbf{1}$ equal one, $$\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{1})=
\begin{pmatrix}H(\mathbf{1})-1\\H(\mathbf{1})-1\end{pmatrix}
=c_\star\mathbf{1}.$$ Thus the base case is $$u^+_1=B\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{1})=c_\star B\mathbf{1}=c_\star Bv^-_0.$$ If $u^+_{n+1}=c_\star Bv^-_n$, positive homogeneity of $\mathcal{A}$ gives $$\begin{aligned}
u^+_{n+2}
&=B\mathcal{A}(u^+_{n+1})\\
&=B\mathcal{A}(c_\star Bv^-_n)\\
&=c_\star B\mathcal{A}(Bv^-_n)\\
&=c_\star Bv^-_{n+1}.
\end{aligned}$$ Induction proves the identity.

## Norm comparison and exact boundary

[\[cor:rates\]]{#cor:rates label="cor:rates"} For every $n\geq0$, $$c_\star\min(e,f)d_n^-
\leq d_{n+1}^+
\leq c_\star\max(e,f)d_n^-.$$ Consequently the forward and inverse exponential degree-growth rates exist and satisfy $$\lambda_1(F)=\lambda_1(F^{-1}).$$

For every positive $v$, $$\min(e,f)\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert Bv\right\rVert_{\infty}
\leq\max(e,f)\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\infty}.$$ Combine this inequality with Proposition [\[prop:bridge\]](#prop:bridge){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:bridge"} and the two visibility formulas. The two limits exist by the degree-submultiplicativity argument in Subsection 2.1. Taking roots, fixed positive constants and a one-step index shift do not alter those limits, which proves the equality.

The comparison is constant-factor only. Since $\left\lVert Bv\right\rVert_{\infty}$ need not be a constant multiple of $\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\infty}$, the bridge gives neither termwise scalar equality nor a recurrence for $d_n^-$. Section 7 derives the two scalar directions independently.

# Uniform logarithmic contraction

## Projective support function and ratio map

Represent a positive ray by $u=(r,1)^{\mathsf T}$ and define $$\Phi(r)=H(r,1)=\max_{(x,y)\in E}(xr+y),\qquad \kappa=e/f.$$ Then $$\mathcal{A}(r,1)=
\begin{pmatrix}\Phi(r)-r\\\Phi(r)-1\end{pmatrix},$$ so the forward projective map is $$\phi(r)=\kappa\frac{\Phi(r)-r}{\Phi(r)-1}.$$ Both numerator and denominator are positive because $\Phi(r)-r\geq r+2$ and $\Phi(r)-1\geq2r+1$.

[\[prop:projective\]]{#prop:projective label="prop:projective"} The ratio of the forward state evolves by $\phi$. If $s=v_1/v_2$ is an inverse momentum ratio, then $$\psi(s)=\frac{\Phi(\kappa s)-\kappa s}
{\Phi(\kappa s)-1}$$ and $L(s)=\kappa s$ satisfies $L\circ\psi=\phi\circ L$.

## Chamber derivative and monotonicity

On a chamber selected by $\xi=(x,y)$, $$\phi_\xi(r)=\kappa
\frac{(x-1)r+y}{xr+y-1}.$$ Differentiation gives $$\phi_\xi'(r)
=-\kappa\frac{x+y-1}{(xr+y-1)^2}<0.$$ Thus every branch is strictly decreasing. The envelope $\Phi$ is continuous, and branch formulas agree at every wall, so $\phi$ is continuous. If an interval crosses walls, partitioning it into chambers shows that $\phi$ is strictly decreasing on all of $(0,\infty)$.

## Logarithmic derivative and positive gap

Set $$t=\log r,\qquad h(t)=\log\phi(e^t).$$

[\[lem:logderivative\]]{#lem:logderivative label="lem:logderivative"} On the branch $\xi=(x,y)$, $$|h'(t)|=\eta_{x,y}(r)
=\frac{r(x+y-1)}
{((x-1)r+y)(xr+y-1)},
\qquad r=e^t,$$ and $0<\eta_{x,y}(r)<1$.

The chain rule and the two chamber formulas give the displayed fraction. The denominator minus its numerator expands as $$\begin{aligned}
&((x-1)r+y)(xr+y-1)-r(x+y-1)\\
&\qquad=x(x-1)r^2+2(x-1)(y-1)r+y(y-1).
\end{aligned}$$ Every term is positive for $x,y\geq2$ and $r>0$, which proves the strict bound.

## Endpoint compactification for one branch

For a fixed $(x,y)$, the rational function $\eta_{x,y}$ is continuous on $(0,\infty)$, and $$\lim_{r\to0}\eta_{x,y}(r)=0,\qquad
\lim_{r\to\infty}\eta_{x,y}(r)=0.$$ Extend it by zero at the two endpoints of the compactified positive interval. It attains a maximum $q_{x,y}$, and Lemma [\[lem:logderivative\]](#lem:logderivative){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:logderivative"} implies $$q_{x,y}<1.$$ This endpoint step rules out a supremum equal to one escaping through a noncompact end.

## Finite-support uniformity

Since $E$ is finite, the single number $$q=\max_{(x,y)\in E}q_{x,y}$$ satisfies $q<1$. The estimate applies even on a chamber where its support point is active only on a subinterval, because it was proved on the whole positive ray. Finiteness is used precisely here; a pointwise strict estimate over an infinite branch family would not produce this maximum.

## Wall patching

[\[thm:contraction\]]{#thm:contraction label="thm:contraction"} For all $t,t'\in\mathbf{R}$, $$|h(t')-h(t)|\leq q|t'-t|$$ with the single constant $q<1$ above.

Assume $t<t'$. The compact interval $[t,t']$ meets only finitely many Newton walls. Partition it at those wall points. On each open piece, $h$ is differentiable and $|h'|\leq q$, so the mean-value bound holds on the closure by continuity. Summing over the pieces gives $$|h(t')-h(t)|
\leq\sum_jq\,|I_j|
=q|t'-t|.$$ The case $t'<t$ is symmetric. This interval argument, rather than a derivative assigned at a tied point, patches all walls.

## Inverse map and scaled selector

The forward formula follows directly from $B\mathcal{A}(r,1)$. For the inverse state $v=(s,1)^{\mathsf T}$, the intermediate vector $Bv$ has ratio $\kappa s$. Applying $\mathcal{A}$ gives the stated $\psi$. Finally, $$(L\circ\psi)(s)
=\kappa\frac{\Phi(\kappa s)-\kappa s}{\Phi(\kappa s)-1}
=\phi(\kappa s)
=(\phi\circ L)(s).$$ Thus inverse chamber selection is made at $\kappa s$, never at $s$ unless $\kappa=1$.

## Fixed point and numerical-cycle exclusion

[\[cor:fixed\]]{#cor:fixed label="cor:fixed"} The map $\phi$ has a unique fixed ratio $r_\star>0$, and every positive forward ratio converges to it geometrically in logarithmic distance. The inverse map has the unique fixed ratio $s_\star=r_\star/\kappa$. Neither map has a nontrivial numerical periodic orbit.

The real line in logarithmic coordinate is complete, so Theorem [\[thm:contraction\]](#thm:contraction){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:contraction"} and the contraction mapping theorem give a unique fixed point $t_\star$ and convergence to it. A periodic orbit of length $m\geq2$ would be a nonfixed pair of points fixed by the $q^m$-contraction $h^m$, which is impossible. The inverse conclusions follow through the scaling conjugacy.

The proof uses finite support, a one-dimensional projective chart, and diagonal $B$. It gives no infinite-support result, no mixed-$W$ result, and no analogue in projective dimension at least two.

# Selector and spectral rigidity

## Interior fixed ray

Newton walls are the finitely many positive ratios at which at least two affine functions $xr+y$ tie for the maximum. Their complement consists of open chambers.

[\[prop:selectors\]]{#prop:selectors label="prop:selectors"} If $r_\star$ is interior to a chamber, every positive orbit is eventually confined to that chamber. If $r_\star$ is a wall ratio, the fixed orbit remains on the full tied face, while every other orbit eventually alternates the two adjacent open chambers, converges to $r_\star$, and never lands on it after a positive delay. At a multiple tie, the extreme adjacent exponents control the two open sides and the entire tied face controls the fixed orbit.

In logarithmic coordinate, an interior $t_\star$ has positive distance from the finite wall set. Convergence places every orbit eventually in a smaller wall-free neighborhood, proving stationarity. The wall case is proved in the next subsection.

## Wall fixed and strict trajectories

The trajectory starting at $t_\star$ is fixed. Since $h$ is strictly decreasing, $$t<t_\star\Longrightarrow h(t)>h(t_\star)=t_\star,$$ $$t>t_\star\Longrightarrow h(t)<t_\star.$$ Every strict trajectory therefore changes sides at every step. Convergence and finiteness of the wall set imply that, after a finite transient, only the two chambers adjacent to $t_\star$ occur. If $h(t)=t_\star=h(t_\star)$, strict monotonicity forces $t=t_\star$; there is no delayed landing.

When several exponents tie, the upper envelope immediately to either side is determined by the extreme exposed exponents. At the wall itself, Proposition [\[prop:survival\]](#prop:survival){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:survival"} retains every tied term in $P_u$. Thus arbitrary multiple ties do not alter the classification.

## Selector alternation is not a numerical two-cycle

[\[prop:no-cycle\]]{#prop:no-cycle label="prop:no-cycle"} On a strict wall tail, adjacent chamber labels alternate, but the successive numerical ratios are distinct and converge to $r_\star$. They do not form a nontrivial two-cycle.

Side swapping supplies the alternating labels. The contraction estimate gives $$|\log r_n-\log r_\star|\leq q^n
|\log r_0-\log r_\star|.$$ Delayed landing is impossible, so a strict orbit has distinct ratios on the two sides until their common limit. Corollary [\[cor:fixed\]](#cor:fixed){reference-type="ref" reference="cor:fixed"} rules out every nontrivial numerical period.

## Interior selector matrix and spectrum

For $\xi=(x,y)$, define $$A_\xi=
\begin{pmatrix}x-1&y\\x&y-1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
C_\xi=BA_\xi=
\begin{pmatrix}e(x-1)&ey\\fx&f(y-1)\end{pmatrix}.$$

[\[prop:interior-spectrum\]]{#prop:interior-spectrum label="prop:interior-spectrum"} If $r_\star$ is in the chamber selected by $\xi$, then $$\lambda_1(F)=\rho(C_\xi)$$ and this number is a positive algebraic integer of degree at most two.

Proposition [\[prop:selectors\]](#prop:selectors){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:selectors"} gives a threshold after which $u^+_{n+1}=C_\xi u^+_n$. The fixed ray is a positive eigenvector of the positive integral matrix $C_\xi$, hence its eigenvalue is the Perron root. Transient factors do not change the exponential rate. The characteristic polynomial $$t^2-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(C_\xi)t+\det(C_\xi)$$ is monic with integer coefficients, proving the assertion.

## Primitive wall ray and common multiplier

[\[prop:wall-ray\]]{#prop:wall-ray label="prop:wall-ray"} Suppose $r_\star$ is on a Newton wall. Then $r_\star\in\mathbf{Q}_{>0}$. If $\boldsymbol{\omega}=(a,b)^{\mathsf T}$ is the primitive positive integral direction of that ray, every exponent $\xi$ on the tied face satisfies $$C_\xi\boldsymbol{\omega}=\mu\boldsymbol{\omega}$$ with the same $\mu\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}$.

Choose two distinct tied exponents $\xi_-=(x_-,y_-)$ and $\xi_+=(x_+,y_+)$. Their tie gives $$x_-r_\star+y_-=x_+r_\star+y_+.$$ If $x_-=x_+$, this equality would give $y_-=y_+$, contradicting the choice of distinct exponents. Hence $x_-\neq x_+$, and $$r_\star=\frac{y_+-y_-}{x_--x_+}\in\mathbf{Q}_{>0}.$$ The positive rational ray therefore has the stated primitive integral direction $\boldsymbol{\omega}$.

For every tied $\xi=(x,y)$, $$H(\boldsymbol{\omega})=xa+yb$$ and therefore $$A_\xi\boldsymbol{\omega}=
\begin{pmatrix}H(\boldsymbol{\omega})-a\\H(\boldsymbol{\omega})-b\end{pmatrix},$$ independent of $\xi$. Multiplication by $B$ gives the same vector for all tied selectors. Because $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ is the fixed projective ray, this common vector is $\mu\boldsymbol{\omega}$ for a positive rational $\mu$.

It remains to prove integrality. Since $\gcd(a,b)=1$, choose $m,n\in\mathbf{Z}$ with $ma+nb=1$. Both coordinates of $C_\xi\boldsymbol{\omega}$ are integral, and $$\mu=m(\mu a)+n(\mu b)\in\mathbf{Z}.$$ Positivity gives $\mu\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}$. This primitive-ray argument applies to every exponent in a multiple tie.

## Integrality and per-step growth

[\[cor:quadratic\]]{#cor:quadratic label="cor:quadratic"} For every map in the stated family, $$[\mathbf{Q}(\lambda_1(F)):\mathbf{Q}]\leq2.$$ On a fixed wall, $\lambda_1(F)=\mu\in\mathbf{Z}_{>0}$.

The interior case is Proposition [\[prop:interior-spectrum\]](#prop:interior-spectrum){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:interior-spectrum"}. On a strict wall tail, let $C_-,C_+$ be the adjacent matrices in step order. Both send $\boldsymbol{\omega}$ to $\mu\boldsymbol{\omega}$, so $$(C_+C_-)\boldsymbol{\omega}=\mu^2\boldsymbol{\omega}.$$ The positive product has Perron root $\mu^2$, and growth per single iterate is its positive square root $\mu$. If the seed is already on the wall, every tied selector gives the same one-step multiplier directly.

This quadratic cap is confined to the two-dimensional separated family; it is neither a statement about all Hamiltonian shears nor a statement about higher dynamical degrees.

# Forward and inverse scalar recurrences

## State-matrix and visibility notation

Retain $$A_\xi=\begin{pmatrix}x-1&y\\x&y-1\end{pmatrix},
\quad C_\xi=BA_\xi,\quad D_\xi=A_\xi B,$$ and write $$r_n=\frac{u^+_{n,1}}{u^+_{n,2}},\qquad
s_n=\frac{v^-_{n,1}}{v^-_{n,2}}.$$ The fixed ratios satisfy $s_\star=r_\star/\kappa$. Forward selectors are evaluated at $r_n$; inverse selectors are evaluated at $\kappa s_n$, the ratio of $Bv_n^-$. The visible degrees remain $$d_n^+=\left\lVert u_n^+\right\rVert_{\infty},\qquad d_n^-=\left\lVert v_n^-\right\rVert_{\infty}.$$ The maximum must stabilize before a coordinate recurrence can be promoted to either scalar degree sequence.

## Forward interior tail

[\[prop:forward-interior-recurrence\]]{#prop:forward-interior-recurrence label="prop:forward-interior-recurrence"} Suppose $r_\star$ is interior to the chamber $\xi$. Then $d_n^+$ eventually satisfies $$d^+_{n+2}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(C_\xi)d^+_{n+1}-\det(C_\xi)d^+_n.$$ If $r_\star=1$, the ordinary-seed sequence is geometric from time zero.

For some $n_{\mathrm{int}}$, $$u^+_{n+1}=C_\xi u^+_n\qquad(n\geq n_{\mathrm{int}}).$$ Cayley--Hamilton gives $$C_\xi^2-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(C_\xi)C_\xi+\det(C_\xi)I_2=0,$$ so each coordinate obeys the displayed order-at-most-two relation. If $r_\star\neq1$, convergence puts $r_n-1$ eventually at the fixed nonzero sign of $r_\star-1$; the same coordinate then realizes $d_n^+$. If $r_\star=1$, the seed $u_0^+=\mathbf{1}$ is the unique fixed ray, so $C_\xi\mathbf{1}=\nu\mathbf{1}$ and $d_n^+=\nu^n$. This proves both seed cases.

## Forward fixed-wall and strict-wall tails

[\[prop:forward-wall-recurrence\]]{#prop:forward-wall-recurrence label="prop:forward-wall-recurrence"} If the ordinary seed lies on the fixed wall ray, $d_n^+$ is geometric. For a strict wall tail, let the adjacent selector matrices be ordered so that $$M_+=C_+C_-,\qquad M_-=C_-C_+.$$ With $$\tau=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(M_+)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(M_-),\qquad
\Delta=\det(M_+)=\det(M_-),$$ one has, for all sufficiently large $n$, $$d^+_{n+4}=\tau d^+_{n+2}-\Delta d^+_n.$$

On the fixed wall, Proposition [\[prop:wall-ray\]](#prop:wall-ray){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:wall-ray"} sends the seed ray to itself with multiplier $\mu$, so the degree is geometric. On a strict tail, Proposition [\[prop:selectors\]](#prop:selectors){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:selectors"} gives the two alternating step orders. The even and odd state subsequences are advanced by $M_+$ and $M_-$, respectively. Cyclicity of trace and multiplicativity of determinant give their common $\tau,\Delta$. Cayley--Hamilton applied to each product gives the same stride-two coordinate law.

A strict ordinary-seed orbit cannot have $r_\star=1$, because $r_0=1$ would then be fixed. Hence convergence stabilizes the same visible coordinate on both parities beyond some $n_{\mathrm{wall}}$. Applying the two coordinate laws to that maximum proves the displayed eventual scalar relation.

## Inverse chamber matrices

The inverse recurrence starts from its own state equation. If $\kappa s_n$ belongs to the chamber $\xi$, then $$v^-_{n+1}=A_\xi Bv^-_n=D_\xi v^-_n.$$ Moreover, $$D_\xi=A_\xi B=B^{-1}C_\xi B.$$ The similarity identifies trace, determinant, and spectrum, but the first display, together with inverse visibility, is the recurrence proof. The bridge instead controls $\left\lVert Bv_n^-\right\rVert_{\infty}$, which can have a different maximizing coordinate from $\left\lVert v_n^-\right\rVert_{\infty}$.

## Inverse interior cases

[\[prop:inverse-interior-recurrence\]]{#prop:inverse-interior-recurrence label="prop:inverse-interior-recurrence"} If $r_\star$ lies in the chamber $\xi$, then $d_n^-$ eventually satisfies $$d^-_{n+2}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(C_\xi)d^-_{n+1}-\det(C_\xi)d^-_n.$$ When $s_\star=1$, the inverse ordinary-seed sequence is geometric from time zero.

Since $\kappa s_n\to r_\star$, there is $n_{\mathrm{int}}$ such that $$v^-_{n+1}=D_\xi v^-_n\qquad(n\geq n_{\mathrm{int}}).$$ Similarity gives $$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(D_\xi)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(C_\xi),\qquad
\det(D_\xi)=\det(C_\xi),$$ and Cayley--Hamilton gives the stated relation for each inverse state coordinate. If $s_\star\neq1$, convergence makes the sign of $s_n-1$ eventually constant, so the same coordinate realizes $d_n^-$. If $s_\star=1$, the seed $v^-_0=\mathbf{1}$ already lies on the unique inverse fixed ray. Thus $D_\xi\mathbf{1}=\nu\mathbf{1}$, $v_n^-=\nu^n\mathbf{1}$, and the degree sequence is geometric. This establishes the inverse result without using the vector bridge.

## Inverse fixed-wall and strict-wall cases

[\[prop:inverse-wall-recurrence\]]{#prop:inverse-wall-recurrence label="prop:inverse-wall-recurrence"} Assume $\kappa s_\star=r_\star$ is a Newton wall. If $s_\star=1$, the ordinary inverse seed is fixed and $d_n^-$ is geometric. Otherwise a strict tail alternates the adjacent matrices $D_-,D_+$. With $$N_+=D_+D_-,\qquad N_-=D_-D_+,$$ the two parity products satisfy $$N_\pm=B^{-1}M_\pm B,$$ and, for all sufficiently large $n$, $$d^-_{n+4}=\tau d^-_{n+2}-\Delta d^-_n.$$

If $s_\star=1$, then $v_0^-=\mathbf{1}$ is the fixed inverse ray from time zero. Its intermediate state $Bv_n^-$ remains on the tied Newton ray $\kappa s_\star=r_\star$. The full face therefore gives the same homogeneous output at every step, so $v_n^-=\nu^n\mathbf{1}$ and $d_n^-$ is geometric.

For a strict ordinary inverse orbit, necessarily $s_\star\neq1$; otherwise its seed would already be fixed. Scaling conjugacy and Proposition [\[prop:selectors\]](#prop:selectors){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:selectors"} give eventual adjacent-label alternation, evaluated at $\kappa s_n$. Since $D_\xi=B^{-1}C_\xi B$, multiplying in the actual step order gives $$D_+D_-=B^{-1}C_+C_-B,\qquad
D_-D_+=B^{-1}C_-C_+B.$$ Thus the two inverse parity products are similar to the corresponding forward products. Cayley--Hamilton gives the common stride-two coordinate law. Finally $s_n\to s_\star\neq1$, so both parities eventually lie in a neighborhood disjoint from $1$; the same coordinate then realizes the inverse maximum on each parity. The scalar law follows.

## Common coefficients, parity visibility, and indices

The four products have shared coefficients $$\tau=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(M_+)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(M_-)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(N_+)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(N_-),$$ $$\Delta=\det(M_+)=\det(M_-)=\det(N_+)=\det(N_-).$$ The middle trace equality is cyclicity, the determinant equality is multiplicativity, and the forward--inverse equalities follow from similarity. These equalities do not say that the matrices themselves are equal.

Choose $n_0$ larger than the selector thresholds and the forward and inverse visibility thresholds. Then the two independently proved laws are $$d^\pm_{n+4}=\tau d^\pm_{n+2}-\Delta d^\pm_n
\qquad(n\geq n_0)$$ on strict wall tails. Each parity uses the product matching its actual selector word. The condition $n\geq n_0$ is essential because a coordinate recurrence cannot pass through a changing maximum before visibility stabilizes.

## Consequence and boundary

[\[cor:recurrences\]]{#cor:recurrences label="cor:recurrences"} Forward and inverse interior degree tails have recurrence order at most two; strict wall tails have interleaved recurrence order at most four; fixed-ray tails are geometric.

Combine Propositions [\[prop:forward-interior-recurrence\]](#prop:forward-interior-recurrence){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:forward-interior-recurrence"}, [\[prop:forward-wall-recurrence\]](#prop:forward-wall-recurrence){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:forward-wall-recurrence"}, [\[prop:inverse-interior-recurrence\]](#prop:inverse-interior-recurrence){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:inverse-interior-recurrence"}, and [\[prop:inverse-wall-recurrence\]](#prop:inverse-wall-recurrence){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:inverse-wall-recurrence"}. The two directions use their own state matrices and visibility proofs.

Item 1 is Proposition [\[prop:symplectic\]](#prop:symplectic){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:symplectic"}. Item 2 follows from Lemmas [\[lem:minimal-x\]](#lem:minimal-x){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:minimal-x"}--[\[lem:powers\]](#lem:powers){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:powers"} and Proposition [\[prop:survival\]](#prop:survival){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:survival"}. Lemma [\[lem:carries\]](#lem:carries){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:carries"} together with Propositions [\[prop:forward\]](#prop:forward){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:forward"} and [\[prop:inverse\]](#prop:inverse){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:inverse"} proves item 3. Proposition [\[prop:bridge\]](#prop:bridge){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:bridge"} and Corollary [\[cor:rates\]](#cor:rates){reference-type="ref" reference="cor:rates"} prove item 4. Proposition [\[prop:projective\]](#prop:projective){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:projective"}, Lemma [\[lem:logderivative\]](#lem:logderivative){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:logderivative"}, and Theorem [\[thm:contraction\]](#thm:contraction){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:contraction"} prove item 5. Corollary [\[cor:fixed\]](#cor:fixed){reference-type="ref" reference="cor:fixed"} and Propositions [\[prop:selectors\]](#prop:selectors){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:selectors"} and [\[prop:no-cycle\]](#prop:no-cycle){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:no-cycle"} prove item 6. Propositions [\[prop:interior-spectrum\]](#prop:interior-spectrum){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:interior-spectrum"} and [\[prop:wall-ray\]](#prop:wall-ray){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:wall-ray"}, with Corollary [\[cor:quadratic\]](#cor:quadratic){reference-type="ref" reference="cor:quadratic"}, prove item 7. The four scalar-tail propositions in Section 7 and Corollary [\[cor:recurrences\]](#cor:recurrences){reference-type="ref" reference="cor:recurrences"} prove item 8.

No recurrence is asserted to have minimal order. In particular, the inverse recurrence is not transferred through Proposition [\[prop:bridge\]](#prop:bridge){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:bridge"}.

# Exact fixtures, scope, and collision subtraction

## Fixture I: genuinely quadratic interior value

Take $$E=\{(2,2)\},\qquad B=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(3,2),\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}.$$ The only chamber matrix is $$C=BA=\begin{pmatrix}3&6\\4&2\end{pmatrix}.$$ Its trace is $5$, its determinant is $-18$, and its characteristic polynomial is $$\chi_C(t)=t^2-5t-18.$$ The Perron value is therefore $$\lambda_1(F)=\frac{5+\sqrt{97}}2,$$ which is genuinely quadratic.

The bridge can be checked by hand at its first shifted index. Here $$H(\mathbf{1})=4,\qquad c_\star=3,\qquad
u_1^+=B\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{1})=(9,6)^{\mathsf T}.$$ For the inverse state, $$Bv_0^-=(3,2)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
H(3,2)=10,\qquad
v_1^-=(7,8)^{\mathsf T}.$$ Next $H(9,6)=30$, so $$u_2^+=B(21,24)^{\mathsf T}
=(63,48)^{\mathsf T}
=3Bv_1^-.$$ This exact calculation displays the constant, the diagonal factor, and the one-step shift.

## Fixture II: transient to a fixed wall

Take $$E=\{(2,8),(4,5),(5,3)\},\qquad B=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(24,11).$$ The envelope functions $2r+8$, $4r+5$, and $5r+3$ have walls $$r=\frac32,\qquad r=2.$$ The low, middle, and high chamber matrices are $$C_{\mathrm{low}}=
\begin{pmatrix}24&192\\22&77\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C_{\mathrm{middle}}=
\begin{pmatrix}72&120\\44&44\end{pmatrix},$$ $$C_{\mathrm{high}}=
\begin{pmatrix}96&72\\55&22\end{pmatrix}.$$ Starting from $u_0^+=\mathbf{1}$, the low selector gives $$u_1^+=(216,99)^{\mathsf T},\qquad r_1=\frac{24}{11}>2.$$ The high selector gives $$u_2^+=(27864,14058)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
r_2=\frac{1548}{781}\in\left(\frac32,2\right).$$ The middle selector then gives $$r_3=\frac{205176}{102476}
=\frac{51294}{25619}>2.$$ Thus the selector word begins $$\mathrm{low},\ \mathrm{high},\ \mathrm{middle},\
\mathrm{high},\ \mathrm{middle},\ldots.$$

The tail follows from interval images, not extrapolation. On the middle chamber, $$\phi_{\mathrm{middle}}(r)
=\frac{24}{11}\frac{3r+5}{4r+4};$$ it is decreasing, equals $2$ at $r=2$, and is greater than $2$ at $r=3/2$. Hence $$\phi_{\mathrm{middle}}\left(\left(\frac32,2\right)\right)
\subset(2,\infty).$$ On the high chamber, $$\phi_{\mathrm{high}}(r)
=\frac{24}{11}\frac{4r+3}{5r+2};$$ it decreases from $2$ at the wall toward $96/55>3/2$, and therefore $$\phi_{\mathrm{high}}\bigl((2,\infty)\bigr)
\subset\left(\frac32,2\right).$$ This proves the alternating label tail and convergence to $r=2$; it is not a numerical cycle.

The primitive wall direction and common multiplier are $$\boldsymbol{\omega}=(2,1)^{\mathsf T},\qquad \mu=132,$$ because $$C_{\mathrm{middle}}\boldsymbol{\omega}=C_{\mathrm{high}}\boldsymbol{\omega}
=(264,132)^{\mathsf T}=132\boldsymbol{\omega}.$$ For the middle-then-high order, $$C_{\mathrm{high}}C_{\mathrm{middle}}
=\begin{pmatrix}10080&14688\\4928&7568\end{pmatrix}.$$ Its trace and determinant are $$\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}=17648,\qquad \det=3902976,$$ and its eigenvalues are $$17424=132^2,\qquad224.$$ All values in this fixture follow by the displayed integer and rational arithmetic.

## Failure modes tied to assumptions

Allowing coordinate-axis support can make one face derivative vanish and destroy the Hessian and independence certificates. Allowing an exponent equal to one removes a full coordinate copy from the strict carry. Permitting a zero coefficient, an uncollected duplicate, or a support list different from the actual collected polynomial makes the envelope record a monomial that need not exist. Positive characteristic can annihilate $x_0y_0(1-x_0-y_0)$ or a pure-power derivative.

A mixed term in $W$, a nonseparated momentum Hamiltonian, or an arbitrary Hamiltonian-shear extension replaces the diagonal power transform and can destroy the scaling conjugacy and decreasing projective map. Infinite support removes the finite maximum used for $q<1$. In dimension at least three there is no ordered one-dimensional wall chart on which the side-swapping proof applies. An altered phase order, a different ordinary seed, or an arbitrary bridge seed changes the base identity $\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{1})=c_\star\mathbf{1}$ and requires a new argument. None of these failures is repaired here by a generic-coefficient assumption.

## Mechanism-level collision subtraction
