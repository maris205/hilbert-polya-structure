# Introduction {#sec:introduction}

The arithmetic symbolic-dynamics program studied here begins from a rigid source rather than a target spectrum. Finite full shifts satisfy $$F_m\boxtimes F_n\cong F_{mn},\qquad h_{\rm top}(F_n)=\log n.$$ Tensor atoms therefore recover the rational primes as internal indecomposables, their entropy is the desired logarithmic clock, and loop repetition supplies prime powers. The resulting diagonal atom transfer gives the Euler product in its honest half-plane. Paper05 added an intrinsic exterior grading, but its strongest same-family completion attempts failed: they generated neither a half-density nor an Archimedean factor.

The present note takes a deliberately narrow next step. The tensor source has a unique least nonunit atom, $F_2$. Its maximal-entropy Markov kernel is $$K_2=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.$$ More importantly, symbol exchange decomposes its permutation representation into two multiplicity-one lines. The trivial line is stationary, while the sign line is centered. These two sectors furnish an exact finite-prime channel and a fluctuation channel without adding a second system family.

Our key organizing identity uses the sign operator $Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ and the tilted Parry matrix $$H(z)=e^{zQ/2}K_2e^{zQ/2}
 =\frac12\begin{pmatrix}e^z&1\\1&e^{-z}\end{pmatrix}.$$ It has rank one and nonzero eigenvalue $\cosh z$, so $$\operatorname{tr}H(z)^r=(\cosh z)^r.                     \tag{1.1}$$ At $z=0$, this is the unit power trace needed for every prime-power Euler coefficient. At $z=iu/\sqrt r$, it is the exact characteristic function of a normalized binary Birkhoff sum and tends to $e^{-u^2/2}$. The Mellin transform of the resulting self-dual Gaussian is $\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$. Thus the two channels are not coordinates copied from unrelated candidates: they are two specializations of one source-internal trace family.

This produces the new symbolic candidate SD-C08. In its initial common domain $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, its two canonical transforms give $$\mathfrak Z_{\rm SD}(s)
 =\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s).$$ The formula is a *Mellin--Fredholm factorization*. We do not call it a single dynamical determinant. We prove neither meromorphic continuation nor the functional equation; we do not insert $s(s-1)/2$; and we do not score zeros. This claim boundary is the difference between an A3 mechanism and a purported solution of RH.

The negative theorem is as useful as the positive construction. A natural Hellinger pairing replaces $p^{-s}$ and $p^{-(1-s)}$ by a chiral block. On $s=1/2+it$ the paired weights have modulus $p^{-1/2}$, suggesting a centered self-adjoint family. We show that every one-sided implementation $A_t=G^{1/2+it}K$ with bounded $K$ is unitarily conjugate to its value at $t=0$, without any commutation hypothesis. The entire vertical parameter is a phase gauge. In particular, cross-atom noncommutation does not create spectral motion in this ansatz, even though it still risks manufacturing mixed primitive cycles and corrupting the exact Euler ledger. A live replacement must change the phase architecture itself while preserving that ledger.

The contributions are:

1.  an intrinsic selection theorem for the binary scalar fluctuation sector inside the full-shift tensor source;

2.  a single tilted-trace identity generating both the Euler and Gaussian channels;

3.  locally uniform Mellin convergence to the Archimedean Gamma factor;

4.  dimension-shifted $q$-symbol controls and explicit universality limitations;

5.  a general critical-axis chiral no-motion theorem and an exact $\det_3$ loss formula;

6.  a preregistered Route-A decision: A3 advances, global completion stops, and Route B remains locked.

All primary constructions remain in Symbolic Dynamics. Analogies with other system families are outside the scope of this note and are recorded, if at all, only as future clues.

# Symbolic zeta, Parry measures, and fluctuation twists {#sec:related}

The use of finite-state matrices to encode symbolic zeta functions goes back to the determinant formulas of @bowenlanford1970; transfer-operator continuation in hyperbolic dynamics was developed in a much broader setting by @ruelle1976. These results make a determinant representation natural, but they do not identify the tensor atoms used here or manufacture the Riemann Euler product from a full-shift monoidal source.

The maximal-entropy Markov measure for a finite-state shift is intrinsic to the transition structure [@parry1964]. Normalized transfer operators and $g$-measures provide a standard language for this probabilistic sector [@keane1972; @walters1975]. We use only the elementary full two-shift case: its transition matrix is $K_2=J_2/2$, and successive symbols are independent and uniform. Calling $K_2$ a Parry Markov fiber does not turn the matrix-weighted atom loop into a new skew-product dynamical system.

Central and local limit theorems for symbolic systems, as well as analytic twists of transfer operators, are classical [@lalley1986; @parrypollicott1990; @coelhoparry1990; @aaronsondenker2001]. In particular, one frequently studies a family obtained by multiplying a transfer operator by $e^{z f}$; derivatives of its leading eigenvalue encode fluctuations. Our finite matrix $H(z)$ is the completely explicit two-state instance. The point of the present synthesis is not a new CLT. It is that the same tilted trace family has two arithmetic-symbolic specializations: its untwisted trace preserves the tensor-prime Euler ledger, and its diffusive imaginary twist generates the Gaussian whose Mellin transform is the Archimedean Gamma factor.

The literature audit supports the ingredients but not a broad priority claim. Within the finite primary-source search performed for this stage, we did not find the exact package consisting of the tensor-prime full-shift monoid, its minimal binary Parry sector, the same-trace Euler/Gamma specialization, and the universal one-sided chiral no-motion theorem. Absence from a finite search is not evidence of first-ever novelty, so our novelty claim is limited to this source-locked synthesis and its falsification boundary.

Finally, a fluctuation Gamma factor is generic unless its observable and dimension are selected. The binary sign representation is canonical up to orientation, but biased binary chains and scalar projections in larger alphabets still have Gaussian limits after centering and variance normalization. We retain those controls explicitly rather than presenting the Gamma limit as sufficient evidence on its own.

# The minimal binary source and one tilted trace {#sec:binary}

Let $F_n=\{1,\ldots,n\}^{\mathbb Z}$ with the shift map. We work with the symmetric monoidal skeleton generated by Cartesian product.

[\[prop:binary-selection\]]{#prop:binary-selection label="prop:binary-selection"} The unique nonunit tensor atom of minimum topological entropy is $F_2$. Moreover, among the permutation representations of the full shifts, $F_2$ is the unique one whose centered standard representation is a canonical scalar line.

The tensor-isomorphism classes form $(\mathbb N_{\ge1},\times)$ and entropy is $\log n$. Nonunit indecomposables are therefore $F_p$, and strict monotonicity of $\log n$ selects $F_2$. For $F_q$, the symbol-permutation representation decomposes as $$\mathbb C^q=\mathbb C\Omega_q\oplus E_q,
 \qquad \Omega_q=q^{-1/2}(1,\ldots,1),$$ where $E_q=\Omega_q^\perp$ is the standard representation of dimension $q-1$. It is one-dimensional exactly when $q=2$. Its two unit generators differ only by sign, which disappears under an absolute moment.

The unique positive $S_q$-invariant unit vector is $\Omega_q$, and its projection is $$K_q=|\Omega_q\rangle\langle\Omega_q|=J_q/q.$$ For $q=2$, introduce the sign involution $Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. Relabeling the two symbols sends $Q$ to $-Q$ but leaves every even or absolute quantity below unchanged.

[\[thm:tilted-trace\]]{#thm:tilted-trace label="thm:tilted-trace"} For $$H(z)=e^{zQ/2}K_2e^{zQ/2}
 =\frac12\begin{pmatrix}e^z&1\\1&e^{-z}\end{pmatrix},$$ one has, for every integer $r\ge1$, $$\operatorname{tr}H(z)^r=(\cosh z)^r.$$ In particular, $z=0$ gives $\operatorname{tr}K_2^r=1$, while $$\operatorname{tr}H(iu/\sqrt r)^r
 =\cos(u/\sqrt r)^r\longrightarrow e^{-u^2/2}.$$

The matrix $H(z)$ has rank one. Its trace, hence its only nonzero eigenvalue, is $(e^z+e^{-z})/2=\cosh z$. The power-trace formula follows. The last limit is obtained from $r\log\cos(u/\sqrt r)=-u^2/2+O(r^{-1})$ locally uniformly in $u$.

By cyclicity, these are also the power traces of the cyclic representative $H_{\rm cyc}(z)=K_2e^{zQ}$ (up to the harmless convention $Q\mapsto-Q$). On the active rank-one sector it has the same nonzero eigenvalue and Fredholm block determinant as the symmetric representative; the numerical implementation uses $H_{\rm cyc}(iu)$.

The final expression is also exactly the characteristic function of $r^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^rX_j$ for independent $X_j\in\{-1,1\}$. Thus a single finite symbolic trace family contains both channels before any Gamma function is mentioned.

## Euler transfer as the untwisted channel

Let $\operatorname{At}$ denote the nonunit tensor atoms and set $$\mathcal H=\ell^2(\operatorname{At})\otimes\mathbb C^2,
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_{s,z}=\bigoplus_{F_p\in\operatorname{At}}p^{-s}H(z).$$ This is a matrix-weighted transfer on the atom-loop symbolic suspension. It is not a group cocycle and we do not claim an independently constructed skew shift.

[\[thm:euler\]]{#thm:euler label="thm:euler"} For $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, $$\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal A_{s,0}^r=\sum_p p^{-rs},
 \qquad
 \det(I-\mathcal A_{s,0})=\prod_p(1-p^{-s})=\zeta(s)^{-1}.$$ With the fixed purely odd one-particle convention, $\operatorname{Ber}(I-\mathcal A_{s,0})=\zeta(s)$.

Since $K_2^r=K_2$ and $\operatorname{tr}K_2=1$, each atom block contributes $p^{-rs}$. Trace-class convergence holds for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, where the logarithmic Fredholm expansion is absolutely convergent: $$\log\det(I-\mathcal A_{s,0})
 =-\sum_{r\ge1}\frac1r\sum_pp^{-rs}
 =\sum_p\log(1-p^{-s}).$$ The Berezinian is the reciprocal determinant on a purely odd space.

The exact ledger selects rank one, not binary fairness. Uniform $K_q$ and biased rank-one kernels have the same power traces. Conversely, for a finite reversible stochastic kernel, the identities $\operatorname{tr}K^r=1$ for all $r$ force all nonstationary eigenvalues to vanish; reversibility then gives a rank-one orthogonal projection. Binary selection enters through the tensor minimum and the one-dimensional centered representation, not through the Euler identity alone.

# From binary fluctuations to the Gamma factor {#sec:gamma}

Let $(X_j)_{j\ge0}$ be the sign observable along the Parry process of $F_2$, so the $X_j$ are independent and uniform on $\{-1,1\}$. Put $$S_N=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}X_j,
 \qquad Y_N=\frac{S_N}{\sqrt{2\pi N}}.$$ We take $N$ odd. This removes the finite-$N$ atom at zero, which would make negative moments undefined even though the limiting singularity is integrable.

[\[thm:gamma\]]{#thm:gamma label="thm:gamma"} For odd $N$ and $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, define $$M_N(s)=\mathbb E|Y_N|^{s-1}.$$ Then, locally uniformly on the half-plane $\operatorname{Re}s>0$, $$M_N(s)\longrightarrow
 \int_{\mathbb R}|x|^{s-1}e^{-\pi x^2}\,\mathrm dx
 =\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2).$$

The specialization in [\[thm:tilted-trace\]](#thm:tilted-trace){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:tilted-trace"} gives the central limit theorem. A lattice local central limit estimate gives uniform Gaussian approximation on bounded windows, while the binomial tail estimate controls large $|Y_N|$. Near zero, the local estimate implies a uniform small-ball bound $\mathbb P(|Y_N|\le x)\le Cx+C N^{-1/2}$. For every compact $C\Subset\{\operatorname{Re}s>0\}$ this controls $|Y_N|^{\operatorname{Re}s-1}(1+|\log|Y_N||^k)$ uniformly for the finitely many $s$-derivatives needed by normal-family convergence. Truncation at small and large radii therefore upgrades weak convergence to locally uniform Mellin convergence. Finally, $u=\pi x^2$ gives $$2\int_0^\infty x^{s-1}e^{-\pi x^2}\,\mathrm dx
 =\pi^{-s/2}\int_0^\infty u^{s/2-1}e^{-u}\,\mathrm du.$$

The $1/2$ is now an internal fluctuation exponent, not a number copied from the target critical line. The constant $\pi$ is the normalization for the self-dual Gaussian density $e^{-\pi x^2}$ under Fourier transform. Changing Gaussian convention would multiply the Mellin transform by a zero-free exponential in $s$, but we freeze the self-dual convention before comparison.

## Representation-dimensional controls

For the uniform $q$-shift, place its $q$ symbols at the vertices $v_1,\ldots,v_q$ of a centered regular simplex in $E_q\cong\mathbb R^{d}$, $d=q-1$, with $\|v_j\|=1$ and $\langle v_j,v_k\rangle=-1/d$ for $j\ne k$. This uses the full standard representation and does not select a favorable scalar projection.

[\[prop:qcontrol\]]{#prop:qcontrol label="prop:qcontrol"} If $V_j$ are independent uniform simplex vertices and $$R_N=\frac{\|\sum_{j=1}^NV_j\|}{\sqrt{2\pi N}},$$ then, for $\operatorname{Re}s>1-d$ where the limiting moment is locally integrable, $$\mathbb E R_N^{s-1}\longrightarrow
 M_d(s):=(\pi d)^{-(s-1)/2}
 \frac{\Gamma((d+s-1)/2)}{\Gamma(d/2)}.$$ For $d=1$ this is $\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$; for every $d>1$ the Gamma shift is different.

The simplex covariance is $I_d/d$, so $\sqrt{d/N}\sum_jV_j$ converges to a standard $d$-dimensional Gaussian $Z$. The radial moment formula $$\mathbb E\|Z\|^{s-1}
 =2^{(s-1)/2}\frac{\Gamma((d+s-1)/2)}{\Gamma(d/2)}$$ and the scale $R_N=\|\sqrt{d/N}\sum_jV_j\|/\sqrt{2\pi d}$ give the claim.

This control restores a meaningful part of binary specificity: the canonical full standard representation has the exact Archimedean shape only in dimension one. It does not defeat every genericity challenge. A biased binary chain, after centering and variance normalization, has the same one-dimensional Gaussian limit; an arbitrarily selected scalar projection of $F_q$ does too. Those choices are not the multiplicity-one $S_q$-representation supplied by the source, but their success means the Gamma limit alone cannot carry the arithmetic claim.

## The same-source Mellin--Fredholm factorization

The stationary and sign channels are specializations of the same tilted trace family $H(z)$. Combining [\[thm:euler,thm:gamma\]](#thm:euler,thm:gamma){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:euler,thm:gamma"} therefore defines, on their common initial domain, $$\mathfrak Z_{\rm SD}(s)
 :=\left(\lim_{N\to\infty}M_N(s)\right)
   \operatorname{Ber}(I-\mathcal A_{s,0})
 =\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
 \qquad \operatorname{Re}s>1.                       \tag{4.1}$$ Equation (4.1) is an exact factorization on that half-plane. It is stronger than coordinate splicing because both transforms descend from the same multiplicity-free $S_2$ source and the same matrix trace deformation. It is weaker than a unified determinant because the Mellin limit and Berezinian are still distinct analytic operations. No continuation or functional equation is inferred from their product.

# Hellinger centering and the chiral no-motion theorem {#sec:chiral}

The factor $p^{-1/2}$ can also be generated without inserting a square root by hand. Pair the weights $p^{-s}$ and $p^{-(1-s)}$ in an off-diagonal block; their product is $p^{-1}$. On the line $s=1/2+it$ the block is self-adjoint. This Hellinger-style pairing selects the center exactly, but exact centering does not imply spectral motion.

Let $$G=\bigoplus_{p}p^{-1}I_{E_p}$$ on a Hilbert sum of finite atom fibers $E_p$. Let $K$ be any bounded operator on that sum and define $$A_t=G^{1/2+it}K,
 \qquad
 B_t=\begin{pmatrix}0&A_t\\A_t^*&0\end{pmatrix}.$$

[\[thm:no-motion\]]{#thm:no-motion label="thm:no-motion"} For every real $t$, $$B_t=U_tB_0U_t^*,
 \qquad
 U_t=\operatorname{diag}(G^{it},I).$$ Consequently the spectrum, singular values, and every unitarily invariant regularized determinant of $B_t$ are independent of $t$. No commutation condition on $G$ and $K$ is required.

Put $A_0=G^{1/2}K$. Since $G$ is positive and injective, Borel functional calculus makes $G^{it}$ unitary, and $$A_t=G^{it}A_0.$$ Conjugating $B_0$ by $\operatorname{diag}(G^{it},I)$ therefore gives upper-right block $A_t$ and lower-left block $A_t^*$. This proves the displayed identity.

The theorem is not special to $K_2$ or to block-preserving couplings. In the one-sided phase ansatz, even $[G,K]\ne0$ leaves $t$ as a removable phase; noncommutation alone can never produce height-dependent spectrum. Moreover, a free cross-atom transition still creates temporal cycles such as $p\to q\to p$ whose lengths involve $\log p+\log q$. These are mixed primitive cycles rather than repetitions of one tensor atom. Thus a viable replacement must simultaneously use a genuinely different phase architecture that is not removable by one block unitary and supply an intrinsic homological or signed cancellation theorem preserving A1.

## The exact regularized determinant and what it deletes

For the frozen rank-one binary fiber, write the off-axis formal pairing as $$\mathcal B_s=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&G^sK\\G^{1-s}K&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad K=\bigoplus_pK_2.$$ Each active atom block squares to $p^{-1}$ times the rank-one projection and has nonzero eigenvalues $\pm p^{-1/2}$. The two off-diagonal pieces belong simultaneously to $S_3$ when $$\frac13<\operatorname{Re}s<\frac23.$$

[\[prop:det3\]]{#prop:det3 label="prop:det3"} In the common $S_3$ strip, $$\det{}_3(I-z\mathcal B_s)
 =\prod_p(1-z^2/p)e^{z^2/p}.$$ The product is independent of $s$. Moreover, $$\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal B_s^{2r}=2\sum_pp^{-r}$$ converges only for $r\ge2$, whereas odd traces vanish. The divergent quadratic trace $r=1$ is precisely the term removed by $\det_3$.

Pair the regularized factors for the two active eigenvalues: $$(1-zp^{-1/2})e^{zp^{-1/2}+z^2/(2p)}
 (1+zp^{-1/2})e^{-zp^{-1/2}+z^2/(2p)}
 =(1-z^2/p)e^{z^2/p}.$$ Its logarithm begins at order $p^{-2}$, so the prime product converges. The power traces follow from the eigenvalues $\pm p^{-1/2}$.

Thus the first natural determinant reaching the critical line is both vertically sterile and amputated at the lowest nonzero trace. It receives no credit for a Riemann zero divisor. The result sharpens the next target: one needs a source-internal multi-phase chiral architecture not reducible to $A_t=G^{it}A_0$, together with exact cancellation of mixed cycles inside one trace formula. Mass noncommutation by itself does not meet this target.

## Controls that prove too much

Uniform $K_3$, uniform $K_4$, and a biased rank-one binary kernel all satisfy $\operatorname{tr}K^r=1$. A reversible binary control with second eigenvalue $0.4$ instead has $\operatorname{tr}K^r=1+0.4^r$ and fails immediately. Hence the Euler ledger selects rank-one idempotence rather than the fair binary process.

After centering and variance normalization, the biased binary process and a noncanonical scalar observable on $F_3$ also approach the one-dimensional Gamma target. All nonconstant binary relabelings and rescalings become identical after standardization. These are honest `PROVES_TOO_MUCH` outcomes.

The canonical radial controls behave differently. At their final cutoffs, the $K_3$ radial statistic has median error $1.5793$ against the binary target but $0.00996$ against its native two-dimensional law. The corresponding $K_4$ errors are $2.5506$ and $0.01009$. The dimension shift predicted by [\[prop:qcontrol\]](#prop:qcontrol){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:qcontrol"} is therefore visible at finite cutoff.

An auxiliary Parry-inventory branch was also falsified. A pressure-normalized Hellinger identity holds for shifted and random positive inventories, hence proves too much; moreover, the identity-adjacency atom source of SD-C07 has no native nontrivial transition probability. Adding full mixing would create the mixed temporal cycles excluded by the source lock. This auxiliary branch is not part of SD-C08.

The experiment therefore supports three exact conclusions: the stationary binary fiber preserves the Euler trace ledger, the sign sector produces the Gamma skeleton, and the frozen block-preserving chiral centering is gauge-trivial. # Conclusion: the next phase architecture must change {#sec:conclusion}

# Proof details, domains, and scope discipline {#app:proofs}

## Local uniformity of the Mellin limit

We record the analytic point hidden by a bare appeal to the central limit theorem. Let $C$ be a compact subset of $\{\operatorname{Re}s>0\}$ and choose $0<\sigma_-\le\operatorname{Re}s\le\sigma_+$. Split the lattice expectation into $|Y_N|<\delta$, $\delta\le |Y_N|\le R$, and $|Y_N|>R$.

On the middle region, the local central limit theorem gives a Riemann-sum approximation uniform in $s\in C$. Hoeffding's binomial bound makes the tail uniformly smaller than a Gaussian moment of order $\sigma_+-1$ as $R\to\infty$. At the origin, the maximal lattice mass is $O(N^{-1/2})$ and the lattice spacing of $Y_N$ is $O(N^{-1/2})$. Summing $x^{\sigma_--1}(1+|\log x|^k)$ over lattice cells in $(0,\delta)$ is bounded by its integrable continuum counterpart plus a vanishing endpoint term. This controls the complex powers and their derivatives uniformly on $C$. First let $N\to\infty$, then $\delta\downarrow0$ and $R\uparrow\infty$. Vitali's theorem, or the direct derivative bounds, gives local uniformity.

The restriction to odd $N$ is important. For even $N$, $S_N=0$ has positive probability, and $|S_N|^{s-1}$ is not finite when $0<\operatorname{Re}s<1$.

## Rank-one power-trace criterion

Suppose a finite reversible stochastic matrix $K$ has eigenvalues $1,\lambda_2,\ldots,\lambda_q$. If $\operatorname{tr}K^r=1$ for all $r\ge1$, then $\sum_{j=2}^q\lambda_j^r=0$ for all $r$. The first $q-1$ power sums vanish, so Newton identities make every elementary symmetric polynomial in the nonstationary eigenvalues zero. Hence all $\lambda_j=0$. Reversibility makes $K$ diagonalizable in its stationary inner product, so it is a rank-one projection. This proves that the Euler ledger tests rank one, not alphabet size.

## Operator ideals for the chiral pair

The singular values of the upper-right atom blocks scale as $p^{-\operatorname{Re}s}$, and those of the lower-left blocks as $p^{-(1-\operatorname{Re}s)}$. Both lie in $S_q$ exactly when $$q\operatorname{Re}s>1,
 \qquad q(1-\operatorname{Re}s)>1.$$ For $q=2$ this gives the open strip $1/2<\operatorname{Re}s<1/2$, which is empty. The first integer class with a nonempty common strip is $q=3$, giving $1/3<\operatorname{Re}s<2/3$. The $\det_3$ subtraction removes trace powers one and two. Odd chiral traces already vanish, while the power-two trace is the divergent $2\sum_pp^{-1}$. The resulting determinant therefore cannot be credited with preserving the low-order Euler ledger.
