# Scope and source hierarchy

Volume II begins at RH-161 rather than RH-160 because RH-161 is the first paper to state the complete typed assembly interface. It is followed by eight ten-paper phases ending at RH-171, 181, 191, 201, 211, 221, 231, and 241. Review papers summarize status and finite counts; analytic claims remain sourced to the individual theorem papers.

We distinguish analytic theorems, finite certificates, conditional implications, scoped negatives, and open/not-testable leaves. A packet in an abstract finite algebra is not automatically an actual noisy spectral packet. A local probability law is not a trace observation without an identification theorem. A finite list of moments is not an all-order analytic envelope.

# RH-161: the typed assembly theorem

Let a finite packet block be isolated by a contour $\Gamma$. Write $M$ for the declared block resolvent supremum and $\varepsilon$ for the off-packet coupling. RH-161 proves rank stability under $$M\varepsilon<1$$ and an invertible graph bridge under the stronger bound $$\label{eq:delta}
 \delta=\frac{|\Gamma|M^2\varepsilon}
 {2\pi(1-M\varepsilon)}<1.$$

The assembly then requires one of two reset seeds together with five physical interfaces: $$\label{eq:assembly}
 (S_{\rm native}\vee S_{\rm lagged})\wedge R\wedge Q\wedge U\wedge Z\wedge T.$$ Here $R$ is the physical packet-to-Riesz lift, $Q$ the cloud coefficient and pole ledger, $U$ a common-space Schatten complement limit, $Z$ canonicity and normalization, and $T$ directed marked-trace convergence.

[\[thm:assembly\]]{#thm:assembly label="thm:assembly"} Under one seed and all five interfaces in [\[eq:assembly\]](#eq:assembly){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:assembly"}, exact moving cloud division, complementary $\mathcal S_p$ convergence, complete pole accounting, target-independent normalization, schedule independence, and a fixed separating family of directed marked traces produce an enhanced relative-determinant datum. The theorem covers $p=1$ and $p=2$ as typed alternatives and does not identify them.

The packet homotopy and [\[eq:delta\]](#eq:delta){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:delta"} identify a same-rank Riesz space. Exact reducing factorization divides out the finite cloud. Common-space $\mathcal S_p$ convergence gives locally uniform $\det_p$ convergence for the complement. $Q$ restores the deterministic pole divisor, $Z$ fixes the zero-free factor and removes schedule/gauge dependence, and $T$ retains the directed finite words. Every step uses a named hypothesis. None of $R,Q,U,Z,T$ is proved for the all-level prime-dynamics family by RH-161.

The two inclusion-minimal completion bundles are therefore $$\{S_{\rm native},R,Q,U,Z,T\},\qquad
 \{S_{\rm lagged},R,Q,U,Z,T\}.$$ This is an exact dependency statement, not Gate-A closure.

# RH-162--RH-171: physical Riesz shells

This block translates the assembly variables into shellwise physical obligations. It distinguishes rank isolation, graph stability, coefficient transport, complement control, and temporal marking. The RH-171 review contains $3{,}584$ finite cases and $63$ omission witnesses. Its four-leaf frontier is a conditional implication: satisfying the named physical leaves would yield shell conclusions.

The finite cases test formulas and logical minimality. They do not provide a uniform contour or a small-noise coupling theorem. A globally bounded projection norm is neither proved nor required as a substitute for the typed local graph estimates.

# RH-172--RH-181: history and cycle realization

The history/cycle phase proves exact algebra connecting finite temporal histories, cyclic closures, markers, and determinant contributions. It identifies which finite words retain orientation and which quotient operations erase it. The $2{,}600$-item review verifies the declared finite identities.

A literal infinite-history Fredholm construction is stopped in its named form: the needed compactness and uniform trace-class structure are absent. The negative conclusion does not reject every adapted history space. The surviving route uses finite separating words transported through an actual common-space limit.

# RH-182--RH-191: biorthogonal and Feshbach structure

These papers develop balanced left/right frames, exact Feshbach identities, and Schur-budget criteria for nonnormal packets. The point is not to make the operator normal, but to control the correct biorthogonal coordinates and resolvent factors. The RH-191 audit contains $2{,}960$ finite items.

The conditional Riesz conclusions still require validated physical block separation and coupling. Orthogonal finite clocks and unqualified condition numbers cannot replace those inputs. A lower bound on conditioning is not an upper stability theorem.

# RH-192--RH-201: source-channel quotients

This phase distinguishes source-cyclic from source-observation channels and constructs exact quotient and packet dictionaries. It proves canonicality relative to the declared finite source algebra and audits $1{,}352$ items.

The word "canonical" remains typed. It does not prove independence from every mesh, schedule, reset, or cutoff in the physical continuum family. Those are the $Z$ obligations of [\[eq:assembly\]](#eq:assembly){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:assembly"}. Nor does a source channel by itself supply the cloud coefficient bridge $Q$.

# RH-202--RH-211: transport failures and divisor-first pivot

Exact intertwining and transport budgets expose the cost of moving quartets and edge clouds between representations. Several natural routes fail: naive Haar transport, scalar residue renormalization, and an expanding-edge cloud transport lack the required uniformity or normalization. RH-210 and RH-211 therefore pivot to a divisor-first formulation. The review contains $649$ finite records.

This pivot is a change in proof organization, not a zeta-divisor theorem. The physical divisor, coefficient bridge, and complete no-missing/no-spurious statement remain absent.

# RH-212--RH-221: quartet shapes and fixed degree

Centered conjugate quartets admit intrinsic shape coordinates, monotone axial clocks, boundary strata, and gauge-complete reconstruction formulas. The phase also proves transverse quenching and recurrence obstructions. These are exact results in the quartet data type, supported by a $2{,}140$-item review.

A fixed quartic has fixed degree and cannot by itself encode a rank-growing spectral cloud. This is a structural obstruction to a fixed-quartic route, not to all rank-growing packet constructions. Gauge-complete reconstruction of one divisor shape does not identify a physical all-level divisor.

# RH-222--RH-231: reciprocal clouds and determinants

This block builds rank-growing conjugate cloud atlases, reciprocal dictionaries, empirical tightness criteria, and regularized determinant formulas. Projection-free descriptions reduce dependence on unstable projector coordinates. The RH-231 review audits $9{,}870$ finite items.

The determinant formulas are exact for their declared clouds. They do not show that the cloud is the actual physical resonance divisor, that its rank matches a canonical spectral count, or that its complement has a uniform common-space ideal limit. Direct promotion from resonance samples to a completed divisor is therefore stopped.

# RH-232--RH-241: the trace-envelope wall

The final phase constructs projection-free finite $\det_2$ factors and analyzes which coefficient information controls a normal analytic family. Finite jets can match while higher coefficients vary arbitrarily; local polynomial data therefore do not imply determinant convergence.

RH-240 states the positive all-order criterion. Schematically, a uniform geometric envelope for every moving noisy trace coefficient, together with the correct coefficient identification and analytic tail, yields local normality and a relative-determinant limit. RH-241 audits $7{,}280$ finite items and obtains a subunit envelope only for orders $2$--$12$.

[\[thm:jet\]]{#thm:jet label="thm:jet"} Control of a fixed finite set of Taylor or trace coefficients, even with arbitrarily accurate finite fits, does not imply a uniform all-order analytic envelope or convergence of the associated determinant family.

For any fixed jet order $m$ and nonzero $z_0$, the zero-free entire functions $$F_K(z)=\exp\!\left(K(z/z_0)^{m+1}\right)$$ have identical Taylor coefficients through order $m$ while their values and higher coefficients are unbounded as $K$ varies. Thus finite jets do not control a normal family. The same logical gap applies to the orders $2$--$12$ ledger unless a uniform all-order source theorem is added.

The endpoint has two separate missing leaves:

1.  the moving noisy all-order trace envelope; and

2.  a no-over-extraction coefficient anchor identifying the selected noisy cloud coefficients with the determinant numerator.

The deterministic coefficient dictionary developed later in Volume III solves a target-side problem and does not retroactively prove either leaf.

# Frontier and reopening conditions

The physical route may reopen through an actual moving contour/coupling theorem, a common-space $\mathcal S_1$ or $\mathcal S_2$ complement limit, complete $Q/Z/T$ identifications, or a direct all-order noisy envelope with coefficient bridge. Each input must use one clock, normalization, and operator data type.

Fixed finite endpoint extensions, synthetic clouds, local probability models, and finite regression do not reopen the all-order leaf. The route coordinate is $$\boxed{\texttt{physical\_riesz\_cloud\_assembled\_moving\_noisy\_trace\_envelope\_open}}.$$
