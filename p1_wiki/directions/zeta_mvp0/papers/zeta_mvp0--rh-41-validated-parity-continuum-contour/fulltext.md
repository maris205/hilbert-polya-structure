# Introduction {#sec:introduction}

The nested-grid program of @WangNested2026 [@WangIterated2026] follows a noisy quadratic transfer operator through dyadic discretizations. Its physical two-step operator removes a Perron contribution and a negative peripheral contribution before squaring the remainder. A smooth-kernel Haar theorem subsequently explained the observed quarter--half law $$\left\lVert E_h\right\rVert,\left\lVert D_h\right\rVert=O(h^2),\qquad
 \left\lVert C_h\right\rVert,\left\lVert B_h\right\rVert=O(h),$$ and a separate Gaussian-tail theorem treated the hard support cutoff [@WangHaar2026; @WangCutoff2026].

The remaining peripheral issue was spectral rather than algebraic. @WangRiesz2026 replaced gauge-dependent left and right eigenvectors by the intrinsic weighted Riesz term $$\mathcal Q(A;\Gamma)
 =\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma z(z-A)^{-1}\,dz.$$ For a smooth compact operator, a simple isolated nonzero eigenvalue gives a second-order Nyström bridge for this weighted term. Strong positivity makes the Perron application unconditional. The negative branch was left conditional on a simple isolated continuum resonance.

That condition cannot be inferred from a short table of finite-grid eigenvalues. Nonnormal spectra can move much farther than eigenvalue separation suggests, and a perturbation theorem must see the resolvent [@TrefethenEmbree2005; @Kato1995]. In the present problem the missing statement has two parts:

1.  a contour must contain exactly one eigenvalue of the full continuum operator, counted algebraically; and

2.  the resolvent on that contour must have an explicit norm upper that survives the finite-to-continuum passage.

We prove both statements by combining a one-center Grushin reduction with a conditional-expectation Galerkin hierarchy. The main contributions are:

1.  a moment proof of explicit first- and second-derivative row-$L^1$ bounds for the normalized folded-Gaussian kernel;

2.  exact coarse consistency for cell-average Galerkin operators and explicit $L^\infty$ Haar block bounds;

3.  a rank-one Grushin--Rouché theorem that turns one validated inverse at the circle center into an exact eigenvalue count and contour resolvent;

4.  a 224-bit Arb bridge from the archived sparse binary64 matrix to the exact-parameter continuum midpoint matrix;

5.  two dyadic Schur continuations and a final finite-rank-to-continuum Neumann transfer; and

6.  a sharp theorem boundary separating the resulting continuum $L^\infty$ statement from any unproved dimension-uniform Euclidean claim for sparse midpoint matrices.

Three evidence levels are kept separate throughout.

Analytic operator theorem

:   Moment inequalities, Galerkin identities, Schur bounds, perturbation invariance, and the continuum conclusion.

Outward certificate

:   Componentwise binary64 residual enclosures and 224-bit Arb evaluations of exact stored and exact-parameter quantities.

Floating diagnostic

:   The leading-spectrum points used only in the summary figure. They are never used to establish the root count.

# The exact operator and the main theorem {#sec:model}

Let $$p(u)=u^3-2u^2+2u-2.$$ Since $$p'(u)=3u^2-4u+2
      =3\left(u-\frac23\right)^2+\frac23>0,$$ the polynomial has a unique real root $u_{\mathrm c}$ in $(1.5,1.6)$. Fix $$\sigma=\frac1{100},\qquad m(x)=1-u_{\mathrm c}x^2,\qquad 0\le x\le1.$$ Because $1<u_{\mathrm c}<2$, one has $m([0,1])=[1-u_{\mathrm c},1]\subset[-1,1]$. For $m\in[-1,1]$, define $$g_m(y)=
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{(y-m)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right]
 +
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{(y+m)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right],
 \qquad 0\le y\le1,$$ $$Z(m)=\int_0^1g_m(y)\,dy,
 \qquad
 k(x,y)=\frac{g_{m(x)}(y)}{Z(m(x))}.$$ The continuum Markov operator is $$(\mathcal Kf)(x)=\int_0^1k(x,y)f(y)\,dy
 \quad\text{on }L^\infty([0,1]).
 \label{eq:continuum-operator}$$ Its kernel is real, strictly positive, and smooth on the compact square. Thus $\mathcal K$ is a compact Markov operator; in particular, $\left\lVert \mathcal K\right\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}=1$.

The numerical contour data are $$c=-0.9865481927458079,\qquad
 r=0.05,\qquad
 \Gamma=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z-c|=r\}.
 \label{eq:main-contour}$$ The certificate treats the round-trip binary64 values represented by the displayed decimals as exact real inputs and rounds every subsequent inequality outward. The contour obeys $$\min_{z\in\Gamma}|z|
 \ge0.9365481927458077,
 \qquad
 \max_{z\in\Gamma}\operatorname{Re}z
 =-0.9365481927458078<0.$$

[\[thm:main\]]{#thm:main label="thm:main"} For the exact operator [\[eq:continuum-operator\]](#eq:continuum-operator){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:continuum-operator"} at $\sigma=1/100$ and the exact critical parameter $u_{\mathrm c}$:

1.  $\Gamma\subset\rho(\mathcal K)$;

2.  the Riesz projection $$\Pi_\Gamma(\mathcal K)
       =\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma(z-\mathcal K)^{-1}\,dz$$ has rank one;

3.  the interior of $\Gamma$ contains one real negative algebraically simple eigenvalue $\lambda_-$; and

4.  $$\sup_{z\in\Gamma}\left\lVert (z-\mathcal K)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}
      \le148.84153451786617.$$

The proof is completed in [7](#sec:continuum-closure){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:continuum-closure"}. The reality conclusion does not require a separate numerical enclosure of the imaginary part: a real operator has conjugate-paired spectrum, while a conjugation-invariant disk of algebraic count one cannot contain a nonreal pair.

[\[cor:riesz\]]{#cor:riesz label="cor:riesz"} At $\sigma=1/100$, the simple-isolated negative-resonance premise in the full-kernel parity corollary of @WangRiesz2026 is satisfied. Hence the gauge-free weighted Nyström term for this branch has the second-order full-kernel continuum bridge proved there.

[\[rem:norm-boundary\]]{#rem:norm-boundary label="rem:norm-boundary"} is an $L^\infty$ operator theorem. It does not assert a dimension-uniform $\ell^2$ resolvent bound for the support-truncated stored matrices. This distinction is essential for a nonnormal family.

# Moment and derivative bounds for the folded kernel {#sec:derivatives}

The continuum step requires explicit row-integrated derivative bounds rather than pointwise Gaussian peaks. They follow from a truncated-normal moment inequality.

Let $T$ have density $$\pi_m(t)=\frac{\exp[-(t-m)^2/(2\sigma^2)]}
 {\int_{-1}^1\exp[-(s-m)^2/(2\sigma^2)]\,ds},
 \qquad -1\le t\le1,$$ where $m\in[-1,1]$. Folding $T$ by $t\mapsto|t|$ gives the density $g_m/Z(m)$ on $[0,1]$.

[\[lem:moment\]]{#lem:moment label="lem:moment"} For every $m\in[-1,1]$, $$\mathbb E(T-m)^2\le\sigma^2,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(T)\le\sigma^2.$$

Put $X=(T-m)/\sigma$. Its conditioning interval is $[a,b]$ with $a\le0\le b$. Since $$\int_a^b(x^2-1)e^{-x^2/2}\,dx
 =[-xe^{-x^2/2}]_a^b
 =-be^{-b^2/2}+ae^{-a^2/2}\le0,$$ the conditional expectation satisfies $\mathbb E X^2\le1$. This proves the first inequality, and variance is no larger than the second moment about $m$.

[\[lem:folded-derivatives\]]{#lem:folded-derivatives label="lem:folded-derivatives"} Write $q_m(y)=g_m(y)/Z(m)$. Then $$\begin{aligned}
 \left\lVert \partial_m q_m\right\rVert_{L^1(0,1)},
 \ \left\lVert \partial_y q_m\right\rVert_{L^1(0,1)}
 &\le \sigma^{-1},
 \label{eq:first-folded}\\
 \left\lVert \partial_{mm}q_m\right\rVert_{L^1(0,1)},
 \ \left\lVert \partial_{my}q_m\right\rVert_{L^1(0,1)},
 \ \left\lVert \partial_{yy}q_m\right\rVert_{L^1(0,1)}
 &\le2\sigma^{-2}.
 \label{eq:second-folded}\end{aligned}$$

Let $\mu=\mathbb ET$ and $v=\operatorname{Var}(T)$. Before folding, the parameter score identities are $$\partial_m\pi_m(t)=\frac{t-\mu}{\sigma^2}\pi_m(t),$$ $$\partial_{mm}\pi_m(t)
 =\frac{(t-\mu)^2-v}{\sigma^4}\pi_m(t).$$ By [\[lem:moment\]](#lem:moment){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:moment"}, Cauchy--Schwarz gives the first parameter bound and $$\left\lVert \partial_{mm}\pi_m\right\rVert_1
 \le\frac{2v}{\sigma^4}\le\frac2{\sigma^2}.$$ The target identities are $$\partial_t\pi_m(t)
 =-\frac{t-m}{\sigma^2}\pi_m(t),
\qquad
 \partial_{tt}\pi_m(t)
 =\left(\frac{(t-m)^2}{\sigma^4}-\frac1{\sigma^2}\right)\pi_m(t),$$ and $$\partial_{mt}\pi_m(t)
 =\left(
 \frac1{\sigma^2}
 -\frac{(t-m)(t-\mu)}{\sigma^4}
 \right)\pi_m(t).$$ Again [\[lem:moment\]](#lem:moment){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:moment"} and Cauchy--Schwarz give the displayed bounds. Pushforward by the folding map cannot increase total variation, so the same $L^1$ inequalities hold for $q_m$.

For $m(x)=1-u_{\mathrm c}x^2$, use the validated analytic upper $u_{\mathrm c}<1.544$. Define $$L_x=\sup_x\int_0^1|\partial_xk(x,y)|\,dy,
 \quad
 L_y=\sup_x\int_0^1|\partial_yk(x,y)|\,dy,$$ and analogously $L_{xx},L_{xy},L_{yy}$.

[\[cor:derivative-ledger\]]{#cor:derivative-ledger label="cor:derivative-ledger"} The exact continuum kernel satisfies the outward bounds $$\begin{aligned}
 L_x&\le308.80000000000024,
 &L_y&\le100.00000000000001,\\
 L_{xx}&\le191023.68000000023,
 &L_{xy}&\le61760.000000000044,\\
 L_{yy}&\le20000.000000000004.\end{aligned}$$

The chain rule and [\[lem:folded-derivatives\]](#lem:folded-derivatives){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:folded-derivatives"} give $$L_x\le2u\sigma^{-1},\qquad
 L_{xx}\le4u^2(2\sigma^{-2})+2u\sigma^{-1},$$ $$L_{xy}\le2u(2\sigma^{-2}),\qquad
 L_y\le\sigma^{-1},\qquad
 L_{yy}\le2\sigma^{-2}.$$ Insert $u=1.544$ and $\sigma=1/100$, rounding upward.

# Cell-average Galerkin and contour transport {#sec:galerkin}

Let $I_i=[ih,(i+1)h)$, $h=1/n$, and let $P_n$ be conditional expectation onto functions constant on the cells $I_i$. The finite-rank operator $$\mathcal A_n=P_n\mathcal KP_n$$ is represented on cell values by the row-stochastic matrix $$(A_n)_{ij}
 =\frac1h\int_{I_i}\int_{I_j}k(x,y)\,dy\,dx.
 \label{eq:galerkin-matrix}$$ The continuum-normalized midpoint matrix is $$(M_n)_{ij}=h\,k(x_i,y_j),
 \qquad
 x_i=y_i=\left(i+\frac12\right)h.
 \label{eq:midpoint-matrix}$$ All matrix norms in this paper are induced row-$\ell^\infty$ norms unless another norm is explicitly written.

[\[prop:defects\]]{#prop:defects label="prop:defects"} The derivative constants of [\[cor:derivative-ledger\]](#cor:derivative-ledger){reference-type="ref" reference="cor:derivative-ledger"} give $$\begin{aligned}
 \left\lVert M_n-A_n\right\rVert_\infty
 &\le\frac{h^2}{8}(L_{xx}+L_{yy}),
 \label{eq:midpoint-galerkin}\\
 \left\lVert \mathcal K-P_n\mathcal KP_n\right\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}
 &\le\frac h2(L_x+L_y).
 \label{eq:continuum-galerkin}\end{aligned}$$

For [\[eq:midpoint-galerkin\]](#eq:midpoint-galerkin){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:midpoint-galerkin"}, apply the one-dimensional midpoint remainder successively in $x$ and $y$ on every cell rectangle. The entrywise error is bounded by $h^3(L_{xx}+L_{yy})/8$; summing one matrix row over $n=1/h$ cells gives the claim.

For [\[eq:continuum-galerkin\]](#eq:continuum-galerkin){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:continuum-galerkin"}, write $$\mathcal K-P_n\mathcal KP_n=(\mathrm I-P_n)\mathcal K+P_n\mathcal K(\mathrm I-P_n).$$ Output averaging costs at most $hL_x/2$. On each input cell, $f-P_nf$ has mean zero, so subtracting a cellwise constant from $k(x,\cdot)$ and using the target derivative costs at most $hL_y/2$.

The advantage of cell averages over midpoint samples is exact nesting. Let $J,W:\mathbb C^n\to\mathbb C^{2n}$ be replication and alternating-detail injections, $$(Jv)_{2i}=(Jv)_{2i+1}=v_i,\qquad
 (Wv)_{2i}=v_i,\quad(Wv)_{2i+1}=-v_i,$$ with restrictions $R=J^*/2$ and $S=W^*/2$. In these coordinates, $$\begin{bmatrix}R\\S\end{bmatrix}
 A_{2n}[J\ W]
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 A_n&B_n\\
 C_n&D_n
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:haar-blocks}$$ The upper-left identity is exact because $P_nP_{2n}=P_n$.

[\[prop:haar\]]{#prop:haar label="prop:haar"} For a coarse mesh $h=1/n$, $$\left\lVert C_n\right\rVert_\infty\le\frac h4L_x,\qquad
 \left\lVert B_n\right\rVert_\infty\le\frac h2L_y,\qquad
 \left\lVert D_n\right\rVert_\infty\le\frac{h^2}{8}L_{xy}.
 \label{eq:haar-bounds}$$ The coarse-consistency defect in [\[eq:haar-blocks\]](#eq:haar-blocks){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:haar-blocks"} is exactly zero.

The $C_n$ block contains one signed difference between the two source half-cells, the $B_n$ block one mean-zero target-cell cancellation, and the $D_n$ block both cancellations. Integrating the corresponding first or mixed derivative over the half-cell rectangles gives [\[eq:haar-bounds\]](#eq:haar-bounds){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:haar-bounds"}. Exact coarse consistency follows from the tower property of conditional expectation.

We use two standard contour-transfer lemmas. They are stated explicitly to keep every nonnormal constant visible.

[\[lem:neumann\]]{#lem:neumann label="lem:neumann"} Let $\Gamma\subset\rho(A)$ and $$M=\sup_{z\in\Gamma}\left\lVert (z-A)^{-1}\right\rVert.$$ If $\left\lVert B-A\right\rVert\le\delta$ and $M\delta<1$, then $\Gamma\subset\rho(B)$, $$\sup_{z\in\Gamma}\left\lVert (z-B)^{-1}\right\rVert
 \le\frac{M}{1-M\delta},
 \label{eq:neumann-resolvent}$$ and the Riesz projections of $A$ and $B$ inside $\Gamma$ have the same finite rank.

Factor $z-B=(z-A)[\mathrm I-(z-A)^{-1}(B-A)]$ and use a Neumann series uniformly on the contour. The same estimate applies to the homotopy $A+t(B-A)$, $0\le t\le1$, so the Riesz rank is constant [@Kato1995].

[\[lem:schur\]]{#lem:schur label="lem:schur"} Suppose the coarse contour resolvent is bounded by $M$, and put $$\rho=\min_{z\in\Gamma}|z|,\quad
 b=\left\lVert B_n\right\rVert_\infty,\quad
 c_1=\left\lVert C_n\right\rVert_\infty,\quad
 d=\left\lVert D_n\right\rVert_\infty.$$ If $d<\rho$, set $$R_d=\frac1{\rho-d},\qquad
 \varepsilon=bR_dc_1.$$ If $M\varepsilon<1$, the fine and coarse matrices have the same count inside $\Gamma$. Moreover, with $$S=\frac{M}{1-M\varepsilon},$$ the fine resolvent is bounded by $$S+SbR_d+R_dc_1S+
 R_d+R_dc_1SbR_d.
 \label{eq:schur-resolvent}$$

Take the Schur complement of $z-D_n$. Its inverse is bounded by $R_d$, and its self-energy has norm at most $\varepsilon$. Apply [\[lem:neumann\]](#lem:neumann){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:neumann"} to the coarse Schur complement and then bound the four blocks of the inverse. For count preservation, use the homotopy $$\begin{pmatrix}A_n&tB_n\\tC_n&tD_n\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad0\le t\le1.$$ Every displayed bound is dominated by its value at $t=1$, so the contour remains in the resolvent set throughout. Formula [\[eq:schur-resolvent\]](#eq:schur-resolvent){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:schur-resolvent"} also includes the exact conversion from coarse/detail coordinates back to the fine $\ell^\infty$ coordinates.

[\[lem:zero-complement\]]{#lem:zero-complement label="lem:zero-complement"} If the Galerkin matrix $A_n$ has contour-resolvent upper $M_n$, then the finite-rank operator $\mathcal A_n=P_n\mathcal KP_n$ on all of $L^\infty$ satisfies $$\sup_{z\in\Gamma}\left\lVert (z-\mathcal A_n)^{-1}\right\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}
 \le M_n+\frac2{\rho},
 \qquad
 \rho=\min_{z\in\Gamma}|z|.
 \label{eq:zero-complement}$$

On $\operatorname{Ran}P_n$, the operator is represented by $A_n$; on $\ker P_n$ it is zero. Therefore $$(z-\mathcal A_n)^{-1}
 =J_n(z-A_n)^{-1}P_n+z^{-1}(\mathrm I-P_n),$$ where $J_n$ includes cell values as piecewise-constant functions. Since $\left\lVert P_n\right\rVert=1$ and $\left\lVert \mathrm I-P_n\right\rVert\le2$, [\[eq:zero-complement\]](#eq:zero-complement){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:zero-complement"} follows.

# The exact stored Grushin certificate {#sec:grushin}

Let $P^{\mathrm s}_{4096}$ denote the sparse Markov matrix archived in the RH-36 snapshot. In this section every stored binary64 entry is treated as an exact real number. The second stored peripheral mode supplies a center $c$, right vector $r_0$, and left vector $\ell_0$. No assumption that these are exact eigenvectors is made.

With the exactly representable scale $s=16$, define the bordered problem $$\mathcal G(z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 z\mathrm I-P^{\mathrm s}_{4096}&s r_0\\
 s^{-1}\ell_0^T&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:grushin}$$ Write its inverse blocks as $$\mathcal G(z)^{-1}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 E(z)&E_+(z)\\
 E_-(z)&E_{-+}(z)
 \end{pmatrix}.$$

[\[prop:grushin\]]{#prop:grushin label="prop:grushin"} Let $$g_- \le \ell_0^Tr_0\le g_+,\qquad
 \delta_r=\left\lVert (P^{\mathrm s}_{4096}-c\mathrm I)r_0\right\rVert_\infty,$$ $$\delta_\ell=
 \left\lVert ((P^{\mathrm s}_{4096})^T-c\mathrm I)\ell_0\right\rVert_1,
\qquad
 e_0\ge\left\lVert E(c)\right\rVert_\infty.$$ If $re_0<1$, put $$M_E=\frac{e_0}{1-re_0},$$ $$M_+=\frac{s\left\lVert r_0\right\rVert_\infty}{g_-}
      +\frac{M_Es\delta_r}{g_-},
\qquad
 M_-=\frac{\left\lVert \ell_0\right\rVert_1}{sg_-}
      +\frac{\delta_\ell M_E}{sg_-},$$ $$\varepsilon_*=\frac{M_-s\delta_r}{g_-}.$$ If $$\varepsilon_*<\frac r{g_+},
 \label{eq:rouche-gate}$$ then the disk $|z-c|<r$ contains exactly one eigenvalue of $P^{\mathrm s}_{4096}$ counted algebraically, and $$\sup_{|z-c|=r}\left\lVert (z-P^{\mathrm s}_{4096})^{-1}\right\rVert_\infty
 \le
 M_E+\frac{M_+M_-}{r/g_+-\varepsilon_*}.
 \label{eq:grushin-resolvent}$$

The bordered resolvent identity gives $$E(z)=E(c)[\mathrm I+(z-c)E(c)]^{-1}.$$ Thus $re_0<1$ keeps $\mathcal G(z)$ invertible on the closed disk and bounds the leading block by $M_E$. Applying the exact bordered inverse identities to $r_0$ and $\ell_0$ gives the channel bounds $M_+$ and $M_-$. Applying $\mathcal G(z)^{-1}$ to the candidate border column $$\begin{pmatrix}
 sr_0/g\\-(z-c)/g
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad g=\ell_0^Tr_0,$$ shows that on the boundary $$\left|E_{-+}(z)+\frac{z-c}{g}\right|
 \le\varepsilon_*.$$ Condition [\[eq:rouche-gate\]](#eq:rouche-gate){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:rouche-gate"} and scalar Rouché imply that $E_{-+}$ has one zero in the disk [@Ahlfors1979]. Since the bordered problem has no pole there, zeros of the effective scalar are eigenvalues of the original matrix with algebraic multiplicity [@SjoestrandZworski2007; @GohbergSigal1971]. Finally, $$(z-P^{\mathrm s}_{4096})^{-1}
 =E(z)-E_+(z)E_{-+}(z)^{-1}E_-(z),$$ which gives [\[eq:grushin-resolvent\]](#eq:grushin-resolvent){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:grushin-resolvent"}.

The approximate inverse of $\mathcal G(c)$ is generated by sparse LU. Its residual is then recomputed against the exact stored graph $$cX-P^{\mathrm s}_{4096}X+s r_0\alpha,
 \qquad
 s^{-1}\ell_0^TX,$$ using componentwise outward arithmetic [@Rump2010; @WangOutward2026]. In particular, the rounded sparse assembly used to generate the LU factor is not reused as the exact target.

# Consequences and theorem boundary {#sec:boundary}

The present result closes one precise gate and leaves several others untouched.

1.  **Full-kernel parity simplicity is now proved.** At $\sigma=1/100$, the negative continuum resonance required by the weighted-Riesz theorem is real, algebraically simple, and isolated.

2.  **A continuum $L^\infty$ resolvent is explicit.** The same circle carries the uniform bound $148.84153451786617$.

3.  **The exact continuum-normalized midpoint family is controlled.** supplies an explicit all-$n$ statement from $32768$ onward in the lifted $L^\infty$ norm.

4.  **Adaptive cutoff transfer is available in $L^\infty$.** Smooth discrete normalization converges at second order, while the adaptive support schedule of @WangCutoff2026 has vanishing row-operator defect. The resolvent perturbation theorem therefore transfers the isolated branch and its weighted Riesz term for all sufficiently fine adaptive grids in this norm.

5.  **No uniform Euclidean sparse-matrix theorem is claimed.** The $\ell^2$ condition required by the Euclidean cutoff proposition of @WangRiesz2026 is a different statement. Norm equivalence at a fixed dimension cannot be used uniformly as $n\to\infty$.

6.  **The noise width is fixed.** All derivative constants grow as $\sigma\downarrow0$. No small-noise or double-limit theorem follows from the present constants.

The center of the certified circle is a stored numerical datum, not a closed-form expression for $\lambda_-$. The theorem localizes the exact continuum eigenvalue only to the real interval cut out by the circle. It does not connect that eigenvalue to a zeta zero, construct a self-adjoint generator, prove a spectral counting law, or imply the Riemann hypothesis.
