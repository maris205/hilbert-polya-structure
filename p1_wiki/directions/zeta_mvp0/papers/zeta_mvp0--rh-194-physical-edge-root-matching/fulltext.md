# Question after the multiplicity correction

The full matrix state at $\sigma=0.01$ has width $m=256$. Hence one simple base eigenvalue produces ambient Frobenius Riesz rank 256, and a literal rank-one root shell is impossible [@WangRH192]. The source-cyclic restriction generated by the single matrix $S$, however, can contain one direction from that repeated eigenspace [@WangRH193].

There are therefore two sharply different explanations of the RH-185 local candidate:

1.  the four compressed roots are Ritz artifacts with no nearby physical spectrum;

2.  they are approximations to four actual base eigenvalues, viewed through source-excited matrix directions.

This paper performs the direct finite test.

# Frozen data contract

No window or contour is selected after inspecting the base spectrum. The inputs are frozen by RH-185 and RH-190:

-   scale $\sigma=0.01$;

-   packet length $L=4$;

-   starts 14--18 on the left and 12--18 on the right;

-   two-sided relative residual threshold $0.10$;

-   root-disc radius $0.4\rho\sin(\pi/4)$, where $\rho$ is the source-cycle radius of that window.

The base dimensions are 512 on the left and 256 on the right. Both matrix sources have 256 columns.

For each accepted window we reconstruct the balanced right and left frames $V,W$, the compressed matrix $$\label{eq:K}
 K=W^*\mathcal L_AV,$$ and its four eigenvalues. We then compute the complete floating spectrum of the base $A$ and solve a distinct nearest-neighbor assignment.

# Root matching and contour count

Let $\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_4$ be the packet roots and let $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_4$ be their distinct matched base eigenvalues. The matching error is $$\label{eq:matching-error}
 \epsilon_{\rm match}=\max_j|\kappa_j-\lambda_j|.$$

[\[prop:matching\]]{#prop:matching label="prop:matching"} Across the twelve accepted windows:

1.  all 48 packet roots have distinct matched base eigenvalues;

2.  every frozen root disc contains exactly one base eigenvalue;

3.  $\epsilon_{\rm match}<1.3\times10^{-3}$;

4.  all windows on one side select the same four base modes.

The final clause is important. Window motion does not chase different eigenvalues. It improves the approximation to one stable edge quartet.

Representative physical eigenvalues have the geometry $$\label{eq:quartet-geometry}
 -0.495\pm0.620i,
 \qquad
 0.474\pm0.557i,$$ with small left/right discretization differences. Their moduli form two nearby radii, approximately $0.793$ and $0.731$. This explains why the compressed roots are close to, but not exactly on, one circle.

# Why the result is compatible with RH-192

For a root disc containing one simple base eigenvalue, $$\label{eq:counts}
 N_A=1,
 \qquad N_{\mathcal L_A}=256.$$ The temporal packet count is one. It agrees with the base count and with a source-cyclic count, but not with the full Frobenius count. The finite match therefore validates the spectral location while confirming the state-type correction.

Equivalently, if $P_\lambda$ is the rank-one base spectral projector, the full ambient range is $$\label{eq:ambient-range}
 \{P_\lambda X:X\in\mathbb C^{n\times256}\},$$ of dimension 256. The source orbit selects the single matrix $P_\lambda S$ from this range.

# Source-observation residues

Let $v_\lambda,w_\lambda$ be right and left eigenvectors normalized by $w_\lambda^*v_\lambda=1$, so $P_\lambda=v_\lambda w_\lambda^*$. Define $$\label{eq:source-observation-states}
 X_\lambda=P_\lambda S,
 \qquad
 Y_\lambda=P_\lambda^*O^*.$$ Their Frobenius pairing is $$\label{eq:residue}
 \langle Y_\lambda,X_\lambda\rangle_F
 =\operatorname{tr}(OP_\lambda S)
 =:r_\lambda.$$ The same scalar is the residue at $\lambda$ of $$\label{eq:transfer}
 g(z)=\operatorname{tr}\!\left(O(zI-A)^{-1}S\right).$$

Every matched mode has $r_\lambda\ne0$ at floating precision. The minimum recorded residue modulus across repeated window records is greater than $1.1\times10^{-2}$. Therefore all four modes are both source-excited and observation-visible. RH-195 proves the exact projector/residue theorem used here without relying on diagonalizability away from the selected simple modes.

# Canonical spectral subspaces

For one side, define $$\label{eq:canonical-spaces}
 E_R=\operatorname{span}\{X_{\lambda_1},\ldots,X_{\lambda_4}\},
 \qquad
 E_L=\operatorname{span}\{Y_{\lambda_1},\ldots,Y_{\lambda_4}\}.$$ These spaces are exactly invariant under $\mathcal L_A$ and $\mathcal L_A^*$, respectively. In contrast, the temporal spaces $T_R(t),T_L(t)$ are approximate invariant Krylov windows.

For equal-dimensional subspaces $E,F$, write $$\label{eq:gap}
 \operatorname{gap}(E,F)=\sin\theta_{\max}(E,F),$$ where $\theta_{\max}$ is the largest principal angle.

[\[prop:alignment\]]{#prop:alignment label="prop:alignment"} For the accepted windows, $$\begin{aligned}
 0.0348&<\operatorname{gap}(T_R,E_R)<0.0978,\\
 0.0384&<\operatorname{gap}(T_L,E_L)<0.1133.\end{aligned}$$ The smallest gaps occur near the latest accepted windows.

The alignment is stronger information than eigenvalue proximity alone. It shows that the four-dimensional temporal state is approaching the physical source and observation mode spans, not merely reproducing four scalar roots through an ill-conditioned compression.

# Projector and transversality diagnostics

The selected base eigenvalues are nonnormal modes. Their spectral projector norms are finite but not near one: the maximum is about $17.7$. The normalized source-observation overlap $$\label{eq:normalized-overlap}
 \gamma_\lambda=
 \frac{|r_\lambda|}{\left\lVert X_\lambda\right\rVert_F\left\lVert Y_\lambda\right\rVert_F}$$ ranges down to approximately $1.13\times10^{-3}$ on the right channel and $5.46\times10^{-3}$ on the left. Thus the canonical packet is genuinely oblique. The poor cross-angle conditioning seen in RH-185 is partly physical, not solely a temporal-window artifact.

This observation also prevents an overstatement. Root matching does not automatically provide robust all-level coordinates. Residues and transversality must be controlled under refinement.

# Determinant and trace comparison

For the matched quartet define the exact finite spectral polynomial $$\label{eq:physical-determinant}
 d_E(z)=\prod_{j=1}^4(z-\lambda_j).$$ The temporal compression gives $d_T(z)=\det(zI-K)$. We store the determinant error and the trace-power differences $$\label{eq:trace-errors}
 \operatorname{tr}K^q-\sum_{j=1}^4\lambda_j^q,
 \qquad 1\le q\le8.$$ These data quantify more than pointwise root matching and will be used in RH-199 to formulate a source-channel determinant and trace ledger.

The errors decrease strongly as the temporal subspaces align, but no asymptotic rate is inferred from seven starts. The data are a finite calibration, not a convergence theorem.

# Numerical reliability and boundary

The base eigendecompositions are recomputed once per side using a standard complex nonsymmetric solver. Matching is distinct and contour counts are performed against the complete returned spectrum. The minimum distance of any computed eigenvalue from a frozen contour is stored to expose contour fragility.

What is not supplied is an interval enclosure of the matrix entries, eigenvalues, or contour winding number. Therefore "one eigenvalue per disc" is a floating finite statement. The matching errors are roughly two orders of magnitude smaller than the contour radii, which makes the signal clear, but it is not a computer-assisted proof.

Nor is the quartet yet intrinsic. It is found by comparing a late temporal window with the complete physical spectrum. A Gate-A construction must select and transport the relevant modes without target-dependent matching.

# Updated frontier

The local logical chain is now $$\begin{aligned}
 &\text{temporal packet [approximate, source-generated]}\\
 &\quad\longrightarrow\text{one genuine physical edge quartet [finite]}\\
 &\quad\longrightarrow\text{source-observation spectral channels [next]}\\
 &\quad\longrightarrow\text{canonical packet and transport [open]}.
 \end{aligned}$$ This is a meaningful advance within Gate A. It does not address the completion to a unitary/scattering object, a self-adjoint generator, the $T\log T$ law, von Mangoldt traces, zeta divisors, Hilbert--Pólya, or RH.
