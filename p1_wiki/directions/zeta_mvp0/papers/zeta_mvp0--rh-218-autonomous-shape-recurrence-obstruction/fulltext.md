# The stronger claim behind a shape clock

RH-214 establishes a finite monotone axial ordering, and RH-217 shows that the coefficient map increasingly suppresses transverse motion [@WangRH214; @WangRH217]. Neither statement implies that equal increments of log scale are generated by one map. That stronger hypothesis would be $$\label{eq:autonomous}
 x_{n+1}=F(x_n),qquad x_n=(u_n,\eta_n),$$ where $F$ is independent of $n$, channel, and scale.

An autonomous map becomes useful for Gate A only if it has more than interpolation value. It should extrapolate, remain stable as data are added, respect both channels, and ideally embed into a semigroup $F_t$ with $F_{t+s}=F_t\circ F_s$.

# Equal-log-step subsequence

To avoid confusing unequal scale increments with non-autonomy, we use the dyadic levels $$\label{eq:dyadic}
 0.04,\ 0.02,\ 0.01,\ 0.005,\ 0.0025,\ 0.00125.$$ They have constant time step $\Delta t=\log2$ for $t=\log(1/\sigma)$. The branch coordinates are:

     $\sigma$       $u_L$     $\eta_L$       $u_R$     $\eta_R$
  ----------- ----------- ------------ ----------- ------------
       $0.04$   $0.02027$   $-0.57806$   $0.02077$   $-0.59358$
       $0.02$   $0.24887$   $-0.06886$   $0.24794$   $-0.07031$
       $0.01$   $0.40529$   $-0.10443$   $0.40229$   $-0.10748$
      $0.005$   $0.54098$   $-0.08934$   $0.54034$   $-0.09080$
     $0.0025$   $0.65874$   $-0.09050$   $0.65801$   $-0.09181$
    $0.00125$   $0.71835$   $-0.09303$   $0.71750$   $-0.09444$

The first three transitions are used for training and the last two for holdout scoring. This split is deliberately harsh: it asks a map fitted through the transient to predict the mature corridor.

# Scalar axial recurrences

We test three transforms $g$ with $$\label{eq:scalarrec}
 g(u_{n+1})=a\,g(u_n)+b:$$ $$g_{\rm aff}(u)=u,qquad
 g_{\rm log}(u)=\log\frac{u}{1-u},
 \qquad
 g_{\rm gap}(u)=\log(1-u).$$

  ----------- ----------- ------------- ----------- -------------

  model         train RMS   holdout RMS   train RMS   holdout RMS
  affine        $0.00797$     $0.01644$   $0.00926$     $0.01596$
  logistic      $0.03126$     $0.11603$   $0.03226$     $0.11550$
  power gap     $0.00802$     $0.01585$   $0.00942$     $0.01589$
  ----------- ----------- ------------- ----------- -------------

Power gap wins narrowly on both holdouts, consistent with RH-215, but the error is not smaller than the observed fine-scale increments. The logistic recurrence fitted across the transient is decisively poor.

# Two-dimensional affine audit

Let $$\label{eq:affinemap}
 F(x)=Ax+b,qquad A\in\mathbb R^{2\times2},\quad b\in\mathbb R^2.$$ There are six scalar parameters. Three two-dimensional transitions provide exactly six scalar equations in generic position, so channelwise training interpolation should not be mistaken for evidence.

On the left, the fitted matrix and offset are approximately $$A_L=\begin{pmatrix}0.8505&-0.0746\\0.0731&-0.1027\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad b_L=\binom{0.1885}{-0.1297}.$$ Its training residual is $7.9\times10^{-17}$, while its two holdout state errors have maximum $0.04256$. On the right, the maximum is $0.05058$.

A pooled fit uses six training transitions and one common map: $$A_{LR}=\begin{pmatrix}0.8618&-0.0793\\0.0773&-0.1044\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad b_{LR}=\binom{0.1843}{-0.1324}.$$ Its maximum training error is $0.00205$, but its maximum holdout error is $0.04685$.

[\[prop:affinefail\]]{#prop:affinefail label="prop:affinefail"} The tested equal-step affine maps do not provide stable out-of-sample transport of the physical shape orbit: every channelwise or pooled holdout maximum exceeds $0.04$.

This is the frozen least-squares audit above. The proposition concerns only the declared affine class and split.

# Why exact finite recurrence is non-identifying

The opposite danger is to enlarge the model class until interpolation is guaranteed.

[\[thm:interpolation\]]{#thm:interpolation label="thm:interpolation"} Let $$x_k=(u_k,\eta_k)\in\mathbb R^2,qquad k=0,\ldots,n,$$ and suppose $u_0,\ldots,u_{n-1}$ are distinct. Then there exists an autonomous polynomial map $F:\mathbb R^2\to\mathbb R^2$ of the form $$F(u,\eta)=(P(u),Q(u))$$ with degrees at most $n-1$ such that $$F(x_k)=x_{k+1},\qquad k=0,\ldots,n-1.$$

Let $L_k(u)$ be the Lagrange basis polynomials for the distinct nodes $u_0,\ldots,u_{n-1}$. Define $$P(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}u_{k+1}L_k(u),
 \qquad
 Q(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\eta_{k+1}L_k(u).$$ Then $P(u_k)=u_{k+1}$ and $Q(u_k)=\eta_{k+1}$.

[\[cor:noidentify\]]{#cor:noidentify label="cor:noidentify"} The mere existence of an autonomous map fitting a finite monotone shape orbit provides no evidence for a physical semigroup.

For each six-state physical channel, Theorem [\[thm:interpolation\]](#thm:interpolation){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:interpolation"} gives a degree-four map fitting all five transitions. The numerical construction has maximum residual below $2\times10^{-15}$.

# What would identify a semigroup

A meaningful positive result would require constraints not supplied by finite interpolation, for example:

1.  a map fixed before additional scales are computed;

2.  a uniform one-step error tending to zero;

3.  consistency across both channels and alternative discretizations;

4.  a composition estimate comparing one double step with two single steps;

5.  compactness or regularity sufficient to extract a limiting map;

6.  for a continuous generator, embeddability into $F_t$.

The current data satisfy none of these at theorem level. Exact polynomial interpolation actually underscores why such additional structure is needed.

# Route decision and claim boundary

Further high-degree recurrence fitting to the same fixed quartet is deprioritized. The next question is structural: even if one perfectly controlled this four-root orbit, could it generate the growing zero count required of an infinite determinant? RH-219 answers that question.

This paper identifies no scale semigroup, continuous generator, Fredholm operator, or Gate-A closure. Gates B--E and all zeta/RH statements remain untouched.
