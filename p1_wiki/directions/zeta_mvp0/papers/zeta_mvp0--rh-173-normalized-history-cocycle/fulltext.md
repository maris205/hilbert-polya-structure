# From a static realization to time evolution

Let $A\in\mathcal B(\mathcal Y)$, let $S_0:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y$, and set $S_t=A^tS_0\ne0$. With $\alpha_t=\left\lVert S_t\right\rVert_F$ and $\widehat S_t=S_t/\alpha_t$, RH-172 defines $$F_tx=(\widehat S_tx,\sqrt\eta\widehat S_{t-1}x,
 \ldots,\eta^{t/2}\widehat S_0x)
 \in\mathcal K_t:=\mathcal Y^{\oplus(t+1)}$$ and proves $M_t=F_t^*F_t$ [@WangRH172]. Since $\mathcal K_t$ changes with $t$, the update is not an ordinary power of one finite square matrix. It is a nonautonomous rectangular cocycle.

The normalization matters. Although $S_{t+1}=AS_t$, generally $\widehat S_{t+1}\ne A\widehat S_t$. The missing scalar is $$\label{eq:ratio}
 r_t=\frac{\alpha_t}{\alpha_{t+1}},
 \qquad
 \widehat S_{t+1}=r_tA\widehat S_t.$$

# The rectangular history cocycle

Define $T_t:\mathcal K_t\to\mathcal K_{t+1}$ by $$\label{eq:cocycle}
 T_t(y_0,y_1,\ldots,y_t)
 =\bigl(r_tAy_0,\sqrt\eta y_0,\sqrt\eta y_1,
 \ldots,\sqrt\eta y_t\bigr).$$

[\[thm:factor-cocycle\]]{#thm:factor-cocycle label="thm:factor-cocycle"} For every $t\ge0$, $$\label{eq:exact-intertwining}
 F_{t+1}=T_tF_t.$$ Consequently $$\label{eq:memory-from-cocycle}
 M_{t+1}=F_t^*T_t^*T_tF_t.$$

The first block of $T_tF_tx$ is $r_tA\widehat S_tx=\widehat S_{t+1}x$ by [\[eq:ratio\]](#eq:ratio){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:ratio"}. Every remaining block is the corresponding block of $F_t x$ multiplied by $\sqrt\eta$. This is exactly the ordered definition of $F_{t+1}x$. Taking adjoints gives [\[eq:memory-from-cocycle\]](#eq:memory-from-cocycle){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:memory-from-cocycle"}.

The theorem does more than restate the scalar memory recursion. It identifies the full analysis operator that transports every source vector, including phase and cross-coordinate information discarded by $M_t$ alone.

# Adjoint, norm, and lower bound

For $z=(z_0,\ldots,z_{t+1})\in\mathcal K_{t+1}$, direct calculation gives $$\label{eq:adjoint}
 T_t^*z=
 \bigl(r_tA^*z_0+\sqrt\eta z_1,
 \sqrt\eta z_2,\ldots,\sqrt\eta z_{t+1}\bigr).$$

[\[thm:singular\]]{#thm:singular label="thm:singular"} Relative to $\mathcal K_t=\mathcal Y\oplus\mathcal Y^{\oplus t}$, $$\label{eq:tstar-t}
 T_t^*T_t=
 \operatorname{diag}\bigl(r_t^2A^*A+\eta I,
 \eta I,\ldots,\eta I\bigr).$$ Hence $$\label{eq:norm}
 \left\lVert T_t\right\rVert=\sqrt{r_t^2\left\lVert A\right\rVert^2+\eta}.$$ If $t\ge1$, then $$\label{eq:lower}
 \left\lVert T_ty\right\rVert\ge\sqrt\eta\left\lVert y\right\rVert
 \quad\text{for every }y\in\mathcal K_t.$$ For $t=0$, the least singular value is $\sqrt{r_t^2s_{\min}(A)^2+\eta}$.

Using [\[eq:cocycle\]](#eq:cocycle){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:cocycle"}, $$\left\lVert T_ty\right\rVert^2
 =r_t^2\left\lVert Ay_0\right\rVert^2+\eta\sum_{j=0}^t\left\lVert y_j\right\rVert^2.$$ There are no cross terms because the outputs occupy distinct direct-sum coordinates. This proves [\[eq:tstar-t\]](#eq:tstar-t){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:tstar-t"}. Its largest eigenvalue is $r_t^2\left\lVert A\right\rVert^2+\eta$. When $t\ge1$, the later diagonal blocks have eigenvalue $\eta$, while the first block is bounded below by $\eta I$. The stated formulas follow.

Thus every positive forgetting weight regularizes the finite rectangular map: $T_t$ is injective with closed range even when $A$ has a kernel. This finite-time fact should not be confused with compactness of an infinite history completion; RH-175 will show that the latter contains a unilateral shift obstruction.

# Transported packets and two directional defects

Let $P_t$ be a rank-$r$ reset packet for $M_t$, and let $J_t:\operatorname{Ran}P_t\to\mathcal K_t$ be its canonical polar realization. Choose orthonormal frames $V_t$ and $V_{t+1}$ for the realized ranges. Define the reduced cocycle $$\label{eq:reduced}
 B_t=V_{t+1}^*T_tV_t.$$ The two natural residuals are $$\begin{aligned}
 R_t^{\rm pr}&=(I-V_{t+1}V_{t+1}^*)T_tV_t,
 \label{eq:primal}\\
 R_t^{\rm ad}&=(I-V_tV_t^*)T_t^*V_{t+1}.
 \label{eq:adjoint-residual}\end{aligned}$$ The first asks whether the old packet transports into the new packet. The second asks whether the new packet pulls back into the old packet. For a nonnormal or rectangular map these are independent. A small primal defect does not imply a small adjoint defect.

[\[prop:reduction\]]{#prop:reduction label="prop:reduction"} The identities $$T_tV_t=V_{t+1}B_t,
 \qquad
 T_t^*V_{t+1}=V_tB_t^*$$ hold simultaneously if and only if $R_t^{\rm pr}=R_t^{\rm ad}=0$. In that case the two realized packet ranges form a reducing pair for the rectangular cocycle.

Orthogonal decomposition of $T_tV_t$ into the range of $V_{t+1}$ and its orthogonal complement gives the first equivalence. The coefficient of the range component is $V_{t+1}^*T_tV_t=B_t$. Applying the same argument to the adjoint gives the second equivalence and coefficient $B_t^*$.

The Gram recursion alone does not imply either residual vanishes. At time $t+1$, the reset packet optimizes the new quadratic form $M_{t+1}$; it is not defined as the image of the old packet under $T_t$.

# An exact ninety-degree reset counterexample

Take $\mathcal X=\mathcal Y=\mathbb C^2$, choose $0<\varepsilon<1$, and set $$S_0=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&\varepsilon\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 A=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon&0\\0&\varepsilon^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.$$ Then $$S_1=AS_0=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.$$ For every $0\le\eta<1$, the rank-one top packet of $M_0=\widehat S_0^*\widehat S_0$ is $\mathbb Ce_1$, while the rank-one top packet of $M_1=\widehat S_1^*\widehat S_1+\eta M_0$ is $\mathbb Ce_2$.

In the preceding example, the canonical realized range of the old top packet after transport by $T_0$ is orthogonal to the canonical realized range of the new top packet. Therefore $$\left\lVert P_{T_0V_0}-P_{V_1}\right\rVert=1.$$

All matrices are diagonal. The image of the first source coordinate occupies only first-coordinate output axes in both history blocks, whereas the image of the second source coordinate occupies only second-coordinate axes. The two history columns are orthogonal. Polar normalization changes their lengths but not their ranges. Orthogonal rank-one projections have operator distance one.

The counterexample is not a statement about the physical frozen models. It shows that no theorem can deduce packet invariance solely from positivity, the memory recursion, and exact factor evolution. Additional dynamical structure is indispensable.

# Block-time cocycle and an explicit long-step formula

The one-step maps compose without ambiguity even though their domains change. For integers $s<t$, define $$T_{t:s}=T_{t-1}T_{t-2}\cdots T_s:\mathcal K_s\to\mathcal K_t.$$

[\[prop:block-time\]]{#prop:block-time label="prop:block-time"} For every $0\le s<t$, $$\label{eq:block-factor}
 F_t=T_{t:s}F_s.$$ Writing $n=t-s$, $q=\sqrt\eta$, and $y=(y_0,y_1,\ldots,y_s)$, the first $n+1$ output blocks of $T_{t:s}y$ are $$\label{eq:block-formula}
 q^j\frac{\alpha_s}{\alpha_{t-j}}A^{n-j}y_0,
 \qquad 0\le j\le n,$$ and the remaining blocks are $$\label{eq:old-tail}
 q^ny_1,q^ny_2,\ldots,q^ny_s.$$

Equation [\[eq:block-factor\]](#eq:block-factor){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:block-factor"} follows by iterating Theorem [\[thm:factor-cocycle\]](#thm:factor-cocycle){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:factor-cocycle"}. For the explicit blocks, induction shows that the $j$th newly generated block contains $j$ shifts and $n-j$ first-block updates. The product of normalization ratios telescopes: $$r_s r_{s+1}\cdots r_{t-j-1}
 =\frac{\alpha_s}{\alpha_{t-j}}.$$ Every old tail block is shifted exactly $n$ times, giving [\[eq:old-tail\]](#eq:old-tail){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:old-tail"}.

The formula separates two mechanisms: physical propagation by powers of $A$, and memory aging by powers of $q$. It also gives a type-correct starting point for lagged or block packets. Such packets need not inherit the severe one-step adjoint defect observed later, because their reduction target can be a whole product $T_{t:s}$ rather than one $T_s$.

# From primal residual to subspace distance

Let $W=T_tV_t$ and assume $W$ has full column rank. Its polar frame is $\widehat W=W(W^*W)^{-1/2}$. The projection distance from the transported range to the new packet is $$d_t=\left\lVert(I-V_{t+1}V_{t+1}^*)\widehat W\right\rVert.$$

[\[prop:distance\]]{#prop:distance label="prop:distance"} With $R_t^{\rm pr}$ from [\[eq:primal\]](#eq:primal){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:primal"}, $$\label{eq:distance-bound}
 d_t\le
 \frac{\left\lVert R_t^{\rm pr}\right\rVert}{s_{\min}(W)}
 =\epsilon_t^{\rm pr}\,\kappa(W),$$ where $\epsilon_t^{\rm pr}=\left\lVert R_t^{\rm pr}\right\rVert/\left\lVert W\right\rVert$ and $\kappa(W)=\left\lVert W\right\rVert/s_{\min}(W)$.

Since $\widehat W=W(W^*W)^{-1/2}$, $$(I-V_{t+1}V_{t+1}^*)\widehat W
 =R_t^{\rm pr}(W^*W)^{-1/2}.$$ The inverse positive factor has norm $s_{\min}(W)^{-1}$. Taking norms gives the first inequality; multiplying and dividing by $\left\lVert W\right\rVert$ gives the second.

Thus a small normalized primal residual need not force a small subspace distance unless the transported packet is also well conditioned. Conversely, the adjoint residual is not controlled by [\[eq:distance-bound\]](#eq:distance-bound){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:distance-bound"} at all. This quantitative separation motivates recording all three diagnostics in the physical audit rather than treating one as a proxy for the others.

# Finite identity audit

The implementation audit uses dimensions $2,3,5,8$, input history lengths $1,2,4,7$, and ten random trials per configuration, for 160 cases. It compares explicit rectangular matrices with blockwise action and adjoint formulas, and compares numerical singular values with Theorem [\[thm:singular\]](#thm:singular){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:singular"}.

  quantity                                   maximum residual
  ------------------------------------ ----------------------
  matrix action                          $1.48\times10^{-15}$
  adjoint action                         $4.45\times10^{-16}$
  largest singular value                 $1.34\times10^{-15}$
  smallest singular value                $5.00\times10^{-16}$
  counterexample projection distance         $1.000000000000$

These are implementation checks, not physical interval certificates.

# Route consequence and boundary

RH-172 and RH-173 now provide exact finite history kinematics: $$M_t=F_t^*F_t,
 \qquad F_{t+1}=T_tF_t,
 \qquad T_t^*T_t\text{ explicit}.$$ The next executable question is empirical but type-correct: on the 130 archived physical reset snapshots, how large are the transported packet distance and the two directional residuals? That is the task of RH-174.

Nothing here maps $\mathcal K_t$ into the RH-80 transfer space. The maps $T_t$ are rectangular and time dependent, so they do not themselves possess a standard Fredholm determinant. No all-level autonomous completion, Riesz cloud, Schatten limit, self-adjoint generator, zeta divisor, or Riemann-hypothesis claim is made.
