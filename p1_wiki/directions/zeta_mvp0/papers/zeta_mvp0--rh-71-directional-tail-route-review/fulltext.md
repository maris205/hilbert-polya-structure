# Introduction

The post-RH-50 roadmap placed the program inside Stage A: construct a canonical small-noise intrinsic determinant before discussing scattering, self-adjointness, counting, or arithmetic trace identities [@WangRoadmap2026]. The immediate obstruction was a pair of directional Hardy energies. Subsequent work closed the sufficient peripheral-factor transfer, the analytic adaptive cutoff transfer, and a conditional intrinsic-identification composition theorem [@WangFactorTransfer2026; @WangIdentification2026; @WangStrongWeak2026]. The uniform Hardy input itself remained open.

RH-62--RH-70 then followed the tail mechanism through nine local layers. The exploration was not monotone: some layers strengthened an upper, some identified a necessary cost, and some proved exact no-go results. RH-70 finally executed a complete outward-rounded terminal audit on the frozen production matrices [@WangFrozen2026].

This review has two purposes. First, it extracts a theorem-level composition from the local mechanisms. Second, it prevents the statement that RH-70 is green from being overextended. A frozen terminal certificate and an end-to-end physical-family theorem are different statements.

## Main conclusions {#main-conclusions .unnumbered}

1.  Terminal candidate selection and an upstream transfer-difference certificate form a simple, rigorous Hilbert-space stack.

2.  The RH-70 balls leave certified finite-scale room for such a bridge, although the one-percent room is narrow at two middle scales.

3.  The finite-scale open frontier has one gate; Stage A1 has two.

4.  Additional terminal-tail variants are lower priority than upstream inclusion and uniform family scaling.

5.  The Hilbert--Polya-oriented Stages B--D remain logically untouched.

# Certificate-stack closure {#sec:stack}

Let $$\mathcal H=\ell^2\!\left(\mathbb N_0;\mathbb C^{t\times s}\right),
 \qquad
 \left\lVert K\right\rVert_{\mathcal H}^2=\sum_{m\ge0}\left\lVert K_m\right\rVert_{\mathrm F}^2.$$ For a physical or analytic transfer triple $(A,X,Y)$, the coefficients are $K_m=YA^mX$. Let $\widehat K$ denote coefficients from one frozen finite-matrix triple.

At a fixed scale, terminal candidate $c$ carries a valid upper $U_c$ for $\left\lVert\widehat K\right\rVert_{\mathcal H}$ and a nonnegative cost vector $b_c$. RH-69 formalized Pareto pruning of these candidates [@WangPortfolio2026].

[\[thm:stack\]]{#thm:stack label="thm:stack"} Suppose $$\left\lVert\widehat K\right\rVert_{\mathcal H}\le U_c
 \quad(c\in\mathcal C),
 \qquad
 \left\lVert K-\widehat K\right\rVert_{\mathcal H}\le\Delta.$$ Then:

1.  $$\left\lVert K\right\rVert_{\mathcal H}\le \min_{c\in\mathcal C}U_c+\Delta.$$

2.  If $\Delta$ is candidate independent, replacing each $U_c$ by $U_c+\Delta$ leaves every upper-and-cost Pareto dominance relation, and hence the Pareto frontier, unchanged.

3.  Let $F$ be the frozen finite-prefix norm. If interval data give $F^-\le F$ and $\min_cU_c\le U^+$, then $$\Delta\le (1+\tau)F^- - U^+
      \label{eq:slack}$$ is sufficient for $\left\lVert K\right\rVert_{\mathcal H}\le(1+\tau)F$ whenever the right side is nonnegative.

4.  If $\min_cU_c=O(\log(1/\sigma)^a)$ and $\Delta=O(\log(1/\sigma)^b)$, then $$\left\lVert K\right\rVert_{\mathcal H}
      =O\!\left(\log(1/\sigma)^{\max(a,b)}\right).$$

The triangle inequality in $\mathcal H$ gives $$\left\lVert K\right\rVert_{\mathcal H}\le\left\lVert\widehat K\right\rVert_{\mathcal H}
                  +\left\lVert K-\widehat K\right\rVert_{\mathcal H}
 \le U_c+\Delta$$ for every $c$; minimizing proves (i). Adding the same scalar to every upper does not change any pairwise upper inequality, so all dominance relations are preserved, proving (ii). Under [\[eq:slack\]](#eq:slack){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:slack"}, $$\left\lVert K\right\rVert_{\mathcal H}\le U^++\Delta
 \le(1+\tau)F^-\le(1+\tau)F,$$ which is (iii). The sum of two polylogarithmic quantities has the larger of the two exponents, proving (iv).

If the bridge changes with the terminal representation, candidate $c$ must carry total upper $U_c+\Delta_c$ and any new bridge cost. Pareto pruning is then rerun on the augmented candidate; the common-bridge invariance does not apply.

Equation [\[eq:slack\]](#eq:slack){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:slack"} is useful for designing a sharp finite-scale validation. Stage A1 itself asks for an asymptotic upper compatible with the RH-54 exponent budget. It does not require one-percent agreement with a frozen diagnostic.

## Positive chain

RH-62 and RH-63 replaced a worst-direction norm envelope by exact residual identities and coherent recursive closure [@WangKrylov2026; @WangNested2026]. RH-64 moved the terminal remainder into a positive Lyapunov metric [@WangWeighted2026]. RH-66 then preserved cancellation across source columns with a block Galerkin identity and coefficient-aware Grams [@WangBlockGram2026]. ## No-go results and correction

RH-65 proves that canonical full-space Lyapunov weighting can become polynomially or super-polynomially ill-conditioned when nilpotent coupling exceeds the peripheral gap scale [@WangConditioning2026]. This closes a global shortcut, not localized residual weighting.

RH-68 proves that perfect stability and metric conditioning do not imply a universal fixed block depth: normalized Krylov vectors can be a Fourier basis [@WangDepth2026]. This closes fixed depth from stability data alone, not physical phase compression or a controlled growing depth.

RH-67 also corrected the RH-66 cancellation diagnostic. Forming a large Gram in the original coefficient coordinates produced the archived binary64 gain $410.16$; factor-first formation and exact scalar reduction give the mathematical isotropic gain $2.92454$. The RH-66 identities and upper theorems were unchanged. The correction is part of the durable record, not an inconvenient outlier.

# First-open dependency frontiers {#sec:frontier}

To avoid conflating a finite audit with a family theorem, we encode the remaining claims as a finite acyclic prerequisite graph.

Let $\mathcal G$ be a finite acyclic graph whose nodes close monotonically once all named prerequisites close. Given a closed set $\mathcal C$ and a target $v$, recursively descend through every nonclosed prerequisite. If a nonclosed node has no nonclosed prerequisite, retain it. The resulting antichain is the first-open frontier $\mathfrak F(v;\mathcal C)$.

[\[prop:frontier\]]{#prop:frontier label="prop:frontier"} Relative to a fixed prerequisite graph:

1.  every unresolved derivation branch from $v$ reaches a node of $\mathfrak F(v;\mathcal C)$;

2.  closing all nodes of the frontier permits closure to propagate upward until either $v$ closes or a new first-open frontier is exposed;

3.  no proof following only the named dependency edges can close $v$ while leaving a current frontier node open.

Topologically order the finite graph. Recursive descent must terminate at a closed node or a first nonclosed node with no nonclosed prerequisite, proving (i). Upward induction in the reverse topological order proves (ii). Statement (iii) is the contrapositive of the monotone prerequisite rule.

This proposition is bookkeeping relative to the declared graph; it does not prove that no future theorem can bypass a named node. Its value is claim hygiene.

The current graph contains the closed terminal frozen upper and the closed augmented difference identity from RH-70. The difference *instance* still requires an interval-enclosed upstream triple. A finite-scale end-to-end bound requires the terminal upper and that bridge. Stage A1 then additionally requires a uniform family-scaling theorem.

[\[cor:two-frontiers\]]{#cor:two-frontiers label="cor:two-frontiers"} For the archived dependency graph, $$\mathfrak F(\text{finite-scale Hardy})
 =\{\text{upstream interval triple}\},$$ whereas $$\mathfrak F(\text{Stage A1})
 =\{
 \text{upstream interval triple},
 \text{uniform family scaling}
 \}.$$

The same two nodes are inherited by unconditional Stage A4 because the peripheral-factor transfer, adaptive cutoff transfer, and conditional identification composition theorems are already green in their stated scopes. The renormalized determinant target has one additional future node: the pole-renormalized limit theorem itself.

## Immediate priority

The next productive target is an upstream interval triple, not another terminal-tail formula:

1.  enclose folded-Gaussian row construction and normalization;

2.  exploit the exact row-stochastic Perron right vector;

3.  validate the stationary left vector and parity Riesz pair by residual, primal--dual, or contour methods;

4.  enclose the rank-two bulk subtraction and coarse/detail source and observation blocks;

5.  execute the RH-70 augmented difference certificate.

A direct interval eigendecomposition is not compulsory. The contour, residual, and low-rank certification technology developed earlier in the series can validate only the two peripheral factors needed by the bulk subtraction.

## Continuation, fallback, and stopping signals

Continue if the upstream bridge is finite with cost compatible with the RH-54 quarter-power ledger and if one Hardy radius survives on the intended dyadic schedules. The next analytic theorem must control horizon, phase support, or effective rank; RH-68 forbids assuming fixed depth from stability alone.

Fallbacks remain legitimate:

-   relax the one-percent engineering target;

-   use strong--weak analytic perturbation rather than a complete interval eigensolver;

-   permit a proved growing block depth;

-   use localized weighted residuals after deflation rather than a global metric.

A decisive stopping signal for the present Stage-A route would be a validated proof of no common Hardy radius, an unavoidable combined energy exponent greater than $1/4$ under every admissible mesh regime, or super-polynomial directional/upstream growth with no canonical renormalization. Missing the one-percent finite-scale target alone is not such a signal.

# Position on the Hilbert--Polya-oriented route {#sec:hp}

The current result is still inside the small-noise transfer-determinant stage. It neither constructs nor suggests declaring the noisy Markov operator to be self-adjoint. Stage A5 is the pole-renormalized determinant limit; only after it closes do Stages B--D retain their original order:

1.  close the pole-renormalized small-noise determinant;

2.  construct a canonical order-sensitive scattering or inner object;

3.  derive a self-adjoint realization and its intrinsic $T\log T$ counting law;

4.  derive a target-independent prime-power trace formula;

5.  prove a completed-zeta identity with multiplicities and no spurious or missing levels;

6.  only then ask for a Riemann Hypothesis implication.

The independent TPC branch may contribute arithmetic techniques, but there is still no interface theorem preserving test functions, weights, multiplicities, normalization, and limiting errors. Shared terminology does not create such an interface.

# Conclusion

The RH-62--RH-71 block has a coherent outcome. Residual identities, nested closure, localized weighting, block fusion, covariance adaptation, lower gates, portfolio selection, and exact-dyadic execution now form a composable certificate stack. The terminal frozen calculation survives outward rounding and leaves positive room for an upstream bridge on every archived row.

Two broad shortcuts are definitively too strong: global weighting and universal fixed depth. Their failure narrows rather than ends the route. The next finite-scale wall is upstream inclusion; the next asymptotic wall is uniform family scaling. Keeping those two gates separate is the main route correction of this review.
