# Why a channel needs both source and observation

The source-cyclic space removes directions that cannot be reached from $S$. It may still contain modes invisible to the physical observation. A spectral packet intended for determinant or trace data must therefore be typed by both ends of the experiment.

Let $A\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}$, $S\in\mathbb C^{n\times m}$, and $O\in\mathbb C^{m\times n}$. The scalar transfer function is $$\label{eq:transfer}
 g(z)=\operatorname{tr}\!\left(O(zI-A)^{-1}S\right).$$ For large $z$ it has the moment expansion $$\label{eq:moment-expansion}
 g(z)=\sum_{q\ge0}\frac{\operatorname{tr}(OA^qS)}{z^{q+1}}.$$ This is precisely the Frobenius source-observation transfer preserved by the RH-193 cyclic restriction.

# Riesz projectors and matrix states

Let $\Gamma$ be a positively oriented contour in the resolvent set of $A$ and define $$\label{eq:riesz}
 P=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma(zI-A)^{-1}\,dz.$$ Then $P^2=P$ and $PA=AP$. On the Frobenius space let $\mathcal L_AX=AX$.

[\[prop:source-channel\]]{#prop:source-channel label="prop:source-channel"} The state $$\label{eq:right-state}
 X_P=PS$$ belongs to the source-cyclic space and satisfies $$\label{eq:right-channel-action}
 \mathcal L_A^qX_P=P A^qS
 \quad(q\ge0).$$ The cyclic space generated by $X_P$ is the part of the source orbit whose base spectrum lies inside $\Gamma$.

Since $P$ is a polynomial in $A$ in finite dimension, $PS$ belongs to the span of powers of $A$ applied to $S$. Commutation gives the orbit identity.

The dual state is $$\label{eq:left-state}
 Y_P=P^*O^*.$$ It is generated by the adjoint dynamics and the observation seed.

# The residue pairing

[\[thm:residue\]]{#thm:residue label="thm:residue"} For every Riesz projector $P$, $$\label{eq:pairing-residue}
 \boxed{
 \langle Y_P,X_P\rangle_F
 =\operatorname{tr}(OPS).
 }$$ Moreover $$\label{eq:contour-residue}
 \operatorname{tr}(OPS)
 =\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma g(z)\,dz.$$

Using $P^2=P$, $$\langle P^*O^*,PS\rangle_F
 =\operatorname{tr}\big((P^*O^*)^*PS\big)
 =\operatorname{tr}(OP^2S)
 =\operatorname{tr}(OPS).$$ Insert the Riesz integral and use linearity and cyclicity of the trace to obtain [\[eq:contour-residue\]](#eq:contour-residue){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:contour-residue"}.

This identity is invariant under eigenvector scaling and does not require a choice of basis inside the spectral subspace.

# Disjoint channels

Let $P_1,\ldots,P_r$ be Riesz projectors for pairwise disjoint spectral sets. Then $P_iP_j=0$ for $i\ne j$.

[\[thm:cross-diagonal\]]{#thm:cross-diagonal label="thm:cross-diagonal"} Put $X_j=P_jS$, $Y_i=P_i^*O^*$, and $r_i=\operatorname{tr}(OP_iS)$. Then $$\label{eq:cross-diagonal}
 \boxed{\langle Y_i,X_j\rangle_F=\delta_{ij}r_i.}$$

Directly, $$\langle Y_i,X_j\rangle_F
 =\operatorname{tr}(OP_iP_jS)
 =\delta_{ij}\operatorname{tr}(OP_iS).$$

Thus the observation automatically supplies the correct dual annihilation between distinct spectral channels. Poor conditioning can remain because $|r_i|$ may be small relative to $\left\lVert X_i\right\rVert\left\lVert Y_i\right\rVert$, but there is no off-diagonal algebraic contamination.

# Simple eigenvalues

Suppose $\lambda$ is simple, with right and left eigenvectors normalized by $w_\lambda^*v_\lambda=1$. Then $$\label{eq:rank-one-projector}
 P_\lambda=v_\lambda w_\lambda^*,$$ and $$\begin{aligned}
 X_\lambda&=v_\lambda(w_\lambda^*S),\\
 Y_\lambda&=w_\lambda(Ov_\lambda)^*.\end{aligned}$$ Both are single matrix vectors even though the full Frobenius eigenspace has dimension $m$.

[\[cor:eigenstates\]]{#cor:eigenstates label="cor:eigenstates"} For a simple eigenvalue, $$\label{eq:eigenstate-actions}
 \mathcal L_AX_\lambda=\lambda X_\lambda,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L_A^*Y_\lambda=\overline\lambda Y_\lambda.$$ The scalar residue is $$\label{eq:simple-residue}
 r_\lambda=(w_\lambda^*S)(Ov_\lambda).$$

The mode is source-observable precisely when $r_\lambda\ne0$. For a simple mode this implies both $P_\lambda S\ne0$ and $P_\lambda^*O^*\ne0$.

# Residue-normalized biorthogonality

Choose simple disjoint eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r$ with $r_i\ne0$. Define columns in the Frobenius state by $$\label{eq:raw-frames}
 V_i=X_i,
 \qquad
 W_i=\frac{Y_i}{\overline{r_i}}.$$

[\[thm:packet\]]{#thm:packet label="thm:packet"} The frames $V=[V_1,\ldots,V_r]$ and $W=[W_1,\ldots,W_r]$ satisfy $$\label{eq:biorthogonality}
 W^*V=I_r,$$ and $$\label{eq:exact-compression}
 \mathcal L_AV=V\Lambda,
 \qquad
 \mathcal L_A^*W=W\Lambda^*,
 \qquad
 W^*\mathcal L_AV=\Lambda,$$ where $\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)$.

Equation [\[eq:cross-diagonal\]](#eq:cross-diagonal){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:cross-diagonal"} gives $W^*V=I$. The eigenstate actions hold columnwise, and compression follows.

The packet has zero right and left residual. Its rank is the number of selected source-observable channels, not the full ambient Riesz rank.

# Transfer partial fractions

If $A$ has a complete simple spectrum, summing its rank-one projectors gives $I$ and $$\label{eq:resolvent-partial-fractions}
 (zI-A)^{-1}=\sum_j\frac{P_j}{z-\lambda_j}.$$ Taking the source-observation trace yields $$\label{eq:transfer-partial-fractions}
 \boxed{g(z)=\sum_j\frac{r_j}{z-\lambda_j}.}$$ Zero residues are exact pole cancellations in the scalar transfer function. The minimal source-observable realization keeps precisely the noncanceled Jordan data; in the simple case it keeps the indices with $r_j\ne0$.

For a selected subset, equation [\[eq:transfer-partial-fractions\]](#eq:transfer-partial-fractions){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:transfer-partial-fractions"} splits into the packet contribution and a complementary transfer remainder. This is a transfer decomposition, not a claim that the full Frobenius determinant factors with rank $r$.

# Complex nonnormal identity audit

The implementation uses dimensions 2--9, source widths 1--4, and five independent trials per pair, giving 160 cases. Each test matrix is similar to a diagonal matrix with distinct complex eigenvalues and bounded similarity condition.

The audit checks:

1.  resolution of the identity by computed projectors;

2.  idempotence and cross-projector annihilation;

3.  equality of channel pairing and residue;

4.  diagonal cross-channel Gram matrix;

5.  residue-normalized biorthogonality;

6.  equality of the direct transfer solve and modal partial fractions.

All 160 cases pass the $10^{-9}$ aggregate tolerance.

# Physical interpretation of RH-194

RH-194 finds four simple source-observable modes on each side at $\sigma=0.01$ [@WangRH194]. The present theorem upgrades their interpretation: $$\text{one temporal root}
 \quad\rightsquigarrow\quad
 (P_\lambda S,\,P_\lambda^*O^*,\,r_\lambda).$$ This triple is basis-free and tied to the physical input-output experiment. It is the correct finite rank-one channel.

The raw residue normalization can be poorly conditioned. RH-196 constructs balanced coordinates on the same exact right/left spectral subspaces and proves their optimal cross-angle conditioning.

# Boundary of the theorem

The Riesz projectors are exact finite objects when the contours are known. The physical projectors in RH-194 are presently computed by floating eigenvectors, not interval contour integration. The theorem also supplies no lower bound on $|r_\lambda|$ or its normalized overlap under refinement.

Finally, selecting a spectral quartet by first seeing its eigenvalues is not yet an intrinsic dynamical construction. Gate A requires a rule that finds and transports the channel without importing the desired spectral answer. Gates B--E and all claims concerning zeta zeros or RH remain untouched.
