# Input and scope

The exact primary types, written as exponent partitions, are $$C_{(2)}:(4,3,2,2,2,1^{10}),\qquad C_{(3)}:(2,1),$$ $$D:(3,1,1),\qquad K_{(2)}:(4,2,2,2,1^8),\qquad K_{(3)}:(2,1).$$ Thus $C=D\oplus K$. C69 constructed one such decomposition and showed that the fixed $D$ has $2^{41}$ complements. Here we classify all direct factors and all decompositions of these types.

The scope firewall is $$\texttt{NO\_BAD\_EULER\_OR\_ROOT\_NUMBER}.$$ We do not claim a canonical decomposition, a full table of marks or Burnside ring, arithmetic or local data, Euler factors, root numbers, automorphy, or a Hilbert--Polya operator.

# Automorphism and Hom counts

For a finite abelian $p$-group of exponent type $\lambda$, write $\lambda'$ for the conjugate partition and $m_s(\lambda)$ for the multiplicity of $s$. Then $$\label{eq:aut}
 |\operatorname{Aut}(G_\lambda)|=
 p^{\sum_r(\lambda'_r)^2}
 \prod_s\prod_{j=1}^{m_s(\lambda)}(1-p^{-j}).$$ Indeed, the first power counts endomorphisms. Reduction on the summands of each exact exponent $s$ must be invertible; its relative density is the corresponding product. We also use $$\label{eq:hom}
 |\operatorname{Hom}(G_\lambda,G_\mu)|=
 p^{\sum_{i,j}\min(\lambda_i,\mu_j)}.$$

The conjugate partitions for $C_{(2)},D,K_{(2)}$ are respectively $(15,5,2,1)$, $(3,1,1)$, and $(12,4,1,1)$. Exact evaluation gives $$|\operatorname{Aut}(D)|=2^7\cdot3=384,\qquad |\operatorname{Aut}(C_{(3)})|=|\operatorname{Aut}(K_{(3)})|=108,$$ and $$|\operatorname{Aut}(C)|=2^{194}3^{10}5^2 7^4\,11\,17\,31^2\,73\,127,$$ $$|\operatorname{Aut}(K)|=2^{121}3^9 5^2 7^3\,17\,31\,127.$$ Equation [\[eq:hom\]](#eq:hom){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:hom"} gives $$\log_2|\operatorname{Hom}(K,D)|=3\cdot12+1\cdot4+1\cdot1=41.$$ Although $|\operatorname{Hom}(D,K)|$ happens to have the same value, the setwise stabilizer below specifically uses maps from $K$ to $D$.

# Two homogeneous spaces

Any $D$-type direct factor has some complement. Uniqueness of the cyclic elementary divisors forces every such complement to have type $K$. Given two ordered decompositions, choose isomorphisms between their $D$ components and between their $K$ components; their direct sum is an automorphism of $C$. This proves transitivity on ordered decompositions and, after forgetting the complement, on $D$-type direct factors.

For the fixed ordered pair, the stabilizer is block diagonal: $$\operatorname{Stab}(D,K)=\operatorname{Aut}(D)\times\operatorname{Aut}(K).$$ The setwise stabilizer of $D$ consists of invertible upper block-triangular maps, hence $$\operatorname{Stab}(D)=\operatorname{Hom}(K,D)\rtimes(\operatorname{Aut}(D)\times\operatorname{Aut}(K)).$$ If an abstract copy of $D$ is identified with its image, its pointwise stabilizer is $\operatorname{Hom}(K,D)\rtimes\operatorname{Aut}(K)$.

The number of $D$-type direct factors in $C$ is $$N_D=2^{25}\cdot7\cdot11\cdot31\cdot73=5846893330432.$$ The number of ordered decompositions $C=D'\oplus K'$ of type $(D,K)$ is $$N_{\rm dec}=2^{66}\cdot7\cdot11\cdot31\cdot73
 =12857454406351852314558464.$$

Orbit--stabilizer gives $$N_D=\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}(C)|}{|\operatorname{Aut}(D)||\operatorname{Aut}(K)||\operatorname{Hom}(K,D)|},\qquad
 N_{\rm dec}=\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}(C)|}{|\operatorname{Aut}(D)||\operatorname{Aut}(K)|}.$$ Exact integer division yields the displayed values. The independent mass identities $$N_{\rm dec}=N_D2^{41},\quad
 |\operatorname{Aut}(C)|=N_D|\operatorname{Stab}(D)|=N_{\rm dec}|\operatorname{Stab}(D,K)|$$ hold exactly. The first also recovers the C69 complement count for every member of the direct-factor orbit.

Every direct factor has $|\operatorname{Aut}(D)|=384$ parameterizations by an abstract $D$. Thus split embeddings form a third transitive $\operatorname{Aut}(C)$-space of size $$N_{\rm emb}=N_D|\operatorname{Aut}(D)|=2245207038885888.$$

# Why "direct factor" is essential

Write the $2$-primary diagonal model with a generator $g_4$ of its $\mathbb Z/16$ factor and elementary generators $f_1,f_2$ from two $\mathbb Z/2$ factors. Set $$H=\langle2g_4,f_1,f_2\rangle.$$

The subgroup $H$ is isomorphic to $D$, but it is not a direct factor of $C$.

The three generators have orders $8,2,2$ and are independent, so $H$ has type $(3,1,1)$. Quotienting changes the $\mathbb Z/16$ factor to $\mathbb Z/2$ and removes two elementary factors. Thus $(C_{(2)}/H)$ has type $(3,2,2,2,1^9)$, whereas a complement to a $D$-type direct factor must have type $K_{(2)}=(4,2,2,2,1^8)$. Hence $H$ cannot be a direct factor.

The boundary can be counted globally. For $p$-group types $\mu\subseteq
\lambda$, the number of subgroups of type $\mu$ is $$p^{\sum_i\mu'_{i+1}(\lambda'_i-\mu'_i)}
 \prod_i
 \genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{\lambda'_i-\mu'_{i+1}}
 {\mu'_i-\mu'_{i+1}}_p.$$ For $\lambda=C_{(2)}$ and $\mu=D$, this becomes $$2^{16}\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{14}{2}_2
 \genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{2}{1}_2
 =8794482475008.$$ Subtracting the direct-factor orbit leaves exactly $2947589144576$ non-direct $D$-type subgroups. Multiplication by $|\operatorname{Aut}(D)|$ gives $3377081270403072$ monomorphisms in total, so the split qualification is also essential for the embedding orbit.
