# Introduction and the integrated theorem {#sec:introduction}

Equal Dedekind zeta functions conceal information at ramified primes. They force the same unramified residue-degree data, yet they need not determine the products of local fields at the excluded primes. The phenomenon is classical; the problem addressed here is to realize it primitively and exactly inside the line field of one cubic surface, while retaining every decomposition-group branch left by the preceding ramification analysis.

Let $Y/\mathbf{Q}$ be the fixed smooth cubic surface, let $E$ be its degree-$27$ line field, and let $K$ be its fixed normal closure. The released labelled line action identifies $$G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbf{Q})=W(E_6),
  \qquad |G|=51840.
  \label{eq:intro-G}$$ The complete subgroup computation of James already records $350$ subgroup classes, $339$ permutation-character profiles, and eleven Gassmann collisions [@james2022bayesian §3.1, p. 22]. An abstract collision $K^{H_+},K^{H_-}$, however, does not supply primitive elements in the coordinates of the $27$ lines. Nor does it determine whether the same local separator works for both decomposition groups at $3$ that survive the ramification theorem for $K$.

# Prior work, frozen input, and durable definitions {#sec:prior-definitions}

## Arithmetic equivalence and its local boundary

Gassmann's character condition and Perlis's number-field formulation identify equality of Dedekind zeta functions with equality of the relevant induced trivial characters [@perlis1977equation Theorem 1]. This criterion is the universal bridge used below; the present work does not propose a new criterion for arithmetic equivalence. Bosma and de Smit explain the common global invariants forced by arithmetic equivalence and use permutation characters computationally [@bosmadesmit2002small printed pp. 67--68]. Our contribution is narrower: we give primitive line-coordinate generators and exact ramified arithmetic for one fixed normal closure.

Equal zeta functions do not imply equality of all arithmetic attached to the ramified completions. Explicit common-normal-closure constructions with different ramified local degrees occur in the literature [@mcreynolds2015spectra proof of Theorem 1.2], and explicit polynomial pairs can have identical zeta data but different ramification tuples [@mantillasoler2019question Theorems 1.3 and 3.7]. Komatsu separates arithmetic equivalence from equivalence of adele rings [@komatsu1984adele]. These works preclude any claim that local inequivalence or nonisomorphic adele rings are new in general. What is specific here is that the same local obstruction is proved for each of the two decomposition groups still possible in this cubic-surface instance.

We call two number fields *locally equivalent* if their places can be put in a bijection under which paired completions are isomorphic as topological fields. Equivalently, their adele rings are isomorphic as topological rings; we use this standard equivalence in the sense discussed by Komatsu [@komatsu1984adele].

## Subgroup collisions and resolvents

James's complete $W(E_6)$ computation is the prior source for the figures $350$, $339$, and eleven collision pairs [@james2022bayesian §3.1, p. 22]. We neither rediscover those collisions nor call the pair in [\[thm:integrated\]](#thm:integrated){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:integrated"} a new Gassmann pair. The phrase "unique minimum-index" will always mean unique among those eleven collision buckets in the complete $350$-class table. It is not a minimum-degree assertion about arithmetically equivalent fields in general.

The resolvent method is likewise classical. Stauduhar constructs resolvents from ordered roots and subgroup invariants [@stauduhar1973determination]; Fieker and Klüners develop practical relative invariants and stabilizer methods [@fiekerklueners2014computation §§1--2 and Remark 2.1]. Our orbit products use this established framework. Their special feature is the exact quadratic support in the labelled $27$-line carrier and a split-prime noncollision proof of primitivity, not a new resolvent algorithm.

Explicit realization of subgroups of $W(E_6)$ through lines on cubic surfaces is also established. Elsenhans and Jahnel give a moduli and inverse Galois construction, including an algorithm that produces cubic surfaces with prescribed line action [@elsenhansjahnel2015moduli Theorem in the introduction, Algorithm 5.1, and Algorithm A.10]. Here that geometric program is an input boundary. The paper studies two particular primitive subfields and their global and local arithmetic inside one already fixed realization.

The source audit authorizes only the following bounded conjunction: for this one cubic-surface line extension, exact integral quadratic orbit sums primitively realize the table-restricted minimum-index $W(E_6)$ Gassmann pair, and the resulting fields have an exact eight-prime global arithmetic package and nonisomorphic finite étale $\mathbf{Q}_{3}$-algebras in both surviving decomposition-group branches. This is a source boundary, not an absolute-priority or "first" claim.

# Primitive integral quadratic resolvents {#sec:primitive}

## Scaled roots and integrality

The leading coefficient is essential. Write $$g(x)=Lx^{27}+b_{26}x^{26}+\cdots+b_1x+b_0,
 \qquad b_i\in\mathbf{Z}.
 \label{eq:eliminant-general}$$

[\[lem:integral-scaling\]]{#lem:integral-scaling label="lem:integral-scaling"} Every $\alpha_i=Ld_i$ is an algebraic integer. Consequently $\eta_\pm$ are algebraic integers and $R_\pm(T)\in\mathbf{Z}[T]$.

Substitute $x=y/L$ in [\[eq:eliminant-general\]](#eq:eliminant-general){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:eliminant-general"} and multiply by $L^{26}$. The result is the monic integral equation $$y^{27}+b_{26}y^{26}+Lb_{25}y^{25}+\cdots
   +L^{25}b_1y+L^{26}b_0=0.$$ It is satisfied by $y=Ld_i$, proving that $\alpha_i$ is integral. Products $\alpha_i\alpha_j$ and the finite sums [\[eq:intro-eta\]](#eq:intro-eta){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:intro-eta"} are therefore integral.

Because $H_\pm$ fix $\eta_\pm$, the products over $G/H_\pm$ are well-defined. Their coefficients are symmetric functions of a full $G$-orbit, hence lie in the fixed field $K^G=\mathbf{Q}$. They are also algebraic integers. A rational algebraic integer lies in $\mathbf{Z}$, so every coefficient of $R_\pm$ is integral.

[\[conv:eta-scaling\]]{#conv:eta-scaling label="conv:eta-scaling"} Throughout the paper, $\eta_\pm$ means exactly the scaled sum in [\[eq:intro-eta\]](#eq:intro-eta){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:intro-eta"}. The optional unscaled quantity is $$\widetilde\eta_\pm
   =\sum_{\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{S}_\pm}d_id_j,
 \qquad
 \eta_\pm=L^2\widetilde\eta_\pm.$$ All integral-polynomial and modular-coefficient statements refer to $\eta_\pm$, never to $\widetilde\eta_\pm$.

The finite product defining $R_\pm$ is an exact characteristic-zero object; an expanded coefficient vector is neither needed nor supplied. In particular, no characteristic-zero coefficient hash is asserted. This distinction matters because the hashes below bind modular products only.

## Support stabilizers and formal orbits

## The split-prime noncollision argument

The prime used for the specialization is $$p=692717.
 \label{eq:split-prime}$$ Exact arithmetic proves that $p$ is prime, is outside the ramified support, and does not divide the eliminant or lexicographic reconstruction denominators. Modulo $p$, the normalized eliminant is squarefree and splits as $1^{27}$. Hence all labelled roots and all scaled values can be reduced in one common residue field.

[\[lem:split-noncollision\]]{#lem:split-noncollision label="lem:split-noncollision"} For each sign, the $320$ translated values $g\eta_\pm$, indexed by $gH_\pm\in G/H_\pm$, are pairwise distinct in characteristic zero. Consequently $$\operatorname{Stab}_G(\eta_\pm)=H_\pm,
 \qquad
 \mathbf{Q}(\eta_\pm)=K^{H_\pm}.$$

Reduce the labelled roots, their scaled coordinates, and the translated support sums modulo a fixed prime of the integral model above $p$. Complete enumeration gives $320$ distinct residues for the $H_+$ family and $320$ distinct residues for the $H_-$ family. If two characteristic-zero conjugates were equal, their difference would be zero as an algebraic integer and would reduce to zero at every common integral reduction. Their residues would then coincide, a contradiction.

It follows that the $G$-orbit of $\eta_\pm$ has size $320$. Since $H_\pm$ fixes $\eta_\pm$ and $[G:H_\pm]=320$, orbit--stabilizer forces $\operatorname{Stab}_G(\eta_\pm)=H_\pm$. Galois correspondence then gives $$[\mathbf{Q}(\eta_\pm):\mathbf{Q}]=320,
 \qquad
 \mathbf{Q}(\eta_\pm)=K^{H_\pm}.$$

 \texttt{\seqsplit{76fa8081c92e58839f60659fa7c9979d9b002fae5408cc30777341d21665acb2}}.
 \label{eq:modhash-minus}\end{aligned}$$ These are hashes of the complete modular coefficient arrays. They are not hashes of expanded characteristic-zero coefficients.

[\[cor:product-minimal\]]{#cor:product-minimal label="cor:product-minimal"} The orbit products $R_\pm(T)$ are the monic integral irreducible separable minimal polynomials of $\eta_\pm$, each of degree $320$.

By [\[lem:integral-scaling\]](#lem:integral-scaling){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:integral-scaling"}, the orbit products lie in $\mathbf{Z}[T]$. By [\[lem:split-noncollision\]](#lem:split-noncollision){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:split-noncollision"}, each product contains every distinct $\mathbf{Q}$-conjugate of $\eta_\pm$ exactly once. It is therefore the minimal polynomial. Characteristic zero gives separability.

## Graph authority and labelling independence

The labelled action is tied back to the cubic surface rather than accepted as a free-standing abstract permutation representation. At the split prime, the exact lexicographic formulas reconstruct all $27$ lines. Substitution into each of four chart line equations gives zero for every line. Pairwise incidence then yields a graph with $27$ vertices, $135$ edges, and degree $10$ at every vertex. We denote this line-intersection graph by $\Gamma_{\mathrm{meet}}$. It is the complement of the graph conventionally called the Schlaefli graph, whose edges join skew lines and which is therefore $16$-regular; complementation does not change the automorphism group.

[\[lem:graph-bridge\]]{#lem:graph-bridge label="lem:graph-bridge"} Inside the fixed labelled action, $$\operatorname{Aut}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{meet}})=G$$ as equality of the two complete $51{,}840$-element permutation sets. Any other graph identification transports $H_\pm$ and $\mathcal{S}_\pm$ by one element of $G$ and merely permutes the $320$ factors of $R_\pm$.

The six fixed simple-reflection generators preserve the reconstructed graph, so the released $G$ is contained in the graph automorphism group. Exact enumeration gives $|G|=51840$. Independently, individualization followed by equitable color refinement with base vertices $1,2,3,4,5$ gives cell-choice sizes $27,16,10,6,2$ and a discrete final partition. Their product is $51840$, an upper bound for the graph automorphism group. Thus containment and the matching upper bound give equality of the complete permutation sets.

Two identifications of the reconstructed graph with the standard labelled graph differ by an automorphism, hence by some $w\in G$. They send $(H_\pm,\mathcal{S}_\pm)$ to $(wH_\pm w^{-1},w\mathcal{S}_\pm)$. Left multiplication by $w$ permutes the full coset orbit, leaving its product and noncollision property unchanged. Literal equality with the particular fixed fields in [\[thm:integrated\]](#thm:integrated){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:integrated"} uses the displayed labelling; a relabelling transports the subgroups with it.

Eight sampled graph isomorphisms would not prove [\[lem:graph-bridge\]](#lem:graph-bridge){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:graph-bridge"}. The argument uses all four chart equations, all $27$ reconstructed lines, and equality of the full permutation sets. This completes the proof of [\[thm:integrated\]](#thm:integrated){reference-type="ref" reference="thm:integrated"}(i).

## Common normal closure and nonisomorphism

[\[lem:core-normal-closure\]]{#lem:core-normal-closure label="lem:core-normal-closure"} For $F=K^H$, the normal closure of $F/\mathbf{Q}$ inside $K$ is $K^{\operatorname{Core}_G(H)}$. In particular, both $F_+$ and $F_-$ have normal closure $K$.

The conjugates of $K^H$ inside $K$ are $K^{gHg^{-1}}$. Their compositum is fixed by the intersection of their fixing groups: $$\bigcap_{g\in G}gHg^{-1}=\operatorname{Core}_G(H).$$ This compositum is the normal closure inside $K$. Both cores are trivial by [2](#tab:subgroup-invariants){reference-type="ref" reference="tab:subgroup-invariants"}, so both normal closures equal $K$.

[\[lem:field-nonisomorphism\]]{#lem:field-nonisomorphism label="lem:field-nonisomorphism"} The fields $F_+$ and $F_-$ are not isomorphic over $\mathbf{Q}$.

Their subgroup abelianizations have invariant factors $[2,3]$ and $[2]$. Thus $H_+$ and $H_-$ are not isomorphic and, in particular, are not conjugate in $G$. This subgroup distinction alone is not yet the desired field statement.

Suppose instead that a $\mathbf{Q}$-isomorphism $F_+\to F_-$ existed. Extend it to an automorphism $\sigma$ of $\overline{\mathbf{Q}}$. By [\[lem:core-normal-closure\]](#lem:core-normal-closure){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:core-normal-closure"}, both fields have the same normal closure $K$, so $\sigma(K)=K$. The restriction of $\sigma$ to $K$ belongs to $G$ and transports point stabilizers: $$\sigma H_+\sigma^{-1}=H_-.$$ This contradicts nonconjugacy. Hence no such field isomorphism exists.

## Artin formalism and the common zeta function

[\[prop:zeta-equality\]]{#prop:zeta-equality label="prop:zeta-equality"} The two Dedekind zeta functions agree: $$\zeta_{F_+}(s)=\zeta_{F_-}(s).$$

By the split-prime noncollision bridge in [\[lem:split-noncollision\]](#lem:split-noncollision){reference-type="ref" reference="lem:split-noncollision"}, $F_\pm=K^{H_\pm}$. (The separate [\[cor:product-minimal\]](#cor:product-minimal){reference-type="ref" reference="cor:product-minimal"} identifies the product-form minimal polynomials.) Substitute $H=H_+$ and $H=H_-$ in [\[eq:artin-zeta\]](#eq:artin-zeta){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:artin-zeta"}. The full character identity in [\[prop:table-minimum\]](#prop:table-minimum){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:table-minimum"} makes the two Artin $L$-functions identical.

From the equality in [\[prop:zeta-equality\]](#prop:zeta-equality){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:zeta-equality"} alone we infer none of integral permutation equivalence, isomorphic rings of integers, equality of class numbers, local equivalence, adelic equivalence, or isomorphic idele groups. ## Double cosets and completion factors

# Scope, limitations, and conclusion {#sec:scope-conclusion}

## Limitations and interpretation

The decomposition group at $3$ remains unresolved. This is not hidden by a choice of notation: [\[tab:c59-local-tom140\]](#tab:c59-local-tom140){reference-type="ref" reference="tab:c59-local-tom140"} and [\[tab:c59-local-tom206\]](#tab:c59-local-tom206){reference-type="ref" reference="tab:c59-local-tom206"} are logically separate inputs, and the theorem was designed to be valid in both cases. The exact minimal polynomials are supplied as orbit products, which define them completely over $\mathbf{Q}$. We neither print nor hash their expanded coefficient vectors. Likewise, the field discriminants follow from permutation conductors. We do not compute an integral basis or maximal order of either degree-$320$ field, assert monogenicity, or identify a polynomial discriminant with the field discriminant.

The local rows record degree, ramification index, residue degree, and a lower- filtration discriminant entry. Their differing factor-degree multisets separate the product algebras. They do not determine an individual high-degree completion up to isomorphism. From the zeta identity alone we infer none of integral permutation equivalence, isomorphic rings of integers, equal class numbers, local equivalence, adelic equivalence, or isomorphic idele groups. The local and adelic nonisomorphisms proved in [\[cor:local-separation\]](#cor:local-separation){reference-type="ref" reference="cor:local-separation"} are separate positive conclusions.

Finally, the prior-work conclusion is deliberately bounded.
