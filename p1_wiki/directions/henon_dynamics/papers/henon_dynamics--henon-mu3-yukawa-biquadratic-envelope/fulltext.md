# Introduction and the integrated theorem {#sec:introduction}

An equality of permutation characters can make two number fields arithmetically equivalent without explaining how they sit inside their common normal closure. In particular, a Gassmann collision does not by itself produce a common intermediate field, a third quadratic companion, or usable primitive elements. Those are embedding-sensitive questions: they depend on the exact subgroups in the exact permutation action, not merely on abstract group isomorphism types or equal character rows. This paper resolves those questions for one normal $W(E_6)$-extension attached to the $27$ lines of a cubic surface.

The construction begins with a fixed degree-$320$ Gassmann pair $K^{H_{+}}$ and $K^{H_{-}}$. We transport the second subgroup into a common normalizer and obtain three index-two subgroups $H_{+},H_{0},H_{3}$ of a group $N$ of order $324$. Their common intersection $J$ has order $81$, and $$N/J\cong V_4.$$ Reversing inclusions by Galois correspondence gives a field diamond $$M=K^N\subset F_{+},F_{0},F_{3}\subset L=K^J.$$ What turns this abstract diamond into an explicit construction is a set of three integral line-coordinate carriers. Quadratic carriers generate $M$ and $L$, while the new companion $F_{0}$ is generated by a cubic orbit sum. The need for degree three is formal and exact: $H_{0}$ and $N$ have the same orbits on points and unordered pairs, so their invariant spaces coincide in total degree at most two.

Arithmetic equivalence and its relation to induced trivial characters go back to the classical criterion of Perlis [@perlis1977equation Theorem 1, printed p. 351]; Bosma and de Smit give computational context and discuss invariants forced by the equality [@bosmadesmit2002small printed p. 68]. James's complete $W(E_6)$ subgroup calculation supplies the ambient figures of $350$ conjugacy classes, $339$ permutation-character profiles, and eleven collisions [@james2022bayesian §3.1, printed p. 22]. The present claim is not a new collision criterion and not a globally unique $V_4$ construction. It is the following exact conjunction for the fixed released action: among those eleven collision buckets, the pair $301/303$ alone has normalizers conjugate in $W(E_6)$ with index two over both members, and that embedded configuration supports the explicit field and arithmetic package below. The classical resolvent framework used to prove primitivity is due to Stauduhar and its computational invariant formulation to Fieker and Klüners [@stauduhar1973determination pp. 981--984] [@fiekerklueners2014computation §§1--2 and Remark 2.1]. Explicit cubic-surface realizations of $W(E_6)$ provide the broader geometric context [@elsenhansjahnel2015moduli].

We now state the result as one theorem. The notation in its statement is defined without software locators in [2](#sec:released-input){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:released-input"}; all finite tables needed to check it appear in the appendices.

[\[thm:integrated\]]{#thm:integrated label="thm:integrated"} Let $K/\mathbf{Q}$ be the fixed normal extension of [2](#sec:released-input){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:released-input"}, with $G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbf{Q})=W(E_6)$ and $|G|=51840$. Let $H_{+}$ and $H_{-}$ be the displayed embedded Gassmann subgroups, let $H_{3}=xH_{-}x^{-1}$ under the left label-map convention, and let $N,H_{0},J$ be the exact subgroups in [3](#sec:envelope){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:envelope"}. Put $$M=K^N,\qquad F_i=K^{H_i}\quad(i\in\{+,0,3\}),\qquad L=K^J.$$ Then the following assertions hold together.

1.  *Lattice.* The orders of $N,H_i,J$ are $324,162,81$; $J=[N,N]$, $N/J\cong V_4$, every pair of the $H_i$ intersects in $J$, and every pair generates $N$. Consequently $$[M:\mathbf{Q}]=160,\qquad [F_i:\mathbf{Q}]=320,\qquad [L:\mathbf{Q}]=640,$$ $L/M$ is biquadratic, and $F_{+},F_{0},F_{3}$ are its three quadratic subfields.

2.  *Closures and automorphisms.* Every field in the diamond has normal closure $K$. Moreover $$\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf{Q}}(M)=1,\qquad \operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf{Q}}(F_i)\cong C_2,
     \qquad \operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbf{Q}}(L)\cong V_4.$$ The field $F_{3}$ is a $\mathbf{Q}$-conjugate of the original embedded minus field $K^{H_{-}}$.

3.  *Primitive generators.* For the integral scaled line roots $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{27}$, the explicitly defined carriers $\mu,\xi_0,\lambda$ satisfy $$M=\mathbf{Q}(\mu),\qquad F_{0}=\mathbf{Q}(\xi_0),\qquad L=\mathbf{Q}(\lambda).$$ Their formal stabilizer orders are $324$, $162$, and $81$, and the corresponding orbit sizes are $160$, $320$, and $640$. All orbit values are distinct after reduction at the unramified completely split prime $p=692717$.

4.  *Formal invariant gap.* The $H_{0}$- and $N$-orbit partitions on points and unordered pairs agree: the point orbit has size $27$, and the pair-orbit sizes are $$27,27,54,81,162.$$ Hence no commutative $\mathbf{Q}$-coefficient formal polynomial of total degree at most two in the $27$ labelled variables has stabilizer exactly $H_{0}$. The displayed $27$-term cubic carrier $\xi_0$ does.

5.  *Restricted uniqueness.* Among the eleven frozen collision buckets, $301/303$ is the only pair for which the two normalizers are conjugate in $G$ and both normalizer indices over the corresponding subgroups are equal to two. No broader uniqueness of a $V_4$ configuration is asserted.

6.  *Characters and zeta functions.* On all $25$ conjugacy classes, the following equality holds in the rational permutation representation ring: $$[G/J]+2[G/N]=[G/H_{+}]+[G/H_{0}]+[G/H_{3}].$$ It follows that $$\zeta_L(s)\zeta_M(s)^2
     =\zeta_{F_{+}}(s)\zeta_{F_{0}}(s)\zeta_{F_{3}}(s)
     =\zeta_{F_{+}}(s)^2\zeta_{F_{0}}(s).$$ The bracket equality is not an isomorphism of finite $G$-sets.

7.  *Global and relative arithmetic.* With $A,B$ defined in [\[eq:A-definition,eq:B-definition\]](#eq:A-definition,eq:B-definition){reference-type="ref" reference="eq:A-definition,eq:B-definition"}, the signatures and signed discriminants are those in [1](#tab:theorem-dashboard){reference-type="ref" reference="tab:theorem-dashboard"}. The exact finite support is $$\begin{aligned}

       &14932047182473291995860108491583652133938007263719\}.
     \end{aligned}$$ The absolute norms of the relative discriminants over $M$ are $$3^8,\quad3^{16},\quad3^8,\quad3^{32}$$ for $F_{+},F_{0},F_{3},L$, respectively.

8.  *Both retained local towers.* The complete relative tables hold for both retained possibilities $D_3=\ensuremath{\mathrm{ToM}\,140}$ and $D_3=\ensuremath{\mathrm{ToM}\,206}$, with relative factor totals $(36,28,36,56)$ and $(18,14,18,28)$. ## The embedded Gassmann pair

The durable one-line generators in [10](#app:group-census){reference-type="ref" reference="app:group-census"} define two exact order-$162$ subgroups $H_{+},H_{-}\le G$. ## Integrality and split-prime noncollision

[\[lem:integral-scaling\]]{#lem:integral-scaling label="lem:integral-scaling"} Each $\alpha_i=L_0d_i$ is an algebraic integer. Hence $\mu,\xi_0,\lambda$ are algebraic integers.

Substitute $d=y/L_0$ into $g(d)=L_0d^{27}+b_{26}d^{26}+\cdots+b_0$ and multiply by $L_0^{26}$. The result is the monic integral equation $$y^{27}+b_{26}y^{26}+L_0b_{25}y^{25}+\cdots
   +L_0^{25}b_1y+L_0^{26}b_0=0.$$ It is satisfied by $y=L_0d_i$. Integer polynomials in algebraic integers are integral, which applies to all three finite sums.

The weighted-carrier stabilizers are exact formal statements: $$\operatorname{Stab}_G(\mu)=N,\qquad \operatorname{Stab}_G(\xi_0)=H_{0},\qquad
 \operatorname{Stab}_G(\lambda)=J
 \label{eq:formal-stabilizers}$$ when the symbols $\alpha_i$ are treated as independently labelled variables. This is an exhaustive assertion for all three carriers: for each of the $51840$ elements of the durable labelled action, the canonical weighted monomial dictionary is transported and compared with the original. The elements passing that equality test form exactly $N$ for $\mu$, exactly $H_{0}$ for $\xi_0$, and exactly $J$ for $\lambda$, of orders $324,162,81$. Thus the calculation excludes a larger ambient stabilizer; it does not merely show that the named subgroup fixes the carrier. Structurally, the two $H_{+}$-support translates in $\mu$ are exchanged by $N/H_{+}$; unequal weights in $\lambda$ recover $\mathcal{S}_+$ and $\mathcal{S}_3$ separately; and the $27$ triples in $\xi_0$ form one $H_{0}$-orbit. Formal stabilizers still do not exclude equalities introduced by evaluation at the actual roots. The next lemma supplies that bridge.

[\[lem:split-noncollision\]]{#lem:split-noncollision label="lem:split-noncollision"} Let $\theta$ be an integral polynomial in the labelled roots, fixed formally by $H\le G$. Suppose that, at a common unramified prime where all labelled roots lie in the residue field, the $[G:H]$ values obtained from the formal $G/H$ orbit of $\theta$ are pairwise distinct. Then $$\operatorname{Stab}_G(\theta)=H,\qquad \mathbf{Q}(\theta)=K^H.$$ Its orbit product is the monic integral irreducible separable minimal polynomial of degree $[G:H]$.

If two characteristic-zero integral conjugates were equal, their difference would be the zero algebraic integer and would reduce to zero at every common integral specialization. This contradicts distinctness of the stated residues. Thus the evaluated orbit has at least $[G:H]$ elements. Since $H$ fixes $\theta$, it has at most that many elements. Orbit--stabilizer therefore gives equality and the exact stabilizer $H$. Galois correspondence gives $\mathbf{Q}(\theta)=K^H$. The orbit product is fixed by $G$, has rational integral coefficients, and contains each conjugate once; hence it lies in $\mathbf{Z}[T]$ and is the minimal polynomial. Characteristic zero gives separability.

The common witness is $$p=692717.
 \label{eq:split-prime}$$ It lies outside [\[eq:released-support\]](#eq:released-support){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:released-support"}. The normalized eliminant is squarefree and factors as $1^{27}$ modulo $p$; the $27$ roots are distinct, the line equations vanish, and the reconstructed line graph carries the full $51840$-element faithful action. The unramified Frobenius fixes all $27$ labels, so faithfulness makes it the identity in $G$; hence $p$ splits completely in $K$ and all orbit values reduce in the same residue field. Complete evaluation gives respectively $160$, $320$, and $640$ distinct values. The exact product fingerprints are as follows.

## Why the companion first appears in degree three

The cubic in [\[eq:xi0\]](#eq:xi0){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:xi0"} is not merely the first successful item in a coefficient search. Its degree is forced by a complete invariant-space argument in the formal coordinate ring.

[\[prop:coefficient-orbit\]]{#prop:coefficient-orbit label="prop:coefficient-orbit"} Let $R=\mathbf{Q}[X_1,\ldots,X_{27}]$ with $G$ permuting the variables. If two subgroups $H\le N\le G$ have the same orbit partitions on points and unordered pairs, then $$R_{\le2}^{H}=R_{\le2}^{N}.$$ In particular, no formal polynomial of total degree at most two can have stabilizer exactly $H$.

Every element of $R_{\le2}$ has a unique expansion in the monomial basis $$1,\qquad X_i,\qquad X_i^2,\qquad X_iX_j\quad(i<j).$$ An $H$-fixed polynomial has coefficients constant on $H$-orbits. The linear and square coefficients are indexed by points; the mixed coefficients are indexed by unordered pairs. If the two partitions agree with those for $N$, each coefficient function is also constant on $N$-orbits. Therefore every $H$-fixed polynomial of degree at most two is $N$-fixed. The reverse inclusion follows from $H\le N$, proving equality. An element whose stabilizer is exactly $H$ would be $H$-fixed but not $N$-fixed, so none exists.

For $H=H_{0}$, exact orbit enumeration gives the data in [\[tab:invariant-gap\]](#tab:invariant-gap){reference-type="ref" reference="tab:invariant-gap"}.

The equality concerns commutative $\mathbf Q$-coefficient formal polynomials in independent labelled variables before quotienting by relations among the line roots. It excludes an exact $H_0$ stabilizer in degree at most two; the displayed cubic carrier escapes the obstruction.

The two partitions agree as sets, not merely as multisets of sizes. By [\[prop:coefficient-orbit\]](#prop:coefficient-orbit){reference-type="ref" reference="prop:coefficient-orbit"}, no commutative $\mathbf{Q}$-coefficient formal polynomial in the labelled variables of total degree at most two has exact stabilizer $H_{0}$. In contrast, the orbit sum [\[eq:xi0\]](#eq:xi0){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:xi0"} has exact stabilizer $H_{0}$ and is primitive by the split witness.

This conclusion is deliberately formal. Evaluating $X_i\mapsto\alpha_i$ may impose algebraic relations among the roots, and we do not classify expressions in the resulting quotient ring. The theorem says precisely that degree two cannot distinguish $H_{0}$ from $N$ at the level of commutative formal coefficient orbits, while the displayed cubic does.

# Exhaustive uniqueness, characters, and zeta functions {#sec:uniqueness-zeta}

The common-normalizer construction sits inside a known finite collision census. We first delimit exactly what is unique, and then use the resulting $V_4$ lattice to obtain a representation-ring identity.

## The exact eleven-bucket predicate

For an unordered collision bucket $\{a,b\}$ represented by embedded subgroups $H_a,H_b\le G$, define the predicate $$\label{eq:uniqueness-predicate}
\begin{split}
\mathcal P(a,b):\quad &N_G(H_a)\text{ and }N_G(H_b)
     \text{ are conjugate in }G,\\
&[N_G(H_a):H_a]=[N_G(H_b):H_b]=2.
\end{split}$$ No degree or target locator is included in $\mathcal P$. The computation starts from all $350$ subgroup conjugacy classes and their $339$ distinct permutation-character profiles; it then evaluates [\[eq:uniqueness-predicate\]](#eq:uniqueness-predicate){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:uniqueness-predicate"} on every one of the eleven collision buckets recorded by James [@james2022bayesian §3.1, printed p. 22].

[\[prop:restricted-uniqueness\]]{#prop:restricted-uniqueness label="prop:restricted-uniqueness"} Among the eleven collision buckets, $\mathcal P(a,b)$ holds exactly for $\{a,b\}=\{301,303\}$.

For each bucket, generate both exact subgroups, compute their normalizers in the full $51840$-element group, test normalizer conjugacy in $G$, and divide the two normalizer orders by the subgroup order. The complete rows are in [5](#tab:collision-census){reference-type="ref" reference="tab:collision-census"}. Only the final row has conjugate normalizers and index pair $[2,2]$. The calculation contains no target-filtering step: all eleven Boolean leaves are computed before the unique qualifying list is formed.

The exhaustive count is summarized in [\[fig:uniqueness-funnel\]](#fig:uniqueness-funnel){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:uniqueness-funnel"}.

This count does not say that the envelope is a globally unique $V_4$ configuration. The bucket $112/120$ is the instructive near-miss: its normalizers are conjugate, and after transport the two order-$16$ groups generate a group of order $32$ with intersection of order $8$. Nevertheless each normalizer has order $64$, so the normalizer indices are $[4,4]$ and $\mathcal P(112,120)$ is false. Dropping the index-two clause would therefore support a false uniqueness sentence.

## The rational $V_4$ relation

Let $V=N/J\cong V_4$ and let $A_+,A_0,A_3$ be its three order-two subgroups, the images of $H_{+},H_{0},H_{3}$. In the rational representation ring of $V$, $$+2[V/V]=[V/A_+]+[V/A_0]+[V/A_3].
 \label{eq:V4-relation}$$ Indeed, the regular character is the sum of the trivial character and the three nontrivial one-dimensional characters. Each two-point permutation character $\mathbf{Q}[V/A_i]$ is the sum of the trivial character and the unique nontrivial character with kernel $A_i$. Both sides of [\[eq:V4-relation\]](#eq:V4-relation){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:V4-relation"} therefore contain the trivial character three times and each nontrivial character once.

Inflate [\[eq:V4-relation\]](#eq:V4-relation){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:V4-relation"} along $N\twoheadrightarrow N/J$ and induce from $N$ to $G$. Transitivity of induction yields $$+2[G/N]=[G/H_{+}]+[G/H_{0}]+[G/H_{3}].
 \label{eq:G-character-relation}$$ The independent computation in [6](#tab:character-vectors){reference-type="ref" reference="tab:character-vectors"} verifies [\[eq:G-character-relation\]](#eq:G-character-relation){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:G-character-relation"} on all $25$ conjugacy classes, with coefficient order $(H_{+},H_{0},H_{3},J,N)=(-1,-1,-1,1,2)$. Checking all classes prevents a matching-dimensions argument from being mistaken for character equality.

The two sides of [\[eq:G-character-relation\]](#eq:G-character-relation){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:G-character-relation"} are equal after passing to rational permutation representations. They are not isomorphic finite $G$-sets: the orbit decompositions have different numbers and stabilizer types. Every use below is additive in characters.

## Dedekind zeta consequence

For $H\le G$, Artin formalism identifies the Dedekind zeta function of $K^H$ with the Artin $L$-function attached to $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\mathbf{1}$. Multiplicativity on character sums, as in Perlis's criterion [@perlis1977equation], converts [\[eq:G-character-relation\]](#eq:G-character-relation){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:G-character-relation"} into $$\zeta_L(s)\zeta_M(s)^2
 =\zeta_{F_{+}}(s)\zeta_{F_{0}}(s)\zeta_{F_{3}}(s).
 \label{eq:zeta-first}$$ The original $H_{+},H_{-}$ pair has equal permutation characters, and conjugation by $x$ preserves the character. Hence $\zeta_{F_{3}}=\zeta_{F_{+}}$ and $$\zeta_L(s)\zeta_M(s)^2
 =\zeta_{F_{+}}(s)^2\zeta_{F_{0}}(s).
 \label{eq:zeta-second}$$ The generic $V_4$ identity is classical; the contribution here is its realization by the exact embedded fields together with their explicit generators and arithmetic.

Equations [\[eq:zeta-first\]](#eq:zeta-first){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:zeta-first"}--[\[eq:zeta-second\]](#eq:zeta-second){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:zeta-second"} are the entire analytic conclusion. We compute no new decomposition Frobenius, bad Artin Euler factor, epsilon factor, or root number, and infer no holomorphy or automorphy statement. The next section uses conductor characters only to compute discriminants, a logically separate finite invariant.

## Archimedean signatures

Let $E=K^H$ have degree $n=[G:H]$, and let $o_c(H)$ be the number of orbits of a complex-conjugation subgroup on $G/H$. Real embeddings are fixed points and nonreal embeddings occur in transposed pairs, so $$r_1+2r_2=n,\qquad r_1+r_2=o_c(H).$$ Therefore $$r_1=2o_c(H)-n,\qquad r_2=n-o_c(H).
 \label{eq:signature-formula}$$ The exact pairs $(n,o_c)$ are $$(160,88),\quad(320,168),\quad(320,160),\quad(640,320)$$ for $M$, $F_{+}/F_{3}$, $F_{0}$, and $L$. Substitution in [\[eq:signature-formula\]](#eq:signature-formula){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:signature-formula"} gives $$(16,72),\quad(16,152),\quad(0,160),\quad(0,320).
 \label{eq:signatures}$$ Each $r_2$ is even, so the sign $(-1)^{r_2}$ of every field discriminant is positive.

## Conductor exponents

The released lower filtration uses the ordered subgroup types $$(I_3,P_3,Q_3,I_5,P_5,C_3,C_2,c_\infty).$$ For a degree-$n$ coset carrier, write $o_U$ for the number of $U$-orbits.  \label{eq:general-conductor}$$ At $3$, the inertia group has order $18$, the wild group has order $9$, and the order-$3$ group $Q_3$ occurs in six positive lower grades. # Both retained $3$-adic towers {#sec:local}

# Conclusion {#sec:conclusion}

The exact common normalizer upgrades one pairwise Gassmann collision into a coherent fixed-field envelope. Its subgroup lattice gives a biquadratic degree-$160/320/640$ diamond; exact line-coordinate carriers make three levels primitive; equality of point and pair partitions explains why the new quadratic companion first appears through a cubic formal invariant; and the $V_4$ character relation organizes both the zeta identity and the relative arithmetic. Global conductor calculations and two separate local double-coset tables then show that all relative ramification is tame above $3$, independently of which retained decomposition-group branch occurs.

Two natural questions remain outside the theorem. One is to resolve which of the two $3$-adic decomposition groups belongs to the fixed normal extension. The other is to study integral refinements of the five fields beyond their discriminants. Neither question is answered by the present zeta or local-tuple data, and neither is needed for the envelope constructed here.
