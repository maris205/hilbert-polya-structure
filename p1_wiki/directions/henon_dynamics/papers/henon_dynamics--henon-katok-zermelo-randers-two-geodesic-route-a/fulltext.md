# Navigation convention

Let $h$ be the round metric of curvature one on $S^2$, with longitude of period $2\pi$. Let $W=\epsilon\partial_\phi$ and $0<|\epsilon|<1$. Since $|W|_h\le |\epsilon|<1$, translating the $h$-unit circle in each tangent plane by $W$ defines a strongly convex Randers metric $F_\epsilon$. We orient its geodesics and use the clock $F_\epsilon(\dot\gamma)=1$. The signs $+$ and $-$ refer to the two directions of the round equator before adding the wind.

On the nonzero cotangent bundle $T^*S^2$, the dual navigation Hamiltonian is $$\label{eq:dual}
 F_\epsilon^*(x,\xi)=|\xi|_h+\xi(W).$$ The convention in [\[eq:dual\]](#eq:dual){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:dual"} is equivalent to the translated indicatrix convention above and fixes every sign in the period formulas.

[\[lem:factor\]]{#lem:factor label="lem:factor"} Let $\Phi_t$ be rotation generated by $W$. Every unit $F_\epsilon$-geodesic, and no other curve, has the form $$\label{eq:navigation}
 \gamma(t)=\Phi_t(\rho(t)),$$ where $\rho$ is a unit-speed round great circle. Moreover, $F_\epsilon$ has constant flag curvature one.

The cotangent lift of $W$ preserves the round cometric because $W$ is Killing. Hence the two homogeneous Hamiltonians in [\[eq:dual\]](#eq:dual){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:dual"} Poisson commute. Their flows compose in either order: the first projects to round unit cogeodesic motion and the second to $\Phi_t$. This proves [\[eq:navigation\]](#eq:navigation){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:navigation"} and its converse. The navigation curvature theorem for a Killing wind on a curvature-one background gives flag curvature one.

# The complete irrational ledger

[\[thm:main\]]{#thm:main label="thm:main"} If $\epsilon\notin\mathbb Q$, the complete oriented prime closed-geodesic ledger of $(S^2,F_\epsilon)$ consists of exactly the two equators. Their least periods are $$\label{eq:periods}
 T_+=\frac{2\pi}{1+\epsilon},\qquad
 T_-=\frac{2\pi}{1-\epsilon}.$$ In canonical transverse Jacobi coordinates their Poincare maps have multipliers $e^{\pm iT_+}$ and $e^{\pm iT_-}$, respectively, and $$\label{eq:det}
 \det(I-P(T))=2-2\cos T=4\sin^2(T/2).$$ Both prime orbits are nondegenerate.

The oriented round unit tangent bundle is $SO(3)$; represent a frame by $g$. If $R_z(\alpha)$ is rotation through $\alpha$ about $e_3$, round geodesic motion is $gR_z(t)$ and the wind acts by $R_z(\epsilon t)g$. Consequently a return at $T>0$ is exactly $$\label{eq:return}
 R_z(\epsilon T)gR_z(T)=g,
 \qquad R_z(\epsilon T)=gR_z(-T)g^{-1}.$$ The rotation on the left of the second equality has axis $e_3$; the conjugated right rotation has axis $ge_3$. At a non-equatorial frame these unoriented axes are distinct. Thus equality forces both rotations to be the identity, including at angle $\pi$, where a nontrivial rotation still determines its unoriented axis. Hence $$\label{eq:closure}
 T=2\pi m,\qquad \epsilon T=2\pi n,\qquad m>0,\quad m,n\in\mathbb Z.$$ This is impossible for irrational $\epsilon$.

The exceptional frames are precisely the two equators, for which $ge_3=\pm e_3$ and the unoriented axes coincide. Their signed angular velocities are $1+\epsilon$ and $-1+\epsilon$. Since $|\epsilon|<1$, their positive least periods are exactly [\[eq:periods\]](#eq:periods){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:periods"}.

Flag curvature one makes a scalar transverse Jacobi coefficient satisfy $j''+j=0$. In the returning canonical normal frame its fundamental matrix is $$P(T)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos T&\sin T\\-\sin T&\cos T\end{pmatrix}.$$ This gives the characteristic polynomial $z^2-2(\cos T)z+1$ and [\[eq:det\]](#eq:det){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:det"}. If an irrational-wind equator were degenerate, $T_\pm/(2\pi)=1/(1\pm\epsilon)$ would be an integer, forcing $\epsilon$ to be rational. Hence both determinants are nonzero.

The phrase "exactly two" counts orientation. The Randers metric is not reversible, so quotienting the two equators by direction would change the dynamical object.

# Complete boundary atlas

[\[thm:boundary\]]{#thm:boundary label="thm:boundary"} Write $\epsilon=p/q$ in lowest terms, where $0<|p|<q$. Every non-equatorial geodesic has least period $2\pi q$. The two equators instead have least periods $$\frac{2\pi q}{q+p},\qquad \frac{2\pi q}{q-p}.$$ At $\epsilon=0$ the metric is the round Zoll metric and all great circles have least period $2\pi$. Longitude reflection conjugates $\epsilon$ to $-\epsilon$ and exchanges the oriented equators. At $|\epsilon|=1$ global strong convexity fails; $|\epsilon|\ge1$ is outside the theorem.

For a non-equatorial frame, equations [\[eq:closure\]](#eq:closure){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:closure"} are necessary and sufficient. Substitution of $p/q$ shows that $q$ divides $m$, so the least positive return is $2\pi q$. On the equator the shared-axis calculation used in [\[eq:periods\]](#eq:periods){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:periods"} gives the two displayed values; they must not be replaced by the generic period. The zero-wind and reflection claims follow directly from the indicatrix. Finally, at the equator, where $|W|_h$ attains its maximum, the strict navigation inequality fails when $|\epsilon|=1$.

An equator on the rational face is degenerate exactly when $q/(q\pm p)$ is an integer. This is a source resonance statement, not an arithmetic-prime correspondence. This section is the *rational boundary owner* added in revision round one.
