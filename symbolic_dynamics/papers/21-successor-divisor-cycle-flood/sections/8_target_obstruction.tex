\section{Exact obstruction to the marked prime Euler target}
\label{sec:target}

The determinant theorem in \cref{thm:fredholm} belongs to the frozen symbolic
object.  We now compare its marked primitive ledger with the target
\[
 D_{\Prime}(s,z)=\prod_p(1-zp^{-s}),
 \qquad
 \RePart s>1.
\]
The marker \(z\) records primitive degree.  It prevents accidental scalar
agreement at \(z=1\) from being mistaken for equality of orbit species.

\begin{theorem}[Degree-one mismatch]
\label{thm:target-mismatch}
On the common domain \(\RePart s>1\), the germs at \(z=0\) of
\(D_{\SD}(s,z)\) and \(D_{\Prime}(s,z)\) are not equal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
An integer \(n\ge2\) never divides \(n+1\), so the source graph has no loop.
Consequently
\[
 [z]D_{\SD}(s,z)=-\Tr L_s=0.
\]
For the target, absolute convergence gives
\[
 D_{\Prime}(s,z)
 =
 1-z\sum_pp^{-s}+O(z^2).
\]
For real \(s>1\),
\[
 \sum_pp^{-s}>0,
\]
so the target coefficient is strictly negative.  The germs differ before
any zero is considered.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Claim boundary at \(z=1\)]
\label{rem:z1}
\Cref{thm:target-mismatch} does not claim that an isolated scalar equality
\[
 D_{\SD}(s,1)=D_{\Prime}(s,1)
\]
is impossible at every individual \(s\).  Such an accident would not restore
the marked trace sequence or primitive factors and therefore would not
constitute a target representation.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Composite-square orbit norms]
\label{thm:orbit-species}
Every closed orbit \(\gamma\) has length at least two.  Its vertex mass
\[
 N(\gamma)=\prod_{v\in\gamma}v
\]
is composite, and its roof norm is
\[
 \exp(T_\gamma)=N(\gamma)^2,
\]
a composite perfect square.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Absence of loops gives length at least two.  Every vertex is at least two, so
\(N(\gamma)\) is a product of at least two integers greater than one.  By
\cref{eq:cyclic-weight}, the orbit roof is
\[
 T_\gamma=2\log N(\gamma).
\]
\end{proof}

Even an unauthorized halving of the frozen endpoint roof would leave
\(N(\gamma)\) composite.  The problem is therefore not merely the factor
two: the orbit inventory itself has the wrong arithmetic species.

\subsection{Why own-object analyticity does not repair the ledger}

The trace-class theorem extends \(D_{\SD}\) as a holomorphic same-object
determinant throughout \(\RePart s>1/2\), including much of the classical
critical strip.  It does not transport the prime Euler product into that
half-plane.  The two marked germs already disagree where both defining
products are absolutely meaningful.  Any later continuation of one object,
were it to exist, would continue the mismatch rather than erase it.

This distinction is the central diagnostic outcome:
\[
\text{strong recurrence and Fredholm control}
\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{prime-orbit selectivity}.
\]
