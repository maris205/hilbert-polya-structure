## Review Body

**Genuine strength.** The proof explicitly freezes the right-translation convention, carries regular multiplicities through the irreducible decomposition, and supplements the nonlinear determinant test with a recurrence proving exact corank one. These steps close the three most common routes to an incorrect nullity jump.

**Strongest counter-argument.** The skeptical reading is that the claimed phase diagram is a direct exercise in the already standard noncommutative Fourier solution of convolution equations on finite Heisenberg groups. Cross-characteristic Maschke decomposition gives the familiar ledger of (ell^2) characters and (ell-1) Schrödinger modules of degree (ell). On the characters, singularity is just a torsion-point count expressed as a polynomial gcd. On each nonlinear type, the cyclic diagonal-plus-shift matrix has a determinant with only two permutation terms, so its singular locus is the elementary Fermat equation. Once regular multiplicity is remembered, the jump (ell(ell-1)) is forced. A shorter alternative would state the finite group-algebra convolution problem first, quote the complete irreducible ledger, and derive the nullity formula without presenting it as a symbolic-dynamics phase diagram. The so-what objection is that the theorem does not evaluate the bounded gcd term in general, excludes the modular case (p=ell) where genuinely new extensions occur, excludes zero coefficients, and draws no infinite-action conclusion. Its residual result may therefore be a narrow specialization of known blockwise algorithms rather than a broadly reusable dynamical theorem. This attack constrains significance but not coherence. The manuscript does not claim the representation ledger, convolution framework, or resultant as new; it isolates the exact weighted cross-characteristic formula. It also proves rather than assumes the right-action block convention, central-character independence, corank-one singularity, and descent of nullity from the splitting field.

**Logic-chain stress test.** The key attack points were completeness of the character/nonlinear split, loss of multiplicity in the regular representation, a possible transpose or inverse from right translation, dependence on the chosen nontrivial central character, and determinant zero with kernel dimension greater than one. The dimension ledger, matrix-coefficient computation, permutation of dual types, primitive-root product, and cyclic recurrence answer each point.

**Alternative path.** A group-algebra proof using central idempotents and reduced norms would reorganize the same computation, while a direct Fourier algorithm would compute the blocks case by case. Neither route supplies a counterexample to the displayed formula; both reproduce its two strata.

**So-what and frame-lock conclusion.** The exact value lies in separating the abelian gcd contribution from the nonlinear regular-multiplicity jump for every allowed coefficient triple. The potentially hidden premise is semisimplicity; it is not hidden, because (p\ne\ell) is structural and the modular regime is explicitly excluded. No coefficient-zero or exceptional-congruence claim leaks beyond the theorem’s hypotheses.
