\section{Finite Heisenberg regular decomposition}

Let $k$ be an algebraic closure of $\Fp$.  We first remove a possible field
splitting ambiguity.

\begin{lemma}[Base change]\label{lem:base-change}
For a matrix $A$ over $\Fp$, its nullity over $\Fp$ equals the nullity of
$A\otimes_{\Fp}k$ over $k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Field extension is flat, so tensoring the exact sequence
$0\to\ker A\to\Fp^n\to\Fp^m$ with $k$ remains exact.  Equivalently, the
nonzero minors determining the rank remain nonzero after embedding
$\Fp\hookrightarrow k$.
\end{proof}

The complex Stone--von Neumann theorem supplies a standard model for finite
Heisenberg representations \citep{GurevichHadani2010}.  Grassberger and
H\"ormann give an elementary construction of all irreducible representations
of $H(\mathbb{Z}_n)$ \citep{GrassbergerHormann2001}; at prime $n=\ell$, their
classification owns the character/nontrivial-central-character type ledger
used below.  Since our splitting field has characteristic $p$, we still record
the cross-characteristic classification directly rather than silently
transferring a complex representation statement.

\begin{lemma}[Cross-characteristic irreducibles]\label{lem:irreducibles}
Over $k$, the irreducibles of $\Ql$ are the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item for every $(u,v)\in\mu_\ell(k)^2$, the character
\[
 \chi_{u,v}(a)=u,\qquad \chi_{u,v}(b)=v,\qquad \chi_{u,v}(c)=1;
\]
\item for every nontrivial $\zeta\in\mu_\ell(k)$, one degree-$\ell$ module
$\pi_\zeta$ on the basis $e_0,\ldots,e_{\ell-1}$, given by
\[
 \pi_\zeta(a)e_j=\zeta^j e_j,
 \qquad
 \pi_\zeta(b)e_j=e_{j+1\bmod\ell},
 \qquad
 \pi_\zeta(c)=\zeta I.
\]
The modules in this list are pairwise inequivalent and the list is complete.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Because $p\ne\ell$, the polynomial $t^\ell-1$ has $\ell$ distinct roots in
$k$.  The characters in (1) are therefore distinct and exhaust the
characters of the abelianization $\Ql/[\Ql,\Ql]\cong\Fell^2$.

For (2), the displayed clock and shift matrices satisfy
$\pi_\zeta(a)\pi_\zeta(b)=
\zeta\pi_\zeta(b)\pi_\zeta(a)$, matching $[a,b]=c$.  The clock matrix has
$\ell$ distinct one-dimensional eigenspaces.  Any invariant subspace is a
sum of some of them, because their spectral projectors are polynomials in
the clock matrix.  The shift matrix cyclically permutes all eigenspaces, so
a nonzero invariant subspace contains all of them.  Thus $\pi_\zeta$ is
irreducible.  Distinct values of $\zeta$ give inequivalent modules because
the center acts by different scalars.

Maschke's theorem applies over $k$, and the sum of the squared degrees of the
displayed pairwise inequivalent irreducibles is
\[
 \ell^2+(\ell-1)\ell^2=\ell^3=|\Ql|.
\]
For a split semisimple group algebra this is the full dimension ledger, so
there are no further irreducibles.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Regular-module ledger]\label{prop:regular-ledger}
Over $k$, the right regular module of $\Ql$ decomposes into the $\ell^2$
characters, each once, and the $\ell-1$ modules $\pi_\zeta$, each with
multiplicity $\ell$.  Consequently,
\begin{align}\label{eq:nullity-ledger}
 \Null(T_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma})
 &=\#\{(u,v)\in\mu_\ell(k)^2:
             \alpha+\beta u+\gamma v=0\}\notag\\
 &\quad+\ell\sum_{\substack{\zeta^\ell=1\\\zeta\ne1}}
  \Null\bigl(\alpha I+\beta\pi_\zeta(a)
                         +\gamma\pi_\zeta(b)\bigr).
\end{align}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Maschke's theorem applies because $p\nmid|\Ql|$, and
\cref{lem:irreducibles} gives the complete split list.  To freeze the
right-translation convention, set
$(R_hf)(q)=f(qh)$.  For a representation $\pi$ on $V$, identify its matrix-
coefficient space with $V^*\otimes V$ by
\[
 \varphi_{\lambda,v}(q)=\lambda(\pi(q)v).
\]
Then
\[
 R_h\varphi_{\lambda,v}(q)
 =\lambda(\pi(q)\pi(h)v)
 =\varphi_{\lambda,\pi(h)v}(q).
\]
Thus $R_h$ acts as $I_{V^*}\otimes\pi(h)$: the right regular module contains
$\dim V$ copies of $V$, and the finite operator acts on each copy by
$\alpha I+\beta\pi(a)+\gamma\pi(b)$.  Inserting the degrees from
\cref{lem:irreducibles} proves \eqref{eq:nullity-ledger}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Right versus left]\label{rem:left-right}
Our finite operator is right translation because the local rule reads
$q,qa,qb$, and the matrix-coefficient calculation above gives the displayed
$\pi(a),\pi(b)$ blocks without a transpose or inverse.  Under the common
dual convention the blocks are contragredient.  On characters this replaces
$(u,v)$ by $(u^{-1},v^{-1})$, a permutation of
$\mu_\ell(k)^2$.  On nonlinear modules it replaces the central scalar
$\zeta$ by $\zeta^{-1}$, a permutation of the nontrivial central characters.
Hence the summed nullities in \eqref{eq:nullity-ledger} are unchanged even
though individual matrices need not be identical.
\end{remark}
