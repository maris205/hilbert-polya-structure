clues exposed by the completed argument.

\(\operatorname{Tr}B^2\) form. Determine whether any functorial

## Structural clues

### Clue 1 — the mixed second term is already summable

For \(\eta>1\), off-diagonal \(G_{pq}\) decays much faster than the
diagonal prime harmonic term. A selective subtraction could therefore
be analytically possible, but it needs a source-natural justification.

### Clue 2 — positive metrics are exhausted

The commutant theorem eliminates the entire class of bounded positive
common orthogonalizing metrics. Repeating the Hellinger search with a
different positive square root cannot help.

### Clue 3 — indefinite or unbounded metrics change the question

Krein-space or unbounded completions evade the positive theorem, but
they also introduce domain, closability, and determinant problems.
They should be studied only if a source-derived necessity appears, not
as an unconstrained system expansion.

### Clue 4 — fourth-order motion is generic

The isolated \(4G_{pq}^2/(pq)\) coefficient is too robust to be a
selective invariant by itself. A next-stage invariant must depend on
global divisibility coherence rather than pairwise nonorthogonality.

## Stop condition

If functoriality forces every admissible counterterm to depend only on
the atom inventory and its generic Gram matrix, record a stronger
renormalization no-go and stop. Do not add another arbitrary block
completion merely to preserve motion.
