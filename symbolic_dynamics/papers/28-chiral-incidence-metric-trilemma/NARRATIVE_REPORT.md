# Narrative report — Paper 28 / SD-C30

## One-sentence result

The canonical reflected completion of Möbius-incidence atoms reaches
the critical line as an honest \(\mathcal S_3\) self-adjoint family
with exact fourth-order spectral motion, but that motion is generic
oblique Gram geometry, and every positive metric that removes the
obliqueness forces independent atom blocks and erases it.

## Why this paper is worth writing

Paper 27 produced something rare in this research sequence: a
source-derived finite and countable compiler whose trace-class
determinant is genuinely the Euler product on \(\Re s>1\). Its defect
was equally clear. The incidence idempotents are oblique and globally
similar to coordinate projections. Paper 28 asks the most direct bold
question: can the adjoint defect itself be used to form a chiral
completion that reaches the critical line?

The answer is not merely negative. The native completion yields an
honest compact self-adjoint family on the line and an exact spectral
moment that moves with \(t\). This clears several common formal
obstacles at once. The failure appears only after a stronger test:
the same motion survives when arithmetic is deliberately destroyed.

## The central trilemma

There are three desirable properties:

1. source-derived incidence arithmetic;
2. nontrivial critical-line self-adjoint spectral motion;
3. orthogonal, pure atom blocks retaining Euler-factor selectivity.

For the canonical completions studied here, only two can be displayed
at once.

### Native reflected metric

The native family retains source geometry and spectral motion.
However, the motion is generated by mixed Gram coefficients
\(G_{pq}\). Those coefficients are nonzero for generic oblique
incidence systems and do not distinguish prime atoms from composite
or synthetic atoms.

### Positive Hellinger/Löwdin metric

A positive metric can make the idempotents self-adjoint. The metric
rigidity theorem shows that this cannot produce a subtly coupled
orthogonal system: after the incidence similarity, every active atom
must be a one-dimensional coordinate block. The reflected spectrum is
then \(\{\pm p^{-1/2}\}\), independent of \(t\).

## What the regularization teaches

The second trace is the most visually attractive expression: a prime
harmonic diagonal plus pairwise cosine phases. It is also inadmissible
in the countable model because the operator is not Hilbert–Schmidt.
The first honest determinant is \(\det_3\). It deletes exactly the
first two powers, including the divergent second trace. Block parity
then kills the third power, so the fourth moment is the first legal
spectral signal.

This deletion is not a nuisance to hide. It is the analytic content of
the problem. The paper therefore reports the finite \(B^2\) formula
only as a cutoff diagnostic and bases its infinite motion theorem on
\(\operatorname{Tr}B^4\).

## Positive progress versus route progress

The exact fourth-frequency theorem is real mathematical progress:
unique factorization prevents cancellation, so the spectrum of the
native compact self-adjoint family cannot be stationary in \(t\).
Nevertheless it is not Route-A progress in the strict sense:

- \(B_t\) changes with \(t\);
- the determinant is a regularized chiral determinant, not the
  original Euler determinant;
- the effect is reproduced by non-arithmetic controls;
- no target-zero equivalence is present.

The right tone is therefore bold about the theorem and conservative
about its RH meaning.

## Exact controls as part of the argument

Four deterministic systems were tested with exact arithmetic:

1. standard divisibility through \(30\), using atoms \(2,3,5\);
2. a mutated divisibility relation in which \(6\) becomes an atom;
3. a divisibility subposet whose bottom covers are \(4,6,9\);
4. a seeded locally finite DAG with synthetic atom labels.

Every native fixture has a nonzero off-diagonal Gram entry and
phase-dependent second and fourth moments. Every orthogonalized
fixture has a phase-independent characteristic polynomial.

The controls are adversaries, not illustrations added after the fact.
They determine the negative arithmetic-selectivity conclusion.

## Recommended emphasis

Lead with the \(\mathcal S_3\) threshold and fourth-moment theorem.
Then disclose immediately that generic controls reproduce the effect.
Use the metric-rigidity theorem to turn this empirical nonselectivity
into a structural dichotomy. End with a precise rejected route tuple,
not a speculative promise.
