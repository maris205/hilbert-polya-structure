\section{Third regularization and surviving spectral motion}
\label{sec:regularized}

On the strip $1/3<\Re s<2/3$, define the honest modified determinant
\[
 \detthree(I-z\cB_s).
\]
For small $z$ its logarithm is
\[
 \log\detthree(I-z\cB_s)
 =-\sum_{m\ge3}\frac{z^m}{m}\Tr(\cB_s^m).
\]
The regularization deletes powers one and two.  Odd powers of an
off-diagonal block remain off diagonal, so every odd trace vanishes.
Consequently
\[
 \log\detthree(I-z\cB_s)
 =-\frac{z^4}{4}\Tr(\cB_s^4)+O(z^6).
\]
The second-trace Gram ledger is removed together with its harmonic
divergence; the fourth moment is the first legal signal.

\begin{theorem}[Isolated fourth-moment frequency]
\label{thm:fourth}
For every pair of distinct primes $p,q$, the coefficient of
\[
 \cos\left(2t\log\frac qp\right)
\]
in $\Tr\cB_{1/2+it}^4$ is
\[
 \frac{4G_{pq}^2}{pq}>0.
\]
Hence the native compact self-adjoint spectrum is nonconstant in $t$,
and $\detthree(I-z\cB_{1/2+it})$ is nonconstant in $t$ as an analytic
germ in $z$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $a_p=p^{-1/2-it}$.  On the critical line,
\[
 \Tr\cB_s^4=2\Tr\bigl((T_sT_s^*)^2\bigr).
\]
The tuple $(p,q,p,q)$ in the expansion contributes
\[
 2a_p^2\overline{a_q}^{\,2}
 \Tr\bigl((q_pq_q^*)^2\bigr).
\]
Since $q_pq_q^*$ has rank at most one,
\[
 \Tr\bigl((q_pq_q^*)^2\bigr)
 =\Tr(q_pq_q^*)^2=G_{pq}^2.
\]
Adding the conjugate tuple gives the stated cosine coefficient.

No other tuple can cancel it: a quotient of two products of two primes
equals $q^2/p^2$ only when unique factorization forces the numerator
multiset to be $\{q,q\}$ and the denominator multiset to be
$\{p,p\}$.  Prime cutoffs converge uniformly in $\cS_4$ for all
real $t$; continuity of the fourth trace moment passes the isolated
coefficient to the countable limit.  See \cref{app:fourier} for the
continuity estimate.
\end{proof}

For $(p,q,\eta)=(2,3,2)$, the isolated coefficient is exactly
\[
 \frac{3675}{971618\pi^8}
 \cos\left(2t\log\frac32\right).
\]

\begin{remark}[What has and has not survived]
\Cref{thm:fourth} proves genuine spectral motion, not merely motion of
eigenvectors.  It does not restore the original Euler trace ledger:
the determinant is third-regularized, its first visible term is
fourth order, and its coefficients involve mixed Gram cycles.  The
adversaries in \cref{sec:audit} show that this surviving motion is not
arithmetically selective.
\end{remark}
