\section{Comparison, scope, and controls}\label{sec:scope}

The finite-shape theorem sits at the intersection of several established
frameworks, so its claim boundary matters as much as its calculation.

\paragraph{Multiplicative symbolic systems.}
Kenyon, Peres, and Solomyak established a foundational setting for symbolic
spaces invariant under multiplication and developed dimension formulas and a
variational principle \citep{KenyonPeresSolomyak2012}.  Ban, Hu, and Lin
subsequently studied pattern generation, spatial entropy, and Minkowski
dimensions for multiplicative systems \citep{BanHuLin2019}.  Closely related
two-generator dimension questions for the semigroup generated by $2$ and $3$
were studied by Peres, Schmeling, Seuret, and Solomyak
\citep{PeresSchmelingSeuretSolomyak2014}.  These works provide the established
setting for the present problem.  We use that setting, not their dimension or
entropy machinery: the finite-field space here is solved by an explicit
linear parameterization.

Ban, Hu, Lai, and Liao compute Hausdorff and Minkowski dimensions for affine
multiplicative shifts whose constraints couple shifted indices of the forms
$pk+a$ and $qk+b$ \citep{BanHuLaiLiaoAffine2025}.  That affine index geometry is
a direct multiplicative-shift neighbor, but it neither imposes the mixed
plaquette difference in \eqref{eq:def-X} nor identifies the globally extendable
patterns on arbitrary finite coordinate sets.

\paragraph{Coupled and axial-product entropy.}
Ban, Hu, and Lai study entropy formulas for multidimensional multiplicative
integer subshifts with coupling constraints \citep{BanHuLai2021}.  Ban, Hu,
Lai, and Liao calculate entropy and surface entropy for axial products of
subshifts and multiplicative subshifts \citep{BanHuLaiLiao2025}.  Those
results provide broad entropy and surface-complexity context.  Our rectangle
formula is called a boundary law only as an exact count; it is not presented
as a new general surface-entropy theorem.

\paragraph{Valuation coordinates and correlations.}
Mora Cuellar, Rojas Aravena, and Yavicoli use prime-valuation coordinates to
derive additive and multiplicative density statements, exact finite-coordinate
correlations, random models, and symbolic realizations
\citep{MoraCuellarRojasAravenaYavicoli2026}.  Their work is the closest current
source for valuation-coordinate correlations.  The present Haar calculation
concerns a different object: a finite-field linear plaquette constraint whose
arbitrary coordinate dependence is represented by root-wise graphic
matroids.

\paragraph{Matroid and information-theoretic ingredients.}
The linear-dependence language follows Whitney's matroid framework
\citep{Whitney1935}, and total correlation is the multivariate entropy deficit
introduced by Watanabe \citep{Watanabe1960}.  Kir\'aly, Rosen, and Theran study
algebraic matroids and matroids with row--column graph symmetry, motivated in
part by matrix completion and rigidity \citep{KiralyRosenTheran2013}.  Their
framework owns the graph-symmetric matroid neighborhood, but not the arithmetic
evaluation matroid of \eqref{eq:def-X}.  Abbe and Spirkl record the general
mechanism by which a finite-field representable matroid is realized by an
entropy-rank function under uniform linear random variables
\citep{AbbeSpirkl2019}.  Thus neither incidence-matroid language nor the general
linear entropy-rank mechanism is claimed here.

The identity $y_{i,j}=u_i+v_j$ is likewise an elementary integration of a
factorized mixed difference.  After these owners are subtracted, the residual
P67 statement is the explicit global free-axis homeomorphism for
\eqref{eq:def-X}, the identification of every globally extendable finite
projection with the direct sum of the particular graphic matroids $M(G_r(F))$,
and the resulting prefix, rectangle, and Haar forest/cycle formulas.

\paragraph{Deterministic controls.}
The companion standard-library Python program checks root coordinates,
global reconstruction, prefix ranks, every subset of $[12]$ in three
finite-field/multiplier cases, exponent rectangles through side length six,
exact Haar potential counts in characteristics $2$, $3$, and $5$, and the
edge-deletion/addition rank dichotomy.  These checks exercise composite
coprime multipliers and the characteristic-two sign convention.  They are
regression evidence only; none replaces
\Cref{lem:root-decomposition,prop:global-homeo,prop:cycle-complete}.

The structural rank theorem itself works over any field.  Finiteness of
$\F_q$ enters only when rank is converted into pattern count and Shannon
entropy.  Extending the arithmetic decomposition to noncoprime multipliers is
a separate problem, as is computing a dynamical entropy after choosing a
specific multiplicative action and F\o lner sequence.
