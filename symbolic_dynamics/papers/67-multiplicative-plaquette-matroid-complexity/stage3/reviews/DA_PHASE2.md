## Review Body

**Genuine strength.** The manuscript makes its central reduction explicit and testable: the mixed-difference equation is integrated to a row-plus-column potential, and the same potential map is then used consistently for the global inverse, arbitrary finite projections, the graphic matroid, and the Haar entropy identities. The scope paragraph also prevents either finite-geometry rate from being silently promoted to a dynamical entropy.

**Strongest counter-argument.** A skeptical reader can argue that the paper turns one elementary coordinate integration into several headlines without establishing that the resulting package changes what can be done with multiplicative symbolic systems. Once coprimality gives the unique coordinates (n=r a^i b^j), the local rule is simply a vanishing mixed difference and every solution has the form (u_i+v_j). The arbitrary finite projection is then the image of a familiar bipartite vertex-potential map; its rank, cycle constraints, graphic matroid, and forest criterion are standard consequences of incidence linear algebra. The prefix formula merely counts the free axes, while the rectangle formula is the rank of (K_{M,N}). Haar entropy and total correlation add no independent mechanism because uniform measure on a finite linear image converts those same ranks directly into logarithms. On this reading, “arithmetic-prefix complexity,” “boundary law,” and “matroidal dependence” are different descriptions of a single elementary fact, and neither geometry yields an entropy theorem for a specified multiplicative action. A more parsimonious paper could state the factorized group-ring relation, identify the componentwise coboundary complex, and derive all counts in one short proposition. The strongest so-what challenge is therefore whether arbitrary-shape organization produces a genuinely new consequence beyond repackaging. This counterargument limits the perceived contribution, but it does not break the proof: the manuscript openly subtracts the standard ingredients, supplies the global extension step needed for the finite matroid claim, and keeps every dynamical conclusion within its stated boundary.

**Logic-chain stress test.** The vulnerable transitions were the uniqueness of composite-base root coordinates, extension from finitely used vertex potentials to a global solution, identification of evaluation dependence with the incidence representation, and conversion of Haar image size to independence. Each transition is explicitly justified. In particular, the prefix pivot calculation is not used as a substitute for global extension, closing the most plausible circularity.

**Alternative path.** A cochain formulation on each root-wise bipartite graph, or an equivalent linear-code formulation using a parity-check cycle basis, would reach the same finite-projection theorem more economically. That route corroborates rather than contradicts the stated result.

**So-what and frame-lock conclusion.** The strongest residual value is the all-finite-shape organization tying arithmetic coordinates to a concrete representable matroid. The potential hidden premise is that two natural finite geometries should be compared by one normalization; the manuscript expressly rejects that premise and reports the two rates as different sampling laws. No unexamined premise was found that invalidates the central chain.
