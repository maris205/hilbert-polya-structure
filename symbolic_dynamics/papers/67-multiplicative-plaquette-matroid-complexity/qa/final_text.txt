Abstract. Let a, b ≥ 2 be coprime and let Fq be a finite field. We study the compact linear
multiplicative constraint space
Xa,b = {x ∈ FNq : xn − xan − xbn + xabn = 0 for every n ≥ 1}.
Every integer has unique coordinates rai bj , and on each root component the rule integrates to
xrai bj = ui + vj . This yields an explicit topological-group isomorphism between Xa,b and the
unrestricted coordinates whose indices are not divisible by ab. We then calculate every finite
projection. A coordinate rai bj is viewed as an edge from row vertex i to column vertex j; the
projection dimension is the sum of the number of vertices minus the number of connected
components over the resulting root-wise bipartite graphs. Thus the coordinate-dependence
matroid is a direct sum of graphic matroids, and the only finite compatibility conditions
are alternating cycle sums. Under Haar measure, forests are exactly the jointly independent
coordinate families and each cycle-rank unit contributes log q of total correlation. Arithmetic
prefixes have q N −⌊N/(ab)⌋ patterns, whereas an M × N exponent rectangle has q M +N −1
patterns. These are two normalizations of one finite-shape rank formula, not interchangeable
entropy statements.

1. Introduction
Multiplicative symbolic constraints come with more than one natural finite geometry.
Arithmetic prefixes retain the indices 1, . . . , N , while prime-valuation or exponent coordinates
organize a multiplicative orbit into a lattice. Pattern counts in these geometries need not have
the same scale. The difference is structural, not a normalization error.
The foundational multiplicative-integer framework and its dimension theory were developed
by Kenyon, Peres, and Solomyak [4]. Two-generator dimension problems and pattern generation
for multiplicative systems, as well as coupled multidimensional systems, axial products, and
surface entropy, are treated in [1–3, 6]. Prime-valuation coordinates also support exact
density and correlation constructions, including recent symbolic realizations [5]. Against this
established background, we isolate one finite-field linear rule and ask for an exact answer on
every finite coordinate set.

The proof has three short layers. First, the multiplicative root decomposition turns (1)
into a vanishing mixed difference on N20 , whose solutions are sums of one row and one column
potential. Second, restriction to an arbitrary finite set becomes a vertex-potential map on

Haar measure pushes forward uniformly to every finite image, converting rank and cycle rank
into exact entropy and total correlation.

6. Comparison, scope, and controls
The finite-shape theorem sits at the intersection of several established frameworks, so its
claim boundary matters as much as its calculation.

MULTIPLICATIVE PLAQUETTES AND GRAPHIC MATROIDS

9

Multiplicative symbolic systems. Kenyon, Peres, and Solomyak established a foundational
setting for symbolic spaces invariant under multiplication and developed dimension formulas
and a variational principle [4]. Ban, Hu, and Lin subsequently studied pattern generation,
spatial entropy, and Minkowski dimensions for multiplicative systems [1]. Closely related
two-generator dimension questions for the semigroup generated by 2 and 3 were studied by
Peres, Schmeling, Seuret, and Solomyak [6]. These works provide the established setting for
the present problem. We use that setting, not their dimension or entropy machinery: the
finite-field space here is solved by an explicit linear parameterization.
Coupled and axial-product entropy. Ban, Hu, and Lai study entropy formulas for multidimensional multiplicative integer subshifts with coupling constraints [2]. Ban, Hu, Lai, and
Liao calculate entropy and surface entropy for axial products of subshifts and multiplicative
subshifts [3]. Those results provide broad entropy and surface-complexity context. Our
rectangle formula is called a boundary law only as an exact count; it is not presented as a new
general surface-entropy theorem.
Valuation coordinates and correlations. Mora Cuellar, Rojas Aravena, and Yavicoli use primevaluation coordinates to derive additive and multiplicative density statements, exact finitecoordinate correlations, random models, and symbolic realizations [5]. Their work is the
closest current source for valuation-coordinate correlations. The present Haar calculation
concerns a different object: a finite-field linear plaquette constraint whose arbitrary coordinate
dependence is represented by root-wise graphic matroids.
Standard matroid and information-theoretic ingredients. The linear-dependence language
follows Whitney’s matroid framework [8], and total correlation is the multivariate entropy
deficit introduced by Watanabe [7]. Incidence matrices representing graphic matroids and
the entropy definition are standard ingredients. The identity yi,j = ui + vj is likewise an
elementary integration of a factorized mixed difference. The paper-specific result is the explicit
arithmetic assembly of these ingredients into the all-finite-shape projection and forest/cycle
formulas stated here.

decomposition to noncoprime multipliers is a separate problem, as is computing a dynamical
entropy after choosing a specific multiplicative action and Følner sequence.
7. Conclusion
The multiplicative plaquette rule has a global set of free coordinates: on each root component,
the two exponent axes determine the entire array, and across all roots these axes are exactly
the arithmetic indices not divisible by ab. This gives a concrete product homeomorphism
rather than only a finite-rank count.
The arbitrary finite-shape theorem adds the missing local organization. Selected coordinates
become edges of root-wise bipartite graphs, allowed labels are vertex-potential coboundaries,
and graph cycles are the complete compatibility obstruction. As a result, the coordinate
matroid is graphic and normalized Haar dependence is exact: forests give joint independence,
while each cycle-rank unit contributes log q of total correlation. The pairwise-independent but
plaquette-dependent four-corner example is the smallest instance of this rule.
Arithmetic prefixes and exponent rectangles are therefore not isolated formulas. The former
have
q L−⌊L/(ab)⌋
patterns because they retain a positive density of free axes. The latter have q M +N −1 patterns
because a complete exponent box uses only M + N − 1 independent vertex potentials. Both
follow from the same finite-shape rank, but their normalizations describe different geometries.
A separate choice of action and averaging sequence is required before either calculation can be
promoted to a dynamical entropy statement.
The manuscript makes no priority claim. Its proof package is closed at the internal-draft level,
while external release remains contingent on specialist exact-neighbor review in multiplicative
symbolic dynamics, algebraic actions, finite-field coding, and matroidal probability.
