\section{Reflection without vertical sterility}
\label{sec:reflection}

Define the reflected determinant on
\[
 \Omega_{\mathrm{ref}}=
 \{(s,z):0<\RePart s<1,\
 |z|2^{-\min(\RePart s,1-\RePart s)}<1\}       \tag{5.1}
\]
by
\[
 H(s,z)=R(s,z)R(1-s,z).                         \tag{5.2}
\]
The primary slice $z=1$ lies in this domain for every $0<\RePart s<1$.

\begin{theorem}[Common-strip reflection and zero-freeness]
\label{thm:reflection}
The function $H$ is holomorphic and nonzero on $\Omega_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and
satisfies
\[
 H(s,z)=H(1-s,z).                               \tag{5.3}
\]
For real $z$, it also obeys
$H(\bar s,z)=\overline{H(s,z)}$.  In particular,
\[
 H(\tfrac12+it,1)=|R(\tfrac12+it,1)|^2>0.      \tag{5.4}
 \label{eq:critical-modulus}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two factors are holomorphic and nonzero where $\RePart s>0$ and
$\RePart(1-s)>0$, respectively.  Their common domain is the open critical
strip with the stated $z$ bound.  Swapping $s$ and $1-s$ exchanges the two
factors.  Conjugation follows from the real entropy inventory and fixed real
grading.  On the critical line $1-s=\bar s$, which gives \eqref{eq:critical-modulus}.
\end{proof}

Zero-freeness rules out the desired divisor, but it does not mean that the
reflection completion is constant.  The next theorem separates those two
properties exactly.

\begin{theorem}[Strict center curvature]
\label{thm:motion}
Let
\[
 \Phi(t)=\log H(\tfrac12+it,1).
\]
Then $\Phi$ is real analytic, even, and
\[
 \Phi''(0)=2\sum_{n\ge1}
 \bigl[g(p_{2n-1})-g(p_{2n})\bigr]>0,           \tag{5.5}
 \label{eq:curvature}
\]
where
\[
 g(x)=\frac{x^{-1/2}(\log x)^2}{(1-x^{-1/2})^2}.                    \tag{5.6}
\]
Therefore $H(1/2+it,1)$, and hence the modulus of $R$ on the critical line,
has nontrivial vertical motion.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The locally uniform relative product permits termwise differentiation near
$t=0$.  For $q=x^{-1/2}$,
\[
 \frac{\dd^2}{\dd t^2}
 \log|1-qe^{-it\log x}|\bigg|_{t=0}
 =\frac{q(\log x)^2}{(1-q)^2}=g(x).             \tag{5.7}
\]
The modulus square in \cref{eq:critical-modulus} supplies the factor two and
the entropy grading supplies the adjacent difference.

It remains to determine the sign.  Put $y=\sqrt{x}>1$.  Then
\[
 g(x)=\frac{4y(\log y)^2}{(y-1)^2}.
\]
Its logarithmic derivative is negative precisely when
\[
 \log y>\frac{2(y-1)}{y+1}.                     \tag{5.8}
\]
The difference between the two sides vanishes at $y=1$ and has derivative
$(y-1)^2/[y(y+1)^2]>0$.  Thus $g$ is strictly decreasing.  Every summand in
\eqref{eq:curvature} is positive, and the series converges by the alternating
series criterion applied to the decreasing sequence $g(p_k)\to0$.  Its sum is
strictly positive.
\end{proof}

This theorem marks genuine progress over a balanced two-layer double: the
reflection operation no longer cancels every vertical frequency.  Yet the
motion is carried by a relative signed inventory, not by a positive Euler
determinant, and the same theorem proves that no zero occurs in the strip.
