# Paper Plan

## One-sentence contribution

Entropy-adjacent `1|1` pairing gives an exact relative Fredholm determinant on
`Re(s)>0` and a moving reflected completion on `0<Re(s)<1`, but a finite-block
rigidity theorem proves that the same local cancellation necessarily destroys
the all-positive Euler orientation.

## Claims and evidence

| Claim | Evidence |
|---|---|
| The paired difference is trace class on `Re(s)>0` | Disjoint-interval integral bound |
| `R(s,z)` is an exact relative determinant | `I+S_1` quotient and trace-series expansion |
| `H(s,z)` has a formal exact reflection and common strip | Half-plane intersection; symmetry is tautological, not an A3 functional equation |
| `H(s,1)` moves on the critical line | Strict positive center curvature |
| The arithmetic ledger is wrong | Fixed entropy-rank sign in every relative trace |
| Finite local blocks cannot keep all primes positive | Zero-block-sum rigidity theorem |
| The mechanism proves too much | Shifted, blocked, and non-prime inventories pass |

## Section architecture

1. Abstract: theorem, common strip, motion, and obstruction.
2. Introduction: the trace-class bottleneck and one-step result preview.
3. Related work: symbolic zeta determinants, signed symbolic constructions,
   and relative determinants.
4. Source lock: tensor atoms, entropy order, `1|1` grammar, function spaces,
   transfers, pairing unitary, and determinant convention.
5. Relative determinant theorem: trace norm, all repetitions, product, and
   logarithmic derivative.
6. Reflected completion: common strip, zero-freeness, and strict vertical
   motion.
7. Orientation and finite-block rigidity: fixed parity versus cocycle phase.
8. Exact controls: shifted pairings, larger blocks, and arbitrary inventories.
9. Conservative Route-A rejection and Route-B lock.
10. Conclusion and proof appendix.

Target: a compact 10--14 page theorem paper. No peer-review or revision loop
is part of this stage.
