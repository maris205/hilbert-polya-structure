\begin{abstract}
Choose a sink in the $n$-vertex part of the complete bipartite graph
$K_{m,n}$.  On recurrent Abelian sandpiles, any integral loading profile
$w$ defines a permutation $T_w$.  We compute every coordinate of
$Q_{m,n}^{-1}w$ and hence the exact order $L(w)$ as a finite least common
multiple of explicit reduced denominators.  All $m^{n-1}n^{m-1}$ recurrent
states then split into cycles of the same length $L(w)$, giving every iterate
fixed-point count, the Artin--Mazur zeta function, all ergodic invariant
measures, and the Koopman spectrum.  A two-site specialization has up to four
denominator classes, with three when $n=2$.  In particular, adding at a vertex opposite the sink
has period $mn$, whereas adding at another vertex in the sink part has period
$m$.  Exact reduced-Laplacian checks cover both arbitrary and two-site
profiles; literal burning, stabilization, and orbit enumeration cover every
$K_{m,n}$ with $2\leq m,n\leq4$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The Abelian sandpile model turns local chip addition followed by stabilization
into a finite dynamical system.  Dhar's addition operators commute, and their
restriction to recurrent configurations forms a finite Abelian group
\cite{Dhar1990,DharEtAl1995}.  Algebraically this group is the cokernel of the
reduced graph Laplacian.  These facts convert a dynamical question---the
period of a prescribed loading protocol---into an integral lattice question.

Complete bipartite graphs have an especially explicit critical group.
Lorenzini computed its bipartite form \cite{Lorenzini1991};
\citet{JacobsonEtAl2003} review that case and solve the complete multipartite
problem.  Recurrent and parking configurations on $K_{m,n}$ also have a rich
operator and cyclic-lemma description \cite{AvalEtAl2016}, and recent work
compares Abelian and stochastic recurrent models on the same graph family
\cite{SeligZhu2025}.  We take these structures as prior art.  Our narrower
objective is to retain the physical coordinates of an arbitrary loading
profile relative to a fixed sink, rather than pass immediately to an abstract
Smith normal form, and to compute the resulting deterministic orbit data.

The calculation has a useful asymmetry.  The sink breaks the exchange between
the two parts.  A chip added at a vertex opposite the sink generates a cycle
of length $mn$, while a chip added beside the sink has period only $m$.
For a general loading, the exact period is an elementary but nontrivial
arithmetic function of the entire profile.  Once that period is known,
translation on a finite group forces a uniform cycle decomposition, and the
zeta function and Koopman spectrum become closed formulas.

\section{Sandpile translations}

Let
\[
 A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\},\qquad
 B=\{b_1,\ldots,b_{n-1},q\}
\]
be the two parts of $K_{m,n}$, with $q$ designated as the sink.  We assume
$m,n\geq2$.  A stable sandpile has heights $0,\ldots,n-1$ on vertices of
$A$ and $0,\ldots,m-1$ on nonsink vertices of $B$.  When a nonsink reaches
its degree, it topples one chip along every incident edge; chips reaching
$q$ disappear.  Stabilization is independent of toppling order.

Let $\Rcal_{m,n}$ be the recurrent configurations.  Stabilized componentwise
addition makes $\Rcal_{m,n}$ a group isomorphic to
\[
 \Z^{m+n-1}/Q_{m,n}\Z^{m+n-1},
\]
where, in the order $A,B\setminus\{q\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laplacian}
 Q_{m,n}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 nI_m&-J_{m,n-1}\\
 -J_{n-1,m}&mI_{n-1}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The matrix-tree theorem gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tree-count}
 |\Rcal_{m,n}|=\det Q_{m,n}=m^{n-1}n^{m-1}.
\end{equation}

Let
\[
 w=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_m,
       \beta_1,\ldots,\beta_{n-1})\in\Z^{m+n-1}.
\]
For a nonnegative profile, $T_w$ adds the indicated chips and stabilizes.  It
is a permutation of $\Rcal_{m,n}$.  Integral profiles are then defined using
inverse addition operators on the recurrent group.  In the cokernel, $T_w$
is translation by $[w]$.  Put
\[
 A_w=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_i,
 \qquad B_w=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\beta_j.
\]

For $N\geq1$ and $t\in\Z$, put
\[
 d_N(t)=\frac{N}{\gcd(N,t)}.
\]
Thus $d_N(0)=1$ and $d_N(t)$ is the reduced denominator of $t/N$.

\section{The localized period formula}

\begin{lemma}[order from a reduced Laplacian]\label{lem:denominator}
If $Q$ is an invertible integer matrix and $w\in\Z^r$, then the order of
$[w]$ in $\Z^r/Q\Z^r$ is the least positive integer $L$ for which
$LQ^{-1}w\in\Z^r$.  Equivalently, it is the least common multiple of the
reduced denominators of the coordinates of $Q^{-1}w$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The multiple $L[w]$ vanishes exactly when $Lw=Qz$ for some $z\in\Z^r$,
which is equivalent to $LQ^{-1}w=z$ being integral.
\end{proof}

For an arbitrary profile define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-period}
 \begin{split}
 L_{m,n}(w)=\lcmop\Big(&
 \{d_{mn}((n-1)A_w+nB_w+m\alpha_i):1\leq i\leq m\},\\
 &\{d_m(A_w+B_w+\beta_j):1\leq j<n\}\Big).
 \end{split}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[arbitrary-profile loading period]\label{thm:general-period}
For every $m,n\geq2$ and $w\in\Z^{m+n-1}$,
\[
 \ord(T_w)=L_{m,n}(w).
\]
More explicitly,
\begin{align}
 (Q_{m,n}^{-1}w)_{a_i}
 &=\frac{(n-1)A_w+nB_w+m\alpha_i}{mn},\label{eq:general-A}\\
 (Q_{m,n}^{-1}w)_{b_j}
 &=\frac{A_w+B_w+\beta_j}{m}.\label{eq:general-B}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $u_i$ and $v_j$ for the coordinates of $Q_{m,n}^{-1}w$, and put
$U=\sum_i u_i$, $V=\sum_jv_j$.  The reduced-Laplacian equations are
\[
 nu_i-V=\alpha_i,\qquad mv_j-U=\beta_j.
\]
Summing the two families gives
\[
 nU-mV=A_w,\qquad mV-(n-1)U=B_w.
\]
Thus $U=A_w+B_w$ and
$mV=(n-1)A_w+nB_w$.  Substitution proves
equations~\eqref{eq:general-A} and~\eqref{eq:general-B}.  The order formula
follows from \cref{lem:denominator}.
\end{proof}

Fix $a=a_1$ and $b=b_1$, specialize to
$w_{\alpha,\beta}=\alpha e_a+\beta e_b$, and write
$T_{\alpha,\beta}=T_{w_{\alpha,\beta}}$.  Define
\begin{align}
 L_{m,n}(\alpha,\beta)
 =\lcmop\big(&d_{mn}(\alpha(m+n-1)+\beta n),
 d_{mn}(\alpha(n-1)+\beta n),\notag\\
 &d_m(\alpha+2\beta),
 d_m(\alpha+\beta)\big),\label{eq:period}
\end{align}
where the final term is omitted when $n=2$.

\begin{corollary}[two-site and single-site periods]\label{cor:period}
For every $m,n\geq2$ and $\alpha,\beta\in\Z$,
\[
 \ord(T_{\alpha,\beta})=L_{m,n}(\alpha,\beta).
\]
In particular,
\[
 \ord(T_{1,0})=mn,\qquad \ord(T_{0,1})=m.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Specializing \cref{thm:general-period} gives the following coordinates at
the distinguished vertex $a$ and at each other vertex of $A$, respectively:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:A-coordinates}
 \frac{\alpha(m+n-1)+\beta n}{mn},
 \qquad
 \frac{\alpha(n-1)+\beta n}{mn}.
\end{equation}
At $b$ and at every other nonsink vertex of $B$, respectively, they are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B-coordinates}
 \frac{\alpha+2\beta}{m},
 \qquad
 \frac{\alpha+\beta}{m}.
\end{equation}
The second kind in \eqref{eq:B-coordinates} is absent when $b$ is the sole
nonsink vertex of $B$.  These reduced denominators prove \eqref{eq:period}.

For $(\alpha,\beta)=(0,1)$, the $A$ coordinates already have denominator
$m$, and every displayed denominator divides $m$.  Thus the order is $m$.
For $(1,0)$, integrality of the difference between the two $A$ coordinates
forces $n\mid L$, and the $B$ coordinates force $m\mid L$.  If
$g=\gcd(m,n)>1$, write any common multiple of $m,n$ as
$L=(mn/g)t$.  The other-$A$ coordinate after multiplication by $L$ is
$t(n-1)/g$.  Since $g\mid n$ and $\gcd(g,n-1)=1$, integrality forces
$g\mid t$.  Thus $mn\mid L$, and the least common multiple is $mn$.
\end{proof}

The formula retains more information than the abstract decomposition
\[
 K(K_{m,n})\cong(\Z/m\Z)^{n-2}\oplus
 (\Z/n\Z)^{m-2}\oplus\Z/(mn)\Z
\]
because it locates the physical loading vector relative to a chosen sink and
the vertex parts.

\section{Orbit census, zeta function, and spectrum}

Put $M_{m,n}=m^{n-1}n^{m-1}$ and $L=L_{m,n}(w)$.

\begin{theorem}[complete translation dynamics]\label{thm:dynamics}
The map $T_w$ partitions $\Rcal_{m,n}$ into exactly
$M_{m,n}/L$ cycles, all of length $L$.  For every $k\geq1$,
\[
 |\Fix(T_w^k)|=
 \begin{cases}
 M_{m,n},&L\mid k,\\
 0,&L\nmid k.
 \end{cases}
\]
Its Artin--Mazur zeta function is
\[
 \zeta_{T_w}(z)
 =\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{|\Fix(T_w^k)|}{k}z^k\right)
 =(1-z^L)^{-M_{m,n}/L}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On the recurrent group, the map is translation by an element $g$ of order
$L$.  The orbit through any $x$ is the coset $x+\langle g\rangle$, so it has
exactly $L$ points.  The cosets partition the group.  Translation by $kg$ has
a fixed point exactly when $kg=0$, in which case it is the identity.  The
fixed-count formula follows, and
\[
 \exp\left(\frac{M_{m,n}}L
       \sum_{j\geq1}\frac{z^{jL}}j\right)
 =(1-z^L)^{-M_{m,n}/L}
\]
gives the zeta function.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[invariant measures and Koopman spectrum]\label{cor:spectrum}
The ergodic invariant probability measures are precisely the uniform
measures on the $M_{m,n}/L$ cycles.  On
$\ell^2(\Rcal_{m,n})$ with counting measure, every $L$th root of unity is a
Koopman eigenvalue with multiplicity $M_{m,n}/L$, and there are no other
eigenvalues.  The topological and measure-theoretic entropies are zero.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each finite orbit supports one ergodic invariant measure, and every invariant
measure is a convex combination of these.  On each $L$-cycle the Koopman
operator is the regular cyclic permutation, whose eigenvalues are the $L$th
roots of unity, each once.  Taking the direct sum over all cycles gives the
multiplicities.  A finite permutation has zero entropy.
\end{proof}

For example, a single chip added opposite the sink has
\[
 \zeta_{T_{1,0}}(z)
 =(1-z^{mn})^{-m^{n-2}n^{m-2}},
\]
whereas a single chip added in the sink part has
\[
 \zeta_{T_{0,1}}(z)
 =(1-z^{m})^{-m^{n-2}n^{m-1}}.
\]

\section{Literal finite control and ownership boundary}

The companion script carries out two independent checks.  First, for
$2\leq m,n\leq8$, it constructs $Q_{m,n}$, verifies its determinant,
computes $Q_{m,n}^{-1}w$ over exact rationals for both two-site and
arbitrary deterministic profiles, and compares all reduced denominators with
equations~\eqref{eq:general-period} and~\eqref{eq:period}.
Second, for every $2\leq m,n\leq4$, it enumerates stable configurations,
applies Dhar's burning test, performs literal legal stabilization, and checks
the predicted cycle length for four nonnegative loading vectors.  These are
regression controls, not replacements for the proof.

The sandpile group and addition-operator framework are owned by
\citet{Dhar1990,DharEtAl1995}; the bipartite critical group is due to
\citet{Lorenzini1991} and is reviewed in the multipartite treatment of
\citet{JacobsonEtAl2003}; detailed $K_{m,n}$ configuration operators and
cyclic combinatorics appear in \citet{AvalEtAl2016}; and
\citet{SeligZhu2025} is a current comparator on recurrent complete-bipartite
sandpiles.  Finite-group translation orbit facts are also not claimed.  The
residual statement here is only the sink-relative coordinate/order formula
for physical loading profiles and its recorded dynamical consequences.  A
bounded search through 27 August 2026 found no exact collision with that
combined package.  This is not an absolute priority claim, and the manuscript
remains on external-release hold.
