\section{Tree emptiness and Fredholm failure}
\label{sec:tree-fredholm}

\subsection{No periodic actual-edge geodesic}

\begin{lemma}[Full-tree emptiness]
\label{lem:no-period}
The full oriented-edge geodesic shift on $\Trree$ has no periodic point of
positive period.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $(e_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z}$ has period $n>0$.  The word
$e_0e_1\cdots e_{n-1}$ is reduced because immediate reversal is forbidden.
Periodicity identifies the terminal vertex after one period with the initial
vertex, so the word is a positive reduced closed path.  A tree has no such
path.  This contradiction proves the claim.
\end{proof}

The qualifier ``actual edge'' matters.  If $e_n=g e_0$ for some nontrivial
$g\in\Gr$, the segment is an orbital fundamental segment, not a period of the
raw edge sequence.

\subsection{An infinite orthogonal-column witness}

\begin{proposition}[Noncompactness]
\label{prop:noncompact}
The operator $\Hash$ in \eqref{eq:hashimoto} is noncompact and therefore not
trace class.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose oriented edges $e_1,e_2,\ldots$ with distinct terminal vertices.  For
$i\ne j$, the support of $\Hash\delta_{e_i}$ consists of oriented edges whose
origin is $t(e_i)$, while the support of $\Hash\delta_{e_j}$ has origin
$t(e_j)$.  The supports are disjoint.  Since $\Trree$ has degree $r+1$,
\[
  \langle \Hash\delta_{e_i},\Hash\delta_{e_j}\rangle=0,
  \qquad
  \|\Hash\delta_{e_j}\|_2^2=r.
\]
Thus the image of an orthonormal sequence has no convergent subsequence.
The operator is not compact.  Every trace-class operator is compact, which
proves the second assertion.
\end{proof}

For $r=1$, this witness is the bilateral shift along either orientation of
the line.  For $r\ge2$, each image has $r$ legal nonbacktracking
continuations.

\begin{corollary}[Canonical modular weights do not repair trace class]
\label{cor:modular-noncompact}
Fix a finite complex parameter $s$.  Any nonzero per-step scalar weighting
derived only from $\modc^{-s}$ remains noncompact on the full tree.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
One tree step changes height by $+1$ or $-1$.  The two possible transition
magnitudes are $r^{-\operatorname{Re}s}$ and
$r^{\operatorname{Re}s}$ (both one when $r=1$).  Restrict the family in
\Cref{prop:noncompact} to one oriented type.  Its images remain pairwise
orthogonal with a fixed positive norm.
\end{proof}

A potential $r^{-s\beta(x)}$ based on an absolute Busemann coordinate is not
the per-step cocycle: choosing its zero chooses a basepoint, and one tree
direction makes the reciprocal weight grow.  A different radial damping may
be analytically useful, as finite-total-weight graph theory illustrates
\citep{deitmar2015ihara}, but it is outside the frozen object.

\subsection{Why formal zero traces are insufficient}

By \Cref{lem:no-period}, every diagonal matrix coefficient
$\langle\delta_e,\Hash^n\delta_e\rangle$ vanishes.  It is tempting to write
\[
  \Tr(\Hash^n)=0,
  \qquad
  \det(I-z\Hash)=
  \exp\!\left(-\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{z^n}{n}\Tr(\Hash^n)\right)=1.
  \label{eq:unowned-formal}
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:unowned-formal} is not an ordinary Fredholm identity.
The trace-log expansion requires trace-class ownership before the diagonal is
summed.  \Cref{prop:noncompact} rules out that premise.  A groupoid or
von Neumann trace can be meaningful in another framework
\citep{clair2001zeta,lenz2019ihara}; it must be named as that framework and
cannot be relabeled as the ordinary trace on $\ell^2(\Eor\Trree)$.

\subsection{Action, properness, and the common end}

Let $\rho_r:\Gr\to\Aut(\Trree)$ be the action homomorphism and put
$H_r=\rho_r(\Gr)$.  The base vertex is the coset $A$.  Its stabilizer for the
original action is $A\cong\mathbb Z$, and all vertex stabilizers are conjugate
copies; edge stabilizers are also infinite cyclic.  In the compact-open
topology, a subgroup of the automorphism group of a locally finite tree is
discrete precisely when its vertex stabilizer is finite
\citep{clair2001zeta}.  This criterion applies to the image $H_r$.

For $r\ge2$, the kernel is the core of $A$ and
\[
  \ker\rho_r\subseteq
  \bigcap_{n\ge0}v^nAv^{-n}
  =\bigcap_{n\ge0}\langle u^{r^n}\rangle=\{1\}.
\]
Thus the action is faithful.  The base-vertex stabilizer in $H_r$ is the
infinite group $\rho_r(A)\cong\mathbb Z$, so $H_r$ is non-discrete.

For $r=1$, $G_1=A\times\langle v\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2$ and $\Trree$ is a
line.  The subgroup $A=\langle u\rangle$ fixes the line pointwise, while $v$
translates it by one edge.  Hence
\[
  \ker\rho_1=A,
  \qquad H_1\cong\langle v\rangle\cong\mathbb Z,
\]
and $H_1$ is discrete.  The original $G_1$ action is nevertheless non-proper
because its vertex and edge stabilizers contain the infinite kernel.  It is
not a finite-stabilizer tree-lattice action.  Replacing it by $G_1/A$ changes
the acting group and collapses the original group-conjugacy ledger.

For $r\ge2$, the ascending action fixes a common end.  The union
\begin{equation}
  N_r=\bigcup_{j\ge0}v^{-j}Av^j\cong\mathbb Z[1/r]
  \label{eq:end-kernel}
\end{equation}
has zero signed translation and lies in the end stabilizer kernel.  It is
infinite.  Hence the finite-cardinality end average used in the discrete
tree-lattice construction is unavailable.  This observation agrees with the
standard quasi-parabolic description of ascending HNN actions
\citep{fima2022transitivity}.

At $r=1$, the infinite kernel $A$ already fixes every vertex and edge, so the
finite-stabilizer hypothesis fails even though the quotient image is
discrete.  The three statements in this section are logically distinct: no
cycles, non-trace-class edge dynamics, and the $r$-split tree-lattice
hypothesis failure.  Each one independently prevents the requested
same-object determinant/ledger pair.
