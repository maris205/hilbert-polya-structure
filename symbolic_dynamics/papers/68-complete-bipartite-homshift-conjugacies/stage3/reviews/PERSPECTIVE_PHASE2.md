## Review Body

The checkerboard orientation and intrinsic dimerization give a clean bridge between graph coloring and symbolic recoding. The same phase variable is then used across conjugacy, finite dependence, equilibrium measures, and periodic data. The mathematical connections are real, but readers arriving from statistical mechanics are asked to supply part of the phase and pressure dictionary themselves.

### S1: The dimer code exposes the transferable combinatorial mechanism

**Evidence Anchor**: equation: §3, Eqs. (3.1)–(3.2)

Anchoring dimers by the symbol's bipartite part, rather than by an absolute lattice parity, explains why the code commutes with every translation. This is a concrete design principle that can guide searches for recodings of other bipartite constraint systems.

### S2: One phase variable organizes stochastic and thermodynamic phenomena

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §1 "The same global phase has a different effect on stochastic dependence."

The manuscript does more than reuse the word phase: it shows how the global orientation obstructs finite dependence, forces the full-action equilibrium mixture, and controls fixed-point existence. That shared mechanism is valuable to readers working between hom-shifts and probability.

### W1: The statistical-mechanics bridge needs an explicit dictionary

**Severity**: Minor

**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §§1 and 5 — expected a brief dictionary from checkerboard orientation and one-site pressure to hard-constraint spin-system terminology; checked introduction, pressure theorem, equilibrium-state proof, and source-boundary discussion

**Confidence**: 4 — adjacent statistical-mechanics and dynamics perspective

Terms such as phase, pressure, and equilibrium state are mathematically used correctly in context, but the manuscript does not state how its two clopen orientations relate to symmetry sectors or how the one-site potential relates to a hard-constraint spin model. Add a short bridge paragraph that identifies the configuration space, sector variable, single-site weights, and full-action mixture, while noting that no interaction remains after conditioning because the target is complete bipartite. This would make the transfer precise and clarify exactly what fails for a non-complete bipartite target.
