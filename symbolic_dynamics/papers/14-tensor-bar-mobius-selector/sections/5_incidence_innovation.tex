\section{Endpoint incidence and entropy innovation}
\label{sec:incidence}

The raw bar shift is defined by ordered words.  Tensor arithmetic appears
when those words are grouped by their endpoint object.  Because each endpoint
has only finitely many ordered factorizations, this grouping is exact before
any infinite endpoint sum is taken.

\subsection{Tensor-divisor M\"obius coefficient}

For functions on $\M$, define tensor-divisor convolution by
\[
  (f*g)(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}f(d)g(n/d).
  \tag{5.1}
\]
Let $\mathbf1(n)=1$ and define $\muten$ intrinsically by
\[
  \muten*\mathbf1=\delta_1.
  \tag{5.2}
\]
No factorization table is used in the symbolic transfer; (5.2) names the
coefficient that emerges after finite endpoint cancellation.

For $n\ge2$, set
\[
  c(n)=\sum_{k\ge1}\ \sum_{\substack{a_1,\ldots,a_k\ge2\\
                         a_1\cdots a_k=n}}
       (-1)^{k+1}.
  \tag{5.3}
\]
The sum is finite because $k\le\log_2n$.

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint cancellation]
\label{thm:endpoint}
For every $n\ge2$,
\[
  c(n)=-\muten(n).
  \tag{5.4}
\]
The endpoint-first series
\[
  \Fbar^{\mathrm{inc}}(s)
  =-\sum_{n\ge2}\muten(n)n^{-s}
  \tag{5.5}
\]
converges absolutely for $\Ree s>1$ and agrees with the raw transfer in
$\Ree s>\sbar$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient of $n^{-s}$ in $B(s)^k$ is the number of ordered
factorizations in (5.3).  As a formal Dirichlet series,
\[
  1-\bigl(B-B^2+B^3-\cdots\bigr)=(1+B)^{-1}.
\]
The coefficient of $(1+B)^{-1}$ is the convolution inverse in (5.2), so the
nonunit coefficient in the parenthesized series is $-\muten(n)$.  For
$\sigma>1$,
\[
  \sum_{n\ge1}|\muten(n)|n^{-\sigma}
  =\prod_p(1+p^{-\sigma})
  =\frac{\zeta(\sigma)}{\zeta(2\sigma)}<\infty,
\]
which proves absolute convergence after endpoint grouping.  Equality in the
common domain follows from \Cref{prop:raw}.
\end{proof}

The theorem extends the coefficient-grouped formula, not the raw countable
edge sum.  In the strip $1<\Ree s\le\sbar$, cancellation must be completed at
each finite endpoint before endpoints are summed.  This order is part of the
mathematical object called the incidence completion.

\subsection{M\"obius entropy innovation}

Define the entropy newly appearing at endpoint $\F_n$ by
\[
  \Lamten=\muten*h.
  \tag{5.6}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  \Lamten(n)=h(\F_n)-
  \sum_{\substack{d\mid n\\d<n}}\Lamten(d),
  \qquad
  h(\F_n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\Lamten(d).
  \tag{5.7}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Prime-power support]
\label{prop:lambda}
The unique solution of (5.7) is
\[
  \Lamten(p^r)=\log p,
  \qquad
  \Lamten(n)=0
\]
when $n$ contains at least two distinct tensor atoms.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $n=\prod_jp_j^{a_j}$.  The prime-power divisors contribute
\[
  \sum_j\sum_{r=1}^{a_j}\log p_j
  =\sum_ja_j\log p_j=\log n=h(\F_n).
\]
Thus the displayed support rule solves (5.7); induction on divisibility gives
uniqueness.
\end{proof}

The result is the classical identity $\mu*\log=\Lambda$ on the frozen tensor
monoid.  It must not be described as an additive cocycle:
$\Lamten(pq)=0$ for distinct atoms while
$\Lamten(p)+\Lamten(q)>0$.  Nor may it be inserted after the fact as a weight
on a completed closed word.  Its relevance here is that it is derived by the
same bar cancellation and entropy roof.

\begin{corollary}[Canonical roof derivative]
\label{cor:derivative}
On $\Ree s>1$,
\[
  \frac{d}{ds}\log\Dbar(s,1)
  =-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}
  =\sum_{n\ge1}\Lamten(n)n^{-s}.
  \tag{5.8}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate (4.9) in the raw domain.  The derivative inserts the roof
$T(\mathbf a)$ from (4.3).  Endpoint grouping and
\Cref{thm:endpoint} identify its coefficient with (5.6).  Both sides of (5.8)
are absolutely convergent Dirichlet series for $\Ree s>1$, extending the
coefficient identity there.
\end{proof}

This derivation answers a delicate source-lock question.  If one began with
$\Lamten(n)n^{-s}$ as the edge potential, the construction would simply
rewrite $-\zeta'/\zeta$.  SD-C16 instead begins with the local tensor word,
the entropy roof, and its bar parity.  The determinant is formed first; its
canonical roof derivative produces the Mangoldt-shaped output.  The result is
still classical arithmetic after endpoint grouping, but it is not inserted
as a von Mangoldt weight.

\subsection{Universal inversion theorem}

The same calculation also supplies the sharpest obstruction.

\begin{proposition}[Universal reciprocal-partition inversion]
\label{prop:universal}
Let $X$ be any countable weighted nonunit inventory with partition sum
$B_X(s)$, and form the same one-vertex ordered-word shift with product weight
and reduced-bar parity.  Wherever the raw geometric series converges,
\[
  F_X(s)=\frac{B_X(s)}{1+B_X(s)},
  \qquad
  D_X(s,1)=\frac1{1+B_X(s)}.
  \tag{5.9}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The total weight at internal length $k$ is
$(-1)^{k+1}B_X(s)^k$.  Geometric summation and the one-dimensional
determinant give (5.9).  No atom, divisor, or entropy property is used.
\end{proof}

Thus $D=1/\zeta$ is exact, but the inversion mechanism is universal.  It
does not distinguish the full-shift tensor inventory from composite-only,
randomized, or synthetic positive inventories.  This is the central
\textsc{proves too much} result.
