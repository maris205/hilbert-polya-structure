\section{ARS Phase-1 methodology and scope ledger}
\label{app:methodology}
\small

Paper 39 instantiates a compact theorem-relative methodology for negative
research branches.  The steps below describe the audit architecture, not a
general guarantee that every research program can be finitely closed.

\subsection{Methodology blueprint}

\paragraph{1. Choose a theorem-relative paradigm.}
State the object family, success predicate, repair domain, and allowed terminal
claims before evaluating the checker.  A negative conclusion must retain these
quantifiers in its title, abstract, theorem, and stop rule.

\paragraph{2. Freeze the contract as data.}
Represent the authorized request universe by stable IDs and content hashes.
Disclose the temporal boundary: here, predecessor outcomes were known before
the contract was assembled, and only the Paper-39 checker inputs were frozen
before the checker run.

\paragraph{3. Construct a typed graph.}
Every node records the inherited obligation, source object, marker, operator
owner, determinant owner, obstruction, forbidden escape, and terminal code.
Every edge records endpoints, authority, role, and carry/reset modes.  A coarse
view may summarize the graph only if all expanded fibers remain auditable.

\paragraph{4. Prove edge and token coverage.}
Define a finite normalizer on the exact token IDs.  Obstruction records must
end at a source-owned failed-coordinate witness.  Exit records must carry a
boundary witness and no obstruction credit.  A catch-all “other” token is not
an exhaustiveness proof.

\paragraph{5. Test adversarial validity.}
Construct countermodels to every tempting universalization and software
mutations to every finite invariant.  The checker should reject missing graph
records, malformed types, false coverage, illegal ownership carry, and
governance expansion.

\paragraph{6. Freeze the stop rule.}
Closure of the encoded branch authorizes no repair within that branch.  It also
does not authorize an unencoded successor.  Control returns to a separately
governed registry.

\paragraph{7. State the limitation explicitly.}
Finite relative closure is not universal impossibility.  Content hashes
authenticate bytes, not mathematical truth or prospective completeness.

\subsection{Quantifier ledger}

The main theorem quantifies over $r\ge2$ and $c\in\Caff(r)$.  The finite request
theorem quantifies over $q\in\SigmaReq$.  The class census quantifies over the
14 class labels and their exact token fibers.  The registry statement uses a
historical snapshot predicate.  None of these quantifiers ranges over:
\begin{itemize}
  \item arbitrary conceivable affine or symbolic constructions;
  \item unenumerated instances of a repair label;
  \item coordinatewise combinations of distinct endpoint states;
  \item arbitrary finite presentations or alternative splittings;
  \item future registry contents; or
  \item an undefined “unspent successor” predicate.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Paper-40 obligation}

No next experiment follows from this paper.  After the Paper-39 seal, root or
registry governance may independently define and source-lock a genuinely
candidate-specific non-affine object.  That future lock must specify its own
phase space, dynamics, arithmetic origin, primitive ledger, operator,
determinant, marker, controls, and Route stop rule before evaluation.  Historical
registry existence is not such a lock, and Paper 39 performs no selection.
