## 6. Findings by prior-art family

### 6.1 No-go theorem taxonomy: direct conceptual predecessor

Radin Dardashti’s 2021 article is the most direct conceptual predecessor. It models a no-go result using a goal `G`, framework `F`, physical assumptions `P`, mathematical structures/tools `M`, and background assumptions `B`. It then treats denial of each component as a distinct methodological pathway. The paper explicitly warns that no-go results often reveal routes around an assumption or mathematical representation and therefore do not, without further work, close a research programme.

**Collision:** framework/assumption typing, escape pathways, and meta-level interpretation of multiple no-go results are not new.

**Non-collision:** Dardashti offers a philosophical/methodological schema, not a content-addressed finite repair-history encoding, source provenance, a machine-readable obstruction DAG, a checker, or a coverage proof over an encoded contract. Paper 39’s strongest defensible relation to this source is that it operationalizes a deliberately narrow, finite version of the assumption-relative lesson: changes to `M`, `F`, or the goal are explicitly typed as category exits instead of being silently counted as failures.

Primary source: [Dardashti 2021, DOI 10.1016/j.shpsa.2021.01.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsa.2021.01.005).

### 6.2 Classical obstruction theory and local coefficients

Olum’s 1950 paper is a classical primary source on obstructions to extensions and homotopies. Kwasik and Sun revisit basic homology/cohomology results with local coefficients and explicitly situate that machinery in obstruction theory.

**Collision:** a staged sequence in which a vanishing obstruction permits extension to the next layer, while a nonzero class stops extension, is classical; local coefficients are classical.

**Non-collision:** the stages are cells/skeleta and cohomological extension problems, not literature-authorized research repairs. Neither work gives an inventory completeness theorem over heterogeneous symbolic-dynamics mechanisms.

Primary sources: [Olum 1950](https://doi.org/10.2307/1969510); [Kwasik–Sun](https://doi.org/10.4171/LEM/64-3/4-3).

### 6.3 Finite obstruction sets, completeness, and stopping signals

Robertson and Seymour prove that finite graphs are well-quasi-ordered under minors. This underwrites finite forbidden-minor characterizations for minor-closed graph classes. Cattell, Dinneen, Downey, Fellows, and Langston then explicitly distinguish nonconstructive existence from computation and develop methods for computing obstruction sets, including a stopping signal for increasing-pathwidth searches.

**Collision:** “coverage by obstructions” and even an algorithmic stopping signal are established ideas.

**Non-collision:** these are theorems inside a fixed mathematical quasi-order. They do not establish that a historically assembled list of research repairs is complete, and they do not use source locking or category-change exits. This literature is best used to articulate the burden of proof: a finite list is not a closure certificate unless the universe and stopping condition are separately justified.

Primary sources: [Robertson–Seymour 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2004.08.001); [Cattell et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(97)00300-9).

### 6.4 Counterexample-guided refinement as a repair-tree analogue

Clarke, Grumberg, Jha, Lu, and Veith’s CEGAR method starts from an abstract model, determines whether a counterexample is real or spurious, and refines the abstraction when necessary. Recent Goedel-Architect work likewise uses failed lemma nodes to refine a global proof blueprint.

**Collision:** failure-driven branching/refinement is not new.

**Non-collision:** CEGAR searches for a sufficient model abstraction; it does not close a literature-derived candidate registry. Goedel-Architect builds a proof of one theorem, not a content-addressed classification of authorized research successors assembled from heterogeneous predecessor results.

Primary sources: [Clarke et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1007/10722167_15); [Goedel-Architect, arXiv:2606.06468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06468).

### 6.5 Proof certificates and trusted checking

Necula’s proof-carrying code attaches a proof that code satisfies a safety policy, checked by a trusted verifier. Heath and Miller provide foundational proof certificates for reachability, non-reachability, bisimulation, and non-bisimulation over finite-state systems. The latter explicitly separates certificate generation from checking and makes soundness independent of certificate-specific clerk/expert implementations.

**Collision:** the certificate/checker architecture and certificates of graph non-reachability are established.

**Non-collision:** the certified objects are program safety or state-transition properties, not exhaustiveness of a source-derived mathematical research programme. Paper 39 may reuse the certificate/checker pattern but cannot claim to invent it.

Primary sources: [Necula 1997](https://doi.org/10.1145/263699.263712); [Heath–Miller 2015](https://doi.org/10.4204/EPTCS.186.4).

### 6.6 Closest functional predecessor: the Equational Theories Project

The 2025 ETP report is the closest collision with the broad idea “machine-check a complete finite mathematical relation graph.” It fixes a syntactically enumerated universe of 4,694 equational laws of order at most four and reports all 22,028,942 nonreflexive implication pairs for unrestricted magmas as proved or refuted. Human and automated proofs were translated into Lean. The project uses explicit positive implications and countermodels, generates an implication graph, and publishes code/data/dashboard artifacts.

Important full-text qualification: the project did **not** formalize 22 million separate end-to-end theorems. It formalized a reduced generating set (reported as 10,657 positive implications and 586,925 negative implications, with many negatives packed into shared `Facts` theorems), while transitivity and duality expansion to the full graph is currently performed by programs external to Lean. The authors state that an end-to-end Lean theorem could in principle be created. The separate finite-magma implication graph also retained one unresolved implication up to duality in the v2 project paper.

**Material collision:** finite frozen universe; positive/negative edge classification; countermodels; formal checker; explicit graph completion; project-management dashboard; machine-assisted collaboration. A claim like “the first machine-checkable exhaustive mathematical search/closure graph” would be false or at least indefensible.

**Surviving distinction:** ETP’s universe is syntactically enumerable and homogeneous (equational laws), its relation is logical implication, and its truth/refutation semantics are native to Lean. It is not a retrospective audit of a heterogeneous historical repair contract, does not type object/marker/operator changes as escapes, and does not decide how control returns to an external candidate registry. If Paper 39’s checker is end-to-end, including transitive closure and source identifiers, that is also a concrete implementation distinction from ETP’s reported external closure-expansion step.

Primary sources/artifacts: [ETP paper, arXiv:2512.07087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07087); [official repository](https://github.com/teorth/equational_theories); [official dashboard](https://teorth.github.io/equational_theories/dashboard/).

### 6.7 Proof and dependency DAGs

This family creates a substantial novelty collision for any generic “typed DAG” framing:

- **ProofFlow** (2025 preprint) constructs a DAG of human proof steps and formalizes each node as a Lean lemma.
- **LeanArchitect** (2026 preprint) extracts and manages blueprint metadata directly in Lean, infers dependencies, synchronizes formal/informal artifacts, and tracks progress.
- **Goedel-Architect** (2026 preprint) represents definitions and lemmas as a blueprint graph, closes nodes in parallel, and uses failed nodes to drive refinement.
- **The Network Structure of Mathlib** (2026 preprint) extracts 308,129 declarations and 8,436,366 edges and decomposes dependencies into explicit, compiler-synthesized, proof-only, statement-only, module, and namespace layers. It explicitly reports DAG depths and contrasts human blueprint edges with compiler-derived edges.
- **mathlib** and `leanblueprint` provide the underlying formal-library/project-management ecosystem.

**Collision:** DAG structure, node status, automated dependency inference, typed/multilayer edges, source-versus-compiler edge distinctions, and failure-driven blueprint repair are already present.

**Non-collision:** these graphs organize or analyze proofs. Their “completion” means lemmas have been formalized, not that every authorized research repair has been refuted or classified as a category exit. They also do not provide Paper 39’s content-addressed mapping from an encoded successor universe to heterogeneous prior-paper outcomes.

Primary sources: [ProofFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15981), [LeanArchitect](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22554), [Goedel-Architect](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06468), [Mathlib network](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24797), [mathlib paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/3372885.3373824).

### 6.8 Exact terminology collision: “closure certificates”

Murali, Trivedi, and Zamani define a closure certificate for a dynamical system as a real-valued function on pairs of states that acts as a functional analogue of a transition invariant. Their certificates can establish safety, persistence, and LTL/omega-regular properties and can be synthesized with SOS/SMT methods. The work appeared at HSCC 2024 and in extended form in *Nonlinear Analysis: Hybrid Systems* 59 (2026), article 101630. Follow-up papers introduce control closure certificates (2025 preprint) and interpolation-inspired closure certificates (2026 preprint).

**Exact collision:** the unqualified name *closure certificate* already denotes a current dynamical-verification method.

**Non-collision:** those certificates are state-pair functions that overapproximate a transition relation and establish temporal properties. They do not close a research-program repair registry, do not encode literature provenance, and do not classify category-changing exits.

Primary sources: [HSCC 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3641513.3650120); [journal extension](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nahs.2025.101630); [Control Closure Certificates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03947); [Interpolation-Inspired Closure Certificates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12436).

### 6.9 Emerging typed/provenance research-artifact architecture

Lewis, Zueco, and Yi’s 2026 AI-for-Math workshop paper *Recursive Epistemic Engines for Verifiable Open-Ended Scientific Agents* proposes typed, proof- and experiment-carrying claim packages with formal specifications, proof/runtime obligations, proof objects or counterexample certificates, experimental records, and provenance lineage. Failed checks return structured repair signals and rejected artifacts remain diagnostically labeled.

**Collision:** typed research artifacts, proof/counterexample status, provenance lineage, and structured repair signals are now explicit in emerging research-agent literature.

**Non-collision:** the architecture is designed for open-ended expansion and quality-diversity archives, not a finite no-go closure theorem. It does not claim relative exhaustiveness over a retrospectively encoded, content-addressed list of mathematical repairs. Evidential weight is low because this is a new workshop paper describing its own architecture, not an independently validated theorem.

Primary source: [OpenReview paper](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=6pcrtBtYIi).

### 6.10 Presentation and representation invariance; universal-decision boundary

Tietze transformations establish that finite presentations of isomorphic groups are connected by presentation-changing moves. Bass’s covering theory encodes a group action on a tree by a quotient graph of groups and relates morphisms/coverings to tree actions. Krieger constructs a conjugacy-invariant partially ordered object for subshifts, illustrating a distinct symbolic representation-invariance line. Rabin’s paper gives classical recursive-unsolvability results for group-theoretic decision problems, placing a hard boundary on universal algorithms over finite presentations.

**Collision:** presentation invariance and canonical quotient/tree encodings are classical; universal presentation-independent decision is generally impossible.

**Non-collision:** Paper 39 avoids Rabin-type universality only by fixing a finite, explicitly enumerated contract. The corrected narrow claim is canonicality only relative to the frozen ascending-HNN presentation/splitting; no presentation-invariance or presentation-independent decision claim follows from the closure DAG.

Primary sources: [Tietze 1908](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01736688), [Bass 1993](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-4049(93)90085-8), [Krieger 2000](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0143385700000249), [Rabin 1958](https://doi.org/10.2307/1969933).

### 6.11 Constituent Cayley, Hashimoto, cocycle, marker, and Bass–Serre machinery

The following components are clearly prior art:

- Hashimoto’s graph zeta/non-backtracking framework and Bass’s tree-lattice/Ihara determinant theory;
- Bass’s quotient graph-of-groups encoding of tree actions;
- Schmidt’s fundamental cocycles and symbolic `Z^d`-action invariants;
- Bartholdi’s Garden-of-Eden/amenability obstructions for cellular automata on groups;
- Aubrun and Bitar’s 2024 Cayley-skeleton subshift classifications;
- Meyerovitch’s 2025 marker-based embedding theorem for subshifts over countable abelian groups; and
- Barbieri, Blot, Sablik, and Salo’s 2025 geometric obstruction to self-simulation for groups, with examples including amenable, multi-ended, generalized Baumslag–Solitar, surface, and cocompact Fuchsian groups.

Recent screens also found Kang–Hsieh (2026) on information/locality and separating patterns in Cayley graphs and Watanabe (2026) on new determinant formulas for Ihara zeta functions. Neither contains a research-program closure DAG.

**Collision:** every named mathematical mechanism and several domain-specific no-go results are prior art.

**Non-collision:** no source found synthesizes these distinct failures into the supplied retrospective, content-addressed repair taxonomy or proves relative coverage of every successor authorized by that encoding.

## 8. Claim-boundary and novelty decision

### 8.1 Claims that pass the literature check

The following formulation is supportable, contingent on the internal artifact actually satisfying it:

1. **Domain-specific integration:** first identified synthesis of the enumerated P35–P38 affine obstruction families into one typed repair-coverage graph.
2. **Content-addressed retrospective source anchoring:** the predecessor repairs and terminal outcomes were known when the contract was encoded; stable identifiers and hashes freeze only the Paper 39 checker inputs before its run. This supports reproducibility of the audit but does **not** establish outcome-independent selection or protect the historical universe definition against post-result selection.
3. **Relative coverage:** every member of that finite authorized universe is mapped either to a cited/proved obstruction or to a typed category-changing exit.
4. **Machine auditability:** an independent checker can reconstruct node membership, source identity, edge typing, terminal class, and coverage without trusting narrative prose.
5. **Protocol-level closure:** after closure, control returns to a pre-existing registry rather than converting an unregistered escape into a new contribution.

These are a **configuration/integration novelty**, not a new mathematical primitive. Confidence that no exact prior combination was found: **moderate**.

### 8.2 Claims that fail or require deletion

| Proposed claim | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| “We introduce closure certificates.” | **Reject.** | Exact term is established by Murali et al. |
| “First machine-checkable complete graph of mathematical possibilities.” | **Reject.** | ETP is a direct counterexample; other exhaustive formal enumerations also exist. |
| “First typed proof/obstruction DAG.” | **Reject.** | ProofFlow, LeanArchitect, Goedel-Architect, mathlib-network and earlier blueprint systems. |
| “New no-go theorem methodology.” | **Reject.** | Dardashti and wider no-go literature already classify assumptions and escapes. |
| “New obstruction theory.” | **Reject.** | Classical obstruction theory and finite obstruction-set theory. |
| “No affine dynamical successor exists.” | **Reject.** | Only authorized members of the frozen contract are in scope. |
| “Presentation-independent decision procedure.” | **Reject.** | Needs a finite-scope invariance theorem; universal form conflicts with classical undecidability boundaries. |
| “All exits fail.” | **Reject unless typed carefully.** | An object/marker/operator change is outside-contract, not a mathematical failure. |
| “No non-affine candidate remains.” | **Reject for current registry state.** | Supplied registry contains source-locked SD-C01–SD-C06; realized action is return-to-registry. |
| “The DAG proves that the encoded universe was prospectively complete or outcome-independently selected.” | **Reject.** | A self-declared retrospective list and its hashes certify byte identity, not historical or prospective completeness. |

### 8.3 Internal evidence required before the novelty claim becomes a theorem

Literature review cannot establish coverage completeness. At minimum, the Phase-3/implementation artifact must expose:

- a content-addressed snapshot of the encoded repair universe and a timestamp establishing the Paper 39 checker-input boundary—not a pre-failure preregistration claim;
- stable node IDs and explicit object/marker/operator/determinant type fields;
- one-to-one source IDs for every obstruction theorem and the exact proposition/assumption used;
- an explicit distinction among `OBSTRUCTED`, `OUT_OF_CONTRACT_CATEGORY_CHANGE`, `DUPLICATE/EQUIVALENT`, and any other terminal class;
- an enumerator showing that every authorized successor appears exactly once or has an explicit equivalence witness;
- an acyclicity or well-foundedness check for the claimed DAG;
- a total-coverage assertion with no implicit “other” bucket;
- a check that transitive closure, equivalence reduction, and source resolution are themselves inside the trusted computation, or a clear disclosure if they rely on external scripts (the ETP nuance);
- a check that registry handoff does not create, rank, or reclassify a candidate; and
- an adversarial test where an omitted authorized successor causes the checker to fail.

Without these elements, the result is a well-organized literature map, not a machine-checkable relative exhaustiveness theorem.

## 13. Limitations

1. **Internal-source limitation.** This literature audit did not independently establish the mathematical truth of the P35–P38 authority artifacts or the historical completeness of the repair universe. The corrected contract expressly says the encoding was assembled after predecessor outcomes were known. Content hashes can certify the bytes supplied to the Paper 39 checker, but not outcome-independent selection, the assumptions of every source theorem, or completeness beyond the encoded universe.
2. **Vocabulary limitation.** A method with the same structure may use terms other than obstruction, closure, repair, source lock, certificate, or DAG. Literal negative searches therefore have moderate recall.
3. **Indexing limitation.** Very recent August 2026 work may not yet be indexed. The arXiv scan reduces but does not eliminate this problem.
4. **Language limitation.** Searches emphasized English-language metadata plus known German historical sources. Unindexed work in other languages may be missed.
5. **API limitation.** Semantic Scholar was unavailable because of HTTP 429 rate limiting. OpenAlex ranking was noisy for compositional queries. Neither failure affects DOI/arXiv verification of included sources, but it reduces citation-graph breadth.
6. **Preprint volatility.** ProofFlow, LeanArchitect, Goedel-Architect, Mathlib-network, the control/interpolation closure variants, Recursive Epistemic Engines, Barbieri et al., Kang–Hsieh, and Watanabe may change before publication.
7. **No formal semantic equivalence check.** “Closest” is based on claim/function comparison, not an ontology alignment theorem between source vocabularies.
8. **Retraction/integrity scope.** Publisher records were checked for obvious status flags, but no comprehensive Crossmark/PubPeer bulk audit was available.
9. **Novelty is conditional on implementation.** If Paper 39 merely draws a DAG without a total enumerator, independent source anchor, and executable coverage assertion, the qualified novelty pass should be downgraded to **insufficiently demonstrated**.

## 14. Final Phase-2 handoff

The literature supports a narrow and useful result, but not a broad one. Paper 39 should be positioned as a **retrospective, content-addressed heterogeneous typed audit of a finite affine repair encoding**. Its generic ingredients must be attributed to established no-go methodology, obstruction theory, formal certificates, graph completion, dependency-DAG tooling, and the constituent symbolic/group-theoretic machinery.

The two most important disclosures are mandatory:

1. **ETP is the closest functional predecessor** to machine-checked completion of a frozen finite mathematical graph; Paper 39 differs in its retrospective content-addressed mapping of heterogeneous repair outcomes, category exits, and—if implemented—end-to-end coverage checking.
2. **“Closure certificate” is already an active dynamical-verification term**; Paper 39 must qualify or rename its certificate.

Subject to a checker demonstrating the requirements in §8.3, the claim “machine-checkable relative exhaustiveness of this exact retrospective, content-addressed affine repair encoding” receives a **qualified novelty pass**. That pass concerns the domain-specific heterogeneous integration and executable audit, not prospective or outcome-independent universe selection. Any universal affine no-go, first typed-DAG, first closure-certificate, or first finite formal-completion claim receives a **novelty fail**.

Because the supplied pre-existing global registry is nonempty, the externally relevant terminal boundary is recorded as `RETURN_CONTROL_TO_PREEXISTING_GLOBAL_CANDIDATE_REGISTRY`; the empty-registry stop string remains conditional only. This audit makes no recommendation to rank, reuse, or create a candidate.
