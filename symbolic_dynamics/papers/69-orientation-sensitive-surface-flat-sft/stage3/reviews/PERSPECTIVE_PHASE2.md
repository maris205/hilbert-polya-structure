## Review Body

The manuscript successfully makes one object readable in three languages: subgroup-periodic configurations in dynamics, flat connections on surface covers, and character moments in finite-group representation theory. It also marks the reconstruction boundary carefully: the spectra recover degree–indicator data, not the group itself.

### S1: The dynamics-to-topology passage is explicit

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §1 "This makes the topology of the corresponding finite covers available to symbolic dynamics."

The local holonomy rule, quotient-cellulation convention, and rooted-gauge count provide an actual translation rather than an analogy. A reader can see why a fixed configuration is a raw flat connection and where the gauge factor enters before character theory is invoked.

### S2: Orientation sensitivity is made visible in a small control pair

**Evidence Anchor**: equation: §6, Eqs. (d8q8-O)–(q8-N)

The dihedral–quaternion comparison isolates the added information carried by odd nonorientable levels while keeping degree data fixed. This example makes Frobenius–Schur signs and the need for two cover directions intelligible to readers outside representation theory.

### W1: The transfer recipe for other cover towers remains implicit

**Severity**: Minor

**Evidence Anchor**: absence: §§4, 5, and 7 — expected a reusable checklist for selecting cover families that isolate representation-theoretic signatures; checked cover-family construction, reconstruction theorem, scope discussion, and conclusion

**Confidence**: 4 — cross-disciplinary topology and dynamics assessment

The chosen kernels work because their cover topology and parity turn the surface formulas into complementary moment sequences, but the manuscript does not extract this into a portable design criterion. Add a short future-work proposition or checklist: specify the acting surface group, the directed subgroup families, the topological statistic changed by each family, the resulting character weights, and the identifiability test for the desired signature. This would let readers assess which parts extend to other nonorientable surfaces or other cover towers without implying a theorem beyond the present case.
