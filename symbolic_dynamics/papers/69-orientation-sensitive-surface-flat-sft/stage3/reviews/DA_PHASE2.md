## Review Body

**Genuine strength.** The reconstruction does not lose the indicator-zero sector. Orientable moments recover total degree multiplicities, even nonorientable moments recover self-dual multiplicities, and odd moments recover the signed difference; the final subtraction then determines the zero-indicator multiplicity. The (C_3) control directly exercises this easily missed branch.

**Strongest counter-argument.** A skeptical reconstruction of the paper is that the symbolic system is an elaborate wrapper around classical surface-group homomorphism formulas. Raw flat connections on a finite cover differ from homomorphisms only by a rooted gauge factor, and the chosen subgroup families are engineered so their genera run through simple arithmetic progressions. After the known orientable and nonorientable character formulas are inserted, the inverse theorem becomes a standard uniqueness result for finite exponential sums. Thus the recovered object is neither the finite group nor its character table, but only its order and the multiset of degree–indicator pairs; nonisomorphic groups can remain indistinguishable. On this view, orientation sensitivity is already present in the Frobenius–Schur formula, and the (D_8/Q_8) example merely instantiates the classical sign difference. A more direct route would define the two surface homomorphism-count sequences without introducing an SFT, normalize them, and apply Vandermonde inversion immediately. The so-what objection is therefore that the passage through symbolic dynamics may add notation and gauge multiplicity without adding information. This is the strongest attack because every ingredient after the surface formulas is elementary. It nevertheless does not defeat the argument as written. The local holonomy rule supplies one fixed finite-type action whose subgroup fixed sets realize both orientability directions, rather than comparing unrelated count functions. The rooted gauge bijection is proved rather than assumed, the subgroup topology is derived from index and orientation character, and the inverse separates total, self-dual, signed, and zero-indicator multiplicities without claiming group classification.

**Logic-chain stress test.** The potentially fatal points were freeness and uniqueness of rooted gauge fixing, the orientability and genus of both cover families, recovery of the group order before normalization, and recovery of (c_d^0) despite its absence from nonorientable moments. The tree recursion, Euler-characteristic calculation, exponential-growth limit, and four-step moment system close these points without circular dependence.

**Alternative path.** One could work directly with surface representation varieties and their homomorphism counts, then treat the SFT identity as a corollary. That path is shorter for representation theory but loses the paper’s claim that two subgroup directions of a single finite-type action encode complementary information.

**So-what and frame-lock conclusion.** The meaningful residue is the construction of a single symbolic object whose selected periodic spectra recover an orientation-sensitive representation signature. The possible hidden premise that this signature classifies the finite group is explicitly denied. The selected families are tailored, but not illicitly: their definitions and topology are stated before inversion. No unexamined premise invalidates the recovery map.
