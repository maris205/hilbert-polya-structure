\section{The pointed factor poset}
\label{sec:poset}

Once \cref{thm:factor-collapse} has removed all local context, factor
targets are controlled by kernels of finite letter maps.  Cyclic adjacency
is the only obstruction to remaining inside the frozen directive family.

For a partition $\mathcal P$ of $\calA$, let
$\pi_{\mathcal P}:\calA\to\calA/\mathcal P$ send a letter to its block and
write
\begin{equation}
  u^{\mathcal P}_n=\pi_{\mathcal P}(u_n).
  \label{eq:quotient-directive}
\end{equation}
When this directive has a shorter period than $u$, it is always normalized
to its least period.  Write
$T_{\mathcal P}=T_{p,u^{\mathcal P}}$ for this canonical quotient target;
its alphabet is literally the block set $\calA/\mathcal P$.

\begin{theorem}[Admissible partitions classify factor targets]
\label{thm:partition-poset}
  Fix $T_{p,u}$.  The following sets are in bijection:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
    \item pointed-conjugacy classes of same-base factor targets inside the
      affine family;
    \item partitions of $\calA$ whose blocks are independent in $G_u$.
  \end{enumerate}
  At the representative level, there is a factor
  $T_{\mathcal P}\to T_{\mathcal Q}$ exactly when $\mathcal P$ refines
  $\mathcal Q$, and this factor is unique.  Thus the canonical quotient
  targets form a thin category.  Passing to pointed-conjugacy classes
  identifies arbitrary relabelings with these canonical representatives
  and gives the admissible-partition refinement poset.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By \cref{thm:factor-collapse}, a factor is a surjective letter map
  $\lambda:\calA\to\calB$ satisfying $v=\lambda\circ u$.  Its kernel
  partition $\mathcal P$ has the property
  \begin{equation}
    \lambda(u_i)\neq\lambda(u_{i+1})
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \text{no edge $\{u_i,u_{i+1}\}$ lies in one $\mathcal P$-block}.
    \label{eq:kernel-admissible}
  \end{equation}
  Thus the image directive has unequal cyclic neighbors exactly when every
  block of $\mathcal P$ is independent in $G_u$.  Exact source support
  implies that every block occurs in $u^{\mathcal P}$.  Neighbor
  inequality excludes period one, and reduction to the least period
  preserves exact support and neighbor inequality.  Hence every factor
  kernel is admissible and gives a frozen in-family target.

  Conversely, if $\mathcal P$ is admissible, the block map
  $\pi_{\mathcal P}$ is surjective and \cref{eq:quotient-directive} is a
  frozen directive after least-period reduction.  The converse direction
  of \cref{thm:factor-collapse} supplies the pointed factor.

  It remains to identify target classes rather than labeled block maps.
  Two factor maps with the same kernel induce a well-defined bijection
  between their image alphabets, sending the image of each source letter
  under one map to its image under the other.  Their image directives
  therefore differ by a bijective relabeling, so
  \cref{cor:pointed-conjugacy} makes the targets pointedly conjugate.
  Conversely, a pointed conjugacy between two targets is a bijective
  relabeling.  Equality at every directive index then says that two source
  letters have the same image under the first factor exactly when they have
  the same image under the second.  Their kernels are equal.

  Finally, a factor from the canonical $\mathcal P$-quotient to the
  canonical $\mathcal Q$-quotient is, by
  \cref{thm:factor-collapse}, forced on every source letter to satisfy
  $\mu([a]_{\mathcal P})=[a]_{\mathcal Q}$.  This formula is well defined
  exactly when every $\mathcal P$-block lies in one $\mathcal Q$-block,
  which is precisely refinement; in that case it also constructs the
  factor.  Exact support makes this block map unique.  Hence the canonical
  targets form the asserted thin category.  The preceding kernel argument
  then gives the asserted refinement poset only after passage to
  pointed-conjugacy classes.
\end{proof}

The word \emph{poset} is exact here.  We make no general lattice claim:
the meet or join of two set partitions can leave the admissible subposet.

\begin{corollary}[Graphical enumeration]
\label{cor:graph-counts}
  Let $S_{G_u}(k)$ denote the number of partitions of $V(G_u)$ into
  exactly $k$ nonempty independent blocks.  Then $S_{G_u}(k)$ is the
  number of $k$-letter target classes modulo pointed conjugacy.  The
  minimum target-alphabet size is $\chi(G_u)$, and a binary target exists
  exactly when $G_u$ is bipartite.  Moreover,
  \begin{equation}
    P_{G_u}(z)=\sum_k S_{G_u}(k)\fall{z}{k},
    \qquad
    \fall{z}{k}=z(z-1)\cdots(z-k+1).
    \label{eq:falling-expansion}
  \end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The first assertion is \cref{thm:partition-poset}.  The smallest number
  of independent blocks in a vertex partition is the chromatic number.
  The graph has at least one edge by cyclic neighbor inequality, so
  $\chi(G_u)\geq2$; consequently, a two-block partition exists exactly when
  the graph is bipartite.

  To count proper colorings by $z$ labeled colors, first choose the
  partition into $k$ nonempty color classes and then injectively assign
  $k$ color labels to the blocks.  There are $\fall{z}{k}$ such assignments.
  Summing over $k$ proves \cref{eq:falling-expansion}.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[The four-cycle]
\label{ex:c4}
  For $u=(0,1,2,3)^{\Z}$, the graph $G_u$ is $C_4$.  Its admissible
  partitions are
  \begin{align*}
    &\{0\}\mid\{1\}\mid\{2\}\mid\{3\},\\
    &\{0,2\}\mid\{1\}\mid\{3\},
      \qquad
      \{1,3\}\mid\{0\}\mid\{2\},\\
    &\{0,2\}\mid\{1,3\}.
  \end{align*}
  Hence $S_{C_4}(2)=1$, $S_{C_4}(3)=2$, and $S_{C_4}(4)=1$, so
  \[
    P_{C_4}(z)=\fall{z}{2}+2\fall{z}{3}+\fall{z}{4}.
  \]
\end{example}
