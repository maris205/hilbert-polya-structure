\begin{abstract}
Fix $q=p^a$ and bound the degree by $n$.  We give an exact temporal and
component description of the residue-class-zero Cartier section
\[
 \mathcal C\!\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_jx^j\right)
   =\sum_{pj\le n}c_{pj}^{1/p}x^j
 \quad\text{on }\F_q[x]_{\le n}.
\]
Every iterate is a twisted coefficient decimation, so its image has
$q^{\lfloor n/p^t\rfloor+1}$ points and every nonempty fibre has the same
size $q^{n-\lfloor n/p^t\rfloor}$.  An explicit $\F_p$-coordinate
conjugacy splits the map into inverse Frobenius and finite nilpotent shifts;
each weak component is therefore a Frobenius cycle with identical attached
in-trees.  The first entry time $\tau$ into the constant core has exact distribution
$\Prob(\tau\le t)=q^{-\lfloor n/p^t\rfloor}$ and sharp maximum
$1+\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor$ for $n>0$.  On the core the map is inverse
Frobenius: hence $\#\Fix(\mathcal C^m)=p^{\gcd(a,m)}$, and M\"obius
inversion gives every cycle count and a finite Euler product for the zeta
function.  Along $n_L=\lfloor\alpha p^L\rfloor$, $1\le\alpha<p$, the
reverse-depth law stabilizes exactly on every fixed lattice layer; moreover,
phase size together with the fixed sequence recovers $(p,a,n)$.  Classical
Cartier extraction, finite-field Frobenius theory, and generic functional-
graph machinery are treated as owned background.  The residual scope is only
the exact bounded specialization, lattice law, and recovery conjunction; it
remains under external \textbf{HOLD}, with no novelty or priority claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and ownership boundary}
\label{sec:introduction}

Cartier operators convert base-$p$ residue classes of coefficient indices
into new series.  In the convention of Bridy, for a perfect field of
characteristic $p$,
\[
 \Lambda_i\!\left(\sum_j c_jx^j\right)
   =\sum_j c_{pj+i}^{1/p}x^j;
\]
this formula and its connection to automatic sequences are established
machinery \cite[Eq.~(2.7)]{Bridy2017}.  The terminology descends from
Cartier's operation on differential forms \cite{Cartier1957}.  Jeong studies
two related Cartier families on finite-field power-series rings and their
shift/digit extensions \cite{Jeong2018}.  This note claims none of these
constructions.  It asks the narrower question of what exact temporal and
component data result when $\Lambda_0$ is restricted to one polynomial
degree bound and iterated as one map.

The answer separates into a transient coefficient forest and a periodic
field automorphism.  The resulting exact package is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an explicit conjugacy to inverse Frobenius times nilpotent index-chain
      shifts, with the resulting product and weak-component interpretation;
\item a coefficient formula for every iterate, every image, and every fibre;
\item every core-entry shell, the sharp maximum depth, and the size of the
      deepest shell;
\item all fixed points, exact cycles, and the Artin--Mazur zeta function;
\item an exact lattice-depth stabilization and recovery of $(p,a,n)$ from
      temporal data.
\end{enumerate}
The coefficient route follows disjoint $p$-adic chains pointwise.  A
complementary $\F_p$-linear reconstruction factors the iterates into
coordinate restriction, inverse Frobenius, and embedding; rank--nullity then
recounts the fibres and depth CDF after the iterate is known.

Owner subtraction is part of the statement.  Finite-field automorphisms and
their subfields are standard \cite{LidlNiederreiter1997}.  After restriction
of scalars, the present map is an ordinary finite $\F_p$-linear system.
Elspas owns foundational linear state-diagram machinery \cite{Elspas1959};
Wang analyzes transition graphs of affine and linear transformations
\cite{Wang1967}; Hern\'andez Toledo gives the general nilpotent--bijective
decomposition \cite{HernandezToledo2005}; and later functional-graph work
includes Panario--Reis and Reis \cite{PanarioReis2019,Reis2023}.  We assign
zero novelty or priority credit to the generic linear decomposition, its
product of cyclic and nilpotent parts, rank/kernel counts, or attached-tree
machinery.  Likewise, Cartier/section composition, Frobenius subfields,
M\"obius inversion, and zeta conversion are owned inputs.  Internally, the
present system is not the digit-erasure
map of P100, the adjugate map of P103, the annihilator--power ideal map of
P107, or the nilpotent image map on a subspace lattice of P109.  In
particular, the phase points here are coefficient vectors rather than
subspaces, and the recurrent core is the nontrivial inverse-Frobenius
permutation rather than a nilpotent absorber.  Rank is a proof tool, not a
claim to the generic linear-engine territory.  The bounded search is not an
owner certificate; external circulation, novelty language, and priority
language remain on \textbf{HOLD}.

\section{Definition and conventions}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $p$ be prime, let $a\ge1$, set $q=p^a$, and fix $n\ge0$.  Write
$\sigma(c)=c^p$ for Frobenius on $\F_q$.  For $t\ge0$, the notation
\[
 c^{p^{-t}}:=\sigma^{-t}(c)
\]
means the unique $p^t$-th root in $\F_q$; the superscript is automorphism
notation, not a rational exponent.  Thus $c^{p^{-0}}=c$, and the operation
depends only on $t\bmod a$.

The phase space is
\[
 X_{q,n}=\F_q[x]_{\le n}
 =\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_jx^j:c_j\in\F_q\right\},
 \qquad |X_{q,n}|=q^{n+1}.
\]
We keep all vectors at length $n+1$, padding high coefficients by zero, and
define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C\!\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_jx^j\right)
   =\sum_{0\le j\le\lfloor n/p\rfloor}c_{pj}^{p^{-1}}x^j.
 \label{eq:def-C}
\end{equation}
For every $\lambda\in\F_q$ and $f,g\in X_{q,n}$, the defining law is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C(\lambda f+g)
 =\sigma^{-1}(\lambda)\mathcal C(f)+\mathcal C(g),
 \qquad
 \mathcal C^t(\lambda f+g)
 =\sigma^{-t}(\lambda)\mathcal C^t(f)+\mathcal C^t(g).
 \label{eq:semilinearity}
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathcal C$ is additive and $\sigma^{-1}$-semilinear over $\F_q$;
after restriction of scalars it is an ordinary $\F_p$-linear endomorphism.
It is generally not $\F_q$-linear when $a>1$.
For the zero polynomial we set $\deg 0=-\infty$.  The constant subspace is
\[
 K=\F_q\cdot1\subseteq X_{q,n}.
\]
The core-entry time, stable image, and periodic set are
\begin{equation}
 \tau(f)=\min\{t\ge0:\mathcal C^t(f)\in K\},
 \qquad
 \Core(\mathcal C)=\bigcap_{t\ge0}\im(\mathcal C^t),
 \qquad
 \Per(\mathcal C)=\{f:\mathcal C^m(f)=f\text{ for some }m\ge1\}.
 \label{eq:tau-core}
\end{equation}
The minimum will be finite by \cref{thm:depth}.  No value $v_p(0)$ and no
maximum over an empty set will be used.

\section{All iterates, images, and fibres}
\label{sec:iterate}

The local mechanism is visible at coefficient level: only indices divisible
by $p$ survive, and every surviving index is divided by $p$.

\begin{theorem}[Coefficient iterate]
\label{thm:iterate}
For every $f=\sum_{j=0}^{n}c_jx^j\in X_{q,n}$ and every integer $t\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal C^t(f)
   =\sum_{0\le j\le\lfloor n/p^t\rfloor}
      c_{p^tj}^{p^{-t}}x^j.
 \quad}
 \label{eq:iterate}
\end{equation}
In particular, the formula at $t=0$ is the identity, and once $p^t>n$ the
iterate is the constant $c_0^{p^{-t}}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof route I: coefficient chains]
The assertion at $t=0$ is immediate.  Suppose \eqref{eq:iterate} holds at
time $t$.  Applying \eqref{eq:def-C} selects those displayed indices that
are themselves divisible by $p$ and applies one further inverse Frobenius:
\[
 \mathcal C^{t+1}(f)
 =\sum_{p^{t+1}j\le n}
   \left(c_{p^t(pj)}^{p^{-t}}\right)^{p^{-1}}x^j
 =\sum_{p^{t+1}j\le n}c_{p^{t+1}j}^{p^{-(t+1)}}x^j.
\]
This proves the induction.  The last statement follows because
$\lfloor n/p^t\rfloor=0$ when $p^t>n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Index-chain conjugacy and functional components]
\label{thm:conjugacy}
Let
\[
 \mathcal U_n=\{u\in\{1,\ldots,n\}:p\nmid u\},
 \qquad
 \ell_u=\max\{v\ge0:up^v\le n\}\quad(u\in\mathcal U_n),
\]
and let $S_u:\F_q^{\ell_u+1}\to\F_q^{\ell_u+1}$ be the
nilpotent shift
\[
 S_u(z_0,\ldots,z_{\ell_u})=(z_1,\ldots,z_{\ell_u},0).
\]
For $f=\sum_{j=0}^n c_jx^j$, set
\begin{equation}
 d_{u,v}=\sigma^{-v}(c_{up^v}),
 \qquad
 \Psi(f)=\bigl(c_0,(d_{u,v})_{u\in\mathcal U_n,\ 0\le v\le\ell_u}\bigr).
 \label{eq:chain-coordinates}
\end{equation}
Then $\Psi$ is an $\F_p$-linear bijection from $X_{q,n}$ to
\[
 \F_q\times V_+,
 \qquad
 V_+=\prod_{u\in\mathcal U_n}\F_q^{\ell_u+1},
\]
and, with $N=\prod_{u\in\mathcal U_n}S_u$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\quad
 \Psi\mathcal C\Psi^{-1}=\sigma^{-1}\times N.
 \quad}
 \label{eq:product-conjugacy}
\end{equation}
For $n=0$, the product $V_+$ is the one-point zero space.

Consequently the directed functional graph has the following exact
component interpretation.  If $\mathcal O$ is an inverse-Frobenius cycle of
length $d$ in $\F_q$, then $\mathcal O\times V_+$ is one weak component,
its directed cycle is $\mathcal O\times\{0\}$, and its size is $dq^n$.
These are all weak components.  For each $y\in\mathcal O$, the in-tree
attached at $(y,0)$ has $q^n$ vertices; all such trees, over all periodic
points, are isomorphic to the nilpotent in-tree of $N$ at zero after the
root cycle edge is suppressed.

More precisely, put $h(z)=\min\{t\ge0:N^tz=0\}$ and
\[
 \mathcal T_y=\{(\sigma^{h(z)}y,z):z\in V_+\}.
\]
The vertices of $\mathcal T_y$ whose first core entry is $(y,0)$ at time
$t$ number
\begin{equation}
 \#\mathcal T_y(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&t=0,\\
 q^{n-\lfloor n/p^t\rfloor}
  -q^{n-\lfloor n/p^{t-1}\rfloor},&1\le t\le D_{p,n},\\
 0,&t>D_{p,n},
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:component-layers}
\end{equation}
where $D_{p,0}=0$ and $D_{p,n}=1+\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor$ for $n>0$.
Finally, a vertex $(y,z)$ in product coordinates has indegree
\begin{equation}
 \#(\sigma^{-1}\times N)^{-1}(y,z)=
 \begin{cases}
 q^{n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor},&z\in\im N,\\
 0,&z\notin\im N.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:component-indegree}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every positive index has a unique factorization $j=up^v$ with $p\nmid u$,
so the coordinates in \eqref{eq:chain-coordinates} use every positive
coefficient exactly once.  Each power of Frobenius is an $\F_p$-linear
automorphism, and the inverse coordinate change is
$c_{up^v}=\sigma^v(d_{u,v})$; hence $\Psi$ is an $\F_p$-linear bijection.

Write primes for coordinates after one application of $\mathcal C$.  If
$v<\ell_u$, then
\[
 d'_{u,v}
 =\sigma^{-v}(c'_{up^v})
 =\sigma^{-v}\!\left(\sigma^{-1}(c_{up^{v+1}})\right)
 =\sigma^{-(v+1)}(c_{up^{v+1}})=d_{u,v+1},
\]
while $d'_{u,\ell_u}=0$.  The constant coordinate becomes
$\sigma^{-1}(c_0)$.  This proves \eqref{eq:product-conjugacy}; in
particular, $N$ is a finite product of nilpotent shifts.

Every $(x,z)\in\mathcal O\times V_+$ eventually reaches
$\mathcal O\times\{0\}$, and no directed or reverse edge can change the
inverse-Frobenius cycle containing its first coordinate.  Thus
$\mathcal O\times V_+$ is exactly one weak component and has size $dq^n$.
For $z\ne0$, nilpotence gives $h(Nz)=h(z)-1$, and hence
\[
 (\sigma^{-1}\times N)(\sigma^{h(z)}y,z)
 =\bigl(\sigma^{h(z)-1}y,Nz\bigr)\in\mathcal T_y.
\]
At $z=0$ the outgoing edge is the cycle edge.  Therefore
$z\mapsto(\sigma^{h(z)}y,z)$ identifies the rooted in-tree of $N$ with
the tree attached at $(y,0)$, and the sets $\mathcal T_y$ partition the
component.

The rank of $N^t$ over $\F_q$ equals the number of positive indices
divisible by $p^t$, namely $\lfloor n/p^t\rfloor$.  Thus
$\#\ker N^t=q^{n-\lfloor n/p^t\rfloor}$, and differencing consecutive
kernels proves \eqref{eq:component-layers}.  Finally, $Nw=z$ has no
solution outside $\im N$ and is a coset of $\ker N$ inside the image.
Since $\#\ker N=q^{n-\lfloor n/p\rfloor}$ and inverse Frobenius has one
preimage at the first coordinate, \eqref{eq:component-indegree} follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete image and fibre census]
\label{thm:fibres}
Let $t\ge0$ and put $r_t=\lfloor n/p^t\rfloor$.  Under the natural padded
embedding $X_{q,r_t}\subseteq X_{q,n}$,
\begin{equation}
 \im(\mathcal C^t)=X_{q,r_t},
 \qquad
 \#\im(\mathcal C^t)=q^{r_t+1}.
 \label{eq:image}
\end{equation}
For every target $g\in X_{q,n}$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\quad
 \#(\mathcal C^t)^{-1}(g)=
 \begin{cases}
 q^{n-r_t},&\deg g\le r_t,\\
 0,&\deg g>r_t.
 \end{cases}
 \quad}
 \label{eq:fibres}
\end{equation}
Thus all and only targets in \eqref{eq:image} have nonempty fibres, and all
nonempty fibres are uniform.  At $t=0$ they are singletons; if $p^t>n$,
there are $q$ constant targets and every nonempty fibre has size $q^n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:iterate}, the output coordinates are arbitrary precisely in
positions $0,\ldots,r_t$: each prescribed output has a unique preimage
coefficient at each index $p^tj$, because inverse Frobenius is bijective.
The remaining $n-r_t$ input coordinates are unobserved and may be chosen
freely.  This proves both cases of \eqref{eq:fibres}, including the empty
fibre outside the displayed image.
\end{proof}

The following complementary reconstruction starts from the already proved
iterate formula rather than supplying a logically independent derivation of
the dynamics.  It packages that formula as finite-field linear algebra and
recounts the fibre sizes and depth CDF.

\begin{proposition}[$\F_p$-linear rank factorization]
\label{prop:rank-route}
View $X_{q,n}\cong\F_q^{n+1}$ as an $\F_p$-space.  Let
\[
 R_t(c_0,\ldots,c_n)=(c_0,c_{p^t},\ldots,c_{r_tp^t}),
\]
let $\Phi_t$ apply $\sigma^{-t}$ coordinatewise, and let $J_t$ pad with
zeros.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C^t=J_t\Phi_tR_t,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{rank}_{\F_p}(\mathcal C^t)=a(r_t+1),
 \qquad
 \dim_{\F_p}\ker(\mathcal C^t)=a(n-r_t).
 \label{eq:rank-factorization}
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \#\{f:\mathcal C^t(f)\in K\}=q^{n+1-r_t}.
 \label{eq:rank-cdf}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Complementary route II: ranks]
The restriction $R_t$ is an $\F_q$-linear surjection,
$\Phi_t$ is an $\F_p$-linear automorphism, and $J_t$ is an injective
$\F_q$-linear map.  Their factorization follows directly from the displayed
coordinates, and rank--nullity gives \eqref{eq:rank-factorization}.  Hence
every nonempty fibre is an affine coset of a kernel of size $q^{n-r_t}$,
recounting \eqref{eq:fibres}.  Finally,
$\mathcal C^t(f)$ is constant exactly when the $r_t$ selected positive
coordinates vanish.  These are $r_t$ independent $\F_q$ constraints, which
proves \eqref{eq:rank-cdf}.
\end{proof}

\section{Core-entry distribution and sharp depth}
\label{sec:depth}

For an integer $j>0$, let $v_p(j)$ be its $p$-adic valuation.  An occupied
coefficient at $j$ moves down its $p$-chain until the first index not
divisible by $p$, then disappears at the next step.

\begin{theorem}[Every depth shell and the deepest shell]
\label{thm:depth}
If $f$ is constant, then $\tau(f)=0$.  If $f$ is nonconstant, then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\quad
 \tau(f)=1+\max\{v_p(j):1\le j\le n,\ c_j\ne0\}.
 \quad}
 \label{eq:point-depth}
\end{equation}
For every $t\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 \#\{f:\tau(f)\le t\}=q^{n+1-\lfloor n/p^t\rfloor},
 \qquad
 \Prob_{f\ \mathrm{unif.}}(\tau(f)\le t)
 =q^{-\lfloor n/p^t\rfloor}.
 \label{eq:depth-cdf}
\end{equation}
When $n=0$, all $q$ states have depth zero and the maximum depth is
$D_{p,0}=0$.  When $n\ge1$, set
\begin{equation}
 D_{p,n}=1+\lfloor\log_p n\rfloor,
 \qquad
 s_{p,n}=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{p^{D_{p,n}-1}}\right\rfloor
 \in\{1,\ldots,p-1\}.
 \label{eq:D-s}
\end{equation}
Then $D_{p,n}$ is the sharp maximum of $\tau$, and
\begin{align}
 \#\{f:\tau(f)=t\}
 &=q^{n+1-\lfloor n/p^t\rfloor}
   -q^{n+1-\lfloor n/p^{t-1}\rfloor},
 &&1\le t\le D_{p,n},
 \label{eq:depth-shells}\\
 \#\{f:\tau(f)=D_{p,n}\}
 &=q^{n+1}-q^{n+1-s_{p,n}}.
 \label{eq:deepest-shell}
\end{align}
There are no states at larger depth.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Coefficient-forest proof]
Fix an occupied index $j>0$ and write $j=p^vu$ with $p\nmid u$.  At time
$s\le v$, \eqref{eq:iterate} carries its nonzero coefficient to the
positive index $p^{v-s}u$; inverse Frobenius cannot turn a nonzero element
into zero.  At time $v+1$ that coefficient is not selected.  Taking the
last disappearance time over all occupied positive indices proves
\eqref{eq:point-depth}; the constant case is separated because its occupied
positive-index set is empty.

The event $\tau(f)\le t$ says exactly that every positive coefficient whose
index is divisible by $p^t$ vanishes.  There are
$\lfloor n/p^t\rfloor$ such constrained coordinates, so the other
$n+1-\lfloor n/p^t\rfloor$ coordinates are arbitrary.  This proves
\eqref{eq:depth-cdf}; it also agrees with the complementary rank count
\eqref{eq:rank-cdf}.  Subtracting consecutive CDF values gives
\eqref{eq:depth-shells}.

For $n\ge1$, the largest valuation among $1,\ldots,n$ is
$D_{p,n}-1$, since $p^{D_{p,n}-1}\le n<p^{D_{p,n}}$.  Thus the maximum is
sharp, witnessed by $x^{p^{D_{p,n}-1}}$.  Exactly $s_{p,n}$ indices in
$[1,n]$ are divisible by $p^{D_{p,n}-1}$.  A state is deepest precisely
when at least one of those coefficients is nonzero.  Subtracting the
$q^{n+1-s_{p,n}}$ states in which all of them vanish from the full phase
space proves \eqref{eq:deepest-shell} and completes all boundary cases.
\end{proof}

\section{Inverse-Frobenius core, cycles, and zeta}
\label{sec:periodic}

Let $\mob$ denote the number-theoretic M\"obius function.  The transient
coordinates disappear, but the constant coefficient continues to move.

\begin{theorem}[Complete periodic census]
\label{thm:periodic}
For every $n\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 \Core(\mathcal C)=\Per(\mathcal C)=K,
 \qquad
 \mathcal C|_K=\sigma^{-1}.
 \label{eq:core}
\end{equation}
For every $m\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\quad \#\Fix(\mathcal C^m)=p^{\gcd(a,m)}.\quad}
 \label{eq:fixed-count}
\end{equation}
For each divisor $d$ of $a$, define
\begin{equation}
 A_d=\sum_{e\mid d}\mob(d/e)p^e.
 \label{eq:exact-period-points}
\end{equation}
Then $A_d$ is the number of points of exact period $d$, and $A_d/d$ is the
number of $d$-cycles.  No period outside the divisors of $a$ occurs.  The
full functional graph therefore has $A_d/d$ weak components based on
$d$-cycles, each with $dq^n$ vertices and the attached trees described in
\cref{thm:conjugacy}.  The
Artin--Mazur zeta function of the full finite map is the formal series
\begin{align}
 \zeta_{\mathcal C}(z)
 &:=\exp\left(\sum_{m\ge1}
       \#\Fix(\mathcal C^m)\frac{z^m}{m}\right) 
 \notag\\
 &=\boxed{\quad
   \prod_{d\mid a}(1-z^d)^{-A_d/d}.
   \quad}
 \label{eq:zeta}
\end{align}
These statements include $a=1$, where all $q=p$ constants are fixed and
$\zeta_{\mathcal C}(z)=(1-z)^{-p}$, and $n=0$, where the phase space already
equals the core.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Stable-image and subfield proof]
By \eqref{eq:image}, the images decrease to $K$, proving the first equality
in \eqref{eq:core} for the stable image.  If $f$ is periodic, say
$\mathcal C^m(f)=f$, then $f\in\im(\mathcal C^{km})$ for every $k$, so
$f\in K$.  Conversely, \eqref{eq:iterate} sends a constant $c$ to
$\sigma^{-1}(c)$, an automorphism whose order divides $a$; hence every
constant is periodic.

A fixed point of $\mathcal C^m$ is therefore a constant fixed by
$\sigma^{-m}$, equivalently by $\sigma^m$.  To count these constants
directly, note that the elements of $\F_{p^a}$ are the roots of
$T^{p^a}-T$, while the $\sigma^m$-fixed elements are also roots of
$T^{p^m}-T$.  The Euclidean algorithm on the exponents gives
\[
 \gcd\!\left(T^{p^a}-T,T^{p^m}-T\right)
   =T^{p^{\gcd(a,m)}}-T.
\]
This polynomial is separable and has degree $p^{\gcd(a,m)}$, proving
\eqref{eq:fixed-count} without an uncounted field-theory step.  Every orbit
length divides $a$.  For $d\mid a$,
the $p^d$ elements fixed by the $d$th iterate are partitioned by their exact
periods, so $p^d=\sum_{e\mid d}A_e$.  Divisor M\"obius inversion gives
\eqref{eq:exact-period-points}, and division by $d$ counts cycles.

Finally, a cycle of length $d$ contributes
$\exp(\sum_{k\ge1}z^{kd}/k)=(1-z^d)^{-1}$ to the zeta function.  Multiplying
over the $A_d/d$ cycles of each possible length proves \eqref{eq:zeta}.
Transient trees contribute no fixed points and hence no zeta factor.
\end{proof}

\section{An exact lattice-depth limit}
\label{sec:lattice}

The sharp depth changes when $n$ crosses powers of $p$.  Fixing the
position inside one such lattice interval removes that oscillation.

\begin{theorem}[Layerwise stabilization and limiting defect]
\label{thm:lattice}
Fix $p$, $a$, and a real number $1\le\alpha<p$.  For integers $L\ge0$, let
\[
 n_L=\lfloor\alpha p^L\rfloor,
 \qquad F_L\sim\operatorname{Unif}(X_{q,n_L}),
 \qquad \Delta_L=L+1-\tau(F_L).
\]
Then the sharp depth at degree bound $n_L$ is exactly $L+1$, so
$\Delta_L\in\{0,\ldots,L+1\}$.  For every fixed $k\ge1$ and every
$L\ge k-1$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\quad
 \Prob(\Delta_L\ge k)
 =q^{-\lfloor\alpha p^{k-1}\rfloor}.
 \quad}
 \label{eq:lattice-stable}
\end{equation}
Consequently $\Delta_L$ converges in distribution to a nonnegative
integer-valued variable $\Delta$ with tails
\begin{equation}
 \Prob(\Delta\ge0)=1,
 \qquad
 \Prob(\Delta\ge k)=q^{-\lfloor\alpha p^{k-1}\rfloor},\quad k\ge1,
 \label{eq:limit-tail}
\end{equation}
or, equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 \Prob(\Delta=r)=S_r-S_{r+1},\qquad
 S_0=1,\qquad S_r=q^{-\lfloor\alpha p^{r-1}\rfloor}\ (r\ge1).
 \label{eq:limit-mass}
\end{equation}
Thus the asserted ``limit'' is stronger than asymptotic convergence:
every fixed tail is eventually exact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The inequalities
$p^L\le n_L<p^{L+1}$ follow from $1\le\alpha<p$, including the endpoint
$\alpha=1$.  Hence \eqref{eq:D-s} gives maximum depth $L+1$.  If
$L\ge k-1$, then by \eqref{eq:depth-cdf},
\begin{align*}
 \Prob(\Delta_L\ge k)
 &=\Prob\bigl(\tau(F_L)\le L+1-k\bigr)\\
 &=q^{-\left\lfloor n_L/p^{L+1-k}\right\rfloor}.
\end{align*}
For an integer $M\ge1$, the identity
$\lfloor\lfloor x\rfloor/M\rfloor=\lfloor x/M\rfloor$ gives
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{n_L}{p^{L+1-k}}\right\rfloor
 =\left\lfloor\alpha p^{k-1}\right\rfloor,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:lattice-stable}.  The sequence $S_r$ decreases to
zero, so the differences in \eqref{eq:limit-mass} are nonnegative and
sum to one.  Stabilization of every tail proves convergence in distribution.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the lattice window is explicit]
The restriction $\alpha<p$ keeps $n_L$ in the depth interval
$[p^L,p^{L+1})$.  At $\alpha=p$ the maximum depth shifts to $L+2$; that
endpoint is represented instead by the allowed choice $\alpha=1$ after
incrementing $L$.  No assertion is made for $k>L+1$ before stabilization,
when the finite variable has no such tail.
\end{remark}

\section{Temporal recovery of the parameters}
\label{sec:recovery}

The periodic sequence alone loses the degree bound, while phase size alone
cannot separate the field size from the number of coefficients.  Together
they are rigid.

\begin{theorem}[Temporal signature rigidity]
\label{thm:recovery}
Let
\[
 N=|X_{q,n}|,
 \qquad F_m=\#\Fix(\mathcal C^m)\quad(m\ge1).
\]
The signature $(N,(F_m)_{m\ge1})$ determines $(p,a,n)$ by
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\quad
 p=F_1,\qquad
 q=\max_{m\ge1}F_m,\qquad
 a=\min\{m\ge1:F_m=q\},\qquad
 n=\log_qN-1.
 \quad}
 \label{eq:recovery}
\end{equation}
In particular, two bounded Cartier systems with equal signatures have equal
parameter triples.  The formula remains valid for $a=1$ and for $n=0$.
Here $(F_m)_{m\ge1}$ means the full infinite fixed-count sequence; no claim
is made that an unspecified preassigned finite prefix is sufficient.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:fixed-count} gives $F_1=p$.  It also gives
$F_m\le p^a=q$, with equality exactly when $a\mid m$.  Thus the maximum is
$q$ and its first occurrence is $m=a$.  Finally,
$N=q^{n+1}$, so the last formula in \eqref{eq:recovery} follows.  When
$a=1$, the maximum occurs first at one; when $n=0$, $N=q$ and the recovered
degree bound is zero.
\end{proof}
