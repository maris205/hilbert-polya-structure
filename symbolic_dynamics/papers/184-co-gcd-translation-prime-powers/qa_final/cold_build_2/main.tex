\title[Co-gcd translation on prime powers]{Co-GCD Translation on Prime-Power Residues:\\
Valuation Strata, Tail Ladders, and Exact Fibres}

\begin{abstract}
For a prime power $N=p^a$, we determine the finite map
$T(x)=x+N/\gcd(x,N)\pmod N$.  The added integer is the additive order of the
current residue, but the resulting functional graph is not a group
translation.  Valuation strata below $a/2$ are recurrent translations, while
strata above $a/2$ fall to their complementary valuation in one step.  When
$a$ is even, the equality stratum is a conveyor: consecutive unit increments
produce a uniformly populated tail ladder of depths $2,\ldots,p$.  We obtain
every cycle length and multiplicity, every tail population, and the sharp
maximum tail.  We also solve the inverse problem completely.  Every target
has zero, one, or two predecessors; the empty and double fibres both number
$p^{\lfloor(a-1)/2\rfloor}$, and an explicit congruence parametrizes every
double target.  The proofs include $p=2$, $x=0$, and both parities of $a$.
Exact enumeration on 27 prime-power carriers supplies 109,478 author-side
regression assertions, not proof or novelty evidence.  The manuscript remains
\texttt{HOLD\_EXTERNAL} pending a full owner audit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Literal system and contribution boundary}

Let $p$ be a prime, $a\geq1$, and $N=p^a$.  We use the representatives
$0,1,\ldots,N-1$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:map}
 T(x)=x+\frac{N}{\gcd(x,N)}\pmod N,
 \qquad \gcd(0,N)=N.
\end{equation}
The quotient $N/\gcd(x,N)$ is the order of $x$ in the additive cyclic group,
but it is inserted as a new residue rather than used as an iteration count.
Put $\vp(0)=a$ and use the ordinary $p$-adic valuation for $x\ne0$.

Finite dynamics over rings and $p$-adic state spaces are established subjects
\cite{XuZou2009,AnashinKhrennikov2009}; functional-graph analysis over finite
algebraic carriers is likewise standard \cite{KonyaginEtAl2016}.  Those
sources do not state the valuation-switched map \eqref{eq:map}.  We assign
cyclic-group arithmetic, generic valuation stratification, and generic
functional-graph bookkeeping zero contribution credit.  The retained object
is the conjunction, for the literal map, of the middle-layer conveyor, full
cycle/tail atlas, fibre cap, image defect, and explicit double-target set.
A bounded exact-formula search found no primary owner, but a non-hit is not a
novelty certificate; a later literal or equivalent owner triggers withdrawal.

Internally, P142 maps prime-power \emph{divisors} by a valuation--gcd rule,
whereas \eqref{eq:map} acts on all $p^a$ residue classes and translates within
each low valuation stratum.  P128 takes polynomial gcds, and P166 uses a
Hamming-weight-selected phase translation on a cyclic cube.  None has the
even-exponent conveyor or the $0/1/2$ residue-fibre atlas below.  This is an
internal proof-transfer subtraction, not an external priority claim.

For a finite map, the \emph{tail} $\mu(x)$ is the least $m\geq0$ for which
$T^m(x)$ is periodic, and $\lambda(x)$ is the period of that eventual cycle.

\section{Pointwise valuation dynamics}

\begin{theorem}[all valuation strata]\label{thm:strata}
Let $v=\vp(x)$.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item If $2v<a$, then $\mu(x)=0$ and $\lambda(x)=p^v$.
\item If $2v>a$, including $x=0$, then
      $\mu(x)=1$ and $\lambda(x)=p^{a-v}$.
\item Suppose $a=2h$ and $v=h$.  Write $x=p^hu$ with
      $1\leq u<p^h$ and $p\nmid u$, and put
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:rs}
       r=p-(u\bmod p),\qquad s=\vp(u+r).
      \end{equation}
      Here $1\leq r\leq p-1$ and $1\leq s\leq h$, with $s=h$ allowed when
      $u+r=p^h$.  Then
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:middle-orbit}
       \mu(x)=r+1,\qquad \lambda(x)=p^{h-s}.
      \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First take $x\ne0$ and write $x=p^vu$ with $p\nmid u$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:map} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stratum-step}
 T(x)=p^vu+p^{a-v}\pmod {p^a}.
\end{equation}
If $2v<a$, division by $p^v$ turns the action on the unit coordinate modulo
$p^{a-v}$ into translation by $p^{a-2v}$.  The added term is divisible by
$p$ unless $v=0$, where it is zero modulo $p^a$; in either case the coordinate
remains a unit.  The additive order of the translating element is
$p^{a-v}/p^{a-2v}=p^v$.  This proves (i), including the fixed unit stratum.

If $2v>a$, factor \eqref{eq:stratum-step} as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:high-fall}
 T(x)=p^{a-v}\bigl(1+p^{2v-a}u\bigr).
\end{equation}
The parenthesis is a unit, so the next valuation is $a-v<a/2$ and (i) gives
the period $p^{a-v}$.  The old high valuation cannot lie on that low invariant
cycle, so the tail is exactly one.  For $x=0$, equation \eqref{eq:map} gives
$0\mapsto1$, and $1$ is fixed; this agrees with (ii).

It remains to handle equality.  Modulo $p^h$, the middle unit coordinate
evolves by $u\mapsto u+1$.  For $0\leq k<r$, the integer $u+k$ is not divisible
by $p$, while $u+r$ is.  After $r$ steps the state is high.  If $u+r<p^h$ its
valuation is $h+s$; if $u+r=p^h$, the state is zero and our convention gives
the same value $h+s=2h$.  One further step invokes (ii) and lands at valuation
$h-s$, whose period is $p^{h-s}$ by (i).  The successive middle states and the
high state cannot belong to the low invariant cycle, proving the exact tail
$r+1$ and (iii).
\end{proof}

For $p=2$, the middle layer has $r=1$ for every unit $u$, so all its states
have tail two.  No odd-prime assumption is hidden in the theorem.

\section{Cycle and tail censuses}

\begin{theorem}[complete functional-graph census]\label{thm:census}
For every integer $v$ with $0\leq v<a/2$, the map has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycles}
 (p-1)p^{a-2v-1}
\end{equation}
cycles of length $p^v$.  The recurrent population is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrent}
 p^a-p^{\lfloor a/2\rfloor}.
\end{equation}
The positive-tail populations are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item If $a=2h+1$, exactly $p^h$ states have tail one and no state has a
      larger tail.
\item If $a=2h$, exactly $p^{h-1}$ states occur at each tail depth
      $1,2,\ldots,p$, and no other positive depth occurs.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, the sharp maximum tail is one for odd $a$ and $p$ for even
$a$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The stratum of exact valuation $v<a$ has
$(p-1)p^{a-v-1}$ states.  For $2v<a$, Theorem~\ref{thm:strata} partitions it
into cycles of common length $p^v$, giving \eqref{eq:cycles}.  The union of
the low strata is the complement of the multiples of
$p^{\lceil a/2\rceil}$, whose number is $p^{\lfloor a/2\rfloor}$; this proves
\eqref{eq:recurrent}.

For $a=2h+1$, the high region is exactly the set of multiples of $p^{h+1}$,
including zero.  It has $p^h$ elements, all of tail one by
Theorem~\ref{thm:strata}(ii).  For $a=2h$, the strict high region consists of
the $p^{h-1}$ multiples of $p^{h+1}$ and has tail one.  In the middle stratum,
each nonzero residue class of $u$ modulo $p$ occurs $p^{h-1}$ times.  The
quantity $r=p-(u\bmod p)$ runs once through $1,\ldots,p-1$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:strata}(iii) gives $p^{h-1}$ states at each depth
$2,\ldots,p$.  This accounts for every state.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact fibres and image defect}

Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect}
 d=p^{\lfloor(a-1)/2\rfloor}.
\end{equation}
Define the double-target set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:double-set}
 \mathcal D=\{1\}\ \cup\!
 \bigcup_{1\leq w<a/2}
 \left\{p^w\bigl(1+p^{a-2w}u\bigr):
       1\leq u<p^w,\ p\nmid u\right\}.
\end{equation}
All displayed representatives in \eqref{eq:double-set} lie strictly below
$p^a$.  Define the empty-target set by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empty-set}
 \mathcal Z=
 \begin{cases}
 \{y:\vp(y)>h\},&a=2h+1,\\
 \{p^hz:0\leq z<p^h,\ z\equiv1\pmod p\},&a=2h.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[complete fibre atlas]\label{thm:fibres}
For every target $y\in\{0,\ldots,p^a-1\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre-size}
 \#T^{-1}(y)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&y\in\mathcal Z,\\
 2,&y\in\mathcal D,\\
 1,&y\notin\mathcal Z\cup\mathcal D.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Moreover $\mathcal Z$ and $\mathcal D$ are disjoint and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre-census}
 |\mathcal Z|=|\mathcal D|=d.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:image}
 |\im T|=p^a-d,
\end{equation}
and the counts of zero-, one-, and two-element fibres are respectively
$d,p^a-2d,d$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On every low stratum, the translation in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:strata}(i) is a bijection.  Each low target therefore has one
low predecessor.  A strict high source of valuation $a-w$ has the unique form
$p^{a-w}u$ with $1\leq u<p^w$ and $p\nmid u$, and \eqref{eq:high-fall} sends it
to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:high-image}
 p^w\bigl(1+p^{a-2w}u\bigr).
\end{equation}
The zero source maps to $1$.  Valuation $w$ and then $u$ are recoverable from
\eqref{eq:high-image}, so high sources inject into exactly $\mathcal D$.
Every member of $\mathcal D$ already has its low predecessor, and no other
target receives a high predecessor.  This proves the double-fibre part and
the fibre cap.

If $a$ is odd, there is no middle stratum.  Low sources target low strata and
high sources also target low strata, so precisely the high targets have no
predecessor.  They form the first case of $\mathcal Z$.

Let $a=2h$.  On the middle stratum, the unit coordinate map is
$u\mapsto u+1\pmod {p^h}$.  A high or zero target coordinate is divisible by
$p$ and has the unique unit predecessor $u=z-1$.  A middle target coordinate
$z$ is hit exactly when $z-1$ is also a unit, which fails exactly for
$z\equiv1\pmod p$.  Thus the missed targets are precisely the second case of
$\mathcal Z$.  All targets outside $\mathcal Z\cup\mathcal D$ have the one
predecessor already described, proving \eqref{eq:fibre-size}.

For $a=2h+1$, $|\mathcal Z|=p^h=d$.  For $a=2h$,
$|\mathcal Z|=p^{h-1}=d$.  Finally,
\begin{equation*}
 |\mathcal D|=1+\sum_{1\leq w<a/2}(p-1)p^{w-1}=d
\end{equation*}
for either parity, with an empty sum allowed.  The two sets lie in high or
middle versus low strata, so they are disjoint.  Equations
\eqref{eq:fibre-census} and \eqref{eq:image} follow.
\end{proof}

Equivalently, a target other than $1$ is double precisely when, for
$w=\vp(y)<a/2$, the quotient $y/p^w$ is congruent to $1$ modulo
$p^{a-2w}$ and the resulting coefficient is a nonzero unit modulo $p$.
