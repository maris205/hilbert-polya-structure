## 3. Independent reconstruction of the laws

### 3.1 One-clock/one-mark coupling

Assign edge `i` an independent `Exp(lambda_i)` variable and an independent
uniform mark in `E_i`. For a finite active set, the minimum has label `i` with
probability `lambda_i/Lambda(active)`. Conditional on the minimum, the residual
waiting times retain their rates. Reading the marks in increasing-clock order
therefore realizes the literal rate-proportional-edge/uniform-vertex process.
This needs exactly the manuscript's fixed positive rates and independent marks.

A petal mark removes one edge. The first core mark removes every remaining
edge of that component. If no core mark exists, every edge eventually supplies
one petal vertex. Thus the coupling also identifies the recorded endpoint
without silently minimizing it.

### 3.2 Weighted aggregate endpoint

Fix a proper mask `A`. If the terminal core mark occurs at time `t`, every
`i in A` must have a petal mark and `X_i<t`; one edge `j` outside `A` must ring
at `t` with a core mark; and every other clock outside `A` must exceed `t`.
The marks of those late outside edges are unobserved and integrate to one.
Summing over `j` gives exactly

```text
product_(i in A) r_i
* sum_(j notin A) q_j lambda_j
* integral_0^infinity exp(-Lambda(complement A)t)
    product_(i in A)(1-exp(-lambda_i t)) dt.
```

Expanding the final product gives the manuscript's alternating sum `I(A)`.
Because `A` is proper, every denominator is positive; the integral proves the
alternating sum itself is positive. The all-petal cell has mass `product_i r_i`.
These events partition the marked-clock space, so normalization is not being
inferred from the finite verifier.

I also performed an independent exact permutation-and-mark calculation, not
calling any function from `code/verify.py`. For

```text
c=2, p=(1,3,2), lambda=(1,2,4), A={1,3},
```

the six clock orders and eight category vectors give total mass one. They give
`I(A)=1/7` and `pi(A)=2/105`, exactly as the integral formula. This includes a
nonuniform-rate mask with two selected petals and a nontrivial complement.

### 3.3 Actual vertices

The aggregate event depends only on clocks and core-versus-petal categories.
It does not reveal identities inside a category. Conditional identities are
therefore still independent and uniform. Mixing over the possible terminal
edge does not bias the core identity because every mixture component is
uniform on the same core `C`. Hence a specified core-stopped endpoint has mass

```text
pi(A)/(c product_(i in A) p_i),
```

and a specified all-petal endpoint has mass `product_i 1/(c+p_i)`.
For the preceding weighted example these are respectively `1/210` and `1/60`.
Summing the resolved cells recovers the aggregate cells exactly.

### 3.4 Unit rates, the top atom, and moments

At unit rates the edge order is a uniform permutation independent of marks.
For `0<=t<m`, `T>t` holds exactly when the first `t` marks are petals. Averaging
over their uniformly random `t`-set gives

```text
Pr(T>t)=e_t(r_1,...,r_m)/binom(m,t)=s_t.
```

Tail differencing gives the cells below `m`. The top cell is not merely the
all-petal endpoint. It is the disjoint union of

1. all `m` marks being petals, and
2. the first `m-1` marks being petals while the last edge has a core mark.

Consequently

```text
Pr(T=m)
= product_i r_i + (1/m) sum_j q_j product_(i!=j) r_i
= e_(m-1)(r)/m.
```

The equality also survives `m=1`, when the two terms are `r_1` and `q_1` and
`T` is identically one.

As a separate exact check, take `c=2` and `p=(1,2,4)`, so
`r=(1/3,1/2,2/3)`. Then

```text
(s_0,s_1,s_2)=(1,1/2,13/54),
Pr(T=1,2,3)=(1/2,7/27,13/54),
Pr(T=3)=1/9 + 7/54,
E[T]=47/27, E[T^2]=100/27, Var(T)=491/729.
```

The two top-cell terms are the all-petal and final-core mechanisms. The moment
values agree with the identities
`T=sum_(t=0)^(m-1) 1{T>t}` and
`T^2=sum_(t=0)^(m-1)(2t+1)1{T>t}`. No independence of the tail indicators is
assumed.

### 3.5 Forest endpoint and discrete selection-count factorization

For vertex-disjoint components, `(R_a,T_a)` is a measurable function only of
component `a`'s clocks and marks. Those pairs are independent. Sorting all
clocks globally yields the required global rate-proportional scheduler, while
global interleaving preserves every within-component order. Suppressing clocks
after a component stops changes no local history. It follows pathwise that the
forest endpoint is the disjoint union of the component endpoints and

```text
T=sum_a T_a.
```

The endpoint law therefore tensors and the selection-count PGFs multiply,
including at unequal fixed rates. Adding a one-edge component to the preceding
three-edge unit example simply shifts its masses to forest counts `2,3,4`,
with probabilities `1/2,7/27,13/54`, an exact normalization check.

This is not a statement about continuous elapsed time. In the exponential
embedding the forest finishes only when every component has finished, hence

```text
S=max_a S_a.
```

For two one-edge unit-rate components,
`Pr(S<=t)=(1-exp(-t))^2`. A sum would instead have the Erlang convolution law.
The two distributions are different. The Round-1 manuscript states precisely
the maximum law and makes no elapsed-time convolution claim.
