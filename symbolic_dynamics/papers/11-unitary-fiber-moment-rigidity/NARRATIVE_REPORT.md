# Paper11 Narrative Report

## One-sentence contribution

SD-C13 proves that a finite positive unitary fiber over tensor-prime symbolic
loops cannot be both determinant-visible and invisible to every Euler
repetition coefficient.

## What was attempted

The candidate attaches entropy-local Bloch holonomies to the existing
tensor-prime atom loops.  The intended gain was vertical/parameter-dependent
phase motion without changing the prime inventory or the `p^r` repetition
rule.

## What survives

- The block-diagonal twisted transfer has an exact analytic tracial
  determinant on `Re(s)>1`.
- Normalized positive trace gives an unusually sharp obstruction: the first
  moment alone forces the visible unitary to be identity.
- Ordinary Fredholm exactness is stricter; the first `d` moments force a
  one-dimensional trivial fiber.
- A finite recurrent triangle or a finite positive set of parallel returns
  necessarily leaves a mixed repetition at finite order.

## What fails

Nontrivial Bloch motion changes the target ledger.  Nonfaithful states hide
motion from the determinant used to define the ledger.  Matched graded
sectors preserve the moments only because the moving sector cancels from the
Berezinian.  Prime, composite, and random increasing clocks all exhibit the
same finite Bloch motion, so motion by itself proves too much.

## Literature position

Twisted Perron--Frobenius operators, unitary characters in symbolic
`L`-functions, graph-covering zetas, and group extensions of shifts are
classical.  The project-specific synthesis is the application of finite
positive moment rigidity and mixed-cycle survival to the tensor-prime
repetition ledger.  No priority claim is made for unitary moment uniqueness
or twisted zeta functions.
