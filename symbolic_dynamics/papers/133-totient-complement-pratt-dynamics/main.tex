\begin{abstract}
For a nonempty finite prime set $P$, put $n=\prod_{p\in P}p$ and
iterate $F_n(d)=\gcd(n,(n/d)\varphi(d))$ on the divisors of $n$.
We give three exact all-parameter descriptions of this finite arithmetic
dynamics.  Its squarefree support is the signed Boolean map
$S\mapsto(P\setminus S)\cup N(S)$ on the induced prime-chain directed
acyclic graph.  If $s$ vertices have no incoming edge, an explicit
topological decoder extends arbitrary phases on those vertices to every
recurrent state.  Consequently there are no fixed states, there are exactly
$2^s$ recurrent states, and they form $2^{s-1}$ two-cycles.  A two-step
erasure identity shows that every orbit reaches recurrence by time at most
$h+1$, where $h$ is the longest directed-path length; this bound is not
claimed sharp.  Finally, inclusion--exclusion gives a closed formula for the
one-step fibre over every target divisor, including targets outside the
image.  Prime-chain geometry, signed-Boolean formalism, topological
propagation, and inclusion--exclusion are treated as owned background.  The
residual contribution is their complete conjunction for the displayed
literal map.  Exact finite checks are falsification controls only, and
external release remains on hold.
\end{abstract}

\section{The literal map and the theorem}

Let $P$ be a nonempty finite set of primes and $n=\prod_{p\in P}p$.
Every divisor of $n$ is squarefree, so we identify $d\mid n$ with
$S=\supp(d)\subseteq P$.  Put a directed edge
\begin{equation}\label{eq:edge}
                         q\longrightarrow p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p\mid q-1.
\end{equation}
The edge strictly decreases the prime and hence defines a directed acyclic
graph.  Write
\[
 \Par(p)=\{q\in P:q\to p\},\qquad
 \Par(U)=\bigcup_{p\in U}\Par(p),
\]
and call $p$ a \emph{source} when $\Par(p)=\varnothing$.  Thus ``source''
means no incoming edge under the orientation \eqref{eq:edge}.  Let
$s=|\Src|$, and let
\[
 \delta(p)=\max\{\text{length of a directed path from a source to }p\},
 \qquad h=\max_{p\in P}\delta(p).
\]
Every vertex is reached from a source because the graph is finite.
Prime chains and their heights are classical objects; we use that geometry
as background and make no contribution claim for it
\cite{FordKonyaginLuca2010}.

For $S\subseteq P$, set
\[
 N(S)=\{p\in P:\text{some }q\in S\text{ satisfies }q\to p\}.
\]
The complete result is as follows.  Entry time means the least $t$ for which
the state at time $t$ is recurrent.

\begin{theorem}[Decoder, entry bound, and every-target fibres]\label{thm:main}
For
\[
                 F_n(d)=\gcd\!\left(n,\frac nd\ph(d)\right),\qquad d\mid n,
\]
the following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Under squarefree support, $F_n$ is conjugate to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support-map}
                        F(S)=(P\setminus S)\cup N(S).
\end{equation}
\item The recurrent set has $2^s$ states, no state is fixed, and there are
exactly $2^{s-1}$ cycles, all of exact period two.  The two phases are
explicitly decoded from arbitrary source bits by \eqref{eq:source-phase}
and \eqref{eq:decoder} below.
\item Every orbit has entry time at most $h+1$.
\item For every target support $B\subseteq P$, with $Z=P\setminus B$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre-main}
 |F^{-1}(B)|=
 \sum_{\substack{T\subseteq B\\
 (Z\cup T)\cap\Par(Z\cup T)=\varnothing}}
 (-1)^{|T|}
 2^{|P|-|Z\cup T|-|\Par(Z\cup T)|}.
\end{equation}
The formula is valid also when $B$ is outside the image, in which case its
value is zero.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section{Support conjugacy and complemented coordinates}

\begin{proposition}[Statewise arithmetic reduction]\label{prop:conjugacy}
The literal divisor update has support \eqref{eq:support-map}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $p\in P$.  Since $n$ is squarefree, membership of $p$ in the support of
$F_n(d)$ is equivalent to divisibility of $(n/d)\ph(d)$ by $p$.  If
$p\notin S$, then $p\mid n/d$.  If $p\in S$, then $p\nmid n/d$, while
Euler's product formula gives
\[
                              \ph(d)=\prod_{q\in S}(q-1).
\]
Thus $p\mid\ph(d)$ exactly when $p\mid q-1$ for some $q\in S$; the choice
$q=p$ never contributes.  This is precisely membership in
$(P\setminus S)\cup N(S)$, prime by prime.
\end{proof}

Let $x_p=\mathbf1_{p\in S}$ and pass to complemented bits
$y_p=1-x_p$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:conjugacy} is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:y-rule}
 y_p(t+1)=(1-y_p(t))A_p(t),\qquad
 A_p(t)=\prod_{q\in\Par(p)}y_q(t),
\end{equation}
where the empty product is one.  This is an AND--NOT rule on a signed
wiring diagram.  General AND--NOT and signed-Boolean frameworks, including
their graph reductions, are established background
\cite{VelizCubaEtAl2012,AracenaCabreraCrotSalinas2021}.
One immediate identity will drive both the decoder and the transient bound:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:consecutive-zero}
                              y_p(t)y_p(t+1)=0.
\end{equation}

\section{Source phases and complete recurrence}

At a source, \eqref{eq:y-rule} is toggling.  Assign an arbitrary bit
$\eta_r\in\{0,1\}$ to each $r\in\Src$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:source-phase}
                          y_r^0=\eta_r,\qquad y_r^1=1-\eta_r.
\end{equation}
Process the nonsources in any order in which every parent precedes its
child.  Once the parent phases of $p$ have been assigned, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decoder}
 A_p^\epsilon=\prod_{q\in\Par(p)}y_q^\epsilon,\qquad
                         y_p^0=A_p^1,\quad y_p^1=A_p^0.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Unique local phase extension]\label{lem:phase}
For a nonsource $p$, suppose every parent phase pair obeys
$y_q^0y_q^1=0$.  Then $A_p^0A_p^1=0$, and \eqref{eq:decoder} is the unique
pair satisfying the two phase equations induced by \eqref{eq:y-rule}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Because $\Par(p)$ is nonempty, any one parent gives
$A_p^0A_p^1=0$.  The phase equations are
\[
 y_p^1=(1-y_p^0)A_p^0,\qquad
 y_p^0=(1-y_p^1)A_p^1.
\]
If both $A$'s vanish, they force $(y_p^0,y_p^1)=(0,0)$.  If
$(A_p^0,A_p^1)=(1,0)$, the second equation first forces $y_p^0=0$ and the
first then forces $y_p^1=1$; the remaining case is symmetric.  These three
possibilities give exactly \eqref{eq:decoder}.  They also show
$y_p^0y_p^1=0$, closing the topological induction.
\end{proof}

The decoder therefore produces a genuine two-phase orbit from each source
assignment and is injective because it retains that assignment on the
sources.  Completeness will follow from a global entry estimate.

\begin{lemma}[Two-step erasure]\label{lem:erasure}
If $p$ is not a source, then for every $t\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:erasure}
                               y_p(t+2)=A_p(t+1).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:consecutive-zero} and nonemptiness of $\Par(p)$,
$A_p(t)A_p(t+1)=0$.  Applying \eqref{eq:y-rule} twice gives
\[
 y_p(t+2)=A_p(t+1)\bigl(1-A_p(t)(1-y_p(t))\bigr)=A_p(t+1).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform entry bound]\label{prop:entry}
Each coordinate $p$ is two-periodic from time $\delta(p)+1$ onward.
Consequently every orbit enters recurrence by time at most $h+1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Sources toggle from time zero, so the asserted weaker starting time one is
valid.  Let $p$ be a nonsource and assume the claim for all its parents.
For $t\geq\delta(p)+1$ we have $t\geq2$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:erasure}
applied at $t$ and at $t-2$ gives
\[
 y_p(t+2)=\prod_{q\in\Par(p)}y_q(t+1),\qquad
 y_p(t)=\prod_{q\in\Par(p)}y_q(t-1).
\]
Now $t-1\geq\delta(p)=1+\max_{q\in\Par(p)}\delta(q)$.  Every parent is
therefore already two-periodic at time $t-1$, so the products agree.  This
proves the coordinate claim by induction.  At time $h+1$ all coordinates
are two-periodic, hence the whole state is on a cycle of length dividing
two.
\end{proof}

Every periodic orbit thus has period at most two.  No state can be fixed,
because every finite nonempty directed acyclic graph has a source and every
source coordinate toggles.  Hence every periodic orbit has exact period two.
Viewed at either phase, it satisfies the equations of
Lemma~\ref{lem:phase}; topological uniqueness shows that it is the decoder
output for its source bits.  There are consequently $2^s$ recurrent states
and $2^{s-1}$ cycles.  This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(ii)--(iii).
The estimate $h+1$ is deliberately only an upper bound.  It includes the
singleton case $h=0$ and applies componentwise to disconnected graphs.

\section{The fibre over every target}

Return to the support bits $x_p$.  From \eqref{eq:support-map}, a target
zero at $p$ occurs exactly on the event
\begin{equation}\label{eq:event}
 E_p=\{x_p=1\text{ and }x_q=0\text{ for every }q\in\Par(p)\}.
\end{equation}
Fix $B\subseteq P$ and $Z=P\setminus B$.  The constraints at the target
zeros require every event $E_p$ with $p\in Z$, while target ones require
that none of the events $E_p$ with $p\in B$ occur.  Inclusion--exclusion
over the latter events yields the sum over $T\subseteq B$ of
$(-1)^{|T|}$ times the number of assignments satisfying all $E_p$ for
$p\in Z\cup T$.

For $U=Z\cup T$, this intersection forces every bit in $U$ to one and every
bit in $\Par(U)$ to zero.  It is empty if $U\cap\Par(U)\ne\varnothing$.
When those sets are disjoint, precisely
$|P|-|U|-|\Par(U)|$ bits remain free, so there are
$2^{|P|-|U|-|\Par(U)|}$ assignments.  Substitution gives
\eqref{eq:fibre-main}.  This derivation imposes no image hypothesis on
$B$; if the fibre is empty, inclusion--exclusion returns zero.  It completes
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
