Sharp Height Clocks and Exact Target Fibres}

\begin{abstract}
For a positive integer $N$ and a cyclic word of divisors of $N$, divide each
letter synchronously by its gcd with the next letter.  Prime valuations turn
this nonlinear arithmetic rule into the truncated difference
$(e_i-e_{i+1})_+$.  We prove that every orbit becomes fixed, with sharp global
height equal to the largest prime exponent of $N$ for cycles of length at
least three; when $N>1$, lengths one and two have sharp height one.  Fixed words are
counted by weighted cyclic independence polynomials, prime by prime.  We also
solve the labelled one-step inverse problem: every target fibre is a product
of traces of explicit local zero--one matrices, including empty fibres and all
boundary parameters.  Summing these traces recovers the full state-space
mass.  Exact enumeration supplies author-side counterexample pressure but is
not proof or novelty evidence.  Standard difference dynamics, divisor
valuations, and transfer matrices receive no contribution credit, and the
manuscript remains \texttt{HOLD\_EXTERNAL} under an open owner audit.
\end{abstract}

\section{The literal map and its boundary}

Let $N\geq1$, let $\Div(N)$ be its positive divisors, and let $m\geq1$.
On $\Div(N)^m$, with indices in $\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q}
 (Q_Nx)_i=\frac{x_i}{\gcd(x_i,x_{i+1})}.
\end{equation}
This is a deterministic synchronous finite system in the sense of the broad
parallel-dynamics framework surveyed in \cite{AledoMartinezValverde2015}.
Cyclic difference systems such as Ducci maps are established objects
\cite{LewisTefft2025}; their additive modular rule is not \eqref{eq:Q}.
We assign this general vocabulary, prime factorization, and generic
transfer-matrix counting zero contribution credit.  The scoped result is the
joint clock, fixed-locus, and every-labelled-target theorem for \eqref{eq:Q}.
A bounded formula search found no literal owner, but that non-hit establishes
neither novelty nor priority.

Write $N=\prod_{p\mid N}p^{a_p}$.  For $a\geq0$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:D}
 D_a(e)_i=(e_i-e_{i+1})_+,
 \qquad e\in\{0,\ldots,a\}^m.
\end{equation}
Taking $p$-adic valuations in \eqref{eq:Q} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conjugacy}
 \nu_p((Q_Nx)_i)=D_{a_p}(\nu_p(x_0),\ldots,\nu_p(x_{m-1}))_i.
\end{equation}
Thus the arithmetic system is the direct product of the maps \eqref{eq:D}.

For a finite map, the tail of a state is its least entrance time into a
cycle.  All cycles below will be fixed points.

\section{The truncated-difference clock}

\begin{lemma}[frozen top peaks]\label{lem:peaks}
Let $e\in\{0,\ldots,a\}^m$ have maximum $h>0$ and put $z=D_a(e)$.
Every coordinate of $z$ equal to $h$ is bordered by zeros and remains an
isolated value $h$ under all later iterates.  All other coordinates of $z$
are at most $h-1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The equality $z_i=h$ forces $e_i=h$ and $e_{i+1}=0$.  Maximality of $h$
then gives $z_{i-1}=0$, while $z_{i+1}=0$ follows directly.  Let $S$ be the
set of these top-peak positions, put $f_i=h\mathbf1\{i\in S\}$, and write
$z=f+w$.  The vector $w$ vanishes on $S$ and on both neighboring sites of
every member of $S$.  A coordinate check gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:split}
 D_a(f+w)=f+D_a(w).
\end{equation}
The required zeros persist under $D_a$, so \eqref{eq:split} may be iterated.
Thus the top peaks remain frozen and the independent residual vector has
maximum at most $h-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[complete temporal behavior]\label{thm:clock}
Every orbit of $Q_N$ reaches a fixed point.  If $N=1$, the unique state is
fixed.  If $N>1$, the sharp maximum tail is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:height}
 H(N,m)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&m=1\text{ or }m=2,\\
 \max_{p\mid N}a_p,&m\geq3.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
In particular, there are no nontrivial recurrent cycles.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Induct on the largest exponent $h$ in a word for $D_a$.  After one step,
Lemma~\ref{lem:peaks} separates permanent top peaks from subsystems of height
at most $h-1$, with zero boundary letters.  The induction hypothesis fixes
the latter within $h-1$ more steps.  Hence $D_a^h(e)$ is fixed.  For $m\geq3$
the word
\[
 (0,\ldots,0,h,1)
\]
has exact tail $h$: each step decreases the penultimate positive entry by
one, and the final $1$ then becomes isolated.  For $m=1$, $D_a(e)=0$.  For
$m=2$, $D_a(u,v)$ is either $(u-v,0)$ or $(0,v-u)$ and is fixed.  Nonfixed
examples exist in both cases when $a>0$.

Equation \eqref{eq:conjugacy} makes the tail of a divisor word the maximum of
its primewise tails.  Choosing only a prime with largest exponent proves
sharpness in \eqref{eq:height}.  Since every orbit reaches a fixed point,
recurrence equals fixedness.
\end{proof}

\section{Fixed words}

Define the cyclic support polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Im}
 I_m(z)=\sum_{\substack{s\in\{0,1\}^m\\s_is_{i+1}=0\ \forall i}}
 z^{\sum_i s_i}
 =\tr\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&z\\1&0\end{bmatrix}^{m}\right).
\end{equation}
This convention includes the self-neighboring $m=1$ cycle, so $I_1(z)=1$;
$I_2(z)=1+2z$.  For $m\geq2$ one may also write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Iclosed}
 I_m(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}
 \frac{m}{m-k}\binom{m-k}{k}z^k.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[fixed-state census]\label{thm:fixed}
A divisor word is fixed by $Q_N$ exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coprime}
 \gcd(x_i,x_{i+1})=1\qquad\text{for every }i.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixedcount}
 |\Fix(Q_N)|=\prod_{p\mid N} I_m(a_p),
\end{equation}
where the empty product for $N=1$ is one.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The equality $D_a(e)=e$ holds precisely when $e_i>0$ implies
$e_{i+1}=0$.  Thus the positive support is a cyclic zero--one word with no
adjacent ones.  Once its support has size $k$, its positive heights have
$a^k$ choices.  This gives $I_m(a)$ for one prime.  Prime valuations are
independent, proving both \eqref{eq:coprime} and the product
\eqref{eq:fixedcount}.  The two-state transfer matrix in \eqref{eq:Im}
records whether the preceding support site was occupied; expansion by the
support size gives \eqref{eq:Iclosed}.
\end{proof}

The same two-state matrix also gives a compact recurrence that avoids any
ambiguity about the short cyclic supports.

\begin{corollary}[recurrence for the fixed factor]\label{cor:fixed-recurrence}
Put $I_1(z)=1$ and $I_2(z)=1+2z$.  For $m\geq3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Irecurrence}
 I_m(z)=I_{m-1}(z)+zI_{m-2}(z).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, if
$\lambda_\pm=(1\pm\sqrt{1+4z})/2$, then
$I_m(z)=\lambda_+^m+\lambda_-^m$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The matrix in \eqref{eq:Im} satisfies $M^2=M+zI$.  Multiply by $M^{m-2}$
and take traces.  Its two eigenvalues are $\lambda_\pm$.
\end{proof}

\section{Every-target fibres}

For $a\geq0$ and $b\in\{0,\ldots,a\}$, let $L_b^{(a)}$ be the
$(a+1)\times(a+1)$ zero--one matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localmatrix}
 L_b^{(a)}(u,v)=\mathbf1\{(u-v)_+=b\},
 \qquad 0\leq u,v\leq a.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[labelled inverse law]\label{thm:fibre}
Let $y\in\Div(N)^m$ and put $b_{p,i}=\nu_p(y_i)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre}
 |Q_N^{-1}(y)|=
 \prod_{p\mid N}\tr\left(
   L_{b_{p,0}}^{(a_p)}L_{b_{p,1}}^{(a_p)}\cdots
   L_{b_{p,m-1}}^{(a_p)}\right).
\end{equation}
The formula is valid for $N=1$, $m=1$, $m=2$, zero exponents, and empty
fibres.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass}
 \sum_{y\in\Div(N)^m}|Q_N^{-1}(y)|=|\Div(N)|^m.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed prime, a source exponent word $u_0,\ldots,u_{m-1}$ contributes
one to the matrix-product trace exactly when every cyclic local constraint
$(u_i-u_{i+1})_+=b_{p,i}$ holds.  This counts precisely the primewise
predecessors.  Unique factorization makes the choices for distinct primes
independent, yielding \eqref{eq:fibre}.

For each ordered pair $(u,v)$ there is exactly one output exponent
$b=(u-v)_+$.  Hence $\sum_{b=0}^aL_b^{(a)}=J_{a+1}$, the all-ones matrix.
Summing the trace over all primewise targets gives
$\tr(J_{a+1}^m)=(a+1)^m$.  Multiplying these identities over the primes gives
\eqref{eq:mass}.
\end{proof}

The local matrices are particularly concrete.  The zero-output matrix is
the upper-triangular all-ones matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:U}
 L_0^{(a)}(u,v)=\mathbf1\{u\leq v\},
\end{equation}
whereas for $b>0$, $L_b^{(a)}$ has one on the offset diagonal $u=v+b$
and zero elsewhere.  Thus \eqref{eq:fibre} is an effective exact formula,
not an existence statement about an unspecified automaton.

\begin{corollary}[the all-one target and an image obstruction]
Let $\mathbf1=(1,\ldots,1)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:onefibre}
 |Q_N^{-1}(\mathbf1)|=|\Div(N)|.
\end{equation}
Its predecessors are exactly the constant divisor words.  More generally,
if one prime divides every coordinate of a target word, that target has no
predecessor.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For one prime, zero output at every site requires
$u_0\leq u_1\leq\cdots\leq u_{m-1}\leq u_0$, so every exponent is constant;
there are $a+1$ choices.  Multiplication over primes proves
\eqref{eq:onefibre}.  If every target exponent for one prime is positive,
then a predecessor would require the strict cyclic chain
$u_0>u_1>\cdots>u_{m-1}>u_0$, which is impossible.
\end{proof}

The matrix formula specializes to elementary boundary laws that are useful
for checking the cyclic convention.

\begin{corollary}[lengths one and two]\label{cor:short-fibres}
For $m=1$, the image is the single word $(1)$ and its fibre has size
$|\Div(N)|$.  For $m=2$, write the target as $(y_0,y_1)$ and put
$b_{p,j}=\nu_p(y_j)$.  Its fibre is empty unless
$\gcd(y_0,y_1)=1$.  When this condition holds, its size is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:short-fibre}
 \prod_{p^{a_p}\parallel N}
 \begin{cases}
  a_p+1,&b_{p,0}=b_{p,1}=0,\\
  a_p-b_{p,0}+1,&b_{p,0}>0,\\
  a_p-b_{p,1}+1,&b_{p,1}>0.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Every image word at length two is already fixed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
At length one, $(u-u)_+=0$.  At length two, a primewise source pair
$(u,v)$ maps to $((u-v)_+,(v-u)_+)$, so the two target exponents cannot both
be positive.  The zero--zero target has the $a_p+1$ sources $u=v$; a target
$(b,0)$ with $b>0$ has $a_p-b+1$ sources $(v+b,v)$, and the reverse case is
symmetric.  Multiplication over primes proves \eqref{eq:short-fibre}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[a composite example]
Take $N=12=2^2\cdot3$ and $m=3$.  The sharp height is two, witnessed by the
word $(1,4,2)$:
\[
 (1,4,2)\longmapsto(1,2,2)\longmapsto(1,1,2),
\]
and the last word is fixed.  Since $I_3(z)=1+3z$, the fixed-state count is
$I_3(2)I_3(1)=7\cdot4=28$.  This example also shows why the largest prime
exponent, rather than the number or size of the prime divisors, controls the
global tail.
\end{remark}
