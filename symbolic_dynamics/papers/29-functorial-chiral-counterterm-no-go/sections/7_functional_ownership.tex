\section{Ordinary trace, modified determinant, and a new functional}
\label{sec:ownership}

The critical countable family satisfies
$\cB_{1/2+it}\in\bigcap_{r>2}\cS_r$ but
$\cB_{1/2+it}\notin\cS_2$.  Hence $\cB_s^2$ is not trace class on the
critical line.  The expression $Q_F$ remains an exact finite diagnostic; its
renormalized limit is not an ordinary operator trace.

The third-regularized Fredholm determinant is an honest and separate object:
\begin{equation}
 \log\detthree(I-z\cB_s)
 =-\sum_{m\ge3}\frac{z^m}{m}\Tr(\cB_s^m).
 \label{eq:det3-log}
\end{equation}
It removes powers one and two in full.  The chiral block has zero odd traces,
so the first visible logarithmic coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 -\frac{z^4}{4}\Tr(\cB_s^4).
 \label{eq:first-visible}
\end{equation}
This fourth-order coefficient remains owned by $\detthree$.  Its generic
control motion does not make the renormalized quadratic finite part
selective.

\begin{definition}[Scheme-dependent renormalized functional]
For a declared finite-part scheme $\cR$, define
\begin{equation}
 \Dren_{\cR}(s,z)
 :=\detthree(I-z\cB_s)
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{z^2}{2}
 \FP_{\cR}\Tr(\cB_s^2)\right].
 \label{eq:Dren}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Equation \eqref{eq:Dren} defines a new functional.  It is not the ordinary
Fredholm determinant, is not $\det_2$, and is not an alternative definition
of $\detthree$.

\begin{proposition}[Scheme change and divisor]
\label{prop:divisor}
Suppose two finite parts differ by a holomorphic function $h(s)$ on the
honest $\detthree$ strip.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\Dren_{\cR_2}(s,z)}{\Dren_{\cR_1}(s,z)}
 =\exp[-z^2h(s)/2].
 \label{eq:scheme-ratio}
\end{equation}
The ratio is entire and zero-free in $z$.  Therefore scheme changes do not
alter the auxiliary-$z$ divisor, including multiplicities.
\end{proposition}

For leading-only and full-diagonal schemes,
$h(s)=2C_\eta P(1+2\eta)$ is constant.  The ratio is nontrivial, so the
functionals differ, but it is zero-free, so their divisors agree.  This
agreement is analytic bookkeeping.  It supplies no identification of those
auxiliary zeros with any external target set.

The finite ledgers satisfy $Q_F(1-s)=Q_F(s)$, and
$M_\eta(1-s)=M_\eta(s)$.  Thus both minimal finite parts obey reflection.
More generally,
\begin{equation}
 \Dren_{\cR}(1-s,z)=\Dren_{\cR}(s,z)
 \label{eq:Dren-reflection}
\end{equation}
if and only if the chosen finite part is reflection symmetric.  Reflection
does not remove the weighted-$\ell^1$ scheme freedom in
\cref{thm:scheme-classification}.

Standard singular traces do not fill the ownership gap.  From the inherited
similarity theorem and the prime number theorem, the partial singular-value
sums of $\cB^2$ grow only as $O(\log\log N)$.  The usual Dixmier
$1/\log N$ normalization therefore yields zero.  A custom
$\log\log$ normalization would introduce a new ideal and generalized limit;
it would record a leading coefficient rather than the finite mixed
functional.  No Wodzicki, heat, or zeta-trace terminology is used without a
source-native calculus.
