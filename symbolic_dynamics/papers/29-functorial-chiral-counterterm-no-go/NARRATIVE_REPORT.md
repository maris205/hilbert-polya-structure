# Narrative report — Paper 29 / SD-C31

## One-sentence result

The critical incidence quadratic ledger admits natural finite parts but not a naturality-selected finite part, and every diagonal or quadratic pair-local linear-Gram scheme leaves a generic rather than arithmetic-selective residue.

## Why the paper is worth writing

Paper 28 reached the critical line with an honest third-regularized determinant, but that determinant deletes the complete quadratic coefficient.  Its finite-cutoff quadratic formula remained mathematically suggestive: one diagonal prime-harmonic term diverges, while the mixed phase is absolutely summable.  Paper 29 asks whether a source-natural subtraction can rescue precisely that mixed phase.

The useful answer is a classification, not a recovery theorem.  Subtracting the leading harmonic germ works.  Subtracting the full diagonal also works.  They differ by a positive finite sum, and arbitrary summable natural local shifts produce more schemes.  Isomorphism naturality and cutoff compatibility therefore determine the divergent germ but do not normalize the finite part.

The second obstruction is selectivity.  A diagonal counterterm has no pair frequency and cannot alter the mixed cosine ledger.  Once the class is widened to pair-local rules linear in the native Gram contraction, the coefficient must either preserve a local nonzero pair mechanism or cancel it.  Matched arithmetic and control pairs present the same local input, so those two requirements are inconsistent.  Exact mutated, composite-only, generic-DAG, and random-inventory controls realize the obstruction.

## The central distinction

The paper separates three ownership layers.

1. The finite-cutoff expression is an exact diagnostic.  It does not become an ordinary infinite trace at the Hilbert--Schmidt boundary.
2. The third-regularized Fredholm determinant is canonical on its proved Schatten strip, but its definition removes the full quadratic power.
3. Reintroducing a chosen quadratic finite part creates a new scheme-dependent holomorphic functional.  A scheme change multiplies it by a zero-free quadratic exponential and therefore leaves its auxiliary-
   \(z\) divisor unchanged.

That last observation is useful but does not produce arithmetic selectivity or a new ordinary determinant.

## What “functorial” means here

The base objects are pointed finite locally finite posets.  Pointed isomorphisms transport weights and coefficient marks.  Cutoff embeddings are order-ideal embeddings, so old intervals and Möbius coefficients are preserved.  The infinite divisibility calculation compiles the ambient Gram kernel once and then restricts active atoms.

This is intentionally narrower than functoriality for arbitrary monotone maps.  It is also weaker than canonicity.  Any summable equivariant local density may be added without violating these morphisms.

## Strongest limitation

The local no-go is not universal.  An isomorphism-invariant coefficient may inspect the entire filtered tower and recognize its global isomorphism class.  Such a construction can act as an inventory oracle while remaining formally natural.  This paper neither endorses it nor proves it impossible.  Excluding it requires an independently motivated locality, continuity, or additivity axiom.

## English abstract

At the critical line of a Möbius-incidence chiral family, the finite-cutoff quadratic ledger splits into a divergent diagonal prime-harmonic term and an absolutely summable mixed Gram phase.  We freeze a category of finite pointed posets with transported decorations and order-ideal cutoff embeddings, distinguish ambient active cutoffs from finite-source recompilation, and classify reference-independent natural quadratic counterterms.  The diagonal divergence fixes only a germ: every convergent scheme has diagonal coefficient (C_\eta+r_p) with
\(\sum_p|r_p|/p<\infty\), while summable natural mixed kernels remain free.  Full-diagonal and leading-only subtraction are both admissible and differ by the explicit finite shift
\(2C_\eta\sum_pp^{-1-2\eta}\).  A sharp Mertens finite part and an Abel/prime-zeta finite part differ by
\(-2C_\eta\gamma\).  Diagonal schemes preserve every mixed frequency.  More generally, no quadratic, additive, pair-local rule linear in the native Gram contraction can preserve the divisibility mixed mechanism while cancelling matched non-arithmetic controls.  This is a scoped theorem: bare naturality still permits nonlocal filtered-tower invariants.  We keep the nonexistent ordinary quadratic trace, the honest third-regularized Fredholm determinant, and a new scheme-dependent renormalized functional under separate ownership.  The latter differs between schemes by a zero-free exponential and does not change the auxiliary determinant divisor.  Exact relabel, cutoff, mutated, composite-only, generic-DAG, and random-inventory controls support the bounded no-go.  The strict arithmetic-spectral route remains rejected.

**Keywords:** symbolic dynamics; incidence algebra; Möbius inversion; counterterm; finite-part ambiguity; modified Fredholm determinant; naturality

## Recommended emphasis

Lead with nonuniqueness and the scoped no-go together.  The strongest positive result is a complete classification within a declared class.  The strongest negative result is that the retained mixed phase proves too much.  The final tone should be decisive about stopping the same-object local incidence route and explicit that the nonlocal tower problem remains open.
