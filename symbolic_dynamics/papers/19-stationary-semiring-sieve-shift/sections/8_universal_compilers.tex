\section{Universal support and factorial-monoid compilers}
\label{sec:compiler}

The pruning theorem explains why the trial computation disappears.  A
universal construction shows that this is not a harmless presentation
artifact: essentially any decidable accepted inventory can be made to look
the same to a Fredholm determinant.

\begin{theorem}[Universal total-decider wrapper]
\label{thm:universal}
Let $S\subseteq\{2,3,\ldots\}$ be decided by a total deterministic machine
with finite runtime $T(n)\ge1$.  There exists a one-sided countable
functional graph and an $\Sone$-holomorphic weighted adjacency $L_{S,s}$ for
$\RePart s>1$ such that, for every $r\ge1$ and $z\in\C$,
\begin{equation}
\Tr L_{S,s}^r=\sum_{n\in S}n^{-rs},
\qquad
\det(I-zL_{S,s})=\prod_{n\in S}(1-zn^{-s}).
\label{eq:universal}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each input $n$, expose the complete configuration chain
\[
C_{n,0}\longrightarrow C_{n,1}\longrightarrow\cdots
\longrightarrow C_{n,T(n)}.
\]
Send the terminal state to a self-loop $A_n$ when $n\in S$, and otherwise to
a one-way cemetery ray.  Give the $t$th computation or terminal edge weight
$[n(t+2)]^{-s}$, the $k$th cemetery edge weight $[n(k+1)]^{-s}$, and the
accepted loop weight $n^{-s}$.  With $\sigma=\RePart s>1$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\norm{L_{S,s}}_1
&\le \sum_{n\ge2}\sum_{t=0}^{T(n)}[n(t+2)]^{-\sigma}
 +\sum_{n\notin S}\sum_{k\ge1}[n(k+1)]^{-\sigma}
 +\sum_{n\in S}n^{-\sigma}\\
&\le 2\left(\sum_{n\ge2}n^{-\sigma}\right)
       \left(\sum_{j\ge2}j^{-\sigma}\right)
 +\sum_{n\ge2}n^{-\sigma}<\infty.
\end{aligned}
\]
The bound is independent of the growth of $T(n)$ because every finite inner
sum is dominated by the full $j$-series.  Compact lower bounds on $\sigma$
give $\Sone$-holomorphy.  Only the accepted loops are closed walks, so the
proof of \cref{thm:traces,thm:euler} gives \eqref{eq:universal}.
\end{proof}

The theorem is stronger than a random-support experiment.  It applies to
squares, powers of two, Fibonacci numbers, or the output of an arbitrarily
slow total program.  The determinant faithfully records the accepted set but
contains no certificate of why that set was selected.

\begin{theorem}[Factorial-monoid compiler]
\label{thm:factorial}
Let $M$ be a countable effective factorial monoid with atom set
$\mathcal A$, a terminating atom verifier, a multiplicative norm
$N:M\to(1,\infty)$, and source-defined transient roofs summable on a
half-plane.  Suppose
\[
\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}N(a)^{-\sigma}<\infty.
\]
The verifier-to-loop compilation gives a trace-class weighted adjacency
$L_{M,s}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\Tr L_{M,s}^r=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A}N(a)^{-rs},
\qquad
\det(I-zL_{M,s})
=\prod_{a\in\mathcal A}(1-zN(a)^{-s}).
\label{eq:factorial}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The terminating atom verifier supplies the configuration chain in
\cref{thm:universal}; a summable source enumeration supplies the transient
decay.  Correctness, summability, and the absence of reject cycles are the
only inputs to the trace and determinant proofs.  No special property of
rational primes is used.
\end{proof}

For a concrete control, take the monoid of monic polynomials over $\Fq$ and
$N(f)=q^{\deg f}$.  Unique factorization gives
\[
\prod_{\pi\ \mathrm{monic\ irreducible}}
(1-u^{\deg\pi})^{-1}
=\sum_{f\ \mathrm{monic}}u^{\deg f}
=\sum_{d\ge0}q^du^d=\frac1{1-qu}.
\]
Substituting $u=q^{-s}$ and inverting yields
\begin{equation}
\det(I-L_{M,s})
=\prod_\pi(1-q^{-s\deg\pi})=1-q^{1-s}.
\label{eq:poly-control}
\end{equation}

The same logic applies to free commutative monoids with arbitrary summable
generator norms, shuffled atom inventories, and generalized-prime systems.
Thus the exact Riemann Euler factor is a genuine success at A2 but not a
selector theorem.  The graph has computed the predicate before it creates
periodic support.  We call this \texttt{SELECTOR\_TAUTOLOGICAL} and
\texttt{PROVES\_TOO\_MUCH}, not logically circular: the trial-division proof
in \cref{thm:trial} remains a real proof.
