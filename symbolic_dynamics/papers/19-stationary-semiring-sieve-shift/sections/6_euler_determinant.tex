\section{Exact primitive ledger and Euler determinant}
\label{sec:euler}

For $\RePart s>1$, define
\[
D_{\SV}(s,z)=\det_{\Hcal}(I-zL_s),
\qquad
D_{\SV}(s)=D_{\SV}(s,1).
\]
The determinant exists because of \cref{thm:traceclass}.  The extra variable
$z$ records graph-step length; it will be important when discussing cycle
contraction.

\begin{theorem}[Power traces and primitive repetitions]
\label{thm:traces}
For every integer $r\ge1$ and $\RePart s>1$,
\[
\Tr(L_s^r)=\sum_p p^{-rs}.
\]
The primitive periodic orbits are exactly
$\gamma_p=[A_p]$.  Their $r$-fold temporal repetitions have total weight
$p^{-rs}$ and multiplicity one.  There are no mixed primitive cycles.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any vertex $v$, the diagonal coefficient
$\inner{L_s^r\delta_v}{\delta_v}$ is the sum of weights of length-$r$ closed
walks rooted at $v$.  By \cref{thm:trial}, each such walk is the $r$-fold
traversal of one accepted prime loop.  Its loop weight is $p^{-s}$.
Trace class permits the diagonal coefficients to be summed in the standard
basis, giving the stated formula.  The same cycle census proves the
primitive and multiplicity claims.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[One-object Fredholm--Euler identity]
\label{thm:euler}
For $\RePart s>1$ and every $z\in\C$,
\begin{equation}
D_{\SV}(s,z)=\prod_p(1-zp^{-s}).
\label{eq:euler-two-variable}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
D_{\SV}(s)=\prod_p(1-p^{-s})=\zeta(s)^{-1}.
\label{eq:euler-zeta}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $|z|$ initially small, the trace-class determinant expansion and
\cref{thm:traces} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\log D_{\SV}(s,z)
&=-\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{z^r}{r}\Tr(L_s^r)\\
&=-\sum_p\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{(zp^{-s})^r}{r}
=\sum_p\log(1-zp^{-s}).
\end{aligned}
\]
For fixed $\RePart s>1$, the Fredholm determinant is entire in $z$.  The
product on the right of \eqref{eq:euler-two-variable} is also entire and
normally convergent on compact $z$-sets because $\sum_pp^{-\sigma}<\infty$.
Equality near zero extends to every $z$.  At $z=1$, Euler's product is
absolutely convergent and gives \eqref{eq:euler-zeta}.
\end{proof}

The formula clears three bookkeeping gates simultaneously.  Primitive
orbits have the intended support, temporal repetitions carry the exponent
$r$ automatically, and the same parent operator produces all traces and the
determinant.  It does not yet say that the computation producing the support
is recurrently visible.  That question has the opposite answer.
