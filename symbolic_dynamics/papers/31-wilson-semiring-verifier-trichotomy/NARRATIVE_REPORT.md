# Narrative report — Paper 31 / SD-C33

## 1. One-sentence contribution

Finite-full-shift alphabet sum genuinely escapes Paper 30's bare
multiplicative-UFD clone, but the first exact congruence witness is copied by a
matched semiring clone and yields either a pruning-equivalent transient
determinant or a noncompact recurrent operator under the entropy clock.

## 2. Why this paper exists

Paper 30 closes every route that reads only the multiplicative free
commutative monoid of finite full shifts together with transported
divisibility, join, Möbius, roof, Gram, and compiler data.  The obvious next
question is whether a nonmultiplicative operation changes the conclusion.
Alphabet sum is the smallest clean test: it is intrinsic to finite alphabets,
interacts distributively with alphabet product, reconstructs successor and
congruence, and is not preserved by the bare prime-exponent monomial map.

That test produces a real separation.  It does not produce the desired
spectral mechanism.  Once addition is transported to a matched clone, the
separation disappears by isomorphism.  Wilson's theorem then gives a
particularly transparent terminal verifier.  Its arithmetic output is exact,
but its analytic behavior is already classified by Papers 19 and 20.

## 3. The theorem chain

### 3.1 Bare-clone escape

The alphabet-sum/product skeleton of finite full shifts is isomorphic to
\(\mathbb N_0\).  Paper 30's monomial map preserves multiplication but cannot
preserve addition with the ordinary polynomial operation, because
\(\Phi(1+1)=x_2\) while \(\Phi(1)+\Phi(1)=2\).  This is the precise positive
result: the old bare control no longer copies the source.

### 3.2 Matched-clone collapse

The stronger statement “addition defeats transported clones” is false.  A
relabeling \(F_n\mapsto y_n\) equipped with
\(y_m\oplus y_n=y_{m+n}\) and \(y_m\otimes y_n=y_{mn}\) is isomorphic to the
full source.  Successor, congruence, entropy, Wilson states, edge weights, and
markers all transport.  The matched clone reproduces the complete construction
exactly.

### 3.3 Exact Wilson cycles

The recurrence \(r_{n,k+1}=r_{n,k}(k+1)\bmod n\) computes the terminal relation
\((n-1)!\equiv-1\pmod n\) without a supplied prime oracle.  Closing the block
at that relation yields one simple primitive cycle of length \(p-1\) for every
prime and none for a composite.  The \(r\)-fold temporal repetition has weight
\(p^{-rs}\), so the legitimate prime-power ledger is exact.

### 3.4 Formal product but no whole-operator determinant

The graph-step product is
\[
  D_W(s,z)=\prod_p(1-z^{p-1}p^{-s}).
\]
It is a normally convergent periodic function for \(|z|<1\), and at
\(z=1,\Re s>1\) it equals \(1/\zeta(s)\).  That identity belongs to finite
power-diagonal sums, not to an ordinary trace of the infinite adjacency.

Every prime cycle has \(p-1\) edges but total roof only \(\log p\).  For any
nonnegative allocation, one edge has roof at most \(\log p/(p-1)\), so its
weight tends to one.  These edges lie in orthogonal blocks.  The primary
operator is therefore noncompact and belongs to no finite Schatten class.

### 3.5 The two apparent repairs change the claim

First return contracts each cycle to a diagonal entry \(p^{-s}\).  The result
is trace class for \(\Re s>1\) and has an honest determinant, but it changes
the marker from \(z^{p-1}\) to \(z\).  It is a different clock.

A transient Wilson verifier can also be made trace class.  Its acyclic
computation has zero power traces and determinant one, so pruning every
verifier state leaves the accepted-loop diagonal unchanged.  Arbitrary total
deciders reproduce the same architecture.

## 5. Control interpretation

### Composite and pseudoprime controls

Exact rejection validates the Wilson implementation.  It does not establish
an emergent dynamical selector, because the terminal edge is equivalent to
Wilson's primality relation.

### Bare and matched clones

The two clone outcomes answer different questions.  The bare polynomial-UFD
clone is the exact Paper 30 object and fails under ordinary addition.  The
matched semiring clone is an isomorphic enrichment and must copy the result.
Reporting only the first control would overstate the advance.

### Arbitrary semirings and random operations

Finite-characteristic, polynomial, tropical, and random tables fail specific
source-lock axioms.  Their failure does not mean the Wilson graph singles out
the literal integers; it means the full-shift source is the characteristic-zero
semiring generated additively by its unit.

### Universal compilers

Squares, powers of two, Fibonacci numbers, seeded supports, and arbitrary
deciders produce the same transient-pruning and padded-cycle effects when
compiled terminally.  This is the decisive `PROVES_TOO_MUCH` control for the
analytic architecture.

## 6. Abstract record

Paper 30 shows that multiplicative, divisibility, incidence, roof, and Gram
data of finite full shifts are indistinguishable from a transported formal UFD
clone.  We test the minimal nonmultiplicative escape by adjoining alphabet sum,
successor, and congruence.  Alphabet sum reconstructs the nonnegative-integer
semiring and provably breaks the old bare clone, yet an isomorphic matched
semiring clone transports the entire enrichment.  A stationary Wilson residue
graph then has exactly one primitive cycle of length \(p-1\) for each prime
\(p\), giving the marked periodic product
\(\prod_p(1-z^{p-1}p^{-s})\).  This product specializes to
\(1/\zeta(s)\) at \(z=1\), but the whole recurrent adjacency is noncompact for
every nonnegative exact-clock allocation with total roof \(\log p\).  First
return recovers a trace-class Euler determinant only by changing the marker to
\(z\), while a transient realization prunes to its accepted-loop diagonal.
Exact controls through 4096 confirm bare-clone failure, matched-clone equality,
composite rejection, and universal-compiler replication.  Thus source-natural
addition escapes multiplicative indistinguishability but not matched-clone,
pruning, or entropy-clock obstructions.
