\section{Hellinger centering and the chiral no-motion theorem}
\label{sec:chiral}

The factor $p^{-1/2}$ can also be generated without inserting a square root
by hand. Pair the weights $p^{-s}$ and $p^{-(1-s)}$ in an off-diagonal block;
their product is $p^{-1}$. On the line $s=1/2+it$ the block is self-adjoint.
This Hellinger-style pairing selects the center exactly, but exact centering
does not imply spectral motion.

Let
\[
 G=\bigoplus_{p}p^{-1}I_{E_p}
\]
on a Hilbert sum of finite atom fibers $E_p$. Let $K$ be any bounded operator
on that sum and define
\[
 A_t=G^{1/2+it}K,
 \qquad
 B_t=\begin{pmatrix}0&A_t\\A_t^*&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Universal one-sided chiral no-motion]
\label{thm:no-motion}
For every real $t$,
\[
 B_t=U_tB_0U_t^*,
 \qquad
 U_t=\operatorname{diag}(G^{it},I).
\]
Consequently the spectrum, singular values, and every unitarily invariant
regularized determinant of $B_t$ are independent of $t$. No commutation
condition on $G$ and $K$ is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $A_0=G^{1/2}K$. Since $G$ is positive and injective, Borel functional
calculus makes $G^{it}$ unitary, and
\[
 A_t=G^{it}A_0.
\]
Conjugating $B_0$ by $\operatorname{diag}(G^{it},I)$ therefore gives upper-right
block $A_t$ and lower-left block $A_t^*$. This proves the displayed identity.
\end{proof}

The theorem is not special to $K_2$ or to block-preserving couplings. In the
one-sided phase ansatz, even $[G,K]\ne0$ leaves $t$ as a removable phase;
noncommutation alone can never produce height-dependent spectrum. Moreover,
a free cross-atom transition still creates temporal cycles such as
$p\to q\to p$ whose lengths involve $\log p+\log q$. These are mixed
primitive cycles rather than repetitions of one tensor atom. Thus a viable
replacement must simultaneously use a genuinely different phase architecture
that is not removable by one block unitary and supply an intrinsic
homological or signed cancellation theorem preserving A1.

\subsection{The exact regularized determinant and what it deletes}

For the frozen rank-one binary fiber, write the off-axis formal pairing as
\[
 \cB_s=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&G^sK\\G^{1-s}K&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad K=\bigoplus_pK_2.
\]
Each active atom block squares to $p^{-1}$ times the rank-one projection and
has nonzero eigenvalues $\pm p^{-1/2}$. The two off-diagonal pieces belong
simultaneously to $S_3$ when
\[
 \frac13<\RePart s<\frac23.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Paired $\det_3$ collapse]
\label{prop:det3}
In the common $S_3$ strip,
\[
 \det{}_3(I-z\cB_s)
 =\prod_p(1-z^2/p)e^{z^2/p}.
\]
The product is independent of $s$. Moreover,
\[
 \Tr\cB_s^{2r}=2\sum_pp^{-r}
\]
converges only for $r\ge2$, whereas odd traces vanish. The divergent
quadratic trace $r=1$ is precisely the term removed by $\det_3$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Pair the regularized factors for the two active eigenvalues:
\[
 (1-zp^{-1/2})e^{zp^{-1/2}+z^2/(2p)}
 (1+zp^{-1/2})e^{-zp^{-1/2}+z^2/(2p)}
 =(1-z^2/p)e^{z^2/p}.
\]
Its logarithm begins at order $p^{-2}$, so the prime product converges. The
power traces follow from the eigenvalues $\pm p^{-1/2}$.
\end{proof}

Thus the first natural determinant reaching the critical line is both
vertically sterile and amputated at the lowest nonzero trace. It receives no
credit for a Riemann zero divisor. The result sharpens the next target: one
needs a source-internal multi-phase chiral architecture not reducible to
$A_t=G^{it}A_0$, together with exact cancellation of mixed cycles inside one
trace formula. Mass noncommutation by itself does not meet this target.
