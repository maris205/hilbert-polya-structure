\section{Symbolic zeta, Parry measures, and fluctuation twists}
\label{sec:related}

The use of finite-state matrices to encode symbolic zeta functions goes back
to the determinant formulas of \citet{bowenlanford1970}; transfer-operator
continuation in hyperbolic dynamics was developed in a much broader setting
by \citet{ruelle1976}. These results make a determinant representation natural,
but they do not identify the tensor atoms used here or manufacture the
Riemann Euler product from a full-shift monoidal source.

The maximal-entropy Markov measure for a finite-state shift is intrinsic to
the transition structure \citep{parry1964}. Normalized transfer operators and
$g$-measures provide a standard language for this probabilistic sector
\citep{keane1972,walters1975}. We use only the elementary full two-shift case:
its transition matrix is $K_2=J_2/2$, and successive symbols are independent
and uniform. Calling $K_2$ a Parry Markov fiber does not turn the
matrix-weighted atom loop into a new skew-product dynamical system.

Central and local limit theorems for symbolic systems, as well as analytic
twists of transfer operators, are classical
\citep{lalley1986,parrypollicott1990,coelhoparry1990,aaronsondenker2001}. In particular,
one frequently studies a family obtained by multiplying a transfer operator
by $e^{z f}$; derivatives of its leading eigenvalue encode fluctuations. Our
finite matrix $H(z)$ is the completely explicit two-state instance. The point
of the present synthesis is not a new CLT. It is that the same tilted trace
family has two arithmetic-symbolic specializations: its untwisted trace
preserves the tensor-prime Euler ledger, and its diffusive imaginary twist
generates the Gaussian whose Mellin transform is the Archimedean Gamma
factor.

The literature audit supports the ingredients but not a broad priority claim.
Within the finite primary-source search performed for this stage, we did not
find the exact package consisting of the tensor-prime full-shift monoid, its
minimal binary Parry sector, the same-trace Euler/Gamma specialization, and
the mass-commuting chiral no-motion theorem. Absence from a finite search is
not evidence of first-ever novelty, so our novelty claim is limited to this
source-locked synthesis and its falsification boundary.

Finally, a fluctuation Gamma factor is generic unless its observable and
dimension are selected. The binary sign representation is canonical up to
orientation, but biased binary chains and scalar projections in larger
alphabets still have Gaussian limits after centering and variance
normalization. We retain those controls explicitly rather than presenting the
Gamma limit as sufficient evidence on its own.
