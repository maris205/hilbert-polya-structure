\section{The exact clock}
\label{sec:clock}

The recurrence representation isolates the only multiplier at which
leading-term cancellation matters.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:recurrence}
Set $x_0=x$, $x_1=y$ and
\begin{equation}
 x_{n+2}=x_n(t+x_{n+1})\quad(n\geq0).
 \label{eq:recurrence}
\end{equation}
Then $F^n(x,y)=(x_n,x_{n+1})$. Every $x_n$ belongs to $I$, and
$x_n\in t^2R$ for $n\geq2$. Thus $v(t+x_n)=1$ for $n\geq2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The iterate identity follows by induction from~\eqref{eq:map}.
If two consecutive terms belong to $I$, both factors on the right of
\eqref{eq:recurrence} belong to $I$, so their product belongs to $t^2R
\subseteq I$. Starting with $x,y\in I$ proves both inclusions. The last
claim follows from the coefficient of $t$ in $t+x_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pointwise clock]
\label{thm:clock}
For every $(x,y)\in I^2$, formula~\eqref{eq:preview} holds. Zero is the
unique recurrent state. The maximum depth is $2m-2$, attained exactly at
the states with $v(y)=1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $a=v(x)$ and $b=v(y)$. First suppose $b\geq2$. The initial
multiplier $t+y$ and every later multiplier in~\eqref{eq:recurrence}
have valuation one. Induction therefore gives, for $k\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
 v(x_{2k})=\min\{m,a+k\},\qquad
 v(x_{2k+1})=\min\{m,b+k\}.
 \label{eq:exactchains}
\end{equation}
If $a<m$, the last nonzero even-indexed term has index $2(m-a-1)$;
if $b<m$, the last nonzero odd-indexed term has index $2(m-b-1)+1$.
The first zero state occurs one index after the last nonzero term, giving
the maximum in~\eqref{eq:preview}. If $a=m$, the even chain is absent and
its displayed contribution is $-1$, which cannot dominate the nonnegative
odd-chain contribution. If $b=m$, the odd chain is absent and contributes
zero. In particular $a=b=m$ gives depth zero.

Now suppose $b=1$. Cancellation in $t+y$ can only accelerate the even
chain. Lemma~\ref{lem:recurrence} and~\eqref{eq:ideals} imply
\begin{equation}
 v(x_{2k})\geq\min\{m,a+k\}\quad(k\geq1),\qquad
 v(x_{2k+1})=\min\{m,1+k\}\quad(k\geq0).
 \label{eq:cancellationchains}
\end{equation}
For the second equality, the first update of the odd chain uses $t+x_2$;
all such adjacent even terms lie in $t^2R$. Hence $x_{2m-3}\ne0$ but
$x_{2m-2}=x_{2m-1}=0$, proving $\tau=2m-2$. Since $a\geq1$, this is
exactly~\eqref{eq:preview}. The argument includes $m=2$.

Every state reaches the fixed state zero, so no other state is recurrent.
When $b\geq2$, both contributions to~\eqref{eq:preview} are at most
$2m-3$; when $b=1$, the depth is $2m-2$. This proves sharpness and its
equality condition.
\end{proof}

For example, $(t,-t)$ has $x_2=0$, whereas for $k\geq1$ its iterates are
\begin{equation}
 F^{2k}(t,-t)=(0,-t^{k+1}),\qquad
 F^{2k+1}(t,-t)=(-t^{k+1},0).
 \label{eq:example}
\end{equation}
Powers are taken modulo $t^m$. The first zero state is still $2m-2$;
an exact valuation increment for both chains would have been false.

\begin{corollary}[Depth census]
\label{cor:depth}
For every integer $0\leq h\leq2m-2$, exactly $q^h$ states have depth at
most $h$. Depth zero has one state, and each depth $1\leq h\leq2m-2$ has
$(q-1)q^{h-1}$ states.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The two inequalities in~\eqref{eq:preview} give
\[
 \tau(x,y)\leq h\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 v(x)\geq m-\lceil h/2\rceil,\quad
 v(y)\geq m-\lfloor h/2\rfloor.
\]
Both thresholds lie between $1$ and $m$. Formula~\eqref{eq:ideals}
therefore gives
\[
 q^{\lceil h/2\rceil}q^{\lfloor h/2\rfloor}=q^h
\]
states. Subtract the count at $h-1$ for the exact positive-depth count.
\end{proof}
