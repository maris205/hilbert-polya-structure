# Obstruction Registry

## SD-O27 — reflection balance erases the critical frequency

Every identity-visible pure word at atom p contains equal numbers of s and
1-s channel weights. Its mass is p^{-r}, independent of height. Finite
direct sums, constant phases, and finite virtual channel differences cannot
restore a frequency that is absent from every retained pure word.

## SD-O28 — third regularization preserves sterility

The quadratic prime-harmonic term diverges. The first common ideal is S_3,
and

\[
\det{}_3(I-zC_s)=\prod_p(1-z^2/p)e^{z^2/p}.
\]

Regularization gives a convergent canonical product, but it does not restore
s-dependence. The object is not the original Euler determinant.

## SD-O29 — cross-atom motion is mixed-ledger leakage

For p different from q,

\[
p^{-s}q^{-(1-s)}+q^{-s}p^{-(1-s)}
=\frac{2}{\sqrt{pq}}\cosh((s-1/2)\log(q/p)).
\]

The nonzero frequency comes exactly from mixed exponent support (p,q).
Thirty-two random pairings reproduce this behavior, so pairing motion is
PROVES_TOO_MUCH.

## SD-O30 — inverse and finite labels restore unwanted identities

Inverse reflected labels create mixed identity words at length two. Finite
C5 labels create them at length ten, the first length compatible with both
the order-five relation and even cross-step closure. Relations do not create
a pure moving prime repetition.

## SD-O31 — upper-DAG radicals are cyclically invisible

Arbitrary upper-DAG decorations keep both reflected blocks triangular.
Their product has diagonal p^{-1}, so every cyclic trace and determinant
stays pure and vertically constant while singular moments can move. All
24 random controls exhibit this PROVES_TOO_MUCH split.

## SD-O32 — no common trace-class direct sum

T_s is trace class only for Re(s)>1, while T_(1-s) is trace class only for
Re(s)<0. The direct sum has no common S_1 domain; placing the blocks in a
reflection double and passing to S_3 is a genuine regularization, not a
trace-class completion.
