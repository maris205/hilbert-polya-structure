\section{The frozen tensor-subset edge shift}
\label{sec:frozen}

Let $P$ be a finite nonempty set of tensor atoms and put
\[
  \EP=2^P\setminus\{\varnothing\},\qquad \XP=\EP^{\mathbb Z}.
\]
The left shift on $\XP$ is full.  Independent commuting variables
$(x_p)_{p\in P}$ determine
\[
 x_S=\prod_{p\in S}x_p,\qquad
 \eps(S)=(-1)^{|S|+1},\qquad w(S)=\eps(S)x_S.
\]
For the arithmetic specialization, $x_p=p^{-s}$ and
$T(S)=\sum_{p\in S}\log p$, so that $x_S=e^{-sT(S)}$.  All algebraic
claims are proved before this substitution.

We use the two-block presentation of the same full shift: the vertices are
$\EP$ and every ordered pair $(S,T)$ is an edge.  Fix a finite group $G$ and
an edge cocycle $\alpha_P:\EP^2\to G$.  Our convention is
\[
 \widetilde\sigma_\alpha(x,g)
   =(\sigma x,g\alpha_P(x_0,x_1)).
 \tag{3.1}\label{3.1}
\]
Left fiber translations commute with \eqref{3.1}; they do not change atom
labels, weights, or roofs.  The group fiber is therefore a genuine deck
symmetry, not a post-processing of unrelated determinants.

For an irreducible unitary representation
$\rho:G\to U(V_\rho)$, $d_\rho=\dim V_\rho$, define the arrival matrix
\[
 B_{\rho,P}(S,T)=w(T)\rho(\alpha_P(S,T)),\qquad
 D_{\rho,P}=\det(I-B_{\rho,P}).
 \tag{3.2}\label{3.2}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Same-object Artin blocks]
\label{prop:artin}
For the right regular representation,
\[
 D_{\regrep,P}=\prod_{\rho\in\Irr}D_{\rho,P}^{d_\rho}.
 \tag{3.3}\label{3.3}
\]
The trivial block is independent of $\alpha_P$ and satisfies
\[
 D_{\one,P}=\prod_{p\in P}(1-x_p).
 \tag{3.4}\label{3.4}
\]
\end{proposition}

The proof in \cref{app:artin-proof} is only finite-dimensional Fourier
decomposition plus inclusion--exclusion.  Equation \eqref{3.4} is the exact
scalar Euler factor at finite cutoff.  It does not say that the remaining
primitive language has selected arithmetic atoms.

\subsection{Gauge and based holonomy}

A vertex map $b_P:\EP\to G$ acts by
\[
 \alpha_P^b(S,T)=b_P(S)^{-1}\alpha_P(S,T)b_P(T).
 \tag{3.5}\label{3.5}
\]
For a closed directed word
$\gamma=(S_0,\ldots,S_{n-1},S_0)$, define
\[
 \hol(\gamma)=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\alpha(S_j,S_{j+1}).
 \tag{3.6}\label{3.6}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Gauge test]
\label{prop:gauge}
Every $D_{\rho,P}$ is invariant under \eqref{3.5}, and
$\hol(\gamma)$ changes by conjugation with $b_P(S_0)$.  On the finite
connected presentation, two cocycles are gauge equivalent exactly when a
spanning-tree recursion satisfies all non-tree edges, equivalently when all
fundamental based holonomies agree through one simultaneous root
conjugator.
\end{proposition}

Character determinants aggregate closed words.  \Cref{prop:gauge} gives a
test for gauge equivalence, but its converse is not replaced by a claim that
equal determinants classify gauges.
