\section{Functorial incidence grammar and the counting gauge class}
\label{sec:incidence}

The target group carries no action of the atom relabeling group.  We require
one-step locality, naturality under bijections of atom sets, and compatibility
with restriction to a smaller inventory.  For an ordered pair $(S,T)$ write
\[
 u=|S\setminus T|,\qquad v=|S\cap T|,\qquad w=|T\setminus S|.
 \tag{4.1}\label{4.1}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Incidence classification]
\label{thm:incidence}
Every source-locked local cocycle has the form
\[
 \alpha_P(S,T)=g_{u,v,w}.
 \tag{4.2}\label{4.2}
\]
On an inventory of size $n$, the number of stable incidence types is
\[
 N(n)=\binom{n+3}{3}-(2n+1),
 \qquad N(1),N(2),N(3),N(4)=1,5,13,26.
 \tag{4.3}\label{4.3}
\]
Conversely, a stable table on these triples defines such a natural local
rule.
\end{theorem}

The three counts in \eqref{4.1} preserve more information than the arrival
cardinality $|T|=v+w$.  Thus naturality does not collapse transition
cocycles to one-letter clocks.

To identify the clocks that are present, take $a\in G$ and define
\[
 \alpha_a(S,T)=a^{|T|}.
 \tag{4.4}\label{4.4}
\]
A natural vertex gauge is constant on cardinality orbits, so write
$b_P(S)=q_{|S|}$.

\begin{theorem}[Natural count plus coboundary]
\label{thm:count-gauge}
The natural gauge orbit of \eqref{4.4} is exactly
\[
 g_{u,v,w}=q_{u+v}^{-1}a^{v+w}q_{v+w}.
 \tag{4.5}\label{4.5}
\]
Every member of this orbit has
\[
 D_{\rho,P}=\prod_{p\in P}\det(I-x_p\rho(a)).
 \tag{4.6}\label{4.6}
\]
For two atoms, denote the five values by $(a_0,c,h,u_0,v_0)$: singleton
loop, pair loop, disjoint-singleton edge, refinement, and coarsening.  After
$q_1=e$, membership in \eqref{4.5} is equivalent to
\[
 h=a_0,\qquad v_0=u_0^{-1}a_0^3,\qquad
 c=u_0^{-1}a_0^2u_0.
 \tag{4.7}\label{4.7}
\]
\end{theorem}

Equation \eqref{4.6} is the clean benchmark: every irreducible block factors
atom by atom.  The central question is whether a cocycle outside
\eqref{4.5} can remain comparably clean while carrying noncommuting
holonomy.  The answer for our frozen candidate is no.
