\section{Self-commutator ideals}
\label{sec:commutators}

The operator ideal wall arises from the first power of a block singular
value.  A self-commutator sees its square and hence produces a different
threshold.

\begin{lemma}[Rank-one self-commutator]
\label{lem:rankone-commutator}
Let $T=\rho\,u\otimes v$, where $u,v$ are unit vectors, and let $a$ be the
modulus of the unique nonzero eigenvalue.  The two possibly zero singular
values of $T^*T-TT^*$ are equal to
\begin{equation}
  c=\rho^2\sqrt{1-a^2/\rho^2}.
  \label{eq:commutator-c}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$T^*T=\rho^2v\otimes v$ and $TT^*=\rho^2u\otimes u$.  Their difference
is self-adjoint and traceless on $\operatorname{span}\{u,v\}$.  Its
determinant there is
$-\rho^4(1-|\langle u,v\rangle|^2)$.  Since
$a=\rho|\langle u,v\rangle|$, the two eigenvalues are $\pm c$, proving
the claim.
\end{proof}

For the arithmetic blocks, \cref{eq:rho-S,eq:rho-M} gives the exact angle
ratios
\begin{align}
  \frac{|\lambda_m|^2}{\rho_S(m)^2}
    &=\prod_{p\in J_h(m)}(1-p^{-\sigma}),
      \label{eq:S-angle}\\
  \frac{|\lambda_m|^2}{\rho_M(m)^2}
    &=\frac1{\zeta(h\sigma)}.
      \label{eq:M-angle}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Exact commutator wall]
\label{thm:commutator-wall}
For $0<q<\infty$,
\begin{align*}
  [S_{h,s}^*,S_{h,s}]\in\Sq
    &\iff\sigma q>1,\\
  [M_{h,s}^*,M_{h,s}]\in\Sq
    &\iff\sigma>1/h\ \text{and}\ \sigma q>1.
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $c_m$ be the quantity in \cref{eq:commutator-c} for block $m$.
For $S$, $c_m\le\rho_S(m)^2$, so
\[
  \sum_m c_m^q\le\sum_m\rho_S(m)^{2q}<\infty
\]
whenever $\sigma q>1$, by \cref{prop:schatten} with exponent $2q$.

For necessity when $h\ge3$, fix a prime $p_0$ and let
$m_r=p_0^{h-1}r$ as $r\ne p_0$ varies over primes.  The prime $r$ has
exponent one and is not saturated, so $J_h(m_r)=\{p_0\}$.  The angle
factor in \cref{eq:S-angle} is a fixed positive constant and
$c_{m_r}\asymp r^{-\sigma}$.  The prime sum
$\sum_r c_{m_r}^q$ diverges when $\sigma q\le1$.

That witness is ill typed for $h=2$, because exponent one is already
saturated.  Instead fix $p_0$ and take $m_r=p_0r$.  Now
$J_2(m_r)=\{p_0,r\}$, while
\[
  1-(1-p_0^{-\sigma})(1-r^{-\sigma})\ge p_0^{-\sigma}.
\]
Again $c_{m_r}\asymp r^{-\sigma}$, proving divergence at and below the
same wall.

For $M$, \cref{eq:M-angle} makes the angular factor a fixed positive
constant throughout the bounded domain.  Thus $c_m\asymp m^{-\sigma}$,
and the $h$-free sum $\sum_mc_m^q$ converges exactly for
$\sigma q>1$.  The boundedness condition $\sigma>1/h$ remains mandatory.
The factor two from the two singular values per nonzero block does not
affect membership.
\end{proof}

The $h=2$ case also admits a closed Hilbert--Schmidt identity.  For a
squarefree $m$, define
\[
  \Lambda_m=\prod_{p\mid m}(p^\sigma-1)^{-1},
  \qquad
  \Delta_m=\prod_{p\mid m}(1-p^{-\sigma}).
\]
Then $\rho_S(m)^2=\Lambda_m$ and the two commutator singular values are
$\Lambda_m\sqrt{1-\Delta_m}$.

\begin{proposition}[$h=2$ Euler control]
\label{prop:h2-euler-control}
If $\sigma>1/2$, then
\begin{equation}
  \|[S_{2,s}^*,S_{2,s}]\|_2^2
  =2\left\{
    \prod_p\left[1+(p^\sigma-1)^{-2}\right]
    -\prod_p\left[1+\frac{p^{-2\sigma}}{1-p^{-\sigma}}\right]
    \right\}.
  \label{eq:h2-HS}
\end{equation}
Both products converge separately.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Summing the two squared singular values gives
$2\sum_m\Lambda_m^2(1-\Delta_m)$.  Squarefree multiplicativity yields
the first Euler product from $\sum_m\Lambda_m^2$.  The local nontrivial
factor in $\Lambda_m^2\Delta_m$ is
\[
  (p^\sigma-1)^{-2}(1-p^{-\sigma})
  =\frac{p^{-2\sigma}}{1-p^{-\sigma}},
\]
which gives the second product.  Each nonconstant local factor is
$O(p^{-2\sigma})$, so both products converge for $\sigma>1/2$.
At equality, \cref{thm:commutator-wall} already gives divergence; no
subtraction of divergent Euler products is defined.
\end{proof}

No ideal endpoint in this section is inferred from a finite block.  The
strict failure comes from an explicit infinite positive subfamily.
