# Research question brief

## Narrow question

Can a continuous topological factor of the exact Session-4 squarefree
admissible shift create a nontrivial periodic orbit and thereby repair the
source's collapsed primitive ledger?

The quantifiers are fixed:

```text
for every compact metrizable Z-system (Y,S)
for every continuous surjective Z-equivariant map pi:X_sf -> Y
does Per(Y,S) equal {pi(0^Z)}?
```

## Answer

Yes. The equality is proved exactly. No finite-to-one, sliding-block-radius,
expansivity, soficity, or finite-alphabet assumption is needed on the target.

The proof uses only the source admissibility condition, an unlimited supply
of pairwise distinct rational primes, the Chinese remainder theorem,
compactness, uniform continuity, and the elementary separation of a finite
periodic orbit.

## Why this question is useful

Paper 1 proved that \(X_{\rm sf}\) itself has only \(0^{\mathbb Z}\) as a
periodic point. That theorem alone does not normally pass to factors: an
aperiodic system can have periodic factors. The present question isolates the
extra source property that makes passage possible here, namely proximality.

The closure is therefore stronger than the historical source-only census but
strictly narrower than any general aperiodic-factor theorem.

## Frozen theorem output

For every lawful factor \((Y,S,\pi)\):

\[
\operatorname{Per}(Y,S)=\{\pi(0^{\mathbb Z})\},
\quad
\#\operatorname{Fix}(S^m)=1,
\quad
\zeta_{\rm AM,Y}(z)=(1-z)^{-1}.
\]

The sole primitive orbit has least period one. Its \(r\)-fold traversal is a
repetition with marker \(z^r\), not a new primitive atom. It cannot be
retyped as the infinitely many rational primes.
