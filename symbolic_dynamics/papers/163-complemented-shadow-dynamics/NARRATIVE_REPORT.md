## Mathematical progression

The literal operation takes a lower shadow and complements each resulting
set.  Two steps return to the original rank: choosing the same deleted label
returns the source set, while choosing an outside label performs one Johnson
exchange.  Even atomic iterates are therefore Johnson balls.  Reversing the
last step gives the odd intersection threshold.

Union preservation lifts the atomic formulas to arbitrary set families.  An
occupied rank slice becomes full at an even time controlled by its Johnson
covering radius or at an odd time controlled by a dual intersection defect.
The earlier common parity across all occupied slices is the exact tail.  Once
every slice is full, only rank support remains, and that support alternates
under `phi(k)=n-k+1`.

The strengthened result examines equality in the global height bound.  For
even `n`, only the lower central rank can attain the necessary Johnson
diameter, and a family attains that diameter only as a singleton because each
vertex has a unique antipode.  For odd `n`, equality in the odd intersection
defect forces every central source atom to equal one fixed target.  Thus, for
`n>=3`, maximum depth is equivalent to a singleton central slice.

That equivalence turns an extremal witness into a full census.  The central
atom has `M_n` choices and every off-central atom is free, giving the total
`M_n 2^(2^n-M_n)`.  Fixing the labelled endpoint support makes each occupied
noncentral slice an arbitrary nonempty family and leaves the silent atom
optional.  Requiring support invariance under `phi` then factors the
period-one count over rank orbits; subtraction gives period two.

## Boundary that changes the theorem

At `n=2`, the height is one, so every nonrecurrent state is deepest.  There
are twelve such states, split six with eventual period one and six with period
two.  The singleton central-slice predicate selects only eight because the
silent atom can itself create a one-step tail.  The manuscript places this
exception beside the main shell theorem and does not extend the general
formula across it.
