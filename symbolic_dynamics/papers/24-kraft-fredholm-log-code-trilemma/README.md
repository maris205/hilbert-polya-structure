# Paper 24 — The Kraft--Fredholm Trilemma for Logarithmic Prime Codes

Candidate **SD-C26** tests the strongest constructive obligation left by
Paper23: replace unary/factorial filtering by a new stationary recurrent
grammar with a finite (O(\log n)) code, intrinsic total roof (\log n),
prime/composite separation in the primitive algebra, and a Fredholm whole
operator.

## Main result

Inside the frozen class of positive scalar one-step graph operators with a
finite orbit-separating local code, the requirements are incompatible.
Finite coding forces (\ell(p)\ge c\log p) on an infinite subsequence.  A
literal positive prime-only primitive ledger forces distinct prime cycles to
be vertex-disjoint.  Total roof (\log p) then leaves one edge of uniformly
bounded roof on each of those cycles, so the whole adjacency is noncompact.

Sharing a prefix trie or renewal hub does not fix the problem: concatenating
different first returns creates mixed connected primitive necklaces, and the
finite determinant is (1-F), not a product of independent Euler factors.
The only frozen Fredholm escape is the countable one-symbol-per-atom
diagonal; it works equally for composites and pseudorandom inventories and
is therefore the Paper04 selector architecture.

## Exact audit

The CPU-only exact suite reports 35/35 tests.  It audits target-blind
binary/Elias codes, 672 positive roof allocations, shared-trie determinants,
mixed necklaces, diagonal matched inventories, factorization-renewal norm
collisions, and finite-prefix S-adic stationarizations.  Two clean
generations are byte-identical.  These finite artifacts verify formulas and
implementation integrity; the infinite noncompactness result is proved
analytically.
