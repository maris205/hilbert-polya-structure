\title[Minimum-pivot M\"obius feedback]{Minimum-Pivot M\"obius Feedback on Projective-Line Subsets}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Order the finite points of $\PP(\F_p)$ by their standard representatives.
For a $k$-subset $S$, translate its least finite point to zero and then
apply projective inversion.  We determine the resulting state-dependent
finite dynamics for every prime $p$ and $2\leq k\leq p$.  Its first two
images are respectively the subsets containing $\infty$ and those containing
$\{0,\infty\}$; the latter form an inversion core.  Consequently the sharp
maximum tail is two, $\M^4=\M^2$, and all cycles have length at most two.
We give the exact three depth layers and a coefficient formula for the fixed
states.  Independently, every target fibre is determined: for a target
containing $\infty$, the possible feedback pivots form an initial interval,
so its pivot enumerator is $1+z+\cdots+z^{h-1}$ for an explicit
target-dependent $h$; every other target has empty fibre.  A standalone
exhaustive control checks $131{,}018{,}555$ assertions in all $69$ parameter
boxes through $p=19$.  The clock is shallow and the coordinate order is
artificial; this provisional-amber Round-0 note remains on external hold.
\end{abstract}

\section{The map and its claim ceiling}

Fix a prime $p$ and identify
\[
 \PP(\F_p)=\F_p\cup\{\infty\},
 \qquad 0<1<\cdots<p-1<\infty,
\]
where inequalities refer to the displayed integer representatives, not to
an order on the field.  For $2\leq k\leq p$, let
$\X_{p,k}=\binom{\PP(\F_p)}{k}$.  Every state $S\in\X_{p,k}$ contains a
finite point.  Put
\[
 a(S)=\min(S\cap\F_p),
 \qquad
 \gamma_a(x)=\frac{1}{x-a},
\]
with the projective conventions
$\gamma_a(a)=\infty$ and $\gamma_a(\infty)=0$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:literal}
 \M(S)=\gamma_{a(S)}(S).
\end{equation}
The coordinate order and prime-field representatives are part of the
literal rule.  In particular, \eqref{eq:literal} is not claimed to be
projectively natural.

Dynamics of one fixed fractional-linear transformation over a finite field
are established background \citep{ElAbdalaouiShparlinski2019}.  So are
$\mathrm{PGL}(2)$-orbits of projective configurations and the
$\mathrm{PGL}(2,q)$ action on projective-line subsets
\citep{AluffiFaber1993,Tricot2025}.  More generally, minimal and canonical
images of ordered subsets under permutation-group actions, together with
canonizing elements, have a direct algorithmic theory
\citep{JeffersonEtAl2019}.  Those facts, projective inversion, canonical-
image language, and ordinary subset enumeration receive no contribution
credit here.  The map \eqref{eq:literal} is not a canonical-image function:
it need not be constant on a group orbit, it chooses one projectivity from
the current pivot rather than minimizing over the group, and its iterates and
target fibres are the objects being counted.

The internal proof-engine firewall is stricter still.  P96 already covers
finite subsets under a transformation induced from a fixed base map, and
P168 covers spans of inverses of finite-field subspaces.  Our rule chooses
its projectivity from the current subset and takes no span, but fixed-map
hyperspace language and inverse geometry remain zero credit.  The killed AQN
control is closer architecturally: it selects a normalization from the state
and then exposes a classical group action.  Its quotient is adjacent to
simultaneous group multiplication and cyclic rotation on words
\citep{GrinbergMao2024}.  We therefore assign no value to adaptive
normalization or the eventual involution by itself.  The only residual under
evaluation is the literal two-stage containment tower together with the
nonuniform target-local inverse below.  A bounded search found no literal
owner for that conjunction, but a search non-hit is not evidence of novelty,
priority, ownership, or freedom to operate.

Write
\[
 \Z_{p,k}=\{S\in\X_{p,k}:\infty\in S\},\qquad
 \Y_{p,k}=\{S\in\X_{p,k}:\{0,\infty\}\subseteq S\},
\]
and put $R_{p,k}=|\Y_{p,k}|=\binom{p-1}{k-2}$.  For a target
$T\in\X_{p,k}$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:height}
 \beta(T)=\max\bigl(\{\overline{y^{-1}}:
      y\in T\cap\F_p^\times\}\cup\{0\}\bigr),
 \qquad h(T)=p-\beta(T),
\end{equation}
where the bar denotes the representative in $\{1,\ldots,p-1\}$.

\begin{theorem}[complete graph and target inverse]\label{thm:main}
For every prime $p$ and $2\leq k\leq p$, the map \eqref{eq:literal}
has the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item Its first two images are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:images}
 \im\M=\Z_{p,k},\qquad \im\M^2=\Y_{p,k}.
\end{equation}
The recurrent set is $\Y_{p,k}$.  On it, $\M$ fixes $0,\infty$ setwise and
sends $x\in\F_p^\times$ to $x^{-1}$; hence $\M^4=\M^2$ on all of
$\X_{p,k}$.

\item A state has tail zero, one, or two according as it lies in
$\Y_{p,k}$, $\Z_{p,k}\setminus\Y_{p,k}$, or
$\X_{p,k}\setminus\Z_{p,k}$.  Thus the depth enumerator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:depth}
 D_{p,k}(u)=\binom{p-1}{k-2}
 +\binom{p-1}{k-1}u+\binom pk u^2,
\end{equation}
and the maximum tail is sharply two.

\item Suppose $p$ is odd and set $r=k-2$.  The number of fixed states is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed}
 F_{p,k}=[v^r](1+v)^2(1+v^2)^{(p-3)/2}.
\end{equation}
The remaining recurrent states form
$(R_{p,k}-F_{p,k})/2$ two-cycles.  For $p=2,k=2$, the recurrent core is one
fixed state; set $F_{2,2}=1$.  In either case, the number of weak components is
$(R_{p,k}+F_{p,k})/2$, and for every $m\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-iterates}
 |\Fix(\M^m)|=
 \begin{cases}F_{p,k},&m\text{ odd},\\
 R_{p,k},&m\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\item Every one-step target fibre, including empty fibres, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre}
 |\M^{-1}(T)|=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&\infty\notin T,\\
 h(T),&\infty\in T,
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
and, when $\infty\in T$, its pivot-marked form is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marked}
 \sum_{S:\,\M(S)=T}z^{a(S)}
   =1+z+\cdots+z^{h(T)-1}.
\end{equation}

\item For $1\leq q\leq p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre-distribution}
 \#\{T\in\X_{p,k}:|\M^{-1}(T)|=q\}
   =\binom{p-q}{k-2}.
\end{equation}
There are $\binom pk$ zero-fibre targets, and the maximum fibre is
$p-k+2$, attained by a unique target.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Parts (i)--(iii) give the whole temporal graph; part (iv) gives every
incoming branch and its feedback label.  The marked inverse is not inferred
from the depth census.

\section{The two-stage image tower}

\begin{lemma}[forced points]\label{lem:tower}
The pivot always maps to $\infty$, and $\infty$ is the unique point that
maps to $0$.  Consequently
\[
 S\notin\Z_{p,k}\Longrightarrow
 \M(S)\in\Z_{p,k}\setminus\Y_{p,k},\qquad
 S\in\Z_{p,k}\Longrightarrow\M(S)\in\Y_{p,k}.
\]
Moreover, $\M$ restricts to ordinary inversion on $\Y_{p,k}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first two assertions follow directly from the projective conventions for
$\gamma_{a(S)}$.  If $S$ avoids $\infty$, its image avoids $0$; if $S$
contains $\infty$, its image contains both $0$ and the image $\infty$ of the
pivot.  Finally, a state in $\Y_{p,k}$ has pivot zero, so its update is
$x\mapsto x^{-1}$ with $0$ and $\infty$ exchanged.  As a set it still
contains both distinguished points, and a second update returns it.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(i)--(iii)]
Lemma~\ref{lem:tower} gives $\im\M\subseteq\Z_{p,k}$.  Conversely, if
$T\in\Z_{p,k}$, then $S=\gamma_0(T)$ contains zero, has pivot zero, and
satisfies $\M(S)=T$.  Thus $\im\M=\Z_{p,k}$.  The same lemma sends this
first image into $\Y_{p,k}$, while every $T\in\Y_{p,k}$ satisfies
$\M^2(T)=T$.  This proves \eqref{eq:images}.

The three implications in Lemma~\ref{lem:tower} show that states in
$\Y_{p,k}$, $\Z_{p,k}\setminus\Y_{p,k}$, and
$\X_{p,k}\setminus\Z_{p,k}$ have tails zero, one, and two, respectively.
No state outside $\Y_{p,k}$ can therefore recur.  Choosing the remaining
points in each stratum gives the three coefficients in \eqref{eq:depth}.
The last coefficient is nonzero because $k\leq p$, so the height two is
attained.  Since $\M^2(S)\in\Y_{p,k}$ for every $S$ and $\M^2$ is the
identity there, $\M^4=\M^2$ globally.

A recurrent state is $\{0,\infty\}\cup A$ for an $r$-subset
$A\subseteq\F_p^\times$.  At odd $p$, inversion has singleton orbits
$\{1\}$ and $\{-1\}$ and $(p-3)/2$ two-element orbits.  An invariant
$r$-subset is a union of these orbits, which gives the coefficient
\eqref{eq:fixed}.  All other recurrent points are exchanged in pairs.  At
$p=2,k=2$, no nonzero point is selected and the sole core state is fixed.

Every transient state reaches one recurrent cycle, so the weak components
are counted by the recurrent cycles:
$F_{p,k}+(R_{p,k}-F_{p,k})/2=(R_{p,k}+F_{p,k})/2$.  A point fixed by a
positive iterate is recurrent.  Inversion fixes exactly $F_{p,k}$ recurrent
states at odd times and all $R_{p,k}$ recurrent states at even times, proving
\eqref{eq:fixed-iterates}.
\end{proof}

\section{Every-target fibres and marked pivots}

The inverse calculation remembers the state-selected normalization that the
forward image counts forget.

\begin{lemma}[modular no-wrap criterion]\label{lem:nowrap}
Fix $a\in\F_p$.  The inverse projectivity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse-projectivity}
 \gamma_a^{-1}(\infty)=a,\qquad
 \gamma_a^{-1}(0)=\infty,\qquad
 \gamma_a^{-1}(y)=a+y^{-1}\quad(y\in\F_p^\times).
\end{equation}
For integer representatives $0\leq a<p$ and $1\leq b<p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nowrap}
 \overline{a+b}\geq a\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a<p-b.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:inverse-projectivity} follows by solving
$y=1/(x-a)$, including the two projective points.  If $a+b<p$, reduction
does nothing and the left side of \eqref{eq:nowrap} holds.  If $a+b\geq p$,
the representative is $a+b-p<a$ because $b<p$.  These are the only cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(iv)]
Every image contains $\infty$, proving the zero line of \eqref{eq:fibre}.
Now take $T\in\Z_{p,k}$.  A parent with proposed pivot $a$ is forced to be
$S_a=\gamma_a^{-1}(T)$.  Besides the forced point $a$, its finite points are
\[
 \overline{a+y^{-1}},\qquad y\in T\cap\F_p^\times.
\]
Thus $a$ is actually the least finite point of $S_a$ exactly when every one
of these representatives is at least $a$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:nowrap}, this
holds precisely when
\[
 0\leq a<p-\max\bigl(\{\overline{y^{-1}}:
                   y\in T\cap\F_p^\times\}\cup\{0\}\bigr)=h(T).
\]
Each valid $a$ yields a parent, and different values yield different parents
because they are their parents' least finite points.  Hence the valid pivots
are exactly $0,\ldots,h(T)-1$, proving both \eqref{eq:fibre} and
\eqref{eq:marked}.
\end{proof}

The formula also resolves the rooted trees attached to the inversion core.
For $T\in\Y_{p,k}$, its pivot-zero parent is the recurrent point
$\gamma_0(T)$, and its other $h(T)-1$ parents have depth one.  A target in
$\Z_{p,k}\setminus\Y_{p,k}$ has $h(T)$ parents, all at depth two.  Targets
outside $\Z_{p,k}$ have none.  Thus no aggregate branch remains unspecified.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(v)]
Inversion permutes $\F_p^\times$, so count a target through the set
$B(T)=\{\overline{y^{-1}}:y\in T\cap\F_p^\times\}$.  Put $j=p-q$.
For $j\geq1$, a target with $\beta(T)=j$ and without zero has $|B(T)|=k-1$
and is counted by $\binom{j-1}{k-2}$.  A target containing zero has
$|B(T)|=k-2$ and is counted by $\binom{j-1}{k-3}$.  Their sum is
$\binom{j}{k-2}$.  At $j=0$, only $T=\{0,\infty\}$ at $k=2$ occurs, again
matching $\binom{0}{k-2}$.  Substitution of $j=p-q$ proves
\eqref{eq:fibre-distribution}.

Targets avoiding $\infty$ number $\binom pk$.  A positive fibre of size
$q$ exists exactly when $p-q\geq k-2$, so the maximum is $p-k+2$; equality
in the binomial count shows that its target is unique.  As a consistency
check, the distribution has total source mass
\[
 \sum_{q=1}^{p}q\binom{p-q}{k-2}=\binom{p+1}{k},
\]
which is the carrier size.
\end{proof}

\[
