# Narrative report — Paper 32 / SD-C34

## One-sentence contribution

A nonterminal projective-residue grammar repairs same-object Fredholm
ownership but fails prime separation before weighting because universal
modular relations and overlapping cusp diamonds force primitive composite
recurrence, while the available static field separator is a forbidden
terminal block gate.

## Inherited problem

Paper 31 showed that terminal semiring verifiers do not provide a satisfactory
Route-A mechanism.  They may compute primality exactly, but their recurrent
cycles suffer clock dilution, their transient computation prunes from power
traces, and first return changes graph time.  Paper 32 was therefore forbidden
from changing to another terminal decider.  The new grammar had to be
nonterminal, recurrent on shared states, arithmetically selective before
weights, and analytically owned on the same uninduced object.

## What the candidate gets right

The projective residue graph is genuinely different from a terminal verifier.
Every projective state has both \(S\) and \(R\) transitions; no computation
halts, accepts, or rejects.  The two recurrent families share every state.
Bidirectional cusp correspondences make different moduli share recurrent
states as well.  Thus the graph is not a disguised direct sum of
prime-labelled terminal cycles.

The analytic repair is also genuine.  Finite projective blocks are weighted
by \(n^{-s}\), and the cusp correspondences by \((kn)^{-s}\).  The projective
state-count bound reduces the within-block trace norm to the divisor-sum
Dirichlet series, while the cusp maps form an absolutely summable rank-one
series.  The original graph-step operator is trace class on
\(\operatorname{Re}s>2\), with its original edge marker intact.  Paper 31's
ownership failure has therefore been removed rather than hidden by induction.

## Where the route fails

The failure occurs earlier than the analytic layer.  Projectively,
\(S^2=R^3=1\) for every modulus.  Each prime, prime power, and mixed composite
block has both recurrent families before roofs are assigned.  The
cross-modulus graph is no better: every base modulus produces the simple
cycle \(n,2n,6n,3n,n\), and overlapping bases share cusp states.  The primitive
ledger is flooded by composite and mixed-modulus cycles.

This failure is structural rather than numerical.  Random actions satisfying
only the same order-two/order-three relations reproduce recurrence through
every state.  The mechanism therefore proves too much at the modular
presentation level.  No larger cutoff can turn that universal relation into a
prime discriminator.

## The tempting static repair

Projective geometry does contain an exact prime test:
\[
 |P^1(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)|=n+1
\]
holds exactly for prime \(n\).  This fact shows that the source is
arithmetically expressive.  It does not show that recurrence discovered the
primes.  Multiplying the \(n\)-block by the indicator of that equality decides
the completed field test before the block is admitted.  That projector is a
terminal selector in static form and repeats the branch error closed in Paper
31.

## Exact controls

The finite audit covers every \(2\le n\le192\), with independent labels only
after the candidate census.  All 148 composites have recurrent support, and
all 31 visible canonical diamonds have a composite top modulus.  Forty-eight
seeded random \(C_2*C_3\) actions reproduce the universal overlap.  Each of
191 matched residue-semiring presentations transports its complete addition
and multiplication tables, projective states, and \(S/R\) graph exactly.  The
candidate core contains no primality, factorization, target-zero, network, or
subprocess oracle.  The canonical independent evaluator imports no candidate
module and passes all 4,819,026 checks.  Two fresh executions reproduce all 16
primary artifacts byte for byte, all 13 deterministic assertions pass, and
the final-tree integrity audit reports `PASS`.

The inherited bare polynomial-UFD control still fails ordinary alphabet
addition at \(\Phi(2)=\Phi(1+1)\).  That separation is not upgraded to a
universal clone theorem: a matched semiring clone is required to copy the
construction.

## Route interpretation

- **A0:** structural arithmetic is present.  The source reconstructs finite
  residue rings, projective states, and the exact static field defect.
- **A1:** failed.  Universal within-block relations and cusp diamonds give
  composite primitive support before weighting.
- **A2:** passed analytically.  The same uninduced \(B_s\) is trace class and
  owns \(\det(I-zB_s)\) for \(\operatorname{Re}s>2\).
- **A3:** failed.  No critical-line continuation, completion, functional
  equation, explicit-formula bridge, or intrinsic Weil compression exists.
- **A4:** failed.  No fixed self-adjoint carrier or zero correspondence is
  produced.

Thus

    (A0_STRUCTURAL_ARITHMETIC_RELATION,
     A1_FAIL,
     A2_ANALYTIC_DETERMINANT,
     A3_FAIL,
     A4_FAIL)

and the overall decision is `ROUTE_A_REJECTED`.  Route B remains locked.

## Branch decision

The positive projective-residue prime candidate stops.  The negative paper
goes forward because it isolates a sharp trilemma: static field separation,
universal shared-state recurrence, and honest same-object determinant
ownership coexist, but the static separator cannot repair the primitive
ledger without becoming terminal.

The branch action is
`CLOSE_EUCLIDEAN_PROJECTIVE_RESIDUE_RECURRENCE_BRANCH`.  Replacing \(S,R\) by
another residue map without changing the cycle-level architecture does not
reopen it.

## Paper 33 opening

The only remaining in-family move is a source-natural quotient or twist on
the same recurrent object.  It must annihilate \(S^2\), \(R^3\), and cusp
diamonds at chain level before labels or roofs, then exhibit and prove the
complete surviving primitive ledger.  A quotient equivalent to the static
field projector, one that leaves universal cycles, or one whose cancellation
works equally on random actions closes the entire semiring-residue family.
