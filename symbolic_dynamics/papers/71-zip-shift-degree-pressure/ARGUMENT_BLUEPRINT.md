# Argument blueprint

## A. Natural-extension reduction

The inverse history records exactly the forgotten future symbol at each negative time. Therefore the natural extension is `S^Z`; the lifted potential is the one-coordinate weight `log k_(tau(s))`. Finite-dimensional inverse-limit distributions give the affine invariant-measure lift, and a generating-partition calculation preserves metric entropy.

## B. Pressure

For an invariant measure with one-symbol marginal `p`, entropy rate is at most `H(p)`. Gibbs' inequality for weights `k_(tau(s))^t` gives the log-sum pressure and forces the Bernoulli optimizer. Differentiating the log-sum yields the mean and variance of `log k` under `r_t`.

## C. Profile rigidity

A fixed point labelled by `s` has local degree `k_(tau(s))`; hence the number of fixed points of degree `k` is `k m_k`. This recovers the profile under conjugacy. Equal profiles give a coordinatewise conjugacy. Equality of pressure curves gives equality of finite exponential sums; remove the largest base and recurse.

## D. Multifractal spectrum

The orbit degree sum differs by a bounded boundary term from the digit-weight sum on the positive `S` tail. At scale `2^(-M)`, an `n`-Bowen ball fixes exactly `M` past symbols and `n+M` future symbols. Method-of-types cylinders give the variable-length Carathéodory upper bound; a Bernoulli local-entropy estimate and the entropy distribution principle give the lower bound `max H(p)`. Grouping by fibres adds the exact conditional term `alpha`, and Gibbs duality gives the Legendre formula.
