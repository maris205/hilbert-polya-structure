## Review Body

The paper supplies an unusually coherent bridge from a finite-to-one local homeomorphism to thermodynamic formalism, information loss, inverse recovery, and multifractal level sets. The fibre profile remains visible in every translation, and the two four-symbol controls show why ordinary entropy alone loses that information.

### S1: Local degree is turned into a meaningful cross-field observable

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §1 "turns that loss of information into an additive observable."

The degree potential connects a topological preimage count to equilibrium averages and folding entropy. This makes the information-loss interpretation quantitative rather than rhetorical.

### S2: One finite-dimensional optimization links pressure and multifractals

**Evidence Anchor**: equation: §5, Eqs. (5.3)–(5.5)

Presenting the Bowen spectrum both as a constrained Shannon maximum and as a Legendre transform gives information-theory and thermodynamic-formalism readers direct entry points to the same result.

### S3: The controls distinguish identical natural extensions

**Evidence Anchor**: table: §6, two four-symbol controls table

The profiles (1,3) and (2,2) share a full four-shift natural extension and topological entropy, yet have different pressure curvature and exponent spectra. This is an effective counterexample to the assumption that the invertible extension retains the relevant noninvertible information.

### W1: The weighted-transition extension could be stated more concretely

**Severity**: Minor

**Evidence Anchor**: text: §7 "pressure is controlled by a weighted transition operator rather than the scalar sum"

**Confidence**: 4 — cross-disciplinary thermodynamic and inverse-problem assessment

The conclusion identifies mixing SFT futures as the next system family, but the transfer target remains abstract. Add a short roadmap defining the candidate weighted adjacency operator, stating which current scalar identities become spectral-radius statements, and separating the likely survivals of equilibrium and multifractal formulas from the open profile-recovery question. This would make the future program testable without claiming that the present rigidity theorem already extends.
