\title[Odd-outdegree transpose dynamics]{Odd-Outdegree Transpose Dynamics on Looped Digraphs:\\
Parity Collapse, Fibres, and Components}
\author{Anonymous}

\begin{abstract}
On a labelled looped digraph, reverse every arc and then toggle every ordered
pair, loops included, inside the set of vertices of odd current outdegree.
Equivalently, on $M_n(\mathbb F_2)$ we iterate
$\Phi(A)=A^{\mathsf T}+(A\mathbf1)(A\mathbf1)^{\mathsf T}$.
We prove that one step maps onto the even-total-parity hyperplane, which is
the complete recurrent set, and that all recurrent periods divide four.
Every codomain fibre has size $0$, $1$, or $2^{n-1}+1$, according to an
explicit column-parity test.  We then give exact formulas for fixed points,
two- and four-cycles, depth-one states, and the finite dynamical zeta
function.  A second factorisation views the state-dependent left factor as
the identity or an involutory transvection, versus a singular projection,
and independently recovers the second and fourth iterates.  Static graph
complementation, binary-margin counting, and finite-map zeta bookkeeping are
treated as background.  The owner search is bounded; no novelty, priority,
or external-release claim is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{The update and its subtraction boundary}

Fix $n\geq1$, work over $\F$, and let $\one$ be the all-one column.  A matrix
$A\in M_n(\F)$ is the adjacency matrix of a labelled directed graph in which
loops are allowed.  Put
\[
 r=A\one,\qquad c=A^{\mathsf T}\one,\qquad
 \tau=\one^{\mathsf T}A\one.
\]
Thus $r$ and $c$ are the out- and indegree parity vectors and
$\tau=\one^{\mathsf T}r=\one^{\mathsf T}c$ is total arc parity.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(A)=A^{\mathsf T}+rr^{\mathsf T}.                 \label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
The second summand toggles the complete looped subdigraph on the current
odd-outdegree vertices.  It is a rank-\emph{at-most}-one correction and is
recomputed after every step.

Local complementation and its binary-matrix formulation are classical
\cite{Bouchet1993,Traldi2012}; pivot and loop complementation have an
established algebraic calculus \cite{BrijderHoogeboom2011}.  Static subgraph
complementation, including its rank and algorithmic interfaces, is also a
separate mature operation \cite{BuchananEtAl2022,KochPardalSantos2025}; see
also \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15675}{arXiv:2502.15675}.  Modular
invariant rings for transpose group actions on full $2\times2$ matrix spaces
form another neighboring, but static, interface \cite{ChenRen2026}.
All such static operations and invariant rings, transpose, outer products,
transvections, binary margin enumeration, and generic functional-graph
bookkeeping receive zero contribution credit here.  The residual question is
only the exact temporal structure of the state-dependent update
\eqref{eq:update}.

The internal subtraction is equally strict.  P102's involution-norm dynamics
and P103's double-adjugation dynamics receive zero credit.  P125 acts on pairs
in a nonsingular quadratic space, has a quadratic/polar three-bit quotient,
and permits tail two and period three.  In contrast, the present carrier is
all of $M_n(\F)$, its feasible row/column-margin quotient has dimension
$2n-1$, its tail is at most one, and its recurrent periods are only
$1,2,4$.  Thus the literal systems are not conjugate; generic quotient,
matrix, quadratic-map, cycle, fibre, component, and zeta packaging still
receive zero credit.  A bounded formula-and-owner search found no literal-map
match; a bounded non-hit is not a novelty certificate.

\section{The parity quotient and all orbits}

For $B=\Phi(A)$, write its margins as $r(B),c(B),\tau(B)$.

\begin{lemma}[quotient law]\label{lem:quotient}
For every $A$,
\begin{equation}
 r(B)=c+\tau r,\qquad c(B)=(1+\tau)r,\qquad \tau(B)=0.   \label{eq:quotient}
\end{equation}
Consequently the even-total hyperplane
$\mathcal H_n=\{A:\tau(A)=0\}$ is invariant; on it the margin pair is
swapped.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Multiplying \eqref{eq:update} on the right by $\one$ gives
$r(B)=c+r(r^{\mathsf T}\one)=c+\tau r$.  Transposing first gives
$c(B)=r+r(r^{\mathsf T}\one)=(1+\tau)r$.  Finally,
$\tau(B)=\one^{\mathsf T}(c+\tau r)=\tau+\tau^2=0$ in
$\F$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[complete temporal classification]\label{thm:orbits}
The image and complete recurrent set are both $\mathcal H_n$.  Every state
outside $\mathcal H_n$ has exact depth one.  On $\mathcal H_n$,
\begin{equation}
 \Phi^2(A)=A+rr^{\mathsf T}+cc^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad \Phi^4(A)=A.                                  \label{eq:four}
\end{equation}
If $r\ne c$, then $A$ has exact period four.  If $r=c$, its period is one or
two.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The last identity in \eqref{eq:quotient} shows that $\im\Phi\subseteq
\mathcal H_n$.  The reverse inclusion follows from the explicit preimage in
\cref{thm:fibres}.  On $\mathcal H_n$ the margins swap, so a second use of
\eqref{eq:update} yields the first identity in \eqref{eq:four}; this added
matrix has zero row and column margins.  Repeating the same two steps cancels
it in characteristic two, proving the second identity.  Thus every point of
$\mathcal H_n$ is recurrent.  Every odd-total point reaches it after one step
and cannot already be recurrent, proving exact depth one.

If $r\ne c$, then $rr^{\mathsf T}\ne cc^{\mathsf T}$ because the diagonal
of $vv^{\mathsf T}$ is $v$; hence $\Phi^2(A)\ne A$, and the period is exactly
four.  If $r=c$, \eqref{eq:four} gives $\Phi^2(A)=A$.
\end{proof}

Here depth means the least entrance time into the recurrent set.  On a finite
map this equals the distance to the first periodic point.  The unique matrix
in $M_0(\F)$ may separately be declared fixed; all remaining formulas assume
$n\ge1$.

\section{Every one-step fibre and a second route}

\begin{theorem}[codomain-wide fibre trichotomy]\label{thm:fibres}
For every $B\in M_n(\F)$,
\begin{equation}
 |\Phi^{-1}(B)|=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&\tau(B)=1,\\
 1,&\tau(B)=0\text{ and }c(B)\ne0,\\
 2^{n-1}+1,&\tau(B)=0\text{ and }c(B)=0.
 \end{cases}                                           \label{eq:fibres}
\end{equation}
In particular, $|\im\Phi|=2^{n^2-1}$, and precisely $2^{n(n-1)}$ image
targets have the large fibre.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If a preimage has row margin $r$, solving \eqref{eq:update} for it gives
$A=B^{\mathsf T}+rr^{\mathsf T}$.  Requiring $A\one=r$ is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 r=c(B)+(\one^{\mathsf T}r)r.                           \label{eq:solve}
\end{equation}
For even-weight $r$, this forces $r=c(B)$ and exists exactly when
$\tau(B)=0$; it then supplies one preimage.  For odd-weight $r$,
\eqref{eq:solve} is equivalent to $c(B)=0$.  In that case all $2^{n-1}$
odd vectors work, in addition to the even solution $r=0$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:fibres} and also $\im\Phi=\mathcal H_n$.

There are $2^{n-1}$ choices for each even-parity column, independently over
$n$ columns, so exactly $2^{n(n-1)}$ matrices have $c(B)=0$.
\end{proof}

The odd-total zero case in \eqref{eq:fibres} is essential.  For example,
when $n=1$, the target $[1]$ has nonzero column margin but no preimage.

\begin{proposition}[factor-product route]\label{prop:factor}
The update has the factorisation
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(A)=(I+r\one^{\mathsf T})A^{\mathsf T}.            \label{eq:factor}
\end{equation}
If $\tau=0$, the left factor is the identity when $r=0$ and an involutory
transvection otherwise.  If $\tau=1$, it is an idempotent rank-$n-1$
projection with kernel $\langle r\rangle$ and image $\one^\perp$.
Moreover, for $\tau=0$ the factor product independently gives
\begin{equation}
 \Phi^2(A)=(I+c\one^{\mathsf T})A
              (I+\one r^{\mathsf T})
           =A+cc^{\mathsf T}+rr^{\mathsf T},           \label{eq:factor-two}
\end{equation}
and hence $\Phi^4(A)=A$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\one^{\mathsf T}A^{\mathsf T}=r^{\mathsf T}$,
\eqref{eq:factor} is \eqref{eq:update}.  Put
$L_r=I+r\one^{\mathsf T}$.  Direct multiplication gives
$L_r^2=I+\tau r\one^{\mathsf T}$, while
\begin{equation}
 \one^{\mathsf T}L_r=(1+\tau)\one^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad \det L_r=1+\tau.                              \label{eq:factor-rank}
\end{equation}
Thus $L_r^2=I$ when $\tau=0$, with the stated identity/transvection
distinction.  When $\tau=1$, $L_r^2=L_r$ and
\[
 L_rx=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 x=r(\one^{\mathsf T}x).
\]
Here $r\ne0$, and $L_rr=0$, so $\ker L_r=\langle r\rangle$.  Rank--nullity
gives rank $n-1$; \eqref{eq:factor-rank} places the image in the
$(n-1)$-dimensional space $\one^\perp$, hence the two are equal.

It remains to obtain the temporal identity from the factors.  Suppose
$\tau=0$ and put $B=L_rA^{\mathsf T}$.  Direct multiplication of this product
by $\one$ and of its transpose by $\one$ gives row and column margins $c$
and $r$, respectively.  Therefore
\[
 \Phi^2(A)=L_cB^{\mathsf T}=L_cAL_r^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Expanding the two factors gives \eqref{eq:factor-two}; its mixed term is
$c(\one^{\mathsf T}A\one)r^{\mathsf T}=0$.  Because $r$ and $c$ have even
weight, the correction $cc^{\mathsf T}+rr^{\mathsf T}$ has zero row and
column margins.  The same factor-product calculation on $\Phi^2(A)$ adds
that correction once more, proving $\Phi^4(A)=A$ in characteristic two.
\end{proof}

Thus the factor route both identifies the singular odd-coset projection and
recovers the even-coset temporal identities; the direct margin equation in
\cref{thm:fibres} is still needed to distinguish individual target fibres.

\section{Components and zeta function}

\begin{lemma}[prescribed margins]\label{lem:margins}
For $u,v\in\F^n$, there is a matrix with row margin $u$ and column margin
$v$ exactly when $\one^{\mathsf T}u=\one^{\mathsf T}v$.  In that case there
are $2^{(n-1)^2}$ such matrices.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose the upper-left $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ entries freely.  The prescribed row
sums force the last entry of each of the first $n-1$ rows, and the prescribed
column sums force the last entry of each of the first $n-1$ columns.  The two
requirements on the bottom-right corner agree precisely when the total
parities agree.
\end{proof}

Put
\[
 F_n=2^{n(n-1)/2},\qquad
 C_{2,n}=\frac{2^{n(n-1)}-F_n}{2},\qquad
 C_{4,n}=\frac{2^{n^2-1}-2^{n(n-1)}}{4}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[functional components]\label{thm:census}
The map has $F_n$ fixed points, $C_{2,n}$ two-cycles, and $C_{4,n}$
four-cycles.  It has $2^{n^2-1}$ depth-one states.  With the usual finite-map
convention \cite{ArtinMazur1965},
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_{\Phi_n}(z)=
 (1-z)^{-F_n}(1-z^2)^{-C_{2,n}}(1-z^4)^{-C_{4,n}}.       \label{eq:zeta}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A fixed point satisfies $A+A^{\mathsf T}=rr^{\mathsf T}$.  Comparing
diagonals forces $r=0$; then $A$ is symmetric with zero row sums.  Its
off-diagonal entries are arbitrary and its diagonal is forced by the row
sums, giving dimension $n(n-1)/2$ and hence $F_n$ fixed points.

By \cref{lem:margins}, for each of the $2^{n-1}$ even vectors $r$, there are
$2^{(n-1)^2}$ recurrent matrices with margins $(r,r)$.  Thus
$2^{n(n-1)}$ recurrent points have period dividing two.  Removing fixed
points and dividing by two gives $C_{2,n}$.  All remaining recurrent points
have exact period four by \cref{thm:orbits}, giving $C_{4,n}$.  The odd
coset has size $2^{n^2-1}$ and consists exactly of the depth-one points.
Finally, the standard product of one factor $(1-z^k)^{-1}$ per $k$-cycle
gives \eqref{eq:zeta}.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact control and limitations}

The accompanying deterministic verifier exhausts every one of the
$2^{n^2}$ matrices for $1\le n\le4$.  It checks both forms of the update,
the quotient and fourth-iterate identities, exact depths and periods, all
codomain fibres including zero fibres, the affine margin count, and every
component formula.  These bounded computations are counterexample searches,
not proofs of the all-$n$ statements above.

The result is specific to the binary field, square matrices, transpose, and
the recomputed row-margin selector.  Rectangular carriers, other fields,
other bilinear forms, and static graph complementation require new proofs and
owner gates.  Literature screening was bounded and can establish a hit but
not an absence.  Accordingly we make no claim of novelty, priority,
authorship, posting readiness, or external release.
