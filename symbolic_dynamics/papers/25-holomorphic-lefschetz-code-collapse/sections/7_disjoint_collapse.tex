\section{Disjoint components and atom-loop collapse}
\label{sec:disjoint}

Mixed necklaces disappear if every label is placed on a private recurrent
disk.  That modification produces the desired product, but it also exposes
where the arithmetic inventory resides.

\begin{theorem}[Disjoint graded factorization]
\label{thm:disjoint-factor}
Let \(S\subseteq\{2,3,\ldots\}\).  For \(\Re s>1\), the disjoint pair
\[
 \cE^k_{S,s}=\bigoplus_{n\in S}n^{-s}U_{\phi_n,k}
\]
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \det(I-z\cE^0_{S,s})
 =\left[\prod_{n\in S}(1-zn^{-s})\right]
   \det(I-z\cE^1_{S,s}).
 \label{eq:disjoint-det}
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
        \Dgr(z;\cE_{S,s})
        =\prod_{n\in S}(1-zn^{-s}).
 \label{eq:disjoint-product}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\Cref{prop:trace-class} makes both degreewise direct sums trace class.  Apply
\cref{thm:local-cancellation} on each component and multiply.  Absolute
convergence of \(\sum_{n\in S}|n^{-s}|\) gives convergence of the product
and permits the componentwise Fredholm factorization.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cohomological atom-loop equivalence]
\label{thm:atom-collapse}
The graded determinant in \eqref{eq:disjoint-product} is the ordinary
Fredholm determinant of the diagonal trace-class operator
\begin{equation}
        C_{S,s}=\diag(n^{-s})_{n\in S}
        \quad\text{on }\ell^2(S).
 \label{eq:cohomology-diagonal}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, every disk contributes one constant cohomology state, and all
nonconstant analytic modes cancel.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The finite complex \eqref{eq:polynomial-complex} has one-dimensional
cohomology represented by constants.  On the component labelled \(n\), the
weighted pullback acts on that class by \(n^{-s}\).  Direct-sum cohomology is
therefore \(\bigoplus_{n\in S}\C\), and its induced map is
\eqref{eq:cohomology-diagonal}.  Its ordinary determinant is precisely
\eqref{eq:disjoint-product}.  The same conclusion also follows directly
from the telescoping component spectra.
\end{proof}

The theorem is stronger than saying that the output resembles an Euler
product.  It identifies a determinant-equivalent minimal model: one
one-dimensional loop per supplied label.  The affine translations \(a_n\),
contractions \(q_n\), and logarithmic digit histories occur only in paired
acyclic modes.

\subsection{Arbitrary-inventory control}

The disjoint construction is functorial in \(S\).  Without changing a digit
map, function space, form degree, or local determinant rule, it returns a
product for every inventory.

For \(S=\Pp\) and \(z=1\), the product equals \(1/\zeta(s)\) in the Euler
half-plane.  This equality earns analytic determinant credit but no
prime-selection credit.  The prime set appears as the recurrent component
index, and \cref{thm:atom-collapse} deletes every other trace of the code.
In the project's control language, the disjoint architecture
\textsc{Proves Too Much}.

Shared and disjoint assemblies are therefore not two representations of one
dynamical object.  Shared recurrence is connected and contains mixed
necklaces.  Disjoint recurrence removes them by changing the recurrent
component structure.  The next section adds the independent clock
distinction.
