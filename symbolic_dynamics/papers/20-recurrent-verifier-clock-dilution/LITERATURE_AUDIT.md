# LITERATURE AUDIT — SD-C22

## Decision

No direct primary-source collision was found for the full claim bundle:

1. an explicit full-shift semiring quotient-search verifier;
2. successful paths closed into disjoint prime cycles;
3. exact total roof $h(F_p)=\log p$;
4. cycle length $\ell(p)\sim\frac12p\log p$;
5. allocation-independent noncompactness of the natural vertex adjacency;
6. first-return collapse to diagonal prime loops; and
7. an acceptance-independent padded-decider control showing that the compiler
   is support-agnostic.

The defensible novelty is this narrow application and its same-object
firewall.  The underlying weighted-shift compactness idea, cyclic determinant
calculation, state expansion, inducing, and countable Markov zeta formalisms
are classical and are not claimed as new.

## Safe novelty statement

> For this explicit contracted quotient-search compiler, exact arithmetic
> clock $\log p$ and graph-step recurrence are incompatible with compactness
> of the natural counting-space weighted vertex adjacency; inducing restores a
> trace-class Euler determinant only after contracting the computation.

This statement is allocation-independent, mathematically proved, and narrow
enough to avoid claiming classical ingredients.

## Dangerous formulations

- **Do not say:** this is a new compactness criterion for weighted shifts.
- **Do not say:** a dynamical zeta function does not exist.
- **Do not say:** first return is illegitimate.
- **Do not say:** roofs tending to zero invalidate a suspension.
- **Do not say:** no symbolic-dynamical realization of the Euler product can
  be trace class.
- **Do not call:** $L_s$ a Ruelle transfer operator.
- **Do not identify:** the raw product with $\det(I-zL_s)$.

## Route boundary

`A2_FAIL` means only that the frozen whole-vertex adjacency does not admit the
ordinary Hilbert-space Fredholm determinant.  It does not cover nuclear
operators on other Banach spaces, induced/local zeta functions, regularized
determinants, semifinite settings, anisotropic spaces, or different recurrent
grammars.
