\title[Weak-excedance extraction]{Weak-Excedance Extraction:\\
Exact Images, Ferrers Fibres, and Fibonacci Right-Inverse Towers}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
For a permutation $\pi=\pi_1\cdots\pi_n$, retain the letters satisfying
$\pi_i\ge i$ and standardize them in their original order.  We study the
resulting rank-changing permutation map.  If $\sigma\in\mathfrak S_m$ and
$d(\sigma)=\max_i(i-\sigma_i)$, then $\sigma$ occurs from source rank $n$ if
and only if $n\ge m+d(\sigma)$; a high-shift/low-tail section realizes every
admissible rank.  We also give an exact fibre formula for every target as a
sum of deficient Ferrers-board completion counts.  The only recurrent states
are identities.  On nonidentity targets, iterating the minimum-rank section
produces an inverse ray whose rank and maximum drop evolve by
$(m,d)\mapsto(m+d,m)$, hence by Fibonacci matrix powers.  This is a local
one-step minimality theorem, not a global minimum theorem for iterated
preimages and not a maximum absorption clock.  Classical weak-excedance,
bounded-drop, tableau, Bruhat, and Bell-enumeration results are explicitly
subtracted.  An exact audit executes $3{,}689{,}489$ assertions, including
explicit rank-boundary tests and a counterexample to a withdrawn pointwise
clock claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Map, scope, and main theorem}\label{sec:setup}

Let $\Sym_n$ be the permutations of $[n]$ in one-line notation.  If $v$ is a
word of distinct integers, write $\std(v)$ for its standardization.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:map}
 \WEX(\pi)=\std(\pi_i:\pi_i\ge i),
 \qquad \pi\in\Sym_n,
\end{equation}
where the retained letters stay in their original order.  Position one is
always retained, so \eqref{eq:map} is a self-map of
$\Sym_{\le N}=\bigsqcup_{1\le n\le N}\Sym_n$ for every \(N\ge1\).  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drop}
 d(\sigma)=\max_{1\le i\le m}(i-\sigma_i),
 \qquad \sigma\in\Sym_m.
\end{equation}
This is the classical maximum-drop statistic.

The static ownership boundary is deliberately broad.  Excedance-set
enumeration is due to Ehrenborg--Steingr{\'{i}}msson~\cite{EhrenborgSteingrimsson2000};
bounded maximum drop and its enumerators are treated by
Chung--Claesson--Dukes--Graham~\cite{ChungClaessonDukesGraham2010} and
Chen--Chen~\cite{ChenChen2016}; permutation-tableau structure is developed by
Steingr{\'{i}}msson--Williams~\cite{SteingrimssonWilliams2005}; and
Bergeron--Gagnon organize weak-excedance position/value classes through a
Bruhat quotient~\cite{BergeronGagnon2023}.  All of those static statistics,
distributions, and structures receive zero contribution credit here.

There is also an exact collision at the identity aggregate.
Beyene--Backelin--Mantaci--Fufa, using the transposition-array interface of
Baril~\cite{Baril2007,Baril2013}, prove that permutations whose
weak-excedance-letter subword is increasing are Bell-number enumerated
\cite[Theorem~27]{BeyeneBackelinMantaciFufa2023}.  In the notation below this
owns
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bell-owned}
 \sum_{m=1}^n |\WEX_n^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_m)|=B_n.
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:bell-owned} and its transposition-array proof are not
claimed.  Our fibre formula retains both the target permutation and its rank,
including nonidentity targets.  Generic Ferrers-board matching is likewise
background; the residual is the conjunction of the literal extraction map,
its exact target obstruction and sections, its target-resolved fibres, and
the dynamics of one canonical right inverse.  A bounded exact-map search
non-hit is not evidence of novelty, priority, or clearance.

For $n\ge m$, write $h=n-m$.  For $A=\{a_1<\cdots<a_m\}$ and
$P=\{p_1<\cdots<p_m\}$ contained in $[n]$, call $(A,P)$
\emph{$\sigma$-admissible} if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:admissible}
 p_i\le a_{\sigma_i}\quad(1\le i\le m).
\end{equation}
\begin{samepage}
Let $B=[n]\setminus A$ and
$Q=[n]\setminus P=\{q_1<\cdots<q_h\}$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completion}
 K(B,Q)=\prod_{j=1}^h
 \left(|\{b\in B:b<q_j\}|-(j-1)\right),
\end{equation}
interpreted as zero if any factor is nonpositive; when \(h=0\), the empty
product is \(1\).
\end{samepage}

\begin{theorem}[images, fibres, recurrence, and inverse tower]\label{thm:main}
Let $\sigma\in\Sym_m$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item\label{it:image}
For every $n\ge m$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:image}
 \sigma\in\WEX(\Sym_n)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 n\ge m+d(\sigma).
\end{equation}
Whenever $h=n-m\ge d(\sigma)$, the explicit right section is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:section}
 R_n(\sigma)=(\sigma_1+h,\ldots,\sigma_m+h,1,\ldots,h).
\end{equation}
Thus $m+d(\sigma)$ is the exact minimum source rank.
\item\label{it:fibre}
For every source rank \(n\ge1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre}
 |\WEX_n^{-1}(\sigma)|
 =\begin{cases}
 0,&1\le n<m,\\
 \displaystyle\sum_{(A,P)\ \sigma\text{-admissible}}
 K([n]\setminus A,[n]\setminus P),&n\ge m.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This includes zero fibres.  At the same rank \(n=m\), the fibre has size one
for \(\sigma=\mathrm{id}_m\) and zero otherwise.
\item\label{it:recurrent}
$\WEX(\pi)=\pi$ if and only if $\pi$ is an identity.  Every
nonidentity step strictly lowers rank; hence the recurrent states of
$\Sym_{\le N}$ are $\mathrm{id}_1,\ldots,\mathrm{id}_N$.
\item\label{it:tower}
Suppose $\sigma$ is nonidentity, put $d=d(\sigma)>0$, and define
$\sigma^{(0)}=\sigma$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical}
 \sigma^{(t+1)}=R_{m_t+d_t}(\sigma^{(t)}),
 \qquad m_t=|\sigma^{(t)}|,\quad d_t=d(\sigma^{(t)}).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:update}
 (m_{t+1},d_{t+1})=(m_t+d_t,m_t),
 \qquad \WEX(\sigma^{(t+1)})=\sigma^{(t)}.
\end{equation}
With $F_0=0,F_1=1$, for every $t\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibonacci}
 m_t=F_{t+1}m+F_t d,\qquad
 d_t=F_t m+F_{t-1}d.
\end{equation}
Every edge of this inverse ray has minimum possible one-step source rank.  If
$\tau$ is the first hitting time of an identity, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-shift}
 \tau(\sigma^{(t)})=\tau(\sigma)+t.
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Part~\ref{it:tower} says \emph{locally minimum}: each individual lift uses a
minimum-rank preimage of its immediate target.  It does not assert that the
composite tower minimizes rank among all $t$-step preimages.

\section{Images and deficient completions}\label{sec:inverse}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(\ref{it:image})]
Suppose $\pi\in\Sym_n$ maps to $\sigma\in\Sym_m$.  Let
$P=\{p_1<\cdots<p_m\}$ be its selected positions, and let
$A=\{a_1<\cdots<a_m\}$ be its selected values.  Standardization forces the
entry at $p_i$ to be $a_{\sigma_i}$.  Selection gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chain}
 i\le p_i\le a_{\sigma_i}.
\end{equation}
Since only $h=n-m$ values lie outside $A$, the $j$th selected value satisfies
$a_j\le h+j$.  Applying this to \eqref{eq:chain} yields
\[
 i\le h+\sigma_i,\qquad i-\sigma_i\le h.
\]
Taking the maximum proves $d(\sigma)\le h$, hence necessity in
\eqref{eq:image}.

Conversely, suppose $h\ge d(\sigma)$ and use \eqref{eq:section}.  Its first
$m$ entries satisfy $\sigma_i+h\ge i$ and are therefore selected.  Its final
entry $j$ occurs at position $m+j>j$ and is deficient.  Hence the selected
word is exactly the shifted copy
$(\sigma_1+h,\ldots,\sigma_m+h)$, whose standardization is $\sigma$.
This proves the section identity and the sharp threshold.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(\ref{it:fibre})]
If \(n<m\), a rank-\(n\) source cannot produce a rank-\(m\) target, so the
fibre is empty.  Assume henceforth that \(n\ge m\).
Fix selected sets $A$ and $P$.  The selected assignment is forced:
$p_i$ receives $a_{\sigma_i}$.  It consists of weak excedances exactly when
\eqref{eq:admissible} holds.  It remains to biject the complement values $B$
onto the complement positions $Q$ so that each value assigned at $q_j$ is
strictly smaller than $q_j$.

Process $q_1,\ldots,q_h$ increasingly.  At $q_j$ there are
$|\{b\in B:b<q_j\}|$ eligible complement values.  Every one of the $j-1$
previously assigned values is among them, because it was assigned at an
earlier position $q_k<q_j$ and is smaller than $q_k$.  Therefore the number
of choices at step $j$ is precisely the $j$th factor of
\eqref{eq:completion}.  The product counts all deficient complement
assignments, with a nonpositive factor correctly giving zero.

Every source determines one unique pair $(A,P)$ and one such complement
assignment.  Conversely, the forced selected assignment and any deficient
completion give a unique source whose selected standardized word is
$\sigma$.  The classes are disjoint, so summing proves \eqref{eq:fibre}.
When \(n=m\), both complements are empty and
\(K(\varnothing,\varnothing)=1\).  The sole pair \(A=P=[m]\) is admissible
exactly when \(i\le\sigma_i\) for every \(i\), which by equality of coordinate
sums holds exactly for \(\sigma=\mathrm{id}_m\).  This proves the same-rank
boundary.
\end{proof}

Formula \eqref{eq:fibre} is a Ferrers-board product in elementary form: at
each increasing complement position, it counts the unused values lying
strictly below the diagonal.  Summing it over identity targets recovers the
owned Bell aggregate \eqref{eq:bell-owned}; that consistency check does not
transfer ownership of the rank- and target-resolved formula.

\section{Forward absorption and the canonical inverse ray}\label{sec:dynamics}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(\ref{it:recurrent})]
If $\WEX(\pi)$ has the same rank as $\pi\in\Sym_n$, every position was
selected, so $\pi_i\ge i$ for all $i$.  But
$\sum_i\pi_i=\sum_i i$; hence equality holds term by term and
$\pi=\mathrm{id}_n$.  Identities are fixed.  Thus every nonidentity step
strictly lowers positive integer rank, proving both absorption and the
recurrent classification.
\end{proof}

For a nonidentity $\sigma$, $d(\sigma)>0$: otherwise
$\sigma_i\ge i$ for every $i$, and the preceding sum argument would make
$\sigma$ an identity.  The minimum-rank section in
\eqref{eq:canonical} is therefore a genuine rank increase.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(\ref{it:tower})]
Apply Part~\ref{it:image} with $h=d_t$.  It gives the right-section identity
in \eqref{eq:update} and proves that $m_t+d_t$ is the minimum rank of any
one-step preimage of $\sigma^{(t)}$.

The first $m_t$ entries of the section have the form
$\sigma^{(t)}_i+d_t$.  Their drop is
\[
 i-(\sigma^{(t)}_i+d_t)\le d_t-d_t=0.
\]
The final low value $j$ occurs at position $m_t+j$ and has drop exactly
$m_t$.  Consequently $d_{t+1}=m_t$, while the rank is visibly
$m_{t+1}=m_t+d_t$.  This proves the resource update.

Writing the update as multiplication by
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and using its
standard Fibonacci powers proves \eqref{eq:fibonacci} by induction.  Finally,
the lifted state makes one step to its immediate target and is nonrecurrent;
therefore each lift increases the identity hitting time by exactly one,
which proves \eqref{eq:tail-shift}.
\end{proof}

For example, starting from $21$ gives resource pairs
\[
 (m_t,d_t)=(2,1),(3,2),(5,3),(8,5),(13,8),\ldots.
\]
The Fibonacci sequence here describes a chosen backward dynamical system.  It
does not by itself bound all forward orbits at a fixed rank.

\section{Exact control, exclusions, and declarations}\label{sec:control}

The accompanying deterministic verifier enumerates all $409{,}113$
permutations through rank nine, reconstructs the literal functional graph,
and checks the image theorem in $99{,}451$ target/rank cells.  It compares
\eqref{eq:fibre} with literal predecessor counts for all $6{,}985$ target
cells through source rank seven, then separately checks $316{,}646$ cells
with \(n<m\) and all $46{,}233$ same-rank cells through target rank eight.
It also recovers the owned Bell aggregate.  Finally, it takes every one of the $46{,}225$
nonidentity targets through rank eight through six exact section lifts and
checks \eqref{eq:update}--\eqref{eq:tail-shift}.  The frozen transcript records
$3{,}689{,}489$ exact assertions and ends in \texttt{PASS}.  These computations
are falsification pressure, not proofs of the all-rank statements.

\paragraph{Limitations and withdrawn clock claims.}
No maximum absorption clock is claimed.  In particular, the tempting
pointwise inequality
\[
 \tau(\WEX(\pi))\le
 \max_{\rho\in\Sym_{d(\pi)}}\tau(\rho)
\]
is false: for
\[
 \pi=(11,10,9,4,1,2,3,8,5,6,7)
\]
one has $d(\pi)=4$, while
$\WEX(\pi)=(5,4,3,1,2)$ has tail three and the maximum tail in $\Sym_4$ is
two.  The verifier reproduces this counterexample.  The paper also makes no
global minimum-rank claim for $t$-step preimages and does not infer a
fixed-rank maximum from the Fibonacci inverse ray.  The broad carrier pattern
``extract a subsequence and standardize'' receives zero credit; the diagonal
predicate, maximum-drop image obstruction, and deficient completion are the
specific mechanisms used here.
