# Proof Package

## Claim

For the self-map

$$
T_n(w)=R^{m_{w_0}(w)}w,\qquad w\in\{0,1\}^n,
$$

where $R$ is left rotation and $m_a(w)$ is the multiplicity of $a$, the
following statements hold.

1. On a cyclic word of least period $d$ and weight $k$, the pointed dynamics
   decomposes into $gcd(k,d)$ generator cycles of length
   $L=d/\gcd(k,d)$.  Each component has the complete recurrent/transient
   classification stated in Theorem 2.2 of `main.tex`.
2. The possible periods are ${1}$ for $n=1$ and
   ${1,2}\cup\{\ell:\ell\mid n,\ 3\leq\ell<n\}$ for $n\geq2$.
3. The maximum preperiod is $H_1=0$ and $H_n=n-2$ for $n\geq2$.  Exactly
   two states attain $H_n$ for every $n\geq3$.
4. Every target has the complete labelled inverse list and the $0/1/2$
   fibre distribution stated in Theorem 4.1.
5. The fixed-point count is the primitive-block Möbius sum stated in
   Theorem 4.2.

## Assumptions

- $n$ is a positive integer.
- Words are linearly pointed and indexed modulo $n$.
- $R$ rotates coordinates one position to the left.
- The least rotational period $d$ of a word divides $n$.
- Period means eventual exact cycle length; preperiod is the first hitting
  time of the periodic set.

## Notation

- $k=\operatorname{wt}(w)$ is the number of ones.
- A rotation class of least period $d$ is indexed by $j\in\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z$.
- $h=\gcd(k,d)$ and $L=d/h$.
- In one generator component, $b_q$ is the bit read at the vertex reached
  after $q$ additions of $k$.
- $A(d,j)$ denotes the number of length-$d$ linear binary words of least
  period $d$ and weight $j$.

## Proof Strategy

First conjugate the literal word map on each rotation class to an oriented
Cayley graph.  Classify the functional graph of each undirected generator
cycle by reading its binary orientations.  Derive the global period and
clock statements from that local classification.  Prove the inverse axis
directly by undoing the two forced rotations of a target.  Finally decompose
fixed words by their least period and apply Möbius inversion to primitive
blocks.

## Dependency Map

1. Component classification depends on the exact phase reduction and the
   coset decomposition of the subgroup generated by $k$ in
   $\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z$.
2. The period inventory depends on the component classification plus one
   explicit aperiodic witness for each proper divisor.
3. The clock bound depends on the longest-run tail formula; sharpness and
   the deepest census depend on the equality case of that bound.
4. The fibre theorem is independent of the forward component proof and
   depends only on weight preservation and undoing each labelled branch.
5. The fixed census depends on the least-period decomposition and the
   fixed-density primitive-word Möbius formula.
6. The cases $n=1$ and $n=2$ are checked separately where the generic
   longest-run argument degenerates.

## Proof

### Step 1: exact pointed phase map

Fix $u\in\{0,1\}^n$ of least rotational period $d$ and weight $k$.  Its
distinct pointed states are $R^ju$ for $j\in\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z$.  If
$u_j=1$, the first-symbol multiplicity is $k$, so

$$
T_n(R^ju)=R^{j+k}u.
$$

If $u_j=0$, that multiplicity is $n-k$, so

$$
T_n(R^ju)=R^{j+n-k}u=R^{j-k}u,
$$

where the last equality uses $d\mid n$.  Thus the pointed map is

$$
\phi_u(j)=j+k\quad(u_j=1),\qquad
\phi_u(j)=j-k\quad(u_j=0)
$$

in $\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z$.

### Step 2: generator-cycle decomposition

The subgroup generated by $k$ in $\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z$ has order
$L=d/h$, where $h=\gcd(k,d)$.  It has $h$ cosets.  Each coset is an
undirected cycle when its vertices are ordered by successive additions of
$k$.  In this order, a bit $1$ sends a vertex one step forward and a bit
$0$ sends it one step backward.

For $L=1$, both moves are the identity.  For $L=2$, forward and backward
reach the same opposite vertex, so the component is one directed two-cycle.
Assume $L\geq3$.  A constant component points coherently and is one directed
$L$-cycle.

On a nonconstant component, every cyclic occurrence $10$ points inward
across its common edge and is a directed two-cycle.  A vertex labelled $1$
moves forward within its maximal $1$-run until it reaches the terminal $1$
of such an edge.  A vertex labelled $0$ moves backward within its maximal
$0$-run until it reaches the initial $0$ of such an edge.  Hence all vertices
reach the listed two-cycles.  A directed cycle of length at least three
cannot change direction, since its first change immediately traverses the
preceding edge backward and closes a two-cycle.  It also cannot keep one
direction, since that would require every bit in the component to be equal.
The list is exhaustive.

In a constant run of length $s$, the farthest vertex is $s-1$ steps from
the boundary two-cycle.  Therefore the component maximum tail is its longest
cyclic constant-run length minus one.  This proves Claim 1.

### Step 3: complete period inventory

By Step 2, a period greater than two is the length
$L=d/\gcd(k,d)$ of a constant generator component.  Thus $L\mid d\mid n$.
If $L=n$, then $d=n$ and $gcd(k,n)=1$, leaving one generator component.
Constancy of that component makes the entire word constant, whose least
period is $1$, contradicting $d=n>1$.  Every long period is therefore a
proper divisor of $n$.

The constant words realize period $1$.  A word with one $1$ has a recurrent
$10$ edge and realizes period $2$ for every $n\geq2$.

Fix a proper divisor $L\geq3$ and write $n=gL$ with $g\geq2$.  Let the
one-support be

$$
S=\{0,1,\ldots,g-2,g\}.
$$

This set has $g$ elements.  The cyclic zero gap from $g$ back to $0$ has
length $n-g-1$, while every other zero gap has length at most $1$; since
$L\geq3$ and $g\geq2$, the former is uniquely longest.  Any rotational
symmetry preserves that unique gap and both of its boundary ones, forcing
zero rotation.  The word has least period $n$.

Its generator is $k=g$.  Every position congruent to $g-1$ modulo $g$ is
zero, so this generator component is constant and has length $n/g=L$.
Step 2 makes it a directed $L$-cycle.  Every period in the claimed set is
realized, proving Claim 2.

### Step 4: sharp clock and equality case

A nonconstant component of length $L$ has no constant run longer than
$L-1$, so Step 2 bounds every transient tail by $L-2\leq n-2$.  Constant
components are recurrent.  For $n\geq3$, the word $010^{n-2}$ has weight
$1$, least period $n$, and a cyclic zero run of length $n-1$.  Its displayed
pointer is $n-2$ steps from the recurrent $10$ edge.  Complementation
preserves first-symbol multiplicities and commutes with rotation, so the
complement is a second witness.

If a state has tail $n-2$, both inequalities above are equalities.  Hence
$L=n$ and the generator-order word has one constant run of length $n-1$.
It has exactly one minority bit.  There are two rotation necklaces with one
minority bit, according to the minority symbol.  Each has one farthest
pointer, producing exactly $010^{n-2}$ and its complement.  At $n=1$ both
words are fixed.  At $n=2$ the constants are fixed and the nonconstant pair
is a two-cycle, so every state has tail $0=n-2$.  Claim 3 follows.

### Step 5: every-target inverse atlas

Let $y$ have nonconstant weight $k$.  Weight preservation forces every
source to the same layer.  A source beginning in $1$ rotates by $k$, so its
only candidate is

$$
x_1=R^{-k}y.
$$

This candidate begins in $1$ exactly when $y_{-k}=1$.  A source beginning
in $0$ rotates by $n-k$, so its only candidate is

$$
x_0=R^{-(n-k)}y=R^ky.
$$

It begins in $0$ exactly when $y_k=0$.  When both exist, their first bits
differ, so they are distinct.  Constant targets have only their constant
source.

The inspected positions $-k$ and $k$ coincide if and only if $n\mid2k$.
In that case exactly one branch condition holds for every target.  Otherwise
the positions are distinct.  Fibre zero prescribes $(y_{-k},y_k)=(0,1)$
and fibre two prescribes $(1,0)$.  Each prescription leaves $k-1$ ones among
$n-2$ free positions, giving

$$
N_0(n,k)=N_2(n,k)=\binom{n-2}{k-1}.
$$

Subtracting these two classes from all $\binom nk$ weight-$k$ targets gives
the one-fibre count, and subtracting the zero-fibre targets gives the image
formula.  This proves Claim 4.

### Step 6: fixed-point Möbius census

The number of length-$d$ binary linear words of least period $d$ and weight
$j$ is

$$
A(d,j)=\sum_{e\mid\gcd(d,j)}\mu(e)\binom{d/e}{j/e},
$$

by Möbius inversion on the repetition factor.  Repeating such a primitive
block $n/d$ times gives a unique length-$n$ word of least period $d$ and
weight $k=(n/d)j$.

If its first bit is $1$, it is fixed exactly when $R^k$ fixes it, equivalently
$d\mid k$.  If its first bit is $0$, it is fixed exactly when $d\mid n-k$.
Because $d\mid n$, these conditions are equivalent.  Summing precisely the
primitive blocks satisfying $d\mid(n/d)j$ gives

$$
|\operatorname{Fix}(T_n)|=
\sum_{d\mid n}\sum_{j=0}^d
\mathbf 1_{\{d\mid(n/d)j\}}A(d,j).
$$

This proves Claim 5. ∎
