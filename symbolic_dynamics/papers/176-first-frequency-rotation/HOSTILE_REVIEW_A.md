## Independent derivation

### A-T01 — exact `+/-k` pointed-necklace components: no change

Let `u` be an `n`-letter binary word of least rotational period `d`, and
let `k=wt(u)`.  The distinct pointed states are `R^j u`, with
`j in Z/dZ`.  At phase `j`, the first bit is `u_j`.  Hence the literal rule
gives

```text
j -> j+k       if u_j=1,
j -> j+n-k     if u_j=0.
```

Because `d|n`, the second increment is `-k mod d`.  This proves the claimed
phase map without appealing to the author reduction.

Put `h=gcd(k,d)` and `L=d/h`.  The subgroup generated by `k` has order `L`
and exactly `h` cosets.  In generator coordinates on a coset, the successor
of `q` is `q+1` at a `1` and `q-1` at a `0`.

- For `L=1`, the only vertex is fixed.
- For `L=2`, `+1=-1`, so both vertices point to the other and form one
  two-cycle, irrespective of the two labels.
- For `L>=3`, a constant label sequence is coherently oriented and is one
  exact `L`-cycle.
- For a nonconstant label sequence, every cyclic `10` boundary is a
  two-cycle.  A `1` moves right until the last `1` of its run, and a `0`
  moves left until the first `0` of its run.  Thus every point reaches one
  of those edges.  A simple directed cycle using both directions must
  immediately reuse an undirected edge and therefore has length two; a
  longer directed cycle would have to use one direction all the way around,
  forcing all labels equal.  No other recurrence is possible.

For a run of length `s`, exactly one boundary vertex is recurrent and the
farthest run vertex is `s-1` arrows away.  This yields the pointwise depth
formula and the longest-run-minus-one maximum.  The cases `L=1,2` are
genuinely exceptional and are stated separately in the paper.  **No repair
is required.**

### A-T02 — possible-period inventory: no change

Every period greater than two is the order `L=d/gcd(k,d)` of a coherently
oriented generator component, so it divides `n`.  If `L=n`, then necessarily
`d=n` and `gcd(k,n)=1`; there is only one generator component.  Coherent
orientation would make every bit equal, contradicting least period `n>1`.
Thus a long period is a proper divisor of `n`.

Constants give period one, and a one-`1` necklace gives a two-cycle for all
`n>=2`.  For a proper divisor `L>=3`, write `n=gL`, `g>=2`, and take the
support

```text
S={0,1,...,g-2,g}.
```

It has weight `g`.  Its wraparound zero gap has length `n-g-1`, whereas
every other zero gap has length at most one.  Since
`n-g-1=g(L-1)-1>=3`, that gap is uniquely longest; an orientation-preserving
rotation preserving the support must preserve its initial endpoint and is
therefore trivial.  The word has least period `n`.  The generator component
congruent to `g-1 mod g` is all zero and has length `L`, giving an exact
`L`-cycle.  This proves the full inventory, including the realization step
that was most vulnerable to an unproved aperiodicity assertion.  **No repair
is required.**

### A-T03 — sharp `n-2` clock and deepest two: no change

In a mixed component of length `L`, a constant run has length at most
`L-1`, so every tail is at most `L-2<=n-2`; constant components have no
tail.  The word `010^(n-2)` has weight one and its pointed zero lies
`n-2` arrows from the recurrent `10` edge.  Complementation commutes with
the update, giving the second witness.

Equality forces both `L=n` and a run of length `n-1`.  Hence the word has
one minority bit.  There are exactly two such rotation necklaces, one for
each minority symbol, and each directed run has a unique farthest pointer.
The two deepest linear states are therefore exactly `010^(n-2)` and its
complement.  At `n=1`, both words are fixed; at `n=2`, the constants are
fixed and the two mixed words form a two-cycle, so all four states have
depth zero.  **No repair is required.**

### A-T04 — every-target fibres and histogram: no change

Fix a nonconstant target `y` of weight `k`.  Weight preservation forces any
source into the same layer.  A source beginning with `1` can only be
`x_1=R^(-k)y`, and it is valid exactly when `y_{-k}=1`.  A source beginning
with `0` can only be `x_0=R^k y`, and it is valid exactly when `y_k=0`.
When both exist their leading symbols differ, so they cannot coincide.

The inspected coordinates coincide exactly when `n|2k`; then precisely one
of the two complementary branch tests passes.  Otherwise the coordinates
are distinct.  Fibre zero fixes `(y_{-k},y_k)=(0,1)` and fibre two fixes
`(1,0)`, leaving `k-1` ones in `n-2` positions in either case.  Therefore

```text
N_0(n,k)=N_2(n,k)=C(n-2,k-1),
N_1(n,k)=C(n,k)-2C(n-2,k-1),
```

outside the half-weight case, while the half-weight layer has fibre one
everywhere.  The two constants each have their unique constant source.
Summing the nonzero-fibre targets gives the stated image formula.  This is a
complete list, not merely an upper bound.  **No repair is required.**

### A-T05 — primitive-block Möbius fixed census: no change

Let `A(d,j)` count length-`d` linear binary words of least period `d` and
weight `j`.  Repetition-factor inversion gives

```text
A(d,j)=sum_{e|gcd(d,j)} mu(e) C(d/e,j/e).
```

Every length-`n` word is uniquely an `n/d`-fold repeat of such a block and
has total weight `k=(n/d)j`.  On a word of least period `d`, a leading `1`
is fixed iff `d|k`; a leading `0` is fixed iff `d|(n-k)`, equivalent because
`d|n`.  Thus the condition is pointer-independent and summation gives the
displayed census.  The conventions at `j=0,d` are covered by
`gcd(d,0)=d`.  Primitive-word inversion is correctly declared zero credit.
**No repair is required.**

## Collision and owner stress

### A-O01 — P166 mass exhaustion does not specialize literally: no change

P166 acts on `(Z/NZ)^N` by diagonal alphabet translation and, on one
diagonal orbit, has phase map `q -> q+c_q`, where `c_q>=0` and
`sum_q c_q=N`.  P176 acts on coordinate-rotation necklaces.  The shared
phase syntax is real and is correctly assigned zero contribution credit.

There is a sharp hypothesis obstruction to importing P166's mass-exhaustion
proof.  On a P176 generator component of length `L>=3`, identical generator
coordinates would require a P166 occupancy value `1` at every `+1` arrow
and `L-1` at every `-1` arrow.  If there are `z` minus arrows, their sum is

```text
(L-z) + z(L-1) = L + z(L-2).
```

For a mixed component `1<=z<=L-1`, this is strictly greater than `L`, so it
is not a weak composition of `L`.  Reversing generator orientation merely
exchanges the two label counts and leaves the same obstruction for a mixed
word.  The trivial, two-cycle, and coherently oriented boundary components
do overlap elementary P166 phase graphs; they receive no separation credit.

There is also a recurrence obstruction at the whole-necklace level.  P166
proves at most one nontrivial cycle on a diagonal orbit.  For P176,
`u=111000` has `d=6`, `k=3`, `h=3`, `L=2`, and its six pointed states split
into three nontrivial two-cycles.  Consequently one P176 necklace is not a
P166 diagonal orbit under graph conjugacy.  This does not rule out an
artificial embedding into a union of selected P166 subsystems, which is why
the paper must remain amber rather than claiming a separation theorem.

The repeated numerical clock `n-2`, generic cyclic phase language, finite
functional-graph facts, and indicator-style inverse notation are all
properly subtracted.  P166's depth proof uses a bounded total occupancy mass;
P176's equality case uses a nearest-neighbour constant run.  P166's target
sources are alphabet translates selected by histogram equations and have a
growing maximum fibre, whereas P176 has two first-bit-labelled coordinate
rotations.  No carrier-renaming/coefficient-substitution transfer of the
retained theorem package was found in the primary internal comparison.
**No wording repair is required; the amber kill switch must remain.**

`R_z(phi |x|)` as a one-qubit rotation about the `z` axis whose angle is
