\begin{abstract}
Let $V=\F_q^d$ and let $N$ be a regular nilpotent endomorphism.  We study the
finite self-map $T(U)=N(U)$ on the full subspace lattice of $V$.  For
$0\leq t\leq d$, an $s$-subspace $W\leq\im N^t$, and an integer $r$, the
complete fibre formula is
\[
 \#\{U\in\Gr(r,V):N^tU=W\}
 =\qbinom{t}{r-s}q^{s(t-r+s)}.
\]
It yields every joint transition count for $(\dim U,\dim N^tU)$ and a
uniform one-step indegree law.  If $G_j(q)=\sum_a\qbinom{j}{a}$, then the
number of points absorbed by time $t$ is $G_{\min(t,d)}(q)$; hence the exact
depth-$t$ layer has size $G_t(q)-G_{t-1}(q)$ for $1\leq t\leq d$.  Zero is
the unique periodic point, the sharp maximum depth is $d$, and the
Artin--Mazur zeta function is $(1-z)^{-1}$.  The depth census determines
$(q,d)$ whenever $d\geq2$; all one-dimensional fields give the same
two-state map.  Quotient-fibre geometry and an independent hyperplane
recurrence prove the main count.  Exhaustive RREF controls over prime and
extension fields register $515{,}379$ exact assertions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and ownership boundary}

Fix a prime power $q$, an integer $d\geq1$, and a regular nilpotent
endomorphism $N$ of $V=\F_q^d$.  Thus $N^d=0$ and $N^{d-1}\neq0$.  The full
subspace lattice $\Latt(V)$ carries the finite dynamical system
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:T}
 T=T_{q,d,N}\colon\Latt(V)\longrightarrow\Latt(V),
 \qquad T(U)=N(U).
\end{equation}
The update loses both dimension and position relative to the kernel flag.
The resulting functional graph is nevertheless exactly enumerable.

Gaussian coefficients and their interpretation as finite-subspace counts
are classical \citep{GoldmanRota1970,Prasad2010}.  Lattices of subspaces
\emph{fixed} by a linear operator, including finite-field enumeration, form
another established subject
\citep{BrickmanFillmore1967,Fripertinger2011}.  More directly, Bender,
Coley, Robbins, and Rumsey enumerate subspaces by their dimension sequence
relative to a linear endomorphism and obtain a regular-nilpotent product
formula \citep{BenderEtAl1992}; Ram gives a recent general solution of the
finite-field subspace-profile problem \citep{Ram2026}.  We assign no novelty
credit to Gaussian/intersection counts, invariant-subspace lattices,
subspace-profile enumeration, or the regular-nilpotent specialization of
that theory.  Our bounded object is different: the phase contains every
subspace, usually non-invariant, and the question is the pointed temporal map
$U\mapsto N(U)$.  We isolate fibres with a prescribed target $N^tU=W$, then
record its absorption tree, local indegrees, and isomorphism rigidity.  A
targeted search did not locate this exact conjunction, but that query-bounded
observation is not a priority or novelty claim.  External circulation remains
on \textbf{HOLD} pending a specialist owner review.

The note proves four concrete statements.  First, every $t$-step fibre is
uniform once the target dimension is fixed.  Second, the kernel flag gives
the complete absorption distribution.  Third, the functional graph has one
periodic vertex and an explicit local indegree.  Fourth, its depth census
recovers the field size and dimension except for the unavoidable
one-dimensional collapse.  Two proof routes are kept separate: the first
uses exact sequences and graphs of linear maps; the second slices the
Grassmannian by a hyperplane and solves the resulting recurrence.

Internally, \eqref{eq:T} is neither the rejected saturation map
$U\mapsto U+N(U)$ nor Jordan-block substitution discrepancy, invertible
shear on integer sublattices, or adjugate dynamics on a full matrix phase.
Here the states are all finite-field subspaces and the headline invariant is
the full transient fibre census.

\section{Setup and exact iterates}

For $0\leq a\leq b$, write
\[
 \qbinom{b}{a}
 =\prod_{i=0}^{a-1}\frac{q^{b-i}-1}{q^{a-i}-1},
 \qquad
 G_b(q)=\sum_{a=0}^{b}\qbinom{b}{a}.
\]
We set $\qbinom{b}{a}=0$ when $a<0$ or $a>b$.  Thus $G_b(q)$ is the number
of all subspaces of $\F_q^b$.

Choose a Jordan basis $e_1,\ldots,e_d$ with
\[
 Ne_1=0,\qquad Ne_i=e_{i-1}\quad(2\leq i\leq d).
\]
For $t\geq0$, put
\[
 K_t=\ker N^t,\qquad I_t=\im N^t.
\]
When $0\leq t\leq d$, one has $\dim K_t=t$ and $\dim I_t=d-t$.

\begin{proposition}[Iterates and rank loss]
\label{prop:iterate}
For every $U\leq V$ and $t\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:iterate}
 T^t(U)=N^t(U).
\end{equation}
If $0\leq t\leq d$, then
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:ranknullity}
 \dim T^t(U)=\dim U-\dim(U\cap K_t).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The image of a subspace under a composition of linear maps is the iterated
image, so induction gives \eqref{eq:iterate}.  The kernel of the restricted
map $N^t|_U$ is $U\cap K_t$.  Applying rank--nullity to this restriction
gives \eqref{eq:ranknullity}.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact fibres and dimension transitions}

The first main result refines the absorption census down to each target
subspace.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform iterated fibres]
\label{thm:fibre}
Let $0\leq t\leq d$, let $W\leq I_t$ have dimension $s$, and let
$0\leq r\leq d$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:fibre}
 \#\{U\in\Gr(r,V):N^t(U)=W\}
 =\qbinom{t}{r-s}q^{s(t-r+s)}.
\end{equation}
If $W\nleq I_t$, the fibre is empty.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[First proof: quotient-fibre geometry]
The last assertion follows from $N^t(U)\leq I_t$.  Assume $W\leq I_t$ and
let $K=K_t$.  For any subspace in the displayed fibre, rank--nullity gives
\[
 k:=\dim(U\cap K)=r-s.
\]
If $k<0$ or $k>t$, the fibre is empty and the Gaussian coefficient on the
right of \eqref{eq:fibre} is zero.  Hence assume $0\leq k\leq t$.
There are $\qbinom{t}{k}$ choices for $R=U\cap K$.  Fix one such $R$.
The restriction of $N^t$ to $E=(N^t)^{-1}(W)$ gives an exact sequence
\[
 0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow E\xrightarrow{N^t}W\longrightarrow0.
\]
Choose a linear section $\sigma\colon W\to E$ and a complement of $R$ in
$K$.  Every $U$ with $U\cap K=R$ and $N^tU=W$ is then uniquely of the form
\[
 U=R\oplus\{\sigma(w)+\widetilde f(w):w\in W\},
\]
where $f\colon W\to K/R$ is linear and $\widetilde f$ is its representative
in the chosen complement.  Conversely, each such graph has the required
intersection and image.  Since $\dim(K/R)=t-k$, the number of graphs is
$q^{s(t-k)}$.  Substituting $k=r-s$ proves \eqref{eq:fibre}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Joint rank transition]
\label{cor:transition}
For $0\leq t\leq d$ and $0\leq r,s\leq d$,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:transition}
 \#\{U\in\Gr(r,V):\dim N^t(U)=s\}
 =\qbinom{t}{r-s}\qbinom{d-t}{s}q^{(t-r+s)s}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
There are $\qbinom{d-t}{s}$ possible $s$-subspaces $W\leq I_t$.
Multiplying this number by \eqref{eq:fibre} gives the result.
\end{proof}

Summing \eqref{eq:fibre} over the possible input dimensions also gives every
iterated indegree.

\begin{corollary}[Local functional-graph census]
\label{cor:indegree}
If $W\leq I_t$ has dimension $s$, then
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:tindegree}
 \#(T^t)^{-1}(W)
 =\sum_{k=0}^{t}\qbinom{t}{k}q^{s(t-k)}.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:indegree}
 \#T^{-1}(W)=q^s+1\quad(W\leq I_1),
\end{equation}
whereas a subspace outside $I_t$ has no $t$-step predecessor.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In \eqref{eq:fibre}, put $k=r-s$ and sum over $0\leq k\leq t$.
For $t=1$, the two terms are $q^s$ and $1$.
\end{proof}

\section{Absorption, periodic points, and zeta}

Define the absorption depth
\[
 \depth(U)=\min\{t\geq0:T^t(U)=0\},
\]
so that $\depth(0)=0$.  Let $B_t$ denote the number of subspaces absorbed by
time $t$.

\begin{theorem}[Complete depth census]
\label{thm:depth}
For every $t\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:cdf}
 B_t=G_{\min(t,d)}(q).
\end{equation}
Consequently the number $A_t$ of points of exact depth $t$ is
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:layers}
 A_0=1,
 \qquad
 A_t=G_t(q)-G_{t-1}(q)\quad(1\leq t\leq d),
\end{equation}
and $A_t=0$ for $t>d$.  The maximum depth is exactly $d$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:iterate}, a subspace is absorbed by time $t$ precisely when it
is contained in $K_t$.  The kernel has dimension $\min(t,d)$, so the number
of its subspaces is \eqref{eq:cdf}.  Taking successive differences gives
\eqref{eq:layers}.  Finally, $A_d>0$ because $K_{d-1}$ is a proper subspace
of $V$; equivalently, a line generated by a vector $v$ with
$N^{d-1}v\neq0$ has depth $d$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Periodic census and zeta]
\label{thm:zeta}
Zero is the unique periodic point.  Hence, for every $n\geq1$,
\[
 \#\Fix(T^n)=1,
\]
and the Artin--Mazur zeta function is
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:zeta}
 \zeta_T(z)
 =\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{\#\Fix(T^n)}{n}z^n\right)
 =\frac{1}{1-z}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $T^n(U)=U$ for some $n\geq1$.  Iterating this equality gives
$N^{mn}U=U$ for every $m\geq1$.  Choose $m$ with $mn\geq d$.  Then
$N^{mn}=0$, so $U=0$.  The fixed counts follow, and substituting them into
the definition of the Artin--Mazur zeta function
\citep{ArtinMazur1965} yields \eqref{eq:zeta}.
\end{proof}

Thus the functional graph is one rooted in-tree with a loop at zero.  Its
global depth profile is \eqref{eq:layers}, while \eqref{eq:indegree} records
the local branching at every vertex that lies in the first image.

\section{Rigidity and the one-dimensional collapse}

The temporal census distinguishes the family except at a genuine boundary.

\begin{theorem}[Dynamical isomorphism classification]
\label{thm:rigidity}
Let $q,q'$ be prime powers and $d,d'\geq1$.  The finite maps
$T_{q,d,N}$ and $T_{q',d',N'}$, for regular nilpotent $N,N'$, are conjugate
if and only if either
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $q=q'$ and $d=d'$, or
 \item $d=d'=1$.
\end{enumerate}
In particular, for $d\geq2$ the depth census recovers
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:recovery}
 d=\max_U\depth(U),
 \qquad
 q=B_2-3.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All regular nilpotent endomorphisms of a fixed $d$-dimensional vector space
are similar, and similarity induces a conjugacy of their subspace maps.
Thus the first condition is sufficient.

Conversely, a dynamical conjugacy sends the unique fixed point to the unique
fixed point and preserves every absorption layer.  By \cref{thm:depth}, the
largest nonempty layer recovers $d$.  If $d\geq2$, then
\[
 B_2=G_2(q)=1+(q+1)+1=q+3,
\]
so the same census recovers $q$.  If $d=1$, the lattice consists only of
$0$ and $V$, with both vertices mapped to $0$, independently of $q$.
Hence all and only the one-dimensional systems form the exceptional
conjugacy class.
\end{proof}

The exception is necessary rather than technical: neither phase size nor
any temporal statistic can distinguish the ground fields in dimension one.

\section{A second proof route}

We give a hyperplane recurrence for \cref{cor:transition}.  Besides providing
an independent proof, it is the algebraic pattern used by the RREF control.
Fix a $t$-subspace $K\leq\F_q^d$ and define
\[
 C_{d,t}(r,k)
 =\#\{U\in\Gr(r,\F_q^d):\dim(U\cap K)=k\}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Hyperplane recurrence]
\label{prop:recurrence}
For $d>t$,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:recurrence}
 C_{d,t}(r,k)
 =C_{d-1,t}(r,k)+q^{d-r}C_{d-1,t}(r-1,k),
\end{equation}
with boundary
$C_{t,t}(r,k)=\qbinom{t}{r}$ when $k=r$ and zero otherwise.  Its solution is
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:intersection}
 C_{d,t}(r,k)
 =\qbinom{t}{k}\qbinom{d-t}{r-k}q^{(t-k)(r-k)}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose a hyperplane $H$ that contains $K$.  The $r$-subspaces contained in
$H$ contribute the first term of \eqref{eq:recurrence}.  If $U\nleq H$, then
$U_0=U\cap H$ has dimension $r-1$ and satisfies
$U_0\cap K=U\cap K$.  For a fixed $U_0$, the possible $U$ correspond to
lines in $\F_q^d/U_0$ that do not lie in $H/U_0$.  Their number is
\[
 \qbinom{d-r+1}{1}-\qbinom{d-r}{1}=q^{d-r},
\]
which proves the recurrence and its boundary.

It remains to check the solution.  The Gaussian Pascal identity
\[
 \qbinom{n}{j}
 =\qbinom{n-1}{j}+q^{n-j}\qbinom{n-1}{j-1}
\]
shows by substitution that \eqref{eq:intersection} satisfies
\eqref{eq:recurrence}; it also matches the boundary at $d=t$.  Induction on
$d-t$ therefore proves \eqref{eq:intersection}.
\end{proof}

For $K=K_t$, \eqref{eq:ranknullity} gives $k=r-s$.  Substituting this value
in \eqref{eq:intersection} reproduces \eqref{eq:transition}, without using
the quotient-fibre parametrization in the first proof.

\section{Exact control and limitations}

The control script constructs each finite field from a polynomial basis,
enumerates every subspace from its unique reduced-row-echelon basis,
materializes its vectors, and applies the Jordan shift literally.  It then
checks every fibre indexed by $(t,W,r)$, every rank transition indexed by
$(t,r,s)$, all depth layers, all tested periods, the one-step indegree, and
the rigidity signature.  The tested lanes are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 q&\text{dimensions}\\ \hline
 2&1\text{--}6\\
 3&1\text{--}5\\
 5&1\text{--}4\\
 4&1\text{--}4\\
 8,9,16&1\text{--}3.
 \end{array}
\]
The polynomial-basis extension fields are $\F_4$, $\F_8$, $\F_9$, and
$\F_{16}$.  A fresh run registers $515{,}379$ exact assertions, and the
stored output is reproduced byte for byte.

The paper treats a single regular nilpotent block.  For a general nilpotent
operator, \cref{thm:fibre} still applies to each fixed $t$ after replacing
$t$ by $\dim\ker N^t$ and $d-t$ by $\dim\im N^t$, but the depth census then
encodes the whole Jordan partition rather than the single staircase.  That
extension is not claimed here.  Nor do the results concern the lattice of
$N$-invariant subspaces, whose enumeration belongs to the owner literature
cited above.  These boundaries, together with the unresolved specialist
owner gate, keep external status on \textbf{HOLD}.
