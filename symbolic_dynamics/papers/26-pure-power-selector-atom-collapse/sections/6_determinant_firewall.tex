\section{Full determinant and the aggregate firewall}
\label{sec:determinant}

Let
\begin{equation}
 T_\pm(x)=\sum_{i=1}^m x_i\rho_\pm(a_i),
 \label{eq:weighted-color-pencil}
\end{equation}
where the \(x_i\) are independent commuting scalars.  Independence is used
only after the ordered-word trace identities have been imposed.

\begin{theorem}[full cyclic determinant collapse]
\label{thm:determinant-collapse}
Every realization satisfying the wordwise hypothesis of
\Cref{thm:graded-rigidity} obeys, for \(r\ge1\),
\begin{equation}
 \Str T(x)^r=\sum_{i=1}^m x_i^r,
 \label{eq:pure-power-traces}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\det(I-zT_+(x))}{\det(I-zT_-(x))}
 =\prod_{i=1}^m(1-zx_i).
 \label{eq:finite-color-product}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the \(r\)th power gives
\[
 \Str T(x)^r
 =\sum_{i_1,\ldots,i_r}x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_r}
   \Str\rho(a_{i_1}\cdots a_{i_r}).
\]
Every mixed word has supertrace zero, and for each \(i\) the pure word
\(a_i^r\) has supertrace one.  This proves
\eqref{eq:pure-power-traces}.  The formal trace logarithm then gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \log\frac{\det(I-zT_+)}{\det(I-zT_-)}
 &=-\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{z^r}{r}\Str T^r\\
 &=-\sum_i\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{(zx_i)^r}{r}
 =\sum_i\log(1-zx_i),
 \end{aligned}
\]
which exponentiates to \eqref{eq:finite-color-product}.
\end{proof}

The left side of \eqref{eq:finite-color-product} is a graded determinant, or
finite-dimensional Berezinian: a quotient of two ordinary determinants.  It
is not the ordinary determinant of the ungraded block sum, which equals

\[
 \det(I-z(T_+\oplus T_-))=\det(I-zT_+)\det(I-zT_-).
\]

Radical extensions and common even/odd modules may remain in the operator
presentation, but \eqref{eq:finite-color-product} shows that the full cyclic
determinant has already forgotten them.

\subsection{Why a correct scalar product is not enough}

The converse direction fails after abelianization.  Let

\[
 R_0=E_{12},\qquad R_1=E_{23},\qquad R_2=E_{31}.
\]

Put the atom projectors plus \(R_i\) in even degree and \(R_i^T\) in odd
degree.  Transposition makes the non-atom part of every power trace of the
commuting pencil cancel.  Thus the aggregate identity

\begin{equation}
 \Str\left(\sum_{i=0}^2x_iA_i\right)^r=\sum_{i=0}^2x_i^r
 \label{eq:aggregate-passes}
\end{equation}

holds in the exact fixtures.  Yet oriented words retain information erased by
commuting variables:

\begin{equation}
 \Str(A_0A_1A_2)=1,
 \qquad
 \Str(A_2A_1A_0)=-1.
 \label{eq:oriented-witness}
\end{equation}

Both words become the monomial \(x_0x_1x_2\), so their errors cancel in
\eqref{eq:aggregate-passes}.

The implication used in the paper is therefore one way:

\[
 \text{wordwise selector}
 \Longrightarrow\text{all power traces}
 \Longrightarrow\text{determinant product},
\]

where neither reverse arrow is licensed.  This is stronger than a numerical
warning.  It is an exact algebraic counterexample to any protocol that
certifies branch purity only from a commutative Euler product.
