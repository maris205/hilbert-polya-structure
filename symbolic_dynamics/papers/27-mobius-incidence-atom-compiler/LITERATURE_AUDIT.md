## Direct classical collisions

### Incidence and Möbius foundations

Rota (1964) established the locally finite incidence-algebra framework,
zeta inversion, and the Möbius-function viewpoint used throughout this
paper. These ingredients are not new.

Solomon (1967), Greene (1973), Stanley (1970), Baclawski (1972), and
Steinberg (2006, 2008) place Möbius-produced idempotents, primitive systems,
inner automorphisms, character decompositions, and related semigroup
representation methods firmly in the classical literature. Paper27 therefore
does not claim the first Möbius idempotents or the first incidence-algebra
decomposition.

Schmitt (1994) gives broad prior art for primitive and generating structures
in incidence Hopf algebras. The phrase “incidence primitives” is not by itself
a novelty claim.

### Scalar prime extraction and necklaces

Fröberg (1968) makes prime-zeta extraction through Möbius inversion of
\(\log\zeta\) classical. It is a scalar, commutative operation. Paper27's
distinction is wordwise selection before cyclic trace, not scalar prime
extraction.

Metropolis and Rota (1983) make Möbius inversion in necklace/Witt algebra
classical. Paper27 cannot claim the first Möbius/necklace correspondence.
Its narrower contribution is that source covers, rather than primitive word
period alone, determine the surviving loop labels.

### Analytic infrastructure

Ruelle (1976) supplies classical dynamical zeta and transfer-operator
infrastructure. Sarig (1999) establishes thermodynamic formalism for
countable Markov shifts. Bandtlow and Jenkinson (2008) provide holomorphic
transfer-operator trace-ideal estimates, while Simon (1977) supplies the
trace-class determinant identities. None is novelty owned by Paper27.

## Internal predecessor boundary

von-Mangoldt prime-power ledger. It did not kill mixed or composite-letter
words before cyclic trace.
