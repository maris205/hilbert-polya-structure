\section{Related work and ownership boundary}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Carries and congruences.}
For a prime radix, carry counts are classically tied to prime-adic
valuations of binomial coefficients through Kummer's theorem
\cite{kummer1852ergaenzung}; Lucas's congruence gives a complementary
digitwise description modulo a prime \cite{lucas1878congruences}.  Those
results own the relevant arithmetic precedents.  Our source relation,
however, is simply the coordinatewise inequality \(m_j+n_j<b\), defined
for every integer \(b\ge2\).  We use the classical theorems only as
prime-radix comparators and never as a definition or proof in composite
radix.

\paragraph{Finite Pascal, Boolean, and disjointness matrices.}
Finite graphs built from binomial parity and their spectra were studied by
Christopher and Kennedy \cite{christopher1997binomial}; symmetric Pascal
matrices modulo a prime have a substantial finite theory
\cite{bacher2004pascal}.  LaGrange treats eigenvalues of Boolean graphs and
Pascal-type matrices \cite{lagrange2013eigenvalues}.  Related disjointness
matrices also occur in communication-complexity factorization norms
\cite{linial2007factorization}, rectifier-network coverings
\cite{chistikov2017fractional}, and algorithms for Kronecker powers
\cite{alman2023kronecker}.  These works establish ownership of the finite
arithmetic, graph, tensor, spectral, covering, and algorithmic mechanisms
that overlap our controls.  Accordingly, we claim no novelty for finite
digit Kronecker products, finite carry tests, finite singular values as
objects, or finite Boolean/disjointness spectra.

\paragraph{Trace ideals and determinants.}
We use the standard Schatten ideals and the usual Hilbert--Schmidt
regularized determinant \(\Dettwo\); their general theory is described, for
example, by Simon \cite{simon2005trace}.  All estimates particular to the
carry-free operator---the shell factorizations, the uniform transfer from
unweighted to weighted blocks, the two endpoint pinchings, and the
closed-walk limiting argument---are supplied below.

\paragraph{Positioning.}
The distinction relevant to this paper is between a fixed finite digit
tensor and an infinite positive-vertex operator with Dirichlet weights and
unbounded digit length.  The latter requires simultaneous control of shell
separation, digit growth, endpoint equality, and deletion of the all-zero
word.  The bounded source search performed before writing did not locate
the exact combination of quantifiers in \cref{thm:main-intro}; this
statement reports the search outcome only and does not establish priority.
