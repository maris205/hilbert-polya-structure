\begin{abstract}
For $q\geq3$, let $N_q$ be the shift on $q$ colors in which no color may
repeat at any of the next $q-2$ sites.  The constraint is one step short of
forcing a periodic permutation, and this minimal slack has three exact
consequences.  We identify the $(q-1)$-block presentation with a directed
Cayley graph on $S_q$ generated by a $(q-1)$-cycle and a $q$-cycle.  Hence
$N_q$ is mixing, has entropy $\log2$, and has a unique measure of maximal
entropy.  Its short periodic data are sharply nonmonotone:
\[
 \#\Fix(\sigma^n)=0\ (n\leq q-2),\qquad
 \#\Fix(\sigma^{q-1})=\#\Fix(\sigma^q)=q!,
\]
and, when $q\geq4$, followed by a second desert through $2q-3$.  Under the maximal measure, the
first return of a fixed color is $(q-2)+\operatorname{Geom}(1/2)$, and the
conditional return generating function is
$(2-z)/(2-z-z^{q-1})$.  The least positive period is $q-1$, so the parameter
is recovered from periodic data.  Independent state-graph and literal cyclic
word enumerations verify the finite controls registered with the note.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Local exclusion usually becomes harder as the exclusion window grows: the
natural block presentation expands rapidly, and its periodic points become a
graph-coloring problem.  This note treats the boundary case where an alphabet
of $q$ colors is constrained only across the next $q-2$ positions.  Every
legal $(q-1)$-block then consists of distinct colors, leaving exactly one
missing color and exactly two possible continuations.

That one unit of slack produces more structure than the degree count alone
suggests.  Completing a block by its missing color turns the state space into
$S_q$; the two continuations become two long position cycles.  This precise
directed Cayley graph, including its Hamiltonicity, was studied by
Ruskey and Williams \cite{RuskeyWilliams2010}; it is background here, not a
claimed graph-theoretic contribution.  The same constraint makes short
periodic words equivalent to colorings of powers of a cycle, exposing two
consecutive nonzero period counts and a sharply delimited initial zero
ledger.  Finally, the two continuation labels become fair independent choices
under the Parry measure, and the return of one color reduces to a delayed
geometric clock.

Shifts of finite type, their Parry measures, and their zeta functions are
standard tools \cite{LindMarcus1995,Parry1964}.  Cycle powers and their
colorings are also established objects \cite{ProwseWoodall2003}.  After
subtracting those owners, the result proved here is the coupled dynamical
package for the no-repeat family: its exact initial period ledger, parameter
recovery, and rational color-return renewal law.  A bounded search through
28 August 2026 did not identify a direct prior treatment of that coupled
package; the search is not a priority proof, and we make no absolute novelty
claim.

\section{The shift and its permutation presentation}

Fix $q\geq3$ and write $\mathcal A_q=\{0,1,\ldots,q-1\}$.

\begin{definition}
The minimal-slack no-repeat shift is
\[
 N_q=\{x\in\mathcal A_q^{\Z}:x_i\neq x_{i+j}
       \text{ for }1\leq j\leq q-2\text{ and every }i\in\Z\},
\]
with the left shift $\sigma$.
\end{definition}

Every legal word of length $q-1$ has distinct letters.  Such a word
$w=(a_1,\ldots,a_{q-1})$ has a unique missing color $a_q$.  We identify the
state $w$ with the bijection $p_w:[q]\to\mathcal A_q$ whose one-line notation is
\[
 \widehat w=(a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_{q-1},a_q)\in S_q,
\]
where we regard $S_q$ as the set of such bijections.

\begin{proposition}[Cayley presentation]\label{prop:cayley}
In the $(q-1)$-block presentation, each state has two successors.  Under the
completion $w\mapsto\widehat w$, they are obtained by the position cycles
\[
 \alpha=(1\ 2\ \cdots\ q-1),\qquad
 \beta=(1\ 2\ \cdots\ q).
\]
Consequently the presentation is a directed Cayley graph of $S_q$.
It is strongly connected and aperiodic.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After deleting $a_1$, the new symbol may be either $a_1$ or the previously
missing color $a_q$; every other choice repeats one of the preceding $q-2$
symbols.  Completion of the two new blocks gives, respectively,
\[
 (a_2,\ldots,a_{q-1},a_1,a_q),\qquad
 (a_2,\ldots,a_{q-1},a_q,a_1),
\]
which are exactly $p_w\circ\alpha$ and $p_w\circ\beta$.  Thus we use the
right-action convention throughout.

The element $\alpha^{-1}\beta=(q-1\ q)$ is a transposition.  Conjugating it
by powers of $\alpha$, which fixes $q$ and cycles the other positions, gives
all star transpositions $(j\ q)$, so $\alpha$ and $\beta$
generate $S_q$.  This group-generation statement also gives directed
reachability: $\alpha^{-1}=\alpha^{q-2}$ and
$\beta^{-1}=\beta^{q-1}$ are positive words in the allowed generators.
Hence the positive semigroup generated by the two labels is all of $S_q$,
and the directed graph is strongly connected.  Repeating the $\alpha$ edge gives a
closed walk of length $q-1$, while repeating the $\beta$ edge gives one of
length $q$.  Their lengths are coprime, so the graph period is one.
This is the right-action version, canonically isomorphic after the usual
left/right convention change, of the universal-cycle Cayley graph in
\cite{RuskeyWilliams2010}; we use only the elementary connectivity statement
proved above.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[mixing and maximal entropy]\label{cor:parry}
The shift $N_q$ is mixing and
\[
 h_{\mathrm{top}}(N_q)=\log2.
\]
It has a unique measure of maximal entropy $\mu_q$.  In its block
presentation, $\mu_q$ is uniform on the $q!$ states and assigns probability
$1/2$ to each outgoing edge.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The presentation is primitive by \cref{prop:cayley}.  It is both
two-out-regular and two-in-regular, so the all-ones vector is a left and
right Perron vector with eigenvalue two.  The claims follow from the Parry
measure construction \cite{Parry1964}.
\end{proof}

\section{The short-period desert}

Put $F_q(n)=\#\Fix(\sigma^n|_{N_q})$.  An $n$-periodic point is a coloring
of the cyclic positions $\Z/n\Z$ such that positions at positive directed
gap $j\in\{1,\ldots,q-2\}$ receive different colors.  Equivalently, two
distinct positions are adjacent when their shorter cyclic distance is at
most $q-2$; if some forbidden positive gap is $0$ modulo $n$, the graph has a
loop and no coloring is allowed.  Thus this is the usual cycle-power coloring
interpretation, with the small-$n$ loop convention made explicit.

\begin{theorem}[two islands and a desert]\label{thm:desert}
For every $q\geq3$,
\begin{align*}
 F_q(n)&=0 &&(1\leq n\leq q-2),\\
 F_q(q-1)&=F_q(q)=q!,\\
 F_q(n)&=0 &&(q+1\leq n\leq2q-3).
\end{align*}
Thus
\[
 \min\{n\geq1:F_q(n)>0\}=q-1,
\]
and the family parameter is recovered as one plus this conjugacy-invariant
minimum.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $n\leq q-2$, the constraint with gap $j=n$ compares each cyclic position
with itself, so no periodic point exists.  For $n=q-1$, every pair of
positions is constrained and all $q-1$ colors in the word must be distinct;
there are $q!$ ordered choices.  For $n=q$, every pair is again constrained:
if one directed cyclic distance is $q-1$, the reverse distance is one.
Hence all $q$ positions use distinct colors, in $q!$ ways.

Finally, suppose $q+1\leq n\leq2q-3$; for $q=3$ this interval is empty.  The shorter cyclic distance between
any two positions is at most $\lfloor n/2\rfloor\leq q-2$.  The cycle power
is therefore complete.  It would require $n>q$ distinct colors, which is
impossible.  The recovery statement is immediate.
\end{proof}

The theorem concerns the initial periodic ledger, not all periods.  Beyond
$2q-3$ the cycle power is no longer complete and additional periodic points
appear.  Exact controls below deliberately test only what the theorem claims.

\section{Exact color returns under the Parry measure}

Fix a color $c$ and condition on $x_0=c$.  Let
\[
 \tau_c=\min\{n\geq1:x_n=c\}
\]
be its first positive return time.  We use the convention that
$G\sim\operatorname{Geom}(1/2)$ has support $\{1,2,\ldots\}$ and
$\Prob(G=k)=2^{-k}$.

\begin{theorem}[delayed geometric return]\label{thm:return}
Under $\mu_q(\,\cdot\mid x_0=c)$,
\[
 \tau_c\ \stackrel{d}=\ (q-2)+\operatorname{Geom}(1/2).
\]
Successive return gaps to $c$ are independent with this law.  If
\[
 u_n=\mu_q(x_n=c\mid x_0=c),
\]
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:returngf}
 \sum_{n\geq0}u_nz^n=\frac{2-z}{2-z-z^{q-1}}.
\end{equation}
In particular, for the color indicator $Y_i=\mathbf1_{\{x_i=c\}}$,
\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\mu_q}(Y_0,Y_n)
 =\frac1q\left(u_n-\frac1q\right),
\]
and the entire correlation sequence is determined by the denominator in
\eqref{eq:returngf}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under the uniform stationary distribution on states, each outgoing label is
chosen with conditional probability $1/2$, independently of the current
state.  Consequently the sequence of Cayley-edge labels is an i.i.d. fair
Bernoulli sequence (and the time-zero state is uniform).  The exclusion rule
forbids a return during the first $q-2$ steps.  At time
$q-1$, the color $c$ is the oldest color in the current legal block.  One
of the two equiprobable continuations appends it; the other appends the
missing color and makes $c$ the new missing color.  Conditional on not
returning, at every subsequent step one continuation is $c$ and the other
is the oldest block color.  The independent fair edge choices from
\cref{cor:parry} therefore give the stated delayed geometric law.

At a return, the return time is a stopping time for the edge-label filtration.
The unused i.i.d. labels after that stopping time are independent of its past,
and the same state-independent argument restarts.  Thus visits to $c$ form a
renewal process with independent, identically distributed gaps.  Its
first-return generating function is
\[
 f(z)=\sum_{k\geq1}2^{-k}z^{q-2+k}
     =\frac{z^{q-1}}{2-z}.
\]
The renewal identity $u(z)=1+f(z)u(z)$ gives \eqref{eq:returngf}.  Uniformity
of the stationary state distribution gives $\mu_q(x_0=c)=1/q$, from which
the covariance formula follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The pole at $z=1$ in \eqref{eq:returngf} records the limiting value $1/q$.
After subtracting it, the other roots of $2-z-z^{q-1}$ give an exact finite
linear recurrence for the correlation decay.  No spectral diagonalization
of the full $q!$-state matrix is needed.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact controls and scope}

The registered script constructs all $q!$ block states for $3\leq q\leq6$,
checks the right-action orientation, the transposition identity, positive-word
inverses, both in- and out-degree two, and forward and reverse connectivity.
It computes adjacency traces through $2q-3$ by sparse dynamic programming; a
second path enumerates literal cyclic words for $3\leq q\leq5$.  Finally,
exact rational evolution from every eligible marked state is compared with
the delayed-geometric first-return law and its two-gap renewal factorization.
These are finite falsification controls; the proofs above carry the general
statements.
