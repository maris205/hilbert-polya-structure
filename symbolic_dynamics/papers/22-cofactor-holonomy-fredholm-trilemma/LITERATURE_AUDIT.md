# Literature Audit — SD-C24

**Search date:** 2026-08-14  
**Scope:** symbolic zeta functions, twisted dynamical determinants, voltage
graphs and graph covers, group extensions of shifts, cocycle cohomology,
weighted graph zetas, and trace ideals  
**Priority status:** search-bounded; no absolute priority claim

## 1. Search protocol

The research pass covered combinations of:

- successor–divisor graph and the relation \(d\mid n+1\);
- quotient or cofactor cocycles on countable Markov shifts;
- multiplicative holonomy and positive-monoid group completion;
- voltage, gain, and group-labeled graph zeta functions;
- character-resolved dynamical determinants;
- identity-holonomy traces in regular graph extensions;
- weighted infinite-graph Fredholm determinants;
- recent 2024–2026 work on generalized weighted graph zetas.

Primary or publisher-hosted records were used wherever available.  The
search found established frameworks for every piece of general machinery,
but no source stating the candidate-specific combination

\[
 Q(\gamma)=\prod(1+1/n),
 \qquad
 Q=2\iff\gamma=C_k,
\]

the atomic classification \(C_{k,p}\), or the sharp two-parameter
\(\mathcal S_1\) domain for this successor–divisor adjacency.  This is a
bounded non-collision result, not proof of global novelty.

## 2. Determinants and periodic-orbit ledgers

Bowen and Lanford established the determinant/cycle-expansion paradigm for
restrictions of shifts.  Parry and Pollicott systematized dynamical zeta and
periodic-orbit methods for hyperbolic dynamics.  These sources justify the
standard relation between a trace logarithm, rooted periodic points,
primitive classes, and repetitions.

SD-C24 uses only the local Fredholm trace logarithm after trace class has
been proved for its concrete \(\ell^2\) weighted adjacency.  It does not
claim the countable-state system falls under a compact finite-type theorem,
nor does it import analytic continuation from a classical dynamical zeta.

Simon provides the standard trace-ideal and infinite-determinant facts used
after the candidate-specific nuclear estimate.  The sharp two-half-plane
threshold is not taken from Simon; it is proved by the output-row
decomposition and successor Fourier extraction.

## 3. Twists, voltages, and graph covers

Adachi and Sunada developed twisted Perron–Frobenius operators and dynamical
\(L\)-functions.  Gross and Tucker introduced systematic voltage-assignment
methods for graph coverings.  Stark and Terras connected finite graph covers,
multivariable cycle weights, and graph zeta functions.  These are the closest
classical ancestors of the character and group-extension vocabulary.

The novelty boundary is strict.  SD-C24 does not claim to invent twisted
determinants, voltage graphs, character fibers, or coefficient extraction.
Its contribution is the exact cofactor voltage generated by the frozen
successor–tensor relation and the model-specific consequences of that
voltage.

## 4. Infinite covers and group traces

Clair studies \(L^2\)-zeta functions for graphs with infinite cyclic actions,
and Sharp studies zeta approximation for amenable group extensions of shifts.
They establish nearby uses of group extensions and traces per fundamental
domain.  SD-C24's regular deck group
\(\Gamma=\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times}\) is countable abelian, but its concrete
operator and conclusion differ: the identity sector contains no periodic
path and the normalized local semifinite determinant is one.

The manuscript therefore distinguishes two independent facts:

1. ordinary noncompactness on the lifted Hilbert space due to infinite deck
   multiplicity;
2. \(L^1\)-integrability relative to the semifinite trace on
   \(B(\ell^2(V))\bar\otimes L(\Gamma)\).

No ordinary Fredholm determinant is claimed for the regular lift.

## 5. Cocycle cohomology

Livšic theory relates cocycle cohomology to periodic data under appropriate
dynamical hypotheses; Kalinin provides a matrix-cocycle theorem in a
hyperbolic setting.  SD-C24 cites this only as conceptual context.  Its gauge
identity

\[
 \log q(n,d)=\log n-\log d+\log(1+1/n)
\]

is an elementary exact calculation and is proved directly.  No Livšic
regularity theorem is invoked for the countable graph.

## 6. Recent weighted graph-zeta adjacency

Ishikawa and Morita give exponential, Euler, Hashimoto, and Ihara expressions
for a generalized weighted zeta on finite digraphs.  This is relevant recent
machinery and is cited as adjacent work.  It does not analyze the present
countable successor–divisor graph, the cofactor classifications, or the
two-parameter Schatten boundary.

## 7. Positioning table

| Source family | Shared ingredient | Boundary relative to SD-C24 |
|---|---|---|
| Bowen–Lanford / Parry–Pollicott | trace, primitive, and repetition ledgers | general finite/hyperbolic zeta framework; no cofactor graph |
| Simon | trace ideals and Fredholm determinants | standard analytic machinery after trace class is proved |
| Adachi–Sunada | twisted operators and dynamical \(L\)-functions | no successor–divisor cocycle or atomic classification |
| Gross–Tucker | voltage graph covers | combinatorial extension language; no countable weighted operator theorem |
| Stark–Terras | cover zetas and multivariable weights | finite graph-cover setting |
| Clair / Sharp | infinite/group extensions and zeta traces | different groups, operators, and recurrence conclusions |
| Kalinin | periodic data and cocycle cohomology | general hyperbolic theorem; candidate gauge is elementary |
| Ishikawa–Morita | generalized weighted finite-digraph zeta | finite graph; no sharp \(\mathcal S_1\) phase diagram |

## 8. Safe novelty statement

The manuscript uses the bounded formulation:

> To our knowledge, based on searches of primary and publisher records
> through 2026-08-14, we found no directly comparable analysis of the
> successor–divisor cofactor cocycle that combines the exact atomic-holonomy
> classification with the sharp two-parameter trace-class domain proved
> here.

It does not use “first,” “only,” or “no prior work” as an unqualified claim.
