\begin{abstract}
The orthocentric-quartet recurrence is classical, and metrical triangle
geometry over finite fields is established; both receive zero contribution
credit here.  We totalize the sliding window
$(A,B,C)\mapsto(B,C,H(A,B,C))$ over $\mathbb F_p^2$, for primes
$p\equiv3\pmod4$, by sending a degenerate next window to a sink.  The
residual singular dynamics has a complete orientation-sensitive atlas.
Nonright triangles form exact four-cycles.  A triangle right-angled at its
first listed vertex has depth two, whereas right angles at the second or
third vertex have depth one.  We count all three strata, identify the stable
image, and give the fibre of every target: zero over the first two oriented
right-angle classes, one over the third and over every nonright triangle,
and $1+2p^2(p^2-1)(p-1)$ over the sink.  At $p=3$ the periodic triangle core
is empty but the height remains two.  Exact enumeration at $p=3,7$ checks
$1{,}317{,}843$ assertions, including every state, target, and reverse
window.
\end{abstract}

\section{Owned geometry, totalized map, and theorem}\label{sec:setup}

Kocik and Solecki explicitly describe the four vertices of an orthocentric
system, each point being the orthocenter of the triangle formed by the other
three~\cite{KocikSolecki2009}.  Wildberger develops affine metrical triangle
geometry and orthocenters over finite fields~\cite{Wildberger2008}.  Guy also
treats a triangle as an orthocentric quadrangle, but his iterative
``trisequence'' is generated by Steiner-line reflections and circumcircle
intersections, not by the sliding window below~\cite{Guy2019}.  Accordingly,
the orthocentric identity, its four-window recurrence, and the finite-field
setting are background only.

Let $p\equiv3\pmod4$ be prime.  On $V=\Fp^2$ use
\[
 (x,y)\mathbin\cdot(u,v)=xu+yv.
\]
An ordered triple $(A,B,C)$ is a triangle when its points are noncollinear.
Its orthocenter $H(A,B,C)$ is the unique solution of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:altitudes}
 (H-A)\cdot(B-C)=0,\qquad (H-B)\cdot(A-C)=0.
\end{equation}
Let $\X$ contain every ordered triangle and one sink $\dagger$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:map}
 F(A,B,C)=
 \begin{cases}
 (B,C,H(A,B,C)),&B,C,H(A,B,C)\text{ are noncollinear},\\
 \dagger,&\text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \qquad F(\dagger)=\dagger.
\end{equation}
A triangle is \emph{right at coordinate $i$} when its two side vectors from
the $i$th listed vertex are perpendicular.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:counts}
 T=p^2(p^2-1)(p^2-p),\quad
 R=p^2(p^2-1)(p-1),\quad
 Q=T-3R=p^2(p^2-1)(p-1)(p-3).
\end{equation}
For a state $x$, let $d(x)$ be its distance to the recurrent set.

\begin{theorem}[singular and fibre atlas]\label{thm:atlas}
The map $F$ has the following complete description.
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
\item There are $T$ ordered triangles and exactly $R$ right triangles at
each specified coordinate.  The three right-angle classes are disjoint.
The $Q$ nonright triangles have exact period four; the sink is fixed.

\item A triangle right at coordinate one has depth two.  A triangle right at
coordinate two or three has depth one.  Hence the sharp height is two, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:depth-cdf}
 \#\{x:d(x)\leq0\}=1+Q,\quad
 \#\{x:d(x)\leq1\}=1+T-R,\quad
 \#\{x:d(x)\leq2\}=1+T.
\end{equation}
The dynamical zeta function is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeta}
 \zetaF(z)=(1-z)^{-1}(1-z^4)^{-Q/4}.
\end{equation}

\item The one-step target fibres are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibres}
 \#F^{-1}(y)=
 \begin{cases}
 1+2R,&y=\dagger,\\
 0,&y\text{ is right at coordinate one or two},\\
 1,&y\text{ is right at coordinate three or is nonright}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{align}
 \im F&=\{\dagger\}\cup\{\text{nonright triangles}\}
       \cup\{\text{triangles right at coordinate three}\},
       \label{eq:image-one}\\
 \#\im F&=1+T-2R,\qquad
 F^2(\X)=\{\dagger\}\cup\{\text{nonright triangles}\},
 \label{eq:stable-image}\\
 \#F^2(\X)&=1+Q,\qquad F^t(\X)=F^2(\X)\quad(t\geq2).
\end{align}

\item At $p=3$, $T=432$, $R=144$, and $Q=0$.  Thus the sink is the only
recurrent state, while the depth-two shell still contains $144$ triangles.
The one-step image has size $145$, the stable image has size one, and the
sink fibre has size $289$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\section{Anisotropy and the right-angle strata}\label{sec:counts}

Because $p\equiv3\pmod4$, $-1$ is not a square.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anisotropy}
 x^2+y^2=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad (x,y)=(0,0).
\end{equation}
Indeed, if $y\neq0$ then $(x/y)^2=-1$, and if $y=0$ then $x=0$.
Thus the dot product is anisotropic.  A nonzero vector $(x,y)$ has
perpendicular line spanned by $(-y,x)$, and no nonzero perpendicular vector
is parallel to it.

There are $p^2$ choices for $A$, then $p^2-1$ choices for $B-A$, and
$p^2-p$ choices for $C$ off the affine line $AB$.  This proves the formula
for $T$.  To count triangles right at $A$, choose $A$, a nonzero vector
$u=B-A$, and one of the $p-1$ nonzero vectors $v=C-A$ on $u^\perp$.
Anisotropy makes $u,v$ independent, giving $R$.

The three right classes cannot overlap.  For example, if
$u=B-A$ and $v=C-A$ made the triangle right at both $A$ and $B$, then
\[
 u\cdot v=0,\qquad (-u)\cdot(v-u)=0
\]
would imply $u\cdot u=0$, contradicting \eqref{eq:anisotropy}.  The other
pairs follow by relabeling.  Subtracting the three disjoint classes proves
the formula for $Q$ in \eqref{eq:counts}.  The altitude equations
\eqref{eq:altitudes} have a unique solution because the two opposite-side
vectors are independent and the dot pairing is nondegenerate.

\section{Four windows and oriented singular depths}\label{sec:forward}

We include an algebraic verification of the owned orthocentric identity only
to close the field-specific proof.  From \eqref{eq:altitudes}, subtracting the
two zero scalar products gives
$(H-C)\cdot(A-B)=0$.  Thus $H$ lies on all three altitudes.  Conversely,
the same three perpendicularities show that $A$ lies on the altitudes of
$(B,C,H)$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quartet}
 H(B,C,H(A,B,C))=A
\end{equation}
whenever that next triangle is noncollinear; cyclic variants hold as well.

If the original triangle is nonright, $H$ is distinct from $A,B,C$.  No
three of the four points are collinear: otherwise the altitude equations and
anisotropy force the alleged third point to coincide with an endpoint.
Equation~\eqref{eq:quartet} therefore produces the valid windows
\begin{equation}\label{eq:four-windows}
 (A,B,C),\ (B,C,H),\ (C,H,A),\ (H,A,B),\ (A,B,C).
\end{equation}
No return at time one, two, or three is compatible with the three distinct
ordered vertices.  The period is exactly four.  This classical core receives
no contribution credit.

For a right triangle the orthocenter is its right-angle vertex.  If it is
right at $A$, the first successor is the cyclic rotation $(B,C,A)$, which is
right at its third coordinate; its next successor is degenerate and hence
$\dagger$.  If the original triangle is right at $B$ or $C$, the first next
window already repeats that coordinate and goes to $\dagger$.  This proves
the oriented depths, sharp height, stable recurrent set, and
\eqref{eq:depth-cdf}.  There is one fixed cycle and $Q/4$ four-cycles, which
gives \eqref{eq:zeta} by the standard finite-map product formula.

\section{The unique reverse window and all fibres}\label{sec:inverse}

The fibre law is not obtained by dividing orbit counts.  Let $(A,B,C)$ be a
target triangle.  Any nonsink predecessor has the form $(D,A,B)$ and must
satisfy $H(D,A,B)=C$.  Orthocentric symmetry then forces the unique candidate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reverse}
 (D,A,B)=(H(A,B,C),A,B).
\end{equation}
If the target is right at $A$, its orthocenter equals $A$, so
\eqref{eq:reverse} repeats $A$ and is invalid.  If it is right at $B$, the
candidate repeats $B$ and is again invalid.  For a target right at $C$, the
candidate is the cyclic rotation $(C,A,B)$ and maps to the target.  For a
nonright target, the four points in \eqref{eq:four-windows} are distinct with
no collinear triple, so the candidate is valid and maps to the target.
This proves the triangle cases of \eqref{eq:fibres} directly.

The immediate predecessors of $\dagger$ are $\dagger$ itself and precisely
the triangles right at coordinate two or three.  These two disjoint classes
have total size $2R$, proving the sink fibre.  Reading the positive fibres
gives \eqref{eq:image-one}; applying the oriented depth result once more gives
\eqref{eq:stable-image}.  This completes Theorem~\ref{thm:atlas}.

\section{Exact controls and claim ceiling}\label{sec:controls}

The paper-local verifier exhausts all $433$ states at $p=3$ and all
$98{,}785$ states at $p=7$.  It solves every orthocenter over the field,
checks anisotropy and all altitude equations, classifies every oriented right
angle, follows every orbit, reconstructs every reverse window, and compares
all target fibres and images with Theorem~\ref{thm:atlas}.  Its deterministic
transcript contains $1{,}317{,}843$ exact assertions.  These computations are
finite counterexample pressure; Sections~\ref{sec:counts}--\ref{sec:inverse}
prove the all-prime result.

The theorem requires prime $p\equiv3\pmod4$ and this dot product.  It does
not cover isotropic primes, arbitrary quadratic forms, higher dimensions, or
perturbed centers.  A bounded search did not locate the singular sliding-
window fibre conjunction, but that non-hit is not a novelty, priority,
ownership-completeness, or release certificate.  The finite-field geometry,
orthocenter, and orthocentric four-cycle remain zero-credit background.
