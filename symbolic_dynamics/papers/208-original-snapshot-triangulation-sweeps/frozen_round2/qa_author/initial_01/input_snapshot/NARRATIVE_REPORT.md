## One technical story

A complete sweep of the original diagonals of a labelled convex polygon,
in lexicographic order, has a recursive protected-cell dictionary. That
dictionary both decodes every labelled one-step predecessor and transports
iteration through a size-preserving map K to a unique alternating core.

## Exactly two headline claims

1. Every target has a disjoint, operational source-set decoder, not merely
   a cardinality formula. Its evaluated fibres are zero or powers of two;
   for n >= 5 the unique maximum target is the vertex-1 fan, with 2^(n-4)
   predecessors. Triangle and quadrilateral fibres are one.
2. The triangle is fixed; every n >= 4 carrier has exactly one two-cycle.
   Maximum entrance is zero for n = 3,4 and exactly n-2 for n >= 5.
   The proof requires the stronger K closed-class inclusion, both square
   phases, commutation for the odd-phase bound, and both witness parities.

## Deductive evidence and source subtraction

The original 400-line author proof is the all-parameter evidence. Its
protected-cell proof includes original roots of nonleaf branches in each
smaller cell and preserves the lexicographic order under increasing labels.
The inverse induction treats zero, comb, left-length one and two, and
forced longer-comb cases separately. The clock is not a fit to a finite
table. The literal edge-labelled lift, ordinary rotations, deterministic
scheduling, independent binary cuts, generic prefix freezing, and the
static Catalan/avoidance series receive no standalone credit. No claim is
made about every possible composed adapter, arbitrary schedules, global
priority, all-time fibres or basin enumerations.
