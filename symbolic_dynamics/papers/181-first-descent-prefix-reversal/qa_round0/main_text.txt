                                   A COMPLETE DEPTH-TWO ATLAS FOR
                                    FIRST-DESCENT PREFIX REVERSAL


                                                            ANONYMOUS


            Abstract. Given a nonidentity permutation, locate its first descent and reverse the prefix ending at the
            descent’s smaller follower. We determine the complete functional graph of this autonomous map. Its image
            is the half of Sn beginning with an ascent. For n ≥ 3, the recurrent set is the identity together with the n!/3
            permutations having a peak at position two; the latter form n!/6 two-cycles. Every tail has length at most
            two, with exact populations n!/3 + 1, n!/2, n!/6 − 1. The full predecessor set of a target is indexed by the
            decreasing run starting at its second position. Consequently, for n ≥ 4 the maximum fibre is n − 1, attained
            at exactly n − 1 targets. The exceptional n = 2, 3 atlases are explicit. Arbitrary prefix reversal, pancake
            sorting, descent counts, and finite-map bookkeeping are treated as background. The residual conjunction
            is owner_amber, and external circulation is hold_external.

(1)                                    ρk (π1 · · · πk πk+1 · · · πn ) = πk · · · π1 πk+1 · · · πn .
Write ι = 12 · · · n. If π ̸= ι, let
(2)                                                 d(π) = min{i : πi > πi+1 }

(3)                                     Fn (ι) = ι,        Fn (π) = ρd(π)+1 (π)         (π ̸= ι).
The extra position after the first descent is part of the rule.
   Prefix reversal and its pancake-sorting distance are classical [1]; prefix-reversal sorting on fixed-alphabet
strings is also established [2]. Pudwell and Smith study four shuffle maps selected by a cut after the longest
increasing prefix [3], but those maps do not reverse the full prefix in (3). We assign arbitrary prefix reversal,
pancake graphs and distances, longest-increasing-prefix selection, elementary descent and peak counts, and
generic finite-map bookkeeping no contribution credit.
   There is also a sharp negative control. The rule called “First Sort” in Project Euler Problems 523–524
moves the follower πd+1 to the front; it does not reverse the selected prefix.1 For example, it sends 1324 to
2134, whereas (3) sends 1324 to 2314. We therefore do not use “First Sort” as a name for Fn .

                                      2. Half-image and recurrent core
Proof of Theorem 1(i). If d = d(π), the first two entries of Fn (π) are πd+1 < πd . Thus every nonfixed
output begins with an ascent; so does ι. Hence im Fn ⊆ In .
   Conversely, take τ ∈ In and put π = ρ2 (τ ). The first comparison of π is the descent τ2 > τ1 , so
Fn (π) = ρ2 (π) = τ . This proves equality. Swapping the first two entries is a fixed-point-free involution
pairing permutations in In with those beginning with a descent, and therefore |In | = n!/2.               □
Proof of Theorem 1(ii). For τ ∈ Pn , the first descent is at position two. Hence Fn (τ ) = ρ3 (τ ). The new
first three entries are τ3 < τ2 > τ1 , so the image is again in Pn ; applying ρ3 once more returns to τ . The
two states are distinct because their first and third entries cannot agree.
    Now let τ ∈ In \ Rn . Since it is neither a peak at two nor the identity, its first three entries increase and
its first descent occurs at some d ≥ 3. The first three entries of Fn (τ ) are
(10)                                              τd+1 < τd > τd−1 ,
so Fn (τ ) ∈ Pn . Every state first enters In by part (i), and every image state then lies in or enters Rn . This
proves that no other cycle exists.
   Among the six relative orders of the first three entries, exactly two put their maximum in the middle.
Each order occurs for n!/6 permutations, so |Pn | = n!/3. Pairing by ρ3 gives n!/6 two-cycles.                 □

                                   3. Target fibres and exact tail census
Lemma 2 (decreasing-run inverse). For τ ∈ In and 2 ≤ k ≤ n, the state ρk (τ ) has first descent at position
k − 1 if and only if k ≤ r(τ ) + 1. These valid states are distinct as k varies.
Proof. The first k entries of ρk (τ ) are
(11)                                             τk , τk−1 , . . . , τ2 , τ1 .
They increase through position k − 1 precisely when τ2 > τ3 > · · · > τk . The next comparison is then the
descent τ2 > τ1 , which holds because τ ∈ In . This condition is equivalent to k ≤ r(τ ) + 1. Different k give
different first entries τk , proving distinctness.                                                          □
Proof of Theorem 1(iv). Part (i) excludes predecessors for targets outside In . Suppose τ ∈ In . Every
nonfixed predecessor π has some first descent d and satisfies τ = ρd+1 (π); since prefix reversal is an involution,

every such value. The only fixed state is ι, which contributes one further predecessor exactly when τ = ι.
For that target, ρ2 (ι) is distinct from ι, so (8) follows.                                                      □

                                 4. Sharp fibres and the small boundaries
Proof of Theorem 1(v). For a nonidentity image target, (8) is at most n − 1; the identity has fibre two.
When n ≥ 4, equality can therefore occur only when r(τ ) = n − 1, or equivalently τ2 > τ3 > · · · > τn . Since
also τ1 < τ2 , the value n cannot occupy the first position. It lies in the decreasing tail and must be its first,
largest entry: τ2 = n. Conversely, every target in (9) has the full run and hence fibre n − 1. Choose τ1
arbitrarily from [n − 1]; the remaining entries are then forced in decreasing order. This gives exactly n − 1
maximizers.
   At n = 3, the two full-run targets still have fibre two, and the identity’s extra fixed predecessor also gives
fibre two. This is the stated exception.                                                                       □
Proposition 3 (the n = 2, 3 atlases). At n = 2, the arrows are 12 7→ 12 and 21 7→ 12. Thus the image and
recurrent set are both {12}, the depth populations are (1, 1), and F2−1 (12) = {12, 21} is the unique maximum
fibre.
   At n = 3, the full functional graph is
(14)               123 7→ 123,       132 ↔ 231,      213 7→ 123,    312 7→ 132,   321 7→ 231.
The image and recurrent populations are both three, the depth populations are (3, 3, 0), and each image target
has fibre two. Thus the three maximizers are 123, 132, 231.
Proof. Both displays follow by applying (3). Their inverse lists also agree with (7): the identity has its
fixed predecessor in addition to its two-prefix reversal. This direct calculation isolates the only boundary
not covered by the n ≥ 3 recurrent count and the only tie not covered uniformly by the n ≥ 4 maximizer

                                  5. Exact controls and claim boundary
   The paper-local verifier reconstructs the full map, all incoming sets, and all orbit coordinates through
S9 . It makes 6,273,063 integer assertions, including complete checks of the five theorem contracts and
the n = 2, 3 boundaries. It imports no scouting or earlier-paper code. These finite boxes are falsification
pressure, not proofs or ownership evidence.
   The nearest internal proof vocabulary is P122’s deterministic permutation block reversal and target-local
inverse cuts; that vocabulary receives zero separation credit. The killed follower-to-front spike is the Project
Euler rule, not (3). The retained owner-thin conjunction is the literal autonomous reversal together with
its half-image, peak two-cycle core, depth census, and decreasing-run inverse atlas. A bounded search
non-hit is not a novelty result. The gate remains owner_amber, and the external lifecycle remains
hold_external.
