\begin{abstract}
At every vertex of a plane rooted tree, read the order of its fringe subtree
and reverse the child list exactly when that order is odd; apply all such
reversals simultaneously.  The resulting finite self-map preserves every
fringe order and is an involution.  We give a root-local fixed-point
criterion: an even-order root requires its children to be fixed pointwise,
whereas an odd-order root requires a twisted palindrome whose paired
children are $(T,\M(T))$.  If $E(x)$ and $O(x)$ enumerate nonempty fixed
trees of even and odd order and
$A=x/(1-A)$ is the ordinary plane-tree series, this criterion gives
\[
 E=\frac{xO}{(1-E)^2-O^2},\qquad
 O=\frac{x(1+E)}{1-A(x^2)}.
\]
For $F=E+O$ we eliminate the auxiliary series and display an explicit
degree-six equation $P(x,F)=0$; the condition $P_y(0,0)=-3$ singles out the
zero-constant branch.  Thus, if $f_n=[x^n]F$ and
$a_n=C_{n-1}$ for $n\geq1$, the order-$n$ system has exactly $f_n$ fixed
points and $(a_n-f_n)/2$ two-cycles, with zeta function
$(1-z)^{-f_n}(1-z^2)^{-(a_n-f_n)/2}$.  The empty lane is handled separately.
Mirror symmetry, Catalan enumeration, plane-tree involutions, algebraic
generating-function machinery, and zeta bookkeeping receive zero
contribution credit.  The residual scope is only this conjunction for the
specified odd-fringe update; priority and external circulation remain on
hold.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A plane rooted tree equips the children of every vertex with a linear
order.  Reversing all child lists gives the classical global mirror
involution.  We study a state-dependent version: reverse the child list at a
vertex precisely when the fringe subtree rooted there has odd order.  All
vertices read their trigger from the unchanged input before the reversals are
applied.

The period law is immediate only after one identifies the correct invariant:
changing child order does not change any fringe order.  The fixed set is more
delicate.  At odd roots, componentwise recursion interacts with reversal, so
the children need not be individually fixed.  Instead, the child list is a
palindrome twisted by the same map.  Keeping the parity of a possible central
child produces a coupled, rather than scalar, generating-function system.

The paper proves four parts of one exact finite-map package.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The update preserves the underlying rooted tree and every fringe
  order, and it squares to the identity.
\item Fixed trees have an exact parity-sensitive root decomposition by
  ordinary fixed sequences and twisted palindromes.
\item The decomposition gives coupled series $E,O$ and an explicit
  degree-six algebraic equation for $F=E+O$.
\item Since all cycles have length one or two, the fixed coefficients give
  every iterate-fixed count and the complete fixed-order zeta function.
\end{enumerate}

The constituent neighborhoods are mature.  Plane trees and the equation
$A=x/(1-A)$ are standard instances of symbolic enumeration
\cite{FlajoletSedgewick2009}.  Direct involution neighbors include the
parity-reversing construction of Chen--Shapiro--Yang
\cite{ChenShapiroYang2006}, Deutsch's ordered-tree bijection
\cite{Deutsch2000}, and recent binary/plane-tree involutions
\cite{LiLinZhao2024}.  Claesson--Kitaev--Steingr\'{i}msson--Wang give a 2026
abstract Catalan-involution framework, global reversal factorization, and a
Donaghey connection \cite{ClaessonEtAl2026}; current cyclic actions on
corner-rooted plane trees appear in
\cite{BousquetMelouKrattenthaler2025}.  These papers do not use the literal
odd-fringe update below, but every general involution, mirror, Catalan, and
generating-function ingredient receives zero contribution credit.  A bounded
search miss is not a priority certificate.  We claim neither asymptotics nor
minimality of the displayed polynomial, and external dissemination remains
\textbf{HOLD}.

\section{The update, involution, and fixed criterion}\label{sec:update}

For $n\geq1$, let $\PT_n$ be the set of unlabelled plane rooted trees with
$n$ vertices.  A tree is represented recursively by the ordered tuple
\[
                 T=[T_1,\ldots,T_k]
\]
of the fringe subtrees rooted at the children of its root; the leaf is
$\bullet=[]$.  Its order is
$|T|=1+\sum_i|T_i|$.  We also adjoin a separate empty state by setting
$\PT_0=\{\eps\}$.  It is not identified with the leaf.

\begin{definition}[odd-fringe mirror]\label{def:update}
Set $\M(\eps)=\eps$.  For a nonempty tree $T=[T_1,\ldots,T_k]$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:update}
\M(T)=
\begin{cases}
[\M(T_1),\ldots,\M(T_k)],& |T|\text{ even},\\
[\M(T_k),\ldots,\M(T_1)],& |T|\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, every vertex reads the order of its old fringe subtree, and
all child lists with odd trigger are reversed simultaneously.  Write
$\M_n$ for the restriction to $\PT_n$.
\end{definition}

The recursion in \eqref{eq:update} records nested reversals but does not make
the clock asynchronous: every fringe order on the right is an input-tree
quantity.

For a nonempty input tree, define its induced vertex transport to the image
recursively.  The root maps to the root.  At a vertex with \(k\) children,
child position \(i\) maps to position \(i\) when that vertex's old fringe
order is even and to position \(k+1-i\) when it is odd; inside the matched
child subtrees use their recursively induced transports.

\begin{theorem}[involution and local fixed test]\label{thm:involution}
The empty state is fixed.  For every \(n\ge1\) and \(T\in\PT_n\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\M(T)$ has the same order, the same underlying nonplane rooted tree,
  and the same fringe order at each vertex under the induced transport;
\item $\M^2(T)=T$;
\item if $T=[T_1,\ldots,T_k]$ is nonempty, then
\begin{align}
|T|\text{ even}:\quad
 T\in\Fix(\M)&\Longleftrightarrow
 T_i\in\Fix(\M)\quad(1\leq i\leq k),                 \label{eq:even-fixed}\\
|T|\text{ odd}:\quad
 T\in\Fix(\M)&\Longleftrightarrow
 T_i=\M(T_{k+1-i})\quad(1\leq i\leq k).              \label{eq:odd-fixed}
\end{align}
\end{enumerate}
Together with the empty lane, every orbit has period one or two.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Induction on $|T|$ shows that applying \eqref{eq:update} to a child preserves
its order and descendants.  The root step only reverses a list, so it also
preserves order and the underlying nonplane tree.  Following the induced
transport just defined, the fringe subtree at each source vertex is sent to
the image of that same subtree and hence has the same order.  This proves the
pointwise fringe-order assertion.

For the second assertion, use induction again.  The root has the same parity
after one update.  At an even root, the second update applies $\M^2$ to each
child without reversing the list.  At an odd root, it applies $\M^2$ to the
children and reverses the list a second time.  Both cases return $T$.
Equivalently, the input determines a fixed set of local reversals; those
order-two operations act on distinct child lists and commute.  Finally,
comparing the child tuple in \eqref{eq:update} with the original tuple gives
\eqref{eq:even-fixed} and \eqref{eq:odd-fixed} in both directions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[ordinary mirror is different]\label{rem:mirror}
Let $\J[T_1,\ldots,T_k]=[\J(T_k),\ldots,\J(T_1)]$ reverse every child list.
Put $L=[]$, $U=[L]$, $A=[L,U]$, and $B=[U,L]$.  The order-four tree $A$ is
$\M$-fixed but not $\J$-fixed.  Conversely, the order-nine tree $[A,B]$ is
$\J$-fixed but not $\M$-fixed.  Hence the two fixed sets are incomparable,
not merely presented by different recursions.
\end{remark}

\section{The twisted-palindrome fixed grammar}\label{sec:grammar}

Let
\[
 A(x)=\sum_{n\geq1}|\PT_n|x^n
     =\sum_{n\geq1}C_{n-1}x^n
     =\frac{x}{1-A(x)}.
\]
Let $E(x)$ and $O(x)$ enumerate the nonempty $\M$-fixed trees of even and
odd order, respectively, and put $F=E+O$.  Thus all four series in this
section lie in $\mathbb Q[[x]]$ and have zero constant term; $A$ is the
unique Catalan branch with $A(0)=0$.

\begin{theorem}[coupled fixed-tree series]\label{thm:coupled}
The fixed-tree series are the unique pair $(E,O)\in x\mathbb Q[[x]]^2$
satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coupled}
\boxed{
 E=\frac{xO}{(1-E)^2-O^2},
 \qquad
 O=\frac{x(1+E)}{1-A(x^2)}.}
\end{equation}
for the specified Catalan branch $A$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that the root has even order.  By
\eqref{eq:even-fixed}, its children form an arbitrary sequence of fixed
trees.  Their total order must be odd.  Since $F(-x)=E-O$, the odd part of
the sequence series is
\[
 \frac12\left(\frac1{1-F(x)}-\frac1{1-F(-x)}\right)
 =\frac{O}{(1-E)^2-O^2}.
\]
Multiplication by the root marker $x$ gives the first equation.

Now suppose that the root has odd order.  Condition
\eqref{eq:odd-fixed} says that its child tuple is a twisted palindrome.  If
there are $2r$ children, the first $r$ are arbitrary trees and determine the
remaining children as their reversed componentwise $\M$-images.  Each such
pair has total order $2|T|$, so one pair has series $A(x^2)$ and a sequence of
pairs has series $1/(1-A(x^2))$.  If the child count is odd, the central
child must be fixed.  The off-centre pairs have even total order, and the
root's children must also have even total order; hence the central child must
have even order and contributes $E$, not $F$.  The optional centre gives the
factor $1+E$.  Multiplying by $x$ proves the second equation.  After clearing
the two unit denominators, the Jacobian with respect to $(E,O)$ at
$(x,E,O)=(0,0,0)$ is the identity.  The formal implicit-function theorem in
$\mathbb Q[[x]]$ therefore gives the asserted coupled uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\section{An explicit algebraic equation}\label{sec:algebraic}

The coupled system proves algebraicity over the standard Catalan series.  In
this case the auxiliary series can be removed completely.

\begin{theorem}[degree-six annihilating equation]\label{thm:polynomial}
Let $F=E+O$.  Then $P(x,F(x))=0$, where
\begin{align}
P(x,y)={}&(2x^2-x)y^6+(1-2x)y^5
 +(4x^3+6x^2+4x)y^4 \notag\\
&+(4x^3-12x^2-11x-6)y^3 \notag\\
&+(2x^4-11x^3+10x^2+26x+8)y^2 \label{eq:polynomial}\\
&+(4x^4-2x^3-20x^2-19x-3)y
 +2x^4+9x^3+14x^2+3x. \notag
\end{align}
Moreover, $F$ is the unique series in $\mathbb Q[[x]]$ satisfying
$P(x,F)=0$ and $F(0)=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put
\[
 G=E-O,\qquad B=A(x^2),\qquad c=1-B.
\]
Factoring the denominator in the first equation of \eqref{eq:coupled} and
rewriting the second gives
\begin{align}
 (F+G)(1-F)(1-G)&=x(F-G),                                  \label{eq:FG1}\\
 (c+x)G&=(c-x)F-2x,                                        \label{eq:FG2}\\
 B^2-B+x^2&=0.                                             \label{eq:B}
\end{align}
The factor $c+x$ has constant term one, so \eqref{eq:FG2} can be used in the
formal power-series ring.  Eliminating $G$ from \eqref{eq:FG1}--\eqref{eq:FG2}
and then $B$ using \eqref{eq:B} gives the resultant
$4x^2P(x,F)$.  Since $\mathbb Q[[x]]$ has no zero divisors,
\eqref{eq:polynomial} follows.  This is a direct expansion using only the
three displayed identities.  The exact-control script independently
reconstructs this identity: it takes the quadratic--linear resultant in
$G$, reduces its coefficients modulo $B^2-B+x^2$, and evaluates the
resulting quadratic norm coefficient by coefficient.

Finally,
\[
             P(0,0)=0,\qquad P_y(0,0)=-3.
\]
The formal implicit-function theorem therefore gives exactly one
zero-constant branch.  The series obtained in \cref{thm:coupled} lies on
that branch.
\end{proof}

\section{The complete temporal census}\label{sec:census}

For $n\geq1$, put $a_n=C_{n-1}$, and retain $a_0=1$.  Define the fixed-order
Artin--Mazur zeta function in the usual way \cite{ArtinMazur1965}:
\[
 \zeta_{\M,n}(z)
 =\exp\left(\sum_{q\geq1}
       |\Fix(\M_n^q)|\frac{z^q}{q}\right).
\]

\begin{corollary}[all cycles and all iterates]\label{cor:census}
For every $n\geq0$, the order-$n$ functional graph consists of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycle-counts}
       f_n\text{ fixed points}
       \quad\text{and}\quad
       c_{n,2}=\frac{a_n-f_n}{2}\text{ two-cycles}.
\end{equation}
For every $q\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iterate-fixed}
 |\Fix(\M_n^q)|=
 \begin{cases}
 f_n,&q\text{ odd},\\
 a_n,&q\text{ even},
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeta}
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \zeta_{\M,n}(z)
 =(1-z)^{-f_n}(1-z^2)^{-(a_n-f_n)/2}.}
\end{equation}
In particular, both the empty and one-vertex lanes have zeta
$(1-z)^{-1}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:involution}, every component is a fixed point or a two-cycle.
The fixed points are counted by $f_n$, so the remaining $a_n-f_n$ states
pair to give \eqref{eq:cycle-counts}.  An odd iterate fixes only the
one-cycles, whereas an even iterate fixes every state, proving
\eqref{eq:iterate-fixed}.  The standard cycle product for the zeta function
then gives one factor $(1-z)$ per fixed point and one factor $(1-z^2)$ per
two-cycle, which is \eqref{eq:zeta}.
\end{proof}

\section{Owner subtraction, firewalls, and controls}\label{sec:scope}

The literal action separates the present map from its closest neighbors.
Chen--Shapiro--Yang locate one illegal vertex and transfer a prefix of
siblings in order to reverse leaf parity \cite{ChenShapiroYang2006}; Deutsch
redistributes ordered-tree statistics \cite{Deutsch2000}; and the recent
binary/plane-tree involutions of Li--Lin--Zhao are transported mirror and
statistic symmetries \cite{LiLinZhao2024}.  None applies simultaneous
odd-fringe child reversals.

The 2026 Catalan involution $h$ is the strongest current owner objection
\cite{ClaessonEtAl2026}.  On plane trees, $h$ sends the root with three leaf
children to a path of depth three, so it changes the parent--child relation;
the same star is fixed by $\M$.  Claesson et al. grade plane trees by edges,
whereas our order is the number of vertices.  Thus their Proposition~2.11
translates to one $h$-fixed tree at our order one, $C_k$ fixed trees at order
$2k+2$, and none at odd order at least three.  In particular, their counts
at orders four and six are $1$ and $2$, while
\cref{tab:coefficients} gives $f_4=5$ and $f_6=36$.  Their global reversal
factor, Donaghey map, fixed census, and abstract framework receive zero
credit, but they are not the map in \cref{def:update}.  Likewise, the
cyclic actions of Bousquet-Mélou--Krattenthaler move a distinguished root
corner, leaf, or nonleaf corner around a plane tree
\cite{BousquetMelouKrattenthaler2025}; they do not reverse parity-selected
child lists.

\section{Conclusion}

Odd-fringe mirroring is a finite involutive dynamics whose fixed trees are
controlled by a parity-sensitive twisted palindrome.  That local condition
gives the coupled $E/O$ grammar, the explicit algebraic equation for the
fixed series, and every one- and two-cycle count.  The claim stops there:
asymptotics, priority, and broader Catalan-involution conclusions lie outside
the present scope.
