## Two word-transform checks

4. **Pure Burrows--Wheeler transform, without original-row index.**
   On fixed-length words over a finite ordered alphabet, the standard
   last-column operation is a finite endomorphism. Its distinct-letter
   specialization is already the exact CPD row in
   `docs/papers162_166_sequence/scouting/word_combinatorial/SCOUT.md`,
   lines 55--70 read directly (f383d4). That old row records the
   cyclic-predecessor update, direct BWT ownership and missing all-size
   temporal structure. Repeated letters do not remove this owner.

   Direct primary read: Giancarlo, Restivo and Sciortino,
   [From First Principles to the Burrows and Wheeler Transform and Beyond, via Combinatorial Optimization](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~gusfield/spring06readings/bwtnote5.pdf).
   Read opening definitions/Figure 1 (printed pp. 1--3), and Section 5's
   closing recovery discussion (printed p. 15). The pure transform is
   explicitly separated from the augmented end-marker transform, and
   original-row information is required for recovery. This is already
   cyclic-word encoding, not a new inverse mechanism. No exact recurrent
   classification for iterating the pure endomorphism was proved here.
   Reversible indexed/augmented variants are not substituted as its dynamics.

5. **Move-to-front rank encoding with a fixed initial full alphabet.**
   The map on [q]^n outputs each symbol's current list position and moves
   that symbol to the front. It is a bijection, not a promising nonbijective
   residual: start the decoder with the same fixed list; at each output
   rank choose that list entry and move it to the front. Induction shows
   both lists match after every coordinate. Every rank word is valid, so
   decoding is a two-sided inverse. All finite states are recurrent and
   every fibre is one; this does not classify cycle lengths.

   Direct primary read: Bentley, Sleator, Tarjan and Wei,
   [A Locally Adaptive Data Compression Scheme](https://www-2.cs.cmu.edu/~sleator/papers/adaptive-data-compression.pdf),
   Section 2, printed pp. 320--321, describes synchronized list encoding
   and decoding. Its initial empty-list example and occurrence-initialized
   analysis are not falsely identified with our fixed-full-list convention;
   the elementary inverse above is the explicit adapter. Compression
   performance theorems are not dynamical period theorems. The local
   Toom--Tsetlin search hits concern list/word moves, not this entire encoder,
   so no false exact-map collision is claimed for those hits.
