L3@P0: Abstract. We determine the optimal bound for the maximum number of
L4@P0: moves required to reach a periodic configuration of open mancala (also called
L5@P0: open owari), inspired by a popular African game. A mancala move can be
L6@P0: interpreted as a map from the set of compositions of a given integer in itself,
L7@P0: thus relating our result to the study of the corresponding finite dynamical
L8@P0: system.
L9@P0: 1. Introduction
L10@P0: Mancala is a family of traditional African games played in many versions and
L11@P0: under many names. It comprises a circular list of holes containing zero or more
L12@P0: seeds. A move consists of selecting a nonempty hole, taking all its seeds and sowing
L13@P0: them in the subsequent holes, one seed per hole.
L14@P0: Here we study an idealized version of this called open mancala (see [1], where
L15@P0: the game is called open owari). We assume there is an infinite sequence of holes.
L16@P0: Further we assume that the nonempty holes are consecutive; such a configuration
L17@P0: is described by a sequence of positive integers λi, i = 1, . . . , `, giving the number
L18@P0: of seeds in each nonempty hole, starting from the leftmost one. We assume a move
L19@P0: always selects the leftmost nonempty hole, sowing to the right. Thus the leftmost
L20@P0: nonempty hole always advances of one position. It is clear that the sequence of
L21@P0: configurations becomes periodic in a finite time. The periodic configurations have
L22@P0: been completely classified ([1, 3, 2]): see Section 2.
L23@P0: This game is related to a card game called Bulgarian Solitaire, discussed by
L24@P0: Gardner [5] in a 1983 Scientific American column. Indeed, that game is isomorphic
L25@P0: to the case of open mancala where λi ≥ λj for i ≤ j (monotone mancala), see
L26@P0: Section 6. Gardner mentioned a conjecture on the maximum number of moves
L27@P0: before the onset of periodicity when the number of cards is of the form k(k + 1)/2.
L28@P0: The conjecture was proved independently a few years later by Igusa [7] and Etienne
L29@P0: [4]. In 1998, Griggs & Ho [6] revisited the result and proposed a new conjecture in
L30@P0: the case of a generic number of cards. Due to the isomorphism between monotone
L31@P0: mancala and Bulgarian solitaire, our result is strongly related to this conjecture.
L32@P0: However, our results apply to open mancala, not to the monotone variant, hence
L33@P0: the conjecture about the Bulgarian Solitaire remains unproven.
L34@P0: The main result of the paper is the optimal bound for the maximum number
L35@P0: of moves, before open mancala reaches periodicity, as a function of n, the number
L36@P0: of seeds. In Section 3 we prove the lower bound by producing, for any n, a configuration that requires exactly that number of moves. In Section 4 we introduce
L37@P0: the main tool in proving the upper bound, namely s-monotonicity. In Section 5 we
L38@P0: prove that every configuration reaches periodicity within that number of moves.

L182@P3: 2.1. Energy levels interpretation. We can view the mancala configurations and
L183@P3: mancala moves in a way reminiscent of the energy levels for the electrons in an atom.
L184@P3: Let us consider the subset L ⊂ N × N given by
L185@P3: L = {(i, j) ∈ N × N : 1 ≤ i ≤ j}.
L186@P3: It is convenient to think of the integers i and j as a numbering of square cells
L187@P3: instead of coordinates of points, in a way similar to the familiar sea-battle game.
L188@P3: Each square cell in L can either be empty or contain a single seed.
L189@P3: The column index i corresponds to one of the holes in the mancala game, so that
L190@P3: λi, where λ ∈ Λ, is the total number of occupied cells in column i of L. We shall also
L191@P3: associate an energy to a seed positioned in cell (i, j), given by its second coordinate
L192@P3: j. The k-th level of L is the set {(1, k), . . . ,(k, k)} of cells having energy k. Finally
L193@P3: we let the seeds free to immediately fall down without changing their column, but
L194@P3: decreasing their energy (the second coordinate of their current position) provided
L195@P3: the new position (and all those in between) are free.
L196@P3: The mancala configuration λ ∈ Λ then corresponds to a positioning of seeds in
L197@P3: L such that (i, j) is occupied by a seed if and only if 1 ≤ j ≤ λi (see Figure 1).
L198@P3: A mancala move can now be viewed in this setting as follows. Firstly, each seed
L199@P3: is moved one position to the left without changing its energy level, while seeds in
L200@P3: column 1 are rotated to the last column of that energy level. Namely, a seed at
L201@P3: position (i, j) is moved in the new position (i − 1, j) if i > 1, (j, j) if i = 1. This
L202@P3: is just a left rotation of the seeds of each level, so that cells will receive at most
L203@P3: one seed. Secondly we let gravity pack all the seeds in each column at the lowest
L204@P3: energy possible.
L205@P3: We notice that if all energy levels up to k are completely filled, then a mancala
L206@P3: move does not change those levels (up to a rearrangement of the seeds), and all
L207@P3: the action takes place from the lowest level that is not completely filled, say k + 1,
L208@P3: up to the highest level that is not completely empty. The lowest active level is the
L209@P3: lowest level that is not completely filled.
L210@P3: The overall energy of a configuration (the sum of the individual energy of every
L211@P3-4: seed) does not change after the first part of the move (the rotation within eachAN OPTIMAL BOUND ON THE NUMBER OF MOVES FOR OPEN MANCALA 5
L212@P4: energy level) whereas it can decrease during the second part (vertical downward
L213@P4: shift).
L214@P4: Definition 2.11 (gap). We shall call gap an empty cell at the lowest energy level,
L215@P4: say k, that is not completely filled. We shall think of a gap as an absent seed, and
L216@P4: in this respect we can follow a gap through mancala moves as it rotates left along
L217@P4: its energy level.

L223@P4: many seeds at energy levels larger than k.
