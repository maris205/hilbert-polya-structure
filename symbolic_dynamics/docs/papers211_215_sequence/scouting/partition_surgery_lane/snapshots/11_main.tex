\title[Rectangular-corner stripping]{Rectangular-Corner Stripping of Integer Partitions:\\Clocks, Fibres, and Parameter Recovery}

\begin{abstract}
Fix positive integers $a,b$.  From the Ferrers diagram of an integer
partition, delete the first $a$ rows and first $b$ columns and iterate on the
translated southeast remainder.  We determine this rank-changing finite
dynamics on all partitions of weight at most $N$.  A cell-coordinate formula
gives every iterate, a pointwise rectangle-survival clock, and the sharp
height
\[
 \min\{t\geq0:(at+1)(bt+1)>N\}.
\]
Independently, we enumerate every rank-$t$ fibre.  For a nonempty target
$\mu$, the weight series is a forced monomial times
$1/(\qfac{at}\qfac{bt})$; the empty target has a separate finite hook
series.  This yields an exact image threshold under every weight cap.  The
thresholds of the one-cell, two-cell row, and two-cell column targets recover
the ordered pair $(a,b)$.  Generalized rectangular Durfee viewpoints, static
two-boundary symbols and decompositions, and their two-factor partition
products are explicit zero-credit inputs.
A deterministic audit checks $3{,}462{,}895$ exact consequences.
\end{abstract}

\section{System and source boundary}\label{sec:setup}

Let $\Part$ be the set of integer partitions, including $\emptypar$.  We use
English Ferrers diagrams: $(i,j)$ is a cell of
$\lambda=(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\ldots)$ when
$1\leq j\leq\lambda_i$.  For fixed $a,b\geq1$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:map}
 T_{a,b}(\lambda)
  =(\lambda_{a+1}-b,\lambda_{a+2}-b,\ldots)_+,
\end{equation}
where nonpositive parts are omitted.  Geometrically, we delete the first $a$
rows and first $b$ columns, then translate the southeast remainder.  Since
weight decreases on every nonempty state, \eqref{eq:map} restricts to the
finite carrier $\Part_{\leq N}=\{\lambda:|\lambda|\leq N\}$.

The operation has classical inputs.  Barnes and Savage delete the first row
and column in a recurrence for graphical partitions and explicitly note its
Durfee decrement~\cite{BarnesSavage1995}.  Generalized rectangular Durfee
viewpoints, including unequal or rational-slope rectangles, occur in the
classical literature~\cite{GordonHouten1968,Andrews1971}.  In particular,
Chen, Ji, and Zang describe an $m$-Durfee rectangle symbol by the two
partitions beside and below a largest rectangle, with total weight equal to
rectangle area plus the two boundary weights~\cite[Sec.~3]{ChenJiZang2015}.
Andrews and Eriksson give standard Ferrers, Gaussian-polynomial,
bounded-partition, and Durfee background~\cite{AndrewsEriksson2004}.  We
assign zero credit to all of this static rectangle and two-boundary
structure, to Ferrers conjugation, and to the product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pochhammer}
 \frac1{\qfac r}=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac1{1-q^j},\qquad \qfac0=1,
\end{equation}
which enumerates partitions with at most $r$ parts, equivalently those whose
largest part is at most $r$.  Thus the two-factor Pochhammer factorization is
also zero credit.  The scoped residual begins only with iterating the fixed
literal crop $(a,b)$ through every time, prescribing an arbitrary southeast
target, separating the empty branch, proving exact cap support, and recovering
the ordered parameters.  The source search is bounded; no novelty, priority,
or owner-absence conclusion is drawn.

For a nonempty $\mu$, write $\elln(\mu)$ for its length and set, for
$h,w\geq0$,
\begin{align}
 M_{h,w}(\mu)&=|\mu|+h(\mu_1+w)+w\elln(\mu),\label{eq:M}\\
 E_{h,w}(q)&=\frac1{\qfac h}\sum_{k=0}^{w}
       \frac{q^{k(h+1)}}{\qfac k}.\label{eq:E}
\end{align}
Let $\tau_{a,b}(\lambda)$ be the least $t$ for which
$T_{a,b}^{t}(\lambda)=\emptypar$.

\section{The rectangle clock}\label{sec:clock}

The temporal statement is pointwise before it is enumerative.

\begin{theorem}[iterates, clock, and sharp height]\label{thm:clock}
For $a,b\geq1$ and $t\geq0$, put $h=at,w=bt$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iterate}
 T_{a,b}^{t}(\lambda)
   =(\lambda_{h+1}-w,\lambda_{h+2}-w,\ldots)_+.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:point-clock}
 \tau_{a,b}(\lambda)=\min\{t\geq0:\lambda_{at+1}\leq bt\},
 \qquad
 \tau_{a,b}(\lambda)>t\iff (at+1,bt+1)\in\lambda,
\end{equation}
where missing parts are zero.  The empty partition is the unique recurrent
state, and the maximum entry time on $\Part_{\leq N}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:height}
 H_{a,b}(N)=\min\{t\geq0:(at+1)(bt+1)>N\}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, the exact weight-refined absorbed census is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:absorbed}
 \sum_{\lambda:T_{a,b}^{t}(\lambda)=\emptypar}q^{|\lambda|}
   =E_{at,bt}(q).
\end{equation}
Thus $[q^n]E_{at,bt}$ counts weight-$n$ states absorbed by rank $t$;
consecutive differences give every exact clock shell.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
One update retains exactly the cells $(i,j)$ satisfying $i>a,j>b$ and sends
them to $(i-a,j-b)$.  Induction adds these offsets and proves
\eqref{eq:iterate}.  The retained diagram is nonempty exactly when its
northwest cell existed before translation, which is \eqref{eq:point-clock}.
Every nonempty state loses $(1,1)$, so only $\emptypar$ is recurrent.

Survival through rank $t$ forces the rectangle
$[1,at+1]\times[1,bt+1]$ and therefore at least
$(at+1)(bt+1)$ cells.  Conversely, that rectangle itself survives rank $t$.
This proves \eqref{eq:height}, including $H_{a,b}(0)=0$.

It remains to count $T^t(\lambda)=\emptypar$, equivalently
$\lambda_{h+1}\leq w$.  Slice by $k=\lambda_{h+1}$.  For $k=0$, the diagram
has at most $h$ rows and contributes $1/\qfac h$.  For $1\leq k\leq w$,
remove a $k$-by-$(h+1)$ rectangle.  The excess in the first $h$ rows is an
arbitrary partition with at most $h$ parts, while the remaining lower rows
form an arbitrary partition with largest part at most $k$.  This reversible
decomposition contributes
$q^{k(h+1)}/(\qfac h\qfac k)$.  Summing the disjoint slices proves
\eqref{eq:absorbed}.
\end{proof}

For $a=b=1$, \eqref{eq:point-clock} is the classical Durfee-size statement;
that specialization is contextual rather than part of the retained claim.
For unequal $a,b$, the clock tests a moving rectangular corner and the capped
height is the first failure of a quadratic area inequality.

\section{Every target at every time}\label{sec:fibres}

The clock sees one corner cell.  The inverse problem instead records two
entire boundaries around a prescribed southeast remainder.

\begin{theorem}[complete fibre atlas]\label{thm:fibres}
Let $t\geq0$, $h=at$, and $w=bt$.  For every target $\mu\in\Part$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre-series}
 \sum_{\lambda:T_{a,b}^{t}(\lambda)=\mu}q^{|\lambda|}
 =\begin{cases}
 \displaystyle
  \frac{q^{M_{h,w}(\mu)}}{\qfac h\qfac w},&\mu\ne\emptypar,\\[7pt]
 E_{h,w}(q),&\mu=\emptypar.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
For $t\geq1$, a nonempty $\mu$ lies in the rank-$t$ image of
$\Part_{\leq N}$ exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:image-threshold}
 M_{at,bt}(\mu)\leq N.
\end{equation}
When this holds, $\mu$ has a source of every weight from its threshold through
$N$; the corresponding coefficient in \eqref{eq:fibre-series} is the exact
size-refined fibre.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The empty line is \eqref{eq:absorbed}.  Fix nonempty
$\mu=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_r)$.  Formula \eqref{eq:iterate} says that a source
has the forced middle rows
\begin{equation}\label{eq:middle}
 \lambda_{h+j}=\mu_j+w\quad(1\leq j\leq r),
 \qquad \lambda_{h+r+1}\leq w.
\end{equation}
The first $h$ rows have baseline $\mu_1+w$.  Their excesses form an arbitrary
partition $\gamma$ with at most $h$ parts.  The rows below the block in
\eqref{eq:middle} form an arbitrary partition $\beta$ with largest part at
most $w$.  Conversely, after padding $\gamma$ with zeros, any such pair
$(\gamma,\beta)$ reconstructs a unique weakly decreasing source.  The forced
weight is
\[
 h(\mu_1+w)+\sum_{j=1}^r(\mu_j+w)=M_{h,w}(\mu),
\]
and the two independent free pieces contribute $1/\qfac h$ and
$1/\qfac w$.  This proves the nonempty line of
\eqref{eq:fibre-series}.  At $t=0$, both orders are zero and the sole source
is $\mu$, as required.

For $t\geq1$, both $h$ and $w$ are positive.  The product
$1/(\qfac h\qfac w)$ has a positive coefficient in every nonnegative degree
$d$: take $\gamma=(d)$ and $\beta=\emptypar$, interpreting $\gamma=\emptypar$
when $d=0$.  Since $h\geq1$, this $\gamma$ has at most $h$ parts.  Hence no source
exists below the forced weight and a source exists at every weight at or
above it.  Intersecting with $\Part_{\leq N}$ proves
\eqref{eq:image-threshold}.
\end{proof}

The separate empty line is essential.  Substituting an undefined
$\mu_1$ or $\elln(\mu)$ into the nonempty formula would miss all hook-shaped
sources.  Likewise, the threshold claim is restricted to $t\geq1$; at
$t=0$, the fibre consists only of the target at its exact weight.

\section{Coefficient consequences and one worked fibre}\label{sec:coefficients}

Let
\[
 p_r(d)=[q^d]\frac1{\qfac r},
\]
with $p_r(d)=0$ for $d<0$.  Thus $p_r(d)$ counts partitions of $d$ with at
most $r$ parts.  Reading coefficients in Theorem~\ref{thm:fibres} gives the
fully numerical form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient-fibre}
 \#\{\lambda\vdash n:T_{a,b}^{t}(\lambda)=\mu\}
 =\sum_{j=0}^{n-M_{h,w}(\mu)}p_h(j)
    p_w\bigl(n-M_{h,w}(\mu)-j\bigr)
\end{equation}
for nonempty $\mu$, where the sum is zero when $n<M_{h,w}(\mu)$.  This
formula separates the top and bottom freedom even after the total source
weight is fixed.

For example, take $(a,b)=(2,1)$, $t=2$, and $\mu=(3,1)$.  Then
$h=4,w=2$ and
\[
 M_{4,2}((3,1))=4+4(3+2)+2\cdot2=28.
\]
The target therefore first appears at source weight $28$, and its exact
fibre series is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:worked-fibre}
 \frac{q^{28}}{\qfac4\qfac2}
  =q^{28}\bigl(1+2q+5q^2+9q^3+17q^4+\cdots\bigr).
\end{equation}
Hence its fibres at source weights $28,29,30,31,32$ have sizes
$1,2,5,9,17$.  The corresponding empty fibre is not obtained by setting
$\mu=\emptypar$ here; by \eqref{eq:E} it is
\[
 E_{4,2}(q)=\frac1{\qfac4}
 \left(1+\frac{q^5}{\qfac1}+\frac{q^{10}}{\qfac2}\right).
\]

Two aggregate consequences are useful checks on the atlas.  First, the exact
rank-$t$ image size on the capped carrier is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:image-size}
 1+\#\{\mu\ne\emptypar:M_{at,bt}(\mu)\leq N\};
\end{equation}
the initial one counts the empty target.  Second, because the fibres partition
all sources, summing \eqref{eq:fibre-series} over targets gives the formal
mass identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass-identity}
 \frac1{(q;q)_\infty}
 =E_{h,w}(q)+\frac1{\qfac h\qfac w}
   \sum_{\mu\ne\emptypar}q^{M_{h,w}(\mu)}.
\end{equation}
This is not an additional ownership claim; it is the global consistency
equation for the target-resolved bijection.

\section{Duality and parameter recovery}\label{sec:recovery}

Let $\lambda'$ be the conjugate partition.  Transposing cell coordinates in
the definition immediately gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:duality}
 T_{a,b}(\lambda)'=T_{b,a}(\lambda').
\end{equation}
This symmetry explains why a square clock alone cannot orient the two
parameters.  The target thresholds do.

\begin{corollary}[three-probe identifiability]\label{cor:recovery}
Let $m(\mu)$ denote the minimum source weight of the one-step nonempty target
$\mu$.  Then
\begin{align}
 m((1))&=(a+1)(b+1),\label{eq:mcell}\\
 m((2))&=m((1))+a+1,\label{eq:mrow}\\
 m((1,1))&=m((1))+b+1.\label{eq:mcolumn}
\end{align}
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recover}
 a=m((2))-m((1))-1,\qquad
 b=m((1,1))-m((1))-1.
\end{equation}
The ordered update parameters are determined by the one-step target-support
profile.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:fibres} identifies $m(\mu)$ with $M_{a,b}(\mu)$.
Substitution of the three displayed shapes gives
\begin{align*}
 M_{a,b}((1))&=1+a(1+b)+b,\\
 M_{a,b}((2))&=2+a(2+b)+b,\\
 M_{a,b}((1,1))&=2+a(1+b)+2b.
\end{align*}
Taking the two differences proves \eqref{eq:mcell}--\eqref{eq:recover}.
\end{proof}

This interface is stronger than recovering only the symmetric product
$(a+1)(b+1)$.  Row and column probes retain the orientation exchanged by
\eqref{eq:duality}.

\section{Exact control and limitations}\label{sec:control}

weight $32$.  For $(a,b)=(1,1),(2,1),(1,3),(2,2),(3,2)$ and

zero/nonzero image threshold, and all three recovery probes.  The frozen run
contains $3{,}462{,}895$ exact assertions.
