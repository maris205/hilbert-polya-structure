## 2. Replace points by all subspaces: closing wrapper control

This is an author-derived universal obstruction, not a novel system
nomination. Let L be any finite lattice with an order-reversing involution
c. For example, L is the full subspace lattice of a finite-dimensional
finite-field vector space and c is orthogonal complement for a
nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form. Nondegeneracy suffices; isotropic
subspaces do not invalidate the involution or De Morgan identities.

On L^n (n >= 1), with cyclic indices, define

    D(V)_i = c(V_i join V_{i+1}),    S(V)_i = V_{i+1},
    E(V)_i = (V_{i-1} join V_i) meet (V_i join V_{i+1}).

**Control.** D^3 = S D; all states enter a recurrent core within one
step, and every recurrent period divides 2n.

Proof. De Morgan gives D^2 = S E. Each E(V)_i contains V_i. Both
E(V)_i and E(V)_{i+1} are contained in V_i join V_{i+1}; together they
contain V_i and V_{i+1}. Consequently

    E(V)_i join E(V)_{i+1} = V_i join V_{i+1}.

Thus E^2 = E and D E = D. All these translation-invariant operators
commute with S, yielding D^3 = D S E = S D. If y = D(x), then
D^2(y) = S(y); induction and commutation yield D^{2n}(y) = S^n(y) = y.
This proves the control, including n = 1. In particular im(D) is exactly
the recurrent set: its points are periodic, and any periodic point has a
predecessor and hence lies in the image. No sharp maximal-period claim is
made. The depth bound is <= 1, not a dimension-dependent growth bound.
