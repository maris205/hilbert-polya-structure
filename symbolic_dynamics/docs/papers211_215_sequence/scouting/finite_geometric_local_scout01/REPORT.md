### Square loops: direct owner, excluded conjugacy, and a lossy boundary factor

On the finite square fully-packed-loop carrier, the two-checkerboard-pass
local parallel-edge switch is already Wieland's gyration. In his convention
the odd squares precede the even ones; each single-square map is an
involution, and each parity layer consists of commuting involutions. The
reverse layer order therefore gives the inverse. Section 3 proves a bijection
between the appropriate rotated boundary-pairing classes; it does not assert
that boundary rotation returns every full configuration. The original §8
already distinguishes these two questions. This is an existing operator,
not a newly instantiated candidate. [Wieland, author version
math/0006234v2, §§3–5 and 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0006234v2).

Striker–Williams supply the stronger collision: their height-function
description identifies gyration with the odd/even row-toggle product, and
Theorem 8.13 gives its equivariant bijection with rowmotion. Proposition 8.12
and the constructive generator-conjugacy Lemma 5.1 are the actual mechanism,
not a similarity of names. Square/parity conventions differ across sources;
reversing the two involutive layers does not create a new dynamical axis.
The lane explicitly excludes rowmotion and promotion, so this entrance
ends at the source desk. [Striker–Williams, *Promotion and Rowmotion*,
arXiv:1108.1172v3, §5 and §8.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1108.1172v3).

The boundary map is noninjective. Dilks–Pechenik–Striker explicitly give an
order-five FPL orbit of length four whose link-pattern orbit has length two;
the full action at size five has order twenty, although the boundary
frequency is ten. Thus a claim that the full map has order dividing \(2n\)
would already be false. These are published-source observations, not new
locally enumerated states or a new proof here. [*Resonance in orbits of plane
partitions and increasing tableaux*, arXiv:1512.00365v2, §5,
Theorem 5.4 and following examples](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.00365v2).

### Partial rectangles: changing the boundary does not remove the conjugacy

Heuer's partial rectangular FPL action includes explicit boundary local
moves. Lemma 4.2 translates them to height changes and order-ideal toggles;
Lemma 4.5 handles the parity-dependent action versus inverse; Theorem 4.6
states the rowmotion equivariant bijection. This blocks treating the named
partial-rectangle version as a fresh escape. It does **not** establish that
arbitrary boundary conditions or arbitrary selected flips are conjugate.
[Heuer, *Partial Alternating Sign Matrix Bijections and Dynamics*, author
v1, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02242v1); [journal metadata, EJC 31(2),
P2.1, 5 April 2024](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v31i2p1).

### Triangular drift: the nonbijective alternative is also an existing result

Beil–Fischer–Nadeau's Definition 7 already specifies the default left drift:
retain the current left boundary word, extend that side, apply its odd-cell
operation, remove rightmost vertices and shift back. Its half-step convention
differs from the square two-layer switch. Theorems 15–16 give stability by
\(2N-1\) steps and the no-drifter characterization, with column evacuation
in Proposition 22; no sharpness is claimed here. Theorem 13's inverse retains
the appropriate **original boundary parameter**. Remark 14 denies that
the two default drifts are general inverses. These are existing results,
not new residuals. [*Wieland drift for triangular fully packed loop
configurations*, EJC 22(1), P1.26 (2015), §§2–4](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v22i1p26/pdf/).
