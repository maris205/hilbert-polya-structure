## Author deduction: generic local inverse counting has no candidate-specific content

Status: **PROVABLE AS STATED**, for the elementary identity below only.
It is a mechanism-subtraction lemma, not a claimed new research theorem and
not a new literal proposed for admission.

Assume that A is a nonempty finite alphabet, n is at least three, and
$\phi:A^3\to A$ is any function. For a cyclic word $x\in A^n$, put
$T_\phi(x)_i=\phi(x_{i-1},x_i,x_{i+1})$, with all indices modulo n.
Fix a target $y\in A^n$. For each i define an $|A|^2$-square zero-one matrix
indexed by ordered pairs:
$$
M_i[(a,b),(d,c)]
  =\mathbf1\{d=b\}\mathbf1\{\phi(a,b,c)=y_i\}.
$$
Then
$$
|T_\phi^{-1}(y)|=\operatorname{tr}(M_0M_1\cdots M_{n-1}).
$$

Strategy and dependency map: expand the trace -> enforce overlaps ->
identify a labelled cyclic word -> impose exactly the target equations.

1. A term of the trace is a sequence of pair indices
   $p_0,p_1,\ldots,p_{n-1},p_n=p_0$. Its product is one exactly when
   each pair overlaps the next in the required coordinate and every local
   output condition is satisfied.
2. Write $p_i=(u_i,v_i)$. The overlap conditions are
   $v_i=u_{i+1}$ for every i, including the closing edge.
   Define $x_i=v_i$. These equations force
   $p_i=(x_{i-1},x_i)$. Thus each surviving term determines exactly one
   labelled word, not a rotation class.
3. Conversely, any labelled word gives exactly those pair indices, and
   its trace term survives precisely when
   $\phi(x_{i-1},x_i,x_{i+1})=y_i$ for every i.
   This is a bijection between surviving terms and the requested sources.
   The identity follows.

There is no recurrence assumption in this proof. For the already-defined
cycle-mex rule one merely substitutes
$\phi(a,b,c)=\operatorname{mex}\{a,c\}$ with a sufficiently large palette.
The substitution does not exploit mex or graph symmetry in the inverse
argument. Consequently presenting this uncompressed transfer-matrix trace,
even for every target, would not establish the distinct new mechanism
required by this batch. No assertion is made that stronger, genuinely
specific inverse theorems are impossible.
