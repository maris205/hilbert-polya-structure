## Useful scope guard: one addition operator need not have the full process's recurrent set

This is a hand counterexample within the already-known sandpile family, not
a newly nominated literal. Take three nonsink sites, toppling matrix
$$\Delta=\begin{pmatrix}2&0&0\\0&2&-1\\0&-1&2\end{pmatrix},$$
and stable heights $x\in\{0,1\}^3$. The graph is dissipative: site one has
two sink edges, and sites two and three have an edge between them and one
sink edge each. Under the fixed addition operator $a_1$,
$$a_1(x_1,x_2,x_3)=(1-x_1,x_2,x_3).$$
Every stable configuration is therefore periodic of exact period two.

Yet the configurations with $(x_2,x_3)=(0,0)$ are transient for the usual
full-support random-addition process. Once a grain is added to site two or
three, that pair can never return to $(0,0)$: without a toppling some grain
remains, while the last toppling in any nonempty avalanche sends a grain to
the other site, which by definition does not topple afterward. Additions at
site one leave the pair unchanged. With positive probability of additions
at sites two and three, the zero pair is eventually left and never revisited.
This proves that importing the entire random-addition recurrent-set statement
to an arbitrary single-site autonomous operator is invalid without additional
reachability hypotheses. It does not supply a new candidate theorem pair.
