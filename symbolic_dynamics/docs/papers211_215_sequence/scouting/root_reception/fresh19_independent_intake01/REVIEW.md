### 1. Fixed vertex-flip words

The two matrices act on the stated membership-bit order. Distinct coordinates
commute, so a state-independent word acts as one element of S3^V. Each local
coordinate order divides six; therefore the global sixth iterate is identity.
Its restriction to any closed invariant carrier is bijective, every state is
recurrent, and the predecessor is the inverse word. This is the appropriate
negative mechanism. [Brijder–Hoogeboom, Definition 4/Lemma 5 and group paragraph](https://arxiv.org/html/0909.4004v4)

The qualification matters: graph pivots are partial at singular principal

### 2. Numeric Isbell conjugation

For nonnegative f and symmetric d, Q reverses numeric pointwise order.
The displayed inequality implies Q²f≤f. Antitonicity gives Q³f≥Qf;
applying the same Q² inequality to Qf gives Q³f≤Qf. Hence Q³=Q.
If y=Qf, then Q²y=y; conversely a Q²-fixed y equals Q(Qy), so the image
is exactly Fix(Q²), reached within one step. The two-point (0,0)/(1,1)
example is a strict two-cycle and correctly prevents confusing Fix(Q²)
with Fix(Q). This distinguishes conjugate pairs from the tight-span fixed
equation. [Willerton, sections 2.1, 3.1 and 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1302.4370v1)

The proof is an elementary deduction for the displayed numeric operator,
not a new finite-grid registration. P106 uses subset polarity, DHC first
chooses a farthest-subset radius, and OF-K has two registers. Their literal
updates differ; no conjugacy or exact old iteration count is transferred.
