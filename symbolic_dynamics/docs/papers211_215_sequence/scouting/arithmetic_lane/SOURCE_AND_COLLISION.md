## Residual claims and mandatory future desk boundary

SPR time residual: every t-step source weakly majorizes a single explicit
positive tuple c[t], obtained by a support-increasing additive reverse rule;
the tuple attains that height. This gives a sharp joint n,M threshold. The
proof's Lemmas 1–2 must be checked for repeated entries and differing support
lengths, after deducting generic support descent and old reverse-minimum
ideas. It is stronger than a finite pattern or logarithmic potential.

SPR inverse residual: a disjoint two-case pivot/quotient decoder gives every
target fibre, including zero/one/full-support targets; a binomial comparison
over support sizes proves zero is the strict maximum. Division, finite
products, binomial counting and generic pivot coding themselves are old.
The value desk may find the remaining finite decoder/extremum too thin even
if correct. The time proof does not use these counts, and the inverse proof
does not use c[t], but logical separation does not establish independent
research value. No opinion in this author ledger substitutes for that desk.

Minor terminology boundary: preregistration's compressed phrase that CPRM
"retains a divisor" should be read only as retention at zero-quotient
coordinates (in particular a minimum). It has no single globally selected
divisor. Its exact cyclic literal above, rather than that shorthand, governs
the comparison. The immutable preregistration and science inputs are not
rewritten after the run.
