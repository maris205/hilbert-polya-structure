## 1. Euler cyclic Lotka–Volterra feedback

### Claim, assumptions and status

For every finite field $k=\mathbb F_q$ of characteristic greater than three,
consider the autonomous map on $k^3$
$$F(x,y,z)=(x(1+y-z),\ y(1+z-x),\ z(1+x-y)).$$
The maximum one-step fibre cardinality is exactly four. This static claim
is **PROVABLE AS STATED** below. An independent sharp temporal claim is
**NOT CURRENTLY JUSTIFIED**.

### Proof strategy and dependency map

Sum conservation reduces each fibre to two quadratic equations. Their
quotient algebra is spanned by four monomials, which bounds the number of
distinct solutions. The zero fibre supplies equality. No temporal theorem
is used, and no numerical examples are used as proof.

### Proof of the static claim

1. Pairwise cancellation gives $F_1+F_2+F_3=x+y+z$. For target
   $(a,b,c)$ put $s=a+b+c$. Every source has $z=s-x-y$ and satisfies
   $$x^2+2xy+(1-s)x-a=0,\qquad
     y^2+2xy-(1+s)y+b=0.\tag{1}$$
   Conversely (1) and $z=s-x-y$ give the target, including its third
   coordinate by conservation.

2. Work in the quotient $A=k[x,y]/I$, where $I$ is generated by (1).
   These equations express $x^2,y^2$ in the span
   $W=\operatorname{span}_k\{1,x,y,xy\}$. Set $U=x^2y$ and $V=xy^2$.
   Multiplication of the two equations by $y,x$ gives
   $$U+2V=-(1-s)xy+ay,\qquad
     2U+V=(1+s)xy-bx.$$
   Their determinant is $-3\ne0$, so $U,V\in W$. Consequently $W$
   contains 1 and is stable under multiplication by both $x$ and $y$.
   All monomials belong to $W$, hence $\dim_k A\le4$.

3. If (1) has $m$ distinct $k$-solutions, evaluation maps $A$ onto $k^m$.
   To see surjectivity, for each solution $P_i$ and each $P_j\ne P_i$
   choose a coordinate on which they differ. The product of the normalized
   coordinate differences gives a polynomial equal to 1 at $P_i$ and 0
   at all other $P_j$. Thus $m\le\dim A\le4$.

4. The zero fibre is exactly
   $$\{(0,0,0),(1,-1,0),(0,1,-1),(-1,0,1)\}.$$
   A solution with three nonzero coordinates would require
   $1+y-z=1+z-x=1+x-y=0$, whose sum contradicts $3\ne0$.
   Exactly one nonzero coordinate cannot map to zero. With exactly two,
   the equations give the three displayed points, which are distinct.
   Therefore the maximum is four and $F$ is noninjective. $\square$

### Temporal obstruction and decision

All diagonal points $(t,t,t)$ are fixed. The invariant boundary $z=0$,
$x+y=s$ carries the scalar map
$$x\longmapsto x(1+s-x).$$
Equivalently, with
$u=-x$ it is $u\mapsto u^2+(1+s)u$.
After translating $w=u+(1+s)/2$ this becomes
$$w\longmapsto w^2+(1-s^2)/4.$$
Thus a uniform global temporal theorem must account for these quadratic
boundary iterations; this desk does not supply one. The inverse extremum
is genuine but static and elementary. Neither an unresolved quartic
decoder nor larger finite samples would repair the missing temporal axis.
**No promotion or pilot recommendation.**

## 2. Mixed sum/difference-of-squares feedback

### Claim, assumptions and status

For every odd $q$, on $k^2$ define
$$Q(x,y)=(x^2+y^2,\ x^2-y^2).$$
Let $\chi$ be the quadratic character on $k$, with $\chi(0)=0$.
The all-target inverse and maximum-four claims below are **PROVABLE AS
STATED**. A sharp all-parameter temporal law is **NOT CURRENTLY JUSTIFIED**.

### Source and decomposition boundary

The introduction and natural-basis definition in Section 2 of
[Omirov, Rozikov and Velasco, A class of nilpotent evolution algebras](https://arxiv.org/html/1711.05030v1)
were read. The evolution-algebra primitive has distinct basis products
zero and prescribed basis squares. Choosing $e_1^2=e_1+e_2$ and
$e_2^2=e_1-e_2$ gives $Q$ as squaring. No nilpotency or classification
theorem of that paper is applied to this algebra. Classification of
algebras is not classification of state orbits under their square maps.

Bounded local/external exact-expression navigation did not retain a direct
literal hit. That is not a uniqueness or priority certificate. The
invertible linear mixing of two square coordinates fully consumes the
one-step inverse mechanism; calling it nonassociative adds no credit.

### Proof strategy, dependencies and static proof

1. Inverting the linear output mixing gives exactly
   $$x^2=(a+b)/2,\qquad y^2=(a-b)/2.$$
   The elementary finite-field square-root count therefore gives
   $$\#Q^{-1}(a,b)=
   (1+\chi((a+b)/2))(1+\chi((a-b)/2)).$$
   This is evaluated for every target, including the zero and nonsquare
   boundaries; no target-state indicator enumeration is hidden in it.

2. Each factor is at most two. Target $(2,0)$ has precisely the four
   sources $(\pm1,\pm1)$, giving maximum four for every odd field.
   In particular the map is noninjective. Zero has the unique source zero.

3. On nonzero vectors, the induced projective update, in coordinate
   $t=y/x$, is
   $$f(t)=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2},\qquad f(\infty)=-1.$$
   The homogeneous pair has no common nonzero zero because 2 is
   invertible, so this is a total map of $\mathbb P^1(k)$.
   It has the projective two-cycle $0\leftrightarrow1$; this assertion
   does not identify the periods of every lifted vector.

4. Conditional on a projective cycle of length $L$, choose representatives
   $v_j$ with $Q(v_j)=c_jv_{j+1}$, where $c_j\ne0$ and indices are cyclic.
   Homogeneity gives the scalar return map
   $$a\longmapsto C a^{2^L},\qquad
     C=\prod_{j=0}^{L-1}c_j^{2^{L-1-j}}.$$
   Hence that conditional lift returns to an ordinary twisted power map.
   This is not a solution of the unknown projective temporal inventory.

### Decision and open risks

The exact inverse is only two independent square-root choices after an
invertible output change. The residual rational projective dynamics have
not received a sharp clock or evaluated cycle inventory here. No assertion
of conjugacy to a polynomial, Chebyshev or Lattes map is made. A claim of
two independent new axes is therefore unsupported. **No pilot.**
