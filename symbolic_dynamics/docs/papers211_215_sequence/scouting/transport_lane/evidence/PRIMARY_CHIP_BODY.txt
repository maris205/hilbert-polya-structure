L31: # Periods and Atomic Firing Sequences of Parallel Chip-Firing Games on Directed Graphs
L32: 
L33: David Ji Email: david.ji@mtsdstudent.us Affiliation: Montgomery High School, Skillman, 08558, New Jersey, United States of America    Michael Li Email: michaelli20180101@gmail.com Affiliation: University High School, Irvine, 92612, California, United States of America    Daniel Wang Email: danielw25@lakesideschool.org Affiliation: Lakeside School, Seattle, 98125, Washington, United States of America
L34: ###### Abstract
L35: In 1992, Bitar and Goles introduced the parallel chip-firing game on undirected graphs. Two years later, Prisner extended the game to directed graphs. While the properties of parallel chip-firing games on undirected graphs have been extensively studied, their analogs for parallel chip-firing games on directed graphs have been sporadic. In this paper, we prove the outstanding analogs of the core results of parallel chip-firing games on undirected graphs for those on directed graphs.
L36: We find the possible periods of a parallel chip-firing game on a directed simple cycle and introduce the method of Gauss-Jordan elimination on a Laplacian-like matrix to establish a lower bound on the maximum period of a parallel chip-firing game on an orientation of an undirected complete graph and an undirected complete bipartite graph.
L37: Finally, we expand the method of motors by Jiang, Scully, and Zhang to directed graphs to show that a binary string $s$ can be the atomic firing sequence of a vertex in a parallel chip-firing game on a strongly connected directed graph if and only if $s$ contains $1$ or $s=0$.

L47: ### 1 Introduction
L48: The parallel chip-firing game is an automaton on a finite, connected graph $G(V,\,E)$ that was first introduced by Bitar and Goles cite18†Bitar and Goles (1992) in 1992. A parallel chip-firing game begins with some chips distributed among each $v\in V$. On each subsequent round, all vertices with at least as many chips as neighbors simultaneously fire one chip to each of their neighbors. All other vertices wait.
L49: As observed by Bitar and Goles cite18†Bitar and Goles (1992) , all parallel chip-firing games are periodic with some minimal period length $T$, because the quantity of chips and the number of vertices in any parallel chip-firing game are fixed.
L50: The parallel chip-firing game on undirected graphs has been extensively studied for trees cite18†Bitar and Goles (1992) , simple cycles cite19†Dall’Asta (2006) , complete graphs cite20†Levine (2011) , and complete bipartite graphs cite21†Jiang (2010) . See Table cite22†1 for the possible period lengths of parallel chip-firing games on these graphs. For general graphs, Kominers and Kominers cite23†Kominers and Kominers (2010) showed that $T=1$ for all parallel chip-firing games with enough chips.
L51: Later, Bu, Choi, and Xu cite24†Bu et al. (2022) made this range of chips exact. Furthermore, Kiwi et al. cite25†Kiwi et al. (1994) showed that $T$ cannot be bounded by a polynomial in $|V|$. Lastly, Scully, Jiang, and Zhang cite26†Jiang et al. (2015) distinguished the possible atomic firing sequences — length-$T$ binary strings that indicate the rounds in which a vertex fires within a periodic sequence — of a vertex in a parallel chip-firing game.
L52: In 1994, Prisner cite27†Prisner (1994) extended the parallel chip-firing game to directed graphs. Prisner cite27†Prisner (1994) found that $T=1$ for all games on acyclic directed graphs and that there exists no polynomial bound on $|V|$ for the period of a parallel chip-firing game on a directed graph.
L53: Later, Goles and Prisner cite28†Goles and Prisner (2000) found that $T\leq|V|$ for all parallel chip-firing games on orientations of $\overline{K_{n}}*H$, where $\overline{K_{n}}$ is an empty graph, $*$ denotes the graph join operation, and $H$ is an arbitrary graph. Finally, Ndoundam, Tchuente, and Tadonki cite29†Ndoundam et al. (2010) characterized the possible periods on orientations of the $n$-cube graph.
L54: In this paper, we prove the analogs of results for the parallel chip-firing game on undirected graphs for those on directed graphs. In Section cite7†2 , we establish preliminaries. In Section cite8†3 , we characterize the possible periods of parallel chip-firing games on certain classes of directed graphs.
L55: In addition, we observe that while there always exists a parallel chip-firing game with $T=2$ on any undirected graph, the same is not true for orientations of complete directed graphs with at most four vertices. In Section cite13†4 , we prove that a binary string $s$ can be the atomic firing sequence of a vertex in a parallel chip-firing game on a strongly connected directed graph if and only if $s$ contains 1 or $s=0$. Finally, in Section cite14†5 , we present unsolved conjectures and areas for future study.

L64: ### 2 Preliminaries
L65: We define a directed graph $D=(V,\,E)$ as a set of vertices $V$ and a set of ordered pairs of vertices (edges) $E$. The edge $(u,\,v)$ for $u,\,v\in V$ is said to be directed from $u$ to $v$.
L66: We define an orientation of an undirected graph $G=(V,\,E)$ to be a directed graph $D=(V,\,E^{\prime})$ such that either $(u,\,v)\in E^{\prime}$ or $(v,\,u)\in E^{\prime}$ if and only if there exists an edge between $u$ and $v$ in $G$, but there do not exist vertices $u$ and $v$ such that $(u,\,v)$ and $(v,\,u)$ are both elements in $E^{\prime}$. The in-degree of a vertex $v$ is denoted $\deg^{-}v$ and is the number of edges directed from another vertex to $v$.
L67: The out-degree of a vertex $v$ is denoted $\deg^{+}v$ and is the number of edges directed from $v$ to another vertex. In the directed graph in Figure cite33†1 , $\deg^{+}v_{3}=2$ and $\deg^{-}v_{3}=1$. The degree of a vertex $v$ is denoted $\deg v$ and is the total number of edges including $v$. In Figure cite33†1 , $\deg v_{3}=3$. The distance between two vertices $u$ and $v$ is the length of the shortest path from $u$ to $v$ and is denoted $d(u,\,v)$.
L68: In the undirected graph in Figure cite33†1 , $d(v_{1},\,v_{4})=2$. In the directed graph, $d(v_{1},\,v_{4})=3$. For parallel chip-firing games on directed graphs, a vertex fires when the number of chips it possesses exceeds its out-degree, upon which the vertex fires one chip across each outgoing edge.
L69: Figure 1: An undirected graph $G$ (left) and an orientation of $G$.
L70: Bitar and Goles cite18†Bitar and Goles (1992) noted that the fixed numbers of chips and vertices make every parallel chip-firing game on a finite undirected graph periodic. Prisner cite27†Prisner (1994) noted that the same is true for parallel chip-firing games on finite directed graphs.
L71: We say that a parallel chip-firing game on a directed graph has period $T$ if $T$ is the smallest integer for which there exists a round $t_{0}$ such that every vertex has the same number of chips in round $t$ as in round $t+T$ for all $t\geq t_{0}$. We denote the least such $t_{0}$ as round 0. Let $c_{t}(v)$ be the number of chips on a vertex $v$ at the beginning of round $t$. Let $F_{t}(v)$ be $1$ if $v$ fires on round $t$ and $0$ if it does not.
L72: Let $f_{v}=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}F_{t}(v)$ be the total number of times $v$ fires in the first $T$ rounds.
L73: We say that a directed graph $D=(V,\,E)$ is strongly connected if there exists a path in both directions between every pair of vertices $v,\,u\in V$. A strongly connected component of $D$ is a subgraph of $D$ that is strongly connected and maximal: no more vertices or edges may be included while keeping the subgraph strongly connected. We let the condensation of a directed graph $D$ be a directed graph with all strongly connected components of $D$ as vertices.
L74: Borrowing notation from Prisner cite27†Prisner (1994) , we define a sink component as a strongly connected component that forms a sink in the condensation of $D$. In Figure cite34†2 , SCC3 is a sink component. We say that a vertex is forever passive if it does not fire in all future states.
L75: Figure 2: A directed graph (left) with strongly connected components boxed and its condensation (right).
L76: 
L77: Prisner cite27†Prisner (1994) found that only sink components are relevant to the behavior of parallel chip-firing games on a directed graph.
L78: ###### Proposition 2.1 ((cite27†Prisner, 1994 , Proposition 2.2)).
L79: 
L80: In every round $t\geq 0$ in a parallel chip-firing game on $D$, all vertices in nonsink components are forever passive.
L81: By cite35†Proposition 2.1 , in any parallel chip-firing game on a directed graph with multiple sink components, the number of chips in each sink component is eventually fixed. The game can then be considered a superposition of independent parallel chip-firing games on each sink component. In this paper, we will therefore assume that all graphs are strongly connected (i.e. the only sink component of a graph $D$ is $D$ itself) unless otherwise stated.
L82: We first prove that no vertices are forever passive in any parallel chip-firing game on a strongly connected directed graph.
L83: ###### Lemma 2.2.
L84: 
L85: In any parallel chip-firing game on a strongly connected directed graph $D$ with $T>1$, no vertices are forever passive.
L86: ###### Proof.
L87: As $T>1$, at least one vertex $v^{\prime}$ must fire in each period. Since $c_{0}(v)=c_{T}(v)$ for all $v\in V$, each of the vertices which $v^{\prime}$ fires to must also fire in each period. Similarly, all the vertices that are distance 2 from $v^{\prime}$ must fire in each period. In general, every vertex $u$ for which there exists a directed path from $v^{\prime}$ to $u$ must fire in each period. Since $D$ is strongly connected, such a path exists to any vertex $u\in V$.
L88: Thus, all vertices fire at least once in each period. ∎

L91: #### 3.1 Trees
L92: 
L93: The possible periods of parallel chip-firing games on directed trees were identified by Prisner cite27†Prisner (1994) . We begin by citing his result.
L94: 
L95: ###### Proposition 3.1 ((cite27†Prisner, 1994 , Corollary 2.3)).
L96: 
L97: $T=1$ for all parallel chip-firing games on directed acyclic graphs.
L98: 
L99: For completeness, we state the following direct corollaries of cite36†Proposition 3.1 .
L100: ###### Corollary 3.2.
L101: 
L102: $T=1$ for all parallel chip-firing games on directed paths.
L103: 
L104: ###### Corollary 3.3.
L105: 
L106: $T=1$ for all parallel chip-firing games on directed non-path trees.
L107: #### 3.2 Cycles
L108: 
L109: Consider the simple directed cycle $C_{n}$. We label the vertices of the cycle $v_{0},v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n-1}$ such that there exists an edge directed from $v_{i+1}$ to $v_{i}$ for all $0\leq i\leq n-1$, with indices taken modulo $n.$ We first show that if any vertex ever has more than 1 chip, the period of the game is 1.
L110: 
L111: ###### Lemma 3.4.
L112: 
L113: In a parallel chip-firing game on $C_{n}$, if $c_{t}(v)>1$ for any $v$, then $T=1$.
L114: ###### Proof.
L115: 
L116: Suppose $c_{t}(v_{0})>1$. Note that if $c_{t}(v)\geq 1$ then $c_{t+1}(v)\leq c_{t}(v)$, so we must have $1<c_{t}(v_{0})=c_{t+1}(v_{0})=\dots=c_{t+T-1}(v_{0})=c_{t+T}(v_{0})$. We conclude that $v_{0}$ must fire on every round and so $v_{1}$ must fire on every round as well. By induction, we conclude that every vertex fires on every round, so $T=1$. ∎
L117: 
L118: We now prove our main theorem about directed cycles.
L119: ###### Theorem 3.5.
L120: 
L121: The possible periods $T$ of a parallel chip-firing game on $C_{n}$ are $T=i$ for all $i$ such that $i\;|\;n.$
L122: ###### Proof.
L123: 
L124: Consider a parallel chip-firing game on $C_{n}$ with period $T$. If $T=1$, we have $T\;|\;n$. Otherwise, by cite37†Lemma 3.4 , we have $c_{t}(v)\leq 1$ for all $v$ and $t$. We conclude that $c_{t}(v)=F_{t}(v)$ for all $v$ and $t$.
L125: Note that if $F_{t}(v_{i})=1,$ then $v_{i-1}$ has one chip after round $t,$ so $F_{t+1}(v_{i-1})=1.$ Thus, if $F_{t}(v)=1,$ then $F_{t+n}(v)=1.$ Additionally, if $F_{t+n}(v)=1,$ then $F_{t+nT}(v)=1$, and by the definition of a period, $F_{t}(v)=1$ as well. Therefore, $F_{t}(v)=F_{t+n}(v)$ for all $v$ and $t$, so $c_{t}(v)=c_{t+n}(v)$ for all $v$ and $t$. Thus, we must have $T\;|\;n.$
L126: To construct a parallel chip-firing game with period $T=i$ for any $i\;|\;n,$ place a single chip on each vertex $v_{j}$ that satisfies $j\equiv 0\pmod{i}$. On round $t,$ there will be a chip on each vertex $v_{j}$ that satisfies $j\equiv-t\pmod{i}.$ The smallest $T$ for which $c_{v}(t)=c_{v}(t+T)$ is therefore $T=i$. ∎
L127: #### 3.3 Complete Graphs
L128: 
L129: We first note as a surprising aside that while there exists a parallel chip-firing game on any undirected graph with $T=2$, it is possible for there to be no orientations of a graph that allow for a parallel chip-firing game with $T=2$. We begin by citing a lemma from Jiang cite21†Jiang (2010) .
L130: ###### Lemma 3.6 ((cite21†Jiang, 2010 , Proposition 2.5)).
L131: 
L132: For any parallel chip-firing game on an undirected graph, $c_{T}(v)=c_{0}(v)$ for all $v\in V$ if and only if $\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}F_{t}(v)$ is constant for all $v\in V$.
L133: 
L134: In cite38†Lemma 6.1 , we show that there exists a parallel chip-firing game on any undirected graph $G$ such that every vertex fires exactly once in the first 2 rounds. However, parallel chip-firing games on directed graphs behave differently, as demonstrated by the following lemma.
L135: ###### Lemma 3.7.
L136: 
L137: No parallel chip-firing games on any orientation of $K_{n}$ with $n\leq 4$ have $T=2$.
L138: ###### Proof.
L139: 
L140: Note that $K_{2}$ is a path, and $K_{3}$ is a cycle. For these cases, see cite39†Corollary 3.2 and cite30†Theorem 3.5 , respectively. Then consider a parallel chip-firing game on a strongly connected orientation $D=(V,\,E)$ of $K_{4}$ with $T=2$. Recall that because $D$ is strongly connected, each vertex $v$ in $D$ must satisfy $\deg^{+}v>0$ and $\deg^{-}v>0$. Also, by Lemma cite40†2.2 , each vertex must fire at least once in each period.
L141: Since $\deg v=3$, $v$ must be one of two types: $\deg^{-}v=1$ and $\deg^{+}v=2$, or $\deg^{-}v=2$ and $\deg^{+}v=1$. Additionally, since $\sum_{v\in V}\deg^{-}v=\sum_{v\in V}\deg^{+}v$, there must be two vertices of each type. Let $\deg^{+}v_{1}=\deg^{+}v_{2}=1$, and let $\deg^{+}u_{1}=\deg^{+}u_{2}=2$.
L142: Note that there must exist an edge between $v_{1}$ and $v_{2}$ and that regardless of its direction, one of the two vertices must view it as its single out-edge. Thus, there is only one edge directed from $v_{1}$ or $v_{2}$ to $u_{1}$ or $u_{2}$. Without loss of generality, let it be directed from $v_{1}$ to $u_{1}$. On the other hand, there are three edges directed from $u_{1}$ or $u_{2}$ to $v_{1}$ or $v_{2}$.
L143: Since $T=2$, we must have $f_{v_{1}}\leq 2$. However, $f_{u_{1}}\geq 1$ and $f_{u_{2}}\geq 1$. Then there must be at most two chips that travel from a vertex in $V=\{v_{1},\,v_{2}\}$ to one in $U=\{u_{1},\,u_{2}\}$, but at least three chips that travel from a vertex in $U$ to one in $V$. It is impossible for every vertex to have the same number of chips after 2 rounds, so $T\neq 2$ for all parallel chip-firing games on $D$. ∎
L144: Next, let $\tau(n)$ be the maximum possible period of a parallel chip-firing game on an orientation of $K_{n}$. We show that $\tau(n)$ is at least asymptotic to $(n-1)!$. We conjecture that $\tau(n)$ is indeed asymptotic to $(n-1)!$
L145: ###### Conjecture 3.8.
L146: 
L147: It holds that $\tau(n)=\Theta((n-1)!)$.

L166: The following lemma shows that vertex 3 fires the most in all parallel chip-firing games on $K_{n}$ with $n\geq 4$.
L167: ###### Lemma 3.9.
L168: 
L169: For any $n\geq 4,$ all parallel chip-firing games on $K_{n}$ satisfy $f_{3}\geq f_{i}$ for all $1\leq i\leq n$.
L170: ###### Proof.
L171: 
L172: Since $\deg^{+}v_{3}=1$, it must fire once in each period for every chip received in that period. Note that $v_{3}$ receives chips from all other vertices except $v_{4}$. Thus, $f_{3}\geq f_{i}$ for all $i\neq 4$. Also note that $\deg^{+}v_{2}=1$, and that $v_{2}$ receives chips from $v_{4}$. Then $f_{2}\geq f_{4}$. Since $f_{3}\geq f_{2}$, we have $f_{3}\geq f_{4}$. ∎
L173: Let the $c$-convergent period $T_{c}(G)$ of a graph $G$ be $\min(T)$ over all parallel chip-firing games on $G$ with at least $c$ chips. Note that $T_{c}(G)=1$ for any undirected graph $G$, regardless of $c$ cite24†Bu et al. (2022) . We will show that the same is not true for directed graphs.
L174: ###### Theorem 3.10.
L175: 
L176: For the useful orientation of $K_{n}$, with $c=\frac{n(n-3)}{2}+1$, we have $T_{c}(K_{n})=\Omega((n-1)!)$.
L177: ###### Proof.
L178: 
L179: Since the number of chips on each vertex does not change after a full period, the following equations must hold.

We arbitrarily drop the first equation, leaving a system of $n-1$ independent equations in $n$ variables. These remaining equations are linearly independent, as can be seen later in the un-augmented form of Matrix cite43†3.4 , whose rank is equal to the number of rows.
L187: By definition, each of $f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,\,f_{n}\geq 0.$ By cite40†Lemma 2.2 , if any $f_{i}=0$, we have $T=1$ and all vertices are forever passive. However, the largest possible $c$ for which all vertices can be forever passive is $\sum_{v}(\deg^{+}v-1)=|E|-|V|=\frac{n(n-3)}{2}<c$. Thus, we further constrain $f_{i}>0$ for all $i$. Note that for any solution $(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,\,f_{n})$, it must be true that $(\lambda f_{1},\lambda f_{2},\ldots,\,\lambda f_{n})$ is also a solution.
L188: To find the minimal solution, we set $f_{1}=1.$ System cite42†3.1 has now been reduced to the following.

L193: Note that these equations are linearly independent. Therefore, we can let a well-defined $T_{n}$ denote the maximal value of $f_{i}$ satisfying System cite44†3.2 . By cite45†Lemma 3.9 , for any solution with $n\geq 4$, we have $f_{3}\geq f_{i}$ for all $i$. Therefore, $T_{n}=f_{3}$ if $n\geq 4$. We claim that, for $n\geq 4$,
