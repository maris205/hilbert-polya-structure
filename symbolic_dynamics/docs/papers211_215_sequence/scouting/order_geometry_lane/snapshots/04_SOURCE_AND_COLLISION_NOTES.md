## EC: an old static transform, a bounded feedback candidate

1. Thomas Flanitzer, *The eccentricity transform (computation)*,
   PRIP-TR-107, July 26, 2006. [Primary report](https://www.prip.tuwien.ac.at/pripfiles/trs/tr107.pdf).
   Actual PDF opened; title/date, Section 2 Eq. (1), Section 3 and beginning
   of Section 4 read. It assigns the maximum metric distance to every
   point of a fixed graph/shape. Its maximum is the diameter. Translation
   invariance and computational iteration over a fixed shape are discussed.
   Those are old background, not the autonomous remeasurement clock.

2. Majid Banaeyan and Walter G. Kropatsch, *Reducing the Computational
   Complexity of the Eccentricity Transform of a Tree*, GbRPR 2023,
   LNCS 14121, pp. 160–171, DOI
   [10.1007/978-3-031-42795-4_15](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-42795-4_15).
   [Author PDF](https://www.prip.tuwien.ac.at/people/krw/more/download.php?key=Banaeyan2023b),
   [institutional metadata](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/193260).
   Browser PDF opening timed out; a subsequent actual curl-to-pdftotext
   read succeeded. Read manuscript pp. 1–4 and beginning of p. 5, especially
   Section 3.1 Propositions 1–2 and Eq. (4). These explicitly identify
   line-segment eccentric points as its two endpoints and give the maximum
   of the two endpoint distances. This exactly owns EC's static endpoint
   reduction. The inspected context computes a fixed tree's transform;
   it does not state the finite coordinate-feedback height (2.4). That
   scoped observation is not a claim to have inspected every later source.

3. Adrian Ion, *The eccentricity transform of n-dimensional shapes with
   and without boundary*, TU Wien dissertation, 2009.
   [Primary record](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/13572).
   The record and abstract were actually opened. The linked dissertation
   PDF timed out; no full-dissertation read is claimed. The record supports
   the existence of an extensive old static-transform surface. Likewise
   Ion–Kropatsch–Andres' 2008 *Euclidean Eccentricity Transform by Discrete
   Arc Paving* has an opened [primary metadata record](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/52449),
   while its PDF opening failed. It is not cited here for an uninspected
   theorem. Search crawl dates in 2026 are not these works' publication dates.

Exact adapter: for a source interval [a,a+r], EC is the coordinate map
x_i -> max(x_i-a,a+r-x_i). Solving it gives x_i=a+b_i or a+r-b_i. Requiring
both extrema in the source gives the complete inverse, without a hidden
extra dynamical lemma. This static folding/binary-occupancy mechanism is
fully deducted in EC_PROOF_PACKAGE.md Sections 3–5. The author therefore
closes EC with no demonstrated independent inverse research axis. The
image census cannot replace it. No new paper ID, independent review, or
positive residual conjunction is asserted.

Internal searches covered papers and failed/current scout proof/report
files using eccentricity, farthest, maximum distance, midpoint, and literal
max-distance terminology. The located graph-eccentricity mentions describe
different graph maps; no false literal equality with them is claimed.

## OR: located classical coordinates, complete temporal transfer

Yuexiao Xu and Sherry H. F. Yan, *Alternating Permutations with Restrictions
and Standard Young Tableaux*, Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 19(2),
P49 (published June 28, 2012), DOI
[10.37236/2306](https://doi.org/10.37236/2306).
[Publisher PDF](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v19i2p49/pdf/).
The actual PDF title/metadata and Section 2.1, printed pp. 3–4, were read.
It gives the standard prefix-count definition of Yamanouchi words and the
bijection recording the row containing each tableau entry. Our exact
deduction then identifies prefix occurrence rank with the column coordinate,
hence tableau transpose. The full adapter is in OTHER_MAP_ADAPTERS.md.
This is not a claim that Xu–Yan study the full feedback operator on all
words; the entire recurrent temporal mechanism nevertheless transfers.

Internal P194, `papers/194-least-raising-crystal-words/main.tex`, was read
through its classical fixed-word/SYT/RSK census and surrounding proof.
Its operator is a least-colour crystal raising step, not OR. The shared
classical coordinate surface receives zero credit, and the literal
transpose adapter, not a mistaken P194 conjugacy, kills the candidate.

## CP: old circular linear probing and a simple normalizer

Jean-François Marckert, lecture text *Limit of random walks* on his
[author site](https://www.labri.fr/perso/marckert/GRAZ.pdf).
The actual PDF was opened; browser find did not locate the desired section,
so curl-to-pdftotext was actually used to read Section 3.11, printed
pp. 36–37. It defines the precise cyclic linear-probing insertion model,
including displacements and filling the last slot. This is an author
exposition of an old primitive, not a newly dated article and not a claim
that it studies displacement feedback.

The image restriction d_i<=i and complement action i-d_i give the entire
feedback clock. A parking-placement permutation yields the full inverse
feasibility adapter in OTHER_MAP_ADAPTERS.md. Both mechanisms receive zero
independent value credit. Internal P192's actual circular-parking proof
context (main.tex near its fixed-factorization theorem) was read; it uses
N cars on N+1 slots and quotienting by translation, unlike this n/n map.
Do not import its Hurwitz dynamics or its census as a literal CP theorem.

## MG: exact gap adapter, but not the opened candy rule

Deepak Bal and Joseph DeGaetani, *A note on a candy sharing game*, Art of
Discrete and Applied Mathematics 4(1), P1.02 (2021), DOI
[10.26493/2590-9770.1304.2cf](https://doi.org/10.26493/2590-9770.1304.2cf).
[Author PDF](https://msuweb.montclair.edu/~bald/files/candy-sharing.pdf),
[institutional metadata](https://researchwith.montclair.edu/en/publications/a-note-on-a-candy-sharing-game/).
Actual PDF opened; introduction, generalized rule/Theorem 1.1, and
conclusion/reference contexts read. Its players redistribute equal shares
then receive candy to restore divisibility. This is a nearby rounded
sharing process, not MG's sum-conserving ceil/floor gap rule. No exact owner
identity is inferred. MG is killed because no full-parameter two-axis
contract was proved, with its explicit gap/anchor adapter retained.
