## Four literal proposals

All graphs below are labelled, simple and undirected on `[n]`, with isolates
retained. Their names are local handles, not claims of new mathematical
mechanisms. Unless stated otherwise, `n=0,1` have the unique empty graph.

1. **CBF — complemented bridge forest.** Let `B(G)` be the spanning graph
   consisting exactly of those edges whose deletion increases the number of
   connected components. Put `F(G)=K_n minus B(G)`. This is not deletion of
   the bridges: *all* pairs except old bridges become output edges.
2. **ZFW — zero-forcing frontier.** The carrier contains pairs `(G,S)` with
   `S subset [n]`; the graph coordinate never changes. Replace `S` by exactly
   the white vertices `v outside S` for which some black `u in S` has
   `N_G(u) minus S={v}`. Old black vertices are not retained. No force gives
   the empty subset. This differs literally from ordinary monotone zero
   forcing, whose update unions the frontier with the old black set.
3. **MCS — minimum-cut Seidel feedback.** For `n>=2` list all proper subsets
   `S` containing label 0. Minimize the pair `(number of old crossing edges,
   integer bitmask of S)` lexicographically; toggle every pair across the
   selected cut, including nonedges. Ties and complements are fixed by this
   convention; there is no external scheduler.
4. **CII — chromatic implicit-identity graph.** For `n>=1`, join distinct
   `u,v` exactly when `c(u)=c(v)` in every proper coloring
   `c:[n]->[chi(G)]`. Thus the output is the equivalence graph of forced
   equal colors, not the original graph's complement or a chosen coloring.
