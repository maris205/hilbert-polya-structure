\section{Exact entrance times and a sharp clock}\label{sec:clock}

Let $g=f_{Z,W}\in T(\On)$. Call an equal pair $(c,c)$ in its ordered
endpoint/value list an \emph{anchor}. The last pair $(n,n)$ is an
anchor. Before the first anchor and between consecutive anchors, the
strict pairs have the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strictlist}
 w_1<z_1<w_2<z_2<\cdots<w_r<z_r<c,
\end{equation}
where $c$ is the following anchor and the indices are local to this
interval. Define $R(g)$ as the maximum number $r$ of strict pairs in
one such list, allowing empty lists and setting $R(g)=0$ if there are
no strict pairs.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:peeling}
Under one update of $T$, the anchors of $g$ persist exactly. The list
\eqref{eq:strictlist} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:peeling}
 (z_1,w_2),\ (z_2,w_3),\ldots,(z_{r-1},w_r),\ (c,c),
\end{equation}
where only the final anchor remains if $r=0$ or $r=1$.
The lists in different anchor intervals evolve independently.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For the source $g=f_{Z,W}$, the next support pair is $X=W$, $A=Z$,
because $n\in Z$. Lemma~\ref{lem:product} preserves their intersection.
For $i<r$, the least member of $W$ not below $z_i$ is $w_{i+1}$.
The two final labels $z_r$ and $c$ both have ceiling $c$ in $W$.
Recomputing the rightmost kernel endpoints therefore gives precisely
\eqref{eq:peeling}. If the list is empty, $c$ remains its own ceiling.
Each anchor belongs to both supports and separates their ceiling
intervals, so no selected label crosses an anchor. This proves the
independence of the intervals as well as the update formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:time}
The fixed states and recurrent states of $T$ are exactly
$e_C$ with $n\in C\subseteq[n]$. Every source $f=f_{Y,X}$ terminates at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminal}
 e_{X\cap(Y\cup\{n\})}.
\end{equation}
For an image state $g$, its entrance time is $\tau(g)=R(g)$. For a
general source,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:time}
 \tau(f)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,& f=e_C\text{ for some }n\in C\subseteq[n],\\
 1+R(T(f)),&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:peeling}, each nonempty list loses its first and last
endpoints. After $t$ updates, a list initially containing $2r$ labels
retains exactly its middle $2\max\{r-t,0\}$ labels, with their two
endpoint roles alternating. Thus it disappears at exactly time $r$.
At every earlier step a strict pair remains; that list changes because
its two distinct outside endpoints are removed. Hence $g$ reaches an
anchor-only state at exactly time $R(g)$.

An anchor-only state has $W=Z=C$ and is $e_C$, which is fixed by
\eqref{eq:update}. Every source reaches the image after one step, so
all orbits stabilize and no nontrivial cycle exists. The anchors in that
first image are $X\cap(Y\cup\{n\})$ by~\eqref{eq:anchors}; they
never change. This proves~\eqref{eq:terminal}. A fixed initial source
has entrance time zero. Every other source needs its initial step and
then the exact remaining image-state time, proving~\eqref{eq:time}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:sharp}
For $H_n=\max_{f\in\On}\tau(f)$,
\[
 H_1=0,\qquad H_n=\left\lceil\frac n2\right\rceil\quad(n\ge2).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A strict list of $r$ pairs uses $2r$ distinct labels below its following
anchor. Therefore $R(g)\le\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor$ for every image
state. Theorem~\ref{thm:time} gives
$H_n\le1+\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor=\lceil n/2\rceil$ for $n\ge2$.

For $n=2m+1\ge3$, take
\[
 X=\{2,4,\ldots,2m,n\},\qquad
 Y=\{1,3,\ldots,2m-1,n\}.
\]
The first image of $f_{Y,X}$ consists of the $m$ strict pairs
$(1,2),(3,4),\ldots,(2m-1,2m)$ followed by $(n,n)$.
The source is not fixed: its value at $1$ is $1$, whereas its successor's
value there is $2$. Thus its entrance time is $1+m$.

For $n=2m\ge2$, take
\[
 X=\{2,4,\ldots,2m\},\qquad Y=\{1,3,\ldots,2m-1\}.
\]
Completing $Y$ by $n$ makes the last two second-projection values merge
under $e_X$. The first image has $m-1$ strict pairs
$(1,2),\ldots,(2m-3,2m-2)$ and the anchor $(n,n)$.
Again the value at $1$ changes from $1$ to $2$, so the entrance time is
$m$. This includes $m=1$: the first image is already the constant-2
projection. Finally, at $n=1$ the sole map is fixed.
\end{proof}
