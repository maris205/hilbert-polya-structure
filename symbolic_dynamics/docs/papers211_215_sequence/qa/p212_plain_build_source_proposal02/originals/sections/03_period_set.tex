\section{The complete period set}
\begin{corollary}\label{cor:set}
At $n=1$ the sole state is fixed. For $n\ge2$ the attained periods are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:set}
 \{1,\ldots,2n\}\ \cup\ \{2n+2,2n+4,\ldots,4n-4\},
\end{equation}
where a reversed range is empty. The maximum is $4n-4$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Write $s\le n$ for core size. Odd periods beyond $1$ come from short-cycle
rows: the short figure-eight gives $3$, and a short barbell has
$p=2s+2-(a+b)\le2s$, with odd value $2s-1$. A nonshort figure-eight or
nontrivial theta has even period $2s+2\le4n-4$ for $s\ge3$.
A nonshort barbell has $a+b\ge4$ and
$p=4s+4-2(a+b)\le4s-4$. The $n=2$ cases follow directly from the table.

Periods $1,2,3$ arise from a double loop, triple-direct theta, and
figure-eight $(1,2)$. For $2\le k\le n$, a barbell $(a,b,c)=(1,1,k-1)$
has $s=k$ and period $2k$. For $2\le k\le n-1$, the barbell
$(1,2,k-1)$ has $s=k+1$ and period $2k+1$. These give all values through
$2n$. A theta $(1,1,s-1)$, $3\le s\le n$, realizes half-periods
$4,\ldots,n+1$. For each $6\le k\le2n-2$, put
$c=\max(1,k-n-1)$ and $r=k-2c$. Then $r\ge4$ and
$s=r+c-1\le n$: if $k\le n+2$, $s=k-2$; otherwise $s=n$ and
$r=2n+2-k\ge4$. The barbell $(1,r-1,c)$ realizes period $2k$.
Together these cover every remaining even value; the small values of $n$
are included by the stated empty ranges. Pad any smaller core by inactive
fixed loops. For sharpness use the two-loop barbell at $n=2$, theta
$(1,1,2)$ at $n=3$, and barbell $(1,3,n-3)$ for $n\ge4$.
\end{proof}
