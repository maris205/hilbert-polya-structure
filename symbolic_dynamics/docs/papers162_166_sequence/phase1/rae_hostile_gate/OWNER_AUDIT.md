## 2. External source subtraction

### 2.1 Coupon collector

Erdős and Rényi,
[*On a Classical Problem of Probability Theory*](https://real.mtak.hu/201134/),
*Publ. Math. Inst. Hung. Acad. Sci.* 6 (1961), 215--220, is a primary
classical source in the coupon-collection line.  RAE absorption is exactly the
collection time for the letters in the initial support, with null draws among
the other `q-b` letters.  The inclusion--exclusion CDF, `q H_b` expectation,
last-coupon symmetry, and maximal-support extremum receive zero contribution
credit.

### 2.2 Alphabet projection / erasing morphism

Subalphabet projection is a standard free-monoid operation: retain letters in
one subalphabet and erase the others.  As one primary technical use, Lohrey,
[*Word Problems and Membership Problems on Compressed Words*](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/S0097539704445950),
*SIAM J. Comput.* 35 (2006), 1210--1240, DOI
[`10.1137/S0097539704445950`](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539704445950),
explicitly uses subalphabet projection morphisms in its word setting.  This is
not a literal random-chain owner; it makes the map `pi_C`, preservation of
letter order, and composition by alphabet intersection zero credit.

### 2.3 Commuting-idempotent semigroup walks

Brown,
[*Semigroups, Rings, and Markov Chains*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0006145),
*J. Theoret. Probab.* 13 (2000), 871--938, DOI
[`10.1023/A:1007822931408`](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1007822931408),
develops eigenvalues, multiplicities, diagonalization, and eigenspace
projections for random walks on left-regular bands.  Ayyer, Schilling,
Steinberg, and Thiéry,
[*Markov chains, R-trivial monoids and representation theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.4250),
*Internat. J. Algebra Comput.* 25 (2015), 169--231, DOI
[`10.1142/S0218196715400081`](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218196715400081),
extends the spectrum/multiplicity and absorption toolkit to R-trivial
monoids.

RAE's erasers generate a Boolean semilattice of commuting idempotents.  Its
action on capped words is an elementary representation of that semilattice,
and its average transition operator is simultaneously diagonalizable.  The
specialized eigenvalues `1-b/q` and support-count multiplicities are easy to
derive directly, but they are not an owner-resistant theorem axis.

### 2.4 Stirling and binomial ingredients

The identities

```text
# onto histories [t] -> an s-set = s! S(t,s),
# words of length m using exactly b of q letters = (q)_b S(m,b),
# placements of a retained m-letter subsequence in n positions = binom(n,m)
```

are classical elementary enumerations.  The rational OGF in (G11) is the
ordinary binomial series applied to those counts.  All receive zero credit.

## 5. Same-batch audit

### 5.1 RPS precedent

`phase1/rps_hostile_gate/` killed the random-permutation fixed-point sieve.
There, cumulative histories also collapse to a meet/projection object, and
the endpoint kernel, hitting transforms, and elementary markings were judged
one generic random-meet engine.  RAE is word-valued rather than set-valued,
but its history collapses even further, to an ordinary coupon support, and its
second axis is explicitly a column sum.  It cannot be treated more leniently.

### 5.2 RTI

RTI intersects a subset of `F_2^d` with random translates and compresses the
history to a translation span.  Its surviving result is a target- and
source-weighted inverse polynomial indexed by the target stabilizer and
outside affine cosets.  That polynomial is not a marginal of the rank clock.
The independent RTI gate therefore found a genuine second axis after
morphology and random-rank subtraction.  RAE has no analogue: target
dependence reduces to length and support size, and (G1) consumes the fibre.

### 5.3 P158 CIC

P158 intersects a graph with independent random cut graphs.  Complementary
history words determine complete-bipartite components, and the every-labelled
target atlas includes a real resource obstruction (`r=R,z>0`).  RAE shares
random intersection/history-surjection vocabulary but lacks a comparable
target geometry or obstruction.  Keeping it alongside CIC and RTI would spend
a further batch slot on the weakest version of the same silhouette.

### 5.4 Other current survivors

The currently green CEF/CSD mechanisms are deterministic cyclic feedback and
Johnson-shadow dynamics, respectively; they do not directly own RAE.  Their
separation does not repair RAE's own missing second axis.  Same-batch breadth
therefore supports replacement rather than promotion.
