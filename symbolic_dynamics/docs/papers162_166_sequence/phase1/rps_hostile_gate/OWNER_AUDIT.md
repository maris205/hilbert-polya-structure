# RPS focused primary owner audit

**Search date:** 2026-09-03 UTC  
**Decision:** `KILL_ROUTINE_OWNER_AND_PORTFOLIO_COLLISION`

## 1. Claims searched

The search separated five possible contribution axes:

1. the exact common fixed-point set of independent uniform permutations;
2. a Markov chain obtained by repeated intersection with permutation fixed
   sets;
3. Boolean-zeta diagonalization of a random intersection/meet walk;
4. absorption transforms of that decreasing chain; and
5. cycle-marked histories with prescribed fixed labels.

Queries included multiple variants of:

```text
common fixed points independent uniform random permutations
random homomorphism free group symmetric group common fixed points
intersection fixed point sets random permutation Markov chain
random meet semilattice Markov chain zeta Mobius eigenvalues
left regular band random walk Boolean lattice intersection
prescribed fixed points cycle count generating polynomial
common fixed points cycle marked inclusion exclusion
```

The searches included 2024--2026 variants.  No exact paper using the name
“random-permutation fixed-point sieve” was found; that name-level non-hit has
no evidentiary value.

## 2. Common-fixed-point and fixed-set owners

### 2.1 Puder/word-measure programme

Hanany and Puder, [_Word Measures on Symmetric Groups_](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.00897),
IMRN 2023, DOI
[`10.1093/imrn/rnac084`](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnac084), studies
permutations obtained from independent uniform images of free generators.
The paper's current arXiv record was revised in February 2026.

The accompanying FPSAC exposition explicitly passes from fixed points of one
word to **common fixed points of several words**, equivalently common fixed
points of the image of a subgroup under a uniform random homomorphism
`F_r -> S_n`.  When the subgroup is freely generated by the chosen basis
letters, their images are independent uniform permutations.  This is exactly
the random common-fixed-point object in RPS equation (1), before it is
intersected with a deterministic initial set.

Puder and Parzanchevski,
[_Measure Preserving Words are Primitive_](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.3269),
JAMS 28 (2015), 63--97, DOI
[`10.1090/S0894-0347-2014-00796-7`](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-2014-00796-7),
also formulates its subgroup analysis through average numbers of common
fixed points of random symmetric-group homomorphisms.

These sources do not appear, on the inspected evidence, to print RPS's full
labelled endpoint polynomial or hitting-time PGF.  They nevertheless directly
own the group-action object and make “common fixed points of independent
permutations” zero credit.  Once the free generators are specialized, RPS's
remaining exact distribution is elementary inclusion--exclusion rather than
a new word-measure theorem.

### 2.2 Diaconis fixed-point/fixed-set neighbourhood

Diaconis, Fulman, and Guralnick,
[_On fixed points of permutations_](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.2643),
studies fixed-point and derangement distributions in symmetric-group actions.
It makes ordinary fixed-point distribution, prescribed point stabilizers,
and Poisson/derangement framing zero credit.

More directly set-valued, Diaconis, Evans, and Graham,
[_Unseparated pairs and fixed points in random permutations_](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.5459),
studies the random fixed-point **set**, proves exchangeable-set distribution
statements, and explicitly uses inclusion--exclusion and Markov-chain
constructions.  It does not define the RPS repeated-intersection chain, but it
occupies the precise one-epoch fixed-set distribution that drives the chain.

Consequently, the facts that fixing a prescribed `s`-set leaves `(n-s)!`
permutations and that exact fixed sets are recovered by Boolean
inclusion--exclusion are direct inputs, not residual claims.

## 3. Semigroup-walk spectral owner

RPS is a random multiplicative walk on the finite meet-semilattice

```text
(2^[n], intersection),
```

driven by the distribution of `Fix(pi)`.  Every element is idempotent and
the multiplication is commutative; hence this is a particularly elementary
left regular band / `R`-trivial monoid walk.

Brown,
[_Semigroups, Rings, and Markov Chains_](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0006145),
Journal of Theoretical Probability 13 (2000), 871--938, DOI
[`10.1023/A:1007822931408`](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1007822931408),
develops diagonalization, eigenvalues, and projections for left-regular-band
walks.

Ayyer, Schilling, Steinberg, and Thiery,
[_Markov chains, R-trivial monoids and representation theory_](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.4250),
International Journal of Algebra and Computation 25 (2015), 169--231, DOI
[`10.1142/S0218196715400081`](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218196715400081),
gives general eigenvalue and multiplicity formulas through lattice Möbius
inversion, diagonalizability criteria, and absorption/mixing tools for
`R`-trivial monoid walks.

The explicit functions `1[S subseteq A]` make the specialization unusually
simple, but simplicity of the instance is not a new spectral engine.  The
Boolean-zeta basis and repeated eigenvalue bookkeeping receive zero
contribution credit.

## 4. Cycle-mark owner subtraction

For permutations of `m` labels, the cycle-count polynomial is the unsigned
Stirling/rising-factorial identity

```text
sum_(sigma in S_m) u^cyc(sigma)=u(u+1)...(u+m-1).
```

Prescribing `s` fixed labels merely multiplies it by `u^s` and replaces
`m` by `n-s`.  Cycle polynomials of permutation groups and the multivariate
cycle index are established objects; see, for example,
[_The cycle polynomial of a permutation group_](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.06954).

RPS's marked formula applies the same inclusion--exclusion as the unmarked
kernel after this classical scalar-to-polynomial substitution.  It is a
correct refinement, but not a logically independent owner-resistant axis.

## 6. Internal P1--P161 and same-batch collision

### P158

P158 is not a literal owner: it intersects graphs with independent random
cuts, encodes histories by complementary binary words, and obtains a
nontrivial complete-bipartite component image/fibre classification.  But it
already occupies the batch portfolio position “monotone random intersection
with exact absorption and every-endpoint history enumeration.”  RPS's
labelled endpoint law has less target geometry and a more generic proof.

### RTI

RTI is also not a literal formula transfer.  It intersects arbitrary subsets
of `F_2^d` with random translates, while histories generate a translation
subspace.  Its target condition `H<=Stab(B)` and proper-subset enumeration on
outside affine cosets produce a genuinely target-dependent weighted inverse
atlas.

That distinction makes RTI stronger, not a reason to keep both.  RPS and RTI
share the same high-level random-meet silhouette; RPS has no analogue of the
stabilizer/coset inverse axis after its common-fixed-point and semigroup-walk
owners are removed.

### Other internal neighbours

- P136 and P151 have stochastic stopping laws but unrelated state spaces and
  kernels; only generic hitting-time manipulation is subtracted.
- P155 uses cycle structure of a single permutation in a deterministic
  extraction dynamic.  It is not a literal collision, but it makes a second
  same-batch story sold primarily through classical permutation cycles even
  less attractive.
- P97 and generic powerset direct-image work reinforce that relabelling an
  elementary subset transform as a new dynamic is not sufficient.

## 7. Final owner and coexistence decision

Puder's common-fixed-point object, Diaconis's fixed-set/inclusion--exclusion
neighbourhood, and the semigroup-walk spectral theory jointly remove the
three advertised structural axes.  The cycle mark is a classical decoration,
not an escape from those owners.
