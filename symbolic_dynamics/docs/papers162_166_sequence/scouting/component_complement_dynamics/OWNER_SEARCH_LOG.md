## 2. Direct mechanism owner: complements and transitive closures

R. L. Graham, D. E. Knuth, and T. S. Motzkin,
[*Complements and Transitive Closures*](https://ftpmirror.your.org/pub/misc/bitsavers/pdf/stanford/Stanford_CS_TR_Collection_2025-12-12/PDF/1971/CS-TR-71-214.pdf),
*Discrete Mathematics* 2 (1972), 17--29,
[DOI 10.1016/0012-365X(72)90057-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(72)90057-X),
systematically study the semigroup generated by relation complementation and
transitive closure.  Their Theorem 1 bounds all relations obtainable under
the two operations and proves a stabilization identity; the paper also treats
reflexive closure explicitly in its extensions.

CCD lies directly inside this operator algebra.  If `E_G` is the irreflexive
adjacency relation and

```text
R_G = I union E_G,
```

then the transitive closure `R_G^+` is the equivalence relation “lies in the
same connected component of `G`.”  Its off-diagonal complement is the edge
relation of `C(G)`.  Equivalently, on reflexive symmetric relations, CCD is

```text
reflexive off-diagonal complement after transitive closure.              (2.1)
```

Thus the literal update is not merely analogous to closure/complement; it is
a special case after the standard simple-graph/reflexive-relation encoding.
The old paper does not appear, in the inspected text, to state the exact
labelled edge-polynomial fibres of this symmetric special case.  It does own
the mechanism and the expectation that alternating closure and complement
has a short operator orbit.  The CCD height proof is far easier than their
general relation theorem because a complete multipartite graph with at least
two parts is connected.

## 3. Cluster and complete-multipartite image

Completing each component of `G` gives a cluster graph, a disjoint union of
cliques.  Its complement is a complete multipartite graph.  The nonadjacency
classes of a complete multipartite graph uniquely recover its part
partition, so labelled complete multipartite graphs on `[n]` are in immediate
bijection with set partitions and are counted by `B_n`.

These are defining properties of the two complementary graph classes, not a
new CCD image theorem.  The image statement is still proved in the scout
because it fixes every target and every zero fibre, but it receives zero
contribution credit.

The current repository's
`scouting/true_twin_projection/` provides an especially strong internal
control: it already considered a graph projection onto Bell-many cluster
graphs with prescribed-partition edge-weighted fibres and killed the system
as a static decomposition dressed as dynamics.  Complementing the target
class from cluster to complete multipartite does not repair that defect.

## 4. Connected labelled graph polynomial owner

E. N. Gilbert,
[*Enumeration of Labelled Graphs*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/AE983E082134E56900EE87A88F95A72C/S0008414X00036907a.pdf/enumeration-of-labelled-graphs.pdf),
*Canadian Journal of Mathematics* 8 (1956), 405--411,
[DOI 10.4153/CJM-1956-046-2](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1956-046-2),
counts connected labelled graphs by the number of lines/edges.  His Theorem I
derives the connected enumerator from the total enumerator and explicitly
works with the decomposition of a labelled graph into connected components.

CCD's target condition `C(G)=K_pi` is simply `pi(G)=pi`.  Once the target
blocks are fixed, placing an independent connected graph on each block gives

```text
product_(B in pi) Conn_(|B|)(z).
```

This is the prescribed-block specialization of the classical labelled
connected-component construction.  The minimum term selects a tree on every
block; the maximum term selects a clique on every block.  Neither extremum
supplies a separate inverse theorem.

The global mass identity

```text
sum_pi product_(B in pi) Conn_(|B|)(z)=(1+z)^(binom(n,2))
```

is exactly the labelled exponential formula for graphs as sets of connected
components.  It receives zero credit.

operators through their images, fixed graphs, inverse problems, and iterates,
and surveys the mature operator literature.  This is not claimed as a direct
owner of CCD.  It fixes the standardness of the questions being asked:
calling a shallow closure/complement composite a “dynamics” does not itself
create contribution value.


and printing its complete weighted functional graph.  That bounded non-hit is
not evidence of novelty.  The defining composite and both enumerative inputs
are already directly owned.

## 7. Required P1--P161 internal audit

### P123 — decisive component-complement collision

P123 complements selected actual components under an odd-order scheduler.  It
survived only because parity prunes a recursive component/co-component split
tree, producing an order-growing sharp clock and an all-depth labelled EGF.
Its owner gate explicitly observes that removing the parity trigger returns
to familiar component/co-component behavior.  The P142--P161 historical
firewalls then list “another graph component complement” as a permanent
intake kill absent a new engine.

CCD does not use P123's exact literal map, but it is much thinner: it first
forgets all internal component structure, globally complements the resulting
cluster graph, and reaches the core in at most two steps.  Its census is only
`all - connected - empty`.  This is a direct proof-engine and policy kill.

### Additional current collision

The true-twin projection scout maps graphs to Bell-many cluster graphs, has an
every-target block-size polynomial, and is idempotent.  It was killed because
Gessel--Li's species decomposition owns its fibre engine and the update has
no temporal content.  CCD changes “true-twin blocks” to “connected-component
blocks,” complements the output, and gains only the forced
`E_n <-> K_n` alternation.  Since connected-component decomposition is more
elementary than the co-point quotient, CCD cannot rationally receive a more
favorable gate.
