# Proof Package

## Claim

Let $V=\mathbb F_2^d$.  From a source $A_0\subseteq V$, sample independent
uniform $v_i\in V$ and set

$$
A_i=A_{i-1}\cap(A_{i-1}+v_i).
$$

For $H\le V$, write

$$
\mathcal E_H(A)=\bigcap_{h\in H}(A+h),\qquad
H_t=\langle v_1,\ldots,v_t\rangle.
$$

The claim is the complete four-part theorem in the pinned Round-1 manuscript:

1. $A_t=\mathcal E_{H_t}(A_0)$ and the displayed exact rank law holds.
2. The only states fixed by every possible update are $\varnothing$ and $V$;
   the full-span time is a sharp worst-non-full-source emptying clock, with the
   stated CDF and mean.
3. For every target and time, the displayed stabilizer-weighted polynomial
   counts every ordered history and every source size.
4. Phase cardinality, target size, and one-step inverse mass recover the
   stabilizer dimension.

## Assumptions

- $d,t$ are arbitrary nonnegative integers.
- The translation samples are independent and uniform on $V$.
- Sources and targets are labelled subsets of $V$.
- Histories are ordered and are not quotiented by their spans.
- The one-step recovery result is conditioned on knowing the phase dimension
  and target cardinality, exactly as stated.

## Notation

- $N=|V|=2^d$.
- $b=|B|$ for a target $B$.
- $\operatorname{Stab}(B)=\{v\in V:B+v=B\}$ and
  $s=\dim\operatorname{Stab}(B)$.
- $S(t,r)=\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(2^t-2^i)$, with $S(t,0)=1$ and
  $S(t,r)=0$ for $r>t$.
- $\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{m}{r}_2$ is the Gaussian binomial coefficient.

## Proof Strategy

First compress a literal history to the subspace generated by its translation
vectors.  Count histories by the rank of that subspace.  For the inverse
direction, freeze a span $H$ and partition $V$ into $H$-cosets: erosion retains
exactly the cosets fully contained in the source.  This gives a target
stabilizer filter and a proper-subset generating polynomial.  Summing over
subspaces of the target stabilizer gives the every-target formula.  Finally,
specialize it at one step and invert the strictly monotone expression.

## Dependency Map

1. The history identity depends on erosion composition and
   $\langle v\rangle=\{0,v\}$ in characteristic two.
2. The rank law depends on the number of fixed $r$-subspaces and the number of
   surjections $\mathbb F_2^t\to\mathbb F_2^r$.
3. The sharp clock depends on the full-span implication and the explicit
   witness $V\setminus\{0\}$.
4. The fibre polynomial depends on the fixed-span coset lemma, the stabilizer
   filter, and the rank-history count.
5. Recovery depends on the separate $s=0$ branch and strict monotonicity in
   $s$ at fixed $(d,b)$.

## Proof

### Step 1. Compress every literal history to its span

For subspaces $H,K\le V$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal E_K(\mathcal E_H(A))
 &=\bigcap_{k\in K}\left(\bigcap_{h\in H}(A+h)+k\right)\\
 &=\bigcap_{k\in K}\bigcap_{h\in H}(A+h+k)\\
 &=\bigcap_{u\in H+K}(A+u)
 =\mathcal E_{H+K}(A).
\end{aligned}
$$

The third equality is valid because every $u\in H+K$ has at least one
representation $h+k$, and repeated factors do not alter an intersection.
Since $\langle v\rangle=\{0,v\}$,

$$
\mathcal E_{\langle v\rangle}(A)=A\cap(A+v),
$$

which is exactly one literal update.  Induction on the history length now
gives

$$
A_t=\mathcal E_{\langle v_1,\ldots,v_t\rangle}(A_0).
$$

### Step 2. Derive the rank law

Fix an $r$-subspace $H\le V$.  An ordered history
$(v_1,\ldots,v_t)$ spans $H$ exactly when the linear map

$$
\phi:\mathbb F_2^t\longrightarrow H,
\qquad \phi(e_i)=v_i,
$$

is surjective.  Dually, its $r$ coordinate functionals form an ordered
linearly independent $r$-tuple in $(\mathbb F_2^t)^*$.  The choices are

$$
(2^t-1)(2^t-2)\cdots(2^t-2^{r-1})=S(t,r).
$$

There are $\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{d}{r}_2$ choices of $H$.  Dividing the
resulting history count by $|V|^t=2^{dt}$ proves

$$
\Pr(\dim H_t=r)=
\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{d}{r}_2\frac{S(t,r)}{2^{dt}}.
$$

For $t=0$, only $r=0$ contributes, and both empty products equal one.

### Step 3. Identify the two universal fixed states

Suppose $A$ is fixed by every possible sampled update.  Then

$$
A=A\cap(A+v),
$$

so $A\subseteq A+v$ for every $v$.  Translation preserves cardinality, hence
$A=A+v$ for every $v$.  The translation action of $V$ on itself is
transitive; an invariant subset is therefore either $\varnothing$ or $V$.
Both are indeed fixed.  This includes $d=0$, where the phase has the two
distinct subsets of its one-point vector space.

### Step 4. Prove the sharp worst-non-full-source clock

If $H_t=V$, then

$$
\mathcal E_V(A)=\bigcap_{v\in V}(A+v).
$$

For $A=V$ this equals $V$.  If $A\ne V$, choose $a\notin A$.  For every
$x\in V$, the translate indexed by $v=x+a$ omits $x$, because in
characteristic two $x+v=a$.  Hence $\mathcal E_V(A)=\varnothing$.

The bound is attained by $A_\star=V\setminus\{0\}$.  Since

$$
A_\star+h=V\setminus\{h\},
$$

one has

$$
\mathcal E_H(A_\star)
=\bigcap_{h\in H}(V\setminus\{h\})
=V\setminus H.
$$

Thus this source empties if and only if $H=V$.  If
$\sigma=\min\{t:H_t=V\}$, the rank law at $r=d$ gives

$$
\Pr(\sigma\le t)=0\quad(t<d),
$$

and, for $t\ge d$,

$$
\Pr(\sigma\le t)
=\frac{\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}(2^t-2^i)}{2^{dt}}
=\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}(1-2^{i-t}).
$$

At rank $r<d$, a new uniform vector lies outside the current span with
probability $p_r=1-2^{r-d}$.  Independence of the future samples and the
strong Markov property make the successive waiting times geometric with
means $p_r^{-1}$.  Therefore

$$
\mathbb E\sigma=\sum_{r=0}^{d-1}\frac{1}{1-2^{r-d}}.
$$

The empty sum at $d=0$ is zero, matching $\sigma=0$.

### Step 5. Prove the fixed-span fibre polynomial

Fix $H\le V$ with $\dim H=r$.  Partition $V$ into its $2^{d-r}$ cosets of
$H$.  For any coset $C=x+H$, either all of $C$ lies in $A$, in which case
each $x\in C$ belongs to every translate $A+h$, or at least one point of $C$
is absent from $A$, in which case translation within $H$ shows that no point
of $C$ survives the whole intersection.  Consequently
$\mathcal E_H(A)$ is precisely the union of the $H$-cosets wholly contained
in $A$.

It follows that $\mathcal E_H(A)=B$ is possible only if $B$ is $H$-invariant,
equivalently $H\le\operatorname{Stab}(B)$.  Conversely, under that condition
$B$ is a union of $b/2^r$ full $H$-cosets.  A source producing $B$ must
contain every one of those cosets.  On each of the remaining
$2^{d-r}-b/2^r$ cosets, it may independently choose any proper subset.  A
forced target coset contributes $z^{2^r}$, while an outside coset contributes

$$
(1+z)^{2^r}-z^{2^r}.
$$

Hence

$$
\sum_{A:\mathcal E_H(A)=B}z^{|A|}
=z^b\left((1+z)^{2^r}-z^{2^r}\right)^{2^{d-r}-b/2^r}.
$$

The exponent is a nonnegative integer because $B$ is a union of $H$-cosets.

### Step 6. Sum over every history span

The stabilizer of $B$ is a subspace: it contains zero and is closed under
addition.  Its $r$-subspaces number
$\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{s}{r}_2$, and every fixed such subspace is generated
by exactly $S(t,r)$ ordered histories.  Multiplying the fixed-span polynomial
by these two factors and summing over $0\le r\le s$ proves

$$
F_t(B;z)=z^b\sum_{r=0}^{s}
\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{s}{r}_2 S(t,r)
\left((1+z)^{2^r}-z^{2^r}\right)^{2^{d-r}-b/2^r}.
$$

At $t=0$, $S(0,r)=0$ for $r>0$, and the $r=0$ factor equals one, so
$F_0(B;z)=z^b$.  For $B=V$, the outside-coset exponent is zero and the rank
sum counts all $2^{dt}$ histories, giving $2^{dt}z^{2^d}$.  For
$B=\varnothing$, no forced coset occurs.  At $d=0$, the formula gives $1$
for the empty target and $z$ for the full target at every time.

### Step 7. Derive the one-step boundary and recovery law

At $t=1$, the zero history has rank zero and contributes one at $z=1$.  A
nonzero $v\in\operatorname{Stab}(B)$ has rank one.  Each two-point coset
outside $B$ can be filled by any of its three proper subsets.  If $s\ge1$,
there are $2^s-1$ nonzero stabilizing histories and $B$ is a union of
two-point cosets, so $b$ is even.  Therefore

$$
F_1(B;1)=1+(2^s-1)3^{2^{d-1}-b/2}.
$$

If $s=0$, there is no nonzero stabilizing history, so $F_1(B;1)=1$.  Keeping
this branch separate prevents a half-integral exponent when $b$ is odd.

The phase cardinality $2^{2^d}$ determines $d$.  At fixed $(d,b)$, set
$C=3^{2^{d-1}-b/2}$ on the feasible $s\ge1$ branch.  Then

$$
F_1(B;1)=1+(2^s-1)C,
$$

which is strictly increasing in $s$ and is greater than one.  The value one
therefore recovers $s=0$; otherwise

$$
s=\log_2\left(1+\frac{F_1(B;1)-1}{C}\right).
$$

This proves the stated recovery result.  Therefore all four parts of the
claim follow. $\square$
