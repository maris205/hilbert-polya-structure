\begin{abstract}
We study the synchronous map $D(x)_i=\sgn(x_{i+1}-x_i)$ on labelled
ternary cyclic words. Its recurrent set is exactly the local language
$D^4x=\rho^2x$, where $\rho$ is left shift. The maximal transient is
$n-1$ at even length and $n-2$ at odd length at least three, with a separate
singleton boundary. Finite local certificates prove attraction for every
length, and de Bruijn traces enumerate all depths and cycles. Independently,
every one-step fibre is a comparison-walk trace. Contracting equality edges
and cutting at doubled sign runs gives an explicit product of Fibonacci
numbers. This yields the sharp Lucas maximum and all maximizing targets,
including the additional ties at lengths two and three. The local rule,
proof, and exact verifier are distinguished from generic cellular-automaton
and transfer-matrix methods. The bounded source audit leaves this anonymous
theorem note \texttt{OWNER\_AMBER / HOLD\_EXTERNAL}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The map and its claim boundary}
Fix $n\ge1$, use indices in $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:map}
X_n=\{-1,0,1\}^n,\qquad D(x)_i=\sgn(x_{i+1}-x_i),\qquad
(\rho x)_i=x_{i+1}.
\end{equation}
All coordinates are read before the update. The tail $\tau(x)$ is the
first entrance time into a periodic orbit, so periodic points have tail
zero. For an open word $w$, let $\delta w$ be its adjacent sign-difference
word without wraparound; each application shortens the word by one.

Finite-alphabet cellular dynamics, shifts of finite type, trace enumeration,
and M\"obius inversion are standard tools \cite{LindMarcus1995,Stanley2011}.
Fuk\'s studies solvable ternary rules induced by associative operations
\cite{Fuks2026}. Our operation $d(a,b)=\sgn(b-a)$ is not associative:
$d(d(-1,-1),0)=0$ whereas $d(-1,d(-1,0))=1$. Thus it is not an associative
rule under a direct alphabet isomorphism or passage to the opposite
operation. This argument does not exclude higher-block factors, time
iterates, or other encodings.

The closest internal comparison is P164's cyclic equality front: at its
three-letter input slice, $\mathbf1\{x_i=x_{i+1}\}=\mathbf1\{D(x)_i=0\}$
exactly. We concede this first-step projection, not a dynamical conjugacy
of the full ternary evolution to the subsequent binary equality dynamics.
P90's traffic rule, P117's run reversal, P187's positive valuation
differences, P190's support erosion, and P196's one-step implication core
are further occupied neighbors. Generic local erosion, cyclic traces and
Fibonacci identities receive no contribution credit. The residual claim is
the joint sharp temporal and target-resolved inverse theorem for
\eqref{eq:map}; no novelty or priority certification is asserted.

\section{The recurrent core and sharp attraction}
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:core}
K_n=\{x\in X_n:D^4x=\rho^2x\}.
\end{equation}
For a nonconstant cyclic word write $R(x)$ for its longest constant run.

\begin{lemma}[finite local certificate]\label{lem:local}
If $w$ has length six and no equal adjacent letters, then
$\delta^5(w)_0=\delta(w_2w_3)_0$. If $w$ has length seven and no constant
factor of length three, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local}
\delta^6(w)_0=\delta^2(w_2w_3w_4)_0.
\end{equation}
Consequently $R(x)=1$ implies $Dx\in K_n$, and $R(x)\le2$ implies
$D^2x\in K_n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
These are finite local identities, independent of the cyclic length.
Negation and reversal reduce the middle pair in the first identity to
$-0$ or $-+$, whose symmetry orbits have sizes four and two. Each admits
16 outer extensions with no adjacent equality; direct successive comparison
gives $+$ in all 32 representative extensions. This covers $96=3\cdot2^5$
words. Here and below $-$ and $+$ mean $-1$ and $1$.

For the second identity, the complete representative calculation is
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|r r c}
middle triple & orbit size & outer extensions & both sides of \eqref{eq:local}\\\hline
$--0$&4&48&$+$\\
$--+$&4&48&$+$\\
$-0-$&2&64&$-$\\
$-00$&4&48&$-$\\
$-0+$&2&64&$0$\\
$-+-$&2&64&$-$\\
$-+0$&4&64&$-$\\
$0-0$&2&64&$+$
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For each row, range the four outer letters over the three-letter alphabet,
discard constant triples, and successively replace adjacent pairs by their
sign difference. The displayed output is constant on exactly the indicated
extensions. The orbit-weighted count is 1,344, all length-seven words with
no constant triple. Negation and reversal transform both sides identically,
so the table proves the identity. Applying the identities at each cyclic
position proves the conclusions, even when a window wraps repeatedly.
The exact verifier reproduces the entire finite certificate, not just
sample cyclic words.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[core and parity-sharp tail]\label{thm:tail}
For every $n\ge1$, $K_n$ is exactly the recurrent set. On this set,
$D^{-1}=\rho^{-2}D^3$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clock}
\tau(x)=\min\{t\ge0:D^{t+4}x=\rho^2D^t x\}.
\end{equation}
For every nonconstant $x$, $\tau(x)\le R(x)$. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:height}
\max_{x\in X_n}\tau(x)=
\begin{cases}1,&n=1,\\n-1,&n\text{ even},\\n-2,&n\ge3\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Every recurrent period divides $4n/\gcd(n,2)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A zero run of $Dx$ of length $q$ comes from $q+1$ equal old letters.
A nonzero constant run comes from a strictly monotone chain in three levels,
so has length at most two. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:run}
R(Dx)\le\max\{R(x)-1,2\}
\end{equation}
whenever the new word is nonconstant. If it is constant, it must be zero,
because cyclic strict increase or decrease is impossible. Combining
\eqref{eq:run} with Lemma~\ref{lem:local} gives entrance into $K_n$ in at
most $R(x)$ steps. A constant word maps immediately to zero.

The map commutes with $\rho$, so $K_n$ is invariant. Equation
\eqref{eq:core} makes $\rho^{-2}D^3$ a two-sided inverse there. It is
therefore a permutation of this finite set, and every orbit enters it.
This proves the recurrence and pointwise-tail claims. On the core,
$D^{4n/\gcd(n,2)}=\rho^{2n/\gcd(n,2)}=\mathrm{id}$.

For sharpness, choose $x=0^{n-1}1$. More generally, for a one-exception
word $a^{n-1}b$ put $s=\sgn(b-a)$, where $a\ne b$.
Then $Dx=0^{n-2}s(-s)$. Write $\Alt_l(s)=(s,-s,s,\ldots)$ and observe,
for $r\ge1$, $l\ge2$, that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witness}
D(0^r\Alt_l(s))=0^{r-1}\Alt_{l+1}(s).
\end{equation}
At even length, the fully alternating word is mapped to its negative
and belongs to $K_n$. At odd length $n=2m+1$, the one-zero endpoint
$w=0\Alt_{2m}(s)$ has the four phases
\begin{align*}
Dw&=\Alt_{2m+1}(s),&D^2w&=\Alt_{2m}(-s)0,\\
D^3w&=\Alt_{2m}(s)(-s),&D^4w&=\Alt_{2m-1}(-s)0s=\rho^2w.
\end{align*}
The last two letters of $D^3w$ agree; these formulas include $m=1$.

None of the earlier phases $z=0^r\Alt_l(s)$ enters the core. For $r\ge4$,
$(D^4z)_{r-4}=s$ but $(\rho^2z)_{r-4}=0$. At $r=3$ the discrepancy
is $-s$ versus zero at coordinate zero. At $r=2$ it is a nonzero sign
versus zero at coordinate $n-2$, since $D^2z$ is fully alternating.
At $r=1$ and even $n$, $D^4z$ is nonzero at $n-2$, whereas $\rho^2z$
is zero there. Thus the first entrance occurs at $n-1$ for even $n$
and $n-2$ for odd $n\ge3$. The original one-exception word cannot
already be recurrent unless its successor is; for $n=2,3$ directly
checking the chosen $0^{n-1}1$ supplies the one-step lower bound.
Not every choice of $a,b$ is sharp at these small lengths.

For the global upper bound, all nonconstant words except the one-exception
form have $R\le n-2$, and all constants have tail at most one. This proves
\eqref{eq:height} for $n\ge2$. At $n=1$, both nonzero letters map to the
unique fixed letter zero, giving the separate boundary.
\end{proof}

\section{Depths and cycles as exact finite traces}
For $t\ge0$ define the zero-one de Bruijn matrix $A_t$ on words of length
$t+4$. An overlap edge associated with $w=w_0\cdots w_{t+4}$ is allowed
exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:At}
\delta^{t+4}(w)_0=\delta^t(w_2\cdots w_{t+2})_0.
\end{equation}
For $p\ge1$ define $C_p$ on words of length $p$ by allowing an edge
$w_0\cdots w_p$ exactly when $\delta^p(w)_0=w_0$.

\begin{theorem}[exact enumeration]\label{thm:trace}
For all $n\ge1,t\ge0,p\ge1$,
\begin{align}
\#\{x:\tau(x)\le t\}&=\tr(A_t^n),\label{eq:cdf}\\
\#\Fix(D^p)&=\tr(C_p^n).\label{eq:fix}
\end{align}
The depth-$t$ count is the difference of consecutive CDF values (with
previous value zero at $t=0$). The number of least-period-$p$ cycles is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycles}
\frac1p\sum_{d\mid p}\mu(p/d)\tr(C_d^n),
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is the number-theoretic M\"obius function.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A closed overlap walk of length $n$ uniquely specifies its labelled
cyclic word, including when $n$ is shorter than a window. The local
constraint \eqref{eq:At} is precisely the equality in \eqref{eq:clock}
at every coordinate. The analogous statement for $C_p$ is $D^px=x$.
This proves both traces; partitioning iterate-fixed points by least
period and applying M\"obius inversion proves \eqref{eq:cycles}.
\end{proof}

For explicit core counts, $A_0$ has 81 vertices, 165 edges, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:charpoly}
\det(zI-A_0)=z^{74}(z-1)(z^3-z^2-2z-1)(z^3+z^2+2z+1).
\end{equation}
This is a finite exact matrix calculation from \eqref{eq:At}. A short
reproducible certificate computes $s_j=\tr(A_0^j)$ for $1\le j\le81$,
sets $c_0=1$, and uses Newton's identity
$jc_j=-\sum_{i=1}^j c_{j-i}s_i$.
It gives $(c_1,\ldots,c_7)=(-1,-1,-3,-2,2,3,1)$ and
$c_8=\cdots=c_{81}=0$, which is \eqref{eq:charpoly}. Integer matrix
multiplication and exact divisions suffice; the verifier checks all 81
coefficients, not a fitted short recurrence. Therefore, writing $R_n=|K_n|$,
\begin{gather*}
(R_1,\ldots,R_7)=(1,3,13,27,41,93,225),\\
R_n=R_{n-1}+R_{n-2}+3R_{n-3}+2R_{n-4}
-2R_{n-5}-3R_{n-6}-R_{n-7}\quad(n\ge8).
\end{gather*}
The dimensions of $A_t$ and $C_p$ grow with $t,p$. These are exact
finite algorithms, not efficient fixed-size formulas; the divisibility
bound is not a claim that every divisor occurs.

\section{Every target fibre and its exact gap product}
Index the three levels increasingly and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrices}
U=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&1\\0&0&1\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
V=U^{\mathsf T},\qquad M_+=U,\quad M_-=V,\quad M_0=I.
\end{equation}
Let $F_0=0,F_1=1$ and $L_0=2,L_1=1$ denote Fibonacci and Lucas numbers.
For a target $y$, its strict skeleton is obtained by deleting all zeros,
retaining cyclic order. Its length is denoted $r$.

\begin{theorem}[one-step inverse atlas]\label{thm:fibre}
For every $y\in X_n$, including targets outside the image,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre}
|D^{-1}(y)|=\tr(M_{y_0}\cdots M_{y_{n-1}}).
\end{equation}
An all-zero target has fibre three. A nonempty skeleton is realizable
exactly when it contains both signs and each cyclic sign run has length
at most two. If all runs have length one, its fibre is $L_r$. Otherwise
let $q$ be its number of doubled runs, and $g_j$ the number of singleton
runs between doubled runs $j$ and $j+1$, cyclically. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
|D^{-1}(y)|=\prod_{j=1}^q F_{g_j+1},\qquad
r=2q+\sum_jg_j,\qquad q\equiv r\pmod2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The entry $M_s(a,b)$ is the indicator of $\sgn(b-a)=s$. Multiplication
and trace count precisely all closed level assignments, proving
\eqref{eq:fibre}. Identity matrices may be deleted. A single-sign
skeleton gives a strictly triangular product with zero trace. Also
$U^2=E_{13}$, $V^2=E_{31}$ and $U^3=V^3=0$, so a cyclic run of length
at least three has zero fibre. A realizable nonconstant skeleton has an
even number of runs, giving the parity relation in \eqref{eq:gap}.

Each doubled run is rank one. For successive blocks $u_jv_j^{\mathsf T}$
and intervening products $B_j$, their cyclic trace factors as
\begin{equation*}
\tr(u_1v_1^{\mathsf T}B_1\cdots u_qv_q^{\mathsf T}B_q)
=\prod_j v_j^{\mathsf T}B_ju_{j+1},\qquad u_{q+1}=u_1.
\end{equation*}
Starting at $U^2$, a gap of $g=2a$ singleton runs contributes
$e_3^{\mathsf T}(VU)^ae_3=F_{2a+1}$; a gap of $g=2a+1$ contributes
$e_3^{\mathsf T}(VU)^aVe_1=F_{2a+2}$. Indeed the lower-right block
of $(VU)^a$, for $a\ge1$, is
\begin{equation*}
\begin{pmatrix}F_{2a-1}&F_{2a}\\F_{2a}&F_{2a+1}\end{pmatrix};
\end{equation*}
induction proves this identity, and at $a=0$ both required scalars are
one. Reversing the level order treats a starting $V^2$. This proves
\eqref{eq:gap}, whose factors are positive, establishing sufficiency of
the image condition. With no doubled run the even alternating product
has trace $\tr((UV)^{r/2})=F_{r-1}+F_{r+1}=L_r$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[sharp maximum and all equality cases]\label{thm:max}
For $n\ge2$, the maximal fibre is $L_{2\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$.
For even $n\ge4$ exactly the two fully alternating targets maximize.
For odd $n\ge5$ exactly the $2n$ targets with one zero and alternating
remaining signs maximize. At $n=2,3$ the all-zero target also ties, giving
three and seven maximizers respectively. At $n=1$ the only image target
is zero and its fibre is three.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $a,b\ge1$, $F_{a+b-1}=F_aF_b+F_{a-1}F_{b-1}\ge F_aF_b$.
Thus a skeleton with $q>0$ satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\prod_jF_{g_j+1}\le F_{1+\sum_jg_j}=F_{r-2q+1}.
\end{equation*}
If $r$ is even, then $q\ge2$ and this is at most $F_{r-3}<L_r$.
Hence only alternating skeletons maximize at even strict length. If
$r\ge3$ is odd, then $q$ is odd: at $q=1$ the value is exactly
$F_{r-1}$, while at $q\ge3$ it is at most $F_{r-5}<F_{r-1}$.
We do not require each individual Fibonacci merge to be strict.

Optimize over $0\le r\le n$, remembering the all-zero value three and
the impossible one-sign skeletons. At even $n\ge4$, $L_n$ strictly
dominates every smaller strict length. At odd $n\ge5$, $L_{n-1}$
strictly dominates $F_{n-1}$ and every smaller strict length. The asserted
targets and small ties follow immediately.
\end{proof}
