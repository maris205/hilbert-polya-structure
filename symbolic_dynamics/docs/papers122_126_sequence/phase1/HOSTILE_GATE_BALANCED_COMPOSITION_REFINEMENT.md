## 1. Literal reconstruction and boundary conventions

Let \(\mathcal C_n\) be the compositions of \(n\), with the empty
composition \(\epsilon\) as the sole member of \(\mathcal C_0\).  On the
free monoid \(\mathbb Z_{>0}^{*}\), define the morphism

\[
 \phi(1)=1,\qquad
 \phi(m)=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\,\lceil m/2\rceil\quad(m\ge2),
\]

and extend it by concatenation.  Thus all parts are updated
simultaneously.  It preserves total weight.  Every occurrence of a part at
least \(2\) is replaced by two positive parts, so length increases strictly
at every nonfixed state.  Consequently:

- \(\epsilon\) is the fixed state at weight \(0\);
- \(1^n\) is the unique fixed state at each weight \(n\ge1\);
- there is no nontrivial directed cycle; and
- every positive-weight orbit terminates at \(1^n\).

These elementary facts are correct, but they are not a novelty claim.

## 2. Balanced clock: correct, sharp, and low credit

Write \(W_t(m)=\phi^t(m)\), and let \(M_t(m)\) be its largest letter.
The exact identity is

\[
 M_t(m)=\left\lceil \frac{m}{2^t}\right\rceil .
\]

Indeed \(M_0(m)=m\), and the rightmost child is the larger child, so

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_{t+1}(m)
 &=\max\!\left\{
 M_t(\lfloor m/2\rfloor),M_t(\lceil m/2\rceil)\right\}\\
 &=\left\lceil
 \frac{\lceil m/2\rceil}{2^t}\right\rceil
 =\left\lceil\frac{m}{2^{t+1}}\right\rceil .
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore, with \(\operatorname{depth}(\epsilon)=0\),

\[
\operatorname{depth}(a_1,\ldots,a_r)
 =\max_i\lceil\log_2 a_i\rceil ,
\qquad
\max_{a\in\mathcal C_n}\operatorname{depth}(a)
 =\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\quad(n\ge1).
\]

The one-part composition \((n)\) is a witness for every \(n\).  This settles
the sharp-clock issue, including powers of two and \(n=0,1\).  Balanced
divide-and-conquer halving and its logarithmic clock must receive zero
credit.

## 3. The missing theorem: canonical normal form at every time

Fix \(t\ge0\) and put \(K=2^t\).  Define

\[
 A_K=\{1\}\cup\{K+1,K+2,\ldots\}.
\]

For a composition \(u\), define \(N_K(u)\) by replacing each part
\(m\in\{2,\ldots,K\}\) by \(1^m\), while leaving the letters in \(A_K\)
unchanged.  For \(K=1\), this is the identity.

### Theorem 3.1 (kernel normal form)

For all compositions \(u,v\),

\[
 \phi^t(u)=\phi^t(v)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 N_K(u)=N_K(v).
\]

Equivalently, the kernel congruence of \(\phi^t\) is generated by

\[
 m\equiv 1^m\qquad(2\le m\le K),
\]

and \(A_K^*\) is a canonical transversal.

### Proof

If \(m\le K\), all descendants of \(m\) are \(1\) after \(t\) rounds, so
\(W_t(m)=1^m\).  Hence

\[
 \phi^t=\phi^t\circ N_K.
\]

It remains to prove that \(\phi^t\) is injective on \(A_K^*\).  The relevant
codewords have the following exact properties:

\[
 |W_t(1)|=1,\qquad
 |W_t(m)|=K\quad(m>K),
\]

and the final letter of a long codeword is

\[
 \operatorname{last}(W_t(m))
 =\left\lceil\frac mK\right\rceil\ge2.
\]

Two long codewords of equal length are distinct when their source letters
are distinct, because their total weights are their source letters.  The
only short codeword is \(W_t(1)=1\), and it cannot be a suffix of a long
codeword because every long codeword ends in a letter at least \(2\).
Thus

\[
 \{W_t(m):m\in A_K\}
\]

is a suffix code.  A concatenation can be decoded uniquely from right to
left: a final \(1\) forces the letter \(1\); otherwise the final block has
length \(K\), and its weight identifies the unique source letter.  Repeating
the procedure gives injectivity on \(A_K^*\).

Now, if \(\phi^t(u)=\phi^t(v)\), then

\[
 \phi^t(N_K(u))=\phi^t(N_K(v)),
\]

and injectivity on \(A_K^*\) yields \(N_K(u)=N_K(v)\).  The reverse
implication follows from \(\phi^t=\phi^t\circ N_K\).  This proves the
theorem. \(\square\)

This proof is materially stronger than merely observing that a target can
be parsed by a dynamic program.  It identifies the entire equivalence
relation induced by every iterate.

## 4. Exact pointwise fibres: the DP collapses to a product

If a target \(y\) is in \(\operatorname{Im}\phi^t\), Theorem 3.1 gives a
unique canonical source

\[
 c_t(y)\in A_K^*,
\qquad \phi^t(c_t(y))=y.
\]

Let \(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_s\) be the lengths of the maximal runs of \(1\)'s
in \(c_t(y)\).  Empty runs are omitted.  Let

\[
 F_K(0)=1,\qquad
 F_K(r)=\sum_{j=1}^{\min(K,r)}F_K(r-j)\quad(r\ge1).
\]

Thus \(F_K(r)\) counts compositions of \(r\) whose parts are at most \(K\).

### Theorem 4.1 (all-\(t\) pointwise fibre product)

\[
 \left|(\phi^t)^{-1}(y)\right|=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{s}F_K(\rho_j),
     &y\in\operatorname{Im}\phi^t,\\[6pt]
 0,  &y\notin\operatorname{Im}\phi^t.
 \end{cases}
\]

The empty product is \(1\), including the empty composition.

### Proof

By Theorem 3.1 the fibre consists exactly of the compositions \(u\) with
\(N_K(u)=c_t(y)\).  Every letter greater than \(K\) in the canonical word is
forced: no smaller letter can normalize to it, and a larger letter is left
unchanged.  A maximal run \(1^\rho\), independently of the other runs,
may be lifted to an arbitrary composition of \(\rho\) with parts at most
\(K\).  There are \(F_K(\rho)\) such lifts.  Multiplying over the disjoint
runs proves the formula. \(\square\)

The dossier's prefix-factorization DP is correct, but it obscures this
product structure.  The all-\(t\) DP should appear only as an algorithmic
corollary or a verifier implementation.

### Corollary 4.2 (maximum fibre)

For every \(n,t\ge0\),

\[
 \max_{y\in\mathcal C_n}
 \left|(\phi^t)^{-1}(y)\right|=F_{2^t}(n),
\]

attained at \(y=1^n\).

There are two clean proofs.  First, Theorem 4.1 and the concatenation
supermultiplicativity

\[
 F_K(r_1)\cdots F_K(r_s)\le F_K(r_1+\cdots+r_s)\le F_K(n)
\]

give the upper bound, while \(c_t(1^n)=1^n\) gives equality.  Second, in the
original parsing DP a terminal factor has one of at most \(K\) lengths and
its weight determines its source letter; the resulting recurrence is
bounded by \(F_K\), and every cut of \(1^n\) into blocks of length at most
\(K\) is legal.  These are genuinely different local-product and
last-factor routes.

The phrase “\(2^t\)-step Fibonacci” is standard bounded-part composition
enumeration and receives zero credit.  What may remain is its exact
appearance as the maximum fibre of this literal temporal map.

## 5. Every image layer and its OGF

The restriction

\[
 \phi^t:A_K^*\longrightarrow\operatorname{Im}\phi^t
\]

is a weight-preserving bijection: surjectivity follows by normalizing an
arbitrary source, and injectivity is the suffix-code argument.  Hence the
size \(I_t(n)=|\operatorname{Im}(\phi^t:\mathcal C_n\to\mathcal C_n)|\)
equals the number of compositions of \(n\) with parts in
\(A_K=\{1\}\cup\{m>K\}\).

Including \(I_t(0)=1\), the exact OGF is

\[
\begin{aligned}
 I_t(x)
   &=\sum_{n\ge0}I_t(n)x^n\\
   &=\frac{1}{1-\left(x+\dfrac{x^{K+1}}{1-x}\right)}
     =\frac{1-x}{1-2x+x^2-x^{K+1}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Indeed one canonical letter has weight series
\(L_K(x)=x+x^{K+1}/(1-x)\), and a canonical composition is an arbitrary
finite sequence of such letters.  Summing
\(\sum_{r\ge0}L_K(x)^r\) proves the displayed OGF; multiplying by its
denominator gives the coefficient recurrence below.

With \(I_t(j)=0\) for \(j<0\), its coefficients satisfy, for \(n\ge2\),

\[
 I_t(n)=2I_t(n-1)-I_t(n-2)+I_t(n-K-1),
\]

with \(I_t(0)=I_t(1)=1\).  At \(t=0\), this recovers all ordinary
compositions.  At \(t=1\), \(K=2\), the image is exactly the class of
compositions with no part \(2\):

\[
 1,1,1,2,4,7,12,21,37,\ldots.
\]

That \(t=1\) class and its enumeration are directly owned by Chinn and
Heubach.  The general part-set OGF is also standard restricted-composition
technology.  Both must be stated as zero-credit background.  The admissible
residual is only the temporal identification
\(\operatorname{Im}\phi^t\leftrightarrow A_{2^t}^*\), derived from the
kernel normal form.
