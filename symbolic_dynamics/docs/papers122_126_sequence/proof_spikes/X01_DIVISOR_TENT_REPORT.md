## 1. Literal system

Fix

\[
                 N=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{a_i},\qquad a_i\geq1,
\]

and let `Div(N)` be its positive divisor lattice.  For `d|N`, define

\[
 \Phi_N(d)=
 \frac{\operatorname{lcm}(d,N/d)}{\gcd(d,N/d)}.
 \tag{1}
\]

Both numerator and denominator are divisors of `N`, and the denominator
divides the numerator prime by prime, so (1) is again a divisor of `N`.

This is a deterministic finite dynamical system constructed only from a
divisor and its complementary divisor.  It is not the one-step unitary
projection: nontrivial cycles of lengths two, three, and higher already occur
for prime powers.

## 2. Valuation tent map

Write `d=product p_i^e_i`.  Since gcd and lcm take coordinatewise minima and
maxima,

\[
 v_{p_i}(\Phi_N(d))
 =\max(e_i,a_i-e_i)-\min(e_i,a_i-e_i)
 =|2e_i-a_i|.
 \tag{2}
\]

Thus `Phi_N` is the direct product of the local maps

\[
 f_a:\{0,\ldots,a\}\longrightarrow\{0,\ldots,a\},
 \qquad f_a(e)=|2e-a|.
 \tag{3}
\]

Put `y=a-e`.  Then

\[
 a-f_a(a-y)=a-|a-2y|=2\min(y,a-y).
 \tag{4}
\]

Let `C_(2a)=Z/(2a)Z`, quotient it by the involution `z~-z`, and represent
each sign class by its unique element of `{0,...,a}`.  Doubling a class and
folding to that representative sends

\[
 [y]\longmapsto[2y],\qquad
 y\longmapsto\min(2y\bmod 2a,-2y\bmod2a)=2\min(y,a-y).
 \tag{5}
\]

Equations (2) and (5) are the two structural routes: direct divisor
valuations prove product separation, while the cyclic sign quotient gives
the complete temporal and fibre calculus.

## 3. Pointwise orbit theorem

For a local state `e`, put `y=a-e`.  If `y=0`, define

\[
                  M(e)=1,\quad \alpha(e)=0,\quad m(e)=1.
\]

Otherwise set

\[
 M(e)=\frac{2a}{\gcd(y,2a)}=2^{\alpha(e)}m(e),
 \qquad m(e)\ \text{odd}.
 \tag{6}
\]

For an odd positive integer `m`, define

\[
 \pi(m)=\min\{k\geq1:2^k\equiv1\ \text{or}\ -1\pmod m\},
 \qquad \pi(1)=1.
 \tag{7}
\]

### Theorem 3.1

The local preperiod of `e` under `f_a` is exactly `alpha(e)`, and its eventual
least period is exactly `pi(m(e))`.

### Proof

The cyclic subgroup generated by `y` in `C_(2a)` has order `M(e)`.  Before
quotienting by sign, doubling on a cyclic group of order `2^alpha m` has
preperiod `alpha`: each step removes one remaining factor of two, after which
doubling is a permutation of the odd-order subgroup.  Passing to sign cannot
shorten this tail.  Indeed, periodicity after `t<alpha` steps would give

\[
 2^{t+k}y\equiv \pm2^t y\pmod{2a}
\]

for some `k>0`, so `2^(alpha-t)` would divide the odd number `2^k-1` or
`2^k+1`, an impossibility.  On the odd subgroup, two iterates represent the
same sign class precisely when their powers of two differ by sign.  The least
period is therefore (7).  Equation (5) transfers the result to (3).  QED.

For a divisor `d` with coordinates `e_i`, define `alpha_i` and `m_i` by (6).
Direct products now give

\[
 \operatorname{preper}(d)=\max_i\alpha_i,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{per}(d)=\operatorname{lcm}_i\pi(m_i).
 \tag{8}
\]

This classifies every vertex of the functional graph without iteration.

## 4. Recurrent set and all depth layers

Write

\[
 a=2^s b,\qquad b\ \text{odd},\qquad R(a)=v_2(2a)=s+1.
\]

By Theorem 3.1, `e` is recurrent exactly when

\[
             2^{R(a)}\mid a-e.
 \tag{9}
\]

There are `(b+1)/2` such exponents in `{0,...,a}`.  Consequently the number
of recurrent divisors is

\[
 \#\operatorname{Rec}(\Phi_N)
 =\prod_{i=1}^r\frac{\operatorname{odd}(a_i)+1}{2}.
 \tag{10}
\]

For `t>=0`, let `B_N(t)` count divisors of preperiod at most `t`.  Locally,
Theorem 3.1 says that `alpha(e)<=t` exactly when

\[
 2^{\max(R(a)-t,0)}\mid a-e.
\]

Hence

\[
 B_N(t)=\prod_{i=1}^r
 \left(
 \left\lfloor
 \frac{a_i}{2^{\max(R(a_i)-t,0)}}
 \right\rfloor+1
 \right).
 \tag{11}
\]

The exact depth-`t` layer is `B_N(t)-B_N(t-1)`, with `B_N(-1)=0`.  The sharp
maximum preperiod is

\[
                    \max_i R(a_i),
 \tag{12}
\]

attained by taking `a_i-e_i` odd in any coordinate achieving the maximum.

## 5. Every iterated fibre

Fix a local target `j`, put `z=a-j`, and let

\[
                  g_t=2^{\min(t,R(a))},\qquad t\geq1.
\]

Solving `2^t[y]=[z]` in the sign quotient gives the complete local fibre:

\[
 \#(f_a^t)^{-1}(j)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&g_t\nmid z,\\
 (g_t+2)/2,&z=0,\\
 g_t/2,&z=a\ \text{and}\ g_t\mid a,\\
 g_t,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{13}
\]

To prove (13), solve `2^t y=+z` and `2^t y=-z` modulo `2a`.  Each soluble
signed equation has `g_t` residue solutions.  For a generic sign class the
two solution sets are disjoint and quotient to `g_t` classes.  At `z=0`, the
union has `g_t` residues and contains both self-inverse residues `0,a`, giving
`(g_t+2)/2` classes.  At `z=a`, solvability requires `g_t|a`; no solution is
self-inverse and the `g_t` residues form `g_t/2` classes.  This exhausts the
cases.

For the full divisor map, the iterated fibre is the product of the local
quantities (13).  In particular, every image and every indegree are explicit
at every time, not merely at absorption.

## 6. Fixed iterates, cycles, and zeta

The sign class `[y]` is fixed by the `n`th iterate precisely when

\[
                  2^n y\equiv y\quad\text{or}\quad
                  2^n y\equiv-y\pmod{2a}.
\]

The two solution subgroups have sizes `gcd(2^n-1,2a)` and
`gcd(2^n+1,2a)`.  Their intersection is only zero.  The union is invariant
under sign; zero is its only self-inverse member because the other
self-inverse residue `a` is not a solution.  Burnside counting therefore
gives

\[
 \operatorname{Fix}(f_a^n)=
 \frac{\gcd(2^n-1,2a)+\gcd(2^n+1,2a)}2.
 \tag{14}
\]

Thus

\[
 \operatorname{Fix}(\Phi_N^n)=
 \prod_{i=1}^r
 \frac{\gcd(2^n-1,2a_i)+\gcd(2^n+1,2a_i)}2.
 \tag{15}
\]

The number of points of exact period `n` and the number of `n`-cycles are

\[
 P_N(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)\operatorname{Fix}(\Phi_N^d),
 \qquad C_N(n)=P_N(n)/n.
 \tag{16}
\]

Equivalently, the finite Artin--Mazur zeta function is

\[
 \zeta_{\Phi_N}(u)=
 \exp\!\left(\sum_{n\geq1}
 \frac{\operatorname{Fix}(\Phi_N^n)}n u^n\right)
 =\prod_{n\geq1}(1-u^n)^{-C_N(n)},
 \tag{17}
\]

with only finitely many nonzero exponents.
