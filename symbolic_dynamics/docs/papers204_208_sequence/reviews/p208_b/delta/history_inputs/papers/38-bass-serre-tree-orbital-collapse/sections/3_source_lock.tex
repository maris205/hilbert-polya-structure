\section{Frozen new object and ownership}
\label{sec:source-lock}

\subsection{Original Bass--Serre tree}

Put $A=\langle u\rangle\cong\mathbb Z$.  We use only the Bass--Serre tree
$\Trree$ of the original HNN splitting in \eqref{eq:presentation-intro}.  Its
vertices are left cosets of $A$ and its edge incidence indices are $1$ and
$r$.  For $r\ge2$, every vertex has degree $r+1$; for $r=1$, the tree is a
line.  The action has one vertex orbit and one unoriented-edge orbit.

\begin{convention}[Height and modular cocycle]
\label{conv:height}
The HNN height is the homomorphism
\[
  \height:\Gr\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,
  \qquad \height(u)=0,\quad \height(v)=1.
\]
We use the modular convention $\modc(g)=r^{\height(g)}$.  The inverse
convention replaces $s$ by $-s$ in every weighted formula and has no effect on
the verdict.
\end{convention}

\subsection{Literal full-tree shift}

Let $\Eor\Trree$ be the actual oriented edges of the universal tree.  A
transition $e\to f$ is legal when $o(f)=t(e)$ and $f\ne\bar e$.  The full
two-sided edge shift consists of sequences $(e_i)_{i\in\mathbb Z}$ satisfying
this rule.  The corresponding operator on $\ell^2(\Eor\Trree)$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Hash\delta_e
  =\sum_{\substack{o(f)=t(e)\\f\ne\bar e}}\delta_f.
  \label{eq:hashimoto}
\end{equation}
One application of the shift is one new Bass--Serre edge marker.

The allowed coefficient may multiply a positive or negative height step by
the corresponding nonzero scalar power of $r$.  It may not depend on a chosen
root, a fitted representation, a divisor splitting, or an accepted-support
predicate.

\subsection{Acceptance and falsification}

The candidate would pass only if the primary row simultaneously supplied a
nonempty source-selective primitive ledger, an ordinary Fredholm determinant,
and a consistent new marker.  One positive reduced closed path would falsify
the emptiness claim.  A nonzero canonical modular weighting that made the
same full-tree operator trace class would falsify the analytic obstruction.
A proper finite-stabilizer action satisfying the tree-lattice hypotheses
without quotienting the acting group would falsify the tree-lattice boundary.

The controls include balanced $r=1$, prime $r=2,3,5,7$, composite baseline
$r=4$, composites $r=6,8,9,10,12$, deliberate ascending and non-ascending
$\BS(p,q)$ presentations, seeded random one-relator eligibility rows, finite
trees, orthogonal columns, divergence, and marker collisions.  Any empty,
generic, divergent, non-trace-class, or marker-incompatible outcome is
terminal; no repair search follows it.
