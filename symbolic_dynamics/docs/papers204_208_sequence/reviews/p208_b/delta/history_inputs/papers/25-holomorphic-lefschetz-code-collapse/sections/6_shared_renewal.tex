\section{Shared renewal: stability cancels, mixed words remain}
\label{sec:shared}

Let \(J\) be finite, or countable with \(\sum_{j\in J}|w_j|<\infty\), and
let \(\phi_j(z)=a_j+q_jz\) satisfy the common compact-containment condition.
On one shared disk define
\[
        L_k=\sum_{j\in J}w_jU_{\phi_j,k},
        \qquad S_w=\sum_{j\in J}w_j.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Shared cohomology collapse]
\label{thm:shared-collapse}
For every \(r\ge1\),
\begin{equation}
        \Tr L_0^r-\Tr L_1^r=S_w^r.
 \label{eq:shared-supertrace}
\end{equation}
The degreewise determinants satisfy
\begin{equation}
        \det(I-zL_0)=(1-zS_w)\det(I-zL_1).
 \label{eq:shared-det}
\end{equation}
Hence the shared graded determinant is \(1-zS_w\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expand \(L_k^r\) over ordered words
\(\alpha=(j_1,\ldots,j_r)\in J^r\).  The corresponding composition is
affine with derivative
\[
        q_\alpha=\prod_{m=1}^rq_{j_m},
\]
and its scalar weight is
\(w_\alpha=\prod_{m=1}^rw_{j_m}\).  The fixed-word trace formula gives
\[
 \Tr U_{\alpha,0}=\frac1{1-q_\alpha},\qquad
 \Tr U_{\alpha,1}=\frac{q_\alpha}{1-q_\alpha}.
\]
Their weighted difference is \(w_\alpha\).  Absolute weight summability
justifies the word sum, so
\[
 \Tr L_0^r-\Tr L_1^r
 =\sum_{\alpha\in J^r}w_\alpha
 =\left(\sum_{j\in J}w_j\right)^r.
\]
Substitution in the trace logarithm proves \eqref{eq:shared-det} near the
origin.  Both degreewise determinants are entire, and the identity theorem
completes the proof.
\end{proof}

On the exact polynomial complex, every weighted sum still obeys
\(dL_0=L_1d\).  Constants carry multiplication by \(S_w\), and the
nonconstant quotient is intertwined with the one-form action.  Thus the
finite characteristic-polynomial identity is
\[
 \det(I-zL_0|P_N)
 =(1-zS_w)\det(I-zL_1|P_{N-1}dz).
\]
This is the cohomological meaning of \cref{thm:shared-collapse}: the whole
shared disk retracts to one constant state.  The infinite proof, however,
is the fixed-word trace proof above and does not assume a bounded Bergman
differential.

\subsection{Primitive necklace ledger}

The one-state return alphabet with weights \(w_j\) has the standard
primitive-necklace factorization
\begin{equation}
 1-z\sum_jw_j
 =\prod_{[\alpha]\ \mathrm{primitive}}
   \left(1-z^{|\alpha|}w_\alpha\right)
 \label{eq:necklace-product}
\end{equation}
as a formal identity, or analytically near the origin.  The product ranges
over cyclic rotations of primitive return words.  Thus the linear-looking
left side does not mean that only one-letter primitive objects exist; its
trace logarithm contains every primitive necklace on the shared alphabet.

\begin{corollary}[Mixed necklaces survive]
\label{cor:mixed-survive}
For two return weights \(x,y\), the shared and disjoint connected logarithms
differ by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 &-\log(1-z(x+y))
 +\log((1-zx)(1-zy))\\
 &\hspace{16mm}=z^2xy+z^3(x^2y+xy^2)+O(z^4).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:mixed-expansion}
\end{equation}
In particular, the primitive mixed necklace \([xy]\) survives at length
two, and \([xxy]\), \([xyy]\) survive at length three.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
At the power-trace level,
\[
        \Str L^2=x^2+2xy+y^2,
\]
while a disjoint two-loop ledger has \(x^2+y^2\).  Expanding the two
logarithms gives \eqref{eq:mixed-expansion}.  The indicated monomials have
the stated primitive cyclic representatives.
\end{proof}

The mechanism is now exact.  For each word \(\alpha\), exterior grading
cancels \((1-q_\alpha)^{-1}\) and leaves \(w_\alpha\).  It cannot then
cancel a word merely because the labels in \(\alpha\) differ.  In a
constrained branch graph, the identical proof leaves all legal graph cycles
on degree-zero cohomology.  A tangent exterior complex repairs local
stability; it is not a branch-combinatorial selector.

For the arithmetic weights \(w_n=n^{-s}\), \cref{thm:shared-collapse}
becomes
\begin{equation}
 \Dgr(z;\cL_{S,s})=1-z\sum_{n\in S}n^{-s}.
 \label{eq:shared-dirichlet}
\end{equation}
Even when \(S=\Pp\), this is the determinant of a shared return full shift,
not the disconnected Euler product over prime loops.
