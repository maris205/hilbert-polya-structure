\section{Exact density and complement separation}

Let $L_n=|v_n|$, let $W_n=|v_n|_1$, and define the signed discrepancy
\[
 d_n=2W_n-L_n.
\]
By \cref{lem:alternating},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:initial-counts}
 L_N=2^{N+1}-1,\qquad W_N=2^N-1,\qquad d_N=-1.
\end{equation}
For every $n\geq N$, the tail construction gives the exact recurrences
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count-recurrences}
 L_{n+1}=4L_n+2,\qquad W_{n+1}=4W_n+2,\qquad d_{n+1}=4d_n+2.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[exact one-density]\label{prop:density}
The common one-density of the points in $X_N$ is
\[
 \rho_N=\frac{3\cdot2^N-1}{3\cdot2^{N+1}-1}<\frac12.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The affine shifts in \eqref{eq:count-recurrences} satisfy
\[
 L_{n+1}+\frac23=4\left(L_n+\frac23\right),
 \qquad
 d_{n+1}+\frac23=4\left(d_n+\frac23\right).
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:initial-counts}, this yields
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{d_n}{L_n}
 =\frac{d_N+2/3}{L_N+2/3}
 =-\frac{1}{3L_N+2}.
\]
Since $W_n/L_n=(1+d_n/L_n)/2$, substitution of
$L_N=2^{N+1}-1$ gives
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{W_n}{L_n}
 =\frac{3\cdot2^N-1}{3\cdot2^{N+1}-1}.
\]
This is the frequency of the one-letter word $1$ by
\cref{prop:unique-ergodicity}.  Its strict inequality from $1/2$ follows
equally from the negative limiting discrepancy.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[complement disjointness]\label{cor:complement-disjoint}
The subshifts $X_N$ and $\comp(X_N)$ are disjoint.  Consequently the XOR
derivative is a conjugacy from $X_N$ onto $Y_N=\Delta(X_N)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Every $x\in X_N$ has one-density $\rho_N$.  Its complement $\comp(x)$ has
one-density $1-\rho_N$, which differs from $\rho_N$.  Thus $x$ and
$\comp(x)$ cannot both belong to $X_N$, proving
$X_N\cap\comp(X_N)=\varnothing$.  The binary case of
\cref{thm:finite-group-intro}, recorded in \cref{rem:binary}, now applies.
\end{proof}

The role of unique ergodicity is quantitative but clean: it promotes the
prefix count to a density shared by every point.  The strict density imbalance
then separates the two compact subshifts globally, while the next section
shows that this global separation can require long words to detect.
