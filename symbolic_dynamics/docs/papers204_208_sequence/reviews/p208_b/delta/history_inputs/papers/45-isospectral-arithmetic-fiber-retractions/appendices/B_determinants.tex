\section{Legality of the common regularized determinant}
\label{app:determinants}

For an integer $r\ge1$ and $T\in\Sr$, the order-$r$ regularized
determinant is standard \cite[Ch.~9, pp.~75--80]{Simon2005}.  In terms of the algebraic
nonzero eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity,
\begin{equation}
  \det_r(I-zT)
  =\prod_{\lambda\in\spec(T)\setminus\{0\}}
    \left[(1-z\lambda)
      \exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\frac{(z\lambda)^j}{j}\right)
    \right].
  \label{eq:regularized-product}
\end{equation}
The empty sum at $r=1$ gives the Fredholm determinant.  Membership in
$\Sr$ guarantees convergence and an entire function of $z$.

For the operators in this paper, \cref{prop:schatten} shows that both
$S_{h,s}$ and $M_{h,s}$ lie in $\Sr$ exactly on the common legal domain
\begin{equation}
  \sigma>1/h,
  \qquad r\sigma>2.
  \label{eq:det-legal-domain}
\end{equation}
Their nonzero eigenvalues are the same simple sequence
$\lambda_m=m^{-s/2}$, $m\in\Fh$.  Substitution in
\cref{eq:regularized-product} therefore proves
\begin{equation}
  \det_r(I-zS_{h,s})=\det_r(I-zM_{h,s})
\end{equation}
on \cref{eq:det-legal-domain}.  When $r=1$, this condition reads
$\sigma>2$.  No product is asserted for either unbounded algebraic map, and
determinant equality is not used to infer bounded similarity.
In particular, throughout the isospectral nonsimilarity band
$1/h<\sigma\le1$, equality means equality in each common legal integer
order $r>2/\sigma$; order $r=1$ does not occur there.
