\section{Split factors and entropy rigidity}
\label{sec:rigidity}

We first isolate a standard structural fact used to position the local
construction.  Recall that a continuous injective map from a compact space
to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism onto its image.

\begin{lemma}[Entropy minimality]
\label{lem:entropy-minimality}
If $X$ is an irreducible one-dimensional SFT and
$Z\subsetneq X$ is a subshift, then $h(Z)<h(X)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a word $w\in\cL(X)\setminus\cL(Z)$.  After passing to a sufficiently
high block presentation, forbidding $w$ is represented by deleting at least
one edge from an irreducible graph presentation of $X$.  Let $A$ be its
irreducible adjacency matrix and let $B\le A$, $B\ne A$, be the adjacency
matrix after the deletion.  Strict Perron monotonicity gives
$\rho(B)<\rho(A)$.  Since $Z$ is contained in the SFT defined by $B$,
\[
 h(Z)\le\log\rho(B)<\log\rho(A)=h(X).
\]
This is the familiar entropy-minimality property of irreducible SFTs; see
also the standard background in \cite{LindMarcus2021}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Rigidity of split irreducible factors]
\label{prop:rigidity}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be irreducible one-dimensional SFTs, let
$\pi:X\to Y$ be a factor map, and suppose a continuous shift-commuting
$\iota:Y\to X$ satisfies $\pi\circ\iota=\id_Y$.  Then
\[
 h(X)=h(Y)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \pi\text{ is a conjugacy}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The right-inverse identity makes $\iota$ injective.  It is therefore a
conjugacy from $Y$ onto the subshift $\iota(Y)\subseteq X$.  If the inclusion
is proper, \cref{lem:entropy-minimality} gives
\[
 h(Y)=h(\iota(Y))<h(X).
\]
If $\iota(Y)=X$, then $\iota$ is a conjugacy onto $X$ and
$\pi=\iota^{-1}$.  The reverse implication is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Irreducibility cannot be dropped]
\label{ex:reducible}
Let $X=Y\sqcup Y$ be the disjoint union of two copies of an irreducible SFT,
map both components identically onto $Y$, and use inclusion of the first
component as a section.  The factor is noninjective but
$h(X)=h(Y)$.  Hence \cref{prop:rigidity} is not a theorem for reducible
domains.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}
\Cref{prop:rigidity} is not the principal contribution of this note.  Its
role is to certify that the local extensions constructed below have strictly
positive entropy excess once nonconjugacy and irreducibility are proved.
\end{remark}
