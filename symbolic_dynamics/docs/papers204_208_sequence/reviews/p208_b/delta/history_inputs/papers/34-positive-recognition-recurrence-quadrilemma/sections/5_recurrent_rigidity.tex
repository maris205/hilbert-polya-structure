\section{Recurrent rigidity and the connector repair}

\begin{lemma}[simple and disjoint atom cycles]\label{lem:disjoint}
Under the literal ledger and \eqref{eq:free}, every atom cycle is simple and
two distinct atom cycles share no vertex.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If distinct cycles meet, rotate their edge words $x,y$ at a shared vertex and
write $xy=w^m$ with $w$ primitive.  The literal ledger identifies $w$ with
some $\gamma_d$.  Roof additivity gives
$N(a)N(c)=N(d)^m$, contradicting \eqref{eq:free}.  If one atom cycle repeats
a vertex before closing, split it there into nonempty closed words and take
their primitive roots.  The same argument yields
$N(a)=N(c)^mN(d)^n$, another forbidden exponent relation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[mixed root in a shared component]\label{lem:scc}
Every recurrent strongly connected component is exactly one atom cycle.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose one component contains distinct atom cycles $\gamma_a$ and
$\gamma_c$.  They are vertex-disjoint by \cref{lem:disjoint}.  Choose any
path $\alpha$ from an attachment vertex of the first cycle to one on the
second, and any path $\beta$ back.  The closed word
\[
W=\gamma_a\alpha\gamma_c\beta
\]
has a primitive root $w$.  Since $W=w^m$, $W$ and $w$ have identical edge
support.  The ledger identifies $w$ with an atom cycle.  Because it meets
$\gamma_a$, \cref{lem:disjoint} forces equality with $\gamma_a$; yet $w$
also contains an edge of $\gamma_c$, a contradiction.  Every extra edge in
a recurrent component lies on a closed walk and yields the same argument.
\end{proof}

No shortest-path or interior-avoidance normal form is used.  This distinction
is essential.  The preregistered finite proxy required both connectors to
avoid both cycles internally and failed on $18{,}272$ examples.  The repaired
statement asks only for mutual reachability, which is exactly what a strongly
connected component supplies.
