\section{The reduced tensor bar-code shift}
\label{sec:bar}

The rigidity result says that the next candidate must retain higher
factorization chains rather than compress them into an abelian one-cocycle.
This section freezes that candidate completely.

\subsection{Phase space, clock, and potential}

Let $\M_+=\{\F_n:n\ge2\}$.  Define the countable code alphabet
\[
  \C=\coprod_{k\ge1}\M_+^k.
  \tag{4.1}
\]
A symbol $\mathbf a=(\F_{a_1},\ldots,\F_{a_k})$ is a nonempty ordered tensor
word.  Equivalently, it is the strict cumulative divisor chain
\[
  \F_1<\F_{a_1}<\F_{a_1a_2}<\cdots<\F_{a_1\cdots a_k}.
  \tag{4.2}
\]
The phase space is the one-vertex countable edge shift with one return edge
for each $\mathbf a\in\C$.  Thus a point is a bi-infinite sequence of code
edges, and temporal recurrence is the ordinary shift of that sequence.

Freeze the roof, parity, and complex weight
\[
\begin{split}
  T(\mathbf a)&=\sum_{j=1}^k h(\F_{a_j})
               =\log(a_1\cdots a_k),\\
  \eps(\mathbf a)&=(-1)^{k+1},\\
  \phi_s(\mathbf a)&=\eps(\mathbf a)e^{-sT(\mathbf a)}.
\end{split}
\tag{4.3}
\]
The sign is determined by reduced-bar length after the bar grammar has been
chosen.  The choice of this grammar is a modeling choice, not an output of
the entropy and not evidence for arithmetic selectivity.  Crucially, no
prime predicate, M\"obius coefficient, Mangoldt coefficient, zero table, or
per-atom phase occurs in (4.1)--(4.3).

The construction remembers ordered histories.  It is functorial under
isomorphisms of the tensor monoid, but it does not descend to the unique arrow
of the thin divisor category.  This is exactly why
\Cref{thm:coboundary} does not gauge it away.

\subsection{Raw alphabet sum}

Let
\[
  B(s)=\sum_{n\ge2}e^{-s h(\F_n)}
      =\sum_{n\ge2}n^{-s}=\zeta(s)-1.
  \tag{4.4}
\]
The total weight of length-$k$ code edges is $(-1)^{k+1}B(s)^k$ whenever the
sum may be rearranged.  Absolute summability of the raw alphabet requires
\[
\begin{split}
  \sum_{\mathbf a\in\C}|\phi_s(\mathbf a)|
  &=\sum_{k\ge1}\sum_{a_1,\ldots,a_k\ge2}
    (a_1\cdots a_k)^{-\Ree s}\\
  &=\sum_{k\ge1}B(\Ree s)^k<\infty.
\end{split}
\tag{4.5}
\]

\begin{definition}[Raw convergence boundary]
Let $\sbar$ be the unique real solution of
\[
  \zeta(\sbar)=2.
\]
Numerically,
$\sbar=1.7286472389981836181\ldots$.
\end{definition}

Uniqueness follows because $\zeta(\sigma)-1$ is continuous and strictly
decreasing from $+\infty$ to $0$ on $\sigma>1$.  Equation (4.5) is finite
exactly for $\Ree s>\sbar$.

\begin{proposition}[Raw reduced-bar transfer]
\label{prop:raw}
For $\Ree s>\sbar$, the raw edge sum is absolutely convergent and
\[
  \Fbar(s)
  :=\sum_{\mathbf a\in\C}\phi_s(\mathbf a)
  =\sum_{k\ge1}(-1)^{k+1}B(s)^k
  =\frac{B(s)}{1+B(s)}.
  \tag{4.6}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the stated half-plane, $|B(s)|\le B(\Ree s)<1$.  The absolute bound (4.5)
permits Fubini rearrangement by internal word length.  The resulting series
is geometric, which proves (4.6).
\end{proof}

The domain in \Cref{prop:raw} belongs to the source lock.  An analytic formula
obtained by regrouping coefficients below $\sbar$ must not be mislabeled as
the raw sum over code edges.

\subsection{Function space and canonical determinant}

The weighted vertex adjacency of a one-vertex edge shift acts on
\[
  \B=\ell^2(\{*\})\cong\CC.
\]
After the alphabet sum has converged, define
\[
  L_{\mathrm{bar},s}c=\Fbar(s)c.
  \tag{4.7}
\]
This is a one-dimensional trace-class operator.  Its $r$th trace is
$\Fbar(s)^r$, the weight sum of all based temporal words of $r$ code edges.
Thus the scalar operator is the weighted adjacency of the frozen recurrent
shift, rather than an unrelated scalar attached after factorization.

\begin{theorem}[Same-object Fredholm determinant]
\label{thm:determinant}
For $\Ree s>\sbar$, the canonical determinant of SD-C16 is
\[
  \Dbar(s,z)
  :=\det_{\CC}(I-zL_{\mathrm{bar},s})
  =1-z\Fbar(s).
  \tag{4.8}
\]
At the frozen specialization $z=1$,
\[
  \Dbar(s,1)=\frac{1}{\zeta(s)}.
  \tag{4.9}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation (4.8) is the determinant of the one-dimensional operator (4.7).
Using (4.4) and (4.6),
\[
  1-\Fbar(s)=1-\frac{B(s)}{1+B(s)}
             =\frac1{1+B(s)}=\frac1{\zeta(s)}.
\]
\end{proof}

The theorem is stronger than merely rewriting the classical logarithmic
derivative: it identifies a single weighted symbolic adjacency whose
determinant has the required value in a proved raw domain.  It is also much
weaker than an RH mechanism.  The partition sum $1+B(s)$ equals $\zeta(s)$
because the source inventory already contains exactly one $\F_n$ at every
integer entropy $\log n$.

\subsection{Trace-log and primitive cycles}

Whenever $|z\Fbar(s)|<1$, the determinant has the convergent trace expansion
\[
  \log\Dbar(s,z)
  =-\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{z^r}{r}\tr L_{\mathrm{bar},s}^r
  =-\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{z^r}{r}\Fbar(s)^r.
  \tag{4.10}
\]
The sign in (4.3) is an ordinary scalar edge weight.  Repeating a single code
edge $\mathbf a$ exactly $r$ times contributes
\[
  \eps(\mathbf a)^r e^{-rsT(\mathbf a)}.
  \tag{4.11}
\]
It is not a chain grading or supertrace parity: an odd supertrace sector would
carry one fixed minus sign at every repetition order, whereas a negative
scalar edge alternates with $r$.  We therefore claim no chain complex,
contractible-pair cancellation, or homological reduction in this paper.

The primitive cycles behind (4.10) are cyclic necklaces in the countable
alphabet $\C$.  A one-cycle may itself be an ordered factorization chain, and
longer primitive cycles concatenate several such chains.  There is no
canonical bijection between these necklaces and the tensor atoms $\F_p$.

At $z=1$, (4.9) gives a nonzero analytic logarithm throughout $\Ree s>1$.
We do not silently identify that logarithm with the power series (4.10) at
points where $|\Fbar(s)|\ge1$.  The distinction between analytic logarithm,
trace-log germ, and raw alphabet sum will be maintained in the audit.
