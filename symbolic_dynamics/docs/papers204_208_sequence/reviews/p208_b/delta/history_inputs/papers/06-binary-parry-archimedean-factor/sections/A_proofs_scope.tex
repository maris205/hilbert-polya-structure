\section{Proof details, domains, and scope discipline}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Local uniformity of the Mellin limit}

We record the analytic point hidden by a bare appeal to the central limit
theorem. Let $C$ be a compact subset of $\{\RePart s>0\}$ and choose
$0<\sigma_-\le\RePart s\le\sigma_+$. Split the lattice expectation into
$|Y_N|<\delta$, $\delta\le |Y_N|\le R$, and $|Y_N|>R$.

On the middle region, the local central limit theorem gives a Riemann-sum
approximation uniform in $s\in C$. Hoeffding's binomial bound makes the tail
uniformly smaller than a Gaussian moment of order $\sigma_+-1$ as
$R\to\infty$. At the origin, the maximal lattice mass is $O(N^{-1/2})$ and
the lattice spacing of $Y_N$ is $O(N^{-1/2})$. Summing
$x^{\sigma_--1}(1+|\log x|^k)$ over lattice cells in $(0,\delta)$ is bounded
by its integrable continuum counterpart plus a vanishing endpoint term.
This controls the complex powers and their derivatives uniformly on $C$.
First let $N\to\infty$, then $\delta\downarrow0$ and $R\uparrow\infty$.
Vitali's theorem, or the direct derivative bounds, gives local uniformity.

The restriction to odd $N$ is important. For even $N$, $S_N=0$ has positive
probability, and $|S_N|^{s-1}$ is not finite when $0<\RePart s<1$.

\subsection{Rank-one power-trace criterion}

Suppose a finite reversible stochastic matrix $K$ has eigenvalues
$1,\lambda_2,\ldots,\lambda_q$. If $\tr K^r=1$ for all $r\ge1$, then
$\sum_{j=2}^q\lambda_j^r=0$ for all $r$. The first $q-1$ power sums vanish,
so Newton identities make every elementary symmetric polynomial in the
nonstationary eigenvalues zero. Hence all $\lambda_j=0$. Reversibility makes
$K$ diagonalizable in its stationary inner product, so it is a rank-one
projection. This proves that the Euler ledger tests rank one, not alphabet
size.

\subsection{Operator ideals for the chiral pair}

The singular values of the upper-right atom blocks scale as $p^{-\RePart s}$,
and those of the lower-left blocks as $p^{-(1-\RePart s)}$. Both lie in
$S_q$ exactly when
\[
 q\RePart s>1,
 \qquad q(1-\RePart s)>1.
\]
For $q=2$ this gives the open strip $1/2<\RePart s<1/2$, which is empty.
The first integer class with a nonempty common strip is $q=3$, giving
$1/3<\RePart s<2/3$. The $\det_3$ subtraction removes trace powers one and
two. Odd chiral traces already vanish, while the power-two trace is the
divergent $2\sum_pp^{-1}$. The resulting determinant therefore cannot be
credited with preserving the low-order Euler ledger.

\subsection{Data and interpretation firewall}

The candidate definition uses no Riemann-zero list, no fitted critical-axis
scale, and no target-root loss. Prime atoms are recovered as tensor
indecomposables, not read from a prime file. The $1/2$ appearing in the
Gaussian channel is the diffusive exponent and the $1/2$ appearing in the
chiral channel is the equality point of paired exponents $s$ and $1-s$.
Neither produces the Riemann functional equation by itself.

The following implications are forbidden:
\begin{align*}
 \text{Gamma factor}&\not\Rightarrow\text{analytic continuation},\\
 \text{self-adjoint blocks}&\not\Rightarrow\text{Hilbert--P\'olya},\\
 \text{critical centering}&\not\Rightarrow\text{vertical zeros}.
\end{align*}
Any idea requiring a different primary system family is excluded from the
paper and may only be stored as a future \texttt{ROUND2\_CLUE}.
