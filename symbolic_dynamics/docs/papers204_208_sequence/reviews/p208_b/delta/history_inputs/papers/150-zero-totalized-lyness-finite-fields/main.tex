\begin{abstract}
Let $\F_q$ be a finite field of odd order, set $\invz(0)=0$ and
$\invz(x)=x^{-1}$ for $x\ne0$, and consider the all-affine self-map
$\Lyn(x,y)=(y,(1+y)\invz(x))$.  We determine its complete functional graph.
The plane splits disjointly into a generic Lyness locus, the two coordinate
axes, and three exceptional tail layers.  The recurrent set has
$q^2-3q+5$ points, while the tail polynomial is
\[
 (q^2-3q+5)+(q-1)z+(q-2)z^2+(q-2)z^3;
\]
thus the maximum tail is exactly three for every odd $q$.  If $r_q$ counts
the roots of $a^2-a-1$, then the graph has $1+r_q$ fixed points, two
2-cycles, $(q-3)/2$ 4-cycles, and
$((q-2)(q-3)-r_q)/5$ 5-cycles.  We also prove a codomain-wide inverse law:
fibres have size $q$ at $(-1,0)$, size zero at $(-1,v)$ for $v\ne0$, and
size one elsewhere.  Classical Lyness five-periodicity, QRT and cluster
interpretations, and general finite-field birational dynamics receive zero
contribution credit.  The residual result is the exact zero-totalized affine
boundary completion.  External release remains on hold.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and complete statement}

The parameter-one Lyness recurrence and its five-cycle are classical.  The
same map is a basic QRT system and a type-$A_2$ cluster example
\citep{Lyness1942,HoneKouloukas2023,Hone2020}.  Integrable birational maps
over finite fields also admit general algebraic-geometric and
almost-good-reduction treatments~\citep{JogiaRobertsVivaldi2006,Kanki2013}.
Kanki resolves division-by-zero obstructions by extending/reducing the state
space, not by setting inverse zero to zero.  All of that material is
background here.
We study one boundary convention on the whole affine plane, including every
point at which the usual rational formula has a zero denominator.

Fix a finite field $\F_q$ of odd order.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:invzero}
 \invz(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&x=0,\\
 x^{-1},&x\ne0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \Lyn(x,y)=\bigl(y,(1+y)\invz(x)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Let $r_q$ be the number of roots in $\F_q$ of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-polynomial}
 a^2-a-1=0.
\end{equation}
For a point $P$, its tail $\tau(P)$ is the least $t\geq0$ such that
$\Lyn^t(P)$ is recurrent.

The five sets below will describe every point.  Put
\begin{align}
 \G&=\{(x,y):xy(1+x)(1+y)(1+x+y)\ne0\},\label{eq:generic}\\
 \A&=(\{0\}\times\F_q)\cup(\F_q\times\{0\}),\label{eq:axes}\\
 \E_1&=\{(a,-1):a\in\F_q\setminus\{0\}\},\label{eq:e1}\\
 \E_2&=\{(-1-a,a):a\in\F_q\setminus\{0,-1\}\},\label{eq:e2}\\
 \E_3&=\{(-1,-1-a):a\in\F_q\setminus\{0,-1\}\}.\label{eq:e3}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Complete all-affine atlas]\label{thm:main}
For every odd prime power $q$, the following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item The five sets in \eqref{eq:generic}--\eqref{eq:e3} are pairwise
disjoint and their union is $\F_q^2$.  Their respective sizes are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:strata-sizes}
 (q-2)(q-3),\qquad 2q-1,\qquad q-1,\qquad q-2,\qquad q-2.
\end{equation}
The recurrent set is $\G\cup\A$, and $\tau$ equals $j$ on $\E_j$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:temporal}
 \sum_{P\in\F_q^2}z^{\tau(P)}
 =(q^2-3q+5)+(q-1)z+(q-2)z^2+(q-2)z^3.
\end{equation}
In particular, the maximum tail is exactly three.
\item The cycle census is
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}cc@{}}
\toprule
cycle length & number of cycles\\
\midrule
$1$ & $1+r_q$\\
$2$ & $2$\\
$4$ & $(q-3)/2$\\
$5$ & $((q-2)(q-3)-r_q)/5$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
There are no other cycles.  Hence the dynamical zeta function is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeta}
 \zeta_{\Lyn}(z)=
 (1-z)^{-(1+r_q)}(1-z^2)^{-2}
 (1-z^4)^{-(q-3)/2}
 (1-z^5)^{-((q-2)(q-3)-r_q)/5}.
\end{equation}
\item For every target $(u,v)\in\F_q^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre-law}
 |\Lyn^{-1}(u,v)|=
 \begin{cases}
 q,&(u,v)=(-1,0),\\
 0,&u=-1\text{ and }v\ne0,\\
 1,&u\ne-1.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Thus $|\im\Lyn|=q^2-q+1$, and the exceptional in-tree attached to the
two-cycle $(-1,0)\leftrightarrow(0,-1)$ is exactly the one displayed in
\cref{cor:tree} below.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The next three sections prove \cref{thm:main}.  Enumeration is used only for
the independent controls reported in \cref{sec:controls}.

\section{Disjoint strata and the sharp tail polynomial}

We first prove coverage pointwise; the cardinality identity will be a
consequence rather than a substitute.

\begin{proposition}[Pointwise all-plane partition]\label{prop:partition}
The sets $\G,\A,\E_1,\E_2,\E_3$ are pairwise disjoint and cover $\F_q^2$.
Their sizes are given by \eqref{eq:strata-sizes}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every point of $\A$ has a zero coordinate.  Every point in an exceptional
set has two nonzero coordinates: the exclusions on the parameter in
\eqref{eq:e1}--\eqref{eq:e3} guarantee this.  Hence $\A$ is disjoint from
the other four sets.

Now take $(x,y)\notin\A$.  If $y=-1$, then $(x,y)\in\E_1$.  Suppose
$y\ne-1$.  If $x=-1$, set $a=-1-y$.  Since $y\notin\{0,-1\}$, one has
$a\notin\{0,-1\}$ and $(x,y)=(-1,-1-a)\in\E_3$.  Suppose also $x\ne-1$.
If $1+x+y=0$, then $(x,y)=(-1-a,a)\in\E_2$ with $a=y\notin\{0,-1\}$.
If none of these three equalities holds, all five factors in
\eqref{eq:generic} are nonzero, so $(x,y)\in\G$.  The tests are ordered by
mutually exclusive equations.  They therefore prove both coverage and
unique membership.

The axis union has size $2q-1$, while the three exceptional parametrizations
are injective and have sizes $q-1,q-2,q-2$.  Subtracting these disjoint sets
from the $q^2$ points of the plane gives
\[
 |\G|=q^2-(2q-1)-(q-1)-2(q-2)=(q-2)(q-3).
\]
\end{proof}

On $\G$, the zero-totalized map agrees for five steps with the classical
rational recurrence.  We record the algebra because it also verifies that
no zero denominator is crossed.

\begin{lemma}[Five iterates on the generic locus]\label{lem:five}
If $(x,y)\in\G$, then
\begin{align}
 \Lyn(x,y)&=\left(y,\frac{1+y}{x}\right),\nonumber\\
 \Lyn^2(x,y)&=\left(\frac{1+y}{x},
                  \frac{1+x+y}{xy}\right),\nonumber\\
 \Lyn^3(x,y)&=\left(\frac{1+x+y}{xy},
                  \frac{1+x}{y}\right),\label{eq:five-iterates}\\
 \Lyn^4(x,y)&=\left(\frac{1+x}{y},x\right),\nonumber\\
 \Lyn^5(x,y)&=(x,y).\nonumber
\end{align}
In particular every point of $\G$ is recurrent.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The five possible first coordinates at successive divisions are
\[
 x,\quad y,\quad \frac{1+y}{x},\quad
 \frac{1+x+y}{xy},\quad \frac{1+x}{y}.
\]
They are all nonzero by \eqref{eq:generic}, so $\invz$ is ordinary inversion
at every displayed step.  Repeated substitution in \eqref{eq:invzero} gives
\eqref{eq:five-iterates}; the only cancellations use
$(1+x)(1+y)=1+x+y+xy$.  The last line proves recurrence.
\end{proof}

The complement of $\G$ has equally explicit arrows.

\begin{lemma}[Axes and exceptional layers]\label{lem:arrows}
For $a\in\F_q$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:axis-arrows}
 \Lyn(0,a)=(a,0),\qquad
 \Lyn(a,0)=(0,a^{-1})\quad(a\ne0),
\end{equation}
and $(0,0)$ is fixed.  For $a\in\F_q\setminus\{0,-1\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-chain}
 (-1,-1-a)\longmapsto(-1-a,a)
 \longmapsto(a,-1)\longmapsto(-1,0),
\end{equation}
while
\begin{equation}\label{eq:leaf-and-cycle}
 (-1,-1)\longmapsto(-1,0)
 \longmapsto(0,-1)\longmapsto(-1,0).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equations \eqref{eq:axis-arrows} follow directly from $\invz(0)=0$ and
$\invz(a)=a^{-1}$ for $a\ne0$.  For the exceptional arrows, substitute into
\eqref{eq:invzero}:
\begin{align*}
 \Lyn(-1,-1-a)&=(-1-a,a),\\
 \Lyn(-1-a,a)&=\left(a,\frac{1+a}{-1-a}\right)=(a,-1),\\
 \Lyn(a,-1)&=(-1,0).
\end{align*}
The parameter exclusions make each displayed denominator nonzero.  The two
remaining arrows in \eqref{eq:leaf-and-cycle} are immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Recurrent set and exact tails]\label{thm:tails}
The recurrent set is $\G\cup\A$.  The sets $\E_1,\E_2,\E_3$ are exactly the
tail layers of depths $1,2,3$, respectively.  Hence
\eqref{eq:temporal} holds and the maximum tail is three.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The generic points are recurrent by \cref{lem:five}.  Equations
\eqref{eq:axis-arrows} show that $(0,0)$ is fixed and every other axis point
lies in a cycle generated by inversion in $\F_q^*$.  Thus every point of
$\A$ is recurrent.

By \cref{lem:arrows}, $\E_1$ maps into the recurrent two-cycle in one step,
$\E_2$ maps into $\E_1$, and $\E_3$ maps into $\E_2$.  The disjointness in
\cref{prop:partition} makes these depths exact.  No exceptional point is
recurrent because its orbit enters the invariant axis set and never returns
to its exceptional stratum.  This proves the recurrent-set assertion and all
pointwise depths.

The recurrent coefficient is
\[
 |\G|+|\A|=(q-2)(q-3)+2q-1=q^2-3q+5.
\]
Adding the three exceptional layer sizes gives \eqref{eq:temporal}.  Since
$q\geq3$, the set $\E_3$ has size $q-2>0$, so depth three is attained for
every allowed field.
\end{proof}

\section{The complete cycle census and zeta function}

The generic locus contributes only periods one and five.  The axes contribute
the remaining periods.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed points]\label{lem:fixed}
The fixed points are $(0,0)$ and the $r_q$ points $(a,a)$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:fixed-polynomial}.  Every latter point belongs to $\G$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $\Lyn(x,y)=(x,y)$, then $y=x$.  At $x=0$ this gives $(0,0)$.  For
$x\ne0$, the second coordinate equation is
$(1+x)/x=x$, equivalently $x^2-x-1=0$.  Conversely every such root gives a
fixed point.

A root $a$ is neither $0$ nor $-1$.  It also satisfies $1+2a\ne0$: otherwise
$a=-1/2$, and substitution into $a^2-a-1$ gives $-1/4\ne0$ because the
characteristic is odd.  Thus all five factors defining $\G$ are nonzero at
$(a,a)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Axis cycles]\label{lem:axis-cycles}
The axes contain $(0,0)$, exactly two 2-cycles, and exactly $(q-3)/2$
4-cycles.  The 2-cycles are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-cycles}
 (0,1)\longleftrightarrow(1,0),\qquad
 (0,-1)\longleftrightarrow(-1,0).
\end{equation}
For $a\in\F_q^*\setminus\{1,-1\}$, the inversion pair
$\{a,a^{-1}\}$ indexes the 4-cycle
\begin{equation}\label{eq:four-cycle}
 (0,a)\longmapsto(a,0)\longmapsto(0,a^{-1})
 \longmapsto(a^{-1},0)\longmapsto(0,a).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The arrows are \eqref{eq:axis-arrows}.  An element of $\F_q^*$ is fixed by
inversion exactly when $a^2=1$.  Since the characteristic is odd, these are
the two distinct elements $1$ and $-1$, and they give \eqref{eq:two-cycles}.
Inversion acts without fixed points on the remaining $q-3$ elements, giving
$(q-3)/2$ unordered pairs and the cycles \eqref{eq:four-cycle}.  Distinct
inversion pairs give disjoint cycles.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Cycle census]\label{thm:cycles}
The cycle counts in \cref{thm:main}\textup{(ii)} are complete.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The exceptional layers contain no recurrent point by \cref{thm:tails}.
The axis contribution is completely classified by \cref{lem:axis-cycles},
including the fixed point $(0,0)$.  On $\G$, \cref{lem:five} shows that every
least period divides the prime $5$.  By \cref{lem:fixed}, exactly $r_q$
generic points have period one; all other
$(q-2)(q-3)-r_q$ generic points therefore have exact period five.  Dividing
these points into their disjoint five-element $\Lyn$-orbits simultaneously
proves the divisibility of $(q-2)(q-3)-r_q$ by five and gives their cycle
count.  These cases exhaust the disjoint partition from
\cref{prop:partition}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[small-field boundaries]\label{rem:small-fields}
For $q=3$, the generic locus is empty, so the 4- and 5-cycle families are
empty, while $|\E_3|=1$ still attains tail three.  In characteristic five,
the discriminant of $a^2-a-1$ is zero, so the polynomial has one double root
and $r_q=1$ over every finite extension; at $q=5$ this yields one generic
5-cycle.  These degeneracies are already included in the census.
\end{remark}

For a finite self-map, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeta-definition}
 \zeta_{\Lyn}(z)=
 \exp\left(\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{|\Fix(\Lyn^n)|}{n}z^n\right).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, a cycle of length $d$ contributes $(1-z^d)^{-1}$.  Substituting
the four counts from \cref{thm:cycles} gives \eqref{eq:zeta}.  This last Euler
product conversion is standard finite-map bookkeeping and receives zero
contribution credit.

\section{Every-target fibres and the complete singular in-tree}

The inverse problem is simpler than the forward stratification because a
target's first coordinate fixes one source coordinate.

\begin{theorem}[Every-target inverse law]\label{thm:fibres}
Equation \eqref{eq:fibre-law} holds.  Consequently
$|\im\Lyn|=q^2-q+1$, and $(-1,0)$ is the unique target of maximum fibre.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $\Lyn(x,y)=(u,v)$, the first coordinate forces $y=u$, and the remaining
equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse-equation}
 (1+u)\invz(x)=v.
\end{equation}
If $u=-1$, its left side is zero for every $x\in\F_q$.  This gives all $q$
sources $(x,-1)$ when $v=0$ and no source when $v\ne0$.

Suppose $u\ne-1$.  If $v=0$, then \eqref{eq:inverse-equation} forces
$\invz(x)=0$, hence $x=0$, giving the unique source $(0,u)$.  If $v\ne0$,
the unique solution is the nonzero element $x=(1+u)/v$.  These cases prove
\eqref{eq:fibre-law}.

There are $q(q-1)$ targets with $u\ne-1$, all in the image, and the only
image target with $u=-1$ is $(-1,0)$.  Thus the image size is
$q(q-1)+1=q^2-q+1$.  Since $q\geq3$, the fibre of size $q$ is uniquely
maximal.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Whole exceptional component]\label{cor:tree}
The connected functional-graph component containing
$(-1,0)\leftrightarrow(0,-1)$ consists exactly of that two-cycle, the leaf
\begin{equation}\label{eq:single-leaf}
 (-1,-1)\longmapsto(-1,0),
\end{equation}
and, for each $a\in\F_q\setminus\{0,-1\}$, the length-three chain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:all-chains}
 (-1,-1-a)\longmapsto(-1-a,a)\longmapsto(a,-1)
 \longmapsto(-1,0).
\end{equation}
There are no further vertices or incoming branches in this component.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The displayed arrows are \cref{lem:arrows}.  It remains to prove
completeness.  The fibre of $(-1,0)$ is exactly
$\{(x,-1):x\in\F_q\}$.  Among these sources, $(0,-1)$ is the cycle
predecessor, $(-1,-1)$ is \eqref{eq:single-leaf}, and the other $q-2$ points
are the endpoints $(a,-1)$ in \eqref{eq:all-chains}.

For $a\notin\{0,-1\}$, the target $(a,-1)$ has the unique predecessor
$(-1-a,a)$, and that target has the unique predecessor $(-1,-1-a)$, by the
unique-source case of \cref{thm:fibres}.  Finally,
$(-1,-1-a)$ has first coordinate $-1$ and nonzero second coordinate, so its
fibre is empty.  The point $(-1,-1)$ has empty fibre for the same reason.
The target $(0,-1)$ has the unique predecessor $(-1,0)$.  Thus no additional
branch can enter any displayed vertex.
\end{proof}
