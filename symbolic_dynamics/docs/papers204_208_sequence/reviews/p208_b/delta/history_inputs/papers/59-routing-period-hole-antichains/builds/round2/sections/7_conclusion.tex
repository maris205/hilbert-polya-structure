\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A finite clopen irreducible decomposition converts the Myhill property into
an exact route exclusion problem.  Compact separation makes preinjectivity
componentwise, clopen pasting realizes every off-diagonal obstruction as a
nonsurjective endomorphism, and irreducibility assigns one target to each
source piece.  For a positive-entropy mixing SFT with periodic-orbit
components, those routes reduce to fixed-period holes and divisibility, so
finite antichains parametrize precisely the Myhill extensions in this class.
The two-loop construction supplies such extensions with every finite number
of components greater than one.

The result remains a deliberately bounded theorem package.  General
reducible SFTs can have configurations outside their irreducible components,
and the elementary form of the routing argument carries substantial folklore
risk.  A broader theorem would need to account for heteroclinic classes and
the action of endomorphisms on them.  Within the finite-clopen setting, the
proofs and exact arithmetic give a complete decision rule and an explicit
unbounded family.
