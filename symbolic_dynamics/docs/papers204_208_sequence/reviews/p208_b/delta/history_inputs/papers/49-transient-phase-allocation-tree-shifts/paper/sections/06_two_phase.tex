\section{Two phases: parity and strict gain}
\label{sec:two-phase}

For two phases the max--min can be solved in closed form.  The result makes
the integer obstruction visible without Fourier notation.

\begin{theorem}[Closed two-phase formulas]
\label{thm:two-phase}
Let $p=2$ and put
\[
 \mu=\frac{c_0+c_1}{2},
 \qquad
 \delta=|c_1-c_0|.
\]
Then the complete cyclic core has
\begin{equation}
 \dimH T_{C(a)}
 =\mu-\frac{d-1}{2(d+1)}\delta.
 \label{eq:p2-core}
\end{equation}
For the one-level composition $m=(k,d-k)$,
\begin{equation}
 D_1((k,d-k);c)
 =\mu-\frac{(d-1)|2k-d|}{2d(d+1)}\delta.
 \label{eq:p2-composition}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \dimH T_{M_1(a)}=
 \begin{cases}
 \mu, & d\text{ even},\\[1mm]
 \displaystyle \mu-\frac{d-1}{2d(d+1)}\delta,
     & d\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:p2-optimizer}
\end{equation}
If $\delta>0$, the feeder strictly improves on the core for every $d\geq2$
and saturates the spectral mean exactly when $d$ is even.  If $\delta=0$,
every composition saturates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $p=2$, \eqref{eq:H-definition} becomes
\[
 H_0(c)=\frac{dc_0+c_1}{d+1},
 \qquad
 H_1(c)=\frac{dc_1+c_0}{d+1}.
\]
Their mean is $\mu$ and their absolute difference is
$(d-1)\delta/(d+1)$.  The smaller is therefore
\eqref{eq:p2-core}.

For $m=(k,d-k)$, the vector in \eqref{eq:b-definition} has mean $\mu$ and
\[
 b_0-b_1=\frac{2k-d}{d}(c_0-c_1).
\]
Applying the preceding two-entry calculation to $b$ proves
\eqref{eq:p2-composition}.  The minimum of $|2k-d|$ over integers
$0\leq k\leq d$ is zero for even $d$ and one for odd $d$, which gives
\eqref{eq:p2-optimizer}.  If $\delta>0$, the optimized feeder penalty is
strictly smaller than the core penalty; it vanishes exactly in the even case.
If $\delta=0$, every displayed penalty vanishes, independently of parity.
\end{proof}

For a nonconstant two-phase profile, the strict gain occurs even at odd
arity, where exact saturation is arithmetically unavailable.  The feeder can
balance the two phase weights more closely than any single core root, and the
remaining imbalance is only one child out of $d$.
