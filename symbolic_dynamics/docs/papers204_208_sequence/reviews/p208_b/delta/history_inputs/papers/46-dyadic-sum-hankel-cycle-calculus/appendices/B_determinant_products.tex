\section{Direct sums of Hilbert--Carleman determinants}
\label{app:determinants}

We prove the specialized direct-sum statement used in
\cref{prop:det2-product}.  The proof is included so that the block product
does not depend on a brittle page citation.

\begin{lemma}[Square-summable direct-sum product]
\label{lem:abstract-det2-sum}
Let $T_k$ be compact Hilbert--Schmidt operators, and suppose
\[
  \sum_{k\ge0}\norm{T_k}_2^2<\infty.
\]
Then $T=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}T_k$ is Hilbert--Schmidt and
\begin{equation}
  \det_2(I-zT)=\prod_{k\ge0}\det_2(I-zT_k),
  \label{eq:abstract-det2-product}
\end{equation}
with uniform convergence on compact subsets of $\C$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
List the nonzero eigenvalues of $T_k$, with algebraic multiplicity, as
$\lambda_{k,j}$.  Weyl's eigenvalue inequality gives
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{k,j}\abs{\lambda_{k,j}}^2
  \le\sum_k\norm{T_k}_2^2<\infty.
  \label{eq:combined-eigenvalue-l2}
\end{equation}
Also $\norm{T_k}\le\norm{T_k}_2\to0$.  Hence the nonzero eigenvalues of the
compact direct sum $T$ are exactly the combined multiset
$\{\lambda_{k,j}\}_{k,j}$.

Fix a compact set $K\subset\C$ and put $R=\max_{z\in K}\abs z$.  By
\cref{eq:combined-eigenvalue-l2}, only finitely many combined eigenvalues
satisfy $R\abs{\lambda_{k,j}}>1/2$.  For every remaining factor, use the
analytic branch at zero:
\begin{equation}
  \log E_2(w)=\log(1-w)+w
  =-\sum_{r\ge2}\frac{w^r}{r},
  \qquad \abs w\le\frac12.
  \label{eq:E2-log-bound}
\end{equation}
The series gives $\abs{\log E_2(w)}\le\abs w^2$ on this disk.  Therefore
\[
  \sum_{k,j}\sup_{z\in K}
  \abs{\log E_2(z\lambda_{k,j})}
  \le R^2\sum_{k,j}\abs{\lambda_{k,j}}^2<\infty
\]
after removing the finite exceptional set.  The combined canonical product
thus converges normally on $K$.  Normal convergence makes its value
independent of the ordering and permits grouping first over $j$ and then
over $k$.  The ungrouped product is the eigenvalue definition of
$\det_2(I-zT)$; each grouped product is $\det_2(I-zT_k)$.  This proves
\cref{eq:abstract-det2-product}, including compact-uniform convergence.
Zeros among the finitely many exceptional factors cause no difficulty,
because the identity follows between the resulting entire products.
\end{proof}

Apply the lemma with $T_k=2^{-ks}A_s$.  In the domain $\sigma>1/2$,
$A_s\in\Schatten{2}$ and
\[
  \sum_{k\ge0}\norm{T_k}_2^2
  =\norm{A_s}_2^2\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-2k\sigma}<\infty.
\]
The unitary equivalence in \cref{thm:direct-sum} identifies
$T=\bigoplus_kT_k$ with $H_s$, proving \cref{eq:det2-product}.

For completeness, if $\abs z\norm{H_s}<1$, then $I-zH_s$ is invertible by
the Neumann series, so the determinant has no zero on that disk.  The branch
of its logarithm fixed at zero satisfies
\[
  \log\det_2(I-zH_s)
  =-\sum_{r\ge2}\frac{z^r}{r}\Tr(H_s^r).
\]
Indeed,
\[
  \abs{\Tr(H_s^r)}
  \le\norm{H_s^r}_1
  \le\norm{H_s}_2^2\norm{H_s}^{r-2},
\]
so the series converges absolutely under the displayed condition.  Inserting
\cref{eq:trace-factor} gives \cref{eq:local-log-det2}.  This derivation also
shows why the local logarithm cannot be promoted to a single global branch
when the entire determinant has zeros.

If $\sigma>1$, then $A_s\in\Schatten{1}$ and
\[
  \sum_{k\ge0}\norm{2^{-ks}A_s}_1
  =\norm{A_s}_1\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k\sigma}<\infty.
\]
The genus-zero eigenvalue products consequently converge normally on compact
$z$-sets and give \cref{eq:det-product}.  Finally, the defining relation
$\det_2(I+T)=\det(I+T)e^{-\Tr T}$ for trace-class $T$, applied to
$T=-zH_s$ and combined with \cref{eq:ordinary-trace}, gives
\cref{eq:det-overlap}.
