\section{Pure-word vertical sterility}
\label{sec:rigidity}

Independent positive alphabets ensure that no word containing a cross edge
has identity monodromy.  Indeed, its projection to at least one free factor
is a nonempty positive word.  Thus the only $\Phi_2$-visible closed words of
$\cC_s$ are alternating pure loops at one atom.

\begin{theorem}[All-order reflected sterility]
\label{thm:sterility}
For every $r\ge1$ in the common trace domain,
\[
 \Phi_2(\cC_s^{2r})=2\sum_pp^{-r},
 \qquad
 \Phi_2(\cC_s^{2r+1})=0.                    \tag{4.1}
\]
Hence every regularized trace-series determinant of $\cC_s$ is independent
of $s$.  Formally before regularization,
\[
 \det_\Phi(I-z\cC_s)=\prod_p(1-z^2/p).       \tag{4.2}
\]
The product diverges at quadratic order; on the $L^3$ strip,
\[
 \det{}_3(I-z\cC_s)
 =\prod_p(1-z^2/p)e^{z^2/p},                 \tag{4.3}
\]
which is convergent, reflection symmetric, and vertically constant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Odd powers are off-diagonal.  In an even closed word the layer grammar
alternates exactly $r$ plus and $r$ minus steps.  Canonical trace kills every
cross-edge word.  At a pure loop $p$, the weight is
\[
 (p^{-s}p^{-(1-s)})^r=p^{-r}.
\]
There are two starting layers, proving \cref{eq:sterile-traces}:
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_2(\cC_s^{2r})=2\sum_pp^{-r}.
 \label{eq:sterile-traces}
\end{equation}
The logarithmic determinant expansion gives the products.  The $r=1$ prime
harmonic term diverges and is precisely the quadratic subtraction in
$\det_3$; all retained traces begin at $r=2$ and remain independent of $s$.
\end{proof}

The construction therefore avoids Paper08's positive $gg^{-1}$ norm square,
but reaches the same regularized product through a different obstruction:
reflection balance makes every pure two-step mass $p^{-1}$.

\subsection{Finite-channel rigidity}

We now isolate what two layers are doing.  Consider a finite directed channel
graph $H$ with an involution $\iota$ implementing reflection.  Each edge
$e$ carries an atom $p(e)$, a type $\epsilon(e)\in\{0,1\}$, and monomial
weight
\[
 w_e(s)=c_e p(e)^{-[(1-\epsilon(e))s+\epsilon(e)(1-s)]}, \tag{4.4}
\]
where $c_e$ is independent of $s$.  A closed word $W$ has plus count $a_W$,
minus count $b_W$, and weight phase on $s=1/2+it$
\[
 \exp\left[-it\left(\sum_{e:\epsilon=0}\log p(e)
 -\sum_{e:\epsilon=1}\log p(e)\right)\right]. \tag{4.5}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Finite-channel reflection-balance rigidity]
\label{thm:finite-channel}
Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item every identity-visible closed word is pure in its atom label;
 \item the channel grammar is bipartite under reflection, so every closed
       word has $a_W=b_W$;
 \item coefficients and cocycle labels are independent of $s$.
\end{enumerate}
Then every identity-visible weight is independent of $t$ on the critical
line.  Finite direct sums, constant signed phases, and virtual channel
differences preserve this conclusion.  Conversely, a height-dependent
identity-visible word must violate purity or reflection balance.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a pure word at $p$, \cref{eq:channel-phase} becomes
\begin{equation}
 e^{-it(a_W-b_W)\log p}=1.
 \label{eq:channel-phase}
\end{equation}
The remaining real weight is $p^{-(a_W+b_W)/2}$ times the constant product
of $c_e$.  Taking finite sums, supertraces, or multiplying constant phases
cannot reintroduce $t$.  The contrapositive gives the final assertion.
\end{proof}

This theorem is scoped to finite-channel monomial transfers with a bipartite
reflection grammar.  Infinite memory, nonmonomial potentials, or moving
channel number are not ruled out.
