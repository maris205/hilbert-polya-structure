\section{Endpoint estimates and the trace-dual matching}
\label{app:endpoints}

This appendix records the quantitative bounds used in
\cref{sec:bounded,sec:ideals}.  They are included to make the endpoint
strictness independent of any finite-cutoff trend.

\subsection{Anti-diagonal levels}

For $N\ge2$ and $\alpha>0$, put
\[
  S_\alpha(N)=\sum_{m=1}^{N-1}[m(N-m)]^{-\alpha}.
\]
By symmetry,
\[
  S_\alpha(N)
  =2\sum_{1\le m<N/2}[m(N-m)]^{-\alpha}
   +\one_{\{N\ {\rm even}\}}(N^2/4)^{-\alpha}.
\]
On $m\le N/2$,
\[
  N^{-\alpha}m^{-\alpha}
  \le [m(N-m)]^{-\alpha}
  \le 2^\alpha N^{-\alpha}m^{-\alpha}.
\]
For $0<\alpha<1$, the integral comparison
\[
  c_\alpha M^{1-\alpha}
  \le\sum_{m\le M}m^{-\alpha}
  \le C_\alpha M^{1-\alpha}
\]
therefore gives
\[
  c_\alpha N^{1-2\alpha}
  \le S_\alpha(N)
  \le C_\alpha N^{1-2\alpha}.
\]
At $\alpha=1$, summing the partial-fraction identity in
\cref{lem:level-estimates} gives
$S_1(N)=2H_{N-1}/N$.  For $\alpha>1$, the same half-interval estimate and
$\sum_{m\ge1}m^{-\alpha}<\infty$ give
$S_\alpha(N)\le C_\alpha N^{-\alpha}$.

At the Hilbert--Schmidt boundary $\sigma=1/2$, one can see divergence
without invoking the full comparison.  For
$N/4\le m\le3N/4$, both factors are at most $3N/4$, so each of the
$\gg N$ central summands in \cref{eq:hs-levels} is $\gg N^{-1}$.
Every dyadic level therefore has mass bounded below by a positive constant.

\subsection{Compactness from row decay}

The row-decay estimate in \cref{eq:row-decay} gives both
$\sup_mR_m<\infty$ and $R_m\to0$.  The second assertion alone does not
immediately make an arbitrary bounded matrix compact, so we record the
finite-row tail step.  For fixed $M$, absolute summability of each of the
rows $1,\ldots,M$ permits one common $N$ for their tails.  Rows above $M$
are controlled in their entirety by $R_m$.  Thus the modulus of
$H_s-P_NH_sP_N$ has uniformly small row and column sums.  This is the
precise norm-approximation argument used in \cref{thm:bounded}.

\subsection{Disjoint matching details}

For $Q_j=4^j$, the sets in \cref{eq:matching-sets} satisfy
\[
 I_j\subset[Q_j/4,Q_j/3],
 \qquad
 J_j\subset[2Q_j/3,3Q_j/4].
\]
They are disjoint within a scale.  Their union lies below $3Q_j/4$, whereas
the next interval $I_{j+1}$ begins at $Q_{j+1}/4=Q_j$; consequently all
domains and ranges used by the matchings are pairwise disjoint across
scales.

If $m\in I_j$ and $n\in J_j$, then
$11Q_j/12\le m+n\le13Q_j/12$.  The only power of two in that interval is
$Q_j$, so the selected support entry is present exactly when
$n=Q_j-m$.  Define the matrix of $V_J$ on these entries by
\[
  (V_J)_{m,Q_j-m}
  =\frac{h_s(m,Q_j-m)}{\abs{h_s(m,Q_j-m)}}.
\]
Disjointness makes $V_J$ a partial isometry of norm one.  With the matrix
convention $T_{m,n}=\ip{Te_n}{e_m}$,
\[
  \Tr(V_J^*H_s)
  =\sum_{j\le J}\sum_{m\in I_j}
    \overline{(V_J)_{m,Q_j-m}}\,h_s(m,Q_j-m),
\]
which is the positive sum in \cref{eq:trace-dual-pairing}.  Moreover,
$\abs{I_j}\asymp Q_j$ and
$m,Q_j-m\asymp Q_j$ uniformly on $I_j$, so
\[
  \sum_{m\in I_j}[m(Q_j-m)]^{-\sigma/2}
  \asymp_\sigma Q_j^{1-\sigma}.
\]
For $\sigma=1$ each scale contributes a positive constant; for
$\sigma<1$ its contribution grows.  Trace duality would require all finite
partial sums to be at most $\norm{H_s}_1$, completing the endpoint
contradiction without estimating finite-cutoff trace norms.
