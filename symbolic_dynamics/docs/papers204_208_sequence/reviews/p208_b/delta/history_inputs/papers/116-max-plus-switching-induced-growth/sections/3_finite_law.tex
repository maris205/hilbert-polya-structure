\section{Literal projective dynamics and the finite-time law}
\label{sec:finite-law}

Set $\zerovec=(0,0)^{\mathsf T}$ and write
\[
 M_n\trop\zerovec=(u_n,v_n)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 \Delta_n=u_n-v_n.
\]
Because the two coordinates are the row maxima of $M_n$,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:height-vector}
 H_n=\max\{u_n,v_n\}.
\end{equation}
To update the projective state, normalize a vector to $(d,0)^{\mathsf T}$.
Literal max-plus multiplication gives
\begin{align}
 A\trop(d,0)^{\mathsf T}
 &=\bigl(\max\{d-2,-1\},\max\{d+1,-1\}\bigr)^{\mathsf T},
 \label{eq:A-gap-action}\\
 B\trop(d,0)^{\mathsf T}
 &=\bigl(\max\{d-1,1\},\max\{d-1,-2\}\bigr)^{\mathsf T}.
 \label{eq:B-gap-action}
\end{align}
The reward is the change in the coordinate maximum.

\begin{theorem}[Five literal gaps and strong reward lumping]
\label{thm:lumping}
Starting from $\Delta_0=0$, the literal gap belongs to
\[
 \mathcal D=\{-3,-2,0,2,3\}
\]
at every time.  Its next gap and height reward are as follows.
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.12}
\begin{tabular}{c@{\qquad}cc}
\toprule
current gap $d$ & choose $A$: $(d',H'-H)$ & choose $B$: $(d',H'-H)$\\
\midrule
$-3$ & $(0,-1)$ & $(3,+1)$\\
$-2$ & $(0,-1)$ & $(3,+1)$\\
$0$  & $(-2,+1)$ & $(2,+1)$\\
$2$  & $(-3,+1)$ & $(0,-1)$\\
$3$  & $(-3,+1)$ & $(0,-1)$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Group the negative gaps as $N$, zero as $Z$, and the positive gaps as $P$.
Then this partition is strongly lumpable jointly with the reward, and the
lumped table is
\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.12}
\begin{tabular}{c@{\qquad}cc}
\toprule
current state & choose $A$ & choose $B$\\
\midrule
$N$ & $(Z,-1)$ & $(P,+1)$\\
$Z$ & $(N,+1)$ & $(P,+1)$\\
$P$ & $(N,+1)$ & $(Z,-1)$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The accumulated reward equals the literal matrix height $H_n$ for every
chronological word.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Tropical literal/projective route]
Evaluate \eqref{eq:A-gap-action}--\eqref{eq:B-gap-action} at the five values
in $\mathcal D$ and subtract the old maximum $\max\{d,0\}$.  This gives the
first table.  Every listed next gap again lies in $\mathcal D$, so induction
proves confinement.  All five values really occur: the empty word has gap
$0$, the one-letter words $A,B$ have gaps $-2,2$, and the words $AB,BA$
have gaps $3,-3$, respectively.

The two representatives of $N$ have the same target lump and reward under
each generator; the same is true for the two representatives of $P$.
The singleton $Z$ causes no ambiguity.  This proves strong reward lumping
and the second table.  Finally, \eqref{eq:height-vector} identifies each
literal maximum increment with the table reward.  Since $H_0=0$, summing the
rewards gives $H_n$.
\end{proof}

The orientation is fixed before this reduction.  For example, the word
$A,A,B$ means $B\trop A\trop A$ and gives
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\-1&-2\end{pmatrix},
\]
whereas the reversed word gives a different matrix.  Thus the construction
does not silently commute the generators.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite-time transform and cubic]
\label{thm:finite-pgf}
Let $0\leq p\leq1$, $q=1-p$, and $y>0$.  With state order $(N,Z,P)$, define
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:Q}
 Q_p(y)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&p/y&qy\\
 py&0&qy\\
 py&q/y&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Then, for every $n\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:finite-pgf}
 \boxed{\quad
 \Exp[y^{H_n}]=\eZ^{\mathsf T}Q_p(y)^n\one.
 \quad}
\end{equation}
Writing $a=pq$, one also has
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:cubic}
 \boxed{\quad
 \det(rI-Q_p(y))
 =r^3+(2a-1-ay^2)r-ay.
 \quad}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Markov-additive transfer route]
For a transition of probability $w$ and reward $g$, put $wy^g$ in its
matrix entry.  The reward table in \cref{thm:lumping} gives exactly
\eqref{eq:Q}.  If the row vector $m_n$ records
$\Exp[y^{H_n}\mathbf 1_{\{S_n=s\}}]$, then
$m_0=\eZ^{\mathsf T}$ and $m_{n+1}=m_nQ_p(y)$.  Summing over the terminal
state proves \eqref{eq:finite-pgf}, including $n=0$.

For the determinant, the three two-cycles contribute
$p^2$, $q^2$, and $pqy^2$ to the coefficient of $-r$.  The two oriented
three-cycles have total weight $p^2qy+pq^2y=pqy$.  Hence
\[
 \det(rI-Q_p(y))
 =r^3-(p^2+q^2+pqy^2)r-pqy.
\]
Using $p^2+q^2=1-2pq$ gives \eqref{eq:cubic}.
\end{proof}

For $0<p<1$, $Q_p(y)$ is primitive for every $y>0$: its directed graph has
positive two-cycles and three-cycles.  This observation supplies all
finite-dimensional spectral hypotheses used below.
