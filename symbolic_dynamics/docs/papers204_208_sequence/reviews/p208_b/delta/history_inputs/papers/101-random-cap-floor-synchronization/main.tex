\begin{abstract}
Independently at each time, choose a threshold $U\in[0,1]$ from an
atomless law and apply either the floor map $x\mapsto\max\{x,U\}$ with
probability $p$ or the cap map $x\mapsto\min\{x,U\}$ with probability
$1-p$.  We give an exact finite-word normal form: every composition is
either an interval clamp or a constant, and it becomes constant precisely
when the largest floor threshold crosses the smallest cap threshold.  If
$T$ is the first constant time, then for every atomless threshold law
\[
 \Prob(T>n)=\sum_{j=0}^n p^j(1-p)^{n-j}.
\]
For $0<p<1$, the probability generating function identifies $T$ with the
sum of independent geometric variables of parameters $p$ and $1-p$; in
particular its mean and variance are explicit.  For uniform thresholds,
the expected image diameter is exactly $\Prob(T>n)/(n+1)$.  This yields an
annealed exponential rate, a critical cancellation at $p=1/2$, and
polynomial endpoint decay at $p\in\{0,1\}$.  Almost every mixed path instead
has diameter exactly zero after a finite time, so its logarithmic behavior
is finite absorption rather than a conventional finite Lyapunov exponent.
The note is owner-subtracted against general theories of iterated random
functions, monotone synchronization, and finite-chain contraction
semigroups, and makes no absolute novelty or priority claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let a random map of the unit interval either push every point up to a fresh
threshold or pull every point down to it.  Each generator is monotone and
idempotent, but alternating the two types creates an irreversible event:
once a lower threshold reaches an upper threshold, the entire interval has
coalesced to a single point.  The event is governed by ranks rather than by
the numerical threshold distribution.

General iterated-random-function methods, including contractive-on-average
criteria and invariant-law constructions, have a broad established theory
\cite{DiaconisFreedman1999}.  Synchronization for iid monotone random maps
on Euclidean spaces is also treated in a substantially more general setting
\cite{MatiasSilva2021}.  On a finite chain, semigroups generated by
contraction mappings have their own structural literature
\cite{Umar2021}.  Those frameworks, and the general phenomenon of monotone
random synchronization, are not claims of this note.

The residual calculation here is deliberately narrow and exact.  We
combine a finite-word clamp normal form with exchangeability of iid ranks.
This produces the complete survival law, a geometric-sum representation of
the synchronization time, its moments, and an exact uniform-threshold
diameter formula.  It also separates two meanings of contraction that can
otherwise be conflated: pathwise finite absorption and exponential decay of
a positive finite-time expectation.

External release is \textbf{HOLD}.  The proofs and exact controls support
the displayed conjunction for this particular cap--floor family, but no
claim is made that an equivalent specialization is absent from the
iterated-function, synchronization, order-statistic, or semigroup
literatures.

\section{Model and finite-word normal form}
\label{sec:normal}

Put $I=[0,1]$.  For $u\in I$, define the floor and cap maps
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generators}
 F_u(x)=\max\{x,u\},\qquad C_u(x)=\min\{x,u\}.
\end{equation}
For $0\leq a<b\leq1$, write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clamp}
 K_{a,b}(x)=\max\{a,\min\{x,b\}\},
\end{equation}
and let $\kappa_c$ denote the constant map with value $c$.

The order of composition matters.  Given generators $g_1,\ldots,g_n$, our
forward convention is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:composition}
 \Phi_0=\Id,\qquad
 \Phi_n=g_n\circ g_{n-1}\circ\cdots\circ g_1.
\end{equation}
Thus $g_1$ acts first and $g_n$ acts last.  Define the two running threshold
records
\begin{equation}\label{eq:records}
 A_n=\max\bigl(\{u_i:1\leq i\leq n,\ g_i=F_{u_i}\}\cup\{0\}\bigr),
 \qquad
 B_n=\min\bigl(\{u_i:1\leq i\leq n,\ g_i=C_{u_i}\}\cup\{1\}\bigr).
\end{equation}
For a concrete order check,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:order-sentinel}
 C_{1/4}\circ F_{3/4}=\kappa_{1/4},
 \qquad
 F_{3/4}\circ C_{1/4}=\kappa_{3/4}.
\end{equation}
Thus reversing the word can change the collapsed value even though both
words have the same two threshold records.

\begin{theorem}[Clamp-or-constant normal form]\label{thm:normal}
For every finite word in the generators \eqref{eq:generators}, exactly one
of the following alternatives holds.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $A_n<B_n$, then
      \[
        \Phi_n=K_{A_n,B_n},\qquad
        \Phi_n(I)=[A_n,B_n].
      \]
\item If $A_n\geq B_n$, then $\Phi_n$ is a constant map.
\end{enumerate}
The value of the constant in the second alternative generally depends on
chronological order, not only on $(A_n,B_n)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that the current map is $K_{a,b}$ with $a<b$.  Direct
calculation, respecting the left action in \eqref{eq:composition}, gives
\begin{align}
 F_u\circ K_{a,b}
   &=\begin{cases}K_{\max\{a,u\},b},&u<b,\\
                    \kappa_u,&u\geq b,
     \end{cases}                                      \label{eq:floor-update}\\
 C_u\circ K_{a,b}
   &=\begin{cases}K_{a,\min\{b,u\}},&u>a,\\
                    \kappa_u,&u\leq a.
     \end{cases}                                      \label{eq:cap-update}
\end{align}
Composing any generator with a constant map again gives a constant map,
although its value may change.

As long as no constant has appeared, \eqref{eq:floor-update} and
\eqref{eq:cap-update} show inductively that the current endpoints are
exactly $(A_k,B_k)$.  If $A_n<B_n$, then monotonicity of the records gives
$A_k\leq A_n<B_n\leq B_k$ for every $k\leq n$, so no earlier collapse was
possible and the first alternative follows.  If $A_n\geq B_n$, there is a
first $k$ at which the inequality fails; the crossing update is one of the
constant branches in \eqref{eq:floor-update}--\eqref{eq:cap-update}, and
all later maps remain constant.
\end{proof}

For the random model, let $(U_i)_{i\geq1}$ be iid with an atomless Borel
probability law $\nu$ on $I$.  Independently, let $(\varepsilon_i)$ be iid
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:type-law}
 \Prob(\varepsilon_i=F)=p,
 \qquad
 \Prob(\varepsilon_i=C)=q:=1-p,
\end{equation}
and put $g_i=F_{U_i}$ or $C_{U_i}$ according to $\varepsilon_i$.  Define
the synchronization time
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T}
 T=\inf\{n\geq1:\Phi_n(I)\text{ is a singleton}\},
\end{equation}
with $\inf\varnothing=\infty$.  By \cref{thm:normal}, $T$ is exactly the
first threshold-order crossing time $A_n\geq B_n$.  Atomlessness implies
that sampled thresholds are pairwise distinct almost surely, so equality
has probability zero in the stochastic statements below.

\section{Distribution-free synchronization law}
\label{sec:law}

Write $N_n=\#\{1\leq i\leq n:\varepsilon_i=F\}$.  Whenever
$\Prob(N_n=j)>0$, survival conditional on $N_n=j$ means that all $j$ floor
thresholds are smaller than all $n-j$ cap thresholds.  Exchangeability of
the distinct iid ranks makes this one favorable choice among $\binom nj$
choices of the floor ranks.  Here every $0\leq j\leq n$ is reachable when
$0<p<1$; at $p=0$ only $j=0$ is reachable, and at $p=1$ only $j=n$ is
reachable.

\begin{theorem}[Exact survival law and geometric decomposition]
\label{thm:law}
For every atomless threshold law $\nu$, every $0\leq p\leq1$, and every
$n\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:survival}
 s_n(p):=\Prob(T>n)=\sum_{j=0}^n p^j q^{n-j}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:survival-closed}
 s_n(p)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\frac{p^{n+1}-q^{n+1}}{p-q},&p\neq\tfrac12,\\[6pt]
 (n+1)2^{-n},&p=\tfrac12.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
If $0<p<1$, then $T<\infty$ almost surely and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gf}
 \sum_{n\geq0}s_n(p)z^n
   =\frac{1}{(1-pz)(1-qz)},\qquad
 \Exp z^T=\frac{pqz^2}{(1-pz)(1-qz)}
\end{equation}
for $|z|<1/\max\{p,q\}$.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geom}
 T\ \overset{d}{=}\ G_p+G_q,
\end{equation}
where $G_p,G_q$ are independent and
$\Prob(G_r=k)=r(1-r)^{k-1}$ for $k\geq1$.  In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
 \Exp T=\frac1{pq},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(T)=\frac{1-3pq}{p^2q^2}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for $m\geq2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pmf}
 \Prob(T=m)=pq\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}q^kp^{m-2-k};
\end{equation}
at $p=q=1/2$ this is $(m-1)2^{-m}$.
At $p=0$ or $p=1$, one instead has $T=\infty$ almost surely and
$s_n(p)=1$ for every $n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $j$ with $\Prob(N_n=j)>0$.  Conditional on $N_n=j$, all $\binom nj$
assignments of the floor labels to the ordered thresholds have equal
probability.  The event $A_n<B_n$ occurs only when the floors receive the
$j$ smallest ranks, and that assignment is unique.  Hence
\[
 \Prob(T>n\mid N_n=j)=\binom nj^{-1}.
\]
Multiplication by $\Prob(N_n=j)=\binom njp^jq^{n-j}$ and summation over
the reachable $j$ proves \eqref{eq:survival}; the omitted terms have zero
binomial mass.  The finite geometric sum gives
\eqref{eq:survival-closed}.

For $0<p<1$, \eqref{eq:survival-closed} tends to zero, proving almost-sure
finiteness.  Summing \eqref{eq:survival} first in $n$ and then in $j$
gives the first identity in \eqref{eq:gf}.  For a positive
integer-valued random variable,
\[
 \sum_{n\geq0}\Prob(T>n)z^n=\frac{1-\Exp z^T}{1-z};
\]
substitution yields the second identity.  This is the product of the pgfs
$pz/(1-qz)$ and $qz/(1-pz)$, proving \eqref{eq:geom}.  Adding the means and
variances of the two geometric variables gives
\[
 \Exp T=\frac1p+\frac1q=\frac1{pq},\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(T)=\frac q{p^2}+\frac p{q^2}
   =\frac{1-3pq}{p^2q^2}.
\]
Convolving the two displayed geometric masses gives \eqref{eq:pmf}.
At either endpoint all generators have the same type.  Atomlessness gives
$U_i\in(0,1)$ almost surely for each fixed $i$, so every finite pure cap or
pure floor composition has a nondegenerate interval image.  Thus $T$ is
infinite almost surely.
\end{proof}

The critical polynomial factor in \eqref{eq:survival-closed} is exact.  It
also marks the crossover between two off-critical geometric tails.

\begin{corollary}[Critical and off-critical tails]\label{cor:tails}
At $p=1/2$,
\[
 \Prob(T>n)=(n+1)2^{-n}.
\]
If $p\neq1/2$ and $r=\max\{p,q\}$, $a=\min\{p,q\}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:offcritical}
 \Prob(T>n)=\frac{r^{n+1}}{r-a}
 \left[1-\left(\frac ar\right)^{n+1}\right]
 \sim\frac{r^{n+1}}{|2p-1|}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\section{Uniform thresholds and image diameter}
\label{sec:diameter}

The preceding law is distribution-free, but metric image size remembers
the threshold distribution.  We now specialize only this section to
$\nu=\Leb|_{[0,1]}$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Dn}
 D_n=\Exp\bigl[\diameter\Phi_n(I)\bigr].
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Exact annealed diameter]\label{thm:diameter}
For uniform thresholds, every $n\geq0$, and every $j$ for which
$\Prob(N_n=j)>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-diameter}
 \Exp\bigl[\diameter\Phi_n(I)\mid N_n=j\bigr]
   =\frac{1}{(n+1)\binom nj},
 \qquad 0\leq j\leq n.
\end{equation}
For every $0\leq p\leq1$, including both endpoints, the unconditional
identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diameter-exact}
 \boxed{\quad D_n=\frac{s_n(p)}{n+1}.\quad}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diameter-special}
 D_n=
 \begin{cases}
 2^{-n},&p=\tfrac12,\\
 (n+1)^{-1},&p\in\{0,1\},
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Define the signed annealed logarithmic rate
$\lambda_{\mathrm a}(p)=\lim_{n\to\infty}n^{-1}\log D_n$ and the positive
decay exponent $\gamma_{\mathrm a}(p)=-\lambda_{\mathrm a}(p)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:annealed-rate}
 \lambda_{\mathrm a}(p)
 =\begin{cases}
   \log\max\{p,1-p\},&0<p<1,\\
   0,&p\in\{0,1\}.
  \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \gamma_{\mathrm a}(p)=-\lambda_{\mathrm a}(p).
\end{equation}
For $p\neq1/2$ in the open interval, with $r,a$ as in
\cref{cor:tails},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diameter-asymptotic}
 D_n\sim\frac{r^{n+1}}{(n+1)(r-a)}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $V_{(1)}<\cdots<V_{(n)}$ be the order statistics of the $n$ uniform
thresholds, with $V_{(0)}=0$ and $V_{(n+1)}=1$.  Fix a deterministic full
type word containing $j$ floors.  Over the random thresholds, survival has
probability $\binom nj^{-1}$.  On that event the image is exactly
$[V_{(j)},V_{(j+1)}]$; off that event its diameter is zero.  The rank labels
are independent of the ordered values.  Moreover, the $n+1$ uniform
spacings are exchangeable and sum to one, so each has mean $1/(n+1)$.
Every type word with $j$ floors therefore has the same expected diameter.
Whenever $\Prob(N_n=j)>0$, averaging over the reachable type words proves
\eqref{eq:conditional-diameter}.

If $0<p<1$, the law of total expectation and the binomial law of $N_n$ give
\eqref{eq:diameter-exact}.  The endpoints are checked without conditioning
on inaccessible counts.  For $n\geq1$, at $p=0$ the composition is the pure cap
$C_{\min_{1\leq i\leq n}U_i}$ and its image diameter has mean
$1/(n+1)$.  At $p=1$ the composition is the pure floor
$F_{\max_{1\leq i\leq n}U_i}$ and the complementary maximum has the same
mean.  The case $n=0$ is the identity map.  Since $s_n(0)=s_n(1)=1$, this
proves \eqref{eq:diameter-exact} at both endpoints.  The special cases now
follow from \eqref{eq:survival-closed}, and \eqref{eq:annealed-rate}--
\eqref{eq:diameter-asymptotic} follow from \cref{cor:tails}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Quenched absorption versus annealed decay]
\label{rem:quenched}
For every $0<p<1$, \cref{thm:law} says that almost every environment has
\[
 \diameter\Phi_n(I)=0\qquad\text{for all }n\geq T.
\]
Thus the sample path undergoes exact finite-time absorption.  If one
formally permits $\log0=-\infty$, its eventual logarithmic rate is
$-\infty$; this is not a conventional finite Lyapunov exponent.  By
contrast, $D_n>0$ at every finite $n$ and has the finite annealed rate
\eqref{eq:annealed-rate}.  At $p=0$ or $p=1$ under uniform thresholds, the
diameter is positive at every finite time almost surely but decreases to
zero, with mean $(n+1)^{-1}$.  Indeed, in the pure-cap case
$\Prob(\min_{i\leq n}U_i>\epsilon)=(1-\epsilon)^n$, and the pure-floor case
is symmetric.  These endpoint paths never synchronize in the finite-time
sense of \eqref{eq:T}.
\end{remark}

The critical identity $D_n=2^{-n}$ is worth isolating: the factor $n+1$ in
the survival probability is canceled exactly by the mean order-statistic
spacing.  Hence critical survival has a polynomial correction although the
mean image diameter does not.

and checks the crossing criterion.  A separately organized fixed-type
enumeration verifies the conditional rank probability and spacing factor.
Rational arithmetic checks the survival formulas, convolution law, moments,
critical and off-critical identities, and endpoint formulas.  The script is
a finite control, not a substitute for the proofs above; its frozen output

The owner boundary is equally important.  We do not claim ownership of

random maps \cite{MatiasSilva2021}, contraction semigroups on finite chains

paper's auditable residual contribution is the exact conjunction of
\cref{thm:normal,thm:law,thm:diameter} for the specified continuous-interval
cap--floor family.  Specialist comparison against the closest literature
is still required before any external novelty language.

\section{Conclusion}

Random cap and floor maps admit a complete finite normal form.  That form
turns synchronization into a rank-separation problem and yields a
distribution-free survival law.  For mixed types, the synchronization time
is a sum of two independent geometric variables; for uniform thresholds,
the surviving interval contributes one mean spacing and gives the exact
annealed diameter.  The resulting finite absorption, critical prefactor,
and endpoint behavior are elementary but sharply distinct.  External
circulation remains on hold pending a direct-owner literature review.
