\section{Analytic embedding and functional ownership}
\label{sec:mixed-functional}

The no-go does not invalidate every analytic object built from the failed
weight.  It determines what that object can and cannot mean.  For distinct
active atoms with inherited numerical roof marks \(p,q\), take
\begin{equation}
 G_{pq}=\frac{1}{(p^{2\eta}+1)(q^{2\eta}+1)},
 \qquad \eta>0.
 \label{eq:mixed-gram}
\end{equation}
For a bounded pair kernel \(|K_2(p,q)|\le1\), define
\begin{equation}
 \MK(s)=2\sum_{p<q}K_2(p,q)G_{pq}
 \left(p^{-s}q^{s-1}+q^{-s}p^{s-1}\right).
 \label{eq:mixed-functional}
\end{equation}
The Boolean embedding uses \(K_2=\KBool_2\).

\begin{proposition}[Normal convergence]
\label{prop:holomorphy}
The series \eqref{eq:mixed-functional} converges normally on compact subsets
of
\begin{equation}
 1-2\eta<\Ree s<2\eta
 \label{eq:holomorphy-strip}
\end{equation}
and is holomorphic there.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put \(\sigma=\Ree s\).  Equation~\eqref{eq:mixed-gram} gives
\(G_{pq}\le p^{-2\eta}q^{-2\eta}\), so the two absolute summands are bounded
by
\begin{equation}
 p^{-(2\eta+\sigma)}q^{-(2\eta+1-\sigma)},
 \qquad
 q^{-(2\eta+\sigma)}p^{-(2\eta+1-\sigma)}.
 \label{eq:product-majorant}
\end{equation}
Both one-variable exponents exceed one precisely in
\eqref{eq:holomorphy-strip}.  Replacing the atom sums by sums over all
integers gives a product of convergent zeta tails.  On a compact substrip the
exponents have a uniform margin above one.  The Weierstrass \(M\)-test gives
normal convergence, and termwise holomorphy proves the result.
\end{proof}

For the inherited value \(\eta=2\), the strip is
\(-3<\Ree s<4\), so the critical line lies in its interior.

\subsection{Exact marker ledger}

At \(s=\tfrac12+it\), the two monomials in
\eqref{eq:mixed-functional} are conjugates:
\begin{equation}
 p^{-s}q^{s-1}=\frac{e^{it\log(q/p)}}{\sqrt{pq}},
 \qquad
 q^{-s}p^{s-1}=\frac{e^{-it\log(q/p)}}{\sqrt{pq}}.
 \label{eq:conjugate-monomials}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
 \MK\!\left(\tfrac12+it\right)
 =4\sum_{p<q}\frac{K_2(p,q)G_{pq}}{\sqrt{pq}}
 \cos\!\left(t\log\frac qp\right).
 \label{eq:critical-mixed}
\end{equation}
The formal marker is
\(
q/p\leftrightarrow\log(q/p)
\), and its exact squared coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 \frac{16K_2(p,q)^2G_{pq}^2}{pq}.
 \label{eq:squared-marker}
\end{equation}
No cosine is sampled and no external ordinate is used.  For
\(K_2=\KBool_2\), all terms and markers transport to the formal UFD clone,
so marker ownership is rigorous while arithmetic selectivity is false.

\subsection{What object owns the series?}

Equation~\eqref{eq:mixed-functional} defines a \emph{new source-weighted
mixed functional}.  The convergence proof constructs a scalar holomorphic
series; it does not construct a trace-class operator whose ordinary trace is
that series.  It supplies no reference pair or trace-class relative
hypothesis for a relative determinant.

Nor is \(\MK\) a modified Fredholm determinant.  Regularized Fredholm
determinants have a specified operator and power-subtraction law
\citep{BritzEtAl2021}.  The inherited honest \(\detthree(I-z\cB_s)\) removes
the first two logarithmic powers, including the quadratic mixed power in
full.  It therefore cannot own a separately retained quadratic series.  A2
remains owned by that inherited \(\detthree\); \(\MK\) receives no new
determinant gate.

\subsection{A separate auxiliary determinant}

The exact suite does construct a different operator.  On the atom Hilbert
space, define the real zero-diagonal matrix
\begin{equation}
 H_{pq}=\chi_2(p,q)\frac{G_{pq}}{\sqrt{pq}}\quad(p\ne q),
 \qquad H_{pp}=0.
 \label{eq:auxiliary-H}
\end{equation}
For \(\eta=2\),
\(
 |H_{pq}|\le p^{-9/2}q^{-9/2}
\).  Hence \(\sum_{p,q}|H_{pq}|<\infty\).  The matrix-unit nuclear
decomposition makes \(H\) trace class, so
\begin{equation}
 D_H(z)=\det(I+zH)
 \label{eq:auxiliary-det}
\end{equation}
is an honest ordinary Fredholm determinant, entire in \(z\).  Its first two
nontrivial finite characteristic coefficients are
\begin{equation}
 [z^2]D_H(z)=-\sum_{p<q}H_{pq}^2,
 \qquad
 [z^3]D_H(z)=2\sum_{p<q<r}H_{pq}H_{qr}H_{pr}.
 \label{eq:auxiliary-coefficients}
\end{equation}

This is a genuine analytic gain with narrow ownership.  A phase-decorated
finite cutoff is diagonally conjugate to \(H\), so its characteristic cycles
cancel the \(t\)-phase.  The auxiliary determinant has no spectral motion,
and its complete coefficient ledger is cloned by the free/UFD controls.
Moreover, the separately filtered \(\Theta_3\) in \eqref{eq:theta3} uses the
stronger triple predicate rather than merely the three pair edges; it is not
in general the cubic coefficient in \eqref{eq:auxiliary-coefficients}.
Neither \(D_H\) nor \(\Theta_3\) is the original chiral transfer determinant.
