\begin{abstract}
Let $p$ be an odd prime and let $\Phi_p(A)=A+A$ on the nonempty
subsets of the additive group $\mathbb F_p$.  Although the phase space has
$2^p-1$ points, additive growth collapses its recurrent dynamics to a small
explicit core.  We prove that every nonsingleton other than $\mathbb F_p$
is transient, while the nonzero singletons form cycles of common length
$h=\operatorname{ord}_p(2)$.  Consequently
\[
 \#\operatorname{Fix}(\Phi_p^n)
 =2+(p-1)\mathbf 1_{h\mid n},
 \qquad
 \zeta_{\Phi_p}(z)
 =(1-z)^{-2}(1-z^h)^{-(p-1)/h}.
\]
The associated M\"obius census has only two fixed cycles and
$(p-1)/h$ cycles of length $h$.  On the layer of $m$-element subsets,
$2\leq m\leq p$, the exact worst time to reach $\mathbb F_p$ is
\[
 \left\lceil\log_2\frac{p-1}{m-1}\right\rceil;
\]
arithmetic progressions attain the bound.  The first deviation of the fixed
sequence from $2$ occurs at time $h$ and has height $p+1$, recovering both
$p$ and $h$.  Deterministic power-set enumeration and a separately constructed
iterated-sumset control verify the registered finite cases.  Classical
sumset growth, critical-pair rigidity, and iterated-sumset structure are
treated as inputs rather than residual claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Set addition can itself be iterated as a finite dynamical system.  Fix an
odd prime $p$, write $\Fp$ additively, and let
\[
 \calX_p=2^{\Fp}\setminus\{\varnothing\},
 \qquad
 \Phi_p:\calX_p\longrightarrow\calX_p,
 \qquad
 \Phi_p(A)=A+A.
\]
This is not the pointwise hyperspace lift of a self-map of $\Fp$: every
output element uses a pair of input elements, and the same binary operation
is applied again at the next time.  The iterates are therefore sumsets with
exponentially many summands.

The phase portrait separates into two regimes.  Cauchy--Davenport growth
forces every nonsingleton into the full group, whereas a singleton
$\{a\}$ follows the multiplicative orbit $a,2a,4a,\ldots$.  This separation
simultaneously determines recurrence, fixed counts, temporal cycles, zeta
data, and the exact transient depth on every cardinality layer.

The contribution is a closed finite-dynamics package for this particular
self-map:
\begin{enumerate}
\item We classify every recurrent point and cycle, then derive all fixed
  counts, the Artin--Mazur zeta function, and the least-period M\"obius
  census.
\item For each $2\leq m\leq p$, we prove the exact maximum absorption time
  among all $m$-element subsets.  A concrete arithmetic progression reaches
  the Cauchy--Davenport lower envelope at every pre-absorption time.
\item The first fixed-count anomaly recovers $p$ and
  $\ord_p(2)$.  The endpoint $m=p$, the excluded singleton layer, the empty
  set convention, and the exceptional prime $p=2$ are recorded separately.
\end{enumerate}

The central statement is given next.  Its proof occupies
\cref{sec:growth,sec:periodic,sec:depth}.

\section{Statement, notation, and owner boundary}

For a nonempty set $A\subseteq\Fp$ and an integer $q\geq1$, write
\[
 qA=\{a_1+\cdots+a_q:a_i\in A\}
\]
for the $q$-fold sumset.  This notation is deliberately different from the
scalar image $\{qa:a\in A\}$.  We use recurrence in the standard
topological sense: every neighborhood of the state is revisited at
arbitrarily large positive times.  Because $\calX_p$ is finite and
discrete, a state is recurrent exactly when $\Phi_p^n(A)=A$ for some
$n\geq1$, equivalently when it lies on a cycle of the functional graph.

For $\card A\geq2$, define the absorption time
\[
 \tau_p(A)=\min\{t\geq0:\Phi_p^t(A)=\Fp\}.
\]
For $2\leq m\leq p$, put
\[
 T_p(m)=\max\{\tau_p(A):A\subseteq\Fp,\ \card A=m\}.
\]
Finally, let $h=\ord_p(2)$, the multiplicative order of $2$ in
$\Fp^\times$.  Since $p$ is odd, $h$ is defined and $h\geq2$.
Lagrange's theorem also gives $h\mid p-1$.

\begin{theorem}[complete phase portrait and layer depth]\label{thm:main}
Let $p$ be an odd prime and $h=\ord_p(2)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $t\geq0$ and nonempty $A\subseteq\Fp$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:iterate}
   \Phi_p^t(A)=2^tA.
  \end{equation}
\item The recurrent states are precisely
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:recurrent-set}
   \{\Fp,\{0\}\}\cup\{\{a\}:a\in\Fp^\times\}.
  \end{equation}
  The first two states are fixed, and the nonzero singletons split into
  $(p-1)/h$ cycles of length $h$.
\item For every $n\geq1$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-count}
   F_p(n):=\card{\Fix(\Phi_p^n)}
   =2+(p-1)\mathbf 1_{h\mid n}.
  \end{equation}
  Consequently the Artin--Mazur zeta function is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:zeta}
   \zeta_{\Phi_p}^{\AM}(z)
   =(1-z)^{-2}(1-z^h)^{-(p-1)/h}.
  \end{equation}
  This is an identity of formal power series.
\item If $C_p(n)$ denotes the number of cycles of least temporal period
  $n$, then
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:cycle-census}
   C_p(n)=\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\mob\!\left(\frac nd\right)F_p(d)
   =\begin{cases}
     2,&n=1,\\
     (p-1)/h,&n=h,\\
     0,&\text{otherwise}.
   \end{cases}
  \end{equation}
\item On every nonsingleton cardinality layer,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:layer-depth}
   \boxed{\displaystyle
   T_p(m)=\left\lceil\log_2\frac{p-1}{m-1}\right\rceil,
   \qquad 2\leq m\leq p.}
  \end{equation}
  Every affine arithmetic progression
  $a+r\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}$ with $r\in\Fp^\times$ attains this value.
\item The first $n\geq1$ for which $F_p(n)\ne2$ is $n=h$, and
  $F_p(h)=p+1$.  Thus the fixed sequence recovers
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:recovery}
   h=\min\{n\geq1:F_p(n)\ne2\},
   \qquad p=F_p(h)-1.
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The growth input is the Cauchy--Davenport theorem
\cite{Davenport1935,TaoVu2006}: for nonempty $A,B\subseteq\Fp$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cd}
 \card{A+B}\geq\min\{p,\card A+\card B-1\}.
\end{equation}
We use it as a cited theorem, not as a result of this manuscript.  The
critical equality cases also have an established owner.  In the range
$\card A,\card B\geq2$ and
\[
 \card{A+B}=\card A+\card B-1\leq p-2,
\]
Vosper's theorem says that $A$ and $B$ are arithmetic progressions with a
common difference \cite{Vosper1956,TaoVu2006}.  We invoke only this safe
range in \cref{prop:vosper}; no endpoint extension is implicit.

The notation $qA$, its growth, and inverse questions for iterated sumsets
belong to additive combinatorics; modern structural work includes
\cite{Grynkiewicz2020}.  Artin--Mazur introduced the periodic-point zeta
ledger used in \eqref{eq:zeta} \cite{ArtinMazur1965}.  The residual package
here is limited to assembling these inputs into the complete phase portrait
of the specific self-map $A\mapsto A+A$, including its exact cardinality
layers and recovery statement.  A bounded search through 29 August 2026 did
not locate this exact combined dynamics package.  Search absence is not a
priority or novelty proof, and external release remains on hold.

\section{Iterated growth and recurrence}\label{sec:growth}

\begin{lemma}[iterates and the growth envelope]\label{lem:growth}
For every nonempty $A\subseteq\Fp$, every integer $q\geq1$, and every
$t\geq0$,
\begin{align}
 \Phi_p^t(A)&=2^tA,\label{eq:iterate-lemma}\\
 \card{qA}&\geq\min\{p,q(\card A-1)+1\}.\label{eq:q-growth}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The identity at $t=0$ is the convention $1A=A$.  If
$\Phi_p^t(A)=2^tA$, then
\[
 \Phi_p^{t+1}(A)=(2^tA)+(2^tA)=2^{t+1}A,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:iterate-lemma} by induction.

For \eqref{eq:q-growth}, the case $q=1$ is equality.  Apply
\eqref{eq:cd} to $qA$ and $A$.  Unless the full group has already been
reached, each added copy of $A$ increases the lower bound by
$\card A-1$.  Induction gives the displayed minimum.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[strict nonsingleton growth]\label{cor:strict}
If $2\leq\card A<p$, then either $A+A=\Fp$ or
$\card{A+A}>\card A$.  Every such $A$ reaches $\Fp$ in finite time.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:cd} with $B=A$ gives
$\card{A+A}\geq\min\{p,2\card A-1\}$.  If the minimum is not $p$, then
$2\card A-1>\card A$.  Finite absorption follows directly from
\eqref{eq:q-growth} with $q=2^t$.
\end{proof}

We can now prove the recurrent part of \cref{thm:main}.  The full group is
fixed.  A proper nonsingleton cannot return to itself because its
cardinality strictly increases until absorption.  For a singleton,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:singleton}
 \Phi_p^t(\{a\})=\{2^ta\}.
\end{equation}
The state $\{0\}$ is fixed.  If $a\ne0$, the least return time in
\eqref{eq:singleton} is the least $t\geq1$ with $2^t=1$ in
$\Fp^\times$, namely $h$.  All nonzero singletons therefore have period
$h$, and they form $(p-1)/h$ cycles.  This proves parts 1 and 2 of
\cref{thm:main}.

\section{Fixed data, zeta factors, and recovery}\label{sec:periodic}

The recurrent classification makes the entire temporal ledger finite.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:main}, parts 3, 4, and 6]
A state fixed by $\Phi_p^n$ is recurrent.  The two fixed states in
\eqref{eq:recurrent-set} contribute $2$.  Every nonzero singleton is fixed
exactly when $h\mid n$, contributing another $p-1$ in that case.  This
proves \eqref{eq:fixed-count}.

For a finite map with $c_r$ cycles of length $r$, the fixed sequence obeys
\[
 F_p(n)=\sum_{r\mid n}r c_r,
\]
and hence
\[
 \exp\!\left(\sum_{n\geq1}F_p(n)\frac{z^n}{n}\right)
 =\prod_{r\geq1}(1-z^r)^{-c_r}.
\]
Here $c_1=2$, $c_h=(p-1)/h$, and all other $c_r$ vanish, giving
\eqref{eq:zeta}.  M\"obius inversion of the same divisor sum gives the first
expression in \eqref{eq:cycle-census}; substitution of
\eqref{eq:fixed-count} gives its three cases.

Because $h\geq2$, the counts are $2$ through time $h-1$ and jump to $p+1$
at time $h$.  Formula \eqref{eq:recovery} follows.  Equivalently, the first
nontrivial zeta factor has degree $h$ and exponent $(p-1)/h$; the fixed
sequence formulation avoids any factorization convention.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact cardinality-layer absorption}\label{sec:depth}

The lower envelope in \eqref{eq:q-growth} determines an upper bound for
every orbit.  Arithmetic progressions show that the bound cannot be
improved on any layer.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:main}, part 5]
Fix $2\leq m\leq p$ and set
\[
 K=\left\lceil\log_2\frac{p-1}{m-1}\right\rceil.
\]
For every $m$-element set $A$, \cref{lem:growth} gives
\[
 \card{\Phi_p^K(A)}
 \geq\min\{p,2^K(m-1)+1\}=p.
\]
Thus $\Phi_p^K(A)=\Fp$ and $T_p(m)\leq K$.

For sharpness, take an affine arithmetic progression
\[
 A=a+r\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\},\qquad r\in\Fp^\times.
\]
Every integer between $0$ and $q(m-1)$ is a sum of $q$ integers from
$\{0,\ldots,m-1\}$.  Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ap-sumset}
 qA=qa+r\{0,1,\ldots,q(m-1)\}\pmod p.
\end{equation}
If $0\leq t<K$, the definition of $K$ gives
$2^t(m-1)<p-1$.  The residues displayed in \eqref{eq:ap-sumset} are then
distinct, so
\[
 \card{\Phi_p^t(A)}=2^t(m-1)+1<p.
\]
At $t=K$, we have $2^K(m-1)+1\geq p$, so the displayed integer interval
contains at least $p$ consecutive integers.  Their residues cover all of
$\Fp$; any further wraparound does not change that conclusion.  Thus
$\tau_p(A)=K$, proving \eqref{eq:layer-depth}.
\end{proof}

The progression construction proves existence of extremizers.  It does not
by itself classify every set of maximal absorption time.  The following
owner-subtracted statement records exactly the local rigidity that we use.

\begin{proposition}[Vosper range only]\label{prop:vosper}
Let $A\subseteq\Fp$ satisfy $\card A\geq2$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vosper-range}
 \card{A+A}=2\card A-1\leq p-2.
\end{equation}
Then $A$ is an arithmetic progression modulo $p$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply Vosper's theorem to the pair $(A,A)$.  The hypotheses in
\eqref{eq:vosper-range} are precisely the critical equality and endpoint
restriction in the cited form of that theorem \cite{Vosper1956}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[no global rigidity overclaim]\label{rem:no-overclaim}
An orbit can attain the maximal absorption time even when one intermediate
Cauchy--Davenport inequality has slack, provided the slack does not fill the
remaining distance to $p$.  Accordingly, \cref{prop:vosper} is a
classification of the sharp first doubling step in its valid range, not a
claim that every maximizer in \eqref{eq:layer-depth} is an arithmetic
progression.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact controls, endpoints, and authority}

The deterministic script \texttt{code/verify\_sumset\_squaring.py} registers
two complementary exact finite routes.  The first enumerates every nonempty subset
for $p=3,5,7,11,13$, constructs its literal functional orbit, and checks the
recurrent classification, every fixed count through time $2h$, and every
cardinality-layer maximum.  It examines 10,403 states in total.

The second route builds $2^tA$ by adding one copy of $A$ at a time rather
than applying $\Phi_p$ recursively.  It checks the iterate identity on 863
state--time cases, verifies 17,139 ordered Cauchy--Davenport pairs, tests the
safe Vosper equality range, and checks arithmetic-progression extremizers
for every layer of every odd prime through $43$.  M\"obius reconstruction
independently verifies that only periods $1$ and $h$ survive, while the
cycle-product factors reconstruct every registered logarithmic zeta
coefficient.  The final registered run contains 91,509 exact assertions and no floating-point or
randomized test.

The endpoints are material.
\begin{itemize}
\item The singleton layer is not an absorption layer: $\{0\}$ is fixed and
  the nonzero states have period $h$.
\item At $m=p$, the only state is already $\Fp$, and
  \eqref{eq:layer-depth} gives $T_p(p)=0$.
\item The empty set was excluded.  If adjoined, it is one additional fixed
  state and multiplies \eqref{eq:zeta} by $(1-z)^{-1}$.
\item At $p=2$, doubling is not a permutation on nonzero singletons:
  $\{1\}\mapsto\{0\}$.  Direct enumeration gives only the two fixed
  recurrent states $\{0\}$ and $\mathbb F_2$ and no first anomaly.  This is
  why \cref{thm:main} assumes an odd prime.
\end{itemize}
