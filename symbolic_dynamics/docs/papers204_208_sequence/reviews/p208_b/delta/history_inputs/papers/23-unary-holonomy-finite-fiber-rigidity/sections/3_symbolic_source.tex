\section{Frozen symbolic source and operator}
\label{sec:source}

\subsection{Full shifts and the successor--divisor graph}

Let \(F_n\) denote the full shift on an \(n\)-letter alphabet, up to
topological conjugacy.  We freeze
\[
 F_m\boxtimesf F_n\cong F_{mn},\qquad
 F_m\boxplusf F_n\cong F_{m+n},\qquad
 S(F_n)=F_n\boxplusf F_1\cong F_{n+1}.
\]
Here \(\boxplusf\) means alphabet disjoint union followed by the full-shift
functor; it is not asserted to be the categorical coproduct of subshifts.
The intrinsic entropy is \(h(F_n)=\log n\).

The vertex set and edges are
\[
 V=\{2,3,\ldots\},\qquad
 n\longrightarrow d
 \iff d\ge2,\ d\mid n+1.
\]
An edge is equivalently the factor witness
\[
        S(F_n)\cong F_d\boxtimesf F_q,
\qquad
        q=q(n,d)=\frac{n+1}{d}.
\]
The resulting one-sided countable Markov shift is
\[
 X_G^+
 =\{(n_0,n_1,\ldots)\in V^\N:n_j\to n_{j+1}\}.
\]
No prime predicate enters this definition.

\subsection{Canonical holonomy-two cycles}

We import the exact classification that the cofactor product \(Q=2\) occurs
precisely on
\[
        C_k=(k,k+1,\ldots,2k-1),\qquad k\ge2,
\]
up to cyclic rotation.  Each orbit is primitive and has a unique least
vertex.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical ordered word]
\label{thm:word}
Mark \(C_k\) at its unique least vertex.  Then
\[
        \word(C_k)=1^{k-1}2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first \(k-1\) edges are \(n\to n+1\), and
\[
        q(n,n+1)=1.
\]
The closing edge is \(2k-1\to k\), with
\[
        q(2k-1,k)=2.
\]
The unique least vertex fixes the displayed ordering.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Marked versus cyclic response]
The trace of a matrix product is invariant under cyclic rotation.  A
bilinear matrix coefficient is not.  We use the unique-minimum mark for
bilinear responses and never treat a marked coefficient as an unmarked
orbit invariant.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Roof and graph marker}

The graph-step marker is \(z\) per edge, and the frozen endpoint roof is
\[
        \tau(n,d)=\log(nd).
\]
Put
\[
        M_k=\prod_{n=k}^{2k-1}n
            =\frac{(2k-1)!}{(k-1)!}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Canonical monomial]
\label{prop:monomial}
The length, total roof, and scalar monomial of \(C_k\) are
\[
 |C_k|=k,\qquad
 R(C_k)=2\log M_k,\qquad
 z^{|C_k|}\e^{-sR(C_k)}=z^kM_k^{-2s}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every cycle vertex occurs once as a source and once as a target.  Therefore
\[
 R(C_k)
 =\sum_{(n,d)\in C_k}(\log n+\log d)
 =2\sum_{n=k}^{2k-1}\log n.
\]
The word in \cref{thm:word} has \(k\) letters.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Algebraic and analytic fibers}

For the algebraic recurrence theorem, \(\mathbb F\) may be any
characteristic-zero field, with fixed
\[
        A,B\in M_d(\mathbb F),\qquad u,v\in\mathbb F^d.
\]
We study
\[
 x_k=u^{\mathsf T}A^{k-1}Bv,\qquad
 y_k=\operatorname{tr}(A^{k-1}B).
\]

The analytic layer is more restrictive.  It uses
\[
        A,B\in M_d(\C),\qquad
        \cH=\ell^2(V)\otimes\C^d.
\]
On the quotient-\(\{1,2\}\) spine, define the column-source weighted vertex
adjacency
\[
\begin{aligned}
 L_{s,A,B}(e_n\otimes\xi)
 &=[n(n+1)]^{-s}e_{n+1}\otimes A\xi\\
 &\quad+\id_{\{n\ {\rm odd}\}}
 [n(n+1)/2]^{-s}e_{(n+1)/2}\otimes B\xi.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is not called a Ruelle operator.  On \(\Re s>1/2\), where
\cref{thm:traceclass} proves \(L_{s,A,B}\in\Sone\), we set
\[
        D_{A,B}(s,z)=\det_{\cH}(I-zL_{s,A,B}).
\]
The determinant is entire in \(z\).  Its trace logarithm is used only as the
normalized germ at \(z=0\); no global logarithm through determinant zeros is
assumed.

Starting at the least vertex, column composition around \(C_k\) is
\(BA^{k-1}\).  Cyclicity gives
\[
        \operatorname{tr}(BA^{k-1})
        =\operatorname{tr}(A^{k-1}B).
\]
This convention will be essential when the analytic and recurrence layers
meet in \cref{sec:fredholm}.
