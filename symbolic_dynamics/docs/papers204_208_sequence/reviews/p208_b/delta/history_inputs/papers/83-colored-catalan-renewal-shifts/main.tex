\begin{abstract}
For each integer $c\geq1$ we build an aperiodic countable-state loop shift
with exactly $cC_{n-1}$ first-return loops of length $n$, where $C_j$ is a
Catalan number.  Its first-return series is
\[
 F_c(z)=\frac c2\bigl(1-\sqrt{1-4z}\bigr).
\]
The single parameter $c$ produces an exact three-way transition.  The shift
is transient at $c=1$, null recurrent at $c=2$, and strongly positive
recurrent for $c\geq3$.  Its Gurevich entropy is
\[
 h_G(c)=\begin{cases}
 \log4,&c=1,2,\\
 \log\!\bigl(c^2/(c-1)\bigr),&c\geq3,
 \end{cases}
\]
and a measure of maximal entropy exists exactly when $c\geq3$.  In that
regime its normalized first-return law and mean return time are explicit.
The formal periodic-orbit zeta function is algebraic:
\[
 \zeta_c(z)=\frac{1}{1-F_c(z)}.
\]
At the two boundary parameters this yields the exact fixed-point laws
$\#\Fix(\sigma^n)=\frac12\binom{2n}{n}$ for $c=1$ and
$\#\Fix(\sigma^n)=4^n/2$ for $c=2$.  Thus the recurrence transition is also
visible as a change in the dominant zeta singularity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Countable Markov shifts can be transient, null recurrent, or positive
recurrent even when their Gurevich entropy is finite.  The classification by
first-return series, and its consequences for maximal-entropy measures, are
standard parts of the thermodynamic formalism of Gurevich, Savchenko, and
Sarig \cite{GurevichSavchenko1998,Sarig1999}.  Loop and circular-code
presentations also turn first-return generating functions into dynamical
zeta functions \cite{Keller1991}.  These general mechanisms are prior input,
not claims of this note.

Our purpose is to isolate a one-parameter laboratory in which every part of
the classification is elementary and closed form.  Catalan growth is exactly
critical for this task: the first-return series has radius $1/4$, a finite
boundary value, and an infinite boundary derivative.  Multiplying its
coefficients by $c$ therefore separates transience, null recurrence, and
positive recurrence at the consecutive integer values $1,2,3$.

The contribution is the complete computation for the colored Catalan loop
family: entropy, recurrence class, existence and return law of the maximal
measure, zeta singularity, and two exact fixed-point sequences.  We do not
claim to introduce renewal shifts, the Catalan generating function, the
Vere--Jones classification, or circular-code zeta identities.

\section{The loop family}

Write
\[
 C_j=\frac{1}{j+1}\binom{2j}{j},\qquad j\geq0.
\]
Fix $c\in\N$.  Construct a directed multigraph $\mathcal G_c$ with a
distinguished vertex $o$ as follows.  For every $n\geq1$, take
\[
 f_n(c)=cC_{n-1}
\]
simple directed loops of length $n$ based at $o$.  Loops have disjoint
internal vertices; the $c$ loops of length one are parallel edges at $o$.
Let $(\Sigma_c,\sigma)$ be the two-sided edge shift of $\mathcal G_c$.

The graph is strongly connected, and its length-one loops make its period
one.  There are finitely many first-return loops of every fixed length, so
all formal series below are coefficientwise well defined.  The first-return
series at $o$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-return}
 F_c(z)=\sum_{n\geq1}f_n(c)z^n
 =cz\sum_{j\geq0}C_jz^j
 =\frac c2\bigl(1-\sqrt{1-4z}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Its radius is $R=1/4$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary-data}
 F_c(R)=\frac c2,\qquad
 F_c'(z)=\frac{c}{\sqrt{1-4z}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[object boundary]
This is a countable-state Markov shift, not a compact finite-alphabet
subshift.  Entropy below means Gurevich entropy.  Statements about maximal
entropy concern invariant Borel probability measures on $\Sigma_c$.
For an edge sequence $x$, we write $[o]$ for the vertex cylinder on which
the initial vertex of the edge $x_0$ is $o$.
\end{remark}

\section{Entropy and the recurrence transition}

For an irreducible loop shift, let $r_c=e^{-h_G(c)}$.  The standard renewal
criterion says that $r_c$ is the smallest positive solution of $F_c(r)=1$
when such a solution occurs no later than $R$; otherwise $r_c=R$.  At this
parameter the system is transient if $F_c(r_c)<1$, recurrent if equality
holds, and a recurrent system is positive recurrent precisely when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-test}
 \sum_{n\geq1}n f_n(c)r_c^n=r_cF_c'(r_c)<\infty.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[exact trichotomy]\label{thm:trichotomy}
For the colored Catalan loop shift:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $c=1$ is transient and $h_G(1)=\log4$;
\item $c=2$ is null recurrent and $h_G(2)=\log4$;
\item every $c\geq3$ is strongly positive recurrent, with
\[
 r_c=\frac{c-1}{c^2},\qquad
 h_G(c)=\log\frac{c^2}{c-1}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
An invariant probability measure of maximal entropy exists if and only if
$c\geq3$, and is then unique.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At $c=1$, \eqref{eq:boundary-data} gives $F_1(R)=1/2<1$, hence transience
and $r_1=R$.  At $c=2$, one has $F_2(R)=1$, but
$F_2'(R)=\infty$; \eqref{eq:mean-test} gives null recurrence.

If $c\geq3$, solving $F_c(r)=1$ gives
\[
 \sqrt{1-4r}=1-\frac2c,\qquad
 r=\frac{c-1}{c^2}<\frac14.
\]
The solution lies strictly inside the convergence disk, so
$rF_c'(r)<\infty$ and the loop shift is positive recurrent.  The strict
gap $r<R$ is the loop-shift form of strong positive recurrence.  Taking
$-\log r$ gives the entropy formula.  Existence and uniqueness of a
maximal-entropy probability measure for an irreducible finite-entropy
countable Markov shift are equivalent here to positive recurrence
\cite{GurevichSavchenko1998,Sarig1999}.  Concretely, at $c=1$ the candidate
loop weights have total mass $F_1(R)<1$; at $c=2$ they have total mass one
but infinite mean return time; and for $c\geq3$ they have both total mass one
and finite mean.  These are exactly the normalization conditions for the
stationary renewal probability.
\end{proof}

The maximal measure can be written without eigenvectors.  On each return to
$o$, choose a particular first-return loop $\gamma$ of length $|\gamma|=n$
with probability $r_c^n$.  The normalization is exactly $F_c(r_c)=1$.

\begin{proposition}[explicit maximal measure]\label{prop:mme}
Let $c\geq3$.  Under the unique maximal-entropy measure, the return length
$\tau$ to $o$ has law
\[
 \mathbb P(\tau=n)=cC_{n-1}r_c^n,\qquad n\geq1,
\]
and
\[
 \mathbb E\tau=r_cF_c'(r_c)=\frac{c-1}{c-2},\qquad
 \mu_c([o])=\frac{c-2}{c-1}.
\]
The return law has an exponential factor
\[
 4r_c=\frac{4(c-1)}{c^2}<1.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
There are $cC_{n-1}$ loops of length $n$, each with probability $r_c^n$.
The weights of all individual loops sum to $F_c(r_c)=1$, and their mean
length is finite by positive recurrence, so the associated stationary
renewal tower is a probability measure.  Its entropy per excursion is
$(-\log r_c)\mathbb E\tau$; division by the mean excursion length gives
entropy $-\log r_c=h_G(c)$, identifying it with the unique maximal measure.
Differentiating \eqref{eq:first-return} and using
$\sqrt{1-4r_c}=(c-2)/c$ gives
\[
 r_cF_c'(r_c)
 =\frac{c-1}{c^2}\frac{c^2}{c-2}
 =\frac{c-1}{c-2}.
\]
The stationary tower normalization, equivalently Kac's return identity,
gives the reciprocal formula for $\mu_c([o])$.  Finally
$C_{n-1}=\Theta(4^{n-1}n^{-3/2})$, so the displayed strict inequality is
the exponential tail factor.
\end{proof}

\section{Periodic orbits and the algebraic zeta function}

Every periodic path is a circular concatenation of first-return loops.  The
loop code is circular because distinct loops have private internal vertices.
The usual loop-counting identity \cite{Keller1991} therefore gives, as a
formal power series,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeta}
 \zeta_c(z)
 :=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{\#\Fix(\sigma^n)}{n}z^n\right)
 =\frac{1}{1-F_c(z)}
 =\frac{1}{1-\frac c2+\frac c2\sqrt{1-4z}}.
\end{equation}
There are finitely many $n$-periodic points for every $n$: a periodic path
of length $n$ only uses first-return loops of length at most $n$.

\begin{theorem}[boundary fixed-point laws]\label{thm:fixed}
At the transient and null-recurrent parameters,
\[
 \zeta_1(z)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4z}}{2z},
 \qquad
 \zeta_2(z)=(1-4z)^{-1/2},
\]
and, for every $n\geq1$,
\[
 \#\Fix_{\Sigma_1}(\sigma^n)=\frac12\binom{2n}{n},
 \qquad
 \#\Fix_{\Sigma_2}(\sigma^n)=\frac{4^n}{2}.
\]
For $c\geq3$, $\zeta_c$ has a simple pole at
$r_c=(c-1)/c^2$, strictly before its Catalan branch point $1/4$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $c=1$, rationalizing \eqref{eq:zeta} gives the Catalan generating
function.  The classical identity
\[
 [z^n]\log\!\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4z}}{2z}\right)
 =\frac{1}{2n}\binom{2n}{n}
\]
and the definition of dynamical zeta give the first fixed-point law.  For
$c=2$, taking the logarithm of $(1-4z)^{-1/2}$ gives
$[z^n]\log\zeta_2=4^n/(2n)$.  If $c\geq3$, the denominator vanishes at
$r_c<1/4$, and its derivative there is $-F_c'(r_c)\ne0$, proving the simple
pole statement.  Moreover, $|F_c(z)|\leq F_c(|z|)<1$ for $|z|<r_c$.
On $|z|=r_c$, equality $F_c(z)=1$ in the triangle inequality forces the
positive $z$ and $z^2$ terms to have the same argument, hence $z=r_c$.
Thus this pole is the unique singularity of smallest modulus.
\end{proof}

The singularity change is sharper than entropy alone.  The cases $c=1$ and
$c=2$ have the same entropy $\log4$, but at $c=1$ the zeta function remains
finite at $1/4$ and has a branch point, whereas at $c=2$ it diverges with
exponent $1/2$.  At $c\geq3$ a simple pole moves strictly inside the Catalan
disk, exactly when the maximal-entropy probability measure appears.

\section{Checks, ownership, and scope}

The accompanying deterministic control independently generates Catalan
numbers, composes return loops, recovers fixed-point counts from the formal
zeta identity, checks both boundary laws through order $40$, and verifies
the closed entropy and mean-return formulas for $3\leq c\leq50$.  These
finite checks support the algebra and guard indexing; the proofs above carry
the all-parameter statements.

The general recurrence classification and maximal-measure criterion are
owned by countable-state Markov-shift theory
\cite{GurevichSavchenko1998,Sarig1999}.  The zeta reduction to a circular
first-return code is also prior methodology \cite{Keller1991}.  The Catalan
formula and its square-root singularity are classical.  The narrow result
here is their explicit synthesis for the family $f_n=cC_{n-1}$.  No absolute
novelty, priority, compactness, or finite-state claim is made.
