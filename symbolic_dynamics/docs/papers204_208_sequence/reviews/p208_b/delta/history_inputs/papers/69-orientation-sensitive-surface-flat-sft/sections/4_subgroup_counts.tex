\section{Two explicit cover families and their spectra}
\label{sec:subgroup-counts}

The relation $x_1^2x_2^2x_3^2$ has $f$-value
$2-2+0=0$, so the first line of \eqref{eq:f-and-omega} defines a homomorphism
$f\colon\LambdaSurf\to\ZZ$.  It is onto because $f(x_1)=1$.
The resulting families are directed by divisibility: if $a\mid b$, then
$H_b\leq H_a$ and $L_b\leq L_a$.  We retain all
positive moduli because both parities of the nonorientable genus carry
information.

\begin{lemma}[Topology of the two families]
\label{lem:family-topology}
For every $n,m\geq1$:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $[\LambdaSurf:H_n]=n$, and $H_n$ is the fundamental group of a closed
 nonorientable surface of genus $n+2$;
 \item $[\LambdaSurf:L_m]=2m$, and $L_m$ is the fundamental group of a closed
 orientable surface of genus $m+1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The reduction of $f$ modulo $n$ is onto, so $H_n$ has index $n$.  Moreover,
$x_3\in H_n$ and $\omega(x_3)=1$.  Thus $H_n$ is not contained in the
orientation kernel, and the corresponding cover is nonorientable.  Euler
characteristic multiplies under a finite cover, so its Euler characteristic
is
\[
 n\chi(N_3)=-n.
\]
If its nonorientable genus is $\ell$, then $2-\ell=-n$, hence $\ell=n+2$.

Let $\LambdaSurf^+=\ker\omega$.  It has index two and is the fundamental group
of the orientation double cover.  Since
$2\chi(N_3)=-2=2-2\cdot2$, that cover is $\Sigma_2$.  The restriction
$f|_{\LambdaSurf^+}$ is onto: the element $x_1x_3^{-1}$ has even orientation
parity and $f$-value $1$.  Consequently $L_m$ has index $m$ in
$\LambdaSurf^+$ and index $2m$ in $\LambdaSurf$.  It lies inside the
orientation kernel, so its cover is orientable.  Its Euler characteristic is
$-2m$; writing this as $2-2g$ gives $g=m+1$.
\end{proof}

The same integer-valued homomorphism therefore yields both families; only its
domain changes.  This prevents the orientation comparison from being hidden
in unrelated quotient choices.

\begin{theorem}[Exact fixed-point laws]
\label{thm:fixed-laws}
For every finite group $\K$ and every $m,n\geq1$,
\begin{align}
 |\Fix_{L_m}(X_{\K})|
 &=|\K|^{4m}\sum_{\chi\in\Irr(\K)}d_\chi^{-2m},
 \label{eq:orientable-spectrum}\\
 |\Fix_{H_n}(X_{\K})|
 &=|\K|^{2n}\sum_{\chi\in\Irr(\K)}
       \nu_\chi^{n+2}d_\chi^{-n}.
 \label{eq:nonorientable-spectrum}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $L_m$, \cref{lem:family-topology} gives index $V=2m$ and orientable genus
$g=m+1$.  The gauge factor in \eqref{eq:gauge-count} is $|\K|^{2m-1}$, while
\eqref{eq:orientable-hom} gives
\[
 |\Hom(L_m,\K)|
 =|\K|^{2m+1}\sum_{\chi\in\Irr(\K)}d_\chi^{-2m}.
\]
Multiplication gives \eqref{eq:orientable-spectrum}.

For $H_n$, the index is $V=n$ and the nonorientable genus is $\ell=n+2$.
The gauge factor is $|\K|^{n-1}$, and
\eqref{eq:nonorientable-hom} becomes
\[
 |\Hom(H_n,\K)|
 =|\K|^{n+1}\sum_{\chi\in\Irr(\K)}
       \nu_\chi^{n+2}d_\chi^{-n}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:nonorientable-spectrum}.
\end{proof}

Two features of \cref{tab:families} will drive the inverse theorem.  First,
every irreducible contributes positively to the orientable moment.  Second,
the nonorientable parity separates self-duality from its sign: even $n$
replaces every nonzero indicator by $1$, while odd $n$ retains the sign.
