\begin{abstract}
Let $(a_n)_{n\geq1}$ be positive integers and consider the constant-length
directive morphisms
\[
 \sigma_n(0)=0^{a_n+1}1,
 \qquad
 \sigma_n(1)=01^{a_n+1}.
\]
We give a word-level analysis of the resulting binary $S$-adic shift.  The
word $10$ occurs exactly across image boundaries, so it supplies an explicit
marker at every level.  This yields unique desubstitution, two clopen towers
at each scale, minimality, and aperiodicity without invoking an abstract
recognizability theorem.  If
\[
 R=\prod_{n\geq1}\frac{a_n}{a_n+2},
\]
then the invariant-measure simplex is affinely homeomorphic to $[-R,R]$ by
the symbol bias $b(\mu)=\mu([0])-\mu([1])$.  Thus the actual frequency of
$0$ ranges over an interval of radius $R/2$, not $R$.  The system is uniquely
ergodic exactly when $\sum_n1/a_n$ diverges; otherwise it has exactly two
ergodic measures.  For $a_n=n$, the finite-prefix bias radius is
$R_N=2/((N+1)(N+2))$ and tends to zero.  For $a_n=n^2$, the limiting bias radius
is $\pi\sqrt2/\sinh(\pi\sqrt2)$.  Literal marker enumeration and independent
exact incidence arithmetic verify the registered finite controls.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A nonstationary substitution can be primitive at every level and still fail
to be uniquely ergodic.  The obstruction is not visible in any one incidence
matrix: it is the accumulated contraction, or lack of contraction, along the
entire directive sequence.  The binary family in this note isolates that
mechanism in a form where both the symbolic cutting and the measure simplex
are explicit.

Fix positive integers $a_n$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:morphisms-intro}
 \sigma_n(0)=0^{a_n+1}1,
 \qquad
 \sigma_n(1)=01^{a_n+1}.
\end{equation}
Both images have length $a_n+2$, begin with $0$, and end with $1$.  More
decisively, neither image contains $10$, while every concatenation boundary
does.  The same two-letter word therefore marks every level-$n$ cut.  This
first anomaly turns a matrix construction into a concrete symbolic system.

The contribution of the note is an exact, self-contained package for this
marked realization.
\begin{enumerate}
\item We prove all-level recognizability directly from the marker $10$ and
  use the resulting nested cuts to establish minimality and aperiodicity.
\item We identify invariant probabilities with the inverse limit of the two
  normalized incidence simplices.  The proof constructs every measure from
  compatible two-tower weights and does not assume unique ergodicity.
\item We diagonalize that inverse limit in the physical symbol-bias
  coordinate.  Its radius is the infinite product $R$, whereas the radius of
  the $0$-frequency interval is $R/2$.  This also gives the two ergodic
  endpoints and the closed constants for $a_n=n$ and $a_n=n^2$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Prior-results boundary.}
The $S$-adic formalism and incidence-cone approach to frequencies are
standard; see the survey of Berth\'e and Delecroix
\cite{BertheDelecroix2014}.  Berth\'e, Steiner, Thuswaldner, and Yassawi
develop recognizability for sequences of morphisms
\cite{BertheSteinerThuswaldnerYassawi2019}, while B\'edaride, Hilion, and
Lustig develop the tower description of invariant measures
\cite{BedarideHilionLustig2021} and the associated measure-transfer
framework \cite{BedarideHilionLustig2024}.

Most importantly, Ferenczi, Fisher, and Talet already treated the
unique-ergodicity criterion for adic systems with symmetric matrix sequence
\(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}a_n&1\\1&a_n\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\)
\cite{FerencziFisherTalet2009}.  Arbul\'u, Durand, and Espinoza explicitly
record the same summability criterion in their constant-length $S$-adic
discussion \cite{ArbuluDurandEspinoza2024}.  Accordingly, the equivalence
with $\sum1/a_n=\infty$ below is a specialized rederivation, not a priority
claim.  The latter paper also contains close two-letter constant-length
examples in which a reciprocal-sum condition separates unique ergodicity
from a two-measure regime.  Thus neither the inverse-limit mechanism nor the
qualitative phase-transition pattern is claimed as new.  What is recorded
here is the full word-level realization
\eqref{eq:morphisms-intro}: its elementary marker proof, its complete affine
measure interval in the symbol-frequency coordinate, and two exact product
specializations.  A bounded search through 28 August 2026 did not locate this
precise combined package.  That search is not a novelty proof, and external
release remains on hold.

\section{Directive language and the marker}

Let $\A=\{0,1\}$.  For $1\leq m<n$, write
\[
 \tau_{m,n}=\sigma_m\sigma_{m+1}\cdots\sigma_{n-1},
 \qquad
 h_{m,n}=\prod_{j=m}^{n-1}(a_j+2),
\]
and put $\tau_{n,n}=\mathrm{id}$ and $h_{n,n}=1$.  Composition is read from
right to left, so $\tau_{m,n}$ maps level-$n$ letters to level-$m$ words.

\begin{definition}\label{def:tails}
For $m\geq1$, the level-$m$ tail shift $X^{(m)}\subseteq\A^{\Z}$ consists
of the sequences all of whose finite factors occur in
$\tau_{m,n}(c)$ for some $n>m$ and $c\in\A$.  The shift studied in this note
is $X=X^{(1)}$, with the left shift $T$.
\end{definition}

Every incidence matrix is strictly positive, so a word that occurs at one
level occurs inside both sufficiently high supertile types.  The directive
language is therefore recurrent and extendable, and the compact subshifts in
\cref{def:tails} are nonempty.

The next proposition is the symbolic hinge of the paper.

\begin{proposition}[the $10$ marker]\label{prop:marker}
Put $L_m=a_m+2$.  For every $y\in X^{(m+1)}$, the occurrences of $10$ in
$\sigma_m(y)$ start exactly at the positions
\[
 L_m\Z-1.
\]
Consequently every $x\in X^{(m)}$ has a unique centered representation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered}
 x=T^r\sigma_m(y),
 \qquad 0\leq r<L_m,\quad y\in X^{(m+1)}.
\end{equation}
The cut phase $r$ is read from the residue class of the occurrences of $10$,
and each block between consecutive cuts uniquely determines its preimage
letter.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Set $a=a_m$.  The two image words are
\[
 A=0^{a+1}1,
 \qquad B=01^{a+1}.
\]
Neither contains $10$.  Both start with $0$ and end with $1$, so every image
boundary contributes exactly one occurrence of $10$.  In an unshifted image
$\sigma_m(y)$, those boundaries lie between coordinates $kL_m-1$ and
$kL_m$.  This proves the first assertion.

For existence in \eqref{eq:centered}, approximate a point of $X^{(m)}$ by
shifted factors of higher supertiles.  Pass to a subsequence on which the
natural level-$m$ cut has one fixed residue modulo $L_m$.  Compactness of
$\A^{\Z}$ gives a limiting preimage $y$, and its finite factors belong to the
level-$(m+1)$ directive language.  Hence $y\in X^{(m+1)}$.

In any representation, the starts of $10$ form one residue class modulo
$L_m$.  That residue determines $r$ uniquely.  Once the cuts are known,
every intervening length-$L_m$ block is either $A$ or $B$, which are
distinct and decode to $0$ and $1$, respectively.  Thus $y$ is unique as
well.
\end{proof}

Iterating \cref{prop:marker} gives a unique level-$n$ cut phase modulo
\begin{equation}\label{eq:height}
 h_n:=h_{1,n}=\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}(a_j+2).
\end{equation}
This is stronger than an almost-everywhere cutting statement: every point
has one cut phase at every level.

\section{Minimality and aperiodicity}

The marker supplies the cuts; strict positivity supplies recurrence.

\begin{theorem}[minimal aperiodic realization]\label{thm:minimal}
For every sequence of positive integers $(a_n)$, the subshift $(X,T)$ is
minimal and has no periodic point.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $u\in\Lcal(X)$.  Choose $n$ and $c\in\A$ such that $u$ occurs in the
level-$n$ supertile $\tau_{1,n}(c)$.  Each word $\sigma_n(b)$ contains both
letters for $b\in\A$.  Therefore each level-$(n+1)$ supertile
$\tau_{1,n+1}(b)$ contains $\tau_{1,n}(c)$ and hence contains $u$.  By
\cref{prop:marker}, every $x\in X$ is tiled by level-$(n+1)$ supertiles of
common length $h_{n+1}$.  Consecutive occurrences of $u$ in $x$ are thus
separated by at most $2h_{n+1}$.  Every allowed word is uniformly recurrent,
which is equivalent to minimality for a subshift.

Now suppose $T^p x=x$ for some $p>0$.  The unique level-$n$ cut set of $x$
is one residue class modulo $h_n$.  Shifting by $p$ translates that cut set,
while periodicity and uniqueness require the translated set to equal the
original one.  Hence $h_n$ divides $p$ for every $n$.  Since $h_n\geq
3^{n-1}$, no positive integer has this property.  Thus $X$ is aperiodic.
\end{proof}

The proof also explains why recognizability cannot be discarded as a
technical convenience: the divisibility argument uses the uniqueness, not
merely the existence, of every supertile phase.

\section{Two towers and invariant measures}

For $n\geq1$ and $c\in\A$, let
\[
 W_{n,c}=\tau_{1,n}(c),
 \qquad
 B_{n,c}=\tau_{1,n}([c]_{X^{(n)}}).
\]
The set $B_{n,c}$ consists of the points whose level-$n$ cut is at the
origin and whose supertile there has type $c$.  The marker makes these sets
clopen.  The families
\begin{equation}\label{eq:towers}
 \mathcal P_n=
 \{T^jB_{n,c}:c\in\A,\ 0\leq j<h_n\}
\end{equation}
are two-tower clopen partitions, and $\mathcal P_{n+1}$ refines
$\mathcal P_n$.

With rows indexed by output letters and columns by input letters, the
incidence and normalized incidence matrices are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrices}
 M_n=
 \begin{pmatrix}a_n+1&1\\[2pt]1&a_n+1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 P_n=\frac{M_n}{a_n+2}.
\end{equation}
Write $\Delta=\{(p_0,p_1)^{\mathsf T}:p_c\geq0, p_0+p_1=1\}$.

\begin{theorem}[two-tower affine bijection]\label{thm:towers}
For a $T$-invariant probability $\mu$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:towerweights}
 p_c^{(n)}=h_n\mu(B_{n,c}),
 \qquad c\in\A.
\end{equation}
Then $p^{(n)}\in\Delta$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compatibility}
 p^{(n)}=P_np^{(n+1)}.
\end{equation}
The map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-map}
 \mu\longmapsto (p^{(n)})_{n\geq1}
\end{equation}
is an affine bijection from $\Mcal(X,T)$ onto
\[
 \varprojlim(\Delta,P_n)
 =\{(p^{(n)}):p^{(n)}\in\Delta,
                    \ p^{(n)}=P_np^{(n+1)}\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The $h_n$ floors above a fixed base have equal measure.  Since
\eqref{eq:towers} partitions $X$, the two numbers in
\eqref{eq:towerweights} sum to one.  A level-$(n+1)$ supertile of type $b$
contains $M_n(c,b)$ level-$n$ supertiles of type $c$.  Refining the bases,
using shift invariance, and recalling $h_{n+1}=(a_n+2)h_n$ gives
\[
 p_c^{(n)}
 =\sum_{b\in\A}\frac{M_n(c,b)}{a_n+2}p_b^{(n+1)},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:compatibility}.

We prove that no information is lost and that every compatible sequence is
realized.  For a finite word $u$, let $N_u(w)$ count occurrences of $u$
whose full support lies inside the finite word $w$.  Given compatible tower
weights, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma}
 \Gamma_n(u)=\sum_{c\in\A}p_c^{(n)}
                    \frac{N_u(W_{n,c})}{h_n}.
\end{equation}
The level-$(n+1)$ words are concatenations of level-$n$ words.  By
\eqref{eq:compatibility}, occurrences wholly contained in one constituent
block contribute exactly $\Gamma_n(u)$.  Only occurrences crossing a
constituent boundary remain.  There are at most
$(a_n+1)(|u|-1)$ of those in each level-$(n+1)$ supertile, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cauchy}
 |\Gamma_{n+1}(u)-\Gamma_n(u)|
 \leq\frac{|u|-1}{h_n}.
\end{equation}
Because $h_n\geq3^{n-1}$, the sequence $\Gamma_n(u)$ converges.

Define $\mu([u])=\lim_n\Gamma_n(u)$.  Nonnegativity is immediate.  Summing
over all one-letter extensions fails at finite level only for occurrences
touching an endpoint of $W_{n,c}$; the normalized error is at most $1/h_n$.
Taking limits gives both cylinder consistency identities and shift
invariance.  Thus the values extend to a $T$-invariant Borel probability.
Words outside the directive language have zero value, so the measure is
supported on $X$.

Conversely, decompose a cylinder $[u]$ over the towers
\eqref{eq:towers}.  The floors on which $u$ stays inside one supertile give
\eqref{eq:gamma}; the total measure of the boundary floors is at most
$(|u|-1)/h_n$.  Therefore every invariant $\mu$ satisfies
$\mu([u])=\lim_n\Gamma_n(u)$ and is determined by its tower weights.
For the measure constructed from compatible weights, counting the marked
level-$n$ bases inside higher supertiles recovers
$h_n\mu(B_{n,c})=p_c^{(n)}$; high-super-tile endpoints have vanishing
density.  The two constructions are inverse and affine.
\end{proof}

General tower and measure-transfer results cover much wider classes
\cite{BedarideHilionLustig2021,BedarideHilionLustig2024}.  The proof above is included to make the
normalization and the direction of \eqref{eq:compatibility} auditable for
this family.

\section{Bias interval and the phase transition}

The matrices in \eqref{eq:matrices} share the eigenvectors $(1,1)$ and
$(1,-1)$.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rho}
 \rho_n=\frac{a_n}{a_n+2},
 \qquad
 R_N=\prod_{n=1}^{N}\rho_n,
 \qquad
 R=\prod_{n\geq1}\rho_n.
\end{equation}
For $t\in[-1,1]$, write
\[
 v(t)=\frac12\binom{1+t}{1-t}.
\]
Direct multiplication gives the key one-line identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contraction}
 P_nv(t)=v(\rho_nt).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[exact invariant-measure interval]\label{thm:interval}
The map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bias}
 \Theta:\Mcal(X,T)\longrightarrow\mathbb R,
 \qquad
 \Theta(\mu)=b(\mu):=\mu([0])-\mu([1])
\end{equation}
is an affine homeomorphism from $\Mcal(X,T)$ onto $[-R,R]$.
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frequencyinterval}
 \{\mu([0]):\mu\in\Mcal(X,T)\}
 =\left[\frac{1-R}{2},\frac{1+R}{2}\right].
\end{equation}
Thus $R$ is the bias radius, while $R/2$ is the radius of the symbol-frequency
interval around $1/2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $b_n=p_0^{(n)}-p_1^{(n)}$ be the level-$n$ tower bias.  By
\eqref{eq:contraction}, compatibility is equivalent to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:biascompat}
 b_n=\rho_nb_{n+1}.
\end{equation}
In particular, $b_1=R_Nb_{N+1}$, so $|b_1|\leq R_N$ for every $N$ and
$|b_1|\leq R$.

Suppose first that $R>0$.  For any $t\in[-R,R]$, set
\[
 b_1=t,
 \qquad
 b_n=\frac{t}{R_{n-1}}\quad(n\geq2).
\]
Since $R_{n-1}\geq R$, all $b_n$ lie in $[-1,1]$, and
\eqref{eq:biascompat} holds.  This produces one compatible sequence, and
\eqref{eq:biascompat} shows that it is the only one with first bias $t$.

If $R=0$, then for each fixed $n$ the tail product
$\prod_{j=n}^{N}\rho_j$ tends to zero.  Iterating
\eqref{eq:biascompat} gives
\[
 |b_n|\leq\prod_{j=n}^{N}\rho_j,
\]
so every $b_n$ is zero.  The inverse limit is then a singleton.
\Cref{thm:towers} proves bijectivity in both cases.  The map is continuous,
and a continuous bijection from the compact measure simplex to a Hausdorff
interval is a homeomorphism.  Finally,
$\mu([0])=(1+b(\mu))/2$, which proves \eqref{eq:frequencyinterval}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[summability transition]\label{cor:transition}
The shift $(X,T)$ is uniquely ergodic if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:summability}
 \sum_{n\geq1}\frac1{a_n}=\infty.
\end{equation}
If the series converges, then $R>0$ and $(X,T)$ has exactly two ergodic
probability measures, corresponding to the biases $-R$ and $R$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Write $\rho_n=1-y_n$ with $y_n=2/(a_n+2)$.  Since
$0<y_n\leq2/3$,
\[
 y_n\leq-\log(1-y_n)\leq\frac{y_n}{1-y_n}\leq3y_n.
\]
Hence $R>0$ exactly when $\sum_n1/(a_n+2)<\infty$.  For $a_n\geq1$,
\[
 \frac1{3a_n}\leq\frac1{a_n+2}\leq\frac1{a_n},
\]
so this is equivalent to convergence of $\sum_n1/a_n$.

By \cref{thm:interval}, $R=0$ gives one invariant probability.  When
$R>0$, the invariant-measure simplex is a nondegenerate interval, whose only
extreme points are its two endpoints.  Ergodic invariant probabilities are
exactly the extreme points, proving the last assertion.
\end{proof}

As noted in the introduction, the yes/no criterion in
\cref{cor:transition} is already present at the adic matrix level in
\cite{FerencziFisherTalet2009}; the contribution of
\cref{thm:interval} is the explicit affine interval for the marked symbolic
realization and the conversion to physical one-symbol frequencies.

\section{Two closed directive sequences}

The general product becomes elementary for two natural choices of $a_n$.

\begin{corollary}[linear directive]\label{cor:linear}
If $a_n=n$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-product}
 R_N=\prod_{n=1}^{N}\frac{n}{n+2}
 =\frac{2}{(N+1)(N+2)}.
\end{equation}
Thus $R=0$, the system is uniquely ergodic, and its unique measure has
$\mu([0])=\mu([1])=1/2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Cancel $1\cdot2\cdots N$ against $3\cdot4\cdots(N+2)$ in the finite
product.  The remaining assertions follow from \cref{thm:interval}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[quadratic directive]\label{cor:quadratic}
If $a_n=n^2$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-product}
 R=\prod_{n\geq1}\frac{n^2}{n^2+2}
 =\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{\sinh(\pi\sqrt2)}
 =0.104528666176957\ldots.
\end{equation}
There are exactly two ergodic probabilities.  Their $0$-symbol frequencies
are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadratic-frequencies}
 \frac12\left(1\pm\frac{\pi\sqrt2}{\sinh(\pi\sqrt2)}\right)
 =0.4477356669\ldots,
   \ 0.5522643331\ldots.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Euler's product
\[
 \frac{\sinh(\pi x)}{\pi x}
 =\prod_{n\geq1}\left(1+\frac{x^2}{n^2}\right)
\]
at $x=\sqrt2$ gives \eqref{eq:quadratic-product}.  The measure statements
and the factor $1/2$ in \eqref{eq:quadratic-frequencies} follow from
\cref{thm:interval,cor:transition}.
\end{proof}

The contrast is sharp at the first level: the linear directive loses every
nonzero bias in the infinite tower limit, whereas the quadratic directive
retains a bias interval of positive width.  No finite prefix distinguishes
the two regimes categorically; the infinite product does.

\section{Exact controls and scope}

The registered script has two independent exact lanes.  The literal lane
enumerates every binary preimage word of lengths one through seven for
$1\leq a\leq9$.  It checks both linearly and cyclically that $10$ occurs
exactly at image boundaries, that consecutive cyclic markers have gap
$a+2$, and that the cut blocks decode to the original letters.  The matrix
lane uses \texttt{fractions.Fraction} to verify
\eqref{eq:contraction} for individual matrices, all directives of lengths
one through six over $a\in\{1,2,3,4\}$, and compatible finite inverse-limit
biases.  It also verifies \eqref{eq:linear-product} exactly through
$N=200$ and the quadratic partial-product recurrence through $N=100$.

These controls guard marker endpoints, matrix orientation, normalization,
and the factor-of-two conversion from bias to frequency.  They do not
replace the compactness argument in \cref{prop:marker}, the all-level measure
construction in \cref{thm:towers}, or the infinite-product proof.

This manuscript is an internal theorem record.  It does not claim that the
summability criterion is new, and it does not make a global claim about all
constant-length $S$-adic systems.  Public posting, submission, and priority
language remain on hold pending a specialist comparison with the adic and
Toeplitz literature.
