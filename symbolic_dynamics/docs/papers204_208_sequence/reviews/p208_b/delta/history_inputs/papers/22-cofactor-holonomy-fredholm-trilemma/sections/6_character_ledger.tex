\section{Character-resolved connected Fredholm ledger}
\label{sec:ledger}

Character resolution is meaningful only if fixed-period group traces have
finite support.  That follows from the inherited confinement geometry, for
which we include the short proof.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-period confinement]
\label{lem:confinement}
Every length-\(r\) closed path lies in

\[
 \{2,3,\ldots,2r-1\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(M\) be the largest visited vertex.  The edge leaving an occurrence of
\(M\) cannot be the successor edge, because that would visit \(M+1\).  Its
target \(d\) is therefore a proper divisor of \(M+1\), so
\(d\le(M+1)/2\).  Every edge increases its source by at most one.  The
remaining \(r-1\) edges can return to \(M\) only if

\[
 M\le d+r-1\le\frac{M+1}{2}+r-1,
\]

which rearranges to \(M\le2r-1\).
\end{proof}

For a rooted length-\(r\) closed path, \cref{eq:endpoint-cycle-weight} gives
the coefficient \(N(\gamma)^{-2s}\).  Define the finite group-algebra trace

\begin{equation}
 \cT_r(s)=
 \sum_{\gamma\in\Fix_r}N(\gamma)^{-2s}[Q(\gamma)]
\label{eq:group-trace}
\end{equation}

and the connected germ

\begin{equation}
 \cL_s(z)=\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{z^r}{r}\cT_r(s).
\label{eq:connected-ledger}
\end{equation}

At every fixed \(r\), \cref{lem:confinement} makes
\cref{eq:group-trace} an exact finite sum.

\begin{theorem}[Exact holonomy coefficient]
\label{thm:coefficient}
For \(m\in\Gamma\), in the scalar trace-class half-plane and for sufficiently
small \(|z|\),

\begin{align}
 \mathcal H_m(s,z)
 &:=[m]\cL_s(z) \notag\\
 &=\int_{\widehat\Gamma}\overline{\chi(m)}
   [-\log D_\chi(s,z)]\,\dd\chi \notag\\
 &=\sum_{\substack{[\gamma]\ \mathrm{primitive},\ \nu\ge1\\
                    Q(\gamma)^\nu=m}}
   \frac{z^{\nu\ell(\gamma)}}{\nu}
   N(\gamma)^{-2s\nu}.
\label{eq:coefficient-formula}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Evaluate the finite coefficient \cref{eq:group-trace} at a character.  The
result is \(\Tr L_{s,\chi}^r\).  Insert it into the local trace logarithm
\cref{eq:trace-log}, multiply by \(\overline{\chi(m)}\), and use Haar
orthogonality \cref{eq:haar}.  Regrouping rooted closed paths by primitive
rotation class and temporal repetition gives the last line.
\end{proof}

The use of \(-\log D_\chi\) is essential.  A Fourier coefficient of
\(D_\chi\) itself can multiply holonomies from distinct primitive factors and
would not isolate one connected orbit class.

\begin{theorem}[Holonomy-two coefficient]
\label{thm:h2}
In the honest Fredholm half-plane,

\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \mathcal H_2(s,z)=
 \sum_{k\ge2}z^k
 \left(\frac{(2k-1)!}{(k-1)!}\right)^{-2s}.}
\label{eq:H2}
\end{equation}

At rooted trace level,

\begin{equation}
 [2]\cT_r(s)=
 r\left(\frac{(2r-1)!}{(r-1)!}\right)^{-2s}.
\label{eq:T2r}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:q2}, \(C_r\) is the unique primitive rotation class of period
\(r\) and holonomy two.  Its \(r\) rooted rotations give
\cref{eq:T2r}; the factor \(1/r\) in \cref{eq:connected-ledger} cancels
them.  \Cref{cor:no-atomic-repeat} excludes repetitions.
\end{proof}

The series in \cref{eq:H2} has infinite radius in \(z\) when
\(\Re s>0\), radius one when \(\Re s=0\), and radius zero when
\(\Re s<0\).  Only the subdomain \(\Re s>1/2\) will arise from the whole
Fredholm operator.  Convergence of one coefficient for
\(0<\Re s\le1/2\) is not continuation of the determinant.

\begin{corollary}[Atomic coefficient]
\label{cor:Hp}
For every atom \(p\),

\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \mathcal H_p(s,z)=
 \sum_{k\ge2}z^{(p-1)k}
 \left(\frac{(pk-1)!}{(k-1)!}\right)^{-2s}.}
\label{eq:Hp}
\end{equation}

No repetition contributes.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Character persistence]
\label{cor:character-persistence}
For every unitary character \(\chi\), the primitive factor associated to
\(C_k\) is

\[
 1-z^kM_k^{-2s}\chi(2).
\]

It is nontrivial for every \(k\ge2\), and all canonical cycles receive the
same holonomy phase.
\end{corollary}

The exact coefficient is therefore both the strongest positive result and a
no-cancellation certificate.  Within the \(Q=2\) class there is one positive
term at each length and no repeated term.  Any vanishing observed only after
summing different \(Q\)-classes is neither orbit deletion nor a stable
selector.
