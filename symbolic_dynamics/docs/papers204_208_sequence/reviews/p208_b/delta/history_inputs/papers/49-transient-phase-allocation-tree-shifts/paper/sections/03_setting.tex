\section{Setting, metric, and exact cylinders}
\label{sec:setting}

Fix an integer $d\geq2$ and write $\Sigma=\{0,\ldots,d-1\}$.  The rooted
ordered $d$-ary tree is
\[
 \T_d=\Sigma^*=\bigcup_{n\geq0}\Sigma^n,
 \qquad
 \Delta_n=\bigcup_{\ell=0}^n\Sigma^\ell,
 \qquad
 |\Delta_n|=\frac{d^{n+1}-1}{d-1}.
\]
The empty word is the root, and the children of $w$ are $wi$, $i\in\Sigma$.
For a finite alphabet $\A$ and a zero--one matrix $M=(M_{uv})_{u,v\in\A}$,
the associated Markov hom tree-shift is
\begin{equation}
 T_M=\bigl\{x\in\A^{\T_d}:M_{x_w,x_{wi}}=1
       \text{ for every }w\in\T_d,\ i\in\Sigma\bigr\}.
 \label{eq:markov-shift}
\end{equation}
A depth-$n$ pattern is a labeling of $\Delta_n$ satisfying these constraints;
its cylinder is the set of configurations extending it.

\begin{definition}[Rooted-tree metric]
\label{def:metric}
For $x,y\in\A^{\T_d}$, put
\[
 \kappa(x,y)=\sup\bigl(\{\,|\Delta_n|:
 x|_{\Delta_n}=y|_{\Delta_n}\,\}\cup\{0\}\bigr)
\]
and set $D(x,y)=\exp(-\kappa(x,y))$, with $D(x,x)=0$.  We use the restriction
of $D$ to every shift and stratum below.  Also set
$\Delta_{-1}=\varnothing$ and $|\Delta_{-1}|=0$.
\end{definition}

This is the metric of \citet{banLaiWu2025}.  Its endpoint convention is useful:
for $n\geq0$ and
\begin{equation}
 \e^{-|\Delta_n|}\leq r<\e^{-|\Delta_{n-1}|},
 \label{eq:ball-window}
\end{equation}
the closed $D$-ball of radius $r$ around a point is exactly its depth-$n$
cylinder.  Indeed, agreement on $\Delta_n$ gives distance at most the left
endpoint, whereas disagreement somewhere on level $n$ gives distance at
least the excluded right endpoint.  This observation fixes both the metric
normalization and the direction of the Frostman inequality used next.

\begin{lemma}[Equiprobable cylinder dimension]
\label{lem:cylinder-dimension}
Let $X$ be a closed stratum considered in this article.  Suppose that it has
$P_n$ nonempty depth-$n$ cylinders and that a compatible Borel probability
measure $\mu$ assigns mass $P_n^{-1}$ to each of them.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \dimH X=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log P_n}{|\Delta_n|}.
 \label{eq:cylinder-dimension}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let the liminf in \eqref{eq:cylinder-dimension} be $L$.  If $s>L$, choose
$\varepsilon>0$ and a subsequence $n_q$ such that
$\log P_{n_q}\leq(s-\varepsilon)|\Delta_{n_q}|$.  The $P_{n_q}$ cylinders
cover $X$ and have diameter at most $\e^{-|\Delta_{n_q}|}$.  Their total
$s$-cost is bounded by
\[
 P_{n_q}\e^{-s|\Delta_{n_q}|}
 \leq \e^{-\varepsilon|\Delta_{n_q}|}\longrightarrow0,
\]
so $\dimH X\leq L$.

Now take $0\leq s<L$.  For all sufficiently large $n$,
$P_n\geq\e^{s|\Delta_n|}$.  If a ball $B$ has radius in
\eqref{eq:ball-window}, it is contained in a depth-$n$ cylinder and hence
\[
 \mu(B)\leq P_n^{-1}\leq\e^{-s|\Delta_n|}\leq r(B)^s.
\]
The finitely many larger scales are absorbed into one multiplicative
constant.  The mass-distribution argument gives $\dimH X\geq s$; letting
$s\uparrow L$ proves the result.  When $L=0$, the lower bound is automatic.
\end{proof}

All strata below satisfy the measure hypothesis: after phases at the
transient frontier have been fixed, labels at each vertex are chosen
independently and uniformly inside the phase forced at that vertex.  The
finite-dimensional marginals are compatible, and every admissible cylinder
at a given depth has the same mass.  We shall also use the standard identity
\begin{equation}
 \dimH\Bigl(\bigcup_{q=1}^Q X_q\Bigr)=\max_{1\leq q\leq Q}\dimH X_q
 \label{eq:finite-union}
\end{equation}
for a finite union.

\subsection{Complete cyclic phases and the weighted kernel}

Fix $p\geq1$ and pairwise disjoint nonempty phase alphabets
$V_0,\ldots,V_{p-1}$ with
\[
 a_j=|V_j|\in\N,
 \qquad c_j=\log a_j.
\]
All phase indices are read in $\Z/p\Z$.  The \emph{complete cyclic block}
$C(a)$ has all edges from $V_j$ to $V_{j+1}$ and no other edges.  Thus a root
phase determines every descendant phase, but labels inside a forced phase are
independent.

For a real phase vector $x=(x_0,\ldots,x_{p-1})$, define
\begin{equation}
 H_j(x)=\frac{d-1}{d^p-1}
 \sum_{t=0}^{p-1}d^{p-1-t}x_{j-t},
 \qquad
 \bar x=\frac1p\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}x_j.
 \label{eq:H-definition}
\end{equation}
The weights in $H_j$ are positive and sum to one.  For $N\in\N$, let
\begin{equation}
 \Comp_p(N)=\{m\in\Z_{\geq0}^p:\textstyle\sum_{s=0}^{p-1}m_s=N\}
 \label{eq:compositions}
\end{equation}
be the set of weak $p$-compositions of $N$.

\begin{lemma}[Periodic weighted limit]
\label{lem:weighted-limit}
Let $x$ be a real $p$-periodic vector and
$S_{n,h}(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^n d^\ell x_{h+\ell}$.  If
$n\to\infty$ subject to $n\equiv r\pmod p$, then
\begin{equation}
 \frac{S_{n,h}(x)}{|\Delta_n|}\longrightarrow H_{h+r}(x).
 \label{eq:weighted-limit}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $u=n-\ell$.  Along the stated residue class,
\[
 \frac{S_{n,h}(x)}{|\Delta_n|}
 =\frac{d-1}{d-d^{-n}}
   \sum_{u=0}^n d^{-u}x_{h+n-u}
 \longrightarrow \frac{d-1}{d}
   \sum_{u=0}^{\infty}d^{-u}x_{h+r-u}.
\]
Writing $u=t+kp$, $0\leq t<p$, and summing the geometric series gives
\[
 \frac{d-1}{d}
 \sum_{t=0}^{p-1}\frac{d^{-t}}{1-d^{-p}}x_{h+r-t}
 =\frac{d-1}{d^p-1}\sum_{t=0}^{p-1}
 d^{p-1-t}x_{h+r-t},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:weighted-limit}.  The calculation also covers $p=1$.
\end{proof}

The backward index $j-t$ in \eqref{eq:H-definition} is forced by reading
levels backward from the boundary-dominant final level.  It will be retained
in every subsequent circular identity.
