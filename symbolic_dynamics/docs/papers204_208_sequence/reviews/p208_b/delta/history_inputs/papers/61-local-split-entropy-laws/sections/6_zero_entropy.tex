\section{The mixing zero-entropy boundary}
\label{sec:zero}

The positive-entropy proof uses $Nr^N\to0$ and therefore stops when $r=1$.
We now derive the boundary law independently.

\begin{lemma}[Essential zero-entropy reduction]
\label{lem:zero-reduction}
Every mixing zero-entropy SFT is conjugate to the singleton fixed orbit.  Its
essential primitive graph presentation is the one-loop graph with adjacency
matrix $[1]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $A$ be an essential irreducible nonnegative integral adjacency matrix
with $\rho(A)=1$, and choose a positive Perron vector $x$.  Pick a vertex
$i$ for which $x_i=m:=\min_jx_j$.  Since $Ax=x$,
\[
 m=x_i=\sum_j a_{ij}x_j\ge m\sum_j a_{ij}.
\]
Essentiality gives at least one outgoing edge, so row $i$ has exactly one
edge, of multiplicity one, and its terminal vertex also has Perron coordinate
$m$.  Repeating the argument at that vertex produces a deterministic path
all of whose vertices have coordinate $m$.  Finiteness makes it enter a
directed cycle.  If that cycle omitted a vertex, no vertex on the cycle could
reach the omitted vertex, contradicting irreducibility.  Hence the cycle
contains every vertex and the graph is a directed cycle.  Mixing forces its
period, and thus its length, to be one.  Before applying the construction to
a nonessential presentation, remove vertices and edges that belong to no
bi-infinite path; this does not change the presented edge shift.
\end{proof}

For $A=[1]$, the old first-return series is $F(z)=z$.  Duplicating the
length-$N$ return walk gives
\[
 r_N+r_N^N=1.
\]
Set $h_N=-\log r_N$.

\begin{theorem}[Lambert boundary law]
\label{thm:zero}
The number $h_N>0$ is the unique solution of
\begin{equation}
 \ee^{-h_N}+\ee^{-Nh_N}=1.
 \label{eq:zero}
\end{equation}
Moreover, as $N\to\infty$, if $w_N=W(N)$ is the principal Lambert function,
then
\begin{equation}
 h_N=\frac{w_N}{N}
 +\frac{w_N^2}{2N^2(w_N+1)}
 +O\left(\frac{w_N^2}{N^3}\right).
 \label{eq:lambert}
\end{equation}
In particular, $h_N\sim W(N)/N$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The radius equation gives \cref{eq:zero}.  Its left-hand side minus one is
strictly decreasing from one to minus one, proving existence and uniqueness.
For every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, its value at $\varepsilon$ is eventually
negative, so $h_N\to0$.

Put $u_N=Nh_N$ and
\[
 q(t)=\frac{1-\ee^{-t}}{t}.
\]
\Cref{eq:zero} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 \frac{u_N\ee^{u_N}}{N}q(u_N/N)=1.
 \label{eq:u-equation}
\end{equation}
For small positive $t$, $1/2<q(t)\le1$.  Hence, for all sufficiently large
$N$,
\[
 N\le u_N\ee^{u_N}\le2N.
\]
Since $w_N\ee^{w_N}=N$, monotonicity yields
\[
 w_N\le u_N\le W(2N)=O(\log N),
\]
and therefore $u_N/N\to0$.

Let $g(u)=u+\log u$.  Taking logarithms in
\cref{eq:u-equation} and expanding $q$ at zero gives
\begin{align}
 g(u_N)-g(w_N)
 &=-\log q(u_N/N)\notag\\
 &=\frac{u_N}{2N}-\frac{u_N^2}{24N^2}
 +O\left(\frac{u_N^3}{N^3}\right).
 \label{eq:q-expand}
\end{align}
Because $g'(u)=1+1/u$, \cref{eq:q-expand} first implies
$u_N-w_N=O(w_N/N)$.  Substituting this estimate back into
\cref{eq:q-expand} and Taylor expanding $g$ at $w_N$ gives
\[
 (u_N-w_N)\left(1+\frac1{w_N}\right)
 =\frac{w_N}{2N}+O\left(\frac{w_N^2}{N^2}\right).
\]
Consequently,
\[
 u_N=w_N+\frac{w_N^2}{2N(w_N+1)}
 +O\left(\frac{w_N^2}{N^2}\right).
\]
Division by $N$ proves \cref{eq:lambert}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The regime firewall]
Formally inserting $r=1$ into \cref{eq:positive-law} would predict a
nondecaying main term.  The correct gap instead tends to zero at the
subexponential scale $W(N)/N$.  The two formulas are separate theorems with
different proof engines.
\end{remark}
