\section{Why the seventh case contains \texorpdfstring{$\rho_n$}{rho-n}}
\label{sec:case-seven}

We isolate the correction because it is logically prior to the periodic
argument and because merely changing a subscript would conceal both the branch
selection and an endpoint clipping.

\begin{lemma}[Corrected seventh case]
\label{lem:case-seven}
Suppose
\[
 b_{n+1}=a_{n+1}-1,
 \qquad a_{n+1}\ne1,
 \qquad b_n\ne0.
\]
Then the three candidate plateaus on $I_n$ are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{interval for }m&r(x,m)\\ \hline
[q_n-1,\ q_n+q_{n-1}-2]
  &q_n+q_{n-1}-\rho_n\\
{}[q_n+q_{n-1}-1,\ 2q_n+q_{n-1}-\rho_n-2]
  &q_n+q_{n-1}\\
{}[2q_n+q_{n-1}-\rho_n-1,\ q_{n+1}-2]
  &2q_n+q_{n-1}-\rho_n.
\end{array}
\]
As usual, an interval whose lower endpoint exceeds its upper endpoint is
discarded.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $a=a_{n+1}$ and $b=b_{n+1}$.  The hypotheses give
$b=a-1>0$.  If $b_{n+2}=a_{n+2}$, the carry condition
\eqref{eq:legality} would force $b_{n+1}=0$, a contradiction.  Therefore the
truncation rule in \cite[Proposition~2.2.2]{Wojcik2025v1} selects the
$\rho_{n+1}$ branch of the four plateau templates in
\cite[Corollary~2.2.1]{Wojcik2025v1}; this is the applicable branch, not a
formal substitution into an unused case.

With the shorthand in \eqref{eq:table-shorthand}, the recurrence
\eqref{eq:rho-recurrence} gives
\[
 u=(a-1)q+t,
 \qquad Q=aq+p.
\]
Consequently the three template values after the initial value $q$ become
\begin{equation}
 Q-u=q+p-t,
 \qquad Q-bq=q+p,
 \qquad Q+q-u=2q+p-t.
 \label{eq:case7-values}
\end{equation}
Before intersection with $I_n$, the four substituted template rows are
\begin{align*}
 &[q-1,q+p-t-2];q,\\
 &[q+p-t-1,q+p-2];q+p-t,\\
 &[q+p-1,2q+p-t-2];q+p,\\
 &[2q+p-t-1,Q-2];2q+p-t.
\end{align*}
Here $b_n\ne0$ implies
\begin{equation}
 t=\rho_n=\rho_{n-1}+b_nq_{n-1}=s+b_np\geq p.
 \label{eq:t-ge-p}
\end{equation}
The first row is therefore empty: its upper endpoint is at most $q-2$.
The lower endpoint of the second row is at most $q-1$, so intersection with
$I_n=[q-1,Q-2]$ clips it to $q-1$.  The remaining three rows are exactly those
in the statement.  Both the values and the endpoints are thus forced to
contain $t=\rho_n$, not $s=\rho_{n-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[A finite contradiction certificate]
\label{ex:case7-certificate}
Take $a_i=3$ and the legal digits
\[
 (b_1,b_2,b_3,b_4)=(0,1,2,0),
\]
extend the digit sequence by zeros, and set $n=2$.  Then
\[
 q_1=3,\qquad q_2=10,\qquad \rho_1=0,\qquad \rho_2=3.
\]
The case-7 hypotheses hold because $b_3=a_3-1=2$ and $b_2\ne0$.
At $m=q_2-1=9$, the printed v1 subscript gives
\[
 r(x,9)=q_2+q_1-\rho_1=13>10=m+1,
\]
contradicting the Sturmian bound.  The corrected value is
\[
 r(x,9)=q_2+q_1-\rho_2=10=m+1.
\]
Thus the correction is visible at a finite index, independently of the
periodic asymptotic argument.
\end{example}
