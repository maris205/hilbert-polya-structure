\section{Exact checks, boundary behavior, and scope}
\label{sec:examples}

Two period-one/two computations illustrate different parts of the theorem.

\begin{example}[A constant interior tail]
\label{ex:constant-three}
Let $a_i=3$ and $b_i=1$ on the tail.  This is case~5 with $L=1$.  The exact
phase maximum gives
\[
 \dio(x)=\frac{5+\sqrt{13}}{3}
          =2.868517091821330\ldots .
\]
Thus the primitive minimal polynomial is $3X^2-10X+4$.  For example,
\[
  (2868/1000,2869/1000)
\]
is a rational isolating interval for the intended root.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[A boundary Fibonacci tail]
\label{ex:fibonacci-boundary}
Let $a_i=1$ and take the legal periodic digit tail
$0,1,0,1,\ldots$.  Maximal digits occur on every second position, so the
eventual-interior four-quotient shortcut is inapplicable.  The boundary rows
of \cref{tab:eight-cases}, with $L=2$, instead give
\[
 \dio(x)=1+\varphi=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}
          =2.618033988749895\ldots .
\]
Its primitive minimal polynomial is $X^2-3X+1$.  Formally inserting this tail
into an interior-only quotient can make a denominator subscale and suggests a
spurious divergence; the complete table is essential.
\end{example}

The package contains two deterministic regression controls.  The first checks
the structural table and partition consistency on 225,000 legal digit/phase
states, hits every case, certifies
11,682,880,721 individual interval positions by exact interval-length
summation, checks the left-endpoint bound $r(x,m)\leq m+1$, and reproduces the
case-7 values $13$ and $10$ in \cref{ex:case7-certificate}.  The second combines
exact standard-library quadratic polynomial and isolation certificates with
exact integer recurrences on long tails; only its displayed convergence check
uses binary floating point.  It recovers the two algebraic values in
\cref{ex:constant-three,ex:fibonacci-boundary}.  These controls are
regression evidence; the proofs above do not depend on random testing.
The first control does not independently generate a Sturmian word or recompute
$r(x,m)$ point by point; its role is exact structural consistency checking of
the printed table.  A separate external audit performed that word-level
recomputation for all eight cases.

\subsection*{Scope and release status}

The result assumes a quadratic slope and legal ultimately periodic intercept
digits.  It gives no nonperiodic extension and no uniform bit-complexity
estimate.  The state matrix is reducible, and only its continuant block is
primitive.  All eight boundary cases are used; coordinate digit bounds alone
are not treated as legality.

This is an internal verification draft tied explicitly to
arXiv:2504.13636v1.  As checked on 25 August 2026, the official record still
listed only v1.  External release remains on hold until that record is checked
again for a later version and a defensible author-notification or
correction-priority trail is documented.  No author notification or priority
clearance is claimed here.
