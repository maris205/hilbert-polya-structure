\begin{abstract}
We iterate the synchronous part rule
$m\mapsto(\lfloor m/2\rfloor,\lceil m/2\rceil)$ for $m>1$ on integer
compositions.  For every iterate $t$, with
$K=2^t$, we identify the complete kernel congruence: replace each source
part at most $K$ by that many ones and retain every larger part.  Two
compositions have the same $t$-th image exactly when these canonical forms
agree.  The proof exposes a suffix code and gives a right-to-left image
decoder.  Consequently every nonempty $t$-step fibre is a product of
$K$-step Fibonacci counts over the one-runs of its canonical source, and
the largest fibre on weight $n$ is the $K$-restricted composition number.
The same number counts states of depth at most $t$.  Finally, the entire
$t$-th image is in bijection with compositions whose parts lie in
$\{1\}\cup\{K+1,K+2,\ldots\}$, yielding an explicit rational generating
function.  Restricted-composition enumeration, suffix-code theory, and
balanced divide-and-conquer splitting are treated as background.  The owner
search is bounded; no novelty, priority, or external-release claim is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{The map and the subtraction boundary}

Let $\Comp_n$ be the set of integer compositions of $n$, with the empty
composition included when $n=0$.  Define a substitution on positive
integers by
\begin{equation}
 \sigma(1)=1,
 \qquad
 \sigma(m)=\bigl(\lfloor m/2\rfloor,\lceil m/2\rceil\bigr)
 \quad(m>1),                                             \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
and extend it by concatenation.  Our dynamical map is
$\Phi(a_1\cdots a_r)=\sigma(a_1)\cdots\sigma(a_r)$.
All parts are read from the old word, so the update is synchronous.  Weight
is preserved and every changed part raises the word length; hence $1^n$ is
the unique recurrent state and there are no nontrivial cycles.

Simultaneous application of letter rules is a classical parallel-rewriting
interface \cite{LindenmayerRozenberg1972}; its monoid-morphism formulation,
including concatenation of letter images, receives zero contribution credit.
Binary integer fragmentation and balanced splitting are likewise mature
mechanisms \cite{Matsoukas2022}, and the divide-and-conquer clock is not
claimed as value here.  Free-monoid codes, unique decoding, and morphism
ambiguity are established subjects
\cite{BerstelPerrin1985,Honkala1987,FreydenbergerReidenbach2009}; all generic
suffix-code principles receive zero credit.  Enumeration of compositions
with prescribed or bounded parts, including the surrounding analytic
toolkit, is also established
\cite{HeubachMansour2004,Malandro2012,BanderierHitczenko2012}.  In particular,
the one-step image count below is the no-part-$2$ class studied by Chinn and
Heubach \cite{ChinnHeubach2003}.  Its sequence, every bounded-part Fibonacci
count, and every generating function obtained merely by choosing an allowed
part set receive zero contribution credit.

The residual question is dynamical and all-iterate: can one identify the
kernel of $\Phi^t$, decode its image, and count every individual fibre?
The exact rule was subjected to a bounded owner search across rewriting,
fragmentation, substitution, and composition sources.  No direct theorem
package was found; a bounded non-hit is not a novelty certificate.

There is also a strict internal subtraction boundary.  P094 already uses
morphisms, markers, and recognizability; P108 already combines Fibonacci
terminology with clock and fibre geometry; P113 has an integer-sum carrier,
absorption, sharp depth, and product-fibre transport; P115 treats every
iterate, image, and fibre at a logarithmic threshold; P122 combines
target-local fibre factorization with image/Garden enumeration; P123 uses a
refinement engine; and P125 packages pointwise fibres, image layers, and
functional components.  We claim none of those generic silhouettes.  The
residual here is only their literal conjunction for the present
infinite-alphabet balanced morphism: the all-iterate canonical kernel, the
exact one-run fibre product, and the temporal image bijection.

\section{Codewords and the exact clock}

Put
\[
                    W_t(m)=\Phi^t((m)),\qquad K=2^t.
\]
These are words, not unordered fragmentation profiles.

\begin{lemma}[balanced codewords]\label{lem:codewords}
For $m\geq1$, $W_t(m)$ has length $\min(m,K)$, total weight $m$, and
largest and final part $\lceil m/K\rceil$.  In particular,
\begin{equation}
                   W_t(m)=1^m\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\leq K.
                                                               \label{eq:ones}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At each split the two children differ by at most one.  The rightmost path
satisfies the explicit induction
\[
 \operatorname{last}W_{t+1}(m)
 =\operatorname{last}W_t(\lceil m/2\rceil)
 =\left\lceil\frac{\lceil m/2\rceil}{2^t}\right\rceil
 =\left\lceil\frac{m}{2^{t+1}}\right\rceil .
\]
The same balanced-descendant induction makes this the largest part.  If
$m\leq K$, every branch reaches one and weight preservation gives $m$
leaves.  If $m>K=2^t$, then at every level $j<t$ the smallest descendant is
at least $\lfloor m/2^j\rfloor\geq2$; thus there are no early leaves and all
$K$ leaves are present.  This proves every assertion.
\end{proof}

For a nonempty composition $a$, let $M(a)=\max_i a_i$.

\begin{proposition}[pointwise and global clocks]\label{prop:clock}
\[
 \depth(a)=\left\lceil\log_2 M(a)\right\rceil,
 \qquad
 \max_{a\in\Comp_n}\depth(a)=\left\lceil\log_2 n\right\rceil
 \quad(n\geq1).                                         \label{eq:clock}
\]
The empty composition has depth zero, and $(n)$ attains the global bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{lem:codewords}, after $t$ rounds every descendant is one exactly
when every source part is at most $2^t$.  Taking the first such $t$ gives
the pointwise formula.  Since $M(a)\leq n$, the global statement and its
witness follow.
\end{proof}

Let $R_K(r)$ count compositions of $r$ with all parts at most $K$, with
$R_K(0)=1$.  Thus
\begin{equation}
 R_K(r)=\sum_{j=1}^{\min(K,r)}R_K(r-j),
 \qquad
 \sum_{r\geq0}R_K(r)x^r=\frac1{1-x-x^2-\cdots-x^K}.       \label{eq:RK}
\end{equation}
The recurrence is the usual $K$-step Fibonacci convention.  In particular,
$R_1(r)=1$ for every $r\geq0$.

\begin{corollary}[all depth layers]\label{cor:depth}
The number of states in $\Comp_n$ of depth at most $t$ is $R_{2^t}(n)$.
For $t\geq1$, the exact depth-$t$ layer has size
$R_{2^t}(n)-R_{2^{t-1}}(n)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:clock} says precisely that every part is at most $2^t$.
The enumeration in \eqref{eq:RK} is classical; only its identification with
the temporal layer is used here.
\end{proof}

\section{A canonical form for the complete iterated kernel}

For $K=2^t$, define a weight-preserving monoid map
\begin{equation}
 \NF_K(m)=
 \begin{cases}
  1^m,&m\leq K,\\
  m,&m>K,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \NF_K(a_1\cdots a_r)=\NF_K(a_1)\cdots\NF_K(a_r).        \label{eq:normal}
\end{equation}
Its values are precisely the compositions over the canonical alphabet
$\{1\}\cup\{K+1,K+2,\ldots\}$.

\begin{theorem}[canonical kernel and suffix decoder]\label{thm:kernel}
For all compositions $a,b$ of the same weight,
\begin{equation}
                 \Phi^t(a)=\Phi^t(b)
                 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                 \NF_K(a)=\NF_K(b).                     \label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}
Moreover, $\Phi^t$ is injective on canonical forms.  Its image code is
\begin{equation}
        \{1\}\ \cup\ \{W_t(m):m>K\},                    \label{eq:code}
\end{equation}
which is a suffix code.  Hence membership in $\im\Phi^t$ and the unique
canonical source are decided by a right-to-left decoder.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:ones} gives
$\Phi^t(a)=\Phi^t(\NF_K(a))$.  It remains to prove injectivity on canonical
forms.  The codeword for the canonical letter $1$ is the one-letter word
$1$.  Every $W_t(m)$ with $m>K$ has length $K$ and, by
\cref{lem:codewords}, ends in $\lceil m/K\rceil>1$.  Distinct long
codewords have distinct weights.  Thus no word in \eqref{eq:code} is a
proper suffix of another.

Explicitly, if a target ends in $1$, its final canonical letter must be
$1$; delete that symbol.  Otherwise its final codeword must be the last
$K$ symbols.  Their sum determines the sole possible source letter $m>K$,
and the block is accepted exactly when it equals $W_t(m)$.  Delete the
block and repeat.  This proves unique decoding and injectivity.  Applying it
to $\Phi^t(\NF_K(a))=\Phi^t(\NF_K(b))$ proves the converse in
\eqref{eq:kernel}.
\end{proof}

For example, take $t=2$, so $K=4$.  The source $(3,5,2)$ has canonical
form
\[
             (1,1,1,5,1,1)
\]
and image $111\,1112\,11$.  Reading from the right deletes two terminal
ones, recognizes the block $1112=W_2(5)$, and then deletes three ones,
recovering the canonical form.

\section{Every fibre and the clock--fibre duality}

Write a canonical form uniquely as
\begin{equation}
 z=1^{r_0}m_1 1^{r_1}m_2\cdots m_s1^{r_s},
 \qquad m_i>K,\quad r_i\geq0.                            \label{eq:runs}
\end{equation}
For the empty decoded word we use $s=0$ and $r_0=0$.

\begin{theorem}[complete fibre product]\label{thm:fibres}
A target $y$ has no $t$-step preimage exactly when the suffix decoder in
\cref{thm:kernel} rejects it.  If the decoder returns the canonical form
\eqref{eq:runs}, then
\begin{equation}
                    |(\Phi^t)^{-1}(y)|
                    =\prod_{j=0}^{s}R_K(r_j).             \label{eq:fibre}
\end{equation}
For the empty word this is the one-factor product $R_K(0)=1$.
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
             \max_{y\in\Comp_n}|(\Phi^t)^{-1}(y)|=R_K(n), \label{eq:maxfibre}
\end{equation}
attained by $y=1^n$.  Thus the maximum $t$-fibre equals the number of
states of depth at most $t$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:kernel}, the fibre over $y$ is exactly the inverse image under
$\NF_K$ of its decoded canonical form.  Every large letter $m_i$ must
remain the same source part.  Independently, a run $1^{r_j}$ may be grouped
into any composition of $r_j$ with parts at most $K$, giving
\eqref{eq:fibre}.

Concatenation injects a tuple of restricted compositions of weights
$r_0,\ldots,r_s$ into a restricted composition of their total weight, so
the product in \eqref{eq:fibre} is at most
$R_K(\sum r_j)\leq R_K(n)$.  The final inequality follows by appending
ones.  For $y=1^n$, the canonical form is $1^n$ and equality holds.
The last claim now follows from \cref{cor:depth}.
\end{proof}

In the displayed $K=4$ example, the canonical one-runs have lengths three
and two.  Hence the image $111111211$ has
$R_4(3)R_4(2)=4\cdot2=8$ two-step preimages.  This is pointwise information:
different targets of the same weight generally have different products.

\section{The complete image tower}

Let $I_{n,t}=|\im(\Phi^t:\Comp_n\to\Comp_n)|$.

\begin{theorem}[all-iterate image census]\label{thm:image}
For $K=2^t$, restriction of $\Phi^t$ is a weight-preserving bijection
from canonical forms to its image.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{n\geq0} I_{n,t}x^n
 =\frac{1}{1-\left(x+\dfrac{x^{K+1}}{1-x}\right)}
 =\frac{1-x}{1-2x+x^2-x^{K+1}}.                          \label{eq:imageogf}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $I_{0,t}=I_{1,t}=1$ and, for $n\geq2$,
\begin{equation}
 I_{n,t}=2I_{n-1,t}-I_{n-2,t}
          +\mathbf1_{n\geq K+1}I_{n-K-1,t}.              \label{eq:imagerec}
\end{equation}
with the harmless coefficient convention $I_{j,t}=0$ for $j<0$.
For $n\geq1$, the number of Garden states at time $t$ is
$2^{n-1}-I_{n,t}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every source has the same image as its canonical form by
\cref{thm:kernel}; distinct canonical forms have distinct images.  Hence
the image is equinumerous with sequences of parts in
$\{1\}\cup\{K+1,K+2,\ldots\}$.  The ordinary sequence construction gives
\eqref{eq:imageogf}; coefficient extraction gives \eqref{eq:imagerec}.
There are $2^{n-1}$ compositions of positive $n$, proving the last claim.
\end{proof}

At $t=0$ one has $K=1$, so \eqref{eq:imageogf} becomes
$(1-x)/(1-2x)$: explicitly $I_{0,0}=1$ and
$I_{n,0}=2^{n-1}$ for $n\geq1$, as required by the identity iterate.

For $t=1$, the image numbers begin
$1,1,1,2,4,7,12,21,37,65,\ldots$; the equinumerous canonical class is
exactly the classical compositions without the part $2$
\cite{ChinnHeubach2003}.  This enumerative class is zero-credit.  The result
here is the all-$t$ dynamical bijection supplied by the canonical kernel
and suffix decoder.  When $2^t\geq n$, only $1^n$ is canonical, so
$I_{n,t}=1$, in agreement with the sharp clock.

\section{Exact controls, limits, and conclusion}

The paper-local verifier uses only exact integer arithmetic and the Python
standard library.  It exhausts all compositions through weight $18$ for
the clock, and all sources and targets through weight $15$ for every
$0\leq t\leq5$.  It compares literal kernel classes, suffix decoding,
the fibre product, an independent factorization DP, maximum fibres, and the
image census.  Codewords are checked through $m=256,t=8$, and the rational
image recurrence through weight $90,t=8$.  These are falsification
controls; the quantified statements rest on the proofs above.

The scope is deliberately finite and exact.  We do not claim a new general
theory of substitutions, codes, restricted compositions, fragmentation,
or balanced trees.  We do not treat asynchronous splits, other arities,
random schedules, or asymptotic laws.  The exact-map owner search remains
bounded, so novelty, priority, and external release remain on hold.

Within that boundary, the normal form \eqref{eq:normal} gives more than a
terminal clock: it determines the complete kernel of every iterate.  The
suffix code turns that kernel into a pointwise image test, the one-runs give
every fibre, and the canonical alphabet gives the full image tower.  The
coincidence between the largest inverse fibre and the cumulative depth census
is the resulting clock--fibre duality.
