\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Myhill property asks whether every preinjective cellular automaton on a
subshift is surjective.  Preinjectivity is weaker than injectivity: it only
rules out collisions between homoclinic configurations.  On a reducible
system, points in different invariant pieces can therefore have the same
image without violating the hypothesis.  This observation makes the possible
routes between pieces part of the problem rather than a bookkeeping device.

We consider a setting in which those routes can be isolated exactly.  Let

\begin{equation}
  X=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}X_i
  \label{eq:clopen-union-intro}
\end{equation}

be a finite disjoint union of nonempty clopen invariant irreducible
subshifts.  Write $X_i\pito X_j$ when there is a preinjective cellular
automaton from $X_i$ to $X_j$.  Our main structural result is

\begin{equation}
  X\text{ is Myhill}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \left\{
  \begin{array}{l}
    X_i\text{ is Myhill for every }i,\\
    X_i\not\pito X_j\text{ whenever }i\ne j.
  \end{array}
  \right.
  \label{eq:routing-iff-intro}
\end{equation}

Three elementary-looking points carry the quantifiers in
\eqref{eq:routing-iff-intro}.  Distinct compact invariant pieces contain no
homoclinic pair, so preinjectivity is componentwise even when component images
overlap.  A finite clopen pasting of component maps is a cellular automaton on
the whole union.  Finally, the factor image of an irreducible piece lies in one
target piece.  Together these facts prove both implications without assuming
that component images are disjoint.

The routing criterion becomes finite arithmetic for a broad explicit class.
Let $M$ be a positive-entropy mixing one-dimensional shift of finite type and
put

\begin{equation}
  H(M)=\{n\geq 1:\Fix_M(\sigma^n)=\varnothing\}.
  \label{eq:holes-intro}
\end{equation}

The set $H(M)$ is finite and downward closed under divisibility.  If $O_p$
denotes one orbit of minimal period $p$, then

\begin{equation}
 M\sqcup O_{p_1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup O_{p_t}
 \text{ is Myhill}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \{p_1,\ldots,p_t\}\text{ is an antichain in }H(M),
 \label{eq:antichain-intro}
\end{equation}

with pairwise distinct labels required before the tuple is viewed as a set.
The asymmetry behind \eqref{eq:antichain-intro} is exact.  A periodic orbit
can route into $M$ whenever $M$ has a point fixed by the corresponding shift
power.  By contrast, a positive-entropy mixing $M$ cannot map
preinjectively into a finite orbit: the arbitrary-target entropy theorem for
a strongly irreducible source would preserve positive entropy
\cite[Theorem~5.1]{ceccheriniCoornaert2012}.

The arithmetic classification supports systems with unboundedly many
components.  For $s\geq1$, join directed cycles of lengths $m=2s+1$ and
$m+1$ at one vertex and nowhere else.  Its closed-walk lengths form
$\langle m,m+1\rangle$.  The consecutive block

\begin{equation}
  P_s=\{s+1,s+2,\ldots,2s\}
  \label{eq:Ps-intro}
\end{equation}

is an $s$-element divisibility antichain outside that semigroup.  Adding the
orbits with labels in $P_s$ produces a Myhill SFT with exactly $s+1$ clopen
irreducible components.

The component language sits next to current work on reducible SFTs.
Ceccherini-Silberstein, Coornaert, and Salo prove that an endomorphism of a
one-dimensional SFT induces a map on its irreducible-component poset
\cite[Proposition~3.14]{ceccheriniCoornaertSalo2026}; they also record the
preinjective collapses of two fixed components and of a fixed component into a
full shift \cite[Examples~8.3 and~8.6]{ceccheriniCoornaertSalo2026}.  Their
paper leaves a clean Myhill characterization for general one-dimensional SFTs
as a problem \cite[Section~9]{ceccheriniCoornaertSalo2026}.  We use none of
those component-routing facts as a claimed contribution.  Our scope is the
narrower class \eqref{eq:clopen-union-intro}, where the displayed pieces
exhaust the space and make preinjectivity componentwise.  The strongly
irreducible Myhill and entropy results used for the mixing core are also
imported explicitly from \cite{ceccheriniCoornaert2012}.

The contributions are therefore concrete and limited:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item We prove the routing equivalence \eqref{eq:routing-iff-intro} for a
  finite clopen invariant irreducible decomposition.
  \item We classify all disjoint periodic-orbit extensions of a fixed
  positive-entropy mixing SFT by finite antichains of \eqref{eq:holes-intro}.
  \item We give the two-loop family \eqref{eq:Ps-intro}, including exact fixed
  support, hole count, Frobenius number, conductor, and component count.
\end{enumerate}

\Cref{sec:preliminaries} fixes conventions and imported results.
\Cref{sec:routing} proves the routing theorem.  \Cref{sec:period-holes}
derives the antichain classification, and \Cref{sec:renewal} constructs the
unbounded-component family.  \Cref{sec:controls} records exact predicate
checks and the scope firewall.
