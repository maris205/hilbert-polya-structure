\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Compact operators can share every nonzero eigenvalue and still have
incompatible geometry.  The simplest obstruction is already visible in a
rank-one block: the eigenvalue records the action on its eigenline, whereas
the angle between the range and kernel controls the norm of its Riesz
projection.  The point of this paper is that two elementary arithmetic
retractions organize that obstruction into an exact family of phase
transitions.

Fix $h\ge2$.  Let $\Fh$ be the positive integers whose prime exponents are
strictly smaller than $h$, and define
\begin{equation}
  \tau_h(n)=\prod_p p^{\min\{v_p(n),h-1\}},
  \qquad
  \omega_h(n)=\prod_p p^{v_p(n)\bmod h}.
  \label{eq:intro-maps}
\end{equation}
Both maps retract $\N$ onto $\Fh$.  On the finitely supported sequences set
\begin{equation}
  S_{h,s}e_n=n^{-s/2}e_{\tau_h(n)},
  \qquad
  M_{h,s}e_n=n^{-s/2}e_{\omega_h(n)},
  \qquad \sigma=\Re s.
  \label{eq:intro-operators}
\end{equation}
Here and below $n^{-s/2}=\exp(-(s/2)\log n)$ with the real logarithm.
The formulas in \cref{eq:intro-operators} are algebraic until boundedness is
proved.  The symbols $S_{h,s}$ and $M_{h,s}$ denote operators on
$\ell^2(\N)$ only on their respective bounded domains; every spectral,
ideal, trace, determinant, similarity, and commutator assertion is confined
to those domains.  For $0<q<\infty$, $\Sq$ denotes the Schatten class,
understood as the usual quasi-Schatten ideal when $q<1$.

The common fixed points in $\Fh$ force common eigenlines.  The fibers above
those points are very different: saturation can increase only primes already
at exponent $h-1$, whereas reduction modulo $h$ permits an arbitrary
$h$th-power multiplier.  That distinction changes fiber mass and block
angle without changing the eigenvalue on the fixed point.

\begin{theorem}[Paired arithmetic retraction theorem]
\label{thm:main}
Let $h\ge2$, $s\in\C$, and $\sigma=\Re s$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item $S_{h,s}$ extends boundedly exactly for $\sigma>0$, and
  $M_{h,s}$ extends boundedly exactly for $\sigma>1/h$.  Each extension is
  compact throughout its bounded domain.

  \item For $k\ge1$ and $0<q<\infty$,
  \begin{align*}
    S_{h,s}^{k}\in\Sq
      &\iff k\sigma q>2,\\
    M_{h,s}^{k}\in\Sq
      &\iff \sigma>1/h\ \text{and}\ k\sigma q>2.
  \end{align*}

  \item On each bounded domain the simple nonzero eigenvalues are
  $\lambda_m=m^{-s/2}$, $m\in\Fh$.  On the common domain
  $\sigma>1/h$, if $k\sigma>2$, then
  \begin{equation}
    \Tr(S_{h,s}^{k})=\Tr(M_{h,s}^{k})
      =\frac{\zeta(ks/2)}{\zeta(hks/2)}.
    \label{eq:intro-trace}
  \end{equation}
  If $r\ge1$ is an integer, $\sigma>1/h$, and $r\sigma>2$, then
  \begin{equation}
    \det_r(I-zS_{h,s})=\det_r(I-zM_{h,s})
    \label{eq:intro-det}
  \end{equation}
  as entire functions of $z$.  For $r=1$, this is the ordinary Fredholm
  determinant and requires $\sigma>2$.

  \item The exact normal-similarity domains are
  \begin{align*}
    S_{h,s}\sim_{\mathrm{bd}}\text{ a compact normal operator}
      &\iff \sigma>1,\\
    M_{h,s}\sim_{\mathrm{bd}}\text{ a compact normal operator}
      &\iff \sigma>1/h.
  \end{align*}
  Consequently $1/h<\sigma\le1$ is an isospectral band in which
  $M_{h,s}$ is similar to normal and $S_{h,s}$ is not, while the two
  operators have equal order-$r$ regularized determinants for every integer
  $r$ that is legal for both.  The order $r=1$ is never legal inside this
  band, since the Fredholm determinant requires $\sigma>2$.

  \item Let $P_k=p_1\cdots p_k$, with $P_0=1$, and take the largest
  $k=k(x)$ for which $P_k^{h-1}\le x$.  The largest saturated Riesz norm
  among $m\in\Fh$, $m\le x$, is attained at $P_k^{h-1}$.  As
  $x\to\infty$ this maximum tends to $\sqrt{\zeta(\sigma)}$ if
  $\sigma>1$; it is asymptotic to
  $\sqrt{\e^\gamma\log\log x}$ if $\sigma=1$; and if
  $0<\sigma<1$, its logarithm is asymptotic to
  \begin{equation}
    \frac{(h-1)^{\sigma-1}(\log x)^{1-\sigma}}
         {2(1-\sigma)\log\log x}.
    \label{eq:intro-primorial}
  \end{equation}

  \item The singular values, counted with multiplicity in decreasing
  order, satisfy
  \begin{align*}
    s_n(S_{h,s})&\sim(\frac{\Chs}{n})^{\sigma/2}
      &&(\sigma>0),\\
    s_n(M_{h,s})&\sim(\frac{\Dhs}{n})^{\sigma/2}
      &&(\sigma>1/h),\\
    |\lambda_n|&\sim
      (\frac{1/\zeta(h)}{n})^{\sigma/2},
  \end{align*}
  where
  \begin{align}
    \Chs
      &=\prod_p(1-p^{-1})\left[
        \sum_{e=0}^{h-2}p^{-e}
        +p^{-(h-1)}(1-p^{-\sigma})^{-1/\sigma}
        \right],
        \label{eq:intro-C}\\
    \Dhs&=\frac{\zeta(h\sigma)^{1/\sigma}}{\zeta(h)}.
        \label{eq:intro-D}
  \end{align}
  The constants meet at $\Chs=\Dhs=1$ when $\sigma=1$.  No ordering
  between them is asserted away from that point.

  \item For $0<q<\infty$,
  \begin{align*}
    [S_{h,s}^*,S_{h,s}]\in\Sq
      &\iff \sigma q>1,\\
    [M_{h,s}^*,M_{h,s}]\in\Sq
      &\iff \sigma>1/h\ \text{and}\ \sigma q>1.
  \end{align*}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The theorem separates three ledgers.  The cyclic ledger---eigenvalues,
legal traces, and legal determinants---is common.  The metric ledger---block
singular values and their Weyl constants---is not.  The angular ledger---Riesz
projection norms and self-commutators---produces a further pair of sharp
walls.  In particular, equality of the regularized determinants in
\cref{eq:intro-det} is a negative control: it demonstrates how much
geometry a complete nonzero-eigenvalue product can fail to see.

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.}
Weighted composition operators on weighted sequence spaces have general
boundedness, compactness, closed-range, and essential-norm theories
\cite{LuanKhoi2015,AbaninMannanikov2023}.  Carlson's discrete setting gives
spectral and commutant context for weighted composition operators on
$L^2$ spaces \cite{Carlson1990}.  Our basis maps are naturally viewed on the
adjoint side of that general framework; this convention and the generic
fiber-norm mechanism are not contributions.  Power-free parts also have a
classical arithmetic life independent of the operators considered here
\cite{deWegerWoestijne1999}.  The residual result is the paired all-$h$
classification and its exact arithmetic distortion laws, not a generic
weighted-composition theorem and not a claim that the formulas detect the
additive semantics of rational primes.

We use standard trace-ideal and regularized-determinant theory from
\cite{Simon2005}, classical prime asymptotics from
\cite{MontgomeryVaughan2006}, and Wiener--Ikehara in the form discussed in
\cite{Korevaar2004}.  The hypotheses needed here are checked explicitly.
