\title[Primitive-recurrence avoidance shifts]{Primitive-Recurrence Avoidance Shifts:\\
Singer-Cycle Compression and a Delayed Periodic Anomaly}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{Internal Stage 2 draft, 28 August 2026}
\subjclass[2020]{37B10, 37C30, 11T71, 94A55, 05C50}
\keywords{shift of finite type, primitive polynomial, Singer cycle, finite Fourier transform, periodic point, dynamical zeta function}

\begin{abstract}
Fix a primitive degree-$r$ recurrence over $\F_q$ and forbid its discrepancy
from vanishing at every time.  For $q\geq3$ and $r\geq2$, the resulting
$(q-1)$-regular shift of finite type is mixing and has a unique, uniform
measure of maximal entropy.  Put
\[
 L=q^r-1,\qquad H=q^{r-1}-1,\qquad D=(q-1)^H.
\]
Although its state matrix has size $q^r$, finite Fourier transform reduces it
to one scalar block and one weighted Singer cycle.  We obtain
\[
 \det(tI-A)=(t-(q-1))(t^L-D),
 \qquad
 \zeta(z)=\frac{1}{(1-(q-1)z)(1-Dz^L)}.
\]
Consequently every fixed-point count before time $L$ is exactly
$(q-1)^n$, the count of the full $(q-1)$-shift; the first discrepancy occurs
at $L$ and has size $LD$.  Thus $F_1$ and the location of the first anomaly
recover $(q,r)$.  The same algebraic formulas remain valid at $q=2$, where
mixing fails and the system becomes one fixed orbit plus one orbit of length
$2^r-1$.  Exact controls build the full adjacency matrices over
$\F_2,\F_3,\F_4$, and $\F_5$ and verify the Fourier-cycle ingredients, full
characteristic polynomial, periodic counts, and recovery statement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A primitive linear feedback shift register has the simplest possible
nonzero state dynamics: its companion matrix cycles through all nonzero
states.  That deterministic cycle structure and its relation to primitive
polynomials and Singer cycles are classical and remain active subjects in
coding theory \cite{GhorpadeHasanKumari2011,ChangEzermanLingWang2018}.  We
change the local rule in one specific way.  Instead of enforcing zero
recurrence error, we forbid zero error and allow every nonzero error at every
step.  The deterministic register then becomes a regular shift of finite
type with $q-1$ choices from each state.

The change is small at the level of the defining recurrence but not at the
level of periodic data.  A direct determinant has degree $q^r$.  The useful
coordinates are additive characters: the zero character gives the entropy
root, while a primitive companion matrix puts every nonzero character into
one orbit.  Along that orbit the Fourier weights are $q-1$ on one coordinate
hyperplane and $-1$ off it.  Counting the hyperplane intersections produces
the complete determinant with two factors.

The contribution is the following scope-delimited theorem package.
\begin{itemize}
 \item We give an explicit finite affine skew-product model and a full
 Fourier/block proof of the two-factor characteristic polynomial.
 \item We compute every fixed-point count, every least-period orbit count,
 and the Artin--Mazur zeta function.  Periodic data agree with the full
 $(q-1)$-shift until the sharp time $q^r-1$.
 \item We prove mixing and identify the unique maximal-entropy measure for
 $q\geq3$, isolate the reducible binary boundary, and recover $(q,r)$ from
 the first periodic anomaly.
\end{itemize}

The ownership boundary is important.  We do not claim primitive
polynomials, Singer cycles, or the cycle structure of deterministic linear
feedback registers \cite{GhorpadeHasanKumari2011,ChangEzermanLingWang2018}.
We also do not claim the determinant formula for a general shift of finite
type \cite{BowenLanford1970} or the Parry maximal-entropy construction
\cite{Parry1964}.  The residual object is the nonzero-error relation and the
weighted Fourier cycle it creates.  A bounded search found no direct source
for this combined formula and delayed-anomaly theorem, but no absolute
novelty or priority claim is made.  External posting and submission remain
on hold pending specialist literature and priority review.

\section{The avoidance shift and its state model}
\label{sec:model}

Let $q$ be a prime power and $r\geq2$.  Fix a primitive polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:primitive-polynomial}
 f(t)=t^r-c_{r-1}t^{r-1}-\cdots-c_1t-c_0\in\F_q[t]
\end{equation}
and its companion matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:companion}
 C=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  0&1&0&\cdots&0\\
  0&0&1&\cdots&0\\
  \vdots&&&\ddots&\vdots\\
  0&0&0&\cdots&1\\
  c_0&c_1&c_2&\cdots&c_{r-1}
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Primitivity means that a root of $f$ generates $\F_{q^r}^{\times}$.  In
particular, $C$ has order
\[
 L=q^r-1
\]
and acts transitively on $\F_q^r\setminus\{0\}$.

\begin{definition}[Primitive-recurrence avoidance shift]
The shift $X_{q,r,C}\subset\F_q^{\Z}$ consists of the sequences $x$ for
which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error}
 e_n(x):=x_{n+r}-\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}c_jx_{n+j}\in\F_q^{\times}
 \qquad(n\in\Z).
\end{equation}
It is equipped with the left shift $\sigma$.
\end{definition}

For $s=(s_0,\ldots,s_{r-1})^{\mathsf T}\in\F_q^r$, write
$\mathbf e_r=(0,\ldots,0,1)^{\mathsf T}$.  The $r$-block presentation has
state adjacency matrix $A$, indexed by $\F_q^r$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjacency}
 A_{s,t}=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 t=Cs+u\mathbf e_r\text{ for some }u\in\F_q^{\times}.
\end{equation}
The overlap coordinates in \eqref{eq:companion} show that this presentation
is conjugate to $X_{q,r,C}$, not merely a cover.

There is also a useful global description.  Let
$E=(\F_q^{\times})^{\Z}$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:skew-product}
 T(s,e)=\bigl(Cs+e_0\mathbf e_r,\sigma e\bigr)
 \quad\text{on }\F_q^r\times E.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Finite affine skew product]
\label{prop:skew}
The map
\[
 x\longmapsto \bigl((x_0,\ldots,x_{r-1})^{\mathsf T},(e_n(x))_{n\in\Z}\bigr)
\]
is a topological conjugacy from $(X_{q,r,C},\sigma)$ to
$(\F_q^r\times E,T)$.  Moreover, every row and every column of $A$ has sum
$q-1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Given a state and a two-sided error sequence, \eqref{eq:error} determines
the future recursively.  Since $c_0\ne0$, or equivalently $C$ is invertible,
the same equation determines the past.  This construction is inverse to the
displayed map and intertwines the dynamics with \eqref{eq:skew-product}.

For a fixed source state, the $q-1$ nonzero values of $u$ in
\eqref{eq:adjacency} give distinct targets.  For a fixed target $t$ and each
$u\ne0$, invertibility gives the unique source
$C^{-1}(t-u\mathbf e_r)$.  Thus both degrees equal $q-1$.
\end{proof}

\section{Fourier compression}
\label{sec:fourier}

Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parameters}
 H=q^{r-1}-1,
 \qquad
 D=(q-1)^H.
\end{equation}
The exponent $H$ counts the nonzero points of a coordinate hyperplane.

Fix a nontrivial additive character $\psi\colon\F_q\to\mathbb C^\times$.
For $\xi,s\in\F_q^r$, set
\[
 \chi_\xi(s)=\psi(\langle\xi,s\rangle).
\]
The $q^r$ functions $\chi_\xi$ form a basis of the complex functions on
$\F_q^r$.  Indeed, if $\delta\ne0$, choose a nonzero coordinate
$\delta_j$, choose $a\in\F_q$ with $\psi(a)\ne1$, and put
$s_j=a/\delta_j$ with the other coordinates zero; then
$\psi(\langle\delta,s\rangle)\ne1$.  Thus the character pairing is
nondegenerate even when $q$ is not prime.  Regard $A$ as the forward transfer operator
\[
 (A\varphi)(s)=\sum_{u\in\F_q^\times}\varphi(Cs+u\mathbf e_r).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Weighted dual permutation]
\label{lem:fourier-action}
For every $\xi\in\F_q^r$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourier-action}
 A\chi_\xi=w(\xi_r)\chi_{C^{\mathsf T}\xi},
 \qquad
 w(a)=
 \begin{cases}
  q-1,&a=0,\\
  -1,&a\ne0.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Substitution into the transfer operator gives
\[
 (A\chi_\xi)(s)
 =\psi(\langle C^{\mathsf T}\xi,s\rangle)
   \sum_{u\in\F_q^\times}\psi(u\xi_r).
\]
The last sum is $q-1$ when $\xi_r=0$.  When $\xi_r\ne0$, multiplication by
$\xi_r$ permutes $\F_q^\times$, and the sum over the whole field is zero;
deleting the term at zero leaves $-1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two-block characteristic polynomial]
\label{thm:charpoly}
For every prime power $q\geq2$ and every $r\geq2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:charpoly}
 \boxed{\det(tI-A)=(t-(q-1))(t^L-D).}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeta-determinant}
 \det(I-zA)=(1-(q-1)z)(1-Dz^L).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The zero character spans an invariant line, and
\eqref{eq:fourier-action} acts there by $q-1$.  Since $C$ is a Singer cycle,
$C^{\mathsf T}$ is conjugate to a Singer cycle and therefore has one orbit
of length $L$ on the nonzero dual vectors.  The remaining Fourier block is a
weighted cyclic permutation.  Its characteristic polynomial is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-cycle}
 t^L-\prod_{i=0}^{L-1}w\bigl(((C^{\mathsf T})^i\xi)_r\bigr)
\end{equation}
for any $\xi\ne0$.

The orbit visits every nonzero vector exactly once.  Among those vectors,
exactly $q^{r-1}-1=H$ have last coordinate zero.  The other
\[
 L-H=q^{r-1}(q-1)
\]
vectors contribute a factor $-1$.  This number is even: if $q$ is odd then
$q-1$ is even, and if $q$ is even then $q^{r-1}$ is even because $r\geq2$.
The product in \eqref{eq:weighted-cycle} is therefore $(q-1)^H=D$.
Multiplying the zero-frequency factor and the nonzero-frequency factor gives
\eqref{eq:charpoly}.  Replacing $t$ by $z^{-1}$ and multiplying by $z^{q^r}$
gives \eqref{eq:zeta-determinant}.
\end{proof}

The proof identifies the compression mechanism rather than inferring it
from traces: one scalar Fourier mode and one weighted cycle account for all
$q^r$ eigenvalues.

\section{All periods, zeta, and recovery}
\label{sec:periods}

Write
\[
 F_n=\#\Fix(\sigma^n\mid X_{q,r,C}).
\]
The block presentation is a conjugacy, so $F_n=\tr(A^n)$.

\begin{theorem}[Complete periodic data]
\label{thm:periodic}
For every $n\geq1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-count}
 \boxed{
 F_n=(q-1)^n+\one_{\{L\mid n\}}L(q-1)^{Hn/L}.}
\end{equation}
The Artin--Mazur zeta function is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeta}
 \boxed{
 \zeta_{q,r,C}(z)
 =\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{F_n}{n}z^n\right)
 =\frac{1}{(1-(q-1)z)(1-Dz^L)}.}
\end{equation}
The number $O_n$ of shift orbits of least period $n$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:least-period}
 O_n=\frac1n\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)
 \left((q-1)^d+\one_{\{L\mid d\}}L(q-1)^{Hd/L}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is the M\"obius function.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The $L$ roots of $t^L-D$ have $n$th-power sum zero unless $L\mid n$.
When $n=kL$, that sum is $LD^k$.  Adding the $n$th power of the scalar
eigenvalue $q-1$ proves \eqref{eq:fixed-count}.  Summing the contribution of
the multiples $n=kL$ in the defining logarithm gives
\[
 \sum_{k\geq1}\frac{LD^kz^{kL}}{kL}
 =\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{(Dz^L)^k}{k}
 =-\log(1-Dz^L),
\]
while the scalar mode gives $-\log(1-(q-1)z)$.  This proves
\eqref{eq:zeta}.  Finally, $F_n=\sum_{d\mid n}dO_d$; M\"obius inversion gives
\eqref{eq:least-period}.
\end{proof}

The formula has a sharp identification consequence.

\begin{corollary}[First-anomaly rigidity]
\label{cor:recovery}
Let $\Delta_n=F_n-F_1^n$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-anomaly}
 F_1=q-1,
 \qquad
 \min\{n\geq1:\Delta_n>0\}=L=q^r-1,
 \qquad
 \Delta_L=LD.
\end{equation}
Hence the fixed-point sequence recovers
\[
 q=F_1+1,
 \qquad
 r=\log_q(L+1).
\]
In particular, two shifts in this family with identical fixed-point data
have the same pair $(q,r)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Because $r\geq2$, one has $L>1$, and \eqref{eq:fixed-count} gives
$F_1=q-1$.  The correction term in that formula is positive exactly at
multiples of $L$, proving all three assertions in \eqref{eq:first-anomaly}.
The displayed recovery formulas follow.
\end{proof}

The agreement before $L$ is exact, not asymptotic: through time $L-1$ the
periodic counts are indistinguishable from those of the full shift on
$q-1$ symbols.

\section{Mixing, maximal entropy, and the binary boundary}
\label{sec:mixing}

\begin{theorem}[Mixing and uniform maximal entropy]
\label{thm:mixing}
Suppose $q\geq3$.  Then $X_{q,r,C}$ is topologically mixing,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy}
 \htop(X_{q,r,C})=\log(q-1),
\end{equation}
and it has a unique measure of maximal entropy.  In the state presentation,
that measure has stationary distribution $q^{-r}$ on $\F_q^r$ and transition
probabilities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parry}
 P_{s,t}=\frac{A_{s,t}}{q-1}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the recurrence errors form an i.i.d. uniform process on
$\F_q^\times$, independent of the uniform time-zero state.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:skew}, $A$ is both row- and column-$(q-1)$-regular.  Hence
$q-1$ is a Perron eigenvalue with constant left and right eigenvectors.  The
other eigenvalues have modulus
\[
 D^{1/L}=(q-1)^{H/L}<q-1,
\]
because $q-1>1$ and $H<L$.  Thus the Perron eigenvalue is simple and is the
only eigenvalue on its spectral circle.

For completeness, double regularity turns simplicity into irreducibility.
If the directed graph were not strongly connected, a sink strongly
connected component would have no outgoing edges.  Its total internal row
sum would already equal $(q-1)$ times its number of vertices.  The equal
column sums then leave no incoming edges either, so this component and its
nonempty complement would each contribute an eigenvalue $q-1$, contradicting
simplicity.  An irreducible nonnegative matrix of period $d$ has $d$
eigenvalues on its spectral circle.  Therefore $d=1$, and $A$ is primitive.
This proves mixing and \eqref{eq:entropy}.

The Parry construction for a primitive matrix is unique
\cite{Parry1964}.  Substituting the constant Perron vectors gives the
uniform stationary law and \eqref{eq:parry}.
Since each labeled error determines one successor and its conditional law is
uniform independently of the current state, successive errors are i.i.d.;
the same factorization shows independence from the stationary initial state.
\end{proof}

The restriction $q\geq3$ is necessary.

\begin{proposition}[Binary boundary]
\label{prop:binary}
When $q=2$, the algebraic formulas in \cref{thm:charpoly,thm:periodic} remain
valid with $D=1$, but the shift is not mixing.  It is the disjoint union of
one fixed orbit and one orbit of length $L=2^r-1$.  Consequently it has two
ergodic maximal-entropy measures and
\[
 \zeta_{2,r,C}(z)=\frac1{(1-z)(1-z^L)}.
\]
\end{proposition}

All maximal-entropy measures in the binary case are the convex combinations
of these two ergodic periodic-orbit measures.

\begin{proof}
There is only one allowed error, so the state map is the permutation
$s\mapsto Cs+\mathbf e_r$.  Since $1$ is not an eigenvalue of the primitive
companion matrix, $I-C$ is invertible and the affine permutation has a unique
fixed point $s_*$.  Translation by $s_*$ conjugates it to $C$.  The latter
fixes zero and cycles through all $L$ nonzero states, which proves the orbit
decomposition and the measure statement.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact controls and scope}
\label{sec:controls}

The deterministic control program constructs the full state matrices rather
than substituting the theorem formulas.  It also constructs the dual Singer
orbit, counts its coordinate-hyperplane intersections, computes
$\det(tI-A)$ by the integer Faddeev--LeVerrier algorithm, checks strong
connectivity and period, and compares traces through time $L+1$.  The frozen
cases are shown in \cref{tab:controls}.

The $\F_4$ lane uses the independent model
$\F_2[u]/(u^2+u+1)$.  Across the five lanes, the script records 258 exact
assertions.  For example, at $(q,r)=(3,3)$ it verifies
\[
 F_{26}=2^{26}+6656=67{,}115{,}520,
\]
whereas every $F_n$ for $1\leq n<26$ equals $2^n$.

The theorem does not cover nonprimitive recurrence polynomials or arbitrary
allowed error sets.  In those regimes the nonzero dual frequencies can split
into several orbits, and the weight product in \eqref{eq:weighted-cycle}
need not reduce to one factor.  That orbit splitting is a different problem.
Within the primitive, nonzero-error family, the Fourier proof gives the
complete spectrum, dynamics, and recovery statement without an unverified
genericity assumption.
