\section{Period-hole antichains}
\label{sec:period-holes}

Let $M$ be a positive-entropy topologically mixing one-dimensional SFT.  Its
\emph{period-hole set} is
\begin{equation}
 H(M)=\{n\in\N_{>0}:\Fix_M(\sigma^n)=\varnothing\},
 \label{eq:hole-set}
\end{equation}
ordered by divisibility.

\begin{proposition}[Finite downward ideal]
\label{prop:hole-ideal}
The set $H(M)$ is a finite downward ideal.  If $A$ is a primitive adjacency
presentation and $e$ satisfies $A^e>0$, then
\begin{equation}
 H(M)=\{1\leq n<e:\tr(A^n)=0\}.
 \label{eq:hole-traces}
\end{equation}
In particular, $H(M)$ and all of its antichains are effectively computable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $d\mid n$ and $x\in\Fix_M(\sigma^d)$, then
$x\in\Fix_M(\sigma^n)$.  Taking the contrapositive, every positive divisor of
a hole is a hole.

Equation \eqref{eq:trace-fix} identifies holes with zero traces.  A primitive
matrix has no zero column.  Hence $A^e>0$ implies $A^{e+1}=A^eA>0$: for each
column $j$, choose $k$ with $A_{kj}>0$, and then
$(A^{e+1})_{ij}\geq(A^e)_{ik}A_{kj}>0$ for every $i$.  Induction gives
$A^n>0$ for all $n\geq e$, and therefore $\tr(A^n)>0$ for those $n$.  This
proves \eqref{eq:hole-traces} and finiteness.
\end{proof}

Let $O_p$ denote one orbit whose points have minimal period $p$.  We realize
all disjoint unions below on pairwise disjoint alphabets.  Thus their pieces
are clopen; when $M$ is an SFT, the union is the edge shift of a block-diagonal
finite graph and is again an SFT.

\begin{lemma}[Routes involving periodic orbits]
\label{lem:periodic-routes}
For positive integers $p,q$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item $O_p\pito O_q$ if and only if $q\mid p$;
  \item $O_p\pito M$ if and only if
  $\Fix_M(\sigma^p)\ne\varnothing$;
  \item there is no preinjective cellular automaton $M\to O_p$;
  \item $M$ and every $O_p$ are Myhill.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $x\in O_p$.  An equivariant map $O_p\to O_q$ is determined by
$y=\phi(x)$ and is well defined precisely when $\sigma^p y=y$.  Since $y$ has
minimal period $q$, this is equivalent to $q\mid p$.  Distinct phases in
$O_p$ disagree periodically at infinitely many coordinates.  Every
homoclinicity class in $O_p$ is therefore a singleton, so every such map is
preinjective, even when it collapses phases.

The same base-point argument shows that a map $O_p\to M$ exists exactly when
some $y\in M$ satisfies $\sigma^p y=y$.  Define
$\phi(\sigma^k x)=\sigma^k y$.  Singleton homoclinicity classes make $\phi$
preinjective even if the minimal period of $y$ properly divides $p$.

The mixing SFT $M$ is strongly irreducible.  If a preinjective map
$\phi:M\to O_p$ existed, the arbitrary-target entropy identity
\eqref{eq:entropy-preservation} would give
\[
  \ent(\phi(M))=\ent(M)>0.
\]
But $\phi(M)$ is finite and has entropy zero, a contradiction.

The strongly irreducible Myhill theorem makes $M$ Myhill.  An endomorphism of
$O_p$ is determined by the image of one phase in $O_p$ and is a cyclic
rotation, hence bijective.  Thus $O_p$ is Myhill.
\end{proof}

We can now state the complete periodic-extension classification.

\begin{theorem}[Period-hole antichain criterion]
\label{thm:antichain}
For a finite tuple $(p_1,\ldots,p_t)$ of positive integers, put
\begin{equation}
 X(M;p_1,\ldots,p_t)
 =M\sqcup O_{p_1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup O_{p_t}.
 \label{eq:periodic-extension}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 X(M;p_1,\ldots,p_t)\text{ is Myhill}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \begin{cases}
 p_1,\ldots,p_t\text{ are pairwise distinct},\\
 \{p_1,\ldots,p_t\}\text{ is an antichain in }H(M).
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:antichain-iff}
\end{equation}
After the order of the adjoined orbit components is forgotten, the finite
Myhill extensions \eqref{eq:periodic-extension} are parametrized by the finite
antichains of $H(M)$, including the empty antichain.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
All pieces in \eqref{eq:periodic-extension} are Myhill.  By
\cref{lem:periodic-routes}(ii), the absence of $O_{p_i}\pito M$ is equivalent
to $p_i\in H(M)$.  By part (i), the absence of arrows between distinct orbit
components is equivalent to
\[
  p_j\nmid p_i\qquad(i\ne j).
\]
This condition rejects equal labels as well as strict divisibility
comparisons.  Part (iii) excludes all routes from the core to an orbit.
Applying \cref{thm:routing} proves both directions of
\eqref{eq:antichain-iff}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite decision procedure]
\label{cor:finite-decision}
Given a primitive adjacency presentation $A$ of $M$ and a finite tuple of
period labels, the Myhill property of \eqref{eq:periodic-extension} is decided
by exact integer arithmetic: compute zero traces below a primitive exponent,
test membership of every label, then test equality and pairwise divisibility.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is \cref{prop:hole-ideal,thm:antichain}.
\end{proof}
