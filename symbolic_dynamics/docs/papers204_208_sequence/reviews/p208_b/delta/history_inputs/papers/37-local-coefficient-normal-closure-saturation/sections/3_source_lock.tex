\section{Frozen same-object coefficient model}
\label{sec:source}

\subsection{Paths, marker, and edge transport}

Let $\Gr=\Z[1/r]\rtimes_r\Z$ be the enveloping group, with
$vuv^{-1}=u^r$.  We retain the formal-reverse enlargement of the positive
right Cayley graph of $\Mr$.  A Hashimoto state is an oriented edge, and
\begin{equation}
  e\longrightarrow f
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  t(e)=o(f),\qquad f\ne\bar e,
  \label{eq:hashimoto-rule}
\end{equation}
with the same exclusion at the cyclic join.  Paths are not quotiented,
induced, or accelerated.  Every application of \cref{eq:hashimoto-rule}
contributes one free factor of $z$.

Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional complex fiber.  Assign invertible transports
\begin{equation}
  P_e:E_{o(e)}\longrightarrow E_{t(e)},
  \qquad P_{\bar e}=P_e^{-1}.
  \label{eq:inverse-transport}
\end{equation}
A transport family enters the analytic Fredholm statement only when it is
uniformly bounded:
\begin{equation}
  M_P:=\sup_{e\in E_r^{\mathrm{or}}}\lVert P_e\rVert<\infty.
  \label{eq:uniform-transport}
\end{equation}
The frozen shear fixture is generator-role constant, so this condition is
automatic.  The factorwise finite-matrix statements below do not otherwise
depend on uniform boundedness.
With row fibers, a closed word $\gamma=e_0\cdots e_{\ell-1}$ has holonomy
\begin{equation}
  W_\gamma=P_{e_0}\cdots P_{e_{\ell-1}}.
  \label{eq:holonomy}
\end{equation}
A cyclic base change conjugates this matrix and preserves its complete
determinant factor.

\subsection{Damping and matrix Fredholm object}

If $o(e)=(b(e),k(e))$, freeze
\begin{equation}
  d_\theta(e)=\theta^{1+b(e)+k(e)},\qquad
  \TP=(\Dtheta\otimes I_E)H_P(\Dtheta\otimes I_E),
  \qquad 0<\theta<1.
  \label{eq:matrix-operator}
\end{equation}
The damping depends only on affine source coordinates and is applied unchanged
to every control.  For a primitive orbit set
\begin{equation}
  q_\theta(\gamma)=\prod_{e\in\gamma}d_\theta(e)^2>0.
  \label{eq:orbit-weight}
\end{equation}
The accepted ordinary unit is
\begin{equation}
  \det\!\left(I-q_\theta(\gamma)z^{|\gamma|}W_\gamma\right)^{-1}.
  \label{eq:ordinary-factor}
\end{equation}
Neither one trace nor a scalar obtained by merging distinct orbit monomials is
an accepted replacement.

\subsection{Graded ownership and terminology}

For $E_+\oplus E_-$, with each parity family satisfying
\cref{eq:uniform-transport}, keep the two ordinary trace-class operators distinct and
define
\begin{equation}
  \Zgr(z)=\frac{\det(I-zT_-)}{\det(I-zT_+)}.
  \label{eq:graded-ratio}
\end{equation}
This virtual ratio is not called an ordinary positive determinant.  Its
factor at $\gamma$ is the quotient of the odd and even determinant
polynomials from \cref{eq:ordinary-factor}.

A genuine local coefficient system on the filled Cayley $2$-complex is flat:
each relation-cell holonomy is identity.  A rule with nontrivial cell
holonomy is instead an edge connection on the unfilled $1$-skeleton.  The
distinction is essential because the filled complex is contractible.

\begin{convention}[Factorwise cancellation]
An orbit is cancelled only when its complete determinant polynomials agree in
the two parities, equivalently when every super-power-trace vanishes.  Equality
at the first trace, after $z=1$, or after aggregation across primitive orbits
earns no credit.
\end{convention}

The source process may construct presentations, words, bounded mixed products,
and the frozen matrices.  The evaluator independently performs free/cyclic
reduction, affine evaluation, exact matrix multiplication, factor comparison,
and decisions.  Prime or factor tables, accepted support, target zeros,
coefficient fitting, and network data are forbidden from both processes.
