\section{Expanded proof details}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{The cover formula and locally finite completion}

For \(n>1\),
\[
(\zbar*\zbar)(1,n)
=\sum_{1\mid c\mid n}\zbar(1,c)\zbar(c,n).
\]
The factors require \(1<c<n\), so the value is the number of strict
intermediate divisors. It is zero exactly for a cover of \(1\). If \(n=ab\)
with \(1<a<n\), then \(a\) is such an intermediate divisor. Conversely, an
intermediate divisor supplies a nontrivial factorization. This proves
\eqref{eq:atoms-primes} without any scalar prime coefficient.

The intervalwise countable statement in
\cref{thm:primitive-system} requires no topological completion. For fixed
\(a\mid b\), only the finite interval \([a,b]\) can contribute to an
incidence convolution, and only \(n\in[a,b]\) can contribute to
\(\sum_n q_n(a,b)\). Every finite identity therefore stabilizes on that
interval.

\subsection{Matrix orientation and rank one}

With rows and columns indexed by source integers, the kernel
\eqref{eq:kernel} factors as
\[
q_n(a,b)
=\id_{\{a\mid n\}}\,
  \id_{\{n\mid b\}}\mob(b/n).
\]
Thus every finite \(q_n\) has rank one. For an atom \(p\), its range vector is
exactly \(e_1+e_p\), because the only divisors of \(p\) are \(1\) and \(p\).
Its functional is
\[
x\longmapsto\sum_{k\ge1}\mob(k)x_{pk}.
\]
The evaluation of this functional on \(e_1+e_p\) is
\(\mob(1)=1\). Hence the rank-one trace formula gives \(\Tr q_p=1\)
and the same evaluation proves \(q_p^2=q_p\).

For distinct atoms \(p\) and \(q\), the functional associated with \(p\)
vanishes on both nonzero coordinates of \(e_1+e_q\): neither \(1\) nor \(q\)
is a positive multiple of \(p\). This gives pair annihilation directly on
\(H_\eta\), independently of the global similarity theorem.

\subsection{The radical conjugating unit}

The proof of \cref{thm:finite-conjugacy} uses only completeness,
orthogonality, and a nilpotent radical. To verify the intertwining step in
detail, write
\[
v=\sum_y f_ye_y.
\]
Then
\[
f_xv
=\sum_y f_xf_ye_y
=f_xe_x,
\]
whereas
\[
ve_x
=\sum_y f_ye_ye_x
=f_xe_x.
\]
Thus \(f_xv=ve_x\). Since \(v=1+j\) for \(j\in J_N\), the finite geometric
series
\[
v^{-1}=1-j+j^2-\cdots
\]
terminates and proves \(f_x=ve_xv^{-1}\). This proof does not assert that
\(v\) is unitary or that the \(f_x\) are orthogonal in a Hilbert metric.

\subsection{Exact weighted dual norm}

The dual of \(H_\eta\) identifies the coefficient functional
\(\lambda(x)=\sum_n b_nx_n\) with squared norm
\[
\norm{\lambda}^2=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-2\eta}|b_n|^2.
\]
For \(b_{pk}=\mob(k)\) and all other coefficients zero,
\[
\norm{\lambda_p}^2
=p^{-2\eta}\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\mob(k)^2}{k^{2\eta}}.
\]
The squarefree Dirichlet series is absolutely convergent for
\(2\eta>1\), and prime-by-prime expansion gives
\[
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\mob(k)^2}{k^{2\eta}}
=\prod_{\ell\in\Pp}(1+\ell^{-2\eta})
=\prod_{\ell\in\Pp}
  \frac{1-\ell^{-4\eta}}{1-\ell^{-2\eta}}
=\frac{\zeta(2\eta)}{\zeta(4\eta)}.
\]
Multiplying by \(\norm{e_1+e_p}_\eta^2=1+p^{2\eta}\) gives
\eqref{eq:trace-norm}.

\subsection{Downsampling convergence}

Let \(S_kX=(X_{ak})_{a\ge1}\). Then
\[
\norm{S_kX}_{\ell^2}^2
=\sum_{a\ge1}|X_{ak}|^2
\le\sum_{n\ge1}|X_n|^2,
\]
so \(\norm{S_k}\le1\). The series in
\eqref{eq:downsampling-series} therefore converge absolutely in operator norm
when their scalar coefficients lie in \(\ell^1\). For zeta this requires
\(\eta>1\). For Möbius,
\[
\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{|\mob(k)|}{k^\eta}
=\prod_{\ell\in\Pp}(1+\ell^{-\eta})
=\frac{\zeta(\eta)}{\zeta(2\eta)}.
\]
Inversion on finite-support vectors extends because both limits are bounded.

\subsection{The gamma-marker half-plane}

Put \(j=\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor\), so \(2^j\le n<2^{j+1}\) and
\(\ell(n)=2j+1\). For fixed \(\rho>0\), with
\(\alpha=2\log_2\rho\),
\[
\min(\rho,\rho^{-1})n^\alpha
\le \rho^{\ell(n)}
\le \max(\rho,\rho^{-1})n^\alpha.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:gamma-asymptotic}. The prime sum in
\eqref{eq:absolute-domain} therefore has the same convergence as
\[
\sum_p p^{-(\Re s-2\log_2\rho)}.
\]
The prime Dirichlet series converges exactly when its real exponent is greater
than one, proving \eqref{eq:marker-half-plane}. At equality the divergence of
the prime harmonic series supplies the sharp boundary.

\subsection{Trace-log and degreewise ownership}

On compact subsets of \eqref{eq:absolute-domain}, the transfer series is
uniformly summable in trace norm. For sufficiently small \(z\), the
trace-log series is absolutely convergent and the sums over \(r\) and \(p\)
may be interchanged. The resulting product is entire in \(z\), so analytic
uniqueness extends the identity to every \(z\).

For the holomorphic tensor, each degree is formed and shown trace class before
a quotient is taken. Write
\[
d_r=\Tr\cT_0^r-\Tr\cT_1^r.
\]
Then
\[
\log\frac{\det(I-z\cT_0)}{\det(I-z\cT_1)}
=-\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{z^r}{r}d_r.
\]
Incidence pair annihilation reduces \(d_r\) to monochromatic atom words, and
the local de Rham identity makes their fiber contribution one. This yields
\eqref{eq:graded-ratio} without interpreting a formal superdeterminant whose
degreewise factors have not been defined.
