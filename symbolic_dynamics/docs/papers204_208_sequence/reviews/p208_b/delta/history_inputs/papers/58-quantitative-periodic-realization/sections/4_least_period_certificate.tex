\section{A Perron--M\"obius least-period certificate}
\label{sec:least-period}

We now turn from abundance at a prescribed multidimensional period to exact
least periods in one dimension.  Let $A$ be a primitive $r\times r$
nonnegative integral matrix.  Its Perron root $\lambda$ is simple and
strictly dominates the moduli of all other eigenvalues.  Throughout this
section we impose the positive-entropy condition $\lambda>1$.  If $r>1$, set
\[
 \theta=\max_{2\le j\le r}|\lambda_j|<\lambda,
\]
where the eigenvalues are counted with algebraic multiplicity; when $r=1$,
set $\theta=0$.

\begin{theorem}[Perron--M\"obius lower certificate]
\label{thm:least-period}
For every $n\ge1$, the number $L_n$ of points of least period $n$ in $X_A$
satisfies
\begin{equation}
L_n\ge \lambda^n-(r-1)\theta^n
-\tau(n)\left(\lambda^{n/2}
 +(r-1)\max(1,\theta)^{n/2}\right).
\label{eq:least-bound}
\end{equation}
Consequently, every $n$ for which the right-hand side is positive occurs as a
least period.  There is furthermore an effectively computable $N(A)$ such
that the right-hand side is positive for every $n\ge N(A)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $F_n=\tr(A^n)$, as in \cref{eq:trace-fixed}.  The trace of a matrix power
is the sum of the corresponding eigenvalue powers, whether or not the matrix
is diagonalizable.  The triangle inequality therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \lambda^n-(r-1)\theta^n
 \le F_n
 \le \lambda^n+(r-1)\theta^n.
 \label{eq:trace-spectral}
\end{equation}
By \cref{eq:mobius}, or directly by subtracting every possible contribution
from a proper divisor,
\begin{align}
 L_n
 &\ge F_n-\sum_{\substack{e\mid n\\e<n}}F_e. 
 \label{eq:subtract-divisors}
\end{align}
Every proper divisor $e$ of $n$ satisfies $e\le n/2$.  Since $\lambda>1$,
\[
 \lambda^e\le\lambda^{n/2}.
\]
Also
\[
 \theta^e\le \max(1,\theta)^{n/2},
\]
including the case $0\le\theta<1$.  There are at most $\tau(n)$ proper
divisors.  Substitution of \cref{eq:trace-spectral} into
\cref{eq:subtract-divisors} proves \cref{eq:least-bound}.

For effectivity, divide the right-hand side of \cref{eq:least-bound} by
$\lambda^n$ and use $\tau(n)\le n$.  It suffices that
\begin{equation}
 E(n):=(r-1)\alpha^n+n\beta^n+n(r-1)\gamma^n<1,
 \label{eq:normalized-error}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 \alpha=\frac{\theta}{\lambda},\qquad
 \beta=\lambda^{-1/2},\qquad
 \gamma=\frac{\sqrt{\max(1,\theta)}}{\lambda}.
 \label{eq:error-bases}
\end{equation}
Primitivity gives $\alpha<1$, and $\lambda>1$ gives $\beta<1$.  Finally,
$\max(1,\theta)<\lambda$; hence
$\sqrt{\max(1,\theta)}<\sqrt\lambda<\lambda$, so $\gamma<1$.

The characteristic polynomial of $A$ is integral.  Standard algebraic root
isolation therefore computes certified rational upper enclosures
\[
 \alpha<\widehat\alpha<1,\qquad
 \beta<\widehat\beta<1,\qquad
 \gamma<\widehat\gamma<1.
\]
For a rational $0<a<1$, the sequence $na^n$ is nonincreasing once
$n\ge a/(1-a)$, because
\[
 \frac{(n+1)a^{n+1}}{na^n}=a\left(1+\frac1n\right)\le1.
\]
Choose an integer $M$ beyond the corresponding thresholds for
$\widehat\beta$ and $\widehat\gamma$.  Starting at $M$, search for the first
$m$ for which the rational inequality
\begin{equation}
 (r-1)\widehat\alpha^m+m\widehat\beta^m
 +m(r-1)\widehat\gamma^m<1
 \label{eq:rational-certificate}
\end{equation}
holds.  The search terminates because every term tends to zero.  At and after
that $m$, each term in the rational majorant is nonincreasing, so
\cref{eq:normalized-error} holds for every $n\ge m$.  Taking $N(A)=m$ proves
both computability and the tail assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A finite verifier payload]
\label{prop:finite-payload}
Suppose rational numbers $a,b,c\in(0,1)$ satisfy
\[
 \alpha<a,\qquad \beta<b,\qquad \gamma<c
\]
for the bases in \cref{eq:error-bases}.  If an integer $m$ satisfies
\begin{align}
 m&\ge \left\lceil\frac{b}{1-b}\right\rceil,
 &
 m&\ge \left\lceil\frac{c}{1-c}\right\rceil,
 \label{eq:monotone-thresholds}\\
 (r-1)a^m+mb^m+m(r-1)c^m&<1,
 \label{eq:payload-inequality}
\end{align}
then every $n\ge m$ is a least period of $X_A$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first term in \cref{eq:payload-inequality} decreases with $n$.  The two
thresholds in \cref{eq:monotone-thresholds} make $nb^n$ and $nc^n$
nonincreasing for $n\ge m$.  Consequently the rational left-hand side of
\cref{eq:payload-inequality} majorizes $E(n)$ and remains below one for every
$n\ge m$.  The normalized estimate in the proof of
\cref{thm:least-period} then gives $L_n>0$.
\end{proof}

Thus a conductor computation can export the finite tuple
\[
 \mathfrak C(A)=(a,b,c,m)
\]
together with algebraic interval certificates for the three strict upper
bounds.  A verifier need not rerun the search or diagonalize $A$: it checks
the root-isolation intervals, the two rational threshold inequalities, and
the single rational inequality \cref{eq:payload-inequality}.  This separates
the potentially expensive discovery step from a small auditable proof
object.

\begin{remark}[What is certified]
\label{rem:points-orbits}
The theorem certifies $L_n>0$, and therefore the existence of at least one
orbit of least period $n$.  Quantitatively, however, its left-hand side is a
point count.  A lower bound for the number of orbits is obtained only after
division by $n$.  Positivity of \cref{eq:least-bound} is sufficient but not
necessary, so neither the first positive $n$ nor $N(A)$ is asserted optimal.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[A fully rational tail check]
\label{ex:fibonacci}
For the Fibonacci matrix
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
one has $r=2$, $\lambda=\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$, and
$\theta=\varphi^{-1}$.  Certified rational enclosures are
\[
 \alpha=\varphi^{-2}<\frac{191}{500},\qquad
 \beta=\varphi^{-1/2}<\frac{787}{1000},\qquad
 \gamma=\varphi^{-1}<\frac{619}{1000}.
\]
At $n=10$ their rational majorant is
\[
 \left(\frac{191}{500}\right)^{10}
 +10\left(\frac{787}{1000}\right)^{10}
 +10\left(\frac{619}{1000}\right)^{10}
 =0.9941325545\ldots<0.995<1,
\]
where both strict inequalities are checks in exact rational arithmetic.
The two sequences carrying a factor $n$ are already decreasing by this
point.  Thus the procedure proves, without enumerating words, that every
$n\ge10$ is realized as a least period.  This is a certificate, not a claim
that $10$ is the least possible conductor for the shift.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[Why the hypotheses matter]
Primitivity is used exactly at the strict spectral separation
$\theta<\lambda$.  The condition $\lambda>1$ gives a growing main term and
forces $\beta<1$.  A periodic irreducible matrix can have additional
peripheral eigenvalues, while a permutation cycle has $\lambda=1$ and only a
thin set of least periods.  The proof therefore does not extend by simply
removing either hypothesis.
\end{remark}
