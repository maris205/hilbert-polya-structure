\title[Unipotent shear on sublattices]{Unipotent Shear on Fixed-Index Sublattices:\ Complete Cycle Data and Valuation Staircases}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{Internal Stage 2 draft, 29 August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
For a positive integer $N$, let $\cL_N$ be the finite set of index-$N$
sublattices of $\Z^2$.  We determine the complete finite dynamics on
$\cL_N$ induced by the unipotent shear
$U=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Unique column Hermite coordinates turn the action into a disjoint union of
translations $b\mapsto b+N/a$ on $\Z/a\Z$, one for every divisor $a$ of
$N$.  This gives every cycle, every fixed-point count, and the finite
Artin--Mazur zeta function in closed form.  For $N=p^r$, the fixed counts
form a parity-sensitive valuation staircase and the zeta function has an
explicit sparse prime-power product.  Finally, the maximal cycle is unique
and has length $N$, so the full temporal data recover the index.  Exact
integer controls enumerate every canonical phase and trace its orbit for
$1\leq N\leq120$, with separate prime-power tests.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Fix $N\geq1$ and write
\[
 \cL_N=\{L\leq\Z^2:[\Z^2:L]=N\}.
\]
The integral matrix
\[
 U=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
\]
lies in $\operatorname{SL}_2(\Z)$ and therefore acts by the permutation
$T_N(L)=UL$ of $\cL_N$.  Although $U$ has infinite order on $\Z^2$, every
$T_N$ is a finite permutation.  The purpose of this note is to give its
complete temporal census.

The calculation has two ingredients.  First, column Hermite normal form
provides a canonical divisor-layer coordinate system; see, for example,
\citet[Chapter~2]{Cohen1993}.  Second, on each layer the shear is a cyclic
translation.  The resulting formulas simultaneously describe cycle
lengths, fixed points, and the Artin--Mazur zeta function
\citep{ArtinMazur1965}.  The prime-power specialization exposes a sharp
valuation staircase that is less visible in the undigested divisor sum.

Classical ownership includes Hermite normal form, the enumeration
$|\cL_N|=\sigma_1(N)$, and finite-index subgroup and subgroup-zeta theory
\citep{GrunewaldSegalSmith1988}.  Our bounded contribution is the temporal
package for this one fixed-index shear and its recovery consequence.  No
priority is claimed for Hermite normal form, subgroup enumeration,
subgroup-zeta theory, or Hecke correspondences.  The external literature
status of the exact package remains on hold.

\section{Hermite coordinates and the shear action}

For positive integers $a,c$ and $0\leq b<a$, put
\[
 H(a,b,c)=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&c\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 L(a,b,c)=H(a,b,c)\Z^2=\Z(a,0)+\Z(b,c).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Canonical coordinates and exact action]
\label{prop:hnf}
Every $L\in\cL_N$ has a unique expression
\[
 L=L(a,b,c),\qquad ac=N,\quad 0\leq b<a.
\]
In these coordinates,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:action}
 T_N\bigl(L(a,b,c)\bigr)=L(a,b+c\bmod a,c).
\end{equation}
Consequently $|\cL_N|=\sum_{a\mid N}a=\sigma_1(N)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let the image of $L$ under projection onto the second coordinate be
$c\Z$, and let
$L\cap(\Z\times\{0\})=a\Z\times\{0\}$.  Choose $(b,c)\in L$ and reduce
$b$ modulo $a$ into $0\leq b<a$.  If $(x,y)\in L$, then $y=kc$ for some
$k\in\Z$, and
$(x,y)-k(b,c)\in L\cap(\Z\times\{0\})$.  Thus $(a,0)$ and $(b,c)$
generate $L$.  Projection, intersection, and the residue class of $b$
show uniqueness.  The determinant of this basis is $ac$, hence $ac=N$.

Left multiplication acts on the displayed column basis as
\[
 U H(a,b,c)=
 \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&c\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}a&b+c\\0&c\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Reducing the first coordinate of the second generator modulo $a$ proves
\eqref{eq:action}.  For each $a\mid N$ there are exactly $a$ permitted
values of $b$, giving the final assertion.
\end{proof}

Thus the divisor $a$ labels an invariant layer, with $c=N/a$ and phase
$b\in\Z/a\Z$.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:gh}
 g_a=\gcd(a,N/a),\qquad h_a=\frac{a}{g_a}.
\end{equation}

\section{Complete temporal census}

Let $C_N(m)$ denote the number of cycles of exact length $m$, and let
$F_N(n)=\#\Fix(T_N^n)$.

\begin{theorem}[All cycles, fixed points, and zeta]
\label{thm:census}
For every $N,n,m\geq1$,
\begin{align}
 C_N(m)
   &=\sum_{\substack{a\mid N\\h_a=m}}g_a,                                      \label{eq:cycles}\\
 F_N(n)
   &=\sum_{\substack{a\mid N\\a\mid n(N/a)}}a
     =\sum_{\substack{a\mid N\\h_a\mid n}}a.                                \label{eq:fixed}
\end{align}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:mobius}
 C_N(m)=\frac1m\sum_{d\mid m}\mu(m/d)F_N(d),
\end{equation}
and the finite Artin--Mazur zeta function is the rational function
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:zeta}
 \zeta_N(z)
 :=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{F_N(n)}{n}z^n\right)
 =\prod_{a\mid N}(1-z^{h_a})^{-g_a}
 =\prod_{m\geq1}(1-z^m)^{-C_N(m)}.
\end{equation}
The products are finite, and
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:accounting}
 \sum_{m\geq1}mC_N(m)=\sigma_1(N).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On the $a$-layer, \cref{eq:action} is translation by $c=N/a$ on
$\Z/a\Z$.  Such a translation has $g_a=\gcd(a,c)$ orbits, each of length
$h_a=a/g_a$.  Summing these layer inventories proves \eqref{eq:cycles}
and \eqref{eq:accounting}.

The $n$th iterate translates by $nc$.  It fixes a phase if and only if
$a\mid nc$; when this happens, it fixes all $a$ phases in the layer.
Writing $a=g_aa'$ and $c=g_ac'$ with $\gcd(a',c')=1$ shows that
$a\mid nc$ is equivalent to $h_a=a'\mid n$, proving \eqref{eq:fixed}.

Every finite permutation satisfies
$F_N(n)=\sum_{m\mid n}mC_N(m)$.  M\"obius inversion gives
\eqref{eq:mobius}.  Finally, one cycle of length $m$ contributes
\[
 \exp\left(\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{z^{km}}{k}\right)=(1-z^m)^{-1}
\]
to the zeta function.  Multiplication over the cycles proves
\eqref{eq:zeta}.
\end{proof}

characteristic polynomial of a permutation matrix.  For example,

\section{Prime powers and the valuation staircase}

The divisor layers become particularly sparse when $N=p^r$.  Let
$v_p(n)$ be the $p$-adic valuation of $n$.

\begin{theorem}[Prime-power staircase]
\label{thm:primepower}
Let $p$ be prime and $r\geq1$.  For the layer $a=p^j$, $c=p^{r-j}$,
$0\leq j\leq r$, one has
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:prime-layer}
 g_j=p^{\min(j,r-j)},\qquad
 h_j=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&2j\leq r,\\
  p^{2j-r},&2j>r.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Hence, with
\[
 A_{p,r}=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}p^j
 =\frac{p^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor+1}-1}{p-1},
\]
the complete cycle inventory consists of $A_{p,r}$ fixed cycles and,
for each $j>r/2$, exactly $p^{r-j}$ cycles of length $p^{2j-r}$.
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:prime-zeta}
 \zeta_{p^r}(z)
 =(1-z)^{-A_{p,r}}
 \prod_{j=\lfloor r/2\rfloor+1}^{r}
       (1-z^{p^{2j-r}})^{-p^{r-j}}.
\end{equation}
For every $n\geq1$, put $s=v_p(n)$ and
\[
 J=\min\left\{r,\left\lfloor\frac{r+s}{2}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:staircase}
 F_{p^r}(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}p^j=\frac{p^{J+1}-1}{p-1}.
\end{equation}
Thus the fixed count changes only when the valuation crosses a threshold
of the same parity as $r$ and saturates at $\sigma_1(p^r)$ when $s\geq r$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substitution of $a=p^j$ and $c=p^{r-j}$ into \cref{eq:gh} gives
$g_j=p^{\min(j,r-j)}$ and then \eqref{eq:prime-layer}.  The layers with
$2j\leq r$ consist entirely of fixed cycles, whose total number is
$A_{p,r}$.  In every remaining layer, $g_j=p^{r-j}$ and
$h_j=p^{2j-r}$, proving the inventory and \eqref{eq:prime-zeta}.

By \eqref{eq:fixed}, the $j$th layer contributes its $p^j$ states precisely
when $p^j\mid n p^{r-j}$, or equivalently $2j\leq r+s$.  Therefore the
contributing layers are exactly $0\leq j\leq J$, which gives
\eqref{eq:staircase}.  If $s\geq r$, then $J=r$.
\end{proof}

The parity effect is exact: both the nontrivial period exponents in base $p$
and the fixed-count jump valuations $s=v_p(n)$ are $1,3,\ldots,r$ for odd
$r$ and $2,4,\ldots,r$ for even $r$.

\section{Temporal recovery}

The cycle census contains a rigid marker that is present for every index,
not only at prime powers.

\begin{theorem}[Recovery of the index]
\label{thm:recovery}
For every $N\geq1$, the maximal cycle length of $T_N$ is $N$, and there is
exactly one cycle of that length.  Consequently each of the following data
determines $N$:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item the complete cycle inventory $\{C_N(m)\}_{m\geq1}$;
 \item the complete fixed sequence $\{F_N(n)\}_{n\geq1}$;
 \item the formal zeta series $\zeta_N(z)$.
\end{enumerate}
For $N=p^r$, the recovered integer then determines both $p$ and $r$ by
unique factorization.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The layer $a=N$, $c=1$ has $g_N=1$ and $h_N=N$, so it is one $N$-cycle.
For every divisor layer,
$h_a=a/g_a\leq a\leq N$.  Equality $h_a=N$ forces $a=N$ and $g_a=1$,
so the displayed cycle is the unique maximal one.  Thus
\[
 N=\max\{m:C_N(m)>0\}.
\]
The fixed sequence determines the cycle inventory through
\cref{eq:mobius}.  The formal identity
\[
 z\frac{d}{dz}\log\zeta_N(z)=\sum_{n\geq1}F_N(n)z^n
\]
shows coefficient by coefficient that the zeta series determines every
$F_N(n)$; hence it also determines the inventory and $N$.
The case $N=1$ is included: there is one fixed lattice and the maximal
period is $1$.
\end{proof}

The conclusion deliberately uses temporal data.  Although
$|\cL_N|=\sigma_1(N)$ is a useful accounting identity, no injectivity claim
for the divisor-sum function is needed or made.

\section{Exact controls and scope}

The accompanying standard-library script constructs all HNF states, applies
$U$ to the two raw basis columns, reduces the off-diagonal residue, verifies
mutual lattice containment, enumerates the permutation cycles, and compares
the result with \cref{eq:cycles}.  For every
$1\leq N\leq120$ it also checks every fixed count through time $2N$,
M\"obius reconstruction through period $N$, state accounting, and maximal
cycle recovery.  Separate symbolic lanes test \cref{thm:primepower} for
$p\in\{2,3,5,7\}$ and $1\leq r\leq10$.  All calculations use exact
integers; no floating-point spectral inference enters a theorem.

The result is intentionally finite and rank two.  It does not address
higher-rank unipotent actions, random walks on sublattices, orbit statistics
as $N\to\infty$, or the Hecke-algebraic organization of related
correspondences.  Those would require distinct ownership audits and proof
engines.

\section{Conclusion}

The fixed-index sublattice action of a unipotent shear reduces exactly to
divisor-indexed cyclic translations.  This reduction gives a full temporal
census, turns prime powers into explicit valuation staircases, and exposes
the unique maximal orbit that recovers the index.  The formulas are closed
at finite $N$ and are independently checkable by exhaustive integer
enumeration in the accompanying artifact.
