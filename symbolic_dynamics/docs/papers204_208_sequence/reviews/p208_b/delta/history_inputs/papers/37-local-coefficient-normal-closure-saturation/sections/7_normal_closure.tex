\section{Normal-closure saturation erases the ledger}
\label{sec:normal-closure}

Let $F=F(u,v)$ and let
\begin{equation}
  N_r=\langle\!\langle \Rr\rangle\!\rangle_F,
  \qquad \Gr=F/N_r.
  \label{eq:normal-closure}
\end{equation}
Every closed path label maps to the identity in $\Gr$.  Its freely reduced
word therefore lies in $N_r$ and has a finite expression
\begin{equation}
  w=\prod_{j=1}^{m}a_j\Rr^{\varepsilon_j}a_j^{-1},
  \qquad \varepsilon_j\in\{+1,-1\}.
  \label{eq:normal-form-product}
\end{equation}
Hashimoto reduction only removes adjacent inverse letters and does not move a
word outside the normal closure.  By \cref{eq:inverse-transport}, each such
removal also leaves both parity holonomies unchanged.

Equation \eqref{eq:direct-characteristic} cancels each individual conjugate
and repetition, but matrix multiplication does not preserve spectral matching
factor by factor.  The nonzero value in \cref{eq:mixed-leak} is an explicit
counterexample to such an inference.

Now impose the complete inherited obligation: every finite mixed product in
\cref{eq:normal-form-product}, including inverses and repetitions, must have a
trivial graded primitive factor.  This obligation applies to every cyclically
reduced closed path and hence to every primitive root in
\cref{eq:graded-expansion}.  All connected coefficients vanish, so
\begin{equation}
  \log\Zgr(z)=0,
  \qquad
  \Zgr(z)=1.
  \label{eq:empty-graded-ledger}
\end{equation}

The flat branch reaches the same terminal state by a different route.  The
filled Cayley $2$-complex is contractible under the verified one-relator
monoid hypotheses \citep{GraySteinberg2022}.  A flat connection has identity
holonomy on each closed loop.  In ordinary rank $d$, the factor
$(1-t)^{-d}$ survives.  For graded ranks $d_+,d_-$, the ratio is
$(1-t)^{d_--d_+}$.  Only balanced parity cancels the relation, and then it
cancels every closed path.

Four ledgers must not be conflated:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the ordinary positive matrix factors of $T_+$ or $T_-$;
  \item the virtual graded ratio $\Zgr$;
  \item flat holonomy on the filled contractible complex;
  \item the non-flat shear connection on the unfilled graph.
\end{enumerate}
They share a presentation and marker notation but not positivity, flatness, or
cancellation semantics.

The theorem therefore proves an exact dichotomy.  Omitting mixed products
leaves relation leakage; including the complete normal closure erases the
whole closed ledger.  A proper intermediate class would need an independent,
source-canonical definition before coefficients are chosen.  The failed shear
representation cannot define that class after observing its residue.
