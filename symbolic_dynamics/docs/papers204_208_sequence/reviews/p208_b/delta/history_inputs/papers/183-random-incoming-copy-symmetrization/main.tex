\begin{abstract}
On a loopless labelled directed graph, choose a uniform vertex and replace its
outgoing star by a copy of its incoming star.  We determine the resulting
absorbing chain at every time.  The unordered pairs carrying unequal opposite
arcs form a conflict graph, and one update deletes precisely the conflict star
at the chosen vertex.  Hence the length-$t$ absorption probability is an exact
sum over independent vertex sets of the initial conflict graph.  The endpoint
law is finer: the local idempotents do not commute, and each resolved conflict
is oriented by which endpoint appears first in the history.  We turn this
first-occurrence order into a complete source--target kernel using Stirling
numbers.  Finally, for every target we count both labelled predecessor/action
pairs and distinct predecessor states; the two counts differ because all
admissible predecessor families meet at the target.  Exhaustion through four
vertices supplies 47,033 author-side regression assertions, not proof or
novelty evidence.  External ownership remains unresolved, so the manuscript
status is \texttt{HOLD\_EXTERNAL}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The chain and its subtraction boundary}

Fix $n\geq1$.  Let $\Dn$ be the loopless directed graphs on
$[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}$, represented by independent bits $A_{uv}\in\{0,1\}$
for $u\ne v$.  Both opposite arcs may be present.  For $v\in[n]$, define
$C_v:\Dn\to\Dn$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:update}
 (C_vA)_{vu}=A_{uv}\quad(u\ne v),\qquad
 (C_vA)_{ij}=A_{ij}\quad(i\ne v).
\end{equation}
Thus $v$ copies its incoming star to its outgoing star.  The Markov chain
chooses $v$ uniformly and has transition operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:markov}
 P=\frac1n\sum_{v=1}^n C_v.
\end{equation}

Reciprocal arcs are standard statistics of directed-network models.
Yin--Zhu study their equilibrium ensembles \cite{YinZhu2016}, while
Cirkovic--Wang--Resnick add reciprocal edges inside a growing preferential
attachment process \cite{CirkovicEtAl2023}.  Those models do not use the
fixed-carrier incoming-star copy \eqref{eq:update}.  General Markov chains
generated by finite semigroups are also established background
\cite{Brown2000}.  We assign reciprocity as a statistic, generic finite
semigroup walks, coupon support, Stirling enumeration, and independence
polynomials zero contribution credit.  The retained object is the conjunction
of conflict deletion, the independent-set absorption law, the ordered
source--target kernel, and the dual inverse census for the literal chain.
A later literal or equivalent owner triggers withdrawal; a bounded search
non-hit is not a novelty certificate.

The closest internal comparison is P179.  Its singleton-isolation maps are
commuting idempotents, so a history acts only through its support.  Here
$C_uC_v\ne C_vC_u$ whenever $\{u,v\}$ is a conflict: the endpoint appearing
first chooses the surviving common bit.  P145 toggles undirected vertex cuts,
P159 prunes through degree parity, and P177 is an additive Cayley walk.  None
has the monotone conflict deletion plus ordered endpoint kernel below.  These
differences are an internal collision subtraction, not an external priority
claim.

\section{Conflict deletion and recurrence}

Associate to $A\in\Dn$ the simple \emph{conflict graph}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conflict}
 \Conf(A)=\bigl([n],\{\{u,v\}:A_{uv}\ne A_{vu}\}\bigr).
\end{equation}
An isolated vertex is understood relative to this graph, including when
$n=1$.

\begin{lemma}[exact conflict deletion]\label{lem:deletion}
For every $A\in\Dn$ and $v\in[n]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deletion}
 E(\Conf(C_vA))=\{e\in E(\Conf(A)):v\notin e\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $C_v$ is idempotent, but the family $(C_v)_{v\in[n]}$ need not
commute.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For each $u\ne v$, equation \eqref{eq:update} makes the two arcs on
$\{u,v\}$ equal to the old bit $A_{uv}$.  A pair not incident with $v$ is
unchanged.  This proves \eqref{eq:deletion}; applying the same copy again has
no effect.  If $A_{uv}\ne A_{vu}$, applying $C_u$ first makes both bits equal
to $A_{vu}$, whereas applying $C_v$ first makes both equal to $A_{uv}$.
Thus the two possible first moves give different resolved pairs.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[recurrent states]\label{cor:recurrent}
The recurrent states are exactly the $2^{\binom n2}$ symmetric digraphs, and
each is fixed.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A symmetric digraph has no conflict and every update fixes it.  If a state has
a conflict edge, selecting either endpoint deletes that edge, and no update
can recreate any deleted conflict.  A path of positive probability therefore
leaves its conflict level and cannot return.  Such a state is transient in
the finite chain.
\end{proof}

\section{Independent-set absorption law}

Let $W=(w_1,\ldots,w_t)\in[n]^t$ be a vertex history, with support
$\supp(W)$ and missing set $M(W)=[n]\setminus\supp(W)$.  Write $\tau$ for
the first epoch at which the state is symmetric.  Let
$\Stir{t}{r}$ be a Stirling number of the second kind, with
$\Stir00=1$.

\begin{lemma}[history residue]\label{lem:history}
For every initial state $A$ and history $W$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:history}
 E\bigl(\Conf(C_{w_t}\cdots C_{w_1}A)\bigr)
 =E\bigl(\Conf(A)[M(W)]\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:deletion} at each epoch.  Selecting a vertex removes all
incident conflicts, and repeating a selected vertex removes nothing further.
The edges left after the complete history are precisely those whose two
endpoints were never selected, which are the edges induced by $M(W)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[all-time absorption CDF]\label{thm:absorption}
For $A\in\Dn$ and every integer $t\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:absorption}
 \Pr_A(\tau\leq t)=\frac1{n^t}
 \sum_{M\in\mathcal I(\Conf(A))}
 (n-|M|)!\Stir{t}{n-|M|},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal I(H)$ is the family of independent vertex sets of $H$.
The formula includes $t=0$ and the already symmetric case.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:history}, absorption by time $t$ is equivalent to
$\Conf(A)[M(W)]$ having no edge, or to $M(W)$ being independent.  For a fixed
missing set $M$, the histories with exact support $[n]\setminus M$ are the
surjections from $[t]$ onto $n-|M|$ prescribed labels.  Their number is
$(n-|M|)!\Stir{t}{n-|M|}$.  Different missing sets define disjoint history
classes.  Summing their counts and dividing by the $n^t$ equiprobable words
proves \eqref{eq:absorption}.  At $t=0$, only $M=[n]$ can contribute, and it
does so exactly when the initial conflict graph is empty.
\end{proof}

Thus the temporal law remembers the entire independent-set inventory of the
initial conflict graph, not only its number of edges or nonisolated vertices.

\section{First-occurrence endpoint kernel}

For $S\subseteq[n]$, let $\pi=(\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_r)$ be a total order of $S$.
Give a vertex outside $S$ rank $+\infty$.  Define $E_{S,\pi}(A)$ pairwise as
follows.  A nonconflict remains unchanged.  A conflict $\{u,v\}$ disjoint
from $S$ also remains unchanged.  Otherwise let $e$ be its finite-rank
endpoint of smaller rank and let $o$ be the other endpoint; replace both
opposite arcs by the old incoming bit $A_{oe}$.

\begin{theorem}[every source--target multiplicity]\label{thm:endpoint}
Let $N_t(A,B)$ be the number of histories $W\in[n]^t$ sending $A$ to $B$.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint}
 N_t(A,B)=
 \sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[n],\ \pi\in\mathfrak S(S)\\
                  E_{S,\pi}(A)=B}}
 \Stir{t}{|S|}.
\end{equation}
Hence $P^t(A,B)=N_t(A,B)/n^t$ for every labelled source, target, and time.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix a history.  Lemma~\ref{lem:deletion} shows that the first selected
endpoint of a conflict makes its two arcs equal; later updates preserve their
equality.  Equation \eqref{eq:update} shows that the common value is the old
arc entering that first endpoint.  The history endpoint is therefore exactly
$E_{S,\pi}(A)$ for its support $S$ and first-occurrence order $\pi$.

It remains to count histories with a prescribed pair $(S,\pi)$.  Partition
the time positions according to which of the $r=|S|$ labels occurs there.
Ordering these nonempty blocks by their least positions and labelling them
$\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_r$ is a bijection from set partitions of $[t]$ into $r$
blocks to histories with that first-occurrence order.  There are
$\Stir{t}{r}$ such histories.  The support/order classes are disjoint, so
summing those producing $B$ proves \eqref{eq:endpoint}.
\end{proof}

\section{Complete one-step inverse census}

For a target $B$, let $k(B)$ be the number of isolated vertices of
$\Conf(B)$.

\begin{theorem}[labelled actions versus distinct sources]\label{thm:fibres}
The number of labelled pairs $(A,v)\in\Dn\times[n]$ satisfying $C_vA=B$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:labelled-fibre}
 k(B)2^{n-1}.
\end{equation}
The number of distinct source states is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distinct-fibre}
 \#\{A:\exists v,\ C_vA=B\}=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&k(B)=0,\\
 1+k(B)(2^{n-1}-1),&k(B)>0.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $C_vA=B$, all pairs incident with $v$ are symmetric in $B$, so $v$ must
be isolated in $\Conf(B)$.  Conversely, fix such a vertex.  Every arc not
leaving $v$ is forced to equal its target bit, while the $n-1$ arcs leaving
$v$ are overwritten and may be chosen freely.  This gives $2^{n-1}$ sources
for each of the $k(B)$ labelled actions, proving
\eqref{eq:labelled-fibre}.

Each admissible-vertex source family contains $B$.  Suppose a source belongs
to the families for two distinct vertices $v$ and $w$.  Membership in the
$w$-family forces every arc leaving $v$ except possibly $v\to w$ to its
target value; the $v$-family gives the analogous statement for arcs leaving
$w$.  On the remaining pair, the $v$-family fixes $w\to v$ and the
$w$-family fixes $v\to w$.  Thus the source is $B$.  The families have no
other intersections, so their union contains one shared state plus
$2^{n-1}-1$ new states per isolated vertex.  If there is no isolated vertex,
there is no admissible action.  This proves \eqref{eq:distinct-fibre}.
\end{proof}

For $n=1$, the unique empty digraph is fixed, has one labelled predecessor
pair and one distinct source; all formulas include this boundary.
