\title[Odd-component complementation]{Odd-Component Complementation:\\
Pointwise Split Clocks and an All-Depth Labelled Census}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{}
\subjclass[2020]{05C25, 05A15, 37B15, 05C30}
\keywords{graph complementation, finite dynamical system, transient depth,
labelled generating function, cograph, dynamical zeta function}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
On a finite simple labelled graph, synchronously complement the subgraph
induced by every connected component of odd order and leave every even
component unchanged. We show that the resulting finite dynamics is
linearized pointwise by a parity-pruned component/co-component split tree.
Its active height is the exact entrance time into the recurrent set, all
periods divide two, and the largest transient depth on \(n\) vertices is the
sharp value \(\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\). We then derive a recursive labelled
exponential generating function for every cumulative and exact depth layer.
It yields the fixed and recurrent censuses, the number of two-cycles, and the
finite-order dynamical zeta function. Gallai decomposition, cographs and
cotrees, labelled SET calculus, and connected-graph enumeration are treated
as background. The direct-owner search is bounded; no novelty, priority, or
external-release claim is made.
\end{abstract}

\section{The map and the subtraction boundary}

All graphs are finite, simple, and labelled. For a graph \(G\), define
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(G)=\bigsqcup_{C\in\Comp(G)}
 \begin{cases}
   \overline{G[C]},& |C|\text{ odd},\\
   G[C],& |C|\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}                                             \label{eq:map}
\end{equation}
Thus no edge is added between distinct components, and every odd component
is complemented synchronously. We regard \(K_1\) as connected and
co-connected. The \emph{depth} of a state is its first entrance time into
the recurrent set.

Component/co-component decomposition belongs to classical modular
decomposition, beginning with Gallai's work \cite{Gallai1967}. Cographs,
their cotrees, and recognition are likewise classical
\cite{CorneilLerchsStewart1981,CorneilPerlStewart1985}; modern labelled
cotree enumeration appears, for example, in \cite{Stufler2021,BassinoEtAl2022}.
Labelled SET constructions and connected-graph enumeration are standard
\cite{BergeronLabelleLeroux1998,HararyPalmer1973}. None of those facts,
nor the co-connected count used below, receives contribution credit here.
Our residual question is temporal: what is the exact clock of the literal
parity-triggered map \eqref{eq:map}, and can every clock layer be counted?

\begin{lemma}[refinement]\label{lem:refinement}
The component partition of \(\Phi(G)\) refines that of \(G\). Every even
component is fixed forever. If \(H\) is odd and connected, then either
\(\overline H\) is connected and \(H\leftrightarrow\overline H\), or the
components of \(\overline H\) are permanent separate blocks after one step.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:map} never creates a cross-component edge. An even block
is copied unchanged. On an odd connected block \(H\), one step produces
\(\overline H\). If that graph is connected, the next step complements it
back to \(H\). If it is disconnected, no later step can join its components,
so they evolve independently.
\end{proof}

\section{The pointwise split clock}

The classical component/co-component tree is useful here only after a
parity pruning that records which blocks remain dynamically active.

\begin{definition}\label{def:tree}
For a connected odd graph \(H\), build its \emph{parity-pruned split tree} as
follows. If \(\overline H\) is connected, stop. Otherwise create the
connected components of \(\overline H\) as children; declare every even child
a leaf and recurse only on odd children. Let \(D(H)\) be the largest number
of split nodes on a root-to-leaf branch. Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
D(H)=
\begin{cases}
0,&\overline H\text{ connected},\\
1+\max\{D(C):C\in\Comp(\overline H),\ |C|\text{ odd}\},
   &\overline H\text{ disconnected},
\end{cases}                                               \label{eq:D}
\end{equation}
where the maximum of the empty set is \(0\). For arbitrary \(G\), put
\(D(G)=\max D(C)\) over its odd components, with empty maximum \(0\).
\end{definition}

This is not asserted to be a new cotree or modular decomposition. It is a
pointwise clock obtained by pruning the familiar split operation according
to the update parity.

\begin{theorem}[exact entrance clock]\label{thm:clock}
For every graph \(G\),
\[
                         \depth(G)=D(G).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Even components are recurrent fixed blocks. Distinct odd components evolve
independently, so the entrance time of their disjoint union is the maximum
of their entrance times. It remains to consider an odd connected \(H\).
If \(\overline H\) is connected, \(H\) already lies on a cycle. Otherwise
the first step splits \(H\) into the components of \(\overline H\). Its even
children freeze, while its odd children require exactly their individual
entrance times. Hence the entrance time satisfies precisely \eqref{eq:D};
induction on \(|H|\) finishes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[periodic classification]\label{cor:period}
Every eventual period is \(1\) or \(2\). A graph is recurrent exactly when
every nontrivial odd component is co-connected. It is fixed exactly when
every component is either a singleton or has even order. Every other
recurrent graph lies on a genuine two-cycle.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem \ref{thm:clock} reduces every state to leaves of the split tree.
Even leaves are fixed. A singleton is fixed by complementation, whereas a
nontrivial odd co-connected component alternates with its connected
complement. A disjoint union synchronizes these periods.
\end{proof}

\section{The sharp global clock}

\begin{theorem}[sharp maximum depth]\label{thm:sharp}
Among all graphs on \(n\geq1\) vertices,
\begin{equation}
 \max_{|V(G)|=n}\depth(G)=\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor.
                                                               \label{eq:sharp}
\end{equation}
The value for \(n=0\) is \(0\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Follow an active branch from an odd parent \(H\) to an odd child \(C\). The
complement \(\overline H\) has at least two components, and the vertices
outside \(C\) have even positive total order because both \(|H|\) and \(|C|\)
are odd. Thus \(|C|\leq |H|-2\). Every split consumes at least two vertices
along the branch, giving \(D(H)\leq (|H|-1)/2\). Taking the maximum over odd
components proves the upper bound for every \(G\).

For sharpness, let \(H_1=K_1\) and recursively set
\begin{equation}
                  H_{2r+1}=\overline{\,H_{2r-1}\sqcup K_2\,}.
\end{equation}
The complement of \(H_{2r+1}\) has the odd child \(H_{2r-1}\) and the even
child \(K_2\), so \(D(H_{2r+1})=1+D(H_{2r-1})=r\). This attains
\eqref{eq:sharp} for odd order. For even \(n=2r+2\), the graph
\(H_{2r+1}\sqcup K_1\) has depth \(r\), proving sharpness there as well.
\end{proof}

The bound is structural, not an extrapolation from the finite census in
\cref{sec:census}.

\section{All-depth labelled generating functions}

Let \(c_n\) be the number of connected labelled graphs on \([n]\), and work
throughout with formal exponential generating functions. Put
\begin{equation}
 C_{\mathrm e}(x)=\sum_{\substack{n\geq2\\n\ {\rm even}}}
 c_n\frac{x^n}{n!}.
\end{equation}
For odd \(n\), let \(q_n\) count connected graphs whose complements are also
connected, and define
\begin{equation}
 q_1=1,\qquad q_n=2c_n-2^{\binom n2}\quad(n\geq3\text{ odd}),\qquad
 Q(x)=\sum_{n\ {\rm odd}}q_n\frac{x^n}{n!}.               \label{eq:Q}
\end{equation}
Indeed, a graph and its complement cannot both be disconnected when
\(n\geq2\), so \eqref{eq:Q} is inclusion--exclusion; this identity is
zero-credit background. For a series \(A(x)\), write
\(\Odd A=(A(x)-A(-x))/2\).

\begin{theorem}[complete depth recurrence]\label{thm:egf}
Let \(O_t(x)\) be the EGF of connected odd graphs of depth at most \(t\), and
let \(F_t(x)\) be the EGF of all graphs of depth at most \(t\). Then
\begin{align}
 O_0(x)&=Q(x),                                             \label{eq:O0}\\
 O_t(x)&=Q(x)+\Odd\!\left(
  \exp(C_{\mathrm e}(x)+O_{t-1}(x))-1-C_{\mathrm e}(x)-O_{t-1}(x)
                         \right),\qquad t\geq1,           \label{eq:Ot}\\
 F_t(x)&=\exp(C_{\mathrm e}(x)+O_t(x)).                   \label{eq:Ft}
\end{align}
Consequently, for \(t\geq1\), the number of labelled \(n\)-vertex graphs of
exact depth \(t\) is
\begin{equation}
 n![x^n]\bigl(F_t(x)-F_{t-1}(x)\bigr),                    \label{eq:layers}
\end{equation}
while \(n![x^n]F_0(x)\) is the recurrent count.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Depth-zero connected odd graphs are precisely the co-connected ones, giving
\eqref{eq:O0}. Consider a positive-depth connected odd graph \(H\).
Complementation bijects it with a disconnected labelled graph whose
connected components have odd total order. Its even components are
unrestricted connected graphs, counted by \(C_{\mathrm e}\); by
\eqref{eq:D}, every odd component has depth at most \(t-1\), counted by
\(O_{t-1}\). The labelled SET construction gives the exponential in
\eqref{eq:Ot}; subtracting \(1+C_{\mathrm e}+O_{t-1}\) forces at least two
components, and odd extraction forces odd total order. Adding the
co-connected base class \(Q\) proves \eqref{eq:Ot}.

An arbitrary graph of depth at most \(t\) is a SET of unrestricted even
connected components and odd connected components counted by \(O_t\).
The exponential formula gives \eqref{eq:Ft}, and subtraction of successive
cumulative classes gives \eqref{eq:layers}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[fixed census]\label{prop:fixed}
The EGF for fixed graphs is
\begin{equation}
 F_{\mathrm{fix}}(x)=\exp(x+C_{\mathrm e}(x)).            \label{eq:Ffix}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{cor:period}, the allowed connected components are singletons and
arbitrary even connected graphs. Apply the labelled SET construction.
\end{proof}

\section{Recurrent and zeta census}\label{sec:census}

Write
\[
 f_n=n![x^n]F_{\mathrm{fix}}(x),\qquad
 r_n=n![x^n]F_0(x).
\]
Thus \(f_n\) and \(r_n\) are respectively the fixed and recurrent state
counts on the finite state space of labelled graphs on \([n]\).

\begin{corollary}[two-cycles and zeta]\label{cor:zeta}
The number of genuine two-cycles is \((r_n-f_n)/2\). Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \#\Fix(\Phi^k)=
 \begin{cases}f_n,&k\text{ odd},\\r_n,&k\text{ even},\end{cases}
 \qquad
 \zeta_n(z)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{\#\Fix(\Phi^k)}{k}z^k\right)
 =(1-z)^{-f_n}(1-z^2)^{-(r_n-f_n)/2}.                    \label{eq:zeta}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Corollary \ref{cor:period} partitions the recurrent set into \(f_n\)
one-cycles and \((r_n-f_n)/2\) two-cycles. Transient states are fixed by no
positive iterate. Hence the displayed fixed-iterate counts hold. Splitting
the defining Artin--Mazur series \cite{ArtinMazur1965} into odd and even
powers and applying the logarithmic series gives \eqref{eq:zeta}.
\end{proof}

\section{Scope and conclusion}

The parity-pruned split tree simultaneously exposes the pointwise and
enumerative structure of odd-component complementation. It gives an exact
entrance clock, the sharp maximum \(\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\), and a recursive
EGF for every temporal layer. The recurrent and fixed specializations then
produce the two-cycle and zeta censuses without further orbit analysis.

The contribution boundary is deliberately narrow. Gallai decomposition,
cographs and cotrees, connected labelled graph counts, and labelled species
operators remain zero-credit background. The direct-owner search was
bounded rather than exhaustive, and the finite verifier stops at order six.
Accordingly, this manuscript makes no novelty or priority assertion and is
held from external release pending a broader owner audit.
