\section{Three duality tests}

The determinant orientation in \cref{thm:exterior} does not explain why a
completed object should relate $s$ and $1-s$. We test three constructions
that remain attached to the same symbolic source.

\subsection{Stable/unstable symbolic reversal}

Let $V^s$ and $V^u$ be stable and unstable copies of the tensor-atom space,
and let $J:V^s\to V^u$ be induced by reversal of the corresponding two-sided
full shifts. Reversal preserves alphabet size and entropy.

\begin{proposition}[Reversal preserves the spectral variable]
\label{prop:reversal}
The natural chain transfer is
\[
  \cL_s\oplus\cL_s,
  \qquad J\cL_s=\cL_sJ.
\]
Its ordinary determinant is $\zeta(s)^{-2}$ and its superdeterminant is one
in $\RePart s>1$. Replacing the unstable block by $\cL_{1-s}$ breaks the
chain identity except at $s=1/2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both copies of $F_p$ have entropy $\log p$, so both diagonal weights are
$p^{-s}$. Equality $J\cL_s=\cL_{1-s}J$ would require
$p^{-s}=p^{-(1-s)}$ for every atom, which holds only at the center.
\end{proof}

There is also no ordinary Fredholm overlap: $\cL_s$ is trace class for
$\RePart s>1$, while $\cL_{1-s}$ is trace class for $\RePart s<0$.
Time reversal therefore supplies the familiar transpose/reversal invariance
of a symbolic determinant, not the Riemann reflection.

\subsection{Tensor group completion}

The group completion of the object monoid is
\[
  G=M^{\mathrm{gp}}\cong\mathbb Q_{>0}^{\times}
  \cong\bigoplus_p\mathbb Z.
\]
Inversion sends $q^{-s}$ to $q^s=q^{-(-s)}$.

\begin{proposition}[Inversion is parity-even]
\label{prop:inversion}
For every monoidal grading
$\varepsilon:G\to\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$,
\[
  \varepsilon(g^{-1})=-\varepsilon(g)=\varepsilon(g).
\]
Thus group inversion cannot flip determinant parity and naturally implements
$s\mapsto-s$, not $s\mapsto1-s$.
\end{proposition}

Centering $u=s-1/2$ rewrites
\[
 p^{-s}=p^{-1/2}p^{-u},
 \qquad
 p^{-(1-s)}=p^{-1/2}p^u.
\]
The shared factor $p^{-1/2}$ is a half-density character. Entropy permits
one to write it, but the bare tensor skeleton contains no principle that
selects the coefficient $1/2$. Choosing it because the target functional
equation has that center would be circular.

\subsection{Adversarial Schatten regularization}

We now grant the missing half-density pairing and ask whether regularized
determinants could rescue the critical strip. This is an obstruction test,
not a promoted candidate.

\begin{theorem}[First shared Schatten strip]
\label{thm:schatten}
For $q\ge1$,
\[
 \cL_s\in\cS_q\iff q\RePart s>1,
 \qquad
 \cL_{1-s}\in\cS_q\iff q(1-\RePart s)>1.
\]
There is no common $\cS_1$ or $\cS_2$ domain. The first integer order with
an overlap is $q=3$, on
\[
 \frac13<\RePart s<\frac23.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The $q$-th singular-value sum is $\sum_p p^{-q\RePart s}$. It converges
exactly when $q\RePart s>1$. Applying the same calculation to $1-s$ gives
the second condition. The common interval is
$1/q<\RePart s<1-1/q$, which is nonempty first for $q=3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Regularized critical-strip obstruction]
\label{thm:det3}
On $1/3<\RePart s<2/3$, define
\[
 D_3(s)
 =\det\nolimits_3(I-\cL_s)
  \det\nolimits_3(I-\cL_{1-s}).
\]
Then $D_3(s)=D_3(1-s)$, but $D_3$ is zero-free throughout this strip and
\[
 \log D_3(s)
 =-\sum_{r\ge3}\frac1r
  \sum_p\left(p^{-rs}+p^{-r(1-s)}\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The regularized determinant identity starts at trace power $r=3$.
Both diagonal blocks have spectral radius less than one in the open strip,
so none of their regularized factors vanishes. Reflection swaps the two
factors.
\end{proof}

The symmetry is exact but sterile. It has removed the $r=1$ prime trace and
the $r=2$ prime-square trace. Restoring them requires counterterms with no
common nuclear definition near the critical line. The first regularization
that enters the desired region therefore loses the arithmetic divisor it was
meant to complete.
