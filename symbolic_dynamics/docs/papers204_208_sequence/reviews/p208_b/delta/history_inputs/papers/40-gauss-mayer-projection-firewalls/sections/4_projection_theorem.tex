\section{The three-projection firewall}
\label{sec:firewall}

\subsection{Exact comparison target}

The positive trace-series comparison object is the reciprocal determinant
$\Dforty(s,u)^{-1}$.  For a primitive pair word $w$ of pair length $k$, its
source repetition-$r$ coefficient in $-\log\Dforty$ is
\begin{equation}
  c_{w,r}^{\mathrm{src}}(s,u)
  =\frac{u^{2kr}d_w^{rs}}{r(1-d_w^r)}.
  \label{eq:source-coefficient}
\end{equation}
Under a hypothetical one-to-one assignment $w_p\leftrightarrow p$ to
rational primes, the marked target would be
\begin{equation}
  D_{\mathrm{prime}}(s,u)^{-1}
  =\prod_p\left(1-u^{2k(w_p)}p^{-s}\right)^{-1},
  \label{eq:prime-target}
\end{equation}
whose repetition coefficient is
\begin{equation}
  c_{p,r}^{\mathrm{tar}}(s,u)
  =\frac{u^{2k(w_p)r}p^{-rs}}{r}.
  \label{eq:target-coefficient}
\end{equation}
At $u=1$, \eqref{eq:prime-target} is the ordinary reciprocal Euler product
for the Riemann zeta function.  The exponent $2k(w_p)r$ is inherited from the
source digit marker; it is not replaced by $r$ after comparison.

For $P\in\{P_t,P_\Delta,P_N\}$, let $\mathrm{ProjectionGO}(P)$ mean the
conjunction, on the full intrinsic primitive-pair ledger, of:

\begin{itemize}
  \item rational-integer and rational-prime support, with infinitely many
  labels and no composite source species;
  \item exactly one primitive source factor for each target prime;
  \item target powers under every temporal repetition;
  \item unchanged derivative clock and original digit marker;
  \item equality of \eqref{eq:source-coefficient} and
  \eqref{eq:target-coefficient}, including sign, orientation, phase, and
  logarithmic-derivative amplitude;
  \item a declared invariant or reducing operator sector owning the selected
  traces, multiplicities, and marker degrees; and
  \item separation from the mandatory arithmetic and dynamical controls.
\end{itemize}

Thus the rational-prime projection claim is the existential statement
$\bigvee_P\mathrm{ProjectionGO}(P)$.  It is not enough for one theorem clause
to fail one projection; every projection conjunction must be covered.

\subsection{Main theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Three-projection and ownership firewall]
\label{thm:main}
Let $w$ range over cyclic primitive words for the pair shift $\rho$, with monodromy
\eqref{eq:monodromy}, and let the source operator and domain be those of
Section~\ref{sec:source}.  Then:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item the pair return, primitive-pair ledger, monodromy, Gauss branch,
  derivative roof, digit marker, raw $\Ks^k$ summand, and Fredholm determinant
  are one typed source construction;
  \item $P_t$, $P_\Delta$, and $P_N$ are the complete frozen projection
  family, and no member satisfies $\mathrm{ProjectionGO}$;
  \item no integer-valued member simultaneously preserves the exact source
  clock and temporal powers;
  \item the frozen untwisted operator schema declares no reducing owner for
  any rational-prime scalar postselection.
\end{enumerate}

The theorem is relative to these three maps and this operator schema.  It
does not assert universal nonexistence across twists or changed objects, an
objectwise pair/geodesic equivalence, or novelty of any collision witness.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof overview]
The positive construction in item~1 follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:pair-fredholm} and the branch--matrix bridge; the full
derivation is in Appendices~\ref{app:branch-proof} and
\ref{app:fredholm-proof}.  The projection coverage is summarized below.

For every source word, $t\ge3$ and
\begin{equation}
  \Delta=t^2-4=(t-2)(t+2).
  \label{eq:delta-factorization}
\end{equation}
At $t=3$ this is $5$; for $t>3$ both factors exceed one.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}
  (t-1)^2<\Delta<t^2,
  \label{eq:square-interval}
\end{equation}
so $\Delta$ is nonsquare.  The reciprocal numbers $P_N=\lambda^2>1$ and
$d=\lambda^{-2}\in(0,1)$ are the two roots of
\begin{equation}
  x^2-(t^2-2)x+1=0.
  \label{eq:norm-polynomial}
\end{equation}
Its discriminant $t^2\Delta$ is nonsquare, proving $P_N\notin\mathbb Q$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:square-interval} also gives $\lambda>t-1$, hence
$P_N=\lambda^2>t$ for $t\ge3$.  Therefore $T=\log P_N>\log P_t$.  Every
integer $t\ge3$ is realized by
\begin{equation}
  w_t=((1,t-2)),\qquad
  M(w_t)=\begin{pmatrix}t-1&1\\t-2&1\end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:trace-family}
\end{equation}
Since $\log\lambda(t)^2/\log t\to2$ but $\lambda(t)^2<t^2$ at every finite
$t$, no constant rescales $\log t$ to the source clock on all realized
traces.

Cayley--Hamilton gives, for $q_r=\tr(M^r)$,
\begin{equation}
  q_0=2,\quad q_1=t,\quad q_r=tq_{r-1}-q_{r-2}.
  \label{eq:trace-recurrence}
\end{equation}
Thus $q_2=t^2-2\ne t^2$, and
$\Delta(M^2)=t^2\Delta(M)\ne\Delta(M)^2$.  In contrast,
$P_N(w^r)=P_N(w)^r$ and $T(w)=\log P_N(w)$ exactly.  The norm therefore
passes powers and clock but fails integer support.  Finally, all three maps
are functions of $t$ and collide on the explicit species below, while
\eqref{eq:source-coefficient} retains the stability factor
$(1-d_w^r)^{-1}$ even under the formal assignment $p=d_w^{-1}$.  The
operator-owner conclusion is proved in Section~\ref{sec:ownership}.  These
facts make each of the three complete conjunctions false.  Detailed algebra
appears in Appendix~\ref{app:algebra-proof}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Explicit in-domain collision classes}

The six matrices in Table~\ref{tab:collisions} are computed in the stored
left-to-right digit convention.  One-pair words are pair-primitive; the
two-pair trace-$10$ word is not a proper pair power.  Because reversal is not
part of the object quotient, the trace-$4$ reversal phases are distinct
primitive factors.  The trace-$6$ and trace-$10$ pairs are not
reversal-related, and the last pair also crosses pair length.
