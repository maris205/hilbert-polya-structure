\section{From binary fluctuations to the Gamma factor}
\label{sec:gamma}

Let $(X_j)_{j\ge0}$ be the sign observable along the Parry process of $F_2$,
so the $X_j$ are independent and uniform on $\{-1,1\}$. Put
\[
 S_N=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}X_j,
 \qquad Y_N=\frac{S_N}{\sqrt{2\pi N}}.
\]
We take $N$ odd. This removes the finite-$N$ atom at zero, which would make
negative moments undefined even though the limiting singularity is
integrable.

\begin{theorem}[Binary Archimedean limit]
\label{thm:gamma}
For odd $N$ and $\RePart s>0$, define
\[
 M_N(s)=\mathbb E|Y_N|^{s-1}.
\]
Then, locally uniformly on the half-plane $\RePart s>0$,
\[
 M_N(s)\longrightarrow
 \int_{\RR}|x|^{s-1}e^{-\pi x^2}\dd x
 =\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch with the uniformity mechanism]
The specialization in \cref{thm:tilted-trace} gives the central limit
theorem. A lattice local
central limit estimate gives uniform Gaussian approximation on bounded
windows, while the binomial tail estimate controls large $|Y_N|$. Near zero,
the local estimate implies a uniform small-ball bound
$\mathbb P(|Y_N|\le x)\le Cx+C N^{-1/2}$. For every compact
$C\Subset\{\RePart s>0\}$ this controls
$|Y_N|^{\RePart s-1}(1+|\log|Y_N||^k)$ uniformly for the finitely many
$s$-derivatives needed by normal-family convergence. Truncation at small and
large radii therefore upgrades weak convergence to locally uniform Mellin
convergence. Finally, $u=\pi x^2$ gives
\[
 2\int_0^\infty x^{s-1}e^{-\pi x^2}\dd x
 =\pi^{-s/2}\int_0^\infty u^{s/2-1}e^{-u}\dd u.
\]
\end{proof}

The $1/2$ is now an internal fluctuation exponent, not a number copied from
the target critical line. The constant $\pi$ is the normalization for the
self-dual Gaussian density $e^{-\pi x^2}$ under Fourier transform. Changing
Gaussian convention would multiply the Mellin transform by a zero-free
exponential in $s$, but we freeze the self-dual convention before comparison.

\subsection{Representation-dimensional controls}

For the uniform $q$-shift, place its $q$ symbols at the vertices
$v_1,\ldots,v_q$ of a centered regular simplex in
$E_q\cong\RR^{d}$, $d=q-1$, with
$\|v_j\|=1$ and $\langle v_j,v_k\rangle=-1/d$ for $j\ne k$.
This uses the full standard representation and does not select a favorable
scalar projection.

\begin{proposition}[Radial $q$-symbol control]
\label{prop:qcontrol}
If $V_j$ are independent uniform simplex vertices and
\[
 R_N=\frac{\|\sum_{j=1}^NV_j\|}{\sqrt{2\pi N}},
\]
then, for $\RePart s>1-d$ where the limiting moment is locally integrable,
\[
 \mathbb E R_N^{s-1}\longrightarrow
 M_d(s):=(\pi d)^{-(s-1)/2}
 \frac{\Gamma((d+s-1)/2)}{\Gamma(d/2)}.
\]
For $d=1$ this is $\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$; for every $d>1$ the Gamma shift
is different.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The simplex covariance is $I_d/d$, so
$\sqrt{d/N}\sum_jV_j$ converges to a standard $d$-dimensional Gaussian $Z$.
The radial moment formula
\[
 \mathbb E\|Z\|^{s-1}
 =2^{(s-1)/2}\frac{\Gamma((d+s-1)/2)}{\Gamma(d/2)}
\]
and the scale $R_N=\|\sqrt{d/N}\sum_jV_j\|/\sqrt{2\pi d}$ give the claim.
\end{proof}

This control restores a meaningful part of binary specificity: the canonical
full standard representation has the exact Archimedean shape only in
dimension one. It does not defeat every genericity challenge. A biased
binary chain, after centering and variance normalization, has the same
one-dimensional Gaussian limit; an arbitrarily selected scalar projection of
$F_q$ does too. Those choices are not the multiplicity-one
$S_q$-representation supplied by the source, but their success means the
Gamma limit alone cannot carry the arithmetic claim.

\subsection{The same-source Mellin--Fredholm factorization}

The stationary and sign channels are specializations of the same tilted
trace family $H(z)$. Combining \cref{thm:euler,thm:gamma} therefore defines,
on their common initial domain,
\[
 \mathfrak Z_{\rm SD}(s)
 :=\left(\lim_{N\to\infty}M_N(s)\right)
   \Ber(I-\cA_{s,0})
 =\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),
 \qquad \RePart s>1.                       \tag{4.1}
\]
Equation (4.1) is an exact factorization on that half-plane. It is stronger
than coordinate splicing because both transforms descend from the same
multiplicity-free $S_2$ source and the same matrix trace deformation. It is
weaker than a unified determinant because the Mellin limit and Berezinian are
still distinct analytic operations. No continuation or functional equation
is inferred from their product.
