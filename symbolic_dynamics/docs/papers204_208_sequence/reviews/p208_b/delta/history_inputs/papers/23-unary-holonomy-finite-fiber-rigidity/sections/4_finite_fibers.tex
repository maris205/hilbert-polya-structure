\section{Finite-fiber periodicity}
\label{sec:finite}

\begin{definition}
A set \(E\subseteq\N\) is \emph{ultimately periodic} if there are
\(N,m\ge1\) such that
\[
        n\ge N
        \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
        [n\in E\Longleftrightarrow n+m\in E].
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Prime-only tail classes]
\label{lem:primeperiod}
An ultimately periodic subset of the rational primes is finite.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that \(E\subseteq\Pp\) is infinite, with tail threshold \(N\) and
period \(m\).  Choose \(p\in E\), \(p\ge N\).  Repeated periodicity places
\(p+tm\) in \(E\) for every \(t\ge0\).  Taking \(t=p\) gives
\[
        p+pm=p(1+m),
\]
which is composite.  This contradicts \(E\subseteq\Pp\).
\end{proof}

The witness in \cref{lem:primeperiod} is elementary.  It needs no prime
number theorem and proves the stronger statement that \emph{every} infinite
prime-only ultimately periodic support is impossible.

\begin{lemma}[Finite-semigroup powers]
\label{lem:semigrouppower}
Let \(S\) be a finite semigroup and \(a\in S\).  There are
\(\mu,\lambda\ge1\) such that
\[
        a^{n+\lambda}=a^n\qquad(n\ge\mu).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Two terms among
\[
        a,a^2,\ldots,a^{|S|+1}
\]
agree, say \(a^i=a^j\) with \(i<j\).  Right multiplication by \(a^r\)
gives \(a^{i+r}=a^{j+r}\) for every \(r\ge0\).  Set
\(\mu=i\) and \(\lambda=j-i\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed finite-semigroup no-go]
\label{thm:finite-semigroup}
Let \(\phi:\{1,2\}^{+}\to S\) be a morphism into a fixed finite semigroup,
and let \(H\subseteq S\).  Then
\[
 E_{\phi,H}
 =\{k\ge2:\phi(1^{k-1}2)\in H\}
\]
is ultimately periodic.  If \(E_{\phi,H}\subseteq\Pp\), it is finite.
Moreover, every scalar response
\[
        f\bigl(\phi(1^{k-1}2)\bigr)
\]
is eventually periodic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(a=\phi(1)\) and \(b=\phi(2)\), with
\(\phi(xy)=\phi(x)\phi(y)\), so multiplication follows reading order.  The
canonical word evaluates to
\[
        \phi(1^{k-1}2)=a^{k-1}b.
\]
By \cref{lem:semigrouppower}, the power is eventually periodic; right
multiplication by \(b\), membership in \(H\), and evaluation by \(f\)
preserve equality.  Apply \cref{lem:primeperiod}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite groups and characters]
\label{cor:finitegroup}
For a fixed finite group, \(a^{k-1}b\) is periodic from the start with
period dividing \(\ord(a)\).  Membership, class functions, characters, and
matrix coefficients of fixed representations cannot have infinite
prime-only support.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[DFA and NFA fibers]
\label{thm:automata}
A fixed DFA or NFA cannot accept exactly
\[
        \{1^{p-1}2:p\in\Pp\},
\]
or any infinite prime-only sublanguage of the form
\(\{1^{k-1}2:k\in E\}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a DFA, let \(T_1,T_2\) be the two transition maps acting on column
states.  Function composition is right-to-left, so the final state is
\[
        (T_2\circ T_1^{k-1})(q_0).
\]
The transformations of a finite state set form a finite semigroup, so
\cref{thm:finite-semigroup} applies.  For an NFA, either use the subset
construction or view the letter transitions as elements of the finite
Boolean-relation semigroup.
\end{proof}

\Cref{thm:automata} is consistent with the stronger classical
prime-recognition boundary of \citet{HartmanisShank1968}, but its proof is
tied directly to the word derived in \cref{thm:word}.

\begin{remark}[Quantifiers]
\label{rem:quantifiers}
The theorem says
\[
 \forall\text{ fixed finite fiber}\quad
 \exists(\mu,\lambda)\quad
 \text{eventual periodicity}.
\]
The tail and period may depend sharply on the fiber.  The theorem does not
provide one universal period and does not forbid a sequence of fibers whose
size grows with the cutoff.  That change of quantifiers is analyzed in
\cref{sec:growing}.
\end{remark}

The no-go concerns exact acceptance.  A finite fiber may correlate with

Neither observation contradicts \cref{thm:finite-semigroup}.
