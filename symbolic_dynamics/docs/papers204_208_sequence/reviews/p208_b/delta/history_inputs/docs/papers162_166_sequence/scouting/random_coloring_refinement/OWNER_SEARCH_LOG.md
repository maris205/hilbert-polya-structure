## 1. Objects searched

The search separated the proposed name from the actual mathematical objects:

1. kernel partitions of independent uniform finite maps;
2. common refinement / meet of those kernel partitions;
3. separation of every pair, equivalently the birthday collision problem;
4. iid finite-paintbox refinement of each block of an arbitrary partition;
5. partition-valued fragmentation kernels;
6. random walks on finite meet semilattices and left-regular bands;
7. zeta-basis eigenfunctions and lattice-Mobius multiplicities;
8. occupied-box counts `(Q)_j S(b,j)`; and
9. weighted columns indexed by coarsenings of a fixed partition.

## 2. Direct random-map partition owner

D. Krachun and Yu. Yakubovich,
[*Random partitions induced by random maps*](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/znsl6393),
*Zap. Nauchn. Sem. POMI* 454 (2016), 195--215; English translation,
*J. Math. Sci.* 229 (2018), 727--740,
[DOI 10.1007/s10958-018-3712-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10958-018-3712-5),
study the partition lattice ordered by refinement.  A uniform map
`phi:[n]->[n]` gives its kernel partition, and they study the common refinement
of the partitions induced by `t` independent maps, including the probability
that it is discrete.

Starting RCR from the one-block partition and taking `q=n` is exactly that
common-refinement model.  RCR's cumulative signature is the single map
`[n]->[n]^t`, merely re-labelled as an alphabet of size `n^t`.  Allowing a
fixed alphabet size `q` and retaining the exact finite falling-factorial CDF
is a natural finite occupancy extension, not a new literal mechanism.

The same source cites E. Rodney Canfield,
[*Meet and Join within the Lattice of Set Partitions*](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v8i1r15),
*Electron. J. Combin.* 8 (2001), R15,
[DOI 10.37236/1559](https://doi.org/10.37236/1559).  Canfield treats counts of
tuples of partitions whose meet is the discrete partition and situates this
as an established partition-lattice enumeration.  Its random inputs are not
restricted to colouring kernels, so it is background rather than an exact
RCR owner; meet-to-discrete enumeration receives zero credit.

### Owner boundary

Krachun--Yakubovich do not, on the inspected record, advertise RCR's arbitrary
initial source, every-target transition matrix, finite-`q` mean, or weighted
target column.  The audit therefore does **not** claim that their paper states
the whole proposed theorem package verbatim.  It does own the defining
top-source random-map meet and its main absorption question on a full
parameter slice.  The remaining source generality is independently subsumed
by the fragmentation-kernel owner below.

## 3. Paintbox and fragmentation-kernel owner

Jim Pitman,
[*Combinatorial Stochastic Processes*](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/206-Exch/Papers/pitman_CSP.pdf),
Lecture Notes in Mathematics 1875, Springer (2006), develops exchangeable
paintbox partitions and, in Definition 5.10, a `p`-fragmentation Markov
kernel.  Given a source partition, the restriction to each source block is
refined by an independent partition with law `p`; Pitman explicitly stresses
the block-local nature of this kernel.

Take `p` to be the kernel partition generated by iid uniform colours in
`[q]`.  Restrictions of one global RCR colouring to disjoint source blocks
are independent.  Equality of colour labels across different source blocks
is irrelevant because the meet with the source already keeps those blocks
apart.  Hence the RCR one-step Markov kernel is **exactly** this uniform
finite-paintbox fragmentation kernel.  Repeating epochs composes paintboxes;
the product colour is the uniform paintbox on `[q]^t`.

This is stronger than a loose fragmentation analogy: it identifies the full
arbitrary-source transition law.  The product in the RCR transition theorem
is precisely the block-local factorization built into the kernel definition.
The falling factorial evaluates the finite-paintbox probability that the
specified child blocks receive distinct colours.

Kingman's paintbox representation is broader background for colour-generated
exchangeable partitions.  No novelty credit is assigned merely for calling
the equal-mass `q`-atom paintbox a colour kernel.

## 4. Birthday and occupancy subtraction

For a source block of size `b`, absorption by time `t` says that `b` labelled
elements have distinct cells among `Q=q^t` equiprobable cells.  Its probability

```text
(Q)_b / Q^b
```

is exactly the elementary birthday/no-collision probability.  The law of the
number `j` of child blocks,

```text
(Q)_j S(b,j) / Q^b,
```

is the classical number-of-occupied-boxes distribution.  Multiplication over
source blocks is ordinary independence.  The exact expectation obtained by
expanding `product_i (Q)_(b_i)` and summing geometric tails is a correct finite
calculation, but all of its ingredients lie inside this classical occupancy
reduction.

These elementary formulas are recorded as owner-covered inputs, not as a
novelty claim.  The audit did not need a modern secondary survey to establish
them.

## 5. Semilattice-walk spectrum owner

Kenneth S. Brown,
[*Semigroups, Rings, and Markov Chains*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0006145),
*J. Theoret. Probab.* 13 (2000), 871--938,
[DOI 10.1023/A:1007822931408](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1007822931408),
develops diagonalization, eigenvalues, multiplicities, and eigenspace
projections for random walks on left-regular bands.

Arvind Ayyer, Anne Schilling, Benjamin Steinberg, and Nicolas M. Thiery,
[*Markov chains, R-trivial monoids and representation theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.4250),
*Internat. J. Algebra Comput.* 25 (2015), 169--231,
[DOI 10.1142/S0218196715400081](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218196715400081),
give general eigenvalue and lattice-Mobius multiplicity formulas,
diagonalizability criteria, and absorption/mixing tools for random walks on
`R`-trivial monoids, explicitly subsuming the left-regular-band theory.

The partition lattice under meet is a commutative idempotent semigroup.  RCR
samples a kernel partition and left-multiplies the current state by it.  Thus
the Markov chain is the most elementary semilattice-walk specialization of
this theory.  The direct zeta calculation

```text
P Z_rho = q^(#rho-n) Z_rho
```

is clean, but it does not create an owner-resistant spectral axis.  The
Stirling multiplicity only counts the partitions of each rank.

## 6. Exact residual after external subtraction

| proposed component | direct/generic owner | residual |
|---|---|---|
| cumulative `q^t` signatures | product of iid colour maps / finite paintbox | no credit |
| arbitrary-source dynamics | Pitman blockwise fragmentation kernel | exact specialization |
| top-source absorption | Krachun--Yakubovich random-map partition meet; birthday separation | finite-`q` exact formula |
| every-target kernel | finite paintbox, evaluated by within-block injections | useful compact specialization |
| block count | classical occupancy and Stirling numbers | no credit |
| exact mean | falling-factorial expansion plus geometric tail | elementary corollary |
| zeta spectrum | Brown/Ayyer semilattice-walk theory | direct specialization |
| target-weighted history polynomial | marked column transform over the coarsening interval | target-sensitive but dependent |

The every-target kernel is the cleanest formula in the residual.  It already
determines the weighted polynomial through

```text
sum_(pi>=sigma) q^(tn) P^t(pi,sigma)
    times any chosen source statistic.
```

The pair-count and source-size marks make that transform sensitive to the
target block sizes, but they do not reveal information independent of the
kernel or the coarsening interval.  No sharp new factorization, vanishing
class, extremizer, parameter recovery, or history statistic emerged.

## 7. P1--P161 collision audit

The paper inventory, the P157--P161 historical occupancy table, the current
problem anchor, and recent stochastic kill records were checked before the
value decision.

| internal item | occupied mechanism | comparison with RCR |
|---|---|---|
| P110, cyclic shift--join partition dynamics | full set-partition lattice, zeta/Mobius transforms, exact iterates and fibres | literal update differs, but partition-lattice operator technology is already occupied |
| P118, synchronous mex colouring | colouring dynamics with exact source/target colour fibres | not a conjugacy; removes generic colour-fibre novelty value |
| P126, balanced composition refinement | deterministic rank-changing refinement/fragmentation | different carrier and map; generic refinement narrative is occupied |
| P158, cut-intersection collapse | random intersections, cumulative history signatures, occupancy/surjection fibres, target obstruction | not literal collision; P158 has much richer labelled target geometry |
| same-batch RPS | random meet process with elementary rank/hitting transforms | killed for an owner-covered meet silhouette and missing independent inverse axis |
| same-batch RAE | random projection with complete kernel and all-source column fibre | killed because the purported second theorem is a dependent column sum |
| same-batch RTI | random translate intersections with a stabilizer/coset inverse polynomial | survives precisely because its inverse polynomial is not a rank or kernel marginal |

RCR is not a direct conjugate of P110, P118, P126, or P158.  That is
insufficient: the batch threshold requires a separate theorem axis after
owner subtraction, and the marked column does not supply one.  Promoting RCR
would also allocate another slot to the same random-meet/history-signature
silhouette already represented more strongly by P158 and RTI.

## 8. Strict conclusion

The bounded audit found direct ownership of the process at two complementary
levels: random-map kernel meets for the top source and paintbox fragmentation
for arbitrary sources.  Generic semilattice theory owns the spectral axis.
The exact finite formulas and verifier are sound, and the weighted target
polynomial is nontrivially sensitive to block sizes, but it remains a marked
column of the transition matrix rather than an independent inverse theorem.
