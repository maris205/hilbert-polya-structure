\section{Explicit finite-scheme ambiguity}
\label{sec:schemes}

The classification becomes concrete through two atomwise schemes.  The
\emph{leading-only} counterterm is
\begin{equation}
 C_F^{\mathrm{lead}}
 =2C_\eta\sum_{p\in F}\frac1p.
 \label{eq:lead-counterterm}
\end{equation}
It removes exactly the divergent prime-harmonic germ and leaves
\begin{equation}
 R_\eta^{\mathrm{lead}}(t)
 =2C_\eta P(1+2\eta)+M_\eta(t).
 \label{eq:lead-fp}
\end{equation}
The diagonal tail is absolutely summable and carries no cutoff-order
ambiguity.

The \emph{full-diagonal} counterterm is
\begin{equation}
 C_F^{\mathrm{full}}
 =2\sum_{p\in F}\frac{G_{pp}}p.
 \label{eq:full-counterterm}
\end{equation}
It is defined on every finite realized source and leaves
\begin{equation}
 R_\eta^{\mathrm{full}}(t)=M_\eta(t).
 \label{eq:full-fp}
\end{equation}
It removes more than the divergence, but it violates neither transported
isomorphism naturality nor active-prefix compatibility.

\begin{corollary}[Nonzero natural scheme shift]
\label{cor:scheme-shift}
The two counterterms and finite parts differ by
\begin{align}
 C_F^{\mathrm{full}}-C_F^{\mathrm{lead}}
 &=2C_\eta\sum_{p\in F}p^{-1-2\eta},
 \label{eq:finite-shift-cutoff}\\
 R_\eta^{\mathrm{lead}}-R_\eta^{\mathrm{full}}
 &=2C_\eta P(1+2\eta)>0.
 \label{eq:finite-shift-limit}
\end{align}
Therefore pointed-isomorphism naturality, active-cutoff compatibility, and
absolute convergence fix the divergent germ but not a finite part.
\end{corollary}

The experiment specializes to $\eta=2$, where the normalized difference is
$2\sum_pp^{-5}$.  It also preregisters summable shifts
$2\sum_pp^{-(5+k)}$, $k=0,1,2$, and rational combinations fixed before the
controls.  These examples are not needed for existence, but they demonstrate
constructively that the ambiguity is a family rather than a binary choice.

\subsection{Sharp and Abel constants}

An additional scheme choice appears when one subtracts an asymptotic rather
than the atomwise harmonic sum.  Mertens' theorem gives
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{p\le X}\frac1p
 =\log\log X+\Meta+o(1)
 \label{eq:mertens}
\end{equation}
\citep{Mertens1874}.  Thus the sharp-$X$ convention is
\begin{equation}
 \FP_{\mathrm{sharp}}Q(t)
 =2C_\eta\bigl(\Meta+P(1+2\eta)\bigr)+M_\eta(t).
 \label{eq:sharp-fp}
\end{equation}

The prime-zeta expansion at $1$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 P(1+\eps)=\log(1/\eps)+\Meta-\gamma+o(1)
 \label{eq:prime-zeta-expansion}
\end{equation}
\citep{Froberg1968}.  Applying this Abel regulator to the diagonal while
using dominated convergence for the absolutely summable pieces gives
\begin{equation}
 \FP_{\mathrm{Abel}}Q
 -\FP_{\mathrm{sharp}}Q=-2C_\eta\gamma.
 \label{eq:abel-sharp}
\end{equation}
The discrepancy is a constant, so both schemes preserve the mixed phase and
reflection.  It nevertheless rules out regulator-independent canonicity.

The atomwise leading scheme \eqref{eq:lead-counterterm}, the sharp convention
\eqref{eq:sharp-fp}, and the Abel convention are three different statements.
Only the first is independent of the numerical ordering of active cutoffs
after the marked tower has been fixed.  The latter two explicitly use a
cutoff/regulator coordinate.
