\begin{abstract}
Let $q$ be a prime power, let $N$ be the regular upper shift, and fix
$J=I+N$ in the unitriangular group $U_n(\F_q)$.  We determine the finite
dynamics of the single map $E(X)=X^{-1}J^{-1}XJ$.  Bier's arbitrary-field
fixed-$J$ theorem already gives $E(\gamma_k)=\gamma_{k+1}$ and the
corresponding iterated images \cite{Bier2013}; those image equalities receive
zero contribution credit.  We give the finite-field refinement that every
target in $\gamma_{k+1}$ has exactly $q^{n-k}$ predecessors in $\gamma_k$,
identify each fibre
as a left centralizer coset, and derive every iterated restricted fibre and
depth layer.  For $n\geq2$, the resulting filtration-typed census has sharp
height $n-1$ and $(q-1)q^{\binom n2-1}$ vertices in its deepest layer; its zeta function
is $(1-z)^{-1}$.  A complementary proof counts solutions of $XJ=JXY$ by
successive discrete differences on superdiagonals.  In $U_4(\F_q)$, the
choice $I+E_{12}+E_{34}$ has image size $q^2$ instead of $q^3$, showing only
that the theorem does not extend to arbitrary nonregular second entries.
Engel-image existence, Lang terminology, regular centralizers,
lower-central descent, and generic zeta bookkeeping receive zero credit.
Novelty, priority, and external circulation remain on \textbf{HOLD}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and claim boundary}
\label{sec:intro}

Fixing the second entry of a commutator turns the left Engel sequence into
an ordinary finite self-map.  We study that map for one specific second
entry: the regular unitriangular shift.  Nilpotence already says that every
orbit eventually reaches the identity, and Bier identifies the exact
lower-central images.  The question here is the finite-field refinement: how
many predecessors does each target have at every time, and what exact depth
and filtration-typed census do these fibres determine?

Several ingredients are owned background.  The expression
$X\mapsto X^{-1}\phi(X)$ is a Lang-type twisted coboundary; Lang and
Steinberg are foundational sources for that setting
\cite{Lang1956,Steinberg1968}.  Their global surjectivity theorems do not
apply to the inner automorphism used below, so we use only the terminology
and the elementary fixed-coset observation.  Fixed-second-variable iterated
commutators are the classical left Engel sequence, and Engel sinks are
studied explicitly in \cite{AcciarriShumyatsky2019,KhukhroShumyatsky2026}.
Most importantly, \citet[Lemma~1 and the proof of Theorem~1]{Bier2013} use our commutator
convention and the same fixed regular element $J=I+N$ over an arbitrary
field.  In the present notation, Bier proves
$E(\gamma_k)=\gamma_{k+1}$ and obtains the corresponding fixed-$J$ iterated
Engel images.  Every image and existence assertion below is therefore a
self-contained reproduction of owned input and receives zero contribution
credit.
The lower-central filtration of a unitriangular group, the centralizer of a
regular Jordan block, and broader conjugacy questions in maximal unipotent
groups are also established territory \cite{Steinberg1968,Goodwin2006}.
Each of these facts receives zero contribution credit.

After that subtraction, this note records one exact conjunction for the
literal fixed element $J=I+N$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item refining Bier's owned restricted surjectivity, every finite-field
      target has a left-coset fibre of the exact uniform size $q^{n-k}$;
\item every restricted iterated fibre, cumulative depth set, exact depth
      layer, and sharp terminal layer is explicit;
\item filtration strata give an exact multitype predecessor census, while
      the whole graph has one recurrent point and one weak component;
\item the finite-field fibre count has a centralizer--coset proof and a
      separate triangular superdiagonal count; and
\item a near-regular $U_4$ example shows that the fixed regular hypothesis
      cannot be widened to arbitrary noncentral unipotent elements.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Internal firewall.}
The closest internal proof shape is P109, where a regular nilpotent linear
map sends a subspace to its image on the full subspace lattice.  Its phase
points are subspaces and its fibres use Grassmannian intersection counts.
Here the phase points are group elements, the update is a nonlinear fixed
commutator, and the fibres are centralizer cosets.  P111 uses random positive
products in the integer Heisenberg group and studies a word-area cocycle;
neither its update nor its observables occur here.  The generic slogans
``nilpotent descent,'' ``unique absorber,'' and ``uniform fibre'' therefore
carry no credit.  A bounded follow-up search found no source stating the
finite-field fibre, layer, and filtration-typed predecessor census below,
but that search miss is not evidence of novelty or priority.  External use
remains on \textbf{HOLD}.

\section{The fixed regular map and its filtration}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $q$ be a prime power and $n\ge2$.  Write $E_{ij}$ for the usual matrix
unit and put
\[
 N=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}E_{i,i+1},\qquad J=I+N,\qquad
 U_n(q)=U_n(\F_q).
\]
Thus $U_n(q)$ is the group of upper triangular matrices with diagonal
entries one.  We use the commutator convention
\begin{equation}
 [X,J]=X^{-1}J^{-1}XJ
 \label{eq:commutator}
\end{equation}
and define the self-map
\[
                    E:U_n(q)\longrightarrow U_n(q),\qquad E(X)=[X,J].
\]

For $1\le k\le n$, let $\mathfrak n_k$ be the strictly upper triangular
matrices whose entries vanish on superdiagonals $1,\ldots,k-1$, and set
\[
              \gamma_k=I+\mathfrak n_k.
\]
Then $\gamma_1=U_n(q)$, $\gamma_n=\{I\}$, and
\begin{equation}
 |\gamma_k|
   =q^{\sum_{r=k}^{n-1}(n-r)}
   =q^{(n-k)(n-k+1)/2}.
 \label{eq:gamma-size}
\end{equation}
Since $\mathfrak n_r\mathfrak n_s\subseteq\mathfrak n_{r+s}$, the inverse
series for $I+A$ gives
\begin{equation}
 [\gamma_r,\gamma_s]\subseteq\gamma_{r+s}
 \quad\text{whenever }r+s\le n.
 \label{eq:filtration-product}
\end{equation}
In particular, $E(\gamma_k)\subseteq\gamma_{k+1}$.

For $X\in U_n(q)$ define its entry depth
\begin{equation}
 \tau(X)=\min\{t\ge0:E^t(X)=I\}.
 \label{eq:depth}
\end{equation}
The minimum is finite by \cref{thm:iterates}.  Empty sums below are zero,
$E^0$ is the identity map, and every target is understood as an element of
the full phase $U_n(q)$ unless a smaller set is displayed.

\begin{remark}[The $n=1$ and $n=2$ boundaries]
\label{rem:small-n}
For $n=1$ the phase is the singleton $\{I\}$, so the depth is zero and the
zeta function is $(1-z)^{-1}$.  We state the nontrivial filtration formulas
for $n\ge2$.  When $n=2$, $U_2(q)$ is abelian, $E$ is constant at $I$, and
the layers have sizes $1$ and $q-1$, exactly as the general formulas give.
No $n=0$ matrix convention is used.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact one-step fibres: the centralizer route}
\label{sec:coset}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-field fibre refinement of the restricted image]
\label{thm:one-step}
For every $1\le k<n$,
\begin{equation}
                     E(\gamma_k)=\gamma_{k+1}.
 \label{eq:restricted-image}
\end{equation}
More precisely, for $Y\in U_n(q)$,
\begin{equation}
 \#\{X\in\gamma_k:E(X)=Y\}
 =\begin{cases}
   q^{n-k},&Y\in\gamma_{k+1},\\
   0,&Y\notin\gamma_{k+1}.
  \end{cases}
 \label{eq:one-step-fibre}
\end{equation}
Every nonempty fibre is a left coset of $C_{\gamma_k}(J)$.
\end{theorem}

The image equality \eqref{eq:restricted-image} is
\citet[Lemma~1]{Bier2013} in the notation
$\gamma_k=UT_n^{k-1}$ and is included only for a self-contained dynamical
statement.  The asserted finite-field refinement is the exact cardinality
and left-coset description of every fibre.

\begin{proof}[Self-contained fibre count: fixed cosets and the regular centralizer]
Let $\phi(X)=J^{-1}XJ$.  Normality of $\gamma_k$ makes $\phi$ an
automorphism of $\gamma_k$, and $E(X)=X^{-1}\phi(X)$.  If
$E(X_1)=E(X_2)=Y$, then $\phi(X_i)=X_iY$ and hence
\[
 \phi(X_2X_1^{-1})=X_2Y(X_1Y)^{-1}=X_2X_1^{-1}.
\]
Conversely, if $h\in\gamma_k$ satisfies $\phi(h)=h$, then
$E(hX)=E(X)$.  Thus every nonempty fibre is exactly a left coset
$C_{\gamma_k}(J)X$.

We compute this centralizer without importing an orbit formula.  Commuting
with $J$ is equivalent to commuting with $N$.  For a matrix $M$, the equation
$MN=NM$ reads
\[
                  M_{i,j-1}=M_{i+1,j}
\]
where the boundary entries are zero.  The boundary equations force $M$ to
be upper triangular, and the displayed recurrence makes each
superdiagonal constant.  Hence the full matrix centralizer is $\F_q[N]$.
Intersecting its unipotent units with $\gamma_k$ gives
\begin{equation}
 C_{\gamma_k}(J)
  =\left\{I+a_kN^k+\cdots+a_{n-1}N^{n-1}:a_i\in\F_q\right\},
 \qquad |C_{\gamma_k}(J)|=q^{n-k}.
 \label{eq:centralizer}
\end{equation}
The coset result therefore makes every nonempty fibre have size $q^{n-k}$.
Together with the inclusion $E(\gamma_k)\subseteq\gamma_{k+1}$,
\cref{eq:gamma-size} gives
\[
 |E(\gamma_k)|
  =\frac{|\gamma_k|}{q^{n-k}}
  =q^{(n-k-1)(n-k)/2}
  =|\gamma_{k+1}|.
\]
The inclusion is equality, proving both assertions.
\end{proof}

The left-coset orientation in \cref{thm:one-step} matters: for our
convention \eqref{eq:commutator}, equality of two fibres fixes
$X_2X_1^{-1}$ rather than $X_1^{-1}X_2$.  The verifier checks the literal
left coset for every target in its exhaustive lanes.

\section{A second route through superdiagonal equations}
\label{sec:triangular}

The next proof counts every solution without the centralizer theorem,
orbit--stabilizer, or a cardinality comparison.  Bier's earlier
block-inductive argument already supplies existence; the point here is the
finite-field multiplicity and its explicit free coordinates.

\begin{proposition}[Triangular count of every fibre]
\label{prop:triangular}
Fix $1\le k<n$ and $Y\in\gamma_{k+1}$.  The equation $E(X)=Y$ has exactly
$q^{n-k}$ solutions $X\in\gamma_k$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof route II: successive discrete differences]
Write $X=I+A$ and $Y=I+B$.  From \eqref{eq:commutator}, the equation
$E(X)=Y$ is equivalent to $XJ=JXY$.  Expanding both sides and cancelling
their common terms gives the exact matrix equation
\begin{equation}
             AN-NA=B+NB+AB+NAB.
 \label{eq:triangular-equation}
\end{equation}

For $r\ge k$, collect the $r$th superdiagonal of $A$ as
\[
 A^{(r)}=(a_{1,1+r},a_{2,2+r},\ldots,a_{n-r,n})\in\F_q^{n-r}.
\]
On superdiagonal $r+1$, the left side of \eqref{eq:triangular-equation} is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_r A^{(r)},\qquad
 \Delta_r(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-r})
       =(x_1-x_2,\ldots,x_{n-r-1}-x_{n-r}).
 \label{eq:difference}
\end{equation}
The map $\Delta_r:\F_q^{n-r}\to\F_q^{n-r-1}$ is onto: after choosing
$x_1$, the target differences determine $x_2,\ldots,x_{n-r}$.  Its kernel
is the one-dimensional space of constant vectors, so every fibre has
exactly $q$ elements.  For $r=n-1$, the codomain is the zero space and the
same statement remains valid.

It remains to verify that these difference equations are triangular in
$r$.  The target $B$ begins on superdiagonal $k+1$.  In the equation on
superdiagonal $r+1$, the term $NB$ is fixed, while a term from $AB$ uses a
source superdiagonal $u$ with
\[
               u+(k+1)\le r+1,
               \qquad\text{hence }u\le r-k<r.
\]
A term from $NAB$ has $u+(k+1)+1\le r+1$, and again $u<r$.
Consequently the right side at stage $r$ depends only on the prescribed
$B$ and on $A^{(k)},\ldots,A^{(r-1)}$, which have already been chosen.
Starting at $r=k$, solve successively through $r=n-1$.  Each of the
$n-k$ stages contributes one free field coordinate, and no later equation
changes an earlier choice.  Every target therefore has exactly
$q^{n-k}$ solutions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation between the two counting routes]
Route I treats $E$ as a twisted group coboundary and counts a fibre from
fixed cosets and a regular centralizer.  Route II counts solutions of a
nonlinear matrix equation through additive difference maps; it does not use
the centralizer, cosets, or domain/image cardinalities.  Neither route claims
the owned existence theorem as new.  The later temporal corollaries share
the one-step count and are not advertised as independently derived.
\end{remark}

\section{All iterated fibres and exact depth layers}
\label{sec:temporal}

For $0\le t\le n-k$, put
\begin{equation}
 S_{k,t}=\sum_{j=k}^{k+t-1}(n-j),\qquad S_{k,0}=0.
 \label{eq:Skt}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Complete restricted temporal census]
\label{thm:iterates}
Let $1\le k<n$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item For $0\le t\le n-k$,
\begin{equation}
                   E^t(\gamma_k)=\gamma_{k+t},
 \label{eq:iterated-image}
\end{equation}
and for every $Y\in U_n(q)$,
\begin{equation}
 \#\{X\in\gamma_k:E^t(X)=Y\}
 =\begin{cases}
   q^{S_{k,t}},&Y\in\gamma_{k+t},\\
   0,&Y\notin\gamma_{k+t}.
  \end{cases}
 \label{eq:iterated-fibre}
\end{equation}
For $t\ge n-k$, the image is $\{I\}$ and the fibre over $I$ is all of
$\gamma_k$.
\item The cumulative and exact depth counts inside $\gamma_k$ are
\begin{align}
 \#\{X\in\gamma_k:\tau(X)\le t\}&=q^{S_{k,t}},
       &&0\le t\le n-k,                       \label{eq:cdf}\\
 L_{k,0}&=1,                                   \label{eq:layer-zero}\\
 L_{k,t}&=
 \left(q^{n-k-t+1}-1\right)
 q^{\sum_{j=k}^{k+t-2}(n-j)},
       &&1\le t\le n-k.                       \label{eq:layers}
\end{align}
Here $L_{k,t}=\#\{X\in\gamma_k:\tau(X)=t\}$.
\item On the full phase, the sharp maximum depth is $n-1$, and the deepest
layer has
\begin{equation}
                   (q-1)q^{\binom n2-1}
 \label{eq:deepest}
\end{equation}
vertices.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The image equality in part~(i) is also the fixed-$J$ Engel-image construction
in the proof of \citet[Theorem~1]{Bier2013}, or equivalently the iteration of Bier's
restricted lemma; it receives zero credit here.  Composing the exact
finite-field fibre counts in \cref{thm:one-step} gives multiplicity
\[
 \prod_{j=k}^{k+t-1}q^{n-j}=q^{S_{k,t}},
\]
including the empty product at $t=0$.  At $t=n-k$ the target is
$\gamma_n=\{I\}$ and $S_{k,n-k}$ is the exponent in
\cref{eq:gamma-size}; all later iterates remain at $I$.

The set in \eqref{eq:cdf} is the $E^t$-fibre of $I$.  Subtracting the
count at time $t-1$ gives
\[
 q^{S_{k,t}}-q^{S_{k,t-1}}
 =q^{S_{k,t-1}}\bigl(q^{n-k-t+1}-1\bigr),
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:layers}.  Taking $k=1$ shows that every state has
depth at most $n-1$.  The last factor in \eqref{eq:layers} is nonzero for
$t=n-1$, so the bound is attained.  Substitution yields
\[
 L_{1,n-1}=(q-1)q^{\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}(n-j)}
           =(q-1)q^{\binom n2-1}.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{The rooted component and filtration-type indegrees}
\label{sec:tree}

Put $\Gamma_k^\circ=\gamma_k\setminus\gamma_{k+1}$ for $1\le k<n$.

\begin{corollary}[Filtration-typed predecessor census]
\label{cor:tree}
The functional graph of $E$ has one weak component and one recurrent point,
$I$.  After retaining the loop $I\to I$, every other vertex lies in its
rooted in-tree.  For $Y\in U_n(q)$,
\begin{equation}
 \indeg(Y)=\#E^{-1}(Y)
  =q^{n-1}\one_{\gamma_2}(Y).
 \label{eq:full-indegree}
\end{equation}
The loop-suppressed root has $q^{n-1}-1$ children; any other
$Y\in\gamma_2$ has $q^{n-1}$ children; and vertices outside $\gamma_2$
have none.

More finely, for $1\le k\le n-2$,
\begin{equation}
 \#\{X\in\Gamma_k^\circ:E(X)=Y\}
 =q^{n-k}\one_{\gamma_{k+1}}(Y)
  -q^{n-k-1}\one_{\gamma_{k+2}}(Y),
 \label{eq:stratum-indegree}
\end{equation}
while
\begin{equation}
 \#\{X\in\Gamma_{n-1}^\circ:E(X)=Y\}
 =(q-1)\one_{\{I\}}(Y).
 \label{eq:last-stratum}
\end{equation}
Finally,
\begin{equation}
 \#\Fix(E^m)=1\quad(m\ge1),\qquad
 \zeta_E(z)=\exp\!\left(\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{z^m}{m}\right)
            =\frac1{1-z}.
 \label{eq:zeta}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:iterates}, every state reaches $I$ and the deepest layer is
nonempty.  Hence there is one component, its only directed cycle is the
loop at $I$, and its height is exactly $n-1$.  Formula
\eqref{eq:full-indegree} is \cref{eq:one-step-fibre} with $k=1$.

For $k\le n-2$, subtract the number of predecessors lying in
$\gamma_{k+1}$ from the number lying in $\gamma_k$.  Applying
\cref{eq:one-step-fibre} at levels $k$ and $k+1$ gives
\eqref{eq:stratum-indegree}.  At the last level, all $q$ elements of
$\gamma_{n-1}$ map to $I$, and removing the identity leaves $q-1$.
The unique recurrent point is fixed by every positive iterate, so the fixed
counts and the standard Artin--Mazur conversion
\cite{ArtinMazur1965} give \eqref{eq:zeta}.
\end{proof}

\section{Failure for one nonregular second entry}
\label{sec:counterexample}

The preceding theorem does not extend to an arbitrary unipotent choice of
the second commutator entry.

\begin{proposition}[Near-regular failure in $U_4$]
\label{prop:counterexample}
Let
\[
                       J'=I+E_{12}+E_{34}\in U_4(q)
\]
and set $E'(X)=X^{-1}(J')^{-1}XJ'$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 |C_{U_4(q)}(J')|=q^4,\qquad |E'(U_4(q))|=q^2<q^3=|\gamma_2|.
 \label{eq:counterexample-counts}
\end{equation}
Thus $E'(U_4(q))\ne\gamma_2$, and every nonempty full-phase fibre has
$q^4$, not $q^3$, elements.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
 A=a_{12}E_{12}+a_{13}E_{13}+a_{14}E_{14}
   +a_{23}E_{23}+a_{24}E_{24}+a_{34}E_{34}.
\]
Since $X=I+A$ commutes with $J'$ precisely when $A$ commutes with
$E_{12}+E_{34}$, direct multiplication gives the two independent
conditions
\[
                         a_{23}=0,\qquad a_{24}=a_{13}.
\]
Four coordinates remain free, proving the centralizer count.  The same
fixed-coset argument used in \cref{thm:one-step} applies to $E'$ and gives
$|E'(U_4(q))|=q^6/q^4=q^2$.  Every commutator in $U_4(q)$ lies in
$\gamma_2$, whose order is $q^3$, so the inclusion is strict.  The fibre
claim follows from the same coset calculation.
\end{proof}

This example misses only the middle simple-root entry of the regular shift.
It is therefore a structural guard, not the vacuous central choice $J'=I$.
The title, abstract, and all theorem statements deliberately keep the fixed
regular $J=I+N$ hypothesis.

\section{Exact controls and conclusion}
\label{sec:controls}

The standard-library verifier constructs literal polynomial-basis models of
$\F_2,\F_3,\F_4,\F_5,\F_8$, and $\F_9$.  It exhausts $55{,}808$ regular
phase states, checks 39 restricted surjections and 112 iterated-fibre
profiles, and separately exhausts $20{,}514$ states in the $U_4$
counterexample lanes.  Its $1{,}491{,}877$ executed assertions include
statewise matrix inverses, the equation $XJ=JXY$, literal left centralizer
cosets, all depth histograms, all filtration-stratum indegrees, unique
periodicity through several iterates, and the exact table artifact.  These
finite computations are falsification controls, not proofs or owner
certificates.

For the fixed regular shift, Bier's owned image descent and our exact
finite-field fibres reduce the temporal count to multiplication along the
lower-central levels.  The centralizer route explains a fibre as a group
coset; the triangular route explains each of its $n-k$ free coordinates.
Multiplying and subtracting those fibres yields the complete
filtration-typed fibre and depth census.  The $U_4$ calculation shows failure
for one near-regular entry and blocks extension to arbitrary unipotent
second entries; it does not classify every nonregular case.  No statement is
made for another Engel word or for conjugacy dynamics.  Bier's image theorem,
Lang theory, regular centralizers, lower-central filtrations, and generic
finite-map zeta identities retain zero contribution credit.  Novelty,
priority, specialist clearance, and external circulation remain on
\textbf{HOLD}.
