\section{Scalar and ordinary-fiber rigidity}
\label{sec:rigidity}

We first test repairs that stay within an ordinary, ungraded trace.  The
result is rigid already for one affine branch.

\begin{lemma}[Affine fixed-point trace]
\label{lem:fixed-trace}
Let \(\phi(z)=a+qz\) map \(\D\) compactly into itself, with
\(0<|q|<1\), and let \(W_0=wU_{\phi,0}\).  Then
\begin{equation}
        \Tr W_0^r=\frac{w^r}{1-q^r},\qquad r\ge1.
 \label{eq:zero-trace}
\end{equation}
Its ordinary Fredholm determinant is
\begin{equation}
        \det(I-zW_0)=\prod_{m\ge0}(1-zwq^m).
 \label{eq:q-pochhammer}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The iterate \(\phi^r\) has derivative \(q^r\) and one fixed point.  The
holomorphic fixed-point trace formula gives \eqref{eq:zero-trace}.  One may
also center the coordinate at that fixed point.  The monomials then have
eigenvalues \(1,q,q^2,\ldots\), and the trace is
\[
        w^r\sum_{m\ge0}q^{rm}=\frac{w^r}{1-q^r}.
\]
The trace-log formula yields \eqref{eq:q-pochhammer}; equivalently, it is the
product over the centered spectrum.
\end{proof}

The translation \(a\) affects the upper-triangular part of a monomial
matrix but not its diagonal.  Thus the local obstruction is intrinsic to
the code length through \(q_n=2^{-\ell(n)}\).

\begin{proposition}[Scalar all-power rigidity]
\label{prop:scalar-rigidity}
Suppose a scalar \(\alpha(q)\) satisfies
\[
 \Tr\bigl(\alpha(q)wU_{\phi,0}\bigr)^r=w^r
\]
for every \(r\ge1\) and nonzero \(w\).  Then \(q=0\) or \(q=1\).  For a
strict contraction, the only possibility is \(q=0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At \(r=1\), \cref{lem:fixed-trace} forces \(\alpha=1-q\).  At \(r=2\),
the same requirement becomes
\[
        \frac{(1-q)^2}{1-q^2}=1.
\]
Hence \(2q(q-1)=0\).  The solution \(q=1\) is not a strict contraction.
\end{proof}

The first-trace normalization therefore has the exact second-order residual
\begin{equation}
 \frac{(1-q)^2}{1-q^2}-1=-\frac{2q}{1+q}.
 \label{eq:scalar-residual}
\end{equation}
At \(q=0\), composition evaluates a function at the fixed point and includes
the resulting constant.  The operator has rank one: the purported analytic
repair has become an atom loop.

A larger ordinary fiber might try to manufacture the missing moment
\(1-q^r\).  Trace-class determinant theory rules out that attempt for every
ordinary tensor fiber at once.

\begin{theorem}[Ordinary trace-class tensor obstruction]
\label{thm:tensor-rigidity}
Let \(B\) be a finite-dimensional matrix or a trace-class operator, and let
\[
        T=w(B\otimes U_{\phi,0}).
\]
For \(0<|q|<1\), it is impossible that
\(\Tr T^r=w^r\) for every \(r\ge1\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The tensor trace formula and \cref{lem:fixed-trace} would require
\[
        \Tr B^r=1-q^r
        \qquad(r\ge1).
\]
For sufficiently small \(t\), the Fredholm trace logarithm then gives
\begin{align*}
 \det(I-tB)
 &=\exp\left(-\sum_{r\ge1}\frac{1-q^r}{r}t^r\right)\\
 &=\frac{1-t}{1-qt}.
\end{align*}
The determinant of a trace-class operator is entire in \(t\)
\citep{Simon1977}, whereas the last expression has a genuine pole at
\(t=q^{-1}\).  Since \(q\ne1\), the numerator does not cancel it.  This is a
contradiction.
\end{proof}

For a finite matrix the forced power sums formally ask for eigenvalue
multiplicity (+1) at (1) and multiplicity (-1) at (q).  Negative
multiplicity identifies the missing structure: an odd sector.  The theorem
does not exclude every possible nontensor nuclear operator; it isolates the
ordinary tensor loophole and motivates the canonical grading rather than an
arbitrary signed fit.

\begin{remark}[Why the second power is decisive]
Matching only \(\Tr T\) cannot distinguish a valid determinant from a
first-order normalization.  The scalar residual \eqref{eq:scalar-residual}
and the entire-versus-pole theorem are full cyclic tests.  They prevent a
first trace-log coefficient from being promoted to an Euler factor.
\end{remark}
