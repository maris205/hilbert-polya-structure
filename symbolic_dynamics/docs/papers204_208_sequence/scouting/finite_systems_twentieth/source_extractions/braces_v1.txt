L33: # Rota-Baxter operators on braces, post-braces and the Yang-Baxter equation
L34: Li Guo Address: Department of Mathematics and Computer Science, Rutgers University, Newark, NJ 07102 Email address: liguo@rutgers.edu , Yan Jiang Address: Department of Mathematics, City University of Hong Kong, Hong Kong SAR, China Email address: yanjiang22@mails.jlu.edu.cn; yjian24@cityu.edu.hk , Yunhe Sheng Address: Department of Mathematics, Jilin University, Changchun 130012, Jilin, China Email address: shengyh@jlu.edu.cn and You Wang Address: Department of Mathematics, Jilin University, Changchun 130012, Jilin, China Email address: wangyou20@mails.jlu.edu.cn
L35: ###### Abstract.
L36: Combining the notions of braces and relative Rota-Baxter operators on groups in connection with the Yang-Baxter equation and a factorization theorem of Lie groups from integrable systems, relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces and post-braces are introduced. A relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace naturally induces a post-brace, and conversely, every post-brace determines a relative Rota-Baxter operator on its sub-adjacent brace.
L37: Furthermore, a post-brace yields two Drinfel’d-isomorphic solutions to the Yang-Baxter equation. As a special case, enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operators give rise to matched pairs of braces. Focusing on enhanced Rota-Baxter operators on two-sided braces, a corresponding factorization theorem is established. Examples are provided from the two-sided brace associated with the three-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra.

Introduction
L55: 
L56: In this paper, relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces are introduced to connect to post-braces and matched pairs of braces, to construct solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation, and to give a factorization theorem for two-sided braces.
L57: ### 1.1. Braces and set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation
L58: The Yang-Baxter equation first appeared in theoretical physics and statistical mechanics in the works of C. N. Yang [cite19†40 ]. Independently, G. Baxter employed the Yang-Baxter equation in his solution to the eight-vertex model [cite20†8 ]. Since then, the Yang-Baxter equation has been extensively studied, leading to broad achievements in various areas of mathematics and mathematical physics.
L59: In [cite21†17 ], Drinfel’d proposed to study set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation on a set $X$, which is a bijective map $R:X\times X\to X\times X$ satisfying the following equation:
L60:  | $$(R\times{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\times R)(R\times{\rm{Id}})=({\rm{Id}}\times R)(R\times{\rm{Id}})({\rm{Id}}\times R).$$  |
L61: In the seminal work [cite22†18 ], Etingof-Schedler-Soloviev introduced the structure group $G(X,R)$ to classify set-theoretical solutions. Later on, Lu-Yan-Zhu [cite23†28 ] showed that braided groups give rise to set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.
L62: In [cite24†15 ], Doikou-Rybolowicz-Stefanelli introduced the notion of Drinfel’d homomorphisms between set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation and showed that Drinfel’d isomorphic classes of left non-degenerate solutions correspond to isomorphic classes of left shelves endowed with special endomorphisms.
L63: As a generalization of radical rings, braces were introduced by Rump [cite25†30 , cite26†31 ] to produce non-degenerate involutive solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. It was proved that braces are equivalent to bijective $1$-cocycles in [cite27†12 ]. See [cite28†11 , cite29†13 , cite30†19 , cite31†29 , cite32†33 ] for recent achievements in the study of braces and their related set-theoretical solutions to the Yang-Baxter equation.
L64: Afterwards, skew braces were introduced by Guarnieri and Vendramin [cite33†22 ] as a nonabelian generalization of braces. Then the factorization problem for skew-braces was systematically studied in [cite34†25 ]. It is remarkable that skew braces are connected with other algebraic structures in numerous areas, such as matched pairs of groups, groups with exact factorizations, rings and near-rings, regular subgroups, bijective $1$-cocycles and Hopf-Galois extensions.
L65: These connections led to several new types of solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation [cite35†34 ]. Later on, Brzeziński [cite36†10 ] introduced the notion of skew trusses, as a common generalization of skew left rings and skew braces. More recently, Trappeniers [cite37†37 ] studied two-sided skew braces and showed that every two-sided skew brace is an extension of a weakly trivial skew brace by a two-sided brace.
L66: ### 1.2. Rota-Baxter operators on groups and post-groups
L67: The notion of Rota-Baxter operators on associative algebras was introduced by G. Baxter [cite38†7 ] in his probability study to understand Spitzers identity in fluctuation theory, and found applications in Connes-Kreimer’s algebraic approach to the renormalization in quantum field theory [cite39†16 ]. See [cite40†23 ] for more details.
L68: The notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators (also called ${\mathcal{O}}$-operator) on Lie algebras was introduced in [cite41†27 ], which are closely related to the classical Yang-Baxter equation and naturally induce pre-Lie or post-Lie algebras [cite42†3 , cite43†9 ]. This connection generalizes to the splitting of operads [cite44†2 ].
L69: The notion of Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups was introduced in [cite45†24 ], whose differentiation give rise to Rota-Baxter operators on the corresponding Lie algebras. The authors obtained a factorization theorem of Rota-Baxter Lie groups, realizing the Global Factorization Theorem of Semenov-Tian-Shansky on Lie groups [cite46†32 ] without going through integration.
L70: Consequently, Goncharov defined Rota-Baxter operators on cocommutative Hopf algebras in [cite47†20 ] such that many classical results still hold at the Hopf algebra level. In [cite48†5 , cite49†6 ], Bardakov and Gubarev discovered the relation between skew braces and Rota-Baxter groups. In [cite50†4 ], the notion of post-groups was introduced, as the derived structure of Rota-Baxter operators on groups. Post-groups are equivalent to skew braces, and lead to non-involutive solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.
L71: Recently, the notion of Rota-Baxter type operators on trusses was introduced in [cite51†14 ] from the perspective of Rota-Baxter operators on rings.
L72: The notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators on groups was introduced in [cite52†26 ], in order to establish a local Lie theory from the perspective of the local integration and differentiation. It was shown that relative Rota-Baxter operators on groups give rise to matched pairs of groups, a notion critical to the Yang-Baxter equation [cite22†18 , cite23†28 ]. See [cite53†36 ] for further details.
L73: ### 1.3. Main results and outline of the paper
L74: 
L75: In this paper, we introduce the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces, generalizing the Rota-Baxter theory from groups to the significantly richer setting of braces that comprise of two compatible group laws. The added complexity of braces necessitates new ideas and constructions to obtain a coherent theory with expected applications to the Yang-Baxter equation.
L76: We develop the new framework in three steps. First, we introduce the notions of relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces and of post-braces, generalizing the classical correspondence between relative Rota-Baxter operators on groups and post-groups. An interesting phenomenon is that a post-brace produces a pair of Drinfel’d-isomorphic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.
L77: Second, we impose an enhanced condition on relative Rota-Baxter operators, and show that it provides the needed restriction to obtain matched pairs of braces that extend matched pairs of groups. Finally, we apply enhanced Rota-Baxter operators to two-sided braces and establish a new factorization theorem for braces in the sense of [cite34†25 ], generalizing the classical factorization theorems for Lie groups from integrable systems [cite45†24 , cite46†32 ].
L78: The paper is organized as follows. In Section cite10†2 , we introduce the notions of relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces and post-braces, and study their relations. Moreover, we prove that a post-brace gives rise to two set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation that are Drinfel’d isomorphic (Theorem cite54†2.26 ).
L79: In Section cite14†3 , we define enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces and matched pairs of braces, and show that an enhanced relative Rota-Baxter operator on a brace induces a matched pair of braces (Theorem cite55†3.14 ). In Section cite15†4 , we utilize the notion of (enhanced) Rota-Baxter operators on two-sided braces to obtain a factorization theorem of two-sided braces (Theorem cite56†4.9 ).
L80: As an illustration of the general results, in Section cite16†5 , we provide various examples based on the brace induced by the $3$-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra.
L81: ## 2. Relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces and post-braces
L82: 
L83: In this section, we first recall the semi-trivial action of braces. Then we define the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces by using the semi-trivial action. As the underlying structures of relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces, the notion of post-braces are introduced, which can give rise to set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation.
L84: ### 2.1. Relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces
L85: 
L86: Let us first recall the notion of semi-trivial actions of braces. Then we introduce the notion of relative Rota-Baxter operators on braces, which naturally split the brace structures into post-brace structures.
L87: ###### Definition 2.1.
L88: 
L89:   1. (i)
L90: 
L91: A skew brace $(G,\cdot,\circ)$ consists of a group $(G,\cdot)$ and a group $(G,\circ)$ such that
L92: 
L93: (1)  |  | $$a\circ(b\cdot c)=(a\circ b)\cdot a^{-1}\cdot(a\circ c),\quad\forall a,b,c\in G.$$  |
L94: 
L95: Here $a^{-1}$ is the inverse of $a$ in the group $(G,\cdot)$.
L96: 
L97:   2. (ii)
L98: 
L99: A brace is a skew brace in which the group $(G,\cdot)$ is abelian. In addition, a brace $(G,\cdot,\circ)$ is called two-sided if the following condition also holds
L100: (2)  |  | $$(a\cdot b)\circ c=(a\circ c)\cdot c^{-1}\cdot(b\circ c),\quad\forall a,b,c\in G.$$  |
L101: 
L102:   3. (iii)
L103: 
L104: Let $(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})$ and $(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})$ be braces. A homomorphism of braces from $(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})$ to $(H,\cdot_{H},\circ_{H})$ is a map $\Psi:G\to H$ such that
L105: 
L106: (3)  |  | $$\Psi(a\cdot_{G}b)=\Psi(a)\cdot_{H}\Psi(b),\quad\Psi(a\circ_{G}b)=\Psi(a)\circ_{H}\Psi(b),\quad\forall a,b\in G.$$  |
L107: We denote by $\mathrm{Aut}(G)$ the set of automorphisms of the brace $(G,\cdot_{G},\circ_{G})$.
L108: ###### Proposition 2.2.
L109: 
L110: ([cite57†39 ]) If $(G,\cdot,\circ)$ is a brace, then we have
L111: 
L112: (4)  |  | $$a\circ b^{-1}=a\cdot(a\circ b)^{-1}\cdot a,\quad\forall a,b\in G.$$  |
L113: 
L114: Moreover, if $(G,\cdot,\circ)$ is a two-sided brace, then we also have
L115: 
L116: (5)  |  | $$a^{-1}\circ b=b\cdot(a\circ b)^{-1}\cdot b,\quad\forall a,b\in G.$$  |
L117: ###### Definition 2.3.
L118:
