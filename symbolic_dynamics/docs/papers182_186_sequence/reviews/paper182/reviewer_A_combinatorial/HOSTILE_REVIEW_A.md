## Adversarial method

The reviewer verifier does not import or execute the author's verifier.

- Vectors are base-\(q\) integers and subspaces are exact bitsets of all their members,
  generated by repeated span closure.  This differs from the author's
  canonical reduced-row-echelon-basis representation.
- Meets are bitset intersections; joins are freshly generated linear spans.
  Cardinalities, scalar closure, additive closure, containment, and the
  modular rank identity are checked.
- `GF(4)` is implemented as
  \(\mathbb F_2[x]/(x^2+x+1)\).  Its field laws are exhaustively checked before
  it is used, so the non-prime prime-power case is not simulated with integers
  modulo four.
- The functional graph is classified by iterative indegree-zero peeling and
  reverse breadth-first distance propagation.  This differs from forward
  orbit tracing.
- The quantities \(Q_n\) and \(\kappa_k\) are attacked by direct ordered-pair
  enumeration.  For every first subspace of rank \(a\), the reviewer counts
  all complements and checks the individual value \(q^{a(k-a)}\).
- For every carrier target, not merely one representative of each orbit or
  dimension class, the actual indegree is checked against the claimed fibre.
  The complete indegree histogram and the exact sets of maximizers and
  minimum-positive targets are then compared.

## Theorem attack

### 1. Universal identity and temporal conclusion

For \(m=a\wedge b\) and \(j=a\vee b\), direct substitution gives

\[
T^2(a,b,c)=(j,c\wedge m,c\vee m).
\]

Writing \(u=c\wedge m\) and \(v=c\vee m\), the only inequalities needed are
\(u\le m\le j\) and \(u\le v\).  Absorption yields
\(T^3=(v,u,j)\) and \(T^4=(j,u,v)=T^2\).  No distributivity, modularity,
finiteness, or field hypothesis is smuggled into this argument.  The reviewer
checked both the displayed square and `T^4=T^2` at every source in all 16
finite-field boxes.

The inference from `T^4=T^2` is also valid: \(y=T^2x\) satisfies
\(T^2y=y\), hence is periodic with period one or two.  There is no unproved
long-cycle exclusion.

**Verdict:** survives.

### 2. Image, recurrent set, periods, and depth partition

Every image has form \((c,m,j)\) with \(m\le j\), while
\(T(m,j,c)=(c,m,j)\) proves surjectivity onto precisely that set.  Solving
\(T^2(a,b,c)=(a,b,c)\) first forces \(b\le a\), after which the remaining
two equations are equivalent to \(b\le c\).  On that set the update is
\((a,b,c)\mapsto(c,b,a)\), so fixed points are exactly those with \(a=c\)
and all remaining recurrent orbits have length two.

For a nonrecurrent source, its first image is recurrent exactly when
\(a\wedge b\le c\); the other required inequality is automatic.  If this
fails, `T^4=T^2` puts the second image on the recurrent set.  Thus the stated
depth-0/1/2 partition is exhaustive and disjoint.  Indegree peeling and
reverse distances reproduce the predicates pointwise, not just their totals.

**Verdict:** survives.

### 3. Population formulas

The fixed and recurrent counts use a uniquely chosen middle subspace \(B\)
and respectively one or two superspaces in \(V/B\), giving \(\alpha_d\) and
\(\rho_d\).  The image count is an arbitrary first coordinate times an
interval.  Strict two-cycles are the nonfixed recurrent states paired by the
outer-coordinate swap, so division by two counts orbits, consistent with the
paper's explicit definition of “strict two-cycle.”

For \(Q_n\), projection modulo a fixed \(X\) injects each disjoint \(Y\); a
chosen projected \(s\)-space has exactly \(q^{as}\) graph lifts.  For
\(\eta_d\), \(M=A\cap B\) is unique, the quotient pair is disjoint, and
\(C/M\) is arbitrary.  Consequently there is neither omission nor multiple
counting in the depth-at-most-one total.  Direct disjoint-pair enumeration and
the peeled graph totals agree with all displayed formulas.

**Verdict:** survives.

### 4. Every-target fibres and the complete histogram

A predecessor of \((C,M,J)\) has source third coordinate forced to \(C\),
and its other coordinates obey \(A\cap B=M\), \(A+B=J\).  There are no
solutions unless \(M\le J\).  When \(M\le J\), quotienting by \(M\) is a
bijection to ordered complementary pairs in \(J/M\), whose rank is
\(k=\dim(J/M)\).  Choosing the first summand of dimension \(a\) and then one
of its \(q^{a(k-a)}\) complements gives exactly \(\kappa_k\).

The interval count in the histogram is also exact: choose \(M\) of dimension
\(m\), then a \(k\)-subspace of \(V/M\) to determine \(J/M\), and finally
choose \(C\).  Strict growth of \(\kappa_k\) prevents two nominal histogram
classes from collapsing.  The injection from decompositions of a
\(k\)-space into a \((k+1)\)-space misses the explicit decomposition
\((\ell,H)\), so the inequality is genuinely strict.

The reviewer compared all 413,227 target indegrees.  It also checked the
entire histogram, fibre-mass conservation, and set equality—not merely
cardinality—for the extremizers:

- maximum \(\kappa_d\): exactly \((C,0,V)\), one for every \(C\);
- minimum positive fibre 1: exactly \((C,M,M)\), one for every ordered
  choice of \(C,M\).

**Verdict:** survives.
