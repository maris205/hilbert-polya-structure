## Pairwise proof-engine checks

- **MIP versus STF:** both admit combinatorial component/load descriptions,
  but MIP changes an endofunction by first-occurrence positions and may split
  path components.  STF preserves the number of set-partition blocks and
  cyclically transports one maximum per active block.  Its queue cone and
  fibre matrices do not prove MIP's path recursion or predecessor species.
- **QIS versus BGM:** both use matrices or spans only at the level of broad
  vocabulary.  QIS is inversion followed by linear span on a projective
  finite-field lattice; BGM is Boolean row-intersection followed by graph
  squaring.  Field rank/inverse-line arguments cannot establish Boolean
  diameter closure, and clique covers cannot establish inverse-span ranks.
- **RPS versus the deterministic four:** RPS is a Markov kernel generated by
  independent permutation histories.  Its containment spectrum and cycle-
  marked conditional law depend on the sampled updates, not merely on a
  deterministic successor state.  No deterministic paper has an analogous
  history probability space.
- **STF versus RPS:** both mention cycles, but STF's cycle is the fixed cyclic
  order of blocks and controls deterministic state periods.  RPS marks cycles
  inside sampled permutations; its state process is absorbing and has no
  nontrivial recurrent orbit.
- **MIP versus BGM:** both can be represented by directed/undirected graphs,
  but MIP's graph is the current endofunction and evolves by local
  reversal/splitting, while BGM's graph appears only after the first Boolean
  Gram factor and evolves by distance doubling.
