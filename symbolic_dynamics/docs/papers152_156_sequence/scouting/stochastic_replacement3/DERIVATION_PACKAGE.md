# Derivation package — replacement-3 strongest signals

## Target

Determine whether either of the two strongest finite anomalies in the third
stochastic replacement round can support the required paper-sized conjunction:

1. the Latin intercalate-trade chain (`LIT`), whose order-3 graph is totally
   frozen while the order-4 graph is connected; or
2. random blocking-pair matching (`SMB`), whose exact three-by-three profiles
   have two stable endpoints and nontrivial rational absorption clocks.

The required output would be an all-parameter temporal transform or sharp clock
plus a genuinely independent inverse or extremal theorem.  A generic matrix
resolvent, finite linear solve, stationary uniformity, or static count is not an
acceptable output.

## Status

**NOT YET COHERENT.**  After direct-owner subtraction, neither signal supports
the target.  `SMB` is a direct kernel owned by Roth--Vande Vate.  `LIT` has a
direct move-graph owner and no closed all-order temporal invariant beyond the
generic walk on that graph.  The target therefore does not survive unchanged,
and no candidate is promoted.

## Invariant object

- For `LIT`, the only stable dynamic object is the intercalate-switch graph
  `G_n`: labelled Latin squares are vertices, and an edge is a single
  intercalate trade.  The fixed rectangle scheduler produces a lazy symmetric
  walk on `G_n`.
- For `SMB`, the stable dynamic object is the absorbing Markov chain on partial
  matchings generated by matching a currently blocking pair and freeing their
  former partners.

Neither object may be silently replaced by the static intercalate count or the
set of stable matchings; those are observables, not the temporal process.

## Assumptions

### LIT

- The Latin square is fully labelled and has order `n`.
- A scheduler slot is a pair of rows and a pair of columns, so there are
  `R=binom(n,2)^2` slots.
- If the selected rectangle is an intercalate, it is traded; otherwise the
  state holds.

### SMB

- There are equally many agents on both sides, with strict complete preference
  orders and being unmatched ranked below every partner.
- At a nonstable state, one currently blocking pair is chosen uniformly.
- The chosen pair matches and any two former partners become unmatched.

## Notation

- `A_n` is the adjacency matrix of `G_n`.
- `d(L)` is the number of intercalates in Latin square `L`.
- `R=binom(n,2)^2` is the LIT scheduler size.
- `P_n` is the LIT transition matrix.
- `B(mu)` is the set of blocking pairs of matching `mu`.
- `K(mu,nu)` is the SMB transition kernel.
- `T` is the first time SMB reaches a stable matching.
- `F_mu(z,sigma)=E_mu[z^T 1{endpoint=sigma}]` is the marked clock transform.

## Derivation strategy

1. Freeze each literal kernel independently of its finite verifier.
2. Derive the strongest exact formula available without using a black-box
   numerical solve.
3. Identify which formula steps are generic and which are carrier-specific.
4. Apply direct-owner subtraction.
5. Test whether a nontrivial inverse or sharp extremal axis remains.

## Derivation map

### LIT

1. Involutivity of a single intercalate trade gives symmetry of `A_n`.
2. The fixed scheduler gives an exact affine expression for `P_n`.
3. That expression proves uniform stationarity but does not diagonalize `A_n`.
4. An all-time transform therefore reduces to the generic resolvent of an
   already-owned graph.
5. Intercalate counts give local holding probabilities but do not recover a
   Latin square or its component in general.

### SMB

1. First-step decomposition gives a marked transform recurrence.
2. The recurrence is valid for every finite preference profile but is exactly
   generic absorbing-chain elimination.
3. Roth--Vande Vate already own the kernel, almost-sure convergence, and
   positive accessibility of every stable matching from the empty state.
4. The finite profile transforms do not yield a profile-uniform closed form,
   inverse preference reconstruction, or sharp extremizer.

## Main derivation

### Step 1. LIT kernel identity

For a Latin square `L`, every scheduler rectangle either holds or trades to a
unique square `L'`.  Trading the same rectangle in `L'` returns to `L`.
Consequently the off-diagonal move relation is symmetric.  If `A_n` is its
simple adjacency matrix and `D_n=diag(d(L))`, then the exact transition matrix
is the identity

```text
P_n = I - D_n/R + A_n/R.                          (LIT.1)
```

This is an **identity**, not an approximation.  Since every row and column of
`A_n` has sum `d(L)` at the corresponding vertex, every column of `P_n` sums to
one.  Thus uniform measure on all labelled squares is stationary.  If `G_n` is
disconnected, uniform measure restricted to any component is also stationary.

### Step 2. LIT exact temporal ceiling

For an initial row vector `v`, the ordinary generating function of time layers
is

```text
sum_{t>=0} z^t v P_n^t = v (I-zP_n)^(-1).          (LIT.2)
```

Formula (LIT.2) is a **generic finite Markov resolvent** and receives zero
credit.  A useful LIT theorem would require a carrier-specific diagonalization,
factorization, or closed statistic that determines `vP_n^t` for all `n`.
Neither (LIT.1) nor the pilot provides one.  In fact the pilot already shows
that the degree `d(L)` varies from 4 to 12 at order 4, so the chain is not a
routine random walk on a regular association scheme.

The exact order boundary is:

```text
n=3: 12 isolated vertices and no intercalate move;
n=4: one connected component on all 576 labelled squares.
```

This is a finite **observation**.  Wanless directly studies graphs whose edges
are cycle switches, including the intercalate-only variant, so even an
all-order component theorem would require substantial claim subtraction.

### Step 3. LIT inverse and extremal audit

From one transition row one can recover only which rectangles are active, hence
the set of intercalate locations in the labelled square.  The pilot and the
owner literature give no reason that this data uniquely reconstructs the
square.  Intercalate-free squares exist in many orders, immediately ruling out
a universal inverse from holding probability or degree alone.

Likewise, maximizing or minimizing `d(L)` is a static Latin-square extremal
problem.  It is not a temporal extremizer unless one proves a clock or mixing
functional is monotone in `d(L)`, which is unsupported because later degrees
depend on the full switched square.  Thus no independent inverse/extremal axis
survives.

### Step 4. SMB marked first-step recurrence

Let `S` be the set of stable matchings.  For `sigma in S`, the marked transform
satisfies the exact proposition

```text
F_mu(z,sigma) = 1{mu=sigma},                       mu in S,

F_mu(z,sigma) = z/|B(mu)|
                  sum_{b in B(mu)} F_{mu.b}(z,sigma),  mu notin S.   (SMB.1)
```

Here `mu.b` is the partial matching after the blocking pair `b` matches.
Equation (SMB.1) is justified by conditioning on the literal first update.  It
is exact for every finite strict preference profile.

### Step 5. SMB finite exact signal

Solving (SMB.1) over rational functions would produce the complete joint law.
At `z=1`, differentiating and summing over endpoints gives the mean-clock
system.  The verifier solves the corresponding rational systems independently
for two profiles of size three and obtains

```text
profile 0: two stable endpoints, E_empty T = 637/82;
profile 1: two stable endpoints,
           E_empty T = 36186760832205392/4520768244784785.
```

These are **finite exact evaluations**, not an all-parameter theorem.  The
enormous second denominator is evidence against a small universal profile
statistic, not evidence of depth by itself.

### Step 6. SMB owner subtraction and inverse audit

Roth--Vande Vate study the same update: randomly chosen blocking pairs match,
former partners become single, the process converges to stability with
probability one, and every stable matching has positive probability from the
all-single state.  Therefore the kernel, convergence, stable-endpoint support,
and random-path framing receive zero credit.

The remaining finite transform (SMB.1) is generic linear elimination on a
preference-profile-dependent graph.  Endpoint probabilities cannot generally
identify all preference orders: renaming agents, changing rankings below never
reached alternatives, and other observational equivalences preserve the law.
No restricted inverse class or sharp preference extremizer was derived.

## Remarks and interpretation

- LIT's jump from total freezing at order 3 to connectivity at order 4 is a
  good falsifier boundary, but its value is diagnostic rather than paper-sized.
- SMB's nontrivial rational clocks show that the randomness is genuine, unlike
  random scheduling of deferred acceptance.  Direct ownership nevertheless
  removes the mechanism and the main endpoint theorem.
- AIMD was the next-best analytic control: its stationary equations have a
  binary-preimage recursion, but its one-row parameter inverse is trivial and
  it has no independent extremal axis.  It therefore does not repair the
  blocker above.

## Boundaries and non-claims

- No statement here claims novelty or absence of an all-order LIT theorem.
- A bounded source-search non-hit is not used to clear any claim.
- Uniform stationarity, finite resolvents, exact rational linear solves, static
  MacMahon counts, and ordinary absorption recurrences receive zero credit.
- The order-3/order-4 LIT data and the two SMB profiles do not justify
  asymptotic extrapolation.

## Open risks

- A specialized source may analyze precisely the fixed-rectangle lazy LIT
  scheduler, beyond the cycle-switch graph sources already checked.
- Preference classes with high symmetry might admit a closed SMB transform,
  but direct-kernel ownership would still require a new residual conjunction.
- Neither possibility is strong enough for promotion in this gate.
