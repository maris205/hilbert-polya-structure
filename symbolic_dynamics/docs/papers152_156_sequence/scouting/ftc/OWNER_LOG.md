## 2. Primary-source ledger and credit subtraction

### O1. Ostafe--Shparlinski (2009/2010): exact family inclusion

Alina Ostafe and Igor E. Shparlinski,
[*On the Degree Growth in Some Polynomial Dynamical Systems and Nonlinear
Pseudorandom Number Generators*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3884),
Math. Comp. 79 (2010), 501--511,
[publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-09-02271-6).

This is the decisive construction owner.  Their system has

`f_0=X_0 g_0(X_1)+h_0(X_1)`, `f_1=aX_1+b`

when `m=1`.  After swapping FTC's coordinates to `(Y,X)=(y,x)`, FTC is

`(Y,X) -> (YX,X+1)`,

which is the specialization `g_0(X)=X`, `h_0=0`, `a=b=1`.  Moreover, their
remarks explicitly single out `g_i=X_{i+1}` and constant `h_i=a_i` as an
attractive fast family and then identify study of its periods as a question.

**Credit rule:** the triangular family, the literal specialization, polynomial
degree growth, and the rising-product shape obtainable by direct iteration are
all owner-compressed and receive zero credit.  The source studies degree
growth, exponential sums, discrepancy, and pseudorandom generators; its main
orbit estimates assume a purely periodic sequence.  It does not state the FTC
nonpermutation functional graph, labelled arms, temporal polynomial,
codomain-wide all-time fibre atlas, or inverse-identifiability theorem found in
this focused audit.

### O2. Ostafe--Shparlinski (2010): same family, distribution/hash axis

Alina Ostafe and Igor E. Shparlinski,
[*Pseudorandom Numbers and Hash Functions from Iterations of Multivariate
Polynomials*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.4519).

Section 2 uses the same triangular template
`F_i=X_iG_i(later variables)+H_i(later variables)` with an affine last
coordinate.  Thus FTC again lies in the class after coordinate exchange.  The
paper's proved axes are slow degree growth, exponential-sum/discrepancy bounds,
permutation-system averages, and a hash construction.  It does not directly
cover FTC's transient whole-plane graph or all-time fibres.

**Credit rule:** family membership and general iteration context are zero
credit; this source is not an owner of the audited theorem conjunction.

### O3. Maubach (2011/2013): triangular automorphism/permutation owner

Stefan Maubach,
[*Triangular polynomial maps in characteristic p*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.5800),
and
[*Invariants and conjugacy classes of triangular polynomial maps*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.6469).

These papers own major parts of the characteristic-`p` theory of strictly
triangular polynomial automorphisms, triangular permutation groups, maximal
orbits, conjugacy, and efficient iteration.  FTC is not in that invertible
regime: its Jacobian determinant is `x`, and the complete column `x=0`
collapses to `(1,0)` in one step.  Equivalently, its first component in swapped
coordinates has a nonunit multiplier `X`.

**Credit rule:** all generic triangular-map and finite-characteristic
iteration language is zero credit.  The automorphism/permutation theorems do
not supply FTC's noninvertible fibre and arm structure.

### O4. Ostafe (2012): maximal-period triangular systems

Alina Ostafe, *Pseudorandom vector sequences of maximal period generated by
triangular polynomial dynamical systems*, Designs, Codes and Cryptography 63
(2012), 59--72,
[publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-011-9535-8).

This citation-chain branch concerns conditions for maximal-period triangular
systems and pseudorandom sequence estimates.  FTC is not a permutation and
has `p(p-1)` transient states, so it is not a maximal-period system on
`F_p^2`.

**Credit rule:** maximal-period triangular classification is zero-credit
background, not a direct FTC graph theorem.

### O5. Konyagin--Luca--Mans--Mathieson--Sha--Shparlinski (2013/2015)

[*Functional Graphs of Polynomials over Finite
Fields*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.2718).

This primary source defines and studies functional graphs of **univariate**
polynomials on `F_q`, including graph-isomorphism algorithms and estimates for

not treat the bivariate FTC map or state its literal graph.

**Credit rule:** generic finite-field functional-graph definitions and graph
isomorphism methodology are zero credit.
