## Full internal TCSD adapter, including the summed target

The closest evaluated internal source is the prior candidate original
`TCSD_THEOREM_CONTRACT.md` and its complete `TCSD_EXACT_GAP_PROOF.md`,
not merely a matching Lucas sequence. Both were read. Translating the
old height alphabet by +1 preserves its signs. Write
`D(x)_i = sign(x_(i+1)-x_i)` and

`G_U(s)_i = [s_(i-1)=-1] + [s_i=+1]`.

Literal substitution gives U=G_U D. Consequently the **entire** UGR
inverse is the disjoint union

`U^(-1)(b) = disjoint union of D^(-1)(s), over all s with G_U(s)=b`.

The inverse adapter is explicit: every TCSD source is left at its
original labelled positions; its recovered signs determine its unique
stratum. There is no quotient, overlap or missing source. TCSD evaluates
each stratum after deleting equality edges: all-zero signs give three;
an infeasible monotone/long strict run gives zero; an alternating strict
skeleton gives a Lucas number; otherwise its doubled runs separate
Fibonacci gap factors. Hence the sum above is an all-target evaluated
static adapter and receives zero fresh inverse-enumeration credit.

The TCSD global maximum theorem alone is **not** the UGR maximum theorem.
Its bound is for one D-target, whereas multiple feasible sign targets
can map to one b. An explicit n=4 example is b=0101: signs (-,0,0,+)
and (0,+,-,0) each have three sources, giving six in the whole fibre.
Thus even here the union exceeds its largest stratum. The independent
checker recomputes the old Fibonacci formula, verifies every individual
sign fibre against literal source counts, and sums all compatible strata
for every b in all original n=3,...,10 boxes. It never reads/imports the
old TCSD verifier or its canonical.

The original TCSD proof compares one product of Fibonacci gaps by merging
those gaps. It does not give a simultaneous partition or inequality for
all sign words in a fixed G_U fibre. Its parity/equality conclusion also
classifies sign targets, not the aggregated rank targets. Therefore no
all-n transfer of the shared rank family's mixed-target bound was
established by this full summed adapter. The unchanged LNR proof's
length-budget and noncommuting-kernel comparison remains meaningful

second UGR copy. A future full union-bound adapter could still eliminate it.

## Generic period-two mechanisms and the actual scope checked

The primary publisher abstract of Goles and Olivos, *Periodic behaviour
of generalized threshold functions*, Discrete Mathematics 30 (1980),
187-189, DOI [10.1016/0012-365X(80)90121-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(80)90121-1),
states period at most two for symmetric binary threshold maps. The
primary abstract of *A short proof on the cyclic behaviour of
multithreshold symmetric automata*, Information and Control 51 (1981),
95-97, DOI [10.1016/S0019-9958(81)90199-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(81)90199-6),
states an embedding from symmetric multithreshold automata to binary ones.
These abstracts were actually retrieved in `search_4.json`; direct
opening of the 1981 body returned 403. No unread hypothesis or theorem
body is presented as a complete UGR adapter.

The actual primary [Goles et al. 2023 version 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.01854v1)
was read through its model definition, tie conventions and Proposition 1
context (HTML sections 1-3.1). Its coordinates are binary spins and
thresholds of a weighted linear neighbour sum, with an explicit tie rule.
UGR's three-valued strict comparison count is not identified with that
literal model by the shared period-two conclusion. Even the obvious
equal-neighbour-weight scalar multithreshold form fails: triples (0,1,2)
and (1,1,1) have the same centre and neighbour sum but U gives 1 and 0.
This rules out only that direct scalar representation, not all possible
threshold lifts. No complete threshold embedding for UGR was established,
and no generic period-two theorem was credited as new.
