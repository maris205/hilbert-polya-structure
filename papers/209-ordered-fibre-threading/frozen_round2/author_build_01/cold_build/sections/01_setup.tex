\section{The operation and its scope}

Let $[n]=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ and $\X_n=[n]^{[n]}$, with $n\ge0$.
For each nonempty fibre $f^{-1}(v)=\{i_1<\cdots<i_k\}$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rule}
 (Tf)(i_j)=i_{j+1}\quad(1\le j<k),
 \qquad (Tf)(i_k)=v.
\end{equation}
Every fibre and its order are computed from the old function. Since the
fibres partition $[n]$, this simultaneous prescription defines
$T:\X_n\to\X_n$. The empty function is fixed. We retain all labels;
there is no quotient by rotation or graph isomorphism.

The functional digraph of $f$ has arrows $i\to f(i)$. Its directed
cycles, including loops, are called \emph{vertex cycles}. In contrast,
$f$ is \emph{recurrent} when $T^p(f)=f$ for some $p\ge1$, and
$\per(f)$ is the least such $p$. These refer to the update on whole
functions. Every permutation is fixed by \eqref{eq:rule}, whatever its
vertex-cycle lengths, but nonpermutations can have nontrivial periods:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-cycle}
 (1,2,1)\ \longmapsto\ (2,2,1)\ \longmapsto\ (1,2,1).
\end{equation}
Here $1\leftrightarrow2$ is unchanged and the arrow from vertex $0$
alternates between its two attachments.

Our results answer two separate questions. Section~\ref{sec:recurrence}
classifies exactly the labelled graphs supporting recurrent motion and
determines their periods. Section~\ref{sec:inverse} reconstructs every
one-step predecessor directly from the target and proves that the cyclic
successor permutation uniquely maximizes the fibre, with value $2^{n-1}$
for $n\ge1$. All proofs are given here; finite checks are supplementary.

\paragraph{Known rewiring primitives.}
Replacing ordered stars by chains is established graph linearization.
Onus, Richa and Scheideler's pure linearization algorithm operates on
connected undirected graphs, separates smaller and larger neighbours, and
uses an add-wins convention when updates conflict
\citep[Algorithm~1 and Section~2]{onus2007linearization}.
Connectivity by replacing old edges with paths is also a shared primitive.
Those operations and their proofs are not claimed as new here.

The successor-repair protocols of Cramer and Fuhrmann
\citep[Sections~4 and 5.1--5.3]{cramer2005ring} and Shaker and Reeves
\citep[Figure~3 and Section~5.1]{shaker2005ring} provide a closer directed
comparison. Their successor changes shorten clockwise successor distance.
In \eqref{eq:two-cycle}, the successor of $0$ instead moves from $1$ to
$2$ before returning. Thus this loop-free transition is neither a literal
successor repair nor a batch consisting only of such shortening repairs.
This witness does not exclude every abstract conjugacy or enlarged-state
construction. Aradhya and Scheideler's tree-to-path construction
\citep[Section~3.4, Algorithm~10 and Appendix~B]{aradhya2025tree}
also rewires ordered siblings, but takes a rooted tree with depth-parity
labels; its distributed version additionally has supervisor and message
state. It is not repeated \eqref{eq:rule} on arbitrary endofunctions with
vertex cycles. We credit these primitives, ordinary path covers and
rotation/LCM calculations. The contribution here is the exact recurrent
and inverse/extremal conjunction for \eqref{eq:rule}, not a general
rewiring method or a global priority claim.
