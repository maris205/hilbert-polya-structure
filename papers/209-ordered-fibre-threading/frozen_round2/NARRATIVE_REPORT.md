# P209 narrative

The local operation is simple and old in spirit: thread each old fibre in
its increasing order and retain its destination at the last member. The
unexpected issue is that this need not make a permutation. A path can keep
moving its attachment around an unchanged labelled vertex cycle.

Theorem 1 answers exactly which configurations can do so. The proof first
expands old walks to show monotonicity of every vertex-image set. On a
periodic whole-function orbit those labelled sets, and thus every vertex's
finite/infinite backward height, are fixed. Only then can a head-height
comparison force every nonfirst fibre member onto a cycle. The graph becomes
a cycle with unbranched feeding paths, whose final labels must lie below
all cycle labels. The remaining attachment rotation yields the exact LCM
over attached cycles, with pure cycles fixed.

The independent inverse argument reads a target as increasing paths. One
distinctness condition prevents selected-head collisions; a second prevents
endpoint values from merging reconstructed fibres. The code selects all
nonmaximal old fibre members, including numerically unchanged arrows.
Its full equality analysis forces the sole maximum target to be the cyclic
successor permutation, of fibre size $2^{n-1}$ for $n\ge1$.

The mathematical result is the conjunction of those literal-specific
theorems. Ordered graph linearization, rooted tree-to-path conversion,
ordinary path covers and rotation arithmetic are credited background.
No global priority, sharp entrance clock or general-time inverse is offered.
The standalone author checker uses exactly the original $n=0,\ldots,5$
boxes. Its complete target/source and orbit output is pressure on the
proofs, not a substitute for them.
