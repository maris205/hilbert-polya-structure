\title[First-collision Hurwitz dynamics]{First-Collision Hurwitz Dynamics on
Minimal Cycle Factorizations: Sharp Tails, Fixed Census, and Exact Fibres}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{}
\keywords{Hurwitz action, transposition factorization, parking function,
finite dynamical system, transient, fibre}

\begin{abstract}
Let $\mathcal F_n$ be the minimal transposition factorizations of the long
cycle $(1\,2\,\cdots\,n)$.  At each epoch, find the first adjacent pair of
transpositions having the same lower endpoint and apply the right Hurwitz
move there; hold if no such pair exists.  Although each local move is
classical, this state-dependent scheduler creates a terminating finite map.
We prove that successive update positions increase strictly, giving the
sharp tail bound $n-2$.  Via the classical lower-endpoint bijection with
parking functions and Pollak's circular model, the fixed-state count is
$(n-1)^{n-2}$.  Independently, we classify every predecessor of every
labelled target by one inverse Hurwitz test.  The maximum one-step fibre is
$n-1$, attained uniquely by the canonical adjacent-transposition
factorization.  Exact exhaustion through $n=8$ checks 280,392 states and
reveals a stronger binomial law for complete scheduler histories; because an
all-$n$ bijection is not yet proved, that law is stated only as a conjecture.
Hurwitz actions, factorization--parking correspondences, and ordinary
Pr\"ufer enumerations receive zero contribution credit.  The owner gate is
\texttt{OWNER\_RED\_AMBER/HOLD\_EXTERNAL}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Convention, map, and subtraction}

Fix $n\ge2$ throughout.
Permutations compose in the standard order: in $\sigma\rho$, the permutation
$\rho$ acts first.  Put $c_n=(1\,2\,\cdots\,n)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:carrier}
 \Fcal_n=\{(\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_{n-1}):
 \tau_i\text{ is a transposition and }\tau_1\cdots\tau_{n-1}=c_n\}.
\end{equation}
For a transposition $(a,b)$ written with $a<b$, set $\ell((a,b))=a$.
The right Hurwitz move at $i$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hurwitz}
 H_i(\ldots,\tau_i,\tau_{i+1},\ldots)
 =(\ldots,\tau_{i+1},\tau_{i+1}\tau_i\tau_{i+1},\ldots).
\end{equation}
It preserves the product.

Define $T_n:\Fcal_n\to\Fcal_n$ by choosing the least
$i\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collision}
 \ell(\tau_i)=\ell(\tau_{i+1})
\end{equation}
and applying $H_i$; if no such $i$ exists, fix the state.  The product,
Hurwitz orientation, numeric endpoint order, and least-index convention are
part of the definition.  Reversing only the long-cycle orientation does not
preserve the dynamics.

Minimal long-cycle factorizations, their Cayley-tree count, and Hurwitz
actions are classical \cite{Denes1959,GorskyGorsky2013}.  The lower-endpoint
map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parking-map}
 (\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_{n-1})\longmapsto
 (\ell(\tau_1),\ldots,\ell(\tau_{n-1}))
\end{equation}
is the classical bijection from $\Fcal_n$ to parking functions of length
$n-1$ \cite{Stanley1997,IrvingRattan2021}.  All of this receives zero
contribution credit.  The residual claims concern the adaptive
first-collision scheduler and its target-resolved inverse graph.

Campion Loth and Rattan give a deterministic bijection built from ordered
strings of conditional Hurwitz moves in their study of star and monotone
factorizations \cite{CampionLothRattan2025}; in particular, equality of lower
endpoints appears in one case of their local construction.  That conditional
Hurwitz-string machinery also receives zero contribution credit here.  Its
move convention, monotone/string order-change scheduler, reversible-bijection
objective, and theorem output differ from the present repeated map, which
always selects the first adjacent lower-endpoint collision and studies its
tails and target-resolved fibres.  This distinction is an exact object
separation, not a novelty claim.

Internally, direct Hurwitz actions have repeatedly been owner-killed, and P181
uses first-descent prefix reversal on ordinary permutations.  The present
map is not a braid-group orbit census, a $0$-Hecke action, or a prefix
reversal.  Indeed its conditional local operators fail the braid relation
already on $((1,4),(1,3),(1,2))$.  A bounded owner search found the classical
ingredients above but no literal scheduler theorem package.  This non-hit is
not evidence of novelty, priority, completeness, or freedom to operate.

\section{Strictly advancing collisions}

For $f\in\Fcal_n$, let $i_1,i_2,\ldots$ be its successive update positions.

\begin{theorem}[scheduler normal form and sharp tail]\label{thm:tail}
Every nonempty update history is strictly increasing:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:increasing-history}
                  1\le i_1<i_2<\cdots\le n-2.
\end{equation}
Consequently every recurrent state is fixed and every tail is at most
$n-2$.  For $n\ge3$ the bound is sharp; one witness is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deep-witness}
 ((1,n),(1,2),(2,3),\ldots,(n-2,n-1)),
\end{equation}
whose history is $1,2,\ldots,n-2$ and whose terminal state is
$((1,2),(2,3),\ldots,(n-1,n))$.
For $n=2$, the sole factorization $((1,2))$ is fixed, so the same sharp
bound equals zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At an active position write
$\tau_i=(a,b)$ and $\tau_{i+1}=(a,c)$, where $a<b,c$.  The two
transpositions are distinct: otherwise they cancel and the $n$-cycle would
be expressed using fewer than $n-1$ transpositions.  The update replaces
them by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-update}
                   (a,c),(b,c).
\end{equation}
The new lower endpoints at positions $i,i+1$ are
$a,\min(b,c)$, and the second is strictly larger than $a$.  All earlier
lower endpoints are unchanged, including the endpoint at position $i$.
Since $i$ was the first old equality, no equality is created before $i$ and
the equality at $i$ disappears.  The next update position is therefore
strictly larger.  This proves \eqref{eq:increasing-history}, termination, and
the upper bound.

For $n\ge3$, the first move in \eqref{eq:deep-witness} changes the initial two factors
to $(1,2),(2,n)$.  The next collision is at position two.  Inductively the
factor $(j,n)$ meets $(j,j+1)$, and the Hurwitz move replaces them by
$(j,j+1),(j+1,n)$.  The stated history and terminal chain follow.
\end{proof}

The proof supplies a pointwise certificate: the history is a subset of
$[n-2]$ written in increasing order, and the depth is its cardinality.

\section{Exact fixed-state census}

Put $N=n-1$.  A parking function is a word $a\in[N]^N$ whose increasingly
sorted entries $b_1\le\cdots\le b_N$ satisfy $b_i\le i$.

\begin{theorem}[fixed factorizations]\label{thm:fixed}
The fixed states of $T_n$ are exactly the factorizations whose lower-endpoint
word has no equal adjacent letters, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-count}
                  |\operatorname{Fix}(T_n)|=(n-1)^{n-2}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The characterization is the stopping rule.  Under
\eqref{eq:parking-map}, it remains to count parking functions
$a_1\cdots a_N$ with $a_i\ne a_{i+1}$ for $i<N$.

Use Pollak's circular parking model on $N+1$ spots
\cite{Stanley2011EC2}.  There are $(N+1)N^{N-1}$ circular preference words
with adjacent entries unequal: choose the first preference freely and each
later one in $N$ ways.  Simultaneous translation modulo $N+1$ preserves the
inequalities, every translation orbit has size $N+1$, and each orbit has
exactly one representative that becomes an ordinary parking function after
placing the empty spot at the end.  Division by $N+1$ gives $N^{N-1}$,
which is \eqref{eq:fixed-count}.
\end{proof}

\section{Every-target inverse Hurwitz atlas}

Fix a target $y=(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_{n-1})$.  Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first-target-collision}
 j(y)=\min\{j:\ell(\sigma_j)=\ell(\sigma_{j+1})\},
\end{equation}
with sentinel value $j(y)=n-1$ when the set is empty.  For $i<j(y)$, write
$\sigma_i=(a,b)$ with $a<b$.  Call $i$ reverse-admissible when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:admissible}
 \sigma_{i+1}=(b,c)\text{ for some }c>a,
\end{equation}
where the transposition is unordered, so the condition means that it contains
$b$ and its other endpoint exceeds $a$.

\begin{theorem}[complete labelled fibre]\label{thm:fibre}
Every target has exact indegree
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fibre}
 \indeg(y)=\mathbf1_{\{j(y)=n-1\}}
 +\#\{i<j(y):i\text{ is reverse-admissible}\}.
\end{equation}
For each counted $i$, the unique nonself predecessor is $H_i^{-1}y$.
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:max-fibre}
 \max_{y\in\Fcal_n}\indeg(y)=n-1,
\end{equation}
and the unique maximizer is the canonical chain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical}
                 ((1,2),(2,3),\ldots,(n-1,n)).
\end{equation}
For $n=2$, this chain is the sole target and its self-fibre has indegree
$1=n-1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose a nonself source updates at $i$ to $y$.  Positions before $i$ are
unchanged, while the source equality at $i$ is removed, so necessarily
$i<j(y)$.  Inverting \eqref{eq:hurwitz} gives the pair
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse-pair}
 (\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i,\sigma_i).
\end{equation}
For its two lower endpoints to agree, $\sigma_{i+1}$ must contain the upper
endpoint $b$ of $\sigma_i=(a,b)$, and its other endpoint $c$ must exceed
$a$.  The coincident-transposition case would cancel and is impossible.
Thus \eqref{eq:admissible} is necessary.  Conversely it turns
\eqref{eq:inverse-pair} into $(a,c),(a,b)$; positions before $i$ retain no
collision, so the scheduler selects exactly $i$.  This proves the atlas and
uniqueness of each inverse.

A fixed target contributes itself and at most one inverse at each of the
$n-2$ positions; a nonfixed target contributes fewer.  Hence
$\indeg(y)\le n-1$.  Every adjacent pair of \eqref{eq:canonical} is
reverse-admissible, so it attains equality.  Equality forces a fixed target
with every position admissible.  If $\sigma_i=(a_i,b_i)$, admissibility makes
both endpoints of $\sigma_{i+1}$ exceed $a_i$, whence
$a_{i+1}>a_i$.  A strictly increasing parking function of length $n-1$ is
necessarily $(1,2,\ldots,n-1)$, and the classical lower-endpoint bijection
then forces the unique factorization \eqref{eq:canonical}.
\end{proof}

Iterating \eqref{eq:fibre} gives an exact finite reverse dynamic program for
every higher fibre; no uniform closed form is asserted.

\section{A verified history law left open}

The exact controls expose a stronger pattern.  We isolate it to prevent
finite evidence from silently becoming a theorem.

\begin{conjecture}[history-set law]\label{conj:history}
For every $I\subseteq[n-2]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:history-conjecture}
 \#\{f\in\Fcal_n:\Hist(f)=I\}=(n-1)^{n-2-|I|}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the number of states of exact depth $t$ would be
\begin{equation}\label{eq:depth-conjecture}
 \binom{n-2}{t}(n-1)^{n-2-t}.
\end{equation}
\end{conjecture}

The verifier proves \eqref{eq:history-conjecture} only for $2\le n\le8$ by
exhaustion.  A separate Cayley-tree stream also checks $n=9$.  The histogram
matches the occurrence-set law of a distinguished letter in a Pr\"ufer word,
but tested standard factorization--tree bijections do not identify the
scheduler history with those positions.  No all-$n$ bijection is presently
known here.  Accordingly \eqref{eq:history-conjecture}, the resulting unique
deepest-state claim, and any derived basin formula receive no theorem status.

\section{Controls and limitations}

The paper-local standard-library verifier generates the full Hurwitz orbit
from \eqref{eq:canonical}, checks its Cayley cardinality and product, the
lower-endpoint parking bijection, every orbit, every history mask through
$n=8$, and every target fibre against \eqref{eq:fibre}.  It also checks the
orientation-sensitive convention: only the frozen product/order is covered.
These computations are regression pressure, not proof or novelty evidence.

The paper does not claim a new Hurwitz action, conditional Hurwitz-string
bijection, parking-function bijection, or tree code.  It does not cover inverse-cycle conventions, other reflection
groups, nonminimal factorizations, random schedulers, or the conjectural
all-$n$ history law.  External circulation remains unauthorized under
\texttt{HOLD\_EXTERNAL}.
